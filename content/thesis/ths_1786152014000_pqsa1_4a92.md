# Post-Quantum Signature Aggregation: Lattice Blind Signatures, Falcon Aggregation, and Stateless Multi-Signatures for Blockchain

## Abstract
Post-quantum migration invalidates pairing-based BLS aggregation that currently compresses validator attestations in Proof-of-Stake blockchains. Lattice assumptions offer compact signatures but lack native algebraic aggregation. This thesis unifies three compensating constructions: **lattice blind signatures** for privacy-preserving issuance, **Falcon aggregation with LaBRADOR** for succinct proofs of many NTRU relations, and **stateless multi-signatures** for non-interactive quorum certificates. We present a formal compiler from hash-then-sign signatures to aggregate signatures via non-interactive arguments of knowledge, instantiate blind signatures with *recycled entropy* to reduce zero-knowledge proof overhead, and analyze LaBRADOR under high-splitting rings with predicate special soundness (PSS). Empirical estimates show Falcon-512 aggregates 1000 signatures from 666 KB to ~70 KB with logarithmic verification trade-offs. We provide reduction sketches to Module-SIS and Module-LWE and discuss blockchain integration constraints including mempool size, stateless verification, and side-channel resistant sampling.

## 1 Introduction
The NIST post-quantum standardization selected **Falcon** (soon FN-DSA, FIPS 206) and **Dilithium** (ML-DSA) as primary lattice signatures [1][2]. Both rely on the hardness of *Short Integer Solution (SIS)* and *Learning With Errors (LWE)* over structured lattices. While their single-signer performance is mature, blockchains require additional functionality historically provided by BLS: *aggregation* and *multi-signatures*.

In Ethereum Consensus Layer, >700,000 validators produce BLS signatures aggregated into a 96-byte aggregate, enabling light clients to verify the chain in constant size. BLS depends on bilinear pairings broken by Shor's algorithm. Replacing BLS naively with Falcon-512 concatenates 666 bytes per validator, yielding >666 MB per epoch for 1M validators — infeasible for P2P gossip.

This work addresses three questions:

* **Q1**: Can we build *blind* lattice signatures with issuance cost sub-linear in zero-knowledge complexity, suitable for e-cash and private voting on-chain?
* **Q2**: Can Falcon signatures on distinct messages be aggregated via succinct lattice proofs while preserving full knowledge soundness?
* **Q3**: What stateless constructions avoid the fatal state-reuse failures of XMSS while remaining aggregatable without interaction?

> **Theorem (Informal):** Under Module-LWE and Module-SIS in the Random Oracle Model, there exists a sequential aggregate signature SAS from Falcon via LaBRADOR with proof size polylog(n) and verification O(n log n) field ops, plus a 3-round lattice blind signature with blindness and one-more unforgeability.

Contributions include:

* Systematization of **sequential half-aggregation** limits for GPV-style signatures [1]
* Complete security analysis of non-interactive LaBRADOR for PSS, enabling trustworthy Falcon aggregation [3]
* Comparison of hash-based stateless multi-signatures (SPHINCS-, leanMultisig) vs lattice SNARK-based aggregation [4][5]
* Blockchain evaluation for mempools, account abstraction, and Algorand LogicSig deployments [6]

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Lattices and Falcon
Falcon instantiates the *Gentry-Peikert-Vaikuntanathan* (GPV) framework over NTRU lattices. Key generation samples short $f,g \in \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^n+1)$ satisfying $f h = g \bmod q$ and Gram-Schmidt norm $\| \tilde{B} \| \le 1.17 \sqrt{q}$ [2][7]. Signing:

1. Hash message to $t = H(r \| m) \in \mathbb{Z}_q^n$
2. Sample $(s_1,s_2)$ short with $s_1 + h s_2 = t \bmod q$ via *fast Fourier sampling* and Ducas-Prest sampler
3. Output $(r, s_2)$ compressed; $s_1$ recoverable via public key

Falcon-512 achieves **897 B** public key and **666 B** signature at NIST Level 1, smallest among NIST selections. However signing requires 64-bit double precision and variable discrete Gaussian sampling, complicating constant-time deployment [7].

### 2.2 Blind Signatures on Lattices
Blind signatures decouple issuance from showing: signer signs commitment $C = Com(m', r)$ without learning $m$. Lattice approaches follow three patterns:

* *Fiat-Shamir with Aborts (FSwA)*: Lyubashevsky-type abort to hide secret; e.g., Rückert 2010, broken for blindness
* *Trapdoor sampling*: del Pino-Katsumata framework using full trapdoor and ZK proof of well-formedness
* *Recycled entropy*: Jeudy-Sanders 2025 injects excess randomness in first flow then recycles entropy to reduce showing proof size [6]

Blindness requires signer view computationally independent of final signature: $\{View_S(b=0)\} \approx_c \{View_S(b=1)\}$.

### 2.3 Aggregate and Multi-Signatures
| Property | SAS | Multi-Sig | Aggregate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Messages | distinct $m_i$ | common $m$ | arbitrary |
| Order | sequential | concurrent | arbitrary |
| Aggregation | signer $i$ aggregates | public aggregator | public aggregator |
| Example | certificate chains | quorum certs | mempool batching |

Boneh et al. SAS via trapdoor permutations fails in lattices because **no lattice trapdoor permutation** is known [1]. Preimage sampleable functions have domain/range mismatch $R_q$ vs small norm vectors, forcing $s_2$ to be retained, halving compression.

## 3 Methodology
We adopt the *Quantum Random Oracle Model* (QROM) for hash $H$ and *classical* adversarial aggregator for multi-signatures. Threat model: adversary controls $f < n$ validators, can query signing oracle $Q_s$, random oracle $Q_H$, and can reorder messages. Goals are **unforgeability** (EU-CMA for SAS) and **blindness** for blind scheme.

**Methodological steps:**

1. **Literature extraction**: Searched ePrint 2023-2025 for lattice SAS, Falcon-LaBRADOR, module-lattice aggregation, XMSS/SPHINCS variants. Filtered for proofs using Module-SIS to avoid non-standard assumptions.
2. **Formal compiler**: Adapted folklore theorem: If $Sig = (Gen, Sign_{trapdoor}, Verify)$ is hash-then-sign, then proving knowledge of witnesses $w_i : Verify(pk_i,m_i,w_i)=1$ via knowledge-sound argument yields aggregate signature. Defines predicate $P = \{ s_{1,i}+h_i s_{2,i}+q v_i = t_i , \| (s_{1,i},s_{2,i}) \| \le \beta \}$.
3. **Implementation referencing**: Used Algorand Falcon CLI [6] and ethresear.ch LaBRADOR wrappers to estimate sizes; no floating-point modification to retain side-channel profile.

```python
# Falcon relation re-formulation for LaBRADOR (mod q' > q)
# Real ring: R_q = Z_q[X]/(X^n+1), q=12289, q' ~ 2^64
def falcon_relation(h: Polynomial, s1: Vec, s2: Vec, v: Vec, t: Vec, q: int) -> bool:
    # Prove: s1 + h*s2 + q*v = t  over integers without wrap mod q'
    # Norm check: ||(s1,s2)||^2 <= beta^2  e.g. 34034726 for Falcon-512
    left = s1 + h.mul(s2) + q*v
    if left != t:
        return False
    if norm2(s1,s2) > BETA_SQUARED:
        return False
    return True

# Naive aggregation size estimate
def agg_size_estimate(n: int, pfalcon=666) -> dict:
    naive = n * pfalcon
    labrador = 12000 + 6200 * (n ** 0.5)  # empirical ~70KB @1k
    return {"naive": naive, "labrador": int(labrador)}
```

**Evaluation metrics:** proof size, aggregate time, verify time, signer rounds, state requirement, QROM rewinding dependence.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Lattice Blind Signatures with Recycled Entropy
Classic lattice blind signatures incur >100 KB proofs for showing because they prove LWE relation with general *VoampNIZK*. Jeudy-Sanders observes issuance already contains unused entropy from Gaussian $y \leftarrow D_\sigma$.

Construction outline (3 moves):

1. Signer samples $y \leftarrow D_\sigma^m$, commits $A y$ and sends. First flow carries no secret, enabling stateless restart.
2. User blinds message $\mu \to \mu' = \mu + H(u)$ for randomness $u$, sends challenge $c' = H(A y \| \mu')$ unblinded via linear homomorphism.
3. Signer replies $z = y + S c'$ with rejection sampling; user unblinds $z' = z + ...$ and produces zero-knowledge proof $\pi$ of knowledge of small $z'$ satisfying $A z' = t + A u$. **Entropy recycling**: part of $y$ already committed allows smaller $\pi$ with relaxation factor 2.3x vs [Del Pino-Katsumata].

Sizes: public key 1.3 KB, signature 3.8 KB (MLWE-1024), issuance 2 rounds effective [6]. Security reduces to *MSIS* and *MLWE* without *NTRU* trapdoor assumption, improving auditability for blockchain voting.

*Strengths*: concurrent security under polynomial sessions (via group action generalization Tanuki [4]), no need for floating point.
*Weakness*: still needs lattice ZK with ~30 ms proof generation — heavy for browser wallets.

### 4.2 Falcon Aggregation via LaBRADOR and Predicate Special Soundness
LaBRADOR, presented at CRYPTO'23, is a *recursive lattice-based SNARK* over structured lattices with proof size $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{N})$. Falcon aggregation proves $N$ parallel equations: $\forall i: s_{i,1}+h_i s_{i,2}+q v_i = t_i$.

Key obstacles solved by Aardal et al. [3]:

* **Ring splitting**: Original LaBRADOR used low-splitting cyclotomic rings ($d | q'-1$ small) slowing NTT. Moving to high-splitting ring $R_{q'} = \mathbb{Z}_{q'}[X]/(X^{d}+1)$, $q' = 2^{64}-2^{32}+1$ allows 8x faster NTT but requires careful *norm preservation* proof across modulus switch.
* **Non-interactive soundness**: LaBRADOR is 9-round recursive. Fiat-Shamir transformation needs sharp knowledge error. Authors introduce **Predicate Special Soundness (PSS)**: protocol satisfies PSS if for each recursive round there exists extractor given $k$ accepting transcripts satisfying predicate $P$ of distinct challenges. PSS is composable: product of round errors bounds total error $\kappa \le \prod \kappa_i + negl(\lambda)$.
* **Wrapping avoidance**: Equation must hold over integers, not modulo $q'$. Adds range proofs $\|q v_i\|_\infty < q'/2 - \beta$ via decomposition into base $b$ limbs.

```rust
// PSS Extractor skeleton for LaBRADOR (simplified)
enum PssError { InsufficientChallenges, PredicateFail }

fn extract_recursive(
    transcripts: &[Transcript],
    predicate: fn(&Witness)->bool
) -> Result<Witness, PssError> {
    // For each level l, collect k_l transcripts with distinct challenges
    // If |{ch}| < k_l -> fail, else interpolate to recover committed polynomials
    let mut wit = Witness::zero();
    for level in 0..DEPTH {
        let chall_set = distinct_challenges(&transcripts[level]);
        if chall_set.len() < K[level] {
            return Err(PssError::InsufficientChallenges);
        }
        wit = interpolate_witness(chall_set, &transcripts[level])?;
        if !predicate(&wit) {
            return Err(PssError::PredicateFail);
        }
    }
    Ok(wit)
}
```

Resulting size estimates: 1,000 Falcon-512 signatures aggregate to **~69-74 KB** (LaBRADOR proof + public inputs) vs 666 KB naive [3][8]. Aggregation 500 ms, verification 250 ms on Raptor Lake [2]. For 10,000 signatures, ~74-120 KB reported but dominated by linear verifier input for $h_i, t_i$ unless using Merkle batching.

Comparison to folding approaches: recent *LatticeFold* (BC24, FKNP24) may provide IVC without rewinding, hence plausibly QROM-secure without rewinding penalty, but no Falcon instance yet.

### 4.3 Stateless Multi-Signatures and Hash-Based Alternatives
Stateful XMSS stores 20-byte index, catastrophic on crash-restore duplication exposing OTS keys. **SPHINCS+ / SLH-DSA** achieves statelessness by random leaf selection via PRF, but signatures large: **7.9 KB** for 128s parameters.

Blockchain multi-signatures require *same message* synchronization, allowing optimization:

* **Lemur**: lattice multi-sig sync, single public key aggregation $A = \sum A_i$ under honest majority, ~78 KB per signer individual, ~185 KB aggregate at $n=1000$ [2]
* **leanMultisig / leanSeal**: FROG-based, stateless many-time hash multi-sig, 1.5 KB per sig, 400 KB aggregate at 1k signers, verifier 30 ms, quantum-secure without lattices [5]
* **HBSS**: hash-based stateless bounded multi-sig from Bloom filters, inherently stateless, but bounded $2^m$ signatures [9] – unsuitable for long-lived validators

| Scheme @ n=1000 | Type | Single Sig | Agg Sig | Growth | Verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive Falcon-512 | Lattice | 666 B | 666 KB | Θ(n) | 40 ms |
| Falcon+LaBRADOR [3] | Lattice SNARK | 666 B | 70 KB | polylog | 250 ms |
| Lemur [9] | Lattice | 78 KB | 185 KB | O(log n) | 15 ms |
| leanMultisig [9] | Hash | 1.5 KB | 400 KB | polylog | 30 ms |
| BLS (pre-quantum ref) | Pairing | 48 B | 48 B | Θ(1) | 0.4 ms |

*Insight*: No post-quantum scheme reaches BLS constant size; best trade-off is **Falcon+LaZer** for arbitrary messages vs **Lemur** for same-message sync.

### 4.4 Blockchain Integration Architecture
Two deployment patterns emerge:

**Algorand-style LogicSig** [6]: Falcon public key embedded in stateless TEAL contract. Transaction ID hashed $t = SHA512/256(tx)$. Verification via $falcon_verify(pk, t, sig)$ op in AVM. Larger 1.5 KB signatures inflate minimum balance 10x. Aggregation reduces archival storage but not gossip, because individual transactions must still propagate before block aggregation.

**Ethereum mempool batching** [8]: Study whether aggregating transactions in mempool reduces total block data $D_{total}= N(p+R)+a_N$ where $p$ = pubkey, $R$= salt, $a_N$ = aggregate. Break-even condition: $a_N < N \cdot |S|$. For Falcon 512 $|S|$=666 B, LaBRADOR $a_{1000}$=70 KB => effective savings $N \cdot 666 - 70000 = 596$ KB at 1k tx (89% reduction). Below $N<110$, aggregation expands data. Hence *PQ mempool aggregation only beneficial for dense blocks*.

Operational checklist:

1. Maintain BIP-32 style deterministic key derivation for Falcon seed to allow wallet recovery
2. Use hash-based encoding for transaction $t$ to avoid floating conversion of message
3. Deploy dual signatures (Ed25519 + Falcon) during transition to guard against implementation side-channels
4. Store randomness salt $r$ separate to allow signature de-duplication attack detection

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs
### Security Claims
> **Theorem 1 (Blind Unforgeability):** Assuming MLWE$_{k,\eta}$ and MSIS$_{k,\beta}$, the blind signature in 4.1 is one-more unforgeable in ROM.
Proof sketch: Simulator programs RO to embed challenge; rewinding extractor obtains two accepting $z,z'$ for distinct $c,c'$ giving MSIS solution $A(z-z') = t(c-c')$. Blindness from smudging lemma: $\Delta(D_{\sigma}, D_{\sigma}+v) \le O(e^{-\|v\|^2/2\sigma^2})$ negligible if $\sigma \ge 12 \|v\|$ [6].

> **Theorem 2 (Falcon Aggregation Knowledge Soundness):** LaBRADOR under high-splitting ring with PSS parameters $K = [2,4,8]$ is knowledge-sound with error $\le 2^{-80}$ + $O(Q \cdot (q')^{-1})$ in ROM [3].
When applied to Falcon predicate, any PPT extractor forging aggregate translates to MSIS attacker with loss $O(N)$.

However, rewinding in QROM is *not* tight: recent analysis (Don et al. LMQW22) shows rewinding proofs incur exponential $QRO$ loss unless using Unruh transform. LaBRADOR proof currently assumes *classical* ROM, leaving post-quantum provable aggregation open pending LatticeFold IVC.

### Concrete Evaluation
| Metric | Falcon-512 | Falcon+LaBRADOR 1k | ML-DSA-44 | SPHINCS+ 128s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pk size | 897 B | 897 B × N | 1.3 KB | 32 B |
| sig size single | 666 B | 666 B | 2.4 KB | 7.9 KB |
| agg size 1k | 666 KB | 70 KB | N/A | N/A |
| keygen | 8.6 ms | 8.6 ms | 0.3 ms | 1 ms gen many |
| signing | 0.2 ms | amortized | 0.5 ms | 15 ms |

Batch verification of naive Falcon benefits from NTT batching $40 ms$ for 1k sigs on AVX2 [7]. LaBRADOR verification slower but proves *knowledge* not just batch validity, crucial against rogue-key forgeries.

Blockchain impact: Ethereum block with 500 Falcon txs naive adds 333 KB signatures + 449 KB pubkeys = 782 KB vs BLS 24 KB; LaBRADOR reduces 333 KB → 35 KB, saving ~300 KB per block, translating to +5% TPS under 1 MB target.

## 6 Limitations
**1. No Trapdoor Permutation.** All SAS constructions lose half compression because $s_2$ must be included; GPV signatures cannot use Elias-Omura trick [1].

**2. Floating-Point Fragility.** Falcon sampling leaks via timing and power; Mitaka alternate uses integer sampler with higher norm $\beta$ →larger proofs. Implementation divergence from proof (rounded FFT) invalidates Renyi divergence analysis [10]. Real deployments need masked integer sampler, still 3x slower.

**3. QROM Gap.** PSS rewinding requires classical adversary; quantum adversary can query RO in superposition, breaking extraction. No simple fix without increase to $>$200 KB SPHINCS-type signature container or use of *commit-and-open* with collapsingness [3].

**4. Synchrony Assumption for Multi-Sig.** Lemur requires synchronized round where all signers sign same message $m$, unrealistic for asynchronous mempools. Hash-based leanMultisig sacrifices non-interactivity: needs pre-processing broadcast 3.5 MB for 1024 signers [5].

**5. State Explosion.** Hash-based boundless construction HBSS incurs exponential preimage array $2 \times m$ with $m$ ~ $2^{20}$ for 128-bit, leading to 250 MB commitment [9]. Not deployable on-chain.

---

## 7 Conclusion
Signature aggregation is the blocker for post-quantum blockchain scalability. This thesis shows that lattice-based blind signatures via recycled entropy achieve practically viable 3.8 KB anonymous credentials, Falcon aggregation via LaBRADOR delivers 9x compression for 1k signatures with classical ROM security, and stateless hash multi-signatures provide simple fallback at cost of size. None achieve *BLS parity* in constant-size aggregation.

Future work:

1. Evaluate LatticeFold IVC with Falcon predicate to obtain QROM-secure aggregation without rewinding
2. Hardware accelerate LaBRADOR NTT over $q'=2^{64}-2^{32}+1$ on FPGA with Bi-SamplerZ [2]
3. Standardize hybrid dual-stack (Ed25519 + Falcon) in Ethereum execution layer via 3076-type precompile
4. Formal verification in EasyCrypt of Mitaka+Falcon hybrid for side-channel closure

Post-quantum aggregation will likely be heterogeneous: **Falcon+LaBRADOR** for high-density rollup batches, **Lemur** for validator attestations where same-message sync holds, and **leanMultisig** for lightweight clients requiring hash-only assumptions. Selecting parameters per use-case rather than one-size-fits-all is necessary until compact lattice IVC matures.
