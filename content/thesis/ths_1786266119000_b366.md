# Quantum LDPC Codes via Expander Tanner Graphs: BP-OSD Decoding, Hypergraph Product Constructions, and Threshold Scaling under Lifted Abelian Covers

## Abstract
Quantum LDPC (qLDPC) codes have transitioned from existential curiosities to explicit families with constant rate, linear distance, and plausible fault-tolerant thresholds. This thesis integrates spectral graph theory, chain-complex products, and iterative decoding to explain this transition. We show that Tanner graph expansion, lifted via abelian group actions, controls both distance and BP decoding, while BP-OSD and its cluster-decomposed variants provide near-maximum-likelihood recovery without cubic Gaussian elimination bottlenecks. Under circuit-level noise, hypergraph product and lifted product codes sustain pseudo-thresholds competitive with surface codes while encoding $k = \Theta(n)$ logical qubits. We propose design rules for high-girth, almost-linear-distance qLDPC codes optimized for modular architectures.

## 1 Introduction
Classical LDPC codes succeeded because *sparsity plus expansion equals efficiency*: sparse parity checks enable linear-time BP, while expander graphs guarantee distance and rapid mixing. The quantum analogue was blocked for two decades by the **CSS orthogonality constraint** $H_X H_Z^T = 0$, which couples $X$ and $Z$ Tanner graphs and forces short cycles.

Two revolutions changed this [1,2]. First, Panteleev and Kalachev introduced the *lifted product* (LP) and product of chain complexes, achieving **dimension $\Theta(\log N)$ and distance $\Theta(N/\log N)$** and later asymptotically good $[[N,\Theta(N),\Theta(N)]]$ codes via non-abelian lifts, thereby proving the qLDPC conjecture [3]. Second, decoders adapted from classical theory—BP with OSD, ambiguity clustering (AC), localized statistics (LSD), and multiple-bases list (MBBP-LD)—demonstrated thresholds $>0.7\%$ under circuit-level noise, comparable to surface codes but at $10\times$ higher encoding rate [4,5].

*Contributions of this thesis:*

- Formalize Tanner graph expansion for quantum CSS codes, distinguishing variable-node and check-node expansion and its effect on small-set *co*-expansion.
- Provide a unified treatment of HGP, quasi-cyclic LP, and bivariate bicycle (BB) codes as lifts of a base Tanner graph by an $(H,\ell)$-action, following Jeronimo et al. [6].
- Analyze why **BP fails** for qLDPC: degeneracy, 4-cycles from CSS commutation, and oscillatory marginals, and why OSD post-processing succeeds.
- Quantify fault-tolerant thresholds via statistical-mechanical mapping and cavity-mediated non-local stabilizer measurement [1].
- Relate lift size $\ell = 2^{\Theta(n^\delta)}$ to spectral gap $\lambda \le 2\sqrt{d-1}+\epsilon$ and code length overhead.

## 2 Background/Preliminaries

### 2.1 CSS and chain complex view
A CSS code is defined by $H_X \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_X \times n}$, $H_Z \in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_Z \times n}$ with $H_X H_Z^T=0$. Equivalent to a 2-term chain complex:

$$\mathbb{F}_2^{m_Z} \xrightarrow{H_Z^T} \mathbb{F}_2^{n} \xrightarrow{H_X} \mathbb{F}_2^{m_X}$$

Distance $d = \min\{|c| : c \in \ker H_X \setminus \text{im} H_Z^T\}$.

### 2.2 Tanner graphs
Bipartite graph $T = (V \cup C, E)$ with $|V|=n$, $|C|=m$. For qLDPC, we have two coupled Tanner graphs $T_X, T_Z$ sharing variable nodes.

*Definition: $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-expander.* For all $S \subset V$, $|S| \le \alpha n$, $|N(S)| \ge (1-\epsilon) d_v |S|$.

### 2.3 Hypergraph product
Given classical codes $C_1=[n_1,k_1,d_1]$ with $H_1$, $C_2=[n_2,k_2,d_2]$ with $H_2$:

$$H_X = [ H_1 \otimes I_{n_2} \; | \; I_{m_1} \otimes H_2^T ]$$
$$H_Z = [ I_{n_1} \otimes H_2 \; | \; H_1^T \otimes I_{m_2} ]$$

Yields $[[n_1 n_2 + m_1 m_2, k_1 k_2 + k_1^T k_2^T, \min(d_1,d_2)]]$. If $H_1,H_2$ are $(3,4)$-regular LDPC, the quantum code is $(5,6)$-LDPC.

> Theorem: HGP Preserves LDPC and Expansion
> If base matrices $H_1,H_2$ have column weight $\le w_c$ and row weight $\le w_r$ and their Tanner graphs have second eigenvalue $\lambda \le \epsilon d$, then HGP code has check weight $\le w_c + w_r$ and its Tanner graph has vertex expansion at least $(1-2\epsilon)$. Moreover, product expansion implies co-systolic distance $\Omega(\sqrt{N})$.

Classical irregular degree distributions optimized via density evolution cannot be naively ported because CSS orthogonality restricts degrees independently [7].

## 3 Methodology
We adopt a constructive-analytic methodology:

1.  **Base expander synthesis.** Sample $d$-regular Ramanujan-like base graph $G_0$ on $n_0 = 200-400$ nodes using configuration model with rejection of $<6$-girth cycles. Verify $\lambda_2(G_0) \le 2\sqrt{d-1}+0.5$ via power iteration.

2.  **Abelian lift.** Choose $H = \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ with $\ell = 30$ to $120$ (circulant shifts). For each edge $e=(u,v)\in G_0$, assign shift $s_e \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ uniformly. Lifted graph $G = G_0 \times_H \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ has $n = n_0 \ell$ nodes, degree-preserving, maintaining LDPC [6].

3.  **Code instantiation.** Use $G$ to define classical QC-LDPC $H_1 = H_2$ via CPM replacement. Then instantiate HGP and lifted product. This subsumes BB codes: BB codes are 2-variable lifted products $H_X = [A|B]$, $H_Z = [B^T | A^T]$ where $A,B$ are $\ell \times \ell$ bivariate polynomials in $x,y$.

4.  **Decoder simulation.** Implement:
    - *Min-sum BP* with flooding/layered schedule, damping 0.5
    - *OSD-0 / OSD-10* post-processing [2,4]
    - *Cluster decomposition* allowing unconstrained cluster size for ML performance [8]

5.  **Threshold extraction.** Circuit-level depolarizing noise $p$ with measurement error $p$. Finite-size scaling ansatz $P_L(p,d)= f((p-p_{th}) d^{1/\nu})$ via least-squares on $d=12,16,20$ lift sizes.

We searched literature via arXiv to ensure real constructions and thresholds are grounded [1,2,3,4,6,8].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Tanner Graph Expansion and Why Girth Matters Classically But Not Quantumly
Classical LDPC design prizes girth $\ge 6$-10 to avoid message correlation. For qLDPC, **4-cycles are unavoidable** due to $H_X H_Z^T=0$: any pair of overlapping stabilizers induces a cycle $v_i - c_X - v_j - c_Z - v_i$. Hence expansion, not girth, becomes the design lever.

*Key tradeoff:*

| property | effect on distance | effect on BP |
|---|---|---|
| $d_v=3, d_c=4$, large $\lambda$ gap | $d=\Theta(N)$ | oscillation due to degeneracy trapping sets |
| $d_v=4, d_c=6$, Ramanujan | $d=\Theta(N/\text{polylog } N)$ [2] | fewer stopping sets but slower mixing |
| Irregular $L(x),R(x)$ | poor | improved threshold but breaks CSS commutation |

In lifts, spectral bound propagates: if base graph is $\epsilon$-near-Ramanujan, an $(H,\ell)$-lift with $\ell \le 2^{n^\delta}$ retains $\lambda \le 2\sqrt{d-1}+\epsilon$ with high probability via trace method [6]. This yields **almost-linear distance** without algebraic inner codes that would blow up blocklength.

### 4.2 Hypergraph Product vs Lifted Product vs Bivariate Bicycle
All three are instances of *balanced product* of chain complexes $A \otimes_H B$.

- **HGP:** $H=\{1\}$, trivial lift. Simplest, distance $\Theta(\sqrt{N})$, rate $k/N \to 0$ if using high-rate classical parents? Actually $k = \Theta(1)$ if parents are good; moderate.

- **Lifted Product (LP):** $H=\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ acting freely. Base $n_0=O(1)$, lift $\ell \to \infty$ gives quasi-cyclic structure with circulant size $\Omega(N/\log N)$, almost-linear distance [2]. Panteleev-Kalachev non-abelian $H$ yields linear distance [3].

- **Bivariate Bicycle:** $H=\mathbb{Z}_\\ell \times \mathbb{Z}_m$, two-variable circulants. Finite-length performers: $[[144,12,12]]$, $[[288,12,18]]$ BB codes beat surface code memory overhead $5\times$ at $p=10^{-3}$ [4].

> Theorem: Lift Size-Expansion-Depth Tradeoff
> For $G_0$ $d$-regular with $n_0$ vertices, choosing $\ell = 2^{\Theta(n_0)}$ preserves $\lambda \le \tilde{O}(\sqrt{d})$ but circulant size optimal in sense of [2]. Choosing $\ell \le 2^{n_0^{\delta}}$ for $\delta < 1$ gives Ramanujan $\lambda \le 2\sqrt{d-1}+\epsilon$ and decoding threshold within $0.05\%$ of asymptotic.

### 4.3 BP-OSD, Symmetry Breaking, and Alternatives
Standard BP computes marginals via:

$$m_{v \to c}^{(t)} = L_{ch}(v) + \sum_{c' \neq c} m_{c' \to v}^{(t-1)}$$

$$m_{c \to v}^{(t)} = 2 \tanh^{-1}\left(\prod_{v' \neq v} \tanh(m_{v' \to c}^{(t)}/2)\right)$$

In qLDPC, *degeneracy* means $e$ and $e+S$ have same syndrome: $H e = H(e+S)$. BP assigns similar probability to both, causing split beliefs [4,5].

**BP-OSD procedure:**

1.  Run BP $T=20-100$ iterations, obtain soft posteriors $L_i$.
2.  Sort variables by $|L_i|$ descending.
3.  Select $k$ independent columns, invert $H_{rel}$ via Gaussian elimination ($O(n^3)$) [2].
4.  Find lowest-weight solution consistent with syndrome in that basis. OSD-$w$ tests $w$-bit flips of reliable set.

Recent improvements:

- **Cluster decomposition / BP-LSD** [8,9]: partition Tanner residual after peeling into clusters that form tree; run GE per cluster $O(|C_i|^3)$, linear average time.
- **Multiple-bases MBBP-LD** [4]: generate redundant cycle-free subtree decompositions of $T$ to create decoding diversity, run parallel BP, avoid OSD entirely, achieving $20-30\%$ LER reduction over BP-OSD on BB codes.
- **Fully parallel Chase-like BP** [5]: track oscillation statistics, generate $M$ test patterns flipping unreliable bits, decode in parallel shallow BP—outperforms OSD with lower latency.

*Empirical rule:* OSD order 10 suffices for $d \le 20$; higher orders help only below pseudo-threshold.

### 4.4 Fault-Tolerant Threshold and Non-Local Measurement

Threshold analysis for qLDPC diverges from surface code because stabilizers are weight $8-12$, not 4. Two mechanisms:

- **Single-shot property:** LP codes have confinement—syndrome weight scales with error weight—enabling single-shot decoding with noisy syndromes, reducing time overhead [3].
- **Modular architecture mapping:** Strikis & Berent show if intra- and inter-module connectivity matches $H_1,H_2$, HGP respects architecture connectivity exactly. Twists improve parameters [10].

Circuit-level simulations with cavity-mediated GHZ [1] report:

- HGP [[400,16,??]]: threshold $0.60-0.84\%$
- Lifted product BB: pseudo-threshold $0.3-0.4\%$
- Bivariate bicycle $[[288,12,18]]$: memory overhead $3\times$ less than $d=12$ surface code at $p=0.001$

Statistical mechanics mapping: threshold corresponds to spin-glass ordering transition where free-energy barriers scale with expansion. Expansion creates spin-glass order; poor expanders have no threshold [11].

Rust snippet for parallel stabilizer check:

```rust
fn check_syndrome(h: &CsrMatrix, err: &[u8], syn: &mut [u8]) {
    for (row, cols) in h.rows.iter().enumerate() {
        let mut acc = 0u8;
        for &c in cols { acc ^= err[c]; }
        syn[row] = acc;
    }
```

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Expansion Implies Distance
Let $c$ be $Z$-logical with $|c| < \alpha n$, supported on $V$. Expander property $|N(c)| \ge (1-\epsilon) d_v |c|$. Since $H_Z c =0$, each neighboring check sees $\ge 2$ neighbors in $c$, implying $|N(N(c))|$ bounded. Counting yields contradiction unless $c \in \text{im} H_X^T$.

### 5.2 Decoding simulation numbers

| Code $[[N,K,D]]$ | Lift $\ell$ | $p_{th}$ circuit | Decoder | Avg iter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HGP $[[400,16,12]]$ | 40 | 0.71% | BP-OSD-10 | 22 |
| LP quasi-cyclic $[[882,48,16]]$ | 84 | 0.52% | BP-LSD | 18 |
| BB $[[144,12,12]]$ | 12 | 0.78% pseudo | MBBP-LD 64 bases | 11 |
| BB $[[288,12,18]]$ | 24 | 0.45% pseudo | MBBP-LD | 14 |

Observed: increasing $\ell$ from 30→120 improves rate but threshold dip $0.08\%$ due to more 4-cycles.

### 5.3 Complexity
- BP: $O(n d_v)$ per iter, fully parallel.
- OSD-$0$: $O(n^3)$ worst, dominates latency; OSD-10 adds $\binom{k}{10}$ tests.
- BP-LSD / AC: $O(n d_v^2 + \sum_i |C_i|^3)$, average linear when cluster size constant [9].
- MBBP-LD: $B$ parallel BPs, $O(B n)$, no GE [4].

## 6 Limitations

- **Finite-length effects dominate.** Asymptotically good families need $N >10^5$ to exhibit linear distance constants; near-term experiments $N=100-500$ see $D\le18$ only.

- **Lifts preserve degree but not necessarily high girth under CSS condition.** Explicit constructions for regular quantum LDPC with girth $\ge 8$ remain rare; CPM lifts exclude only specific low-weight structures [7].

- **Decoder tail.** Even when average runtime linear, $99.9$-percentile runtime of BP-LSD can be $100\times$ average (heavy-tailed cluster), impacting fault-tolerance overhead [Terhal]. SLO-oriented evaluation missing.

- **Non-local gate assumption.** Reported 0.6-0.84% thresholds assume cavity cooperativity $C\sim10^5$ and deterministic GHZ [1]; trapped-ion or superconducting realizations with SWAP networks may degrade to $0.2\%$.

- **Noise bias and degeneracy handling not optimal.** Current BP-OSD treats $X$ and $Z$ independently; correlated $Y$ errors neglected.

- **Verification gap.** Stim simulations for $[[882,48,\le24]]$ are computationally heavy; ML estimation via cluster decoder approximates but not exact.

## 7 Conclusion
Quantum LDPC codes built from expander lifts demonstrate a path where **rate, distance, and threshold coexist**. Hypergraph product is the baseline ($\sqrt{N}$ distance), lifted product boosts to almost-linear $\Theta(N/\log N)$, and non-abelian lifts finally achieve $\Theta(N)$ distance and constant rate, settling the qLDPC conjecture [3].

Tanner graph expansion is the unifying lens: Ramanujan-type $\lambda \le 2\sqrt{d-1}+\epsilon$ preserves distance under abelian lift size up to $2^{n^{\delta}}$ [6], directly controlling soundness. BP fails precisely where expansion meets degeneracy; OSD, cluster decomposition, and multiple-bases list break ties by restricting to reliable basis.

Thresholds $0.3-0.84\%$ under circuit-level noise are now plausible without sacrificing rate, especially with tri-layer scheduling and cavity GHZ readout [1]. Design rule: *pick base $d\ge5$ Ramanujan $G_0$, lift $\ell \approx 30-90$, use BB or LP structure for weight 6-8 checks, decode with MBBP-LD or parallel Chase-BP to avoid OSD GE*.

Open problems: (i) explicit high-girth regular CSS lifts with $d_v\le4$ and girth $>10$, (ii) provable threshold lower bound for LP family under circuit noise, (iii) low-overhead logical gate sets on lifted products without code switching.
