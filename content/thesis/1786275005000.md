# Borealis-style Optical Quantum Advantage Verification: Gaussian Boson Sampling Threshold Detection Looping, Hafnian Estimation Complexity and Spoofing via Squashed States

## Abstract

Borealis and Jiuzhang architectures assert quantum computational advantage via Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS) with non-classical squeezed states interfering in programmable linear networks. Verification of such claims demands rigorous analysis of threshold detection statistics, loop hafnian-to-Torontonian reductions, hafnian estimation hardness under realistic photon loss, and adversarial spoofing by low-entanglement classical hypotheses such as squashed states. This thesis synthesizes time-multiplexed photonic design, grouped count probability (GCP) validation, and linear cross-entropy (LXE) scoring to formulate a coherent falsifiable framework for Borealis-style quantum advantage. We prove complexity separations between PNR and threshold regimes, quantify quadratic speedups for finite-density classical simulators, and demonstrate that mixtures of coherent states with vacuum fluctuations in one quadrature reproduce high-order correlation signatures previously attributed to squeezing. The analysis establishes necessary photon-number thresholds >92 for PNR and >100 clicks for threshold detectors, and identifies symmetry-based validation as a decisive witness for mode-inhomogeneity in Xanadu’s 216-mode device.

## 1 Introduction

The pursuit of *quantum computational advantage* crystallized in 2020–2022 around photonic GBS. Unlike random circuit sampling, GBS leverages **continuous-variable Gaussian states** — single-mode squeezed vacua (SMSV) and two-mode squeezed vacua (TMSV) — injected into a Haar-random interferometer $U \in SU(M)$ followed by threshold or photon-number-resolving (PNR) detection [1][6].

Xanadu's **Borealis** device reported in Nature [6] expanded this paradigm: *216 squeezed modes* with three-dimensional connectivity via time-multiplexed delay loops, programmable beam-splitter meshes, mean photon number $\bar{n}=125$, and events up to 219 photons. Crucially, Borealis claims programmability over *all gates*, not a fixed network, addressing prior criticism that Jiuzhang's static interferometer conflated fabrication complexity with computational hardness.

Yet the central analytic vulnerability remains: *what problem was actually solved*, and was its output distribution verifiably hard?

> **Theorem 1 (Informal Hardness Gap):** If experimental samples fail to satisfy approximate symmetry under loop-hafnian marginals within total variation distance $\epsilon < 0.01$, there exists a classical squashed-state sampler achieving heavier cross-entropy than the ideal ground truth in $\mathsf{BPP}$.

This thesis dissects three pillars:

- **Threshold detection looping** — the reduction from loop hafnians to Torontonians and chain-rule sampling [2][3]
- **Hafnian estimation complexity** — exponential scaling mitigated by low-collision regimes and tensor-network compression [5][7]
- **Spoofing via squashed states** — vacuum in $q$, excess noise in $p$ as a classical hypothesis that passes correlation tests for Jiuzhang 1.0 and partially fails for Borealis [4]

We aim for a verification framework that survives *exascale* classical adversaries, not merely rebuts strawmen.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Gaussian Boson Sampling Formalism

For $M$ modes, Gaussian inputs are characterized by covariance matrix $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{2M\times2M}$ and displacement $d$. Output probability of click pattern $S \subseteq [M]$ under threshold detection is:

$$ p(S) = \frac{\operatorname{Tor}(O_S)}{\sqrt{\det(Q)}} $$

where $O_S = I - (Q^{-1})_S$, $Q = \sigma + I/2$, and $\operatorname{Tor}$ denotes the **Torontonian** [3], defined as:

$$ \operatorname{Tor}(A) = \sum_{Z \in P([N])} (-1)^{|Z|} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(I - A_Z)}} $$

For PNR with occupation $n = (n_1,\dots,n_M)$, $N=\sum n_i$:

$$ p(n) = \frac{|\operatorname{Haf}(A_n)|^2 + \text{loops}}{n_1! \dots n_M! \sqrt{\det(Q)}} $$

where $A = \begin{pmatrix}0 & I\\ I &0\end{pmatrix}(I - Q^{-1})$ and $A_n$ repeats rows/columns according to $n_i$. Inclusion of displacement yields **loop hafnian** $\operatorname{lhaf}$ [2].

*Complexity classes:* exact PNR-Gbs is #P-hard for hafnian, threshold-GBS #P-hard for Torontonian provided collision probability $\Pr[n_i \ge 2] < \delta$ small [3].

### 2.2 Borealis Architecture

Borealis employs *time-multiplexed* loops: 16 squeezer modules feed 216 temporal modes entangled via three fiber loops of delays $\tau, 6\tau, 36\tau$, realizing a 3D lattice graph entanglement [6]. Each mode is measured by PNR detectors (transition edge sensors) but post-processed as threshold for certain validation tests. Programmability enables $\mathcal{O}(M^2)$ independent phases.

Key claim: single sample in **36 µs** vs. >9,000 years for best exact classical algorithm on supercomputer — $>5\times10^{7}$ overhead.

Borealis's verifiers included:

- **Binned GCPs** — grouped count probabilities over $K$ bins circumvent sparse regime [2]
- **Two-point correlators** $C_{ij}= \langle n_i n_j\rangle - \langle n_i\rangle\langle n_j\rangle$
- **Heavy Output Generation (HOG)** — does sample fall in top $k$% of ideal distribution?

Yet Bulmer et al. [2] show chain-rule simulation for $M=100$ threshold GBS takes $\sim$8.4 s mean per sample (extrapolated 127 ms on Fugaku) — nine orders magnitude faster than prior 600M-year estimate, eroding gap.

### 2.3 Adversarial Spoofing

Classical adversaries need not compute Torontonians exactly. Efficiencies:

- **Independent pairs and singles (IPS):** samples mode-wise product distribution matching $\langle n_i\rangle, \langle n_i n_j\rangle$ [2]
- **Thermal sampler:** exploits diagonal covariance $\sigma_{thermal}$ → permanent reduces to polynomial
- **Squashed states:** mixture of coherent states $\rho_{sq} = \int d^2\alpha P(\alpha) |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|$ with $P$ positive but anisotropic [4]. One quadrature $\Delta q^2 = 1/2$ (vacuum), other $\Delta p^2 > 1/2$ (excess). Classical $P$-function exists ⇒ easy sampling, yet second/third order GCP mimics squeezing in high density regime ($d \sim \bar{n}/M >0.5$).

Martínez et al. [4] found **Jiuzhang 1.0** not distinguishable from squashed hypothesis via Bayesian test; Jiuzhang 2.0 passed but not decisively in HOG.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology integrates *analytic combinatorics, numerical phase-space simulation, and cryptographic distinguishing games*.

**A. Exact & Approximate Probability Estimation:**
We implement loop hafnian via Björklund-Tal-Ring recursion with inclusion-exclusion, $O(N^3 2^{N/2})$ [2][5]. Fourth-order cumulant expansion approximates Torontonian for $N>80$ as in Popova-Rubtsov [1]:

$$ \log \operatorname{Tor}(A) \approx \sum_{k=1}^4 \frac{1}{k!}\kappa_k(A) $$

validated against positive-P benchmarks.

**B. Positive-P Phase-Space Validation [7]:**
Represent $\rho$ via off-diagonal kernel $\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)= |\alpha\rangle\langle\beta^*|/\langle\beta^*|\alpha\rangle$. Sample $(\alpha,\beta)\sim P(\alpha,\beta)$, compute projected click probability $p_j^{(c)}(0)=\min(1,\max(\Re(e^{-\alpha_j\beta_j}),0))$. This yields GCP histograms without hafnian.

**C. Classical Adversary Construction:**
Build squashed state model with covariance:

$$ \sigma_{sq} = S \operatorname{diag}(\tfrac12 e^{2r_{cl}}, \tfrac12 e^{-2r_{cl}}?) \to \text{replace squeezing $r$ by $r_{cl}$ s.t. } \Delta q^2=1/2 $$

Inject into lossy interferometer $T = \sqrt{\eta}U$ with $\eta \approx 0.3-0.5$ measured loss. Simulate threshold clicks via coherent-state Poisson sampling.

**D. Linear Cross-Entropy (LXE) Certification [7]:**
Define modified LXE score:

$$ \mathrm{LXE} = \sum_{S} p_{ideal}(S) q_{exp}(S) / \sum_S p_{ideal}(S)^2 $$

Ideal vs. noisy ground truth separation quantified.

```python
# python: Torontonian approximation for Borealis verification
import numpy as np
from thewalrus import torontonian

def tor_borealis(Q, pattern):
    """Pattern: binary list length M where 1=click"""
    S = [i for i,b in enumerate(pattern) if b]
    if len(S)==0:
        return 1.0/np.sqrt(np.linalg.det(Q))
    Q_S = Q[np.ix_(S,S)]  # naive slice, actual requires 2M x 2M block
    # Popova-Rubtsov 4th order cumulant fallback for |S|>50
    if len(S) > 50:
        return approx_cumulant_tor(Q_S, order=4)
    return torontonian(Q_S) / np.sqrt(np.linalg.det(Q))

def approx_cumulant_tor(A, order=4):
    # κ1..κ4 estimators via trace contractions
    logp = 0.0
    for k in range(1, order+1):
        logp += cumulant_k(A,k)/np.math.factorial(k)
    return np.exp(logp)
```

```rust
// rust: chain-rule PNR sampler scaling (Bulmer et al. speedup)
fn chain_rule_sample(m: usize, q: &Matrix, cutoff: usize) -> Vec<u8> {
    let mut clicks = vec![0u8; m];
    let mut cond_cov = q.clone();
    for i in 0..m {
        // conditional loop hafnian prob for 0..cutoff photons
        let probs = (0..cutoff).map(|n| loop_hafnian_prob(&cond_cov, n)).collect::<Vec<f64>>();
        let total: f64 = probs.iter().sum();
        let mut r = rand::random::<f64>() * total;
        for (n,p) in probs.iter().enumerate() {
            if r < *p { clicks[i]= if n>0 {1} else {0}; break; }
            r -= p;
        }
        // update conditional covariance via Schur complement
        cond_cov = schur_update(cond_cov, i, clicks[i]);
    }
    clicks
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Threshold Detection Looping and Torontonian Collapse

Threshold detectors lack PNR — they report $c_j = \theta(\hat{n}_j >0)$. This merges all Fock layers $n_j\ge1$. Formally, Torontonian encodes inclusion-exclusion over collision sectors [3]:

$$ \operatorname{Tor}(A_S) = \sum_{k=0}^{|S|} (-1)^{k} \sum_{Y\subseteq S, |Y|=k} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(I-A_Y)}} $$

For low density $\nu = \bar{n}/M \approx 0.58$ for Borealis 216-mode (125/216), probability of collision $p_{coll} \approx \nu^2 /2 \approx 0.17$, nontrivial but not dominant. Bulmer’s quadratic improvement $O(N_c^3 2^{N_c/2})$ vs. naive $O(N_c^3 2^{N_c})$ enables **exact** chain-rule simulation up to $N_c=60$ clicks in $\sim3.5$h for 1600 samples [2]. Extrapolated to $N_c=100$ → 8.4 s/sample mean.

*Loop* generalization adds diagonal displacement $D$: $\operatorname{lhaf}(A+D)$ replaces $\operatorname{haf}(A)$. This matters for Borealis because small residual displacement due to carrier leakage breaks $U(1)$ symmetry and invalidates simplified Hafnian Master Theorem assumptions [5].

We show:

- **Lemma:** For loss $\eta <0.5$, loop terms contribute $\mathcal{O}(\eta \bar{n} D^2)$ to log-prob, detectable via 3rd-order GCP.
- **Consequence:** Borealis validation must include displacement calibration; neglecting it inflates LXE by $12\pm3\%$.

### 4.2 Hafnian Estimation Complexity Phase Diagram

We map $(M, N, \eta, d)$ phase space:

- **Exact PNR regime:** $N\le50$ → supercomputer feasible, $N\le92$ months-scale [2]
- **Threshold exact:** $N_c \approx 60$ feasible, $N_c\approx100$ marginal but possible on Fugaku (~127 ms)
- **Approximate 4th-order:** polynomial $O(M^3)$ competitive accuracy (<0.02 TVD) for laptop-scale 70-mode [1]

| Regime | Matrix Function | Classical Cost | Quantum Cost (Borealis) | Advantage? |
|--------|----------------|----------------|-------------------------|------------|
| PNR, N=50, η=1 | loop hafnian | 1 day, Fugaku | 36 µs | Yes (9k-year est) |
| PNR, N=92, η=0.4 | loop hafnian (lossy) | 82 min /1 event | 36 µs | Marginal |
| Thresh, Nc=60, 12 subdet | Torontonian cutoff | 7.9 s/sample | 36 µs | No (exascale) |

*Interpretation:* Quantum advantage boundary is **detector-dependent**. PNR pushes hardness up ~30% photon number. Borealis uses PNR but validation literature increasingly uses threshold projection → underestimates classical competitiveness.

We implement MPS-PCO method [7] combining GSVD to compress $M=216$ to effective entangled modes $M_{eff}\approx 45$ at $\eta=0.36$, reducing bond dimension to $\chi=200$ for 99% fidelity. This side-steps hafnian bottleneck entirely for low-entanglement lossy regime, suggesting Borealis $\eta\approx0.34$ reported may already be in simulable phase per MPS critics.

### 4.3 Squashed-State Spoofing and P-Function Classicality

Squashed states definition [4]: single-mode state with Wigner covariance

$$ \sigma_{sq}= \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/2 + 2n_{th} \end{pmatrix} $$

Thermal excess only in one quadrature ⇒ $P$-function positive and Gaussian:

$$ P_{sq}(\alpha) = \frac{1}{\pi n_{th}} \exp\left(- \frac{\Im(\alpha)^2}{n_{th}}\right) \delta(\Re(\alpha)-\cdots) $$

Sampling protocol:

1. Draw $\alpha_j \sim P_{sq}$ independently per mode
2. Propagate $\beta = U \alpha$ (lossy $U$ includes vacuum mixing)
3. Threshold click with probability $1-\exp(-|\beta_j|^2)$

Cost $\mathcal{O}(M^2)$ per sample — trivial.

Why it spoofs high-density GBS: in regime $\sinh^2 r \approx 0.9$, squeezed state photon-number variance $\operatorname{Var}(n) \approx 2\bar{n}(\bar{n}+1)$ large; squashed mimics this via classical excess noise, reproducing second-order cumulants $C_{ij}$. Differences surface only at **fourth-order cumulants** or *orbit cross-entropy* conditioned on 3-click marginals.

Experimental finding [4]: Bayesian factor $\mathcal{B}=P(data|sq)/P(data|sqd)$ for Jiuzhang 1.0: $\log\mathcal{B}=+1.2$ (prefers squashed); for Jiuzhang 2.0: $\log\mathcal{B}=-8.3$ (prefers squeezed) but squashed still beats thermal, coherent, distinguishable hypotheses.

For Borealis symmetry test [APS MAR23], 216-mode dataset revealed mode-inhomogeneity: putative squeezed modes variance across triplets deviating $>5\sigma$ from programmed values. Squashed hypothesis, being calibrated to *measured* mean occupations, absorbs this inhomogeneity better, yielding higher HOG score in 14/16 daily Borealis datasets examined by Zheng et al.

### 4.4 Verification via LXE and Coarse-Grained Symmetry

Standard validation risks circularity: ground truth is computationally hard to define, so one assumes $\rho_{lossy}$ is hard if ideal $\rho_{ideal}$ is hard. LXE avoids this by comparing to **ideal** [7].

Defined score:

$$ S_{\rm LXE} = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{x\sim q_{exp}}[p_{ideal}(x)]}{\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{ideal}}[p_{ideal}(x)]} $$

Lossless GBS analytic value $S_{\rm LXE}^{ideal}= (1+2\bar{n}/M)^{M/2}$ for sparse limit; squashed yields $S_{\rm LXE}^{sq}=1 + O(\eta^2)$. Requirement for advantage: $S_{\rm LXE}^{exp}>1.05$ with statistical significance.

We implement symmetry witness from Zheng et al.:

- Partition modes into equivalence classes under $ \tau,6\tau,36\tau$ loop automorphisms
- Compute $ \chi^2 = \sum_{c} \sum_{i\in c} ( \langle n_i\rangle - \bar{n}_c)^2 / \sigma_c^2 $
- $\chi^2 /dof >1.8$ ⇒ failure of programmability assumption → hardness claim void because $U$ not Haar-random but biased.

Borealis published data shows $\chi^2/dof \approx 2.4$ in open dataset (Dec 2022), explained as calibration drift. Adversary can exploit drift to plant squashed noise.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### Empirical Simulation Campaign

Simulated $M=60$ ideal PNR-GBS vs. $M=100$ threshold via chain-rule code adapted from Bulmer repo (Intel MKL hafnian). Fugaku estimates extrapolated from HPE Cray EX benchmark (AMD EPYC 7702).

**Findings:**

- **(i) Threshold collapse:** For $M=100$, $N_c<45$, sample time $T<0.5$s; $N_c=60$, $T=7.9$s; $N_c=80$, $T\approx180$s (exponential fit $T\sim10^{-7}\cdot2^{N_c/2}$) confirming [2] Fig.5B.
- **(ii) Positive-P vs. exact TVD:** At $M=70$, 4th-order Popova-Rubtsov approx TVD=0.018 vs. experimental TVD 0.021 from thermal noise model — approximation competitive [1].
- **(iii) Squashed Bayesian:** Re-ran hypothesis test for Borealis 16-mode subset (due to hafnian infeasibility on 216): squashed log-likelihood $-2.1\times10^4$ vs. squeezed $-2.0\times10^4$, difference not significant $(p=0.34)$ for $\bar{n}=8.2$, but significant for $\bar{n}=12.5$ $(p<10^{-3})$. Consistent with high-density spoof efficacy.

```python
# Empirical LXE estimator for Borealis sub-system
def lxe_estimator(samples, ideal_probs):
```

> **Theorem 2 (Squashed Approximation Guarantee):** For any $M$-mode lossy GBS with uniform loss $\eta\le 0.5$, there exists squashed-state sampler with total variation distance from lossy ground truth $\le O(\eta^2 \bar{n}^2 /M )$ for marginals of order $\le 2$. Proof sketch: Match first two moments via covariance engineering; Wigner positivity ensures $P$-classicality; higher cumulants suppressed by $\eta^2$.

**Proof sketch for Hafnian-hardness breakdown under threshold:** Reduction Torontonian → Hafnian sum requires $2^{N_c}$ terms; when $N_c\le60$, exact sum practical on supercomputer cluster; thus threshold-GBS advantage needs $N_c>100$ [2][5]. Borealis reports mean $N_c\approx125$ raw photons but threshold clicks mean $\approx90$ after loss, placing it near threshold boundary, not deep advantage regime.

**Corollary:** Future advantage claims must publish PNR samples, not threshold-post-processed, and include symmetry witness.

## 6 Limitations

- **Exascale uncertainty:** Fugaku projection 127 ms → Frontier exascale 1.5× faster could push $M=100$, $N_c=80$ into feasible overnight runs, invalidating claims that hinge on current Top500 snapshot [2].
- **Displacement ignorance:** Borealis small displacement ($|\alpha|\approx0.07$) omitted in many hafnian analyses [5]; inclusion could increase classical MPS bond dimension or decrease advantage, depending on phase.
- **Squashed model not universal spoofer:** Fails for $d=\bar{n}/M<0.3$ low-density; Borealis $d\approx0.58$ is high-density favorable to spoof, but future ultra-low-loss PNR experiments with $M=500$ would escape squashed regime.
- **Symmetry test assumption mildness:** Theoretical proof requires $\epsilon$-approximate pairwise independent hypothesis [APS MAR23] that lossy interferometers approximately violate; real device loss correlations could invalidate analysis.
- **Phase-space truncation bias:** Positive-P projection $p_j^{(c)}(0)=\min(1,\max(\Re(e^{-\alpha_j\beta_j}),0))$ introduces small bias $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N_{samples}})$ needed to be quantified; up to 16k modes validated but TVD bounds not rigorous for $M=216$ full distribution.
- **Legal/ ethical sharing:** Borealis datasets partially proprietary; our symmetry analysis limited to 16-mode open slice, extrapolation to full 216 may mischaracterize calibration pipeline.

## 7 Conclusion

Borealis represents a qualitative leap — *programmable* time-multiplexed GBS exceeding Jiuzhang in connectivity and mean photon number. Yet our unified analysis shows that **verification, not photon counting, is now the bottleneck**. Threshold-detection looping reduces exact hardness by quadratic exponent, chain-rule simulation slashes months to seconds, and squashed-state adversaries expose that high-density regimes previously deemed quantum are classically explainable to second order.

We advocate mandatory triad for future photonic advantage papers:

1. Publish raw PNR counts, not only threshold clicks, alongside calibrated $U_{meas}$ and loss vector $\eta_j$ per mode.
2. Report LXE score against *ideal* distribution, and symmetry inhomogeneity $\chi^2/dof$, with MPS bond-dimension required for $0.01$ TVD.
3. Test against squashed and IPS adversaries as null hypotheses — if squashed HOG beats experiment, advantage claim fails irrespective of photon number.

Only when Borealis-style devices pass LXE $>1.1$, symmetry $p>0.01$, and squashed Bayes factor $\log B<-5$ on full 216-mode data can quantum advantage be considered validated. Current open data suggests gap remains, echoing Drummond et al. [7]: *what problem did these experiments actually solve?* Answering that with falsifiable metrics, not yearly photon-number inflation, will anchor photonics on the path to practical quantum computing.
