# Differentially Private Synthetic Data Generation: PATE-GAN Teacher Ensembles, Private Aggregation of Teacher Disagreement, Rényi DP Accounting, and Fidelity-Diversity Tradeoffs

## Abstract
Private synthetic data generation seeks to release high-utility surrogates of sensitive datasets under formal differential privacy guarantees. This thesis dissects PATE-GAN and its successors (G-PATE, PATE-TabTransGAN) where an ensemble of teacher discriminators trained on disjoint partitions labels unlabeled public data via noisy aggregation, transferring privacy-preserving supervision to a student generator. We formalize Confident-GNMax and Interactive-GNMax as selective mechanisms that abstain under disagreement, yielding data-dependent Rényi DP accounting with substantially tighter composition than naive $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP. We analyze gradient discretization, random projection, and teacher consensus dynamics as drivers of fidelity-diversity tradeoffs, revealing failure modes in existing open-source implementations. Empirical auditing shows that without rigorous RDP filtering and individual privacy odometers, privacy leakage can exceed claimed budgets by orders of magnitude.

## 1 Intro
Synthetic data promises to decouple *machine learning progress* from *direct data access*. Hospitals, banks, and platforms could share generative models rather than rows. Yet naïve GANs memorize. Membership inference attacks [1][2] demonstrate that generator overfitting leaks training presence with AUC >0.75 on tabular health records.

Differentially private synthetic data generation addresses this by imposing $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP on the generator release. Two families dominate: **DP-SGD GANs** that clip and noise discriminator gradients via moments accountant [6][7], and **PATE-based GANs** that never publish teacher parameters, only noisy votes [2][3].

> **Theorem 1 (Post-Processing Immunity):** If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP, then for any data-independent randomized mapping $F$, $F \circ \mathcal{M}$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP.

This theorem justifies PATE-GAN: ensuring DP for the student discriminator suffices to guarantee DP for the generator trained against it, even though the generator itself sees no raw private data [1][3].

Our contributions:
- Unified formulation of PATE-GAN, G-PATE, and PATE-TabTransGAN under Confident-GNMax with Rényi DP accounting
- Closed-form analysis of teacher disagreement as a privacy amplifier and utility gate
- Fidelity-diversity decomposition under RDP budget constraints
- Audit of six open-source PATE-GAN implementations revealing 19 privacy violations [4]
- Practical guidelines for random projection dimensions, gradient quantization bins, and abstention thresholds $T$

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Differential Privacy Preliminaries
A mechanism $\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{R}$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP if for neighboring $D,D'$ and all $S$:

$$ \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] + \delta $$

Pure $\epsilon$-DP corresponds to $\delta=0$. Approximate DP composes poorly under basic composition: $k$ mechanisms yield $k\epsilon$. Advanced composition yields $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{k}\epsilon)$, still loose for iterative learning.

### 2.2 Rényi Differential Privacy
Mironov [5] defines $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP via Rényi divergence of order $\alpha>1$:

$$ D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D) \| \mathcal{M}(D')) = \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mathcal{M}(D')}\left[\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{M}(D)}(x)}{p_{\mathcal{M}(D')}(x)}\right)^\alpha\right] \le \epsilon $$

Key properties:
- **Composition:** If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1)$-RDP and $\mathcal{M}_2$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_2)$-RDP, then composition is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)$-RDP. *Additive* [5].
- **Conversion:** $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP implies $(\epsilon + \frac{\log 1/\delta}{\alpha-1}, \delta)$-DP for any $0<\delta<1$.
- **Subsampling amplification:** If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP, then $\mathcal{M}\circ \text{Subsample}_\gamma$ satisfies tighter bound $\epsilon' \le \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(1+\gamma^2 {\alpha \choose 2} \min\{4(e^{\epsilon(2)}-1),2e^{\epsilon(2)}\}+...)$ [7].

This additive composition makes RDP the *natural accountant* for PATE queries [5][7].

### 2.3 PATE: Private Aggregation of Teacher Ensembles
Papernot et al. [2] introduced PATE: split private $D$ into $k$ disjoint shards $D_i$, train teacher $T_i$ on $D_i$, and aggregate via noisy voting:

- **LNMax:** $\arg\max_j (n_j + \text{Lap}(1/\gamma))$
- **GNMax:** $\arg\max_j (n_j + \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2))$
- **Confident-GNMax:** return $\arg\max$ only if $\max_j n_j + \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_1^2) > T$; otherwise $\bot$ (abstain). Second GNMax with $\sigma_2$ refines answer.

*Intuition:* Disjoint training gives intuitive privacy—no single teacher sees more than $1/k$ data. Noisy aggregation gives formal guarantee. Consensus reduces privacy cost because data-dependent analysis charges less when teachers agree strongly [2].

| Mechanism | Noise | Sensitivity | RDP $\epsilon(\alpha)$ | Abstention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LNMax | Laplace | 1 | $\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\sum$ | No |
| GNMax | Gaussian $\sigma$ | 1 | $\alpha / (2\sigma^2)$ | No |
| Confident-GNMax | Gaussian $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ | 1 | Data-dependent $\le \alpha/(2\sigma_2^2)$ when confident | Yes $T$ |
| Interactive-GNMax | Gaussian adaptive | 1 | Budget-filtered | Yes |

### 2.4 GANs for Synthetic Data
Jordon et al. [1] adapt PATE to GANs: teachers are *discriminators*, student is *discriminator* labeling fake+public samples, generator $G$ optimizes against student. Long et al. [3] improve with private gradient aggregation: teachers vote on *gradient direction* of generator loss, not labels, using random projection $R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}, m\ll d$ and $k$-means discretization.

## 3 Methodology
### 3.1 System Model
We assume:
- Private dataset $D_{priv} = \{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$, $x\in \mathbb{R}^d$
- Public unlabeled $D_{pub}$ (or synthetic seeds from $G$)
- $k$ teachers $T_i: \mathcal{X}\to [C]$ trained on $D_i$ disjoint
- Student $S$ and generator $G$ trained with DP queries

Threat model: adversary sees $S$, $G$, and all synthetic data $D_{syn}\sim G$, but not $T_i$ or $D_{priv}$ partitions. Goal: $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP w.r.t. single record change in $D_{priv}$.

### 3.2 PATE-GAN Architecture
```python
# Simplified PATE-GAN training loop with Confident-GNMax
def pate_gan_train(teachers, student, generator, D_pub, sigma1=150, sigma2=40, T=0.75*k):
    for step in range(num_steps):
        z = torch.randn(batch, latent_dim)
        x_fake = generator(z)
        x = torch.cat([sample(D_pub), x_fake])
        votes = torch.stack([t(x) for t in teachers])  # [k, B, C]
        counts = votes.sum(dim=0)  # [B, C] histogram
        # Confident check
        noisy_max = counts.max(dim=-1).values + torch.randn(B)*sigma1
        mask = noisy_max > T
        if mask.any():
            noisy_counts = counts[mask] + torch.randn_like(counts[mask].float())*sigma2
            labels = noisy_counts.argmax(dim=-1)
            # RDP accounting update with data-dependent epsilon
            rdp_accountant.step(sigma2, mask.sum())
            student.train_on(x[mask], labels)
        generator.train_against(student, z)
```

### 3.3 G-PATE Gradient Aggregation
Instead of labels, teachers provide gradients $g_i = \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(T_i)$. Mechanism [3]:
1. Project: $\tilde{g}_i = R^T g_i$, $R\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1/m)$
2. Discretize: bin each coordinate into $b$ bins via $k$-means centers $c$
3. Vote per bin per coordinate: $n_j^{(l)} = \sum_i \mathbf{1}[\text{bin}(\tilde{g}_i^{(l)})=j]$
4. GNMax per coordinate, abstain if low consensus
5. Reconstruct aggregated projected gradient, lift via $R$

*Benefit:* Sensitivity 1 in voting space regardless of $d$, enabling $d=10^6$ image GANs under $\epsilon<1$ [3].

```haskell
-- Haskell-style specification of RDP composition for Confident-GNMax
data RDP = RDP { alpha :: Double, eps :: Double }

composeRDP :: [RDP] -> RDP
composeRDP rs = RDP (alpha (head rs)) (sum (map eps rs))

confidentGNMaxRDP :: Double -> Double -> Double -> RDP
confidentGNMaxRDP sigma1 sigma2 t = 
  let pFail = gaussianCDF ((t - maxCount)/sigma1)
  in RDP alphaBound (pFail * epsGNMax sigma2)
```

```rust
// Rust: checking teacher disagreement threshold before DP release
fn confident_gnmax(votes: &[usize; C], sigma1: f64, sigma2: f64, thresh: f64) -> Option<usize> {
    let max_count = *votes.iter().max().unwrap() as f64;
    let noisy_check = max_count + rand_distr::Normal::new(0.0, sigma1).unwrap().sample(&mut rand::thread_rng());
    if noisy_check < thresh { return None; }
    let noisy_votes: Vec<f64> = votes.iter()
        .map(|&c| c as f64 + rand_distr::Normal::new(0.0, sigma2).unwrap().sample(&mut rand::thread_rng()))
        .collect();
    Some(noisy_votes.iter().enumerate().max_by(|a,b| a.1.partial_cmp(&b.1).unwrap()).unwrap().0)
}
```

```tla
---- MODULE PATE_GNMax ----
VARIABLES votes, budget, released
ConfidentCheck(v) == \E max \in Nat: max = Max(v) /\ max + Gaussian(sigma1) > T
GNMax(v) == CHOOSE c \in 0..C-1: c = ArgMax(\j \in 0..C-1: v[j] + Gaussian(sigma2))
Next == \/ \A v \in votes: ConfidentCheck(v) /\ released' = released \cup {GNMax(v)}
        \/ budget' = budget - epsilon_of(GNMax)
----
```

## 4 Deep Dive
### 4.1 Teacher Disagreement as Privacy Amplifier
Classic PATE analysis uses *data-independent* bound: each query costs $\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ RDP regardless of consensus [2][5]. Data-dependent analysis leverages that when $n_{j^*} \gg n_{j\neq j^*}$, the mechanism's output distribution is nearly identical for neighboring datasets that flip one teacher's vote.

Formally, define gap $q = n_{j^*} - \max_{j\neq j^*} n_j$. Then Renyi divergence is bounded by:

$$ D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D) \| \mathcal{M}(D')) \le \frac{1}{\alpha-1} \log\left( (1-q)e^{(\alpha-1)\epsilon_{base}} + q e^{(\alpha-1)\epsilon_{tight}}\right) $$

where $\epsilon_{tight} \ll \epsilon_{base}$ when $q$ large. Papernot et al. prove that with $q\ge 0.8k$, privacy cost drops by >5x [2].

*Practical implication:* **Do not fix number of queries; fix privacy budget and stop early.** Interactive-GNMax uses privacy filter [7]:

> **Filter:** Let $\rho_t$ be RDP cost of $t$-th query. If $\sum_{t=1}^k \rho_t \le B$ a.s., then adaptive composition is $(\alpha,B)$-RDP.

This enables individual privacy accounting: track per-teacher contribution, drop teachers that exceed personalized budget [2].

### 4.2 Rényi DP Accounting in Practice
Moments accountant [6] tracked $\alpha$-th moments of privacy loss RV:

$$ K_{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)=\log \mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda L}] $$

RDP generalizes this: $K(\alpha-1)= (\alpha-1)D_\alpha$. Modern libraries (Opacus, TF-Privacy) implement RDP accountant as [7]:

- Choose $\alpha$ grid $\{1.1,2,4,8,16,32\}$
- Per query compute $\epsilon(\alpha)$ for GNMax: $\epsilon(\alpha)=\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$
- Sum $\epsilon(\alpha)$ across queries
- Convert to $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP via $\epsilon_{DP}=\min_\alpha \epsilon(\alpha)+\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$

**GNMax RDP Accountant Numerics:** Must use log-sum-exp for stability:
```python
def gnmax_rdp(alpha, sigma):
    return alpha/(2*sigma**2)

def rdp_to_dp(rdp_eps, alpha, delta):
    return rdp_eps + math.log(1/delta)/(alpha-1)
```
Failure to grid-search $\alpha$ leads to vacuous bounds (e.g., $\epsilon=100$ when optimal $\alpha=8$ yields $\epsilon=1.2$) [4].

### 4.3 Fidelity-Diversity Tradeoffs under Privacy
Privacy induces two competing errors:

1. **Fidelity loss:** $\mathbb{E}[W_1(P_{real}, P_{syn})]$ increases with noise $\sigma_2$ and threshold $T$. High $T$ abstains on hard samples, student sees only easy high-consensus examples → mode collapse.
2. **Diversity loss:** Low $T$ includes noisy labels, student accuracy drops, generator explores wider but inaccurate modes.

Long et al. [3] quantify via random projection: let $R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$. Johnson-Lindenstrauss preserves inner products within $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ if $m=O(\log n/\varepsilon^2)$. Choosing $m=10$ for $d=10^5$ reduces sensitivity cost by $10^4$x but incurs projection error $\|g - RR^T g\|$. Tradeoff:

| $m$ | Privacy cost | Fidelity (FID ↓) | Diversity (Coverage ↑) |
|-----|--------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| 5 | 0.02/query | 145.2 | 0.41 |
| 10 | 0.05/query | 98.7 | 0.63 |
| 50 | 0.22/query | 67.3 | 0.71 |
| 100 | 0.48/query | 62.1 | 0.73 |

*Lesson:* $m=10$-$20$ sweet spot for tabular; images need $m=50$ [3].

### 4.4 Failure Modes in Open-Source Implementations
De Cristofaro et al. [4] audited six PATE-GAN repos including original author code. Findings:

- **Architecture deviation:** Original paper [1] uses MLP teacher with $k=100$ partitions; implementations use $k=10$ or $k=1$ (no disjointness → no intuitive privacy).
- **Accounting bug:** Using $\sigma=5$ but claiming $\sigma=50$ in RDP formula, inflating claimed $\epsilon=0.5$ to true $\epsilon\approx 50$.
- **Non-disjoint shards:** Sampling with replacement across teachers violates PATE assumption; one record influences multiple teachers → sensitivity $>1$.
- **Missing confident check:** Skipping $\sigma_1$ threshold removes abstention, exposing high-disagreement queries that should have been censored.
- **Gradient leakage:** G-PATE implementations publishing teacher discriminators for evaluation violates post-processing guarantee; generator privacy holds but discriminator leakage still enables membership inference [4].

> **Theorem 2 (Disjointness Requirement):** If teachers share any training record with prob $p>0$, then GNMax sensitivity is $k$ not 1, and $(\alpha,\alpha/(2\sigma^2))$-RDP fails. True cost is $(\alpha, k^2\alpha/(2\sigma^2))$.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs
### 5.1 Formal Privacy Guarantee
**Theorem 3 (PATE-GAN RDP):** Let teachers trained on disjoint $D_i$, student queried $q$ times via Confident-GNMax with $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,T)$. Then for any $\alpha>1$, mechanism releasing student $S$ satisfies $(\alpha, \epsilon(\alpha))$-RDP with:

$$ \epsilon(\alpha) \le q \cdot \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left( (1-\tilde{q})A_\alpha + \tilde{q}B_\alpha \right) $$

where $\tilde{q}=\Pr[\text{confident}]$, $A_\alpha = \mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_1^2)}[e^{(\alpha-1)\text{cost}_{fail}}]$, $B_\alpha = e^{(\alpha-1)\alpha/(2\sigma_2^2)}$ under data-dependent bound. Proof extends [2] Theorem 3 by post-processing immunity for $G$.

*Proof sketch:* Decompose privacy loss into confident-check and GNMax. Confident-check is $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_1^2)$ threshold; its RDP is bounded via Gaussian tail. Conditional on passing, GNMax is standard. Data-dependent term uses that neighboring datasets differ in at most one teacher vote, gap $q$ large with high probability when teachers accurate [2].

### 5.2 Utility Analysis
Assume teachers have accuracy $p>0.8$ and are $\beta$-calibrated. Then with $k=250$, $T=0.75k$, $\sigma_1=150$, $\sigma_2=40$:

- Confident rate $\ge 0.6$ on MNIST, $\ge 0.35$ on Adult tabular [1][3]
- Student accuracy $\ge p - O(1/\sqrt{k}) - O(\sigma_2/k)$ [2]
- Generator TV distance $\|P_G-P_{real}\|_{TV} \le \sqrt{2\cdot \text{JS}(P_G\|P_{student}) + \epsilon_{DP}}$

Empirically on Adult: PATE-GAN with $\epsilon=1.0, \delta=10^{-5}$ achieves AUROC 0.83 vs 0.90 non-private, AUPRC 0.62 vs 0.71; DP-CTGAN baseline AUROC 0.78 [8]. PATE-TabTransGAN improves to 0.86 AUROC via transformer student [8].

### 5.3 Auditing Methodology
We implement DP audit via membership inference distinguishing game: train $m=1000$ shadow models, measure attack advantage $Adv = TPR - FPR$. For claimed $(1.0,10^{-5})$-DP, expected $Adv \le e^{1}-1 \approx 1.7$ (vacuous) but tight $Adv \le 0.3$ under empirical $\epsilon_{emp}$. Implementations in [4] show $Adv=0.68$ indicating $\epsilon_{emp}\ge 4.2$.

## 6 Limitations
- **Public data assumption:** PATE requires unlabeled public data from similar distribution. In medical settings, such data may not exist; using synthetic seeds as public leaks privacy iteratively if not accounted [1][3].
- **Partition cost:** $k$ disjoint shards reduce per-teacher sample size to $n/k$. For $n=10k$, $k=100$ → 100 samples/teacher → poor teacher accuracy → low consensus → high abstention → wasted budget [2]. Optimal $k$ scales as $\sqrt{n}$.
- **RDP conversion looseness:** RDP-to-DP conversion is not tight; PLD accounting [7] yields $30\%$ tighter $\epsilon$ at same $\delta$. Characteristic function accountant [7] should replace RDP for final reporting.
- **Computational overhead:** $k$ teachers train $k$x cost; G-PATE gradient discretization $k$-means per step $O(k m b)$ dominates. Not feasible for $k>500$ on ImageNet.
- **Fairness:** Teacher ensemble inherits subgroup imbalance; noisy aggregation amplifies minority misclassification because low-count subgroups rarely reach consensus $T$ [2]. SF-PATE addresses via fairness-aware voting but doubles privacy cost.
- **Verification gap:** No formal verification of RDP accountant code in PyTorch/Opacus; floating-point log-sum-exp underflows for $\alpha>64$ producing $NaN$ $\epsilon$ silently [4].

## 7 Conclusion
PATE-GAN and its derivatives demonstrate that *private aggregation of teacher disagreement*—not DP-SGD on generator parameters—is a scalable path to differentially private synthetic data. By decoupling teacher training (no DP needed) from student supervision (DP via GNMax), we obtain post-processing immunity for the generator while preserving high-dimensional modeling capacity through random projection and gradient discretization [3]. Rényi DP accounting with confident abstention and individual privacy filters transforms PATE from a heuristic ensemble into a rigorous $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP mechanism with data-dependent amplification [2][5][7].

Yet reproducibility crisis persists: six open-source implementations fail to reproduce claimed utility and leak more privacy than advertised [4]. Future work must adopt **PLD/characteristic function accountants** [7], **automated DP auditing** in CI, and **formal verification of GNMax** (e.g., via Stacked Borrows-style provenance tracking for sensitivity). Integrating transformer-based student discriminators [8] and diffusion-based generators under PATE supervision promises to close fidelity gap while retaining $\epsilon<1$ guarantees.

The fidelity-diversity-privacy trilemma remains: under fixed budget $B$, increasing fidelity via lower $\sigma_2$ costs diversity via higher abstention $T$; increasing diversity via higher $m$ costs privacy linearly. Understanding Pareto frontier via RDP order tuning and adaptive $T$ scheduling is open.
