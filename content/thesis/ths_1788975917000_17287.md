# Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity and Surrogate-Gradient Learning in Event-Driven Neuromorphic Systems: From Leaky Integrate-and-Fire Dynamics to Loihi 2 and Event-Camera Processing

## Abstract

Event-driven neuromorphic computing promises order-of-magnitude energy savings by replacing dense, synchronous tensor operations with sparse, asynchronous spike communication. This thesis develops the mathematical foundations and engineering practice of that paradigm. We derive the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron from first principles and characterize spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) in its pair-based, triplet, and calcium-mediated forms. We survey the canonical silicon substrates — IBM TrueNorth, Intel Loihi, and SpiNNaker — and the address-event representation (AER) that lets dynamic-vision sensors feed them natively. We compare the two dominant training paradigms, ANN-to-SNN conversion and surrogate-gradient backpropagation through time, deriving the bias–variance structure of surrogate estimators. Benchmarks show surrogate-gradient training within a few percentage points of equivalent artificial neural networks on N-MNIST and DVS-CIFAR10 while preserving the sparsity advantage of spiking. We close with open problems: three-factor credit assignment, hardware-aware surrogates, and sensor–processor co-design.

## 1 Introduction

Modern deep learning is an energy catastrophe in slow motion: inference at data-center scale is dominated by moving dense activation tensors between memory and arithmetic units. The brain performs exaflop-equivalent computation at roughly twenty watts with an *event-driven* architecture — sparse, asynchronous spikes, computation only where spikes arrive [1][2].

Neuromorphic engineering takes this observation as a design principle. IBM's TrueNorth packs one million spiking neurons and 256 million synapses into tens of milliwatts [1]; Intel's Loihi adds a programmable microcode learning engine to every core [2]; SpiNNaker routes 40-bit spike packets across a triangular mesh of ARM cores at billion-neuron scale [3]. Event cameras converge on the same primitive: the dynamic-vision sensor emits *(x, y, t, p)* tuples only when log-intensity changes, natively producing the address-event representation (AER) neuromorphic chips consume [4].

The missing piece, for decades, was *learning*. Biological plasticity is local in space and time: spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) modifies a synapse from the relative timing of pre- and postsynaptic spikes [5][6][7]. Machine learning relies on global error backpropagation, which is non-differentiable through the spike threshold. Two resolutions dominate: **ANN-to-SNN conversion**, mapping a trained rate-based network onto spiking dynamics, and **surrogate-gradient learning**, replacing the spike derivative with a smooth proxy in the backward pass [8].

This thesis unifies these threads:

1. A rigorous derivation of the LIF neuron, its discrete-time simulation, and the spike-response formalism, with reference implementations.
2. A taxonomy of STDP — pair-based, triplet, and calcium-mediated — grounded in electrophysiology [5][7] and competitive Hebbian theory [6].
3. A comparative analysis of TrueNorth, Loihi/Loihi 2, and SpiNNaker, and of AER as the sensor-to-chip interface [1][2][3][4].
4. A derivation of the surrogate-gradient estimator with a bias decomposition theorem, and empirical comparison of conversion versus direct training.
5. An assessment of limitations: three-factor credit assignment, hardware-aware surrogates, and sensor–processor co-design.

## 2 Background and Related Work

### 2.1 From Hodgkin–Huxley to integrate-and-fire

The quantitative study of excitable membranes begins with the Hodgkin–Huxley equations, a four-dimensional nonlinear system of ion-channel conductances. While biophysically faithful, it is intractable at network scale. The *leaky integrate-and-fire* (LIF) neuron compresses this into one linear differential equation with a discontinuous threshold-reset rule:

$$\tau_m \frac{dV}{dt} = -(V - V_{\mathrm{rest}}) + R\,I(t), \qquad \text{if } V \ge V_{\mathrm{th}}: \text{ emit spike, } V \leftarrow V_{\mathrm{reset}}.$$

Despite its simplicity, LIF captures the essential primitive — temporal integration with leak, then a binary decision — and is the model of choice on TrueNorth [1] and Loihi [2]. Between LIF and Hodgkin–Huxley sits a hierarchy trading expressivity for cost:

| Model | State dim. | Key features | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leaky integrate-and-fire | 1 | Leak, threshold, reset, refractory | Large-scale SNN simulation, neuromorphic hardware |
| Adaptive exponential IF | 2 | Adaptation current, exponential spike initiation | Cortical neuron fitting |
| Izhikevich | 2 | Bifurcation-rich dynamics, cheap | Large heterogeneous networks |
| Hodgkin–Huxley | 4 | Ion-channel kinetics | Biophysical modeling |

The spike-response reformulation writes the same dynamics as $V(t) = \eta(t-\hat t) + \sum_j w_j \sum_f \varepsilon(t - \hat t, t - t_j^f)$ [8] — the bridge between continuous-time biophysics and the discrete-time programs surrogate-gradient training requires.

### 2.2 Spike-timing-dependent plasticity

Hebb's postulate acquires a precise, *asymmetric* temporal form in STDP. Bi and Poo showed that in cultured hippocampal neurons, pre-before-post pairing within ~20 ms induces long-term potentiation (LTP), while post-before-pre pairing induces long-term depression (LTD), gated by NMDA receptors and initial strength [5]. The canonical pair-based kernel is

$$\Delta w = \begin{cases} A_+ e^{-\Delta t / \tau_+} & \Delta t > 0 \ (\text{pre before post}) \\\\ -A_- e^{\Delta t / \tau_-} & \Delta t < 0 \ (\text{post before pre}) \end{cases}, \qquad \Delta t = t_{\mathrm{post}} - t_{\mathrm{pre}}.$$

Song, Miller, and Abbott showed computationally that this rule, combined with weight-dependent updates, yields *competitive* Hebbian learning: synapses converge to a stable bimodal distribution, receptive fields sharpen, and firing rates stay bounded without explicit normalization — global order from a purely local rule [6]. Feldman's review established that spike timing is one factor within a multi-factor landscape: triplet interactions, firing-rate dependence, and dendritic location all modulate the outcome, with calcium-based models unifying these effects through postsynaptic calcium transients [7].

> **Lemma (STDP weight competition).** *With weight-dependent potentiation $A_+(w) \propto (w_{\max} - w)$ and depression $A_-(w) \propto w$, the stationary weight distribution is bimodal — synapses driven toward $0$ or $w_{\max}$ — with the strong fraction set by $A_+ \tau_+ / A_- \tau_-$ [6].*
>
> *Proof sketch.* The expected drift $\langle \dot w \rangle$ has two stable fixed points ($0$, $w_{\max}$) and one unstable interior point that repels, so trajectories concentrate at the boundaries. ∎

### 2.3 Neuromorphic hardware and event-based sensing

TrueNorth demonstrated that a million-neuron digital SNN is feasible at 63 mW — 46 GSOPS/W — using 4096 neurosynaptic cores with binary crossbars, tileable into cortex-like sheets [1]. Loihi trades some of that efficiency for programmability: hierarchical connectivity, dendritic compartments, synaptic delays, and a microcode learning engine for arbitrary plasticity rules at epoch boundaries [2]. SpiNNaker took the opposite route — general-purpose ARM cores plus a multicast router — becoming the primary platform for studying plastic networks at scale [3].

Event cameras complete the pipeline. A dynamic-vision sensor pixel independently monitors log-intensity and emits an event $(x, y, t, p)$ on change, achieving microsecond temporal resolution and ~140 dB dynamic range at milliwatt power [4]. The event stream is *already* a spike train; sensor and processor speak the same language.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology has three strands: (i) exact and approximate simulation of LIF dynamics in discrete time; (ii) STDP variants as local online rules; (iii) supervised training of deep SNNs by surrogate-gradient BPTT, compared against ANN-to-SNN conversion.

### 3.1 Discrete-time LIF simulation

Neuromorphic chips and GPU simulators alike discretize time into steps $\Delta t$. The exact LIF solution over one step with constant input gives

$$V[t+1] = \beta\, V[t] + (1-\beta)\, R\, I[t], \qquad \beta = e^{-\Delta t / \tau_m},$$

with spike emission $S[t] = \Theta(V[t] - V_{\mathrm{th}})$ and hard reset $V[t] \leftarrow V_{\mathrm{reset}}$ (soft reset, $V[t] \leftarrow V[t] - V_{\mathrm{th}}$, preserves suprathreshold residual and is preferred for conversion):

```python
import numpy as np

class LIFLayer:
    def __init__(self, n, tau_m=20e-3, v_th=1.0, v_reset=0.0, dt=1e-3, t_ref=2e-3):
        self.beta = np.exp(-dt / tau_m)
        self.v_th, self.v_reset = v_th, v_reset
        self.v = np.full(n, v_reset)
        self.ref = np.zeros(n)
        self.ref_steps = int(t_ref / dt)

    def step(self, current):
        not_ref = self.ref <= 0
        self.v[not_ref] = self.beta * self.v[not_ref] + (1 - self.beta) * current[not_ref]
        spikes = (self.v >= self.v_th) & not_ref
        self.v[spikes] = self.v_reset
        self.ref[spikes] = self.ref_steps
        self.ref = np.maximum(self.ref - 1, 0)
        return spikes.astype(float)
```

### 3.2 STDP as an online trace rule

Pair-based STDP admits an $O(1)$-per-spike implementation with exponentially decaying traces — the form Loihi's learning engine evaluates at epoch boundaries [2]:

```haskell
-- Pair-based STDP as an online trace rule (Haskell)
data Trace = Trace { pre :: Double, post :: Double } deriving Show

decay :: Double -> Double -> Trace -> Trace
decay dt tau (Trace x y) = Trace (x * exp (-dt/tau)) (y * exp (-dt/tau))

onPreSpike :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Trace -> (Trace, Double)
onPreSpike aMinus wMax w (Trace x y) =
  (Trace (x + 1.0) y, -aMinus * y * (w / wMax))        -- weight-dependent LTD

onPostSpike :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Trace -> (Trace, Double)
onPostSpike aPlus wMax w (Trace x y) =
  (Trace x (y + 1.0), aPlus * x * ((wMax - w) / wMax))  -- weight-dependent LTP
```

### 3.3 Surrogate-gradient BPTT

Consider an SNN unrolled over $T$ timesteps with parameters $\theta$. The loss $L$ depends on spike trains $S[t] = \Theta(V[t] - V_{\mathrm{th}})$. The true gradient requires $\Theta'$ — zero almost everywhere, infinite at threshold. The surrogate-gradient method substitutes a smooth proxy in the backward pass only:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial V} \;\approx\; \sigma'(V - V_{\mathrm{th}}), \qquad \sigma'(x) = \frac{1}{(1 + \beta |x|)^2} \quad \text{(fast sigmoid)},$$

while the forward pass keeps the exact step function [8]. Gradients then flow through time via the chain rule — *backpropagation through time* applied to a network whose nonlinearity is a spike. We use the §3.1 recurrence with automatic differentiation and $\beta \approx 25$.

### 3.4 ANN-to-SNN conversion

Conversion trains a conventional ANN with ReLU activations, then maps each ReLU unit $a = \max(0, Wx + b)$ to an integrate-and-fire neuron whose *firing rate* approximates $a$. Weight/threshold balancing and soft reset (preserving suprathreshold residual) are essential [8]. The price is *latency*: accurate rate coding needs tens to hundreds of timesteps, eroding the energy advantage that motivated spiking.

### 3.5 Evaluation protocol

We evaluate on **N-MNIST**, **DVS-Gesture**, and **DVS-CIFAR10**, measuring accuracy, timesteps (latency), and synaptic operations (SOPs) as an energy proxy. Conversion uses a VGG-style ReLU ANN; surrogate-gradient training uses the same architecture with LIF neurons and BPTT over 20–50 timesteps.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The LIF neuron: analysis of the integrate-and-fire computation

The LIF neuron is a *leaky integrator with a decision boundary*: subthreshold dynamics are linear and exactly solvable, and it spikes iff the steady state exceeds threshold, giving a monotone firing-rate curve $f(I)$ that is zero below rheobase. Three observations matter for what follows:

1. **Leak as temporal credit control.** Inputs arriving within a few $\tau_m$ summate; older inputs are forgotten — making LIF intrinsically sensitive to *coincidence*, the basis of temporal coding.
2. **The threshold is the only nonlinearity.** Everything differentiable lives below threshold; everything discrete happens at it. Surrogate gradients smuggle derivative information across this boundary.
3. **Refractoriness bounds the rate.** The absolute refractory period caps firing at $1/t_{\mathrm{ref}}$, regularizing rate-coded networks.

The spike-response reformulation writes the same dynamics as $V(t) = \eta(t-\hat t) + \sum_j w_j \sum_f \varepsilon(t - \hat t, t - t_j^f)$, where $\hat t$ is the last spike time, $\eta$ is the refractory kernel, and $\varepsilon$ the PSP kernel [8]. This form exposes the SNN as a *generalized linear model over spike-history features* — the view that makes BPTT derivations clean.

### 4.2 STDP: from pairs to triplets to calcium

Pair-based STDP (Figure 2) is the minimal model. **Triplet STDP** adds terms triggered by spike triplets, reproducing the frequency dependence of pairing experiments that pair-based rules miss [7]. **Calcium-based models** unify timing and rate dependence through postsynaptic calcium transients [7].

![STDP weight update timing curve](/thesis/ths_1788975917000_17287-1.webp)

*Figure 2 — The STDP window: pre-before-post potentiates, post-before-pre depresses.*

From an engineering standpoint, the crucial property shared by all STDP variants is **locality**: the update depends only on quantities available at the synapse. This is why STDP maps so naturally onto Loihi's per-synapse learning engine [2] and SpiNNaker's distributed plasticity kernels [3] — and why the *credit-assignment problem* (local rules optimize no explicit global objective) remains the gap surrogate gradients fill.

### 4.3 Silicon substrates: TrueNorth, Loihi, and SpiNNaker

All three honor the event-driven contract — *no spike, no work* — with different trade-offs:

| | TrueNorth [1] | Loihi [2] | SpiNNaker [3] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | 28 nm digital | 14 nm digital | 130 nm ARM SoC |
| Neurons / chip | 1,000,000 | 131,072 | ~16,000 (per node) |
| Synapses / chip | 256,000,000 | 130,000,000 | SDRAM-bound |
| Neuron model | LIF variants (configurable) | Programmable compartments | Arbitrary (software) |
| Learning | Off-chip (inference) | On-chip microcode STDP | Software plasticity |
| Routing | 2D mesh, AER packets | Hierarchical mesh multicast | Triangular mesh, 40-bit packets |
| Power (typical) | ~70 mW | ~watts (research system) | ~1 W / node |

**TrueNorth** is the purist: fixed-function cores, binary crossbars, deterministic 1 ms ticks, 46 GSOPS/W — but non-plastic synapses, so learning is off-chip [1]. **Loihi** adds a learning engine per core evaluating microcode-programmed rules (pairwise/triplet STDP, reward-modulated, three-factor) at epoch boundaries, with synaptic delays, dendritic compartments, and stochastic noise [2]. **Loihi 2** scales to one million neurons per chip with graded spikes and fully programmable neuron models via Lava. **SpiNNaker** is the maximalist: ARM cores plus a multicast router, simulating arbitrary neuron and plasticity models in real time up to a billion neurons [3].

### 4.4 Event cameras, AER, and event-driven sensing

The address-event representation is the narrow waist of the neuromorphic stack: a spike packet carries only a source identifier, and the receiver looks up its fan-out in local tables [2][3]. A DVS pixel emits exactly the same abstraction — *(x, y, t, p)*, timestamped to microsecond precision [4] — so an event camera streams directly into a neuromorphic chip with no frame buffer.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Conversion versus surrogate gradients: accuracy and latency

| Method | N-MNIST | DVS-Gesture | DVS-CIFAR10 | Timesteps | Relative energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ANN baseline (ReLU) | 99.2% | 94.5% | 78.1% | 1 (frame) | 1.0× |
| ANN→SNN conversion | 98.9% | 93.1% | 74.8% | 100–400 | 0.3–0.6× |
| Surrogate-gradient SNN | 98.7% | 92.8% | 69.4% | 20–50 | 0.1–0.25× |
| STDP (unsupervised, linear readout) | 95.1% | — | — | 100 | 0.05× |

*Table 3 — Representative literature figures [8]: conversion nearly matches the ANN but pays in latency; surrogate-gradient training trades a few accuracy points for an order-of-magnitude latency and energy reduction; unsupervised STDP remains far behind on supervised benchmarks.*

The pattern is consistent [8]: **conversion** inherits the full power of ReLU optimization but long rate-coding windows blunt its energy advantage. **Surrogate-gradient** training learns temporal codes with 5–20× fewer timesteps. **STDP alone** does not scale to deep supervised learning — the empirical confirmation of the credit-assignment gap.

### 5.2 Formal analysis: what does a surrogate gradient estimate?

We now characterize the bias of the surrogate estimator. Consider a single LIF neuron with membrane potential $u(\theta)$, firing probability under injected noise $\varepsilon \sim p$ given by the *escape-noise* model $P(S=1) = \Phi((u - V_{\mathrm{th}})/\sigma)$, where $\Phi$ is a sigmoidal CDF.

> **Theorem (Surrogate bias decomposition).** *Let $L$ depend on $S = \Theta(u - V_{\mathrm{th}})$, and let $\sigma'_\beta$ be a surrogate derivative with $\int \sigma'_\beta = 1$ and $\sigma'_\beta \to \delta_0$ as $\beta \to \infty$. Then $g_{\mathrm{surr}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial S}\,\sigma'_\beta(u - V_{\mathrm{th}})\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta}$ satisfies*
> $$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon[g_{\mathrm{surr}}] \;=\; \nabla_\theta\, \mathbb{E}_\varepsilon[L] \;+\; B(\beta), \qquad \|B(\beta)\| = O(\beta^{-1}) + O(\sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}).$$
>
> *Proof sketch.* Differentiating the noise-smoothed objective $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_\varepsilon[L]$ under the expectation and integrating by parts gives $\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}_\varepsilon[\frac{\partial L}{\partial S}\, p(u - V_{\mathrm{th}})\, \frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta}]$, where $p$ is the noise density — the *exact* gradient uses the noise density as the derivative proxy. The surrogate replaces $p$ with $\sigma'_\beta$; the bias is bounded by $\|\sigma'_\beta - p\|_1$ times Lipschitz constants. Setting $\beta^{-1} \approx \sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}$ matches the surrogate to the noise density and minimizes the bound. ∎

**Corollary.** *Surrogate sharpness should match the effective noise scale: too sharp recovers the dead true gradient; too smooth washes out threshold information. The fast sigmoid with $\beta \approx 25$ sits at the empirical optimum across benchmarks [8].*

A second formal result concerns three-factor learning, the middle ground between STDP and backprop. In *eligibility-trace* methods (e-prop), each synapse maintains a trace $e_{ij}(t)$ of its contribution to recent outputs, gated by a global modulatory signal: $\Delta w_{ij} \propto M(t)\,e_{ij}(t)$ [8]. This *local eligibility × global modulator* factorization is exactly what Loihi's learning engine can evaluate on-chip [2].

### 5.3 Energy analysis

Energy in an SNN is dominated by synaptic operations: each spike triggers one accumulate per fan-out synapse, giving $rNF$ SOPs per timestep versus $NF$ multiply-accumulates per ANN layer. On Loihi-class hardware, surrogate-trained SNNs on DVS-Gesture reach iso-accuracy at roughly 5–10× lower energy, with the gap widening as input sparsity increases — the regime event cameras natively occupy [2][4][8].

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**1. The locality–optimality gap.** STDP and three-factor extensions optimize no explicit global objective; surrogate gradients optimize the objective but need non-local error transport. Whether a *local* rule can match BPTT on hard benchmarks is the field's central open question [6][7][8].

**2. Hardware-aware surrogates.** Theorem 5.2 shows the optimal surrogate matches the neuron's noise density — but digital hardware neurons are *deterministic*, so the "noise" is an artifact of the estimator. Surrogates co-designed with quantized, deterministic dynamics are largely unexplored [1][2].

**3. Temporal credit assignment over long horizons.** BPTT through hundreds of timesteps suffers vanishing gradients in SNNs as in RNNs; eligibility traces mitigate this but accumulate approximation error [8].

**4. Benchmark realism.** N-MNIST and DVS-CIFAR10 are converted frame datasets that understate event-driven advantages. Natively event-based benchmarks with precise temporal structure should be the primary yardstick [4].

**5. Sensor–processor co-design.** Today's event cameras and neuromorphic chips are designed independently and merely share AER. Joint optimization — pixel bandwidth matched to router throughput, adaptive thresholds driven by chip-level feedback — could unlock the next order of magnitude [4].

**6. Verification and safety.** We lack formal methods for verifying SNN behavior: reachability analysis through discontinuous spike dynamics is an open problem, and the stochastic interpretation of §5.2 is a first step, not a solution.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Event-driven neuromorphic computing rests on three interlocking ideas: the LIF neuron as a leaky temporal integrator with a binary decision; STDP as a local, timing-based plasticity rule whose competitive dynamics build structure without supervision; and the address-event representation as the common language of sensors, routers, and processors. The hardware proves the substrate works [1][2][3]. Surrogate-gradient learning brings gradient-based optimization to spiking networks with an understood bias structure, while conversion offers a pragmatic bridge from the ANN world [8]. The remaining gap is credit assignment under locality constraints — and closing it is the problem on which the field's next decade turns.
