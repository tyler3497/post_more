# High-Resolution Climate Modeling via Physics-Informed Neural Operators: Fourier Neural Operator, Spherical Harmonic Transformers, Conservation Law Embedding, and Downscaling with Generative Diffusion

## Abstract
High-resolution climate modeling remains constrained by the computational complexity of primitive equations on the sphere and by the violation of conservation laws in black-box emulators. This thesis develops a unified operator-learning framework integrating Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) for resolution-invariant PDE solution mapping, Spherical Fourier Neural Operators (SFNO) via spherical harmonic transforms for pole-artifact-free autoregressive dynamics, conservation law-encoded neural operators (clawNO) enforcing divergence-free and energy-preserving inference, and residual corrective diffusion (CorrDiff) for stochastic super-resolution from 25 km global states to 2 km regional hazards. We formalize operator learning on $L^2(\mathbb{S}^2;\mathbb{R}^{d_{in}})$ with *spectral convolution* $ \mathcal{K}v = \mathcal{F}^{-1}(R_\phi \cdot \mathcal{F}v)$, prove stability of SFNO rollouts under band-limited spherical filters, and derive architecture-constrained mass conservation that holds to machine precision. Empirically, SFNO achieves 1-year stable rollouts in 13 min, FourCastNet matches IFS at 0.25°, and CorrDiff recovers $k^{-5/3}$ spectra. The synthesis provides a path to sub-seasonal-to-seasonal emulation with physical fidelity.

## 1 Introduction

Global weather and climate prediction requires solving hydrostatic primitive equations on the rotating sphere $\mathbb{S}^2$ coupled to moisture, radiation, and land processes. Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) integrates these PDEs at $O(10^9)$ degrees of freedom per timestep, yielding 51-member ensemble 15-day forecasts in $\sim 10^4$ node-hours on ECMWF IFS [1]. Despite 40 years of Moore's law gains, *time-to-solution* limits resolution to $\sim 9$ km operationally, insufficient for convective extremes, orographic precipitation, and tropical cyclone eyewall structure [2].

Operator learning reframes forecasting as learning mappings between function spaces $\mathcal{G}^\dagger: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{U}$ rather than finite-dimensional vectors. Fourier Neural Operators [3][4] parameterize kernel integral operators in Fourier space, achieving **mesh-invariant**, **zero-shot super-resolution**, and $10^3\times$ speedup over pseudo-spectral solvers. FourCastNet [5][6] scales Adaptive FNO (AFNO) with Vision Transformers to 0.25° global forecasting, matching IFS on geopotential while outperforming on precipitation. However, Euclidean FFT on lat-lon grids introduces *polar artifacts* and energy accumulation near poles, causing long-rollout divergence after $\sim 30$ days [7][8].

Spherical Fourier Neural Operators [7][9] replace DFT with spherical harmonic transform (SHT), respecting $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ symmetry and enabling stable 1-year rollouts. Complementary challenges remain: neural emulators violate *linear* invariants (total mass $\int \rho$, moisture, energy) critical for centennial climate runs [10][11]; Beucler et al. showed unconstrained NNs drift $>10$ W m$^{-2}$ in energy [11]. clawNO [12] encodes conservation via divergence-free skew-symmetric representation $u = \nabla^\perp \psi$, guaranteeing $\nabla \cdot u =0$ by construction.

Finally, global 25 km drivers must be downscaled to 2 km for hazard assessment. Deterministic regression blurs spectra; generative diffusion models [13][14] decompose prediction into mean $ \mu_\theta(x_{low})$ via UNet plus residual diffusion $p_\theta(x_{high} | x_{low}, \mu_\theta)$ that restores $k^{-3}$ to $k^{-5/3}$ kinetic energy spectra.

> **Theorem (Operator Universality Gap):** For any Lipschitz continuous solution operator $\mathcal{G}^\dagger$ of the rotating shallow water equations, there exists FNO $\mathcal{G}_\theta$ with Fourier modes truncated to $k_{max}$ such that $\| \mathcal{G}^\dagger - \mathcal{G}_\theta \|_{L^2} \le C k_{max}^{-s} + \epsilon_{NN}$, whereas a CNN with same parameter count suffers $\Omega(1)$ error on unseen resolution due to aliasing.

*Contributions:* (i) unified PINO architecture stacking SFNO backbone + clawNO conservation head + CorrDiff downscaler, (ii) proof of stable spherical convolution via Wigner-D orthogonality, (iii) architecture-constrained conservation for coupled mass-energy-moisture budgets, (iv) empirical validation on ERA5 1979-2023 and Taiwan WRF 2 km dataset showing spectra, CRPS, and extreme retention.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Neural Operators and Fourier Neural Operator

Classical DeepONet and FNO learn operators between infinite-dimensional Banach spaces [3]. FNO defines iterative update:

$$ v_{t+1}(x) = \sigma\left( W v_t(x) + \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(R_\phi \cdot \mathcal{F} v_t\right)(x) \right) $$

where $R_\phi \in \mathbb{C}^{k_{max} \times d_v \times d_v}$ is learnable. Mesh-invariance follows because $R_\phi$ acts on *modes*, not grid points, enabling training at 5.625° and inference at 0.25° [3].

*Key properties:*
- **Quasi-linear complexity:** $O(n \log n)$ via FFT vs $O(n^2)$ for attention
- **Zero-shot super-resolution:** first ML model to model turbulent Navier-Stokes at $Re \sim 10^4$ with generalization across Reynolds numbers [3]
- **Extension to AFNO:** Guibas et al. adaptive token mixing via block-diagonal $R_\phi$ plus soft-thresholding for sparsity, enabling 0.25° global 20-variable state [5]

### 2.2 Spherical Geometry and Polar Pathology

Lat-lon grid has singularities at poles where meridians converge, causing Courant limit $ \Delta t \le C \Delta x / \cos \phi$. DFT assumes toroidal topology, violating sphere continuity. SFNO [7][9] uses:

$$ \hat{u}_l^m = \int_{\mathbb{S}^2} u(\theta,\phi) \overline{Y_l^m}(\theta,\phi) d\Omega,\quad u(\theta,\phi)=\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l} \hat{u}_l^m Y_l^m $$

SHT complexity $O(L^3)$ naive, $O(L^2 \log L)$ with SHTns, $L=719$ for 0.25°. Spectral filter $R_\phi(l,m)$ is complex-valued, equivariant to $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ rotations via Wigner-D matrices [7].

### 2.3 Conservation Laws in Climate Emulators

Beucler et al. [10][11] distinguish:

- **Loss-constrained:** $ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{MSE} + \lambda \| C u - b \|^2$ reduces violation to $O(\lambda^{-1})$
- **Architecture-constrained:** $u = \tilde{u} - C^T(CC^T)^{-1}(C\tilde{u} - b)$ projects onto nullspace of $C$; error to machine epsilon

For energy $E = c_p T + L_v q + \Phi + k$, linear constraint $ \mathbf{1}^T M E = const$ under advection; nonlinear constraints (e.g., Clausius-Clapeyron) require iterative correction [10].

clawNO [12] generalizes: let $\mathcal{D}$ be differential operator encoding conservation $\mathcal{D}u =0$. Seek $u = \mathcal{D}^\perp \psi$ where $\mathcal{D} \mathcal{D}^\perp =0$. For incompressible 2D, $u = (-\partial_y \psi, \partial_x \psi)$.

### 2.4 Diffusion Downscaling

CorrDiff [13][14] addresses *double penalty* of MSE: location errors penalized twice, causing blur. Two-step:

1. **Regression UNet:** $ \mu = f_\theta(x_{low})$ minimizes MAE
2. **Diffusion Corrector:** $ x_{high} = \mu + \epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, x_{low}, \mu)$ where $z_t = \alpha_t (x_{high}-\mu) + \sigma_t \epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$

Generates ensemble of $N_{ens}=32$ samples for CRPS evaluation, recovering spectra $E(k) \sim k^{-5/3}$ for $k>10^{-4}$ m$^{-1}$.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Define state $X_t \in L^2(\mathbb{S}^2; \mathbb{R}^{74})$ comprising 5 3D atmospheric variables on 13 pressure levels ($Z, T, U, V, Q$), 10 2-m surface, plus static orography, land-sea mask, $\cos(\zeta)$ [5][7]. Dynamics $\partial_t X = \mathcal{N}(X)$ with $\mathcal{N}$ primitive equations.

Goal: learn $\mathcal{G}_\theta: X_t \to X_{t+\Delta t}$, $\Delta t =6$ hr, composition $\mathcal{G}_\theta^N$ for $N=1460$ (1 yr).

*Loss:* $ \mathcal{L} = \| X_{t+1} - \mathcal{G}_\theta(X_t) \|_{L^2(w)}^2 + \lambda_{cons} \|C\mathcal{G}_\theta -b\|^2 + \lambda_{spec} \sum_l w_l \| \hat{X}_{l} - \widehat{\mathcal{G}_\theta}_l \|^2 $ where $w(\phi)=\cos\phi$ area weight.

### 3.2 Fourier Neural Operator Stack

Lifting $P: \mathbb{R}^{74+3} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_v}$ ($d_v=256$), 8 Fourier layers with $k_{max}=40$ zonal modes, $l_{max}=40$ meridional, GeLU, residual $W$.

Python sketch of spectral convolution:

```python
import torch
import torch.fft as fft

class SpectralConv2d(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_ch, out_ch, modes1, modes2):
        super().__init__()
        self.modes1, self.modes2 = modes1, modes2
        self.R = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(in_ch, out_ch, modes1, modes2, dtype=torch.cfloat)*0.02)

    def forward(self, x): # x: B x C x H x W
        B,C,H,W = x.shape
        x_ft = fft.rfft2(x, norm='ortho')
        out_ft = torch.zeros(B, self.R.shape[1], H, W//2+1, dtype=torch.cfloat, device=x.device)
        out_ft[:,:,:self.modes1,:self.modes2] = torch.einsum('bixy,ioxy->boxy', x_ft[:,:,:self.modes1,:self.modes2], self.R)
        return fft.irfft2(out_ft, s=(H,W), norm='ortho')
```

*Why mesh-invariant:* $R$ independent of $H,W$; FFT pads/truncates.

### 3.3 Spherical Fourier Neural Operator

Replace FFT with SHT via torch-harmonics. Encoder MLP $\to$ $d_v$, then $L_{SFNO}=4$ blocks:

$$ v_{l+1} = \sigma( W v_l + \mathcal{S}^{-1}( R_l \cdot \mathcal{S} v_l) ) $$

where $\mathcal{S}$ is forward SHT, $R_l \in \mathbb{C}^{L_{max}\times d_v \times d_v}$ diagonal in $m$ for equivariance. Filter pruning retains $|m| \le 4$ for efficiency (95% energy in low $|m|$ for zonal jets) [7].

Haskell type-level specification of equivariance:

```haskell
-- Spherical convolution is SO(3)-equivariant
type SHT = forall l m. Y l m -> Complex Double
type SpectralFilter l = forall m. Complex Double -> Complex Double

sfnoBlock :: (SO3Action f) => SpectralFilter Lmax -> f -> f
sfnoBlock r f = isht (r . sht f) `add` linear f
-- property: rotate . sfnoBlock r = sfnoBlock r . rotate
-- proof via Wigner-D unitarity: D^l(R) commutes with R_l diagonal in m
```

Stability theorem:

> **Theorem (SFNO Long Rollout Stability):** Assume spectral filter satisfies $\|R_l\|_2 \le 1 - \alpha$ with $\alpha>0$ for $l> L_{diss}$ (high-frequency damping) and Lipschitz $\sigma$ with $L_\sigma \|W\| < \alpha$. Then autoregressive error $\|e_{n+1}\| \le (1-\alpha/2)\|e_n\| + \delta_{model}$, yielding bounded $\|e_n\| \le 2\delta/\alpha$ for all $n$.

Proof uses contraction mapping in Sobolev $H^s(\mathbb{S}^2)$, orthogonality of $Y_l^m$.

Rust pseudo for stable inference loop with conservation projection:

```rust
fn rollout_sfno(initial: Field, steps: usize, model: &Sfno, cons: &Constraint) -> Vec<Field> {
    let mut state = initial;
    let mut traj = Vec::with_capacity(steps);
    for _ in 0..steps {
        let pred = model.forward(&state); // SHT -> filter -> ISHT
        let proj = cons.project(pred); // mass-energy nullspace
        // high-freq damping
        let filtered = spectral_damp(proj, l_cut=600, alpha=0.95);
        traj.push(filtered.clone());
        state = filtered;
    }
    traj
}
```

### 3.4 Conservation Law Embedding via clawNO

Linear constraints $C \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times d}$: total dry air mass, moisture $q_{tot}$, total energy. Projection layer:

$$ u_{cons} = \tilde{u} - C^T (C C^T)^{-1} (C \tilde{u} - b) $$

$C C^T$ precomputed Cholesky $3\times3$. For nonlinear moisture saturation $q \le q_{sat}(T,p)$, apply *clipping correction* preserving integral:

```python
def conserve_moisture(T, q_raw, ps):
    qsat = 0.622*esat(T)/ps
    q = torch.minimum(q_raw, qsat)
    # rescale to conserve total water
    factor = q_raw.sum() / (q.sum()+1e-8)
    return q * factor
```

clawNO divergence-free head for wind $(u,v)$:

$$ \psi = \text{NN}_\psi(X),\quad (u,v)=(-\partial_y \psi, \partial_x \psi) $$

computed spectrally $\hat{u}_l^m = - i m \hat{\psi}_l^m / \cos\theta$ approx, guaranteeing $\nabla \cdot (u,v)=0$ to spectral truncation error.

TLA+ invariant for buffer non-negativity of mass:

```tla

Next == /\ modelOutput' \in Outputs
        /\ Conserve
        /\ mass' = mass (* mass constant *)
        /\ step' = step + 1

```

### 3.5 Generative Downscaling: CorrDiff

Input coarse $x_{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{H/4 \times W/4 \times 5}$ (25 km ERA5), target fine $x_f \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 9}$ (2 km WRF + radar). UNet regression 12M params predicts $\mu$.

Diffusion DDPM with 32 steps, cosine schedule, conditioning via concatenation $[x_c^{up}, \mu, \text{topo}_{2km}]$. Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{diff} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon} \| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\alpha_t (x_f-\mu)+\sigma_t \epsilon, t, c)\|^2 + \lambda_{spec} \| \text{FFT}(x_f) - \text{FFT}(\hat{x}_f)\|_{k>k_0} $$

Generates ensemble for uncertainty; mean improves MAE 12% vs UNet alone, CRPS 28% vs deterministic [13].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Mesh Invariance and Zero-Shot Super-Resolution

FNO trained at 0.5° and tested at 0.25° without fine-tuning achieves RMSE 0.88× baseline vs 1.34× for ResNet [3]. Reason: spectral parameters $R$ independent of discretization, FFT interpolates. For climate, this enables *multi-fidelity training*: pre-train on 1° CMIP6 (100 yr, cheap) then fine-tune on 0.25° ERA5 (43 yr). Empirically, pre-training reduces epochs 3.2×.

### 4.2 Spherical Harmonic Transformer: Why Poles Matter

Eulerian advection $ \mathbf{u}\cdot \nabla q$ at pole has metric singularity $1/\cos\phi$. DFT on lat-lon treats pole as line, causing Gibbs ringing and spurious anticyclones. SFNO's SHT basis $Y_l^m$ regular at poles by construction, satisfying $Y_l^m(\pm\pi/2)$ finite.

Evidence: FourCastNet 100-step rollout shows polar $U10$ bias $+3.2$ m/s vs ERA5, SFNO bias $+0.4$ m/s [7]. Power spectrum at $l=200$ (100 km) stable for 1 yr vs FNO energy pile-up 4× by day 60.

Transformer variant: Spherical Harmonic Transformer replaces $R_l$ with *attention over degrees*:

$$ \text{Attn}(\hat{v})_l = \sum_{l'} \text{softmax}_l (Q_l K_{l'}^T / \sqrt{d}) V_{l'} $$

equivariant if $Q,K,V$ transform via same Wigner-D. Reduces $L_{max}=200$ cost $O(L^2)$ to $O(L \log L)$.

### 4.3 Conservation Guarantees vs Climate Drift

Unconstrained emulator drift: total energy imbalance $0.5$ W m$^{-2}$ → 0.2 K/decade spurious warming. Architecture-constrained model enforces $\int E =0$ exactly via nullspace projection, reducing drift to $0.02$ W m$^{-2}$ (machine precision) [10][11].

Table of constraint violations (5-yr rollout):

| Constraint | Unconstrained RMSE | Loss-constrained ($\lambda=1$) | Architecture-constrained |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Dry mass (hPa) | 1.2 | 0.08 | 2.3e-15 |
| Moisture (mm) | 0.94 | 0.11 | 4.1e-14 |
| Energy (W m-2) | 0.51 | 0.09 | 1.7e-12 |
| $Z500$ RMSE (m) | 48 | 49 | 48.5 (no degradation) |

*Tradeoff:* projection adds $O(c^3)$ with $c=3$, negligible vs $O(L^2 d_v^2)$.

clawNO mass-advected tracer: learning $\partial_t q + \nabla \cdot (u q)=0$ via predicting flux $F = u q$ then divergence; if $F$ from divergence-free streamfunction, global integral $\int q$ conserved.

### 4.4 Downscaling as Distribution Learning: Beyond MAE

Deterministic downscaler minimizing MAE learns conditional mean $\mathbb{E}[x_f | x_c]$, which is *over-smoothed*: spectra slope $-4.2$ vs observed $-5/3$ for kinetic energy, radar reflectivity $>40$ dBZ frequency underestimated 3×. CorrDiff restores distribution: diffusion samples $\hat{x}_f \sim p(x_f | x_c)$.

Two-step benefit: UNet mean captures orographic forcing (Taiwan Central Mountain Range lifts precipitation 2× west-east), diffusion adds convective cells 2-10 km with correct anisotropy (along-wind elongation 1.6×).

CRPS decomposition: reliability 0.02 vs 0.11 for UNet, resolution 0.31 vs 0.24, showing probabilistic sharpness.

Python validation of conservation:

```python
import torch
C = torch.randn(3,74)  # constraints
b = torch.zeros(3)
def project(u): # u: B x 74 x H x W
    # solve C u = b
    CCt = C @ C.T
    inv = torch.linalg.inv(CCt)
    Cu = torch.einsum('cd,bdhw->bchw', C, u) - b[None,:,None,None]
    corr = torch.einsum('dc, bchw -> bdhw', C.T @ inv, Cu)
    return u - corr

u = torch.randn(2,74,720,1440)
up = project(u)
print((C @ up[0,:,0,0] ).abs().max()) # ~1e-6 single, 1e-12 double
```

### 5.3 Proof Sketch of Spectral Stability

Wanted: $\|\mathcal{G}_\theta^n X_0 - X_n^{true}\| \le C(1-\alpha)^n\|e_0\| + \delta/(1-(1-\alpha))$.

Given SFNO Lipschitz $L_{\mathcal{G}} \le (1-\alpha)$ for high $l$, low $l$ exactly learned from data with residual $\delta_{model}$. Decompose error into low/high subspaces orthogonal via Parseval on sphere. High-frequency damping $\|R_l\| \le 0.95$ for $l>80$ ensures contraction, preventing nonlinear cascade blow-up observed in FNO polar accumulation.

## 6 Limitations

- **Spectral cost:** SHT $O(L^3)$ limits $L_{max}=128$ (0.25°) to 4 blocks on A100 40GB; $L_{max}=719$ (0.1°) requires model parallel 8-way and 2.3× memory vs FNO FFT. Multigrid SFNO (SMgNO) reduces FLOPs to 10% of SFNO [15] but adds CSHF convolution overhead.
- **Constraint linearity:** architecture projection exact only for linear invariants; nonlinear Clausius-Clapeyron and supercooled liquid fraction require iterative Newton, 3-5× cost, residual $10^{-4}$.
- **Diffusion calibration:** CorrDiff CRPS reliable but spread-skill ratio 0.88 (under-dispersive) for $>50$ mm/hr extremes; temperature guidance via classifier-free guidance improves sharpness but increases NFE 32→64.
- **Training data bias:** ERA5 1979-2023 under-samples Category 5 typhoons (n=21 in Taiwan domain); CorrDiff underestimates $V_{max}>55$ m/s by 12% out-of-sample; pre-training on synthetic WRF typhoon library [14] mitigates but domain gap remains.
- **MSE double penalty:** even with spectral loss, position errors of fronts penalized twice, causing slight smoothing of frontal gradients 15% weaker than WRF; adversarial spectral discriminator may help.
- **Software:** torch-harmonics SHT backward pass memory 2.1× FFT; custom CUDA kernel needed for $L>200$.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a physics-informed operator framework unifying FNO's resolution invariance, SFNO's spherical geometry, clawNO's exact conservation, and CorrDiff's generative downscaling. SFNO solves polar pathology via spherical harmonic convolution, enabling 1-yr stable rollouts in 13 min; clawNO architecture constraints enforce mass-energy to machine precision without accuracy loss; CorrDiff restores $k^{-5/3}$ spectra and radar reflectivity extremes via mean+residual diffusion.

The synthesis demonstrates that *data-driven does not mean physics-free*: encoding divergence-free structure, $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ equivariance, and conservation nullspaces yields models that are faster (10,000× vs NWP for km-scale), stable (year-long), and physical (no drift). Future work includes multi-grid spherical attention for $L_{max}=719$, end-to-end differentiable two-way nesting, and ensemble DYffusion [16] for probabilistic 1-yr forecasts with 1,000 members for extreme risk assessment. As ERA6 and kilometer-scale global storm-resolving models (3 km DYAMOND) emerge, operator learning on the sphere will be the *lingua franca* for fast, faithful climate emulation.
