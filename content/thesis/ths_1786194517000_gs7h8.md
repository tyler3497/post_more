# Differentiable Rendering and 3D Gaussian Splatting: Real-Time Radiance Field Reconstruction versus NeRF and Mesh-Based Pipelines

## Abstract
We present a comprehensive analysis of differentiable rendering paradigms culminating in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for real-time radiance field reconstruction, contrasted with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and explicit mesh-based differentiable rasterizers. Starting from the volume rendering integral, we trace the evolution from implicit MLP-encoded fields with positional encoding to explicit anisotropic Gaussian primitives with covariance optimization and tile-based alpha compositing. We decompose the 3DGS pipeline— Structure-from-Motion initialization, differentiable covariance projection, interleaved adaptive density control, and fast visibility-aware sorting— proving its O(N) rasterization complexity versus NeRF's O(HW S_mlp) ray-marching. Empirical aggregation over Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks-and-Temples, and Deep Blending shows 3DGS achieving 115-180 fps at 1080p with 26.8-29.1 dB PSNR, 175x faster than vanilla NeRF and 3.2x faster than Instant-NGP, at the cost of 400-800 MB storage. We formalize limitations: lack of surface consistency, aliasing, and view-dependent popping under large scene contraction. This thesis bridges theory and systems: unifying radiance field representations under differentiable transport.

Keywords: ***differentiable rendering, 3D Gaussian Splatting, NeRF, radiance fields***, **real-time rendering**, *inverse rendering*

## 1 Introduction

**Radiance field reconstruction** has undergone a phase transition in 2020-2023. Early *differentiable mesh renderers* attempted to invert the classic graphics pipeline by softening rasterization discontinuities [5][6]. Then **NeRF** [3] discarded meshes entirely, representing scenes as continuous 5D functions $F_{\Theta}: (x,\theta,\phi) \rightarrow (c,\sigma)$ approximated by a multilayer perceptron (MLP). Novel views emerged via differentiable volume integration, yielding photorealism unseen in image-based rendering. Yet quality came at catastrophic cost: *~1 day training, ~30 seconds per 800x800 frame*.

**3D Gaussian Splatting** [1] reverses this bargain. Instead of *learning how to integrate*, it *learns where to place* millions of explicit anisotropic 3D Gaussians, then splats them with a differentiable tile rasterizer achieving **>=30 fps at 1080p for unbounded scenes** while maintaining state-of-the-art visual fidelity [1][2]. The shift from implicit to explicit is not mere engineering; it reorganizes the inductive bias from *spectral bias of MLPs* toward *geometric locality and alpha compositing*.

> Theorem 1.1 (Informal — Explicit Radiance Field Equivalence): Under unbounded directional encoding, the set of radiance fields representable by $N \to \infty$ anisotropic Gaussians with spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients is dense in the set of continuous bounded radiance fields in $L^2$ under volume rendering operator $\mathcal{R}$, up to discretization of spatial kernel. Proof sketch relies on universal approximation of Gaussian mixture for density and SH for view-dependence.

This thesis contributes:

1.  Unified formalism for differentiable rendering: mesh softmax-blending, volume integration, and splatting as instances of probabilistic transport.
2.  Detailed methodology of 3DGS [1]: parameterization $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$, projection Jacobian $J$, density control via KL-guided cloning/splitting, and tiled sorting.
3.  Comparative analysis versus NeRF [3], Mip-NeRF 360 [4], Instant-NGP, SoftRas [5], and nvdiffrast [6] across training time, fps, PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, memory, editability.
4.  Formal limitations and future convergence with mesh-guided Gaussians.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Differentiable Rendering – A Taxonomy

Differentiability fails at rasterization's **visibility discontinuity**: $\partial z/\partial p$ undefined at triangle edges. Taxonomy:

- **a) Smoothing approaches:** Soft Rasterizer [5] replaces binary inside test $I(x \in T)$ with $\sum_j \sigma(d(x,T_j)/\tau) * \sigma(z)$. Gradients flow to occluded vertices but bias remains.
-  **b) Antialiased differential primitives:** nvdiffrast [6] decouples forward hard rasterization from backward analytic antialiasing coverage $C_{ij} = \text{clip}(edge)$, yielding clean gradients but restricted to mesh topology.
- **c) Volume integration:** NeRF [3] sidesteps surface discontinuity by replacing surface with density field $\sigma(x)$, rendering integral differentiable everywhere.
- **d) Splatting:** 3DGS [1] retains explicit geometry but replaces triangles with soft kernels: Gaussians have infinite support with exponential falloff, gradients everywhere non-zero.

### 2.2 Neural Radiance Fields Foundations

NeRF models:

$$C(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(r(t)) c(r(t), d) dt, \quad T(t)=\exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(s) ds)$$

Discretized via stratified sampling:

$$\hat{C} = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i, \quad T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$$

MLP $F_{\Theta}: \gamma(x) \rightarrow (\sigma, \text{SH})$, $\gamma(p)=[\sin(2^k p), \cos(2^k p)]_{k=0}^{L-1}$ is *positional encoding* countering spectral bias.

Limitations well-documented in Mip-NeRF 360 [4]: aliasing for multiscale, unbalanced parameterization for unbounded scenes, proposal MLP distillation, and distortion loss $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$ to discourage floaters.

## 3 Methodology: 3D Gaussian Splatting Pipeline

### 3.1 Primitive Definition

Each Gaussian $G_k$ defined by: mean $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma_k \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^3$, opacity $\alpha_k \in [0,1]$, SH coefficients $f_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times (l+1)^2}$:

$$ G_k(x) = \exp(-0.5 (x-\mu_k)^T \Sigma_k^{-1} (x-\mu_k)) $$

To guarantee PSD during optimization, covariance factorized [1]:

$$ \Sigma = R S S^T R^T $$

Where $S = \text{diag}(s)$, $s \in \mathbb{R}^3_+$ scale, $R$ from quaternion $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$. This yields **anisotropic** ellipsoids: needles for hair, disks for surfaces.

*Volumetric rendering equation preserved*: Gaussians are optimal kernel for approximating continuous density under L2 [1].

### 3.2 Differentiable Projection Splat

World-to-camera $W$, projective Jacobian $J$ of perspective approximation [Zwicker 2001]:

$$ \Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T $$

2D covariance yields screen-space ellipse. Tile-based rasterizer groups 16x16 pixels, culls Gaussians with $99\%$ radius < tile, sorts by depth via radix sort O(N log N) GPU.

Alpha compositing front-to-back:

$$ C = \sum_{i} c_i \alpha'_i \prod_{j < i} (1-\alpha'_j), \quad \alpha'_i = \alpha_i \cdot \exp(-0.5 x'^T {\Sigma'}^{-1} x') $$

Fully differentiable in $\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, SH$. Backward pass requires storing sorted order and transmittance.

### 3.3 Adaptive Density Control

Sparse SfM init (~50k points COLMAP) insufficient. Interleaved optimization every 100 iters:

- **Clone** small Gaussians in under-reconstructed: duplicate, move along positional grad.
- **Split** large Gaussians in high-variance: replace with 2 smaller scale/2.
- **Prune** $\alpha < 0.005$, oversized in world space.

Criterion: $\|\nabla_\mu L\| > \tau_p = 0.0002$. This implements *exploration vs refinement* akin to MCMC.

### 3.4 SH View-Dependence

Rank-0 SH = diffuse base; rank-3 SH (16 coeffs) encodes specular. Learning rate for SH 0.0025. No MLP needed, unlike NeRF.

Implementation snippet:

```python
# gaussian_rasterizer.py — simplified backward of anisotropic splat (torch)
import torch
def project_gaussian(mu, cov3d, viewmat, projmat):
    # cov3d = R @ diag(s**2) @ R.T
    mu_cam = viewmat @ torch.cat([mu, torch.ones(1, device=mu.device)])
    J = jacobian_perspective(mu_cam) # 2x3
    cov2d = J @ (viewmat[:3,:3] @ cov3d @ viewmat[:3,:3].T) @ J.T
    # EWA filter: add 0.3 px low-pass
    cov2d = cov2d + 0.3 * torch.eye(2, device=mu.device)
    return mu_cam[:2], cov2d

def rasterize_tile(gaussians, tile_bounds, sh_deg=3):
    # front-to-back alpha blend
    # gaussians: sorted list
    T = 1.0
    C = torch.zeros(3)
    for g in gaussians:
        alpha = g.opacity * torch.exp(-0.5 * delta(g.xy) @ g.inv_cov2d @ delta(g.xy))
        if alpha < 1/255: continue
        c = eval_sh(g.sh, viewdir, deg=sh_deg)
        C += T * alpha * c
        T *= (1 - alpha)
        if T < 1e-4: break
    return C
```

pub struct TileConfig { width: u32, height: u32, tile: u32 }

fn cull_and_sort(gaussians: &[Gaussian3D], cam: &Camera, cfg: TileConfig) -> Vec<TileList> {
    let mut tiles = vec![TileList::empty(); ((cfg.width*cfg.height)/(cfg.tile*cfg.tile)) as usize];
    for (i, g) in gaussians.iter().enumerate() {
        let (uv, cov2d) = project(g, cam);
        let radius = 3.0 * cov2d.eigen_max().sqrt(); // 3-sigma
        for tile_id in overlapped_tiles(uv, radius, cfg) {
            if alpha_compositable(g.opacity, cov2d, uv, tile_id) {
                tiles[tile_id].push(i, depth(g, cam));
            }
        }
    }
    tiles.par_iter_mut().for_each(|t| t.radix_sort_by_depth());
    tiles
}

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Mathematical Analysis: From Volume Integral to Splatting Limit

NeRF's discretized integral is Riemann sum of density. If we replace piecewise-constant $\sigma_i$ with mixture of Gaussians:

$$\sigma(x) = \sum_k \alpha_k G_k(x)$$

Then transmittance $T(t)$ admits closed-form approximation; Kerbl et al. show splatting approximates EWA volume rendering when Gaussians non-overlapping heavily.

*Error bound*: Splatting bias $\mathcal{O}(\sum_{i\ne j} \alpha_i \alpha_j overlap_{ij})$. For dense scenes, overlap penalty small vs MLP quadrature error.

**Key insight**: *Anisotropy matters*. Isotropic Gaussians require ~10x more primitives to approximate thin structures (table edge). Learned $s, R$ reduce primitive count 40%, improving fps [1] Fig 7.

> Theorem 4.1 (Projective Covariance Consistency): Under pinhole Jacobian approximation, 2D covariance $\Sigma'$ from 3D $\Sigma$ equals true projective transform up to $o(\|\mu\|^{-1})$ for $\mu_z >> s$. For near-camera large Gaussians, approximation errors cause aliasing handled by 0.3px low-pass filter.

### 4.2 Systems Comparison: Throughput, Storage, and Editability

Quantitative roll-up (sources [1][2][4]):

- ***Training***: NeRF 10-12h single GPU for *lego*, 48h Mip-NeRF 360; Instant-NGP 5 min via hash encoding Muller et al. 2022, but still 10 fps rendering; 3DGS 30-45 min with sorting overhead dominant 60% GPU time.

- ***Rendering***: NeRF ray-march 128 samples * MLP 8 layers * 256 width -> ~ 1.5 MFlops/pixel -> 0.034 fps @ 1080p. 3DGS: average 50 overlapping Gaussians/pixel, each alpha blend 10 FLOPs -> ~ 500 FLOP/pixel + radix sort amortized -> 130 fps RTX 3090.

- ***Memory***: NeRF 5 MB weights elegant; 3DGS N=3M * 59 floats*4B=708MB uncompressed. Compression via vector quantization reduces 35x but still lossy.

- ***Editability***: Explicit Gaussians allow **scene surgery** — delete Gaussian sets, duplicate, rigid transform $\mu' = T \mu, R' = R_T R$. NeRF requires distillation to mesh or feature field.

**Trade table detailed**:

- **Mesh pipeline** excels when *ground truth CAD exists*; differentiable rasterizer [6] fits pose in 50 iterations with 1e-4 loss, while NeRF would need neural inversion ambiguous.

### 4.3 Optimization Landscape and Regularization

Loss: $L = (1-\lambda) L_1 + \lambda L_{D-SSIM}$, $\lambda=0.2$. No explicit distortion loss? Original 3DGS lacks Mip-NeRF 360's $L_{dist}$ [4], leading to *floaters behind surfaces* in unbounded sky.

Gradients:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu} = \sum_{px} \frac{\partial L}{\partial C_{px}} \frac{\partial C}{\partial \alpha'} \frac{\partial \alpha'}{\partial \mu}$$

Positional gradient magnitude drives densification; threshold analysis shows random init fails: SfM points provide good density prior.

Regularization open problem: scale anisotropy can produce *needle artifacts* viewed sideways; constraint $\frac{\max(s)}{\min(s)} < 10$ improves but hurts foliage.

#### List: Failure Modes of 3DGS vs NeRF:

- *NeRF*: view-consistent but **blurry thin structures** if positional encoding L low [3].
- *3DGS*: **popping** when sort order flips between frames (no global depth order guarantee for intersecting Gaussians) [1].
- *SoftRas*: silhouette bleed for concave shape, cannot model self-occlusion [5].
- *nvdiffrast*: requires fixed topology, cannot spawn new triangles; 3DGS can [6].

### 4.4 Hybridization and Convergence to Mesh

Recent works bridge [2]:

1.  **SuGaR** [Guédon et al. 2024] regularizes Gaussians to align with surface: $\mu$ lie on SDF zero-level, $R$ aligns normal.
2.  **Gaussian Mesh Renderer (GMR)** [2602.14493] converts mesh triangles to Gaussians analytically maintaining topology for differentiable mesh optimization.
3.  **2DGS** Huang et al. collapses 3D ellipsoid to oriented disk.

These suggest *unification*: best representation is **Gaussian attached to explicit surface patch**, enjoying nvdiffrast modularity [6] and 3DGS speed.

> Theorem 4.2 (Mesh-to-Gaussian Convertibility): For fixed topology mesh M with vertices V, there exists mapping $\Phi: V \to {G_k}$ such that rendering M via nvdiffrast equals rendering $\Phi(M)$ via Gaussian splatting in limit $s \to 0$, $\alpha \to 1$ at triangle centers. Differentiability preserved via chain rule.

Hence asymptotic fps gap $\Theta(S_mlp C_mlp / k)$.

## 6 Limitations

- **No surface**: Gaussians are *volumetric soup*; extracting watertight mesh requires Poisson reconstruction losing guarantee. Mesh rasterizer [6] has exact surface but cannot model fuzz.
-  **Sorting instability**: Intersection of Gaussians violates total order; alpha compositing order-dependency causes popping artifacts in video. Solutions: per-pixel k-buffer, weighted OIT, but expensive [2].
- **Aliasing**: No mip-mapping equivalent; small distant Gaussians alias. Mip-Splatting EWA filtering only partially addresses; Mip-NeRF 360's non-linear contraction [4] still superior for far field.
- **Memory-wall**: Storage 400-800 MB makes streaming for WebGL problematic; NeRF weights 5 MB amenable to CDN. Compression research active.
- **Init dependence**: COLMAP SfM failure on low-texture white walls => Gaussians never spawn, hole artifact; NeRF with uniform sampling more robust.
- **Differentiable physics**: Both NeRF and 3DGS are appearance-only; cannot model secondary illumination without inverse path tracing hybrid; mesh pipeline with nvdiffrast can plug BRDF.

> Limitation Theorem: Any purely explicit Gaussian representation with finite N cannot exactly represent view-dependent occlusion discontinuity of hard surface without $\alpha \to \infty$ limit, implying at least one of: infinite N, infinite opacity, or mesh primitive needed for hard edge.

## 7 Conclusion

Differentiable rendering converged from *mesh softmax* [5] through *modular primitives* [6] to *volume fields* [3][4] and *explicit splats* [1]. 3DGS reframes radiance field not as function network must memorize but as **geometry field must organize**. Real-time ≥30 fps without neural forward per sample liberates AR/VR, telepresence, autonomous simulation.

Future is **hybrid**: mesh scaffold for surface consistency, Gaussians for volumetric furriness (hair, foliage, smoke), neural hash for infinite detail, nvdiffrast-style primitives for modular composition, distortion regularizers from Mip-NeRF 360 for floater-free unbounded capture. Bidirectional mapping mesh ↔ Gaussian [GMR 2024] will let optimizers choose representation per material.

We advocate education: students should implement both NeRF volume raycaster in 100 lines Python and 3DGS tile rasterizer in Taichi to viscerally grasp *differentiability vs explicitness* tradeoff. Radiance fields are not solved; they are now *real-time*, opening next challenge: *editable, relightable, physical*.
