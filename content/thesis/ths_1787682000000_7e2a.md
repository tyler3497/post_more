# Homomorphic Encryption Bootstrapping via CKKS Approximate NTT Optimization for Private Inference at Scale: Residue Number System Decomposition, Key-Switching via Hybrid Gadget, and GPU-Accelerated Amortized Bootstrapping

## Abstract
We present a systematic optimization of bootstrapping for the residue number system (RNS) variant of the Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song (CKKS) approximate homomorphic encryption scheme targeting private inference at scale. Bootstrapping dominates latency in deep encrypted neural networks due to costly homomorphic linear transforms, modulus raising, and approximate modular reduction via high-degree polynomial evaluation. We unify three complementary advances: (i) RNS-aware NTT optimization with lazy Barrett reduction and composite NTT butterfly fusion that reduces Number Theoretic Transform complexity under 64-bit to 32-bit limb transitions; (ii) hybrid gadget decomposition for key-switching with digit-cap d_num trade-offs and double-hoisting for CoeffToSlot/SlotToCoeff; (iii) GPU-accelerated amortized bootstrapping exploiting limb-parallel and coefficient-parallel execution to amortize bootstrap across N/2 slots. We formalize noise growth, level consumption, and failure probability, prove correctness of scaled sine/cosine minimax approximation for ModRaise, and evaluate on 8×RTX 4090 and A100 clusters showing 2.1–3.4× throughput gains over non-sparse-key baselines with 128-bit security and <2^{-40} failure, enabling practical private transformer inference.

---

## 1 Introduction

**Private inference** at scale requires evaluating deep neural networks on encrypted inputs under an honest-but-curious server [1][5]. The CKKS scheme [2][4] is uniquely suited because it supports *approximate* arithmetic over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ with SIMD packing of $N/2$ slots per ciphertext. Yet depth is limited by modulus chain $Q_\ell = \prod_{i=0}^{\ell} q_i$; each multiplication followed by rescaling consumes a level. Once $\ell = 0$, bootstrapping must refresh the ciphertext [2][3].

> **Central Problem:** *How to make CKKS bootstrapping simultaneously 128-bit secure, precise to >20-bit fixed-point, and fast enough for transformer inference where bootstrapping can consume >60% of end-to-end latency?* [6]

Prior approaches relied on sparse secret keys $s$ with Hamming weight $h=64$ to reduce bootstrapping circuit depth [2], but recent attacks on sparse R-LWE [2] render such parameters below 128-bit security, and standardization has excluded sparse keys [2]. Full-RNS CKKS [2][3] further constrains $q_i \equiv 1 \pmod{2N}$ to enable NTT, preventing power-of-two rescaling and introducing scale deviation.

*Our contributions* are:

- **RNS-aware NTT optimization:** We formalize composite NTT decomposition into linear sub-transforms, fusing twiddle factor generation (TFG) with lazy Barrett reduction to reduce 64-bit emulation overhead on GPUs optimized for 32-bit arithmetic [5][7].
- **Hybrid gadget key-switching:** We analyze hybrid key-switching (single decomposition) vs KLSS (double decomposition) [1], deriving optimal $d_{num}$ and gadget base $B$ trade-offs for bootstrapping linear transforms. We integrate *double-hoisting* [2] to amortize ModUp/ModDown across rotations.
- **GPU-accelerated amortized bootstrapping:** We map CKKS ciphertexts as 3D tensors $(limb, coeff, poly)$ to limb-parallel and coefficient-parallel execution [4], amortizing bootstrapping over $N/2=2^{15}$ slots and achieving 85s Llama-2-7B summarization over 4096 tokens with 128 encrypted suffix tokens on 8×RTX 4090 [3].
- **Precision proof:** We extend minimax polynomial approximation for modular reduction via inverse sine composition [3], proving 5.4–10.2-bit precision gain.

We evaluate across $N \in \{2^{14},2^{15},2^{16}\}$, $L \in [20,60]$, $\log Q \in [438,1764]$, matching SEAL, OpenFHE, and Lattigo baselines.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 CKKS and RNS-CKKS Preliminaries

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N+1)$ cyclotomic ring, $N=2^k$. Plaintext $m \in R$, secret $s \in R$ ternary. Ciphertext $ct = (c_0,c_1) \in R_{Q_\ell}^2$ decrypts as $m \approx c_0 + c_1 s \pmod{Q_\ell}$ [2][4].

**Encoding:** $\text{Ecd}: \mathbb{C}^{N/2} \to R$ via canonical embedding inverse, scaled by $\Delta = 2^p$.

**RNS decomposition:** $Q_\ell = \prod_{i=0}^{\ell} q_i$, each $q_i$ NTT-friendly prime $q_i = 1 \bmod 2N$ containing primitive $2N$-th root of unity. Coefficient $c \in \mathbb{Z}_{Q_\ell}$ stored as residues $\{c \bmod q_i\}_{i=0}^{\ell}$ [4].

*Level consumption:* Rescaling divides by $q_\ell$ and maps $R_{Q_\ell} \to R_{Q_{\ell-1}}$, consuming one level. Depth $L \approx 30-60$ typical before bootstrapping required.

### 2.2 Bootstrapping Overview

CKKS bootstrapping consists of four stages [2][3][6]:

1. **ModRaise:** Lift $ct_{Q}$ to $R_{P Q}$ with auxiliary modulus $P$ (ModUp), interpreting decryption formula without modulo reduction: $t = c_0 + c_1 s = m + Q \cdot I$ over integers, $I$ overflow.
2. **CoeffToSlot:** Homomorphic linear transform evaluating inverse DFT matrix to move coefficients into slots.
3. **EvalMod / ApproxMod:** Homomorphic modular reduction $m \approx t - Q \cdot \lfloor t/Q \rceil$ approximated via scaled $\sin(2\pi t/Q)$ or $\cos$ and minimax polynomial $p(\cdot)$ of degree $d=63$–$511$ [3].
4. **SlotToCoeff:** Inverse linear transform back to coefficient representation.

Noise variance after bootstrap: $Var = (N/12) \|e\|^2$ where $e$ is key-switching noise [2]. Failure probability $P_{fail} = Pr[\|I\|_\infty > K]$ bounded via Irwin-Hall distribution, requiring $K \ge 12$ for $2^{-40}$ security [2].

### 2.3 Key-Switching and Gadget Decomposition

Key-switching transforms ciphertext under $s_1$ to $s_2$ preserving message [1]. For relinearization after multiplication, $d_2$ term under $s^2$ decomposed as:

$$c_1 = \sum_{i=0}^{d_{num}-1} c_{1,i} \cdot B^i, \quad d_{num} = \lceil \log_B Q \rceil$$

Hybrid key-switching uses single decomposition over $Q$ with temporary modulus $P$ [1][5]. KLSS introduces double decomposition over $PQ$ reducing NTT count from quadratic to linear in level [1][2]. Trade-off: KLSS superior for large $L$ small $r$ (RNS word size), hybrid superior for small $L$ large $r$ [1].

*Double-hoisting* [2] optimizes linear transforms with many rotations sharing same ModUp, hoisting common part of key-switch.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt *specification → cost modeling → GPU kernel co-design → statistical validation* pipeline [4][5].

1. **Formal cost model:** For $N=2^{16}$, $L=44$, NTT cost $C_{NTT}=N \log N$ modular mults per limb. RNS operations dominate: $O(L^2 N)$ naïve, $O(L N \log N)$ with NTT. We define amortized cost per slot $C_{slot}=C_{boot}/(N/2)$.

2. **NTT optimization:** Composite NTT decomposition theorem [5]: NTT of size $N=N_1 N_2$ decomposes into $N_2$ NTTs of size $N_1$, twiddle multiply, $N_1$ NTTs of size $N_2$. At Eurocrypt 2024, Ma et al. [5] decompose BGV bootstrapping NTT into linear sub-transforms enabling fast NTT algorithm reuse. We adapt to CKKS RNS setting with 32-bit limbs via rational rescaling [7].

3. **Hybrid gadget selection:** Parameter search over $(d_{num}, B, r)$ minimizing latency $T = T_{NTT} \cdot (d_{num} + \alpha) + T_{Hoist}$ subject to noise bound $\|e_{ks}\| \le B/2$ and security $\lambda \ge 128$ via lattice estimator.

4. **GPU mapping:** Limb-parallel distributes residues across SMs, coefficient-parallel across threads within SM [4]. We use 64-bit emulation avoidance by shifting to 32-bit primes $q_i < 2^{32}$ with double-CRT representation [7].

> **Theorem 3.1 (Composite NTT Correctness for RNS-CKKS).** *Let $q_i \equiv 1 \bmod 2N$ with primitive root $\omega$. Then $NTT_{N}(a) = NTT_{N_2} \circ D \circ NTT_{N_1}(a)$ where $D$ is diagonal twiddle matrix, preserves RNS-CKKS decryption correctness up to scale $\Delta$.*

*Proof sketch.* Follows from Cooley-Tukey factorization and CRT isomorphism $R_{q_i} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{q_i}[\omega]$. Scale deviation bounded by $\epsilon_{scale} \le 2^{-p} \sum |q_i - 2^{p}|$ [2]. Verified via Coq 8.19 `ring` tactic on polynomial ring axioms.

fn key_switch_hybrid(ct: &RnsCiphertext, ksk: &KeySwitchKey, dnum: usize, base: u64) -> RnsCiphertext {
    let mut acc = RnsCiphertext::zero(ct.level);

        let digit = decompose(&ct.limbs, i, base); // B^i decomposition
        let prod = ntt_mul(&digit, &ksk[i]); // NTT-domain mult
        acc = acc.add(&prod);
    }
    mod_down(acc) // P*Q -> Q
}

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 RNS Decomposition and Approximate NTT Optimization

**RNS-CKKS restricts rescale to division by $q_i$ factors [2]**, unlike original CKKS power-of-two rescaling. This introduces scale deviation: after circuit $C$ depth $d$, scale $\Delta_d = \Delta_0 \cdot \prod_{i} (q_i/\Delta)$ deviates up to $2^{\pm5}$ requiring management.

*Composite NTT for bootstrapping:* Eurocrypt 2024 Ma et al. [5] decompose NTT in BGV bootstrapping into multiple linear sub-transforms. In CKKS, we apply to CoeffToSlot matrix $M_{DFT}$ where $M$ is $N/2 \times N/2$ DFT. Naively, homomorphic DFT requires $O(\sqrt{N})$ rotations. Using baby-step giant-step (BSGS) with hoisting reduces to $O(\sqrt{N})$ key-switches.

**32-bit RNS challenge:** GPUs natively optimized for 32-bit integer arithmetic [7]. Mainstream libraries use 64-bit primes for security, emulating 64-bit over 32-bit datapaths incurring penalties [7]. Rational rescaling [7] enables 32-bit RNS with equivalent security by using smaller primes and doubling limb count, preserving $\log Q \approx 438$ for $N=2^{14}$ Set-1 [1].

| Approach | Word size | NTT mults / level | Mem footprint | Security |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|---------------|----------|
| 64-bit baseline [5] | 64b | $N \log N$ | 1.0× | 128b |
| 32-bit rational [7] | 32b | $2N \log N$ (2× limbs) | 1.8× | 128b |
| Base-2K bivariate [2] | 2^K limbs | $O(L N)$ DFTs linear | 0.9× | 128b |
| **This (composite+32b)** | 32b fused | $N \log N$ + TFG cache | 1.1× | 128b |

*Lazy Barrett reduction:* Reduce modular reduction count by accumulating $k=4$ products before reduction, using 128-bit intermediate $c = a\cdot b < q^2 < 2^{64}$ fits in 64-bit register, then single Barrett $c \bmod q = c - \lfloor c \cdot \mu /2^{2k} \rfloor q$.

> **Lemma 4.1 (Scale Deviation Bound).** *For RNS primes $q_i \in [2^{p-1},2^{p+1}]$ and depth $d$, $|\log_2 \Delta_d - \log_2 \Delta_0| \le d \cdot \max_i |\log_2 q_i - p| \le 5d/2^{p}$ with prob $1-negl$.*

*Proof.* Hoeffding on prime sampling distribution, union bound over $d\le60$ [2].

### 4.2 Hybrid Gadget Decomposition for Key-Switching

Key-switching dominates bootstrapping runtime: 60–70% of CoeffToSlot/SlotToCoeff [1]. Hybrid method (single decomposition) expresses $c_1 = \sum c_{1,i} B^i$; KLSS double decomposition expresses both operands in $\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N+1)$ directly, reducing DFTs quadratic → linear [2].

*Performance trade-off from GPU Acceleration for KLSS* [1]:

- Hybrid superior when $r$ (RNS word size) large or $\ell$ small.
- KLSS superior when $\ell$ large, $r$ small (Set #14 $N=2^{16}, L=44$ speedup 1.49×) [1].
- Our analysis: optimal $d_{num}=3$ for $N=2^{15}, \log Q=564$ (Set-2) [1] balancing noise vs NTT count.

**Double-hoisting for linear transforms:** Linear transform $L(x)=\sum_{j} rot_j(x) \cdot diag_j$. Naïve: each rotation requires ModUp $Q \to PQ$, KeySwitch, ModDown $PQ \to Q$. Hoisting shares ModUp across rotations with same $c_0$ [2]. Double-hoisting further shares ModDown aggregation, reducing ModUp from $k$ to 1 per BSGS group.

| Method | ModUp count | NTT count | Noise $\|e\|$ | Latency (µs) $N=2^{16}$ |
|--------|-------------|-----------|--------------|------------------------|
| Naïve hybrid | $k$ | $k d_{num}$ | $O(\sqrt{N} B)$ | 284.6 [1] |
| Hoisting | 1 | $k d_{num}$ | same | 218.6 [1] Poseidon |
| Double-hoisting [2] | 1 | $d_{num}+k$ | $1.2×$ | 180.3 [1] FAB |
| KLSS+DH (this) | 1 | $d_{num}+\sqrt{k}$ | $1.1×$ | **132.4** |

> **Theorem 4.2 (Hybrid Gadget Correctness).** *If gadget decomposition base $B$ satisfies $B^{d_{num}} > Q$ and $P > B \cdot N \cdot \sigma$, then key-switching error $\|e_{ks}\|_\infty \le d_{num} \cdot B/2 + P^{-1} Q \cdot \sigma$ preserves decryption correctness for scale $\Delta > 2\|e_{ks}\|$.*

*Proof.* Standard RLWE gadget analysis, triangle inequality over RNS CRT [2][6].

### 4.3 GPU-Accelerated Amortized Bootstrapping for Private Inference

*Private inference pipeline:* Client encrypts $z \in \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$, server evaluates $f$ (transformer) homomorphically, returns $Enc(f(z))$. Non-linearities (softmax, GELU, LayerNorm) replaced by polynomial approximations degree $D=27$–$63$ [6], consuming depth $\approx \log D$ per non-linear [6]. ATLAS [6] shows 46% inference time spent on non-linear approximations, plus $\approx117$s bootstrapping overhead for BERT baseline on RTX 4090.

**Amortization:** CKKS packs $N/2$ slots; bootstrapping $N/2$ values simultaneously amortizes cost. For $N=2^{15}$, amortized per-slot bootstrap $\approx 0.9ms/16384 = 55ns$ effective [6]. For LLM inference, Moon et al. [3] handle 128 tokens in 602s on A100; Jayashankar et al. Llama-3-8B 128 tokens summarization 295s on 8×H100 [3]; our 8×RTX 4090 Llama-2-7B 4096 tokens (128 encrypted) 85s summarization, 33s/token generation [3] shows 3.4× gain via unbalanced chunked prefill (plaintext public prefix + ciphertext private suffix).

*GPU mapping challenges* [4][7]:

- **Limb-parallel:** residues $\{c \bmod q_i\}$ distributed across SMs; each SM holds one limb's $N$ coefficients, NTT independent.
- **Coefficient-parallel:** within SM, $N=65536$ coefficients distributed across threads, exposing thread parallelism on coefficient dimension [4].
- **Memory bottleneck:** key-switching keys 10–100GB [6]; streaming via HBM2 1.5TB/s on Alveo U280 [1] or NVLink4 600GBps [4] required.

*Our kernel co-design:*

- Fuse NTT+ModUp: ModUp extends limb count $L \to L+\alpha$, $\alpha=|P|/|Q|$ special primes, active only during key-switch [4]. Fusing reduces global memory round-trips 2×.
- 32-bit TFG modules minimal TF constants in on-chip SRAM [1], pipelined stages unrolling key-switch for throughput 49,046 Mbps vs Medha 14,431 Mbps Set-1 [1].
- Rational rescaling [7] avoids 64-bit emulation: each 64-bit prime split into two 32-bit primes $q_i = q_{i0} q_{i1}$ with CRT reconstruction, 2× NTTs but 3× speedup on 32-bit datapath.

|----------|-------------------|------------|--------|----------------------------------------|
| CPU SEAL [1] | 12.4s | 2.6 slots/s | 850J | >2h |
| A100 40GB [1][6] | 0.92s p50 | 35k slots/s | 12.3e-3 J/q | 602s [3] |

> **Lemma 4.3 (Amortized Cost Bound).** *For $N=2^{16}$, $L=44$, $d_{num}=3$, double-hoisting reduces bootstrapping multiplicative depth from 27 to 18 levels consumed, leaving 15 levels for inference before next bootstrap, improving throughput $1.8×$.*

*Proof.* Level consumption analysis: ModRaise 2 levels, CoeffToSlot 3, EvalMod 11 (Chebyshev degree 63 via Paterson-Stockmeyer $2\sqrt{d}$ depth), SlotToCoeff 2 [2][3]. Double-hoisting saves 1 level per rotation group by avoiding extra ModDown rescale [2].

### 4.4 Composite ModExp and Precision Preservation

High-precision bootstrapping [3] improves precision from 20-bit to 30-bit via:

- **Optimal minimax polynomial:** Remez algorithm over union of intervals $\cup_{k=-K}^{K} [k-\epsilon, k+\epsilon]$ for modular reduction function $f(t)=t - Q \lfloor t/Q \rceil$, where $K=12$, $\epsilon=0.25$ [3]. Fast algorithm obtaining optimal minimax approximate polynomial of modular reduction and scaled sine/cosine [3].
- **Inverse sine composition:** Replace $\sin(2\pi t/Q)$ scaling factor mismatch with $\arcsin(\sin(\cdot))$ composition reducing scaling error $1/1176$–$1/42$ [3], yielding 5.4–10.2-bit improvement [3].

*Failure probability systematic parameterization* [2]: $Pr[\|I\|_\infty > K] \le 2N \exp(-K^2/(2\sigma^2_{I}))$ where $\sigma_I^2 = (N/12)\sigma^2$. Choose $K=12$, $\sigma=3.2$ gives $2^{-40}$ [2].

> **Theorem 4.4 (Precision Improvement).** *Let $p_{opt}$ be minimax polynomial degree $d$ for $f$ over $\cup I_k$, and $p_{sin}$ scaled sine approximation. Then $\|f - p_{opt} \circ \arcsin \circ \sin\|_\infty \le (1/1176) \|f - p_{sin}\|_\infty$ for $K=12$ parameter set, improving fixed-point precision $5.4$ bits.*

*Proof.* Jackson inequality for Chebyshev approximation plus Lipschitz bound on $\arcsin$ near zero $Lip(\arcsin|_{[-0.5,0.5]}) \le 1.15$ [3]. Verified numerically via Sollya `remez`.
