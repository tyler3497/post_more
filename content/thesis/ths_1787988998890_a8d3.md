# Computational Fluid Dynamics with Physics-Informed Neural Operators: Fourier Neural Operator Spectral Bias, DeepONet Branch-Trunk Decomposition, and Turbulence Closure Modeling

## Abstract
We present a unified analysis of physics-informed neural operators for computational fluid dynamics, focusing on Fourier Neural Operator spectral bias, DeepONet branch-trunk decomposition, and learned turbulence closure modeling. Neural operators learn mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces, enabling zero-shot super-resolution and three-order-of-magnitude speedups over spectral solvers, yet exhibit systematic high-wavenumber attenuation due to truncated spectral convolution and L2-dominated optimization. We characterize spectral bias through energy-spectrum error and binned spectral power loss, contrast FNO's global Fourier inductive bias with DeepONet's separable branch-trunk basis learning, and formulate RANS and LES closure corrections as operator learning problems. We derive adjoint-efficient training for stiff coupled PDE-closure systems and demonstrate that physics-informed training at higher resolution than data restores conservation and divergence-free constraints while mitigating spectral bias. The framework provides discretization-invariant surrogates for parametric Navier-Stokes families.

## 1 Introduction

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) for incompressible and compressible Navier-Stokes equations remains dominated by the **curse of resolution**. Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) resolves Kolmogorov scale $\eta = (\nu^3/\epsilon)^{1/4}$ at $O(Re^{9/4})$ cost, while Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) [7] and Large Eddy Simulation (LES) [8] introduce unclosed terms requiring **turbulence closure modeling**. Classical closures—$k$-$\omega$, $k$-$\epsilon$, SST, Smagorinsky, WALE—assume *isotropic eddy viscosity* $\nu_t = C_s^2 \Delta^2 |S|$ and fail in separation, anisotropy, and bluff-body wakes [2][7].

Neural operators [1][3][4] learn mappings $\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{V} \to \mathcal{U}$ between function spaces, where $\mathcal{V}$ contains parametric input functions (initial conditions, forcing, geometry) and $\mathcal{U}$ contains solutions. Unlike Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) that solve one instance, operators learn an *entire family* of PDEs [1][2]. The **Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)** [1] parameterizes integral kernels directly in Fourier space enabling $O(N \log N)$ global convolution and *zero-shot super-resolution*. **DeepONet** [3] decomposes operators via universal approximation theorem for operators into branch and trunk networks.

This thesis synthesizes three axes:

1. **Physics-Informed Neural Operators (PINO)** [2] combining coarse data with high-resolution PDE constraints
2. **Spectral bias** in FNO and remedies via binned spectral loss and diffusion posterior correction [9][10]
3. **DeepONet branch-trunk decomposition** and **neural turbulence closures** for RANS/LES [6][7][8]

We argue that spectral bias is not merely architectural truncation but optimization-induced: $L^2$ loss is dominated by low-wavenumber energy, causing systematic underprediction of $E(k) \sim k^{-5/3}$ inertial range. We show mitigation requires *frequency-aware losses*, *mode-adaptive factorization*, and *frozen-surrogate diffusion correction*.

> Theorem 1 (Spectral Bias in Truncated Fourier Operators): Let $\mathcal{F}_{k_{max}}$ be FNO with $k_{max}$ retained modes and pointwise residual $W$. Under $L^2$ training on fields with power-law spectrum $E(k) \propto k^{-\alpha}$, $\alpha>1$, the expected spectral error $\mathbb{E}[|\hat{u}_{pred}(k)-\hat{u}_{true}(k)|^2] \propto k^{-2}$ for $k>k_{max}$ if $W$ is Lipschitz, but optimization dynamics bias $R^\ell(k)$ toward low $k$ even for $k \le k_{max}$ with rate $\exp(-\lambda_k t)$ where $\lambda_k \propto |\hat{u}(k)|^2$.

## 2 Background

## 2.1 Navier-Stokes Operator Learning

Incompressible Navier-Stokes:

$$ \partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla) u = -\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 u + f, \quad \nabla \cdot u = 0 $$

Solution operator $\mathcal{G}^\dagger: a \mapsto u$ maps $a=(u_0,f,\nu, \text{geometry})$ to space-time solution. Classical solvers discretize $D$ into $n$ points and solve $O(n^3)$ per step. Neural operators learn $\mathcal{G}_\theta \approx \mathcal{G}^\dagger$ discretization-invariant: train on $64^2$, evaluate on $256^2$ without retraining [1].

*Key properties:*

- *Resolution invariance*: $\mathcal{G}_\theta(x)$ defined for any $x\in D$, not just grid $D_j$
- *Parametric family*: one model for $\nu \in [10^{-4},10^{-3}]$
- *Speed*: 3 orders magnitude faster than pseudo-spectral [1]

## 2.2 Fourier Neural Operator

FNO [1] defines iterative updates:

$$ v^{\ell+1}(x) = \sigma\left(W v^\ell(x) + \mathcal{F}^{-1}(R^\ell(k)\mathcal{F}(v^\ell)(k))(x)\right) $$

where $R^\ell(k) \in \mathbb{C}^{d_v \times d_v}$ learned for $|k| \le k_{max}$, typically 12-20 modes per dimension. Lifting $P: \mathbb{R}^{d_a} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_v}$ and projection $Q$ are shallow MLPs. This is a *spectral convolution* approximating kernel integral $\int \kappa(x-y) v(y) dy$.

**Strengths:** global receptive field, spectral accuracy for smooth periodic data, $O(n \log n)$.

**Weakness:** explicit truncation induces bias; pointwise $W$ can carry high-freq but optimization does not incentivize it under MSE [9].

## 2.3 Physics-Informed Neural Operator

PINO [2] addresses data scarcity by adding PDE loss at higher resolution than data:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + \lambda_{pde} || \mathcal{N}(\mathcal{G}_\theta(a))||^2 + \lambda_{div} ||\nabla\cdot u||^2 + \lambda_{cons} \mathcal{L}_{cons} $$

where $\mathcal{N}$ is Navier-Stokes residual computed via spectral differentiation or Fourier continuation [10]. FC-PINO [10] uses Fourier continuation to handle non-periodic boundaries with $O(N^{-(d-k)})$ derivative convergence, where $d$ is continuation order.

PINO succeeds where PINN fails for Kolmogorov flows and multi-scale chaos because operator learning regularizes optimization landscape [2].

## 2.4 Turbulence Closure Problem

RANS: mean of $u_i u_j$ gives $\bar{u}_i \bar{u}_j + \tau_{ij}$, $\tau_{ij}$ Reynolds stress unclosed. Boussinesq hypothesis: $\tau_{ij} - \frac{2}{3}k\delta_{ij} = -2\nu_t S_{ij}$. LES: filtered product minus product of filtered gives $\tau^{SGS}_{ij}$, Smagorinsky $\tau^{SGS} = -2(C_s\Delta)^2 |\bar{S}| \bar{S}$.

Neural closures learn $C_\theta: (\bar{u}, \nabla \bar{u}, \mu) \mapsto \nabla \tau_{ij}$ [8] or map invariants to EARSM coefficients [6]. DARSM [6] derives implicit algebraic stress equation under weak-equilibrium and trains via adjoint exploiting IMEX solver structure, eliminating distribution shift of offline training.

## 3.2 Spectral Bias Mitigation

We implement three mitigation strategies:

1. **Binned Spectral Power (BSP) Loss** [9]: $\mathcal{L}_{BSP} = \sum_b ||\log E_{pred}(b) - \log E_{true}(b)||^2$ where $b$ bins wavenumbers.
2. **Mode-Adaptive Factorization**: factorize $R$ as $R_1 R_2^T$ to support $k_{max}=64$ vs 12 [1]
3. **Diffusion Posterior Sampling**: frozen FNO + sparse observations + generative prior to correct high-$k$ without retraining [9]

## 3.3 DeepONet Branch-Trunk Formalism

DeepONet approximates operator via Chen & Chen universal theorem:

$$ \mathcal{G}(v)(\xi) = \sum_{k=1}^p b_k(v) t_k(\xi) + b_0 $$

- *Branch*: $\beta: \mathcal{U} \to \mathbb{R}^p$, $\beta(v)=\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{E}(v)$, $\mathcal{E}(v) = (v(x_1),...,v(x_m))$ sensor values
- *Trunk*: $\tau: \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}^{p+1}$, $\tau_k(y)$ learns basis
- Output inner product $\mathbf{b}^T \mathbf{t}$

POD-DeepONet replaces $t_k$ with fixed POD modes $\phi_k$ from training snapshots, mean field $\phi_0$ subtracted [4]. Neural-POD learns modes via autoencoder for viscosity-generalizable bases.

*Advantages for CFD:* handles arbitrary query points, unstructured meshes via GNN extension [7], interpretable basis.

## 3.4 Turbulence Closure as Operator

We formulate closure operator:

$$ \mathcal{C}_\theta: (\bar{S}, \bar{\Omega}, y^+, Re_\tau, \nabla k) \mapsto (\nu_t, a_{ij}) $$

where $a_{ij}$ anisotropy tensor. Training via end-to-end differentiable RANS solver with adjoint [6]:

```python
# PyTorch-style FNO layer with spectral bias monitoring
import torch
import torch.fft as fft

class FNOLayer(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_v, k_max):
        super().__init__()
        self.W = torch.nn.Conv2d(d_v, d_v, 1)
        self.R = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(d_v, d_v, k_max, k_max, dtype=torch.cfloat)*0.1)
        self.k_max = k_max
    def forward(self, v):
        # v: [B, d_v, H, W]
        v_ft = fft.rfft2(v, norm='ortho')
        out_ft = torch.zeros_like(v_ft)
        out_ft[:, :, :self.k_max, :self.k_max] = torch.einsum('ijxy,bjxy->bixy', self.R, v_ft[:, :, :self.k_max, :self.k_max])
        v_spec = fft.irfft2(out_ft, s=v.shape[-2:], norm='ortho')
        # spectral energy diagnostic
        energy = (v_ft.abs()**2).mean()
        return torch.nn.functional.gelu(self.W(v) + v_spec), energy
```

## 4 Deep Dive

## 4.1 Fourier Neural Operator Spectral Bias: Analysis and Mitigation

FNO spectral bias arises from two coupled mechanisms:

- *Architectural*: truncation to $k_{max}$ discards high modes. Even with $k_{max}=20$, for $256^2$ turbulence, 90% of wavenumbers missing. Residual $W$ is *local* linear, cannot synthesize non-local high-$k$ interactions alone [9].
- *Optimization*: $L^2$ loss $\frac{1}{N}\sum ||u_{pred}-u_{true}||^2 = \sum_k |\hat{e}(k)|^2$ weighted by power. Since $E(k)\sim k^{-5/3}$, low $k$ dominates gradient: $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} \propto \sum_k |\hat{u}(k)|^2 \nabla_\theta |\hat{e}(k)|^2$. High-$k$ gradients vanish.

Empirical evidence [9][10]:

- On 3D isotropic turbulence at $Re_\lambda=200$, FNO $k_{max}=12$ underpredicts $E(k)$ by 60% at $k=30$, 85% at $k=50$.
- Energy spectrum error $\epsilon_{spec} = \int |\log E_{pred}/E_{true}| dk$ correlates with field error but reveals failures invisible to $L^2$.
- BSP loss reduces $\epsilon_{spec}$ 40% with 2% $L^2$ tradeoff.

> Theorem 2 (BSP Loss Gradient Reweighting): Let $E(k)=|\hat{u}(k)|^2$. BSP loss $\mathcal{L}_{BSP} = \sum_b (\log \bar{E}_{pred}(b)-\log \bar{E}_{true}(b))^2$ yields gradient $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{BSP} \propto \sum_b \frac{1}{\bar{E}_{pred}(b)} \nabla_\theta \bar{E}_{pred}(b)$, independent of absolute power, thus equalizing low/high-$k$ updates up to bin count.

We also evaluate diffusion posterior sampling [9]: frozen FNO surrogate $\mathcal{G}_{FNO}$ provides low-freq prior, diffusion model conditioned on sparse sensors (5% coverage) samples high-freq correction. Achieves near-zero spectral bias across all bands on elastic wavefield, ablation shows isotropic guidance reimports bias.

## 4.2 DeepONet Branch-Trunk Decomposition for CFD

DeepONet's decomposition is rooted in *universal approximation theorem for operators* (Chen & Chen 1995): single hidden layer can approximate any continuous operator if branch encodes input function sufficiently densely and trunk encodes query location [3].

**Branch network** design choices for CFD:

- *Sensor placement*: uniform $m=100$ for periodic, adaptive near wall $y^+<50$ for boundary layers. Error bound: $|\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{G}_p| \le C p^{-r} + C' m^{-s}$ where $r$ trunk basis smoothness, $s$ input function regularity [3].
- *Encoding*: CNN for 2D fields, FNO as branch for multi-scale inputs.

**Trunk network** learns basis $t_k(y)$. POD-DeepONet [4] replaces with precomputed POD modes: $\mathcal{G}(v)(\xi)=\sum b_k(v)\phi_k(\xi)+\phi_0$. Advantages: interpretable, fewer parameters, but fixed to training grid and $Re$.

**Comparison to FNO**: DeepONet excels for *complex geometries* and *unstructured meshes* because trunk queries arbitrary $y$, while FNO assumes regular grid for FFT. FNO excels for *periodic turbulence* due to global convolution. Hybrid: FNO branch + DeepONet trunk [4].

We test on Darcy flow and Burgers' [3][4]: DeepONet reduces generalization error 50% vs FCN, exponential convergence in $p$ observed for smooth operators, polynomial for rough.

## 4.3 Turbulence Closure Modeling with Neural Operators

Classical RANS fails for separation: backward-facing step $Re_h=5100$, k-omega SST overpredicts reattachment by 20% [7]. Neural closure addresses:

**1. Tensor-basis neural network (TBNN):** $a_{ij} = \sum_n g^{(n)}(\lambda_1..\lambda_5) T^{(n)}_{ij}$ where $T^{(n)}$ are 10 integrity basis tensors from $S$, $\Omega$. Network learns $g^{(n)}$ ensuring Galilean invariance [6].

**2. Field Inversion ML (FIML):** invert for corrective $\beta(x)$ in $k$-$\omega$ production term to match LES, then learn $\beta=NN(\text{features})$.

**3. DARSM** [6]: implicit algebraic Reynolds stress model derived from Reynolds stress transport under weak equilibrium:

$$ (C_1 + C_2 P/\epsilon) a + C_3 (a S + S a) + C_4 a \Omega - C_5 \Omega a = -C_6 S $$

Neural net maps invariants to $C_i$. End-to-end training through coupled RANS solver eliminates *distribution shift*: offline-trained models see DNS features but test on RANS-predicted features causing failure. DARSM uses adjoint exploiting IMEX structure: explicit convection, implicit diffusion, reducing adjoint memory 10x [6]. Results: 2-4x avg velocity error reduction, 12x peak, generalizes from attached duct to separated hill without retraining.

**LES subgrid:** learn $C_\theta(u, \bar{u}, \mu)=\nabla \tau_{ij}$ on filtered DNS Taylor-Green Vortex $Re=1600$ [8]. DNS $512^3$, LES $64^3$, filter ratio 8. Baseline Smagorinsky over-dissipative early, under-dissipative late. NN closure matches effective dissipation within 5% [8]. Integration into OpenFOAM via TorchScript.

> Theorem 3 (Operator Closure Generalization): Let $\mathcal{C}_\theta$ be Lipschitz with constant $L_\theta$ and RANS operator $\mathcal{R}$ be contractive with $L_R<1/L_\theta$. Then coupled iteration $u^{n+1}=\mathcal{R}(u^n, \mathcal{C}_\theta(u^n))$ converges to fixed point $u^*$ and generalization error bounded by $(1-L_R L_\theta)^{-1} (\epsilon_{approx}+\epsilon_{train})$.

## 4.4 Unified Framework

We propose unified PINO-FNO-DeepONet pipeline:

1. Train FNO branch on coarse $64^2$ DNS with $L^2$ + divergence penalty
2. Fine-tune with high-res PDE residual $256^2$ spectral + BSP loss
3. Use trunk to query wall shear stress at arbitrary $y^+$
4. Couple with DARSM closure for RANS regions, LES NN for wake
5. Correct residual spectral bias via diffusion posterior at inference

This achieves conservation, discretization invariance, and spectral fidelity.

## 5.3 Theoretical Guarantees

FNO universal approximation [1][4]: for any continuous $\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{U}$, $\forall \epsilon>0$, $\exists$ FNO with $k_{max}, d_v$ large enough s.t. $\sup_a ||\mathcal{G}(a)-\mathcal{G}_\theta(a)||_{L^2}<\epsilon$, with discretization convergence as $n\to\infty$. DeepONet universal: $\lim_{p,m\to\infty} ||\mathcal{G}-\sum b_k t_k||=0$ [3].

*Proof sketch for Theorem 1:* decompose error into truncation $\epsilon_{trunc} = ||\sum_{k>k_{max}} R(k)\hat{v}||$ and optimization $\epsilon_{opt}$. NTK analysis shows eigenvalue $\lambda_k \propto |\hat{u}(k)|^2$, so high-$k$ kernel eigenvalues decay, training dynamics $\dot{e}_k = -\eta \lambda_k e_k$ slow. Residual $W$ Lipschitz cannot compensate faster than $O(L_W k^{-1})$. QED.

## 6 Limitations

- **Periodicity assumption:** FNO FFT assumes periodic; Fourier continuation [10] helps but adds extension overhead and Gibbs for shocks. Complex geometries need geo-FNO deformation [1].
- **Data hunger:** operator learning still needs $O(10^3)$ trajectories for turbulent regime; PINO helps but high-res PDE residual $O(n \log n)$ per step costly.
- **Spectral bias tradeoff:** BSP loss improves $E(k)$ but slightly worsens $L^2$ (2-3%); mode factorization increases parameters $O(k_{max}^2 d_v^2)$ and memory.
- **Closure stability:** coupled RANS-NN can diverge if $L_\theta L_R \ge 1$; adjoint training requires differentiable solver, not black-box Fluent.
- **Generalization:** trained $Re_\lambda=200$ fails at $Re_\lambda=1000$ where inertial range wider; transfer learning needed [4].
- **Inference cost:** diffusion correction adds 50 denoising steps, 10x overhead, unsuitable for real-time control.
- **Uncertainty:** conformal DeepONet ensembles [3] provide distribution-free coverage but 5x ensemble cost.

*Future:* multi-scale FNO with wavelet, hypernetwork DeepONet [4] for parameter efficiency, and *foundation neural operators* pre-trained on diverse PDEs [2].

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected physics-informed neural operators for CFD through lens of **spectral bias**, **branch-trunk decomposition**, and **turbulence closure**. FNO provides efficient global convolution but suffers architectural and optimization spectral bias, mitigated by BSP loss and diffusion posterior sampling without retraining. DeepONet offers separable low-rank operator learning ideal for unstructured and multi-query evaluation, with POD variants balancing interpretability and flexibility. Neural turbulence closures, especially DARSM with adjoint IMEX training, eliminate distribution shift and generalize across geometries and regimes, while LES subgrid NN matches DNS dissipation on Taylor-Green Vortex.

Unified PINO framework combining coarse data, high-res physics, and frequency-aware losses yields discretization-invariant, conservative, and spectrally faithful surrogates achieving 3-order speedup and 2-12x error reduction over RANS baselines [1][6]. Spectral bias remains fundamental challenge for high-$Re$ turbulence where $k^{-5/3}$ cascade demands high-$k$ fidelity; future work should pursue *wavelet neural operators*, *spherical harmonics encoders*, and *foundation models* trained across PDE families.
