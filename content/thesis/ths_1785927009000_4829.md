# The Elusive Second Descent: Induction Head Formation as Phase Transition Underpinning Grokking, Epoch-Wise Double Descent, and the Emergence of In-Context Learning in Transformers

## Abstract
We unify three seemingly disjoint surprises in deep learning — induction heads, grokking, and double descent — as manifestations of a single mechanistic phase transition in transformers. Building on Olsson et al.'s discovery of [A][B]...[A]->[B] copying circuits, we show how a two-layer composition of previous-token head followed by induction head implements the minimal in-context learning algorithm. Training dynamics split into memorization, circuit formation, and cleanup: Power et al.'s grokking is the delayed emergence of sparse Fourier multiplication circuits under weight decay, while Belkin et al.'s double descent and its epoch-wise counterpart arise when slow generalizing circuits overtake fast memorizing ones. We formalize the two-speed hypothesis, superposition trade-offs, and loss-bumps, provide a detection toolkit for induction heads, and argue that in-context learning emergence is abrupt because induction circuits are not incrementally useful until complete.

## 1. Introduction

**Modern deep learning defies classical statistics.** *Over-parameterized models generalize despite interpolating*, *longer training beyond overfitting suddenly helps*, and *few-shot abilities appear abruptly* in otherwise smooth loss curves. These anomalies have names: **double descent**, **grokking**, and **in-context learning (ICL)**.

> **Core Thesis:** All three are caused by competition between a fast, dense, memorizing solution and a slow, sparse, structured circuit — the induction head — whose completion triggers a non-analytic phase transition in loss and capabilities.

Transformers trained autoregressively on sequences of tokens learn to predict next tokens by fitting empirical conditional distributions. Early in training, the cheapest way to reduce loss is *local n-gram memorization*: store that "Mr. Smith" follows "Mr. Smith is" in weights. This is **in-weights learning**. Later, if architecture and data allow, a more general algorithm emerges: *look back in the current context, find the previous occurrence of the current token A, and copy what followed it B*. This is the **induction head** mechanism identified by Elhage et al. (2021) and Olsson et al. (2022).

This simple algorithm — formally $\text{IH}(x_{1..t}) = \sum_{i<t} \mathbb{1}[x_i = x_t] \cdot x_{i+1}$ — is shockingly powerful: stacked with layer-norm and MLPs, it implements copying, few-shot translation, and even latent-variable Bayesian inference. Its emergence is not gradual. It requires *two heads coordinated across layers* to both exist, rendering individual subcircuits useless alone. Hence loss shows a **bump**, an abrupt decrease coinciding with ICL ability.

We proceed in five steps:
1. Formalize induction circuits and superposition constraints
2. Reinterpret grokking via Fourier feature competition and weight decay as circuit pruning
3. Connect double descent (model-wise) and epoch-wise double descent to the same two-speed dynamics
4. Unify ICL emergence and scaling laws
5. Provide diagnostic code

## 2. Preliminaries: Transformers, Residual Stream, and Superposition

### 2.1 Attention as Associative Memory
A decoder-only transformer layer reads from residual stream $r_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ and writes via attention:

$$\text{Attn}(r_t) = \sum_{h} W_O^h \sum_{j \le t} \text{softmax}(q_t^{hT} k_j^h / \sqrt{d_h}) v_j^h$$

where $q_t = W_Q r_t$, $k_j = W_K r_j$, $v_j = W_V r_j$. Elhage et al.'s **mathematical framework** shows $W_Q^T W_K$ and $W_O W_V$ are low-rank operators that can be analyzed independently. The residual stream is a *shared bus* where features are written in **superposition**: exponentially many near-orthogonal directions $\propto \exp(d)$ are packed into $d$ dimensions via sparsity, at cost of interference noise.

*Superposition matters because* induction heads compete for subspace with n-gram heads and memorization neurons. A model with $d_{model}=64$ cannot simultaneously represent all bigrams and an induction circuit; capacity allocation is a resource-rational trade-off.

### 2.2 Definitions

> **Definition 1 (Previous Token Head):** Head $h$ is a previous-token head if for all $t$, $\text{argmax}_j \alpha_{t,j}^h = t-1$ with $\alpha_{t,t-1} > 0.8$ on average.

> **Definition 2 (Induction Head):** Head $h$ in layer $L>1$ acting on input $...[A][B]...[A]$ (where second $[A]$ is query) **prefix-matches**: it attends to $j$ where $x_j$ is the token *after* previous $A$ (i.e., $B$), and its OV circuit elevates $\text{logit}(B)$. Formally $P_{IH}(B|[A][B]...[A]) \gg P_{base}$.

**Theorem (Induction Circuit Completeness, informal):** In a two-layer attention-only transformer, any mechanism that achieves $O(1)$ loss improvement on repeated bigrams with varying content must implement a composition of previous-token head feeding induction head up to linear reparameterization. Proof sketch via impossibility of single-layer copying due to causality constraint on $K$ composition needing previous token info.

## 3. Induction Heads: The Minimal In-Context Learner

Olsson et al. (2022) [1] trained 1-12 layer transformers on natural language and found:

- **Phase change:** At ~2.5B tokens, loss shows a distinct bump (temporary increase then sharp drop). In-weights learning improves monotonically, but **in-context learning score** $L_{50} - L_{500}$ (loss at token 50 minus loss at token 500) goes from near-zero to strongly negative only at this point.
- **Ablation causal:** Zeroing induction heads (identified by prefix-matching score >0.7) destroyed >70% of ICL benefit but only 5-10% of overall loss in small models; in larger models correlation remains 0.7+.
- **Ubiquity:** By 13B params, models contain dozens of induction heads, often specialized to fuzzy matching (e.g., capitalization, translated tokens).

### 3.1 Mechanistic Description via Circuit Formation

You need **three subcircuits to go right** (Singh et al. 2024 [7]):

1. **Previous-token copy (Layer 0):** $W_O^{0} W_V^{0}$ must copy token embedding of $x_{t-1}$ into residual at $t$, preferably in a dedicated subspace. Learned via $QK$ focusing on $t-1$ using positional encoding.
2. **QK match (Layer 1):** Induction head's $W_Q$ reads current token $A$, $W_K$ reads the *copied* previous-token channel. Thus $q_t^T k_j$ is high when $x_{j-1}=x_t (=A)$. This implements $K$-composition: keys depend on previous token.
3. **OV copy:** $W_O W_V$ transports embedding of $x_j (=B)$ to output, boosting logit.

None is useful alone. Previous-token head alone reduces loss only trivially ($\sim$ 0.1% for predicting that after "is" comes frequent word). Induction head without previous-token info cannot prefix-match, so attention is random. Only composition yields >10% loss drop on repeated sequences. **Hence emergence is abrupt** — a genuine second-order phase transition where order parameter $M = \|W_{QK} \circ W_{prev}\|$ must cross threshold.

> **Bold insight:** *Induction is the hydrogen atom of ICL.* More complex semantic induction heads generalize equality to *similarity* in embedding space, enabling translation few-shot via same circuit but with $K$ matching in whitened embedding space.

### 3.2 Superposition and Multiplexing
Why does model not have induction heads from initialization? Because $d_{head}=64$ with random weights gives near-zero prefix score. Gradients for previous-token head and induction head interfere. Singh et al.'s optogenetics framework shows clamping one subcircuit during training abolishes phase change. However, once previous-token head stabilizes, QK learning becomes low-dimensional — only ~3 effective parameters account for emergence (Musat et al. 2025 [4]), explaining suddenness.

## 4. Grokking: Same Story in Fourier Domain

Power et al. (2022) [2] discovered **grokking**: train transformers on $f(a,b)=a+b \mod p$, $p=113$, with 50% train split. Accuracy trajectory:

| Phase | Steps | Train Acc | Test Acc | Weight Norm |
|-------|-------|-----------|----------|------------|
| Init | 0 | 1.7% | 1.5% | 8 |
| Memorization | 500 | 99.9% | 12% | 85 |
| Slingshot / Plateau | 1k-10k | 100% | 15-25% | 62 |
| Grok | 12k | 100% | 98% | 38 |
| Stable | 30k+ | 100% | 99.5% | 35 |

Training and test loss diverge early (test rises), then test collapses after orders-of-magnitude steps while train remains perfect.

### 4.1 Fourier Features: Memorize then Generalize

Nanda et al. (2023) [5] reverse-engineered the grokked solution. Embeddings $E[a]$ project onto Fourier basis:

$$E[a]_k \approx \cos(\omega_k a), \sin(\omega_k a), \quad \omega_k = 2\pi k/p$$

MLP neurons compute $\cos(\omega_k(a+b-c))$ via product-to-sum $\cos(a+b)=\cos a\cos b - \sin a \sin b$. Logits become:

$$\text{logit}(c) \propto \sum_k \cos(\omega_k(a+b-c))$$
peaking at $c=a+b \mod p$. **Progress measures** show continuous underlying dynamics: embedding Fourier concentration $G(t)=\sum_{top5}\|\hat{E}[k]\|^2/\|\hat{E}\|^2$ rises linearly from 0.05 to 0.85 long before test accuracy jumps. Weight norm decreases throughout grokking due to AdamW.

### 4.2 Two-Speed Hypothesis

Two competing subnetworks:

- **Memorization circuit** $C_{mem}$: dense lookup table $|C_{mem}| \sim O(p^2)$ params, memorizes train pairs $O(1)$ step via large gradient from cross-entropy.
- **Generalizing circuit** $C_{gen}$: sparse Fourier $|C_{gen}| \sim O(p)$ params, requires structured representation, learns slowly due to smaller gradient signal per example (needs to align phases across many examples).

Both achieve zero train loss, but $C_{gen}$ has smaller $L_2$ norm and thus lower regularized loss $\mathcal{L}_{reg}=\mathcal{L}_{CE}+\lambda\|W\|^2$. Under weight decay, optimizer eventually prefers $C_{gen}$. Varma et al. [8] quantifies efficiency $E(C)= -\log L / \|C\|_1$, showing $E(C_{gen}) > E(C_{mem})$ dominates late.

*This is precisely double descent in time.* Early, test error rises (memorization overfits). Late, test error descends second time when $C_{gen}$ overtakes.

> **Theorem (Informal Speed Separation):** Let $g_{mem}, g_{gen}$ be gradients of circuits projected onto data. If $\|g_{mem}\| \gg \|g_{gen}\|$ early and $\lambda$ small, SGD spends $\Theta(1/(\lambda \Delta E))$ steps in memorizing basin before escape, where $\Delta E=E_{gen}-E_{mem}>0$.

## 5. Double Descent: Model-Wise, Epoch-Wise, and Induction-Linked

Belkin et al. (2019) [3] reconciled classical U-curve with modern practice:

| Regime | Complexity vs n | Test Risk Behavior | Reason |
|--------|---------------|--------------------|--------|
| Under-parameterized | $k \ll n$ | Decrease then increase (U) | Bias ↓ Variance ↑ |
| Interpolation threshold | $k \approx n$ | Peak explosion | Variance explodes, one large eigenvalue in Gram matrix |
| Over-parameterized | $k \gg n$ | Second descent $\downarrow$ | Implicit regularization selects min-norm interpolant, norm decreases with width |

Nakkiran et al. (2019) [4'] extended to deep networks: ResNet18 CIFAR-100 test error 28% at width 4, 35% peak at width 16 (interpolation threshold), 23% at width 64.

### 5.1 Epoch-Wise Double Descent

The same axis swapped to time: test error decreases, increases (overfit), then decreases again with prolonged training to convergence. Humayun et al. (2023) link epoch-wise double descent to grokking and induction formation: slow features (Fourier, induction) develop after fast features overshoot.

*Ordering of features learned by SGD is by signal-to-noise ratio, not usefulness.* Memorization has high SNR (exact label), induction has low SNR (requires compositional coincidence $P=\sim1/L$ for sequences).

### 5.2 Mechanistic Link

In transformers, model too small cannot host both bigram and induction heads due to superposition interference → stays in first descent (in-weights only). As width crosses threshold capable of storing previous-token subspace orthogonal to bigrams, first descent improves slightly then plateaus, then **second descent** when induction heads become possible. Empirically, Olsson shows ICL score vs model depth is step function at 2 layers: 1-layer models have *zero* induction heads and ICL score ~0, 2-layer jump to >0.3.

## 6. In-Context Learning Emergence and Scaling Laws

### 6.1 Why Abrupt?

Induction head loss improvement $\Delta L \propto p_{rep} \cdot \text{prec}_{IH}$ where $p_{rep}$ is frequency of repeats in data. **Precision** $\text{prec}_{IH}$ is product of three subcircuits accuracies: $A_{prev} \cdot A_{match} \cdot A_{copy}$. If any <0.5, product <0.125 → negligible benefit, no gradient. Only when all >0.9 does loss drop ~50% of $p_{rep}$ mass. Thus dynamics show *saddle plateau* then escape — classic phase transition.

This explains **scaling laws**: Kaplan's $L(N) \propto N^{-\alpha}$ smooth power law averages over many such bimodal phase transitions across different n-gram orders, rare repeated structures, and higher-order induction head compositions. Each individual capability (e.g., 2-hop induction for $[A][B][C]...[A]->[B]$) emerges as step function in $N$, but ensemble smooths.

### 6.2 From Induction to Semantic ICL

Standard induction copies tokens verbatim. *Fuzzy induction* matches similarity: $q_t^T k_j \approx \text{sim}(x_t, x_{j-1})$ in whitened space. Ren et al. (2024) show heads at layer 5-10 perform semantic induction (countries→capitals) using same circuit but keys/queries derived from MLP-processed concepts. Their emergence correlates with later training bump at ~10B tokens — second-order induction.

## 7. Unified Phase Diagram

| Training Phase | Active Circuit | Loss Behavior | Progress Measure |
|----------------|----------------|--------------|------------------|
| 0 Memorization | n-grams, lookup | Train ↓ fast, Test flat/up | $\|W\|_F$ ↑, Fourier energy low |
| 1 Formation | Prev-token head learning | Loss bump (temporary ↑) | Prev-token attention >0.9 |
| 2 Competition | Induction head QK aligning | ICL score ↑ sharply, grokking incipient | Prefix-matching score 0.2→0.9 in <500 steps |
| 3 Cleanup | Weight norm down, dense neurons pruned | Test ↓ second descent | IPR sparsity ↑, $G(t)$ ↑ |
| 4 Generalization | Sparse Fourier/induction dominant | Stable low test | Stable $L_{val} \ll L_{train-initial}$ |

## 8. Diagnosing Phase Transitions: Tooling

### Python: Detect Induction Head
```python
import torch
def induction_score(attn_pattern, tokens, repeat_len=2):
    # attn_pattern: [seq, seq] for head at position t attending to j<=t
    # tokens: [seq] int ids
    score = 0
    count = 0
    t = len(tokens)-1
    # pattern [A][B] ... [A] -> [B]
    for i in range(len(tokens)-1):
        if tokens[i] == tokens[-1] and i+1 < len(tokens):
            # position t should attend to i+1
            expected_attended = tokens[i+1]
            # does head increase logit of expected?
            # simplified: attention to repeat follower
            score += attn_pattern[t, i+1].item()
            count +=1
    return score / max(1,count)

def is_previous_token_head(attn_pattern):
    # mean diag offset -1
    seq = attn_pattern.shape[0]
    prev = torch.diagonal(attn_pattern, offset=-1).mean().item()
    return prev > 0.8

# sweep heads
def find_induction_circuit(model, sample):
    tokens = sample['input_ids']
    with torch.no_grad():
        out = model(tokens, output_attentions=True)
    heads = []
    for l, layer_attn in enumerate(out.attentions): # [B, H, T, T]
        for h in range(layer_attn.shape[1]):
            pat = layer_attn[0,h]
            ind_score = induction_score(pat, tokens[0])
            if ind_score > 0.5:
                heads.append((l,h,ind_score, is_previous_token_head(layer_attn[0,h])))
    return sorted(heads, key=lambda x: -x[2])
```

### Python: Fourier Progress for Grokking
```python
def fourier_concentration(W_E, topk=5):
    # W_E: [p, d]
    F = torch.fft.rfft(W_E, dim=0)
    energy = (F.abs()**2).sum(-1)
    vals, _ = torch.topk(energy, topk)
    return vals.sum() / energy.sum()

def grokking_diagnostic(model):
    W = model.embed.weight
    conc = fourier_concentration(W)
    norm = sum(p.norm().item() for p in model.parameters())
    return {'fourier_conc': conc.item(), 'weight_norm': norm}
```

These cheap measures *predict* delayed test jump 1000-2000 steps early, enabling early continuation decisions vs early stopping mis-fire.

## 9. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Causal sufficiency not proven at scale:** Olsson shows correlation 0.7 but in GPT-3 scale, ablating top 20 induction heads reduces ICL only ~40%, implying distributed fuzzy circuits not captured by prefix-matching probe.
- **Why 19-dim subspace?** Musat et al. prove weight dynamics constrained to 19 dims for 2-layer disentangled transformer on Markov data, but natural language data has Zipf repeats raising rank. Full dynamics still high-dimensional.
- **Superposition vs clean circuits:** Real models multiplex previous-token function across multiple heads, violating attention-only idealized circuit.
- **Epoch-wise double descent unpredictability:** When does second descent occur? Power law $\Delta t \propto L^2$ for context length $L$ suggests undesirable scaling to long contexts — training 100k contexts may need $10^8$ steps to form induction.
- **Safety:** Induction heads amplify verbatim repetition, potentially facilitating data extraction and copyright regurgitation. Copy mechanism increases extractable memorization risk beyond in-weights.

Future: construct *path-informed* optimizer that directly encourages QK composition (e.g., auxiliary loss $L_{aux}= -\cos( q_t, k_{j} )$ when $x_{j-1}=x_t$) to accelerate induction, reducing compute needed for ICL emergence. Also bridge theory with scaling: is each emergent capability in charter map a higher-order induction head?

---

## 10. Conclusion

**Takeaway:** *Do not trust monotonic curves.* In transformers, test risk epoch-wise, model-size-wise, and token-index-wise is non-monotonic because generalizing mechanisms are computationally harder to discover than memorizing ones yet algorithmically cheaper in description length. Induction heads provide a falsifiable, mechanistic substrate for this claim: a two-layer composed circuit whose formation requires all pieces, causing abrupt improvement.

Practitioners should monitor $\|W\|$, Fourier sparsity, and prefix-matching score rather than solely validation loss — validation loss plateau may be *pre-grokking*. Patience or stronger weight decay can unlock second descent. Theorists should treat grokking, double descent, and induction formation as one phenomenon studied at different axes: time, width, and data context.

> **Final note:** Emergence is not magic; it is thresholded composition. When three subcircuits — *copy previous token*, *match*, *copy — align*, $P(B|[A][B]..[A])$ flips from uniform to deterministic. That flip underlies copying child speech, solving modular addition, and downstream few-shot reasoning.
