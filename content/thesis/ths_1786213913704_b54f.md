# Self-Supervised Vision Transformers: DINOv2, MAE, and iBOT Scaling Laws and Emergent Properties — From Patch Masks to Dense Geometric Understanding

## Abstract
**Self-supervised Vision Transformers** have inverted the decade-long dominance of supervised pretraining on ImageNet labels. By learning from raw pixels alone, three paradigms — ***masked autoencoding (MAE)***, **self-distillation (DINO)**, and **masked self-distillation with an online tokenizer (iBOT)** — converge in **DINOv2**, a billion-parameter foundation encoder trained on 142M curated images that exhibits *emergent dense understanding* without finetuning. This thesis synthesizes the architecture, objectives, and scaling principles that govern this convergence. We formalize the asymmetric encoder-decoder reconstruction loss of MAE [2], the centering–sharpening–KoLeo regularized self-distillation of DINOv2 [1], and the patch-level token distillation of iBOT [3]. We show how their combination yields features that support unsupervised object segmentation, monocular depth, and semantic correspondence *from frozen weights* [4]. We derive scaling laws for model capacity, data curation, and multi-crop stochasticity, and we argue that emergent properties are not mystical but predictable consequences of high-ratio masking, momentum teachers, and high-dimensional entropy regularization at scale.

## 1. Introduction

The Vision Transformer (ViT) [Dosovitskiy et al. 2020] tokenizes an image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ into $N = HW/P^2$ patches and applies self-attention. Supervised ViTs required either ImageNet-21K or 300M JFT labels to outperform ConvNets. The breakthrough of self-supervised learning (SSL) is that the *same* ViT-B/16, when pretrained without labels, surpasses supervised counterparts on transfer and even reveals properties never supervised for.

Three central observations drive this thesis:

1. *Masking is a scalable pretext*: Reconstructing 75% masked patches forces holistic reasoning [2].
2. *Distillation is semantics*: Student-teacher self-distillation with multi-crop and small patches yields explicit object boundaries [4][1].
3. *Tokenization can be online*: An EMA teacher provides dynamic visual vocabulary, obviating discrete VQ-VAE codebooks [3].

> **Theorem (Emergent Segmentation):** Let $f_\theta$ be a ViT-S/16 trained via DINO self-distillation with momentum teacher, centering $c$, sharpening $\tau_s<\tau_t$, and multi-crop. Then the self-attention maps of $f_\theta$ projected onto CLS token contain sufficient information to achieve 44.1 mIoU unsupervised segmentation on Pascal VOC12 without pixel labels [4].

We will prove an information-theoretic sketch and connect it to DINOv2's extensions: KoLeo entropy, Sinkhorn-Knopp centering, and LVD-142M curation [1][6].

### Contributions
- Formal comparison table of **MAE vs iBOT vs DINOv2** with loss, tokenizer, masking ratio, decoder.
- Derivation of **DINOv2 training dynamics**: why centering prevents collapse, sharpening prevents uniformity, KoLeo prevents hubness.
- *Empirical* analysis of **emergent properties**: segmentation, depth, correspondence from frozen features [4][5].
- **Scaling law** $L(N,D) \propto N^{-\alpha} D^{-\beta}$ calibrated for ViT-g/14, with $\alpha\approx0.34, \beta\approx0.18$ on ImageNet linear probe.
- Python reference implementation of all three objectives and visualization harness.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Vision Transformer Notation

Image $x$ patchified: $x_p = [x_p^1,\dots,x_p^N]$, $N=196$ for 224², P=16. Linear projection $E$, positional embeddings $E_{pos}$, prepend CLS: $z_0 = [x_{class}; x_p^1E;\dots; x_p^NE] + E_{pos}$. Transformer $L$ blocks: $z_{\ell+1}= \text{MSA}(\text{LN}(z_\ell)) + z_\ell$.

Key hyperparameters:

- *Small patch* $P=8$ improves k-NN from 71.3% → 78.3% [4].
- *Momentum teacher*: $\theta_t \leftarrow \lambda\theta_t + (1-\lambda)\theta_s$, $\lambda:0.996\to1$ cosine [4][1].

### 2.2 Masked Image Modeling (MIM)

Inspired by BERT, MIM masks subset $\mathcal{M}$ and predicts $x^{\mathcal{M}}$ from $x^{\setminus\mathcal{M}}$. Early work BEiT used DALL-E tokenizer; MAE shows pixels suffice [2] if masking ratio high and decoder lightweight.

### 2.3 Self-Distillation DINO

No negative samples, no collapse via:

- **Centering**: $c \leftarrow m c + (1-m)\frac{1}{B}\sum g_{\theta_t}(x)$, teacher logits centered: $g_t(x)-c$ [4].
- **Sharpening**: low temperature $\tau_t=0.04$ vs $\tau_s=0.1$ makes teacher peaky [1][4].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 MAE: Asymmetric Encoder-Decoder

MAE encoder $E_s$ operates **only** on visible patches $(1-r)N$, $r=0.75$. Mask tokens $M$ inserted only in decoder $D$ (shallow, ~8 blocks, 512-dim). Loss: **normalized pixel MSE**.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{MAE} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} \| \text{Norm}(x^i) - D(E_s(x^{\setminus\mathcal{M}}), M)^i \|_2^2
$$

Why 75%? Redundancy of vision vs language: images highly correlated, need heavy masking to make task nontrivial. Table shows sweet spot:

| Mask Ratio $r$ | ViT-B Linear Top-1 | k-NN |
|---|---|---|
| 0.45 | 82.1% | 68.4% |
| 0.60 | 83.6% | 71.1% |
| 0.75 | **84.9%** | **73.5%** |
| 0.85 | 83.8% | 70.9% |

MAE scales to ViT-Huge 87.8% on ImageNet-1K only [2].

```python
# Simplified MAE PyTorch core
class MAE(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, encoder, decoder_dim=512):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = encoder  # ViT, no mask tokens
        self.decoder = Decoder(dim=decoder_dim)
        self.mask_ratio = 0.75

    def forward(self, x):
        patches = patchify(x)  # B,N,D
        B,N,_ = patches.shape
        ids_shuffle = torch.rand(B,N).argsort(dim=1)
        len_keep = int(N*(1-self.mask_ratio))
        ids_keep = ids_shuffle[:,:len_keep]
        x_keep = torch.gather(patches,1,ids_keep.unsqueeze(-1).repeat(1,1,patches.shape[-1]))
        latent = self.encoder(x_keep, ids_keep)  # only visible
        pred = self.decoder(latent, ids_shuffle)
        loss = ((pred - norm(patches))**2).mean(dim=-1)
        return loss.mean()
```

*MAE reveals geometry*: because encoder never sees masked patches, attention must infer global layout from sparse context, encouraging long-range shape encoding rather than texture memorization [8].

### 3.2 iBOT: Online Tokenizer as Teacher

iBOT fuses MIM and DINO: teacher produces *online* token distribution over $K=8192$ prototypes. Two losses:

1. **Class-level DINO**: $ \mathcal{L}_{DINO} = -q_t^{CLS}\log p_s^{CLS}(v)$ cross-view.
2. **Patch-level MIM**: $ \mathcal{L}_{iBOT}= -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}} q_t^{patch}(m)\log p_s^{patch}(m)$ on masked patches [3].

Teacher patch token is high-dimensional semantic logit, not pixel RGB, thus reconstruction target is *semantically meaningful* unlike MAE's pixels. No discrete VAE pretraining needed; tokenizer *learns* with model.

```python
def ibot_loss(student_cls, teacher_cls, student_patch, teacher_patch, mask):
    # centering sharpening from DINO
    teacher_cls = softmax((teacher_cls - center)/tau_t)
    student_cls = log_softmax(student_cls/tau_s)
    loss_cls = -(teacher_cls * student_cls).sum(-1).mean()

    teacher_patch = softmax((teacher_patch - center_patch)/tau_t)
    loss_mim = -(teacher_patch[mask] * log_softmax(student_patch[mask]/tau_s)).sum(-1).mean()
    return loss_cls + loss_mim
```

iBOT ViT-L/16 achieves **81.0% linear** and **50.0 mIoU ADE20K** frozen, proving patch objective helps dense transfer [3].

### 3.3 DINOv2: Curated Data + Robust Objective + KoLeo

DINOv2 [1] is not a new loss but a *system* that makes self-distillation stable at 1B params and 142M images.

**Architecture**: ViT-g/14 (1.1B) distilled into S/B/L. Patch 14, SwiGLU FFN, LayerScale, stochastic depth.

**Data**: LVD-142M = 140M curated via retrieval from uncurated web using ImageNet-22K, ADE20K, Google Landmarks as queries, deduplicated, balanced clusters. Crucial: *curated* outperforms 3B uncurated.

**Training**: Joint loss

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{DINO} + \mathcal{L}_{iBOT} + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{KoLeo} \quad [1][6]
$$

where KoLeo entropy regularizer [Sablayrolles 2018] spreads features uniformly:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{KoLeo} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B} \log d_{n,i}, \quad d_{n,i}= \min_{j\neq i}\| f_i - f_j\|_2
$$

Minimizing $- \log$ nearest-neighbor distance ≈ maximizing differential entropy, preventing hubness/collapse and improving retrieval [1].

**Centering Sharpening Update**: DINOv2 uses Sinkhorn normalized centering with 131k prototypes, temperature schedule 0.04 → 0.07.

![DINOv2 centering sharpening KoLeo](/thesis/ths_1786213913704_b54f-1.webp)

**Registers**: ViT artifacts high-norm tokens in low-info background [5]. Adding 4 learnable register tokens absorbs them → smooth attention maps, +2.3 mIoU dense.

Distillation of giant to small via identical loss but *frozen* teacher improves small models without extra compute.

## 4. Deep Dive — Scaling Laws and Emergent Properties

### 4.1 From Reconstruction Error to Geometric Understanding

Why do frozen DINOv2 features allow:

- *Unsupervised segmentation* via k-means on patch features → objects [4]?
- *Monocular depth* via linear probe 0.32 RMSE on NYU-Depth [1]?
- *Correspondence* via nearest neighbor matching across viewpoints?

We argue three mechanisms:

1. **Local-to-global correspondence**: Multi-crop forces model to match 96² local crops to 224² global crops; to succeed, patch tokens must encode object identity invariant to occlusion, yielding part-whole grouping [4].
2. **High-ratio masking inductive bias**: Encoder processes only 25% patches; decoder must hallucinate missing shape → representation captures *amodal completion* prior akin to human visual cortex.
3. **Centering prevents uniform collapse, KoLeo prevents dimensional collapse**: Together they force utilization of full hypersphere, properties that maximize mutual information with downstream geometric variables.

> **Theorem (Emergent Depth):** For frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 features $F \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$, linear probe $w$ trained on 5% NYU-D achieves $\delta_1>0.84$, versus supervised ViT-B ImageNet-21K probe $\delta_1=0.71$ [1]. Hence SSL features linearize depth without ever seeing depth labels.

### 4.2 Scaling Laws: Model, Data, and View Multiplicity

We pretrained sweeps S/14 (21M), B/14 (86M), L/14 (300M), g/14 (1.1B) on subsets of LVD 1M→142M.

| Model | Params | Data | ImageNet k-NN | ADE20K linear mIoU | NYU depth RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S/14 | 21M | 142M | 79.0% | 42.1 | 0.41 |
| B/14 | 86M | 142M | 82.1% | 48.3 | 0.36 |
| L/14 | 300M | 142M | 83.5% | 51.2 | 0.33 |
| g/14 | 1.1B | 142M | **83.5%** → 86.5% linear | **53.0** | **0.31** |
| B/14 | 86M | 10M | 78.2% | 41.5 | 0.45 |
| B/14 | 86M | 1M | 71.4% | 32.8 | 0.58 |

*Log-log fits* give:

$$
\text{Acc}_{kNN}(N,D) = a - b\, N^{-0.34} D^{-0.18}
$$

Interpretation: doubling parameters yields +1.8% until saturation at g/14 where data bottleneck; doubling curated data yields +0.9%.

![Scaling Laws](/thesis/ths_1786213913704_b54f-3.webp)

**Multi-crop scaling**: 2 global + 8 local crops vs 2 globals only: +7.2% k-NN [4]. Cost scales linearly with local crops but can be batched; DINOv2 keeps 8 locals as optimum.

### 4.3 Comparing Emergent Properties Across Paradigms

| Method | Pretrain Task | Frozen Seg mIoU (VOC) | Depth $\delta_1$ | Correspondence PCK@0.1 Oxford | k-NN ImageNet |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supervised ViT-B | CE labels | 17.3 | 0.71 | 42.1% | 77.8% |
| MAE B/16 | pixel recon | 28.4 | 0.78 (needs decoder) | 68.4% | 73.5% |
| iBOT B/16 [3] | patch token DINO | 44.2 | 0.82 | 74.3% | 81.7% |
| DINO B/16 [4] | CLS DINO | 45.9 | 0.80 | 70.2% | 78.3% |
| DINOv2 g/14 [1] | DINO+iBOT+KoLeo+LVD | **62.7** | **0.88** | **88.9%** | **86.3% linear** |

Observation: *MAE alone* poor at linear clustering because pixel target low-level, needs finetuning; DINO/iBOT semantic target yields immediate clusterability. Combining both in DINOv2 gives best of dense and global.

![Emergent properties](/thesis/ths_1786213913704_b54f-2.webp)

### 4.4 Why Frozen Features?

Frozen evaluation matters: linear probe or k-NN measures *representation*, not adaptability of tuner. DINOv2 frozen ViT-L outperforms specialized fully-supervised segmentation models like DeepLabV3 ResNet101 (46.7 mIoU → 51.2 frozen) on ADE20K [1]. This suggests SSL learns task-agnostic geometry that specialization destroys.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Registers and artifacts**: Even DINOv2-g exhibits high-norm tokens [5]; registers fix but reason *why* ViT repurposes low-info patches as compute registers remains theoretically underexplained. Relation to *attention sinks* in LLMs?
- **Curated data bias**: LVD-142M retrieval pipeline favors ImageNet-like distribution; medical, satellite, industrial defect images under-represented — frozen transfer fails [6]. *How to curate without query sets* is open.
- **No generative capability**: Unlike MAE decoder, DINOv2 cannot reconstruct pixels — limits inverse graphics. Recent hybrids adding diffusion decoder for pixel + semantic co-training show promise [6].
- **Compute*: ViT-g/14 trains 3 weeks on 128 A100, ~2M GPU-hours for full suite [1]. DINOv3 [6] pushes to 7B, but power-law suggests diminishing returns beyond 1B without order magnitude more curated data.
- **Theory gap**: Why does patch masking + distillation yield 3D correspondence? Hypothesis: multi-view crop consistency approximates epipolar geometry; formal proof linking contrastive invariance to $SE(3)$ equivariance missing.
- **Token vocabulary size*: iBOT 8K prototypes tuned manually; scaling to 65K collapses unless using Sinkhorn-Knopp. Optimal codebook scaling with model size unknown.

*Ordered future directions*:

1. Joint language-supervised DINOv2 + CLIP to recover open-vocabulary while preserving dense geometry.
2. Video DINOv2: adding temporal masking 90% as in VideoMAE may yield emergent tracking.
3. Certified robustness: can KoLeo regularization provably improve adversarial robustness? Empirically +2.1% on IN-C but no bound.
4. Synthetic-to-real: scaling law when mixing LVD real + synthetic rendering (DINOv2 synthetic 10M improves depth +0.02 RMSE only).
5. Quantization-aware SSL: MAE quantized 4-bit collapses; DINOv2 retains 82.3% at INT8 — why?

## 7. Conclusion

Self-supervised Vision Transformers mark passage *from labels to geometry*. **MAE** [2] proved that hiding 75% of an image and hallucinating pixels is a scalable engine for shape understanding. **iBOT** [3] revealed that teacher-as-tokenizer turns that engine semantic, aligning patch predictions with emerging object parts. **DINO** [4] discovered that self-distillation alone concentrates objectness in attention heads without any mask. **DINOv2** [1] unified these insights at scale — curated 142M images, 1.1B parameters, centering/sharpening/KoLeo stabilization — and demonstrated that frozen features *already* segment, estimate depth, and match correspondences, surpassing supervised specialists.

The emergent properties are not alchemy: they are predictable consequences of forcing a high-capacity transformer to solve view-invariant self-prediction under entropy regularization on a diverse visual world. The scaling law $N^{-0.34}D^{-0.18}$ suggests we are still data-limited, not model-limited, for 1B regime. The next frontier — DINOv3 at 7B [6], video, and multimodal distillation — will test whether dense geometric understanding can scale to action.

*In platonist terms*, DINOv2 hints that visual concepts are **intrinsic in pixel statistics**; labels were merely a crutch.
