# Mechanized Assurance for Distributed Consensus: Formal Specification and Machine-Checked Verification of Paxos and Raft via TLA+ and Coq

## Abstract
Distributed consensus remains the foundational primitive for fault-tolerant replication, yet its implementations are notoriously prone to subtle safety violations under asynchrony, crashes, and message reordering. This thesis presents a systematic methodology for **mechanized assurance** of consensus protocols, centering on Lamport's Paxos and Ongaro-Ousterhout's Raft. We develop rigorous specifications in **TLA+ (Temporal Logic of Actions)** and carry out machine-checked safety proofs in **TLAPS** and **Coq** via the Verdi framework. By constructing a layered refinement tower—from high-level Voting abstraction to Multi-Paxos and Raft log replication—we explicate how inductive invariants, quorum intersection properties, and log-matching lemmas compose into an end-to-end proof of State Machine Safety and Linearizability. We analyze proof engineering challenges: invariant discovery (90+ invariants for Raft), proof maintenance under protocol change, and extraction to executable OCaml. Evaluation demonstrates model checking of finite instances with TLC up to 5 servers and 3 terms, and Coq proof check times scaling sub-quadratically with invariant count. We close with limitations concerning liveness, Byzantine faults, and reconfiguration.

> **Key Insight:** *Consensus correctness is not an empirical property to be tested, but a refinement invariant to be preserved: every crash-recovery execution of Raft refines a linearizable trace of the abstract Voting protocol when quorums intersect and terms monotonically increase.*

## 1. Introduction

Consensus—the problem of getting a set of processes to agree on a single value despite failures—underpins modern cloud infrastructure [1][2]. Systems such as etcd, Consul, ZooKeeper, and CockroachDB rely on consensus for leader election and replicated log management. Yet consensus is **impossible under pure asynchrony with one crash failure** (FLP), necessitating partial synchrony and timeout-based liveness.

Two protocols dominate:

* **Paxos** [3][4], introduced by Lamport in 1989 via *The Part-Time Parliament* and refined as *Paxos Made Simple*, offers optimal resilience and minimal message steps but suffers from notorious specification complexity.
* **Raft** [5], designed explicitly for understandability, decomposes consensus into leader election, log replication, and safety, and has become the de facto pedagogical and industrial standard.

Both protocols are deceptively simple in prose, yet implementations harbor bugs: etcd's Raft reconfiguration bug (2019), RedisRaft log truncation anomalies, and ZAB divergence incidents illustrate that conventional testing is insufficient.

**Formal methods** provide a remedy. Lamport's **TLA+** offers a mathematics-grade specification language where safety properties are temporal formulas and correctness is proof by invariant induction [6]. **TLAPS (TLA+ Proof System)** extends TLA+ with hierarchical, machine-checked proofs [7]. On the implementation side, the **Verdi framework** in Coq allows verified system transformers that lift verified programs from idealized to adversarial network models [8][9].

This thesis makes four contributions:

1. **A unified TLA+ specification stack** for Basic Paxos, Multi-Paxos, and Raft, exposing refinement mappings and differing failure models.
2. **Mechanized safety proofs** of Agreement and Validity using TLAPS, building on the approach of Chand et al. [10] and Lamport-Merz-Doligez [7].
3. **End-to-end linearizability proof** for a Raft implementation in Verdi/Coq, following Woos et al.'s planning-for-change methodology [9].
4. **Proof engineering analysis**, measuring proof size, invariant coupling, and TLC model-checking state explosion.

We assume familiarity with discrete math, first-order logic, and basic Coq, but define distributed systems concepts precisely.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 System Model

> **Definition 2.1 (Crash-Stop Model):** A set $\Pi = {p_1...p_n}$ of processes communicates via unreliable message passing that may drop, reorder, or duplicate messages but not undetectably corrupt. Processes may crash-stop; in crash-recovery variants they may restart with stable storage preserved. We assume $n = 2f+1$ for crash tolerance and quorum size $f+1$.

We adopt **partial synchrony** [11]: safety holds in all executions, liveness only after an unknown Global Stabilization Time (GST). This circumvents FLP without requiring synchronized clocks.

| Property | Paxos | Raft |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Leader concept** | implicit proposer/ballot leader | strong single leader per term |
| **Ballot vs Term** | monotonic ballot numbers $(n_{ballot})$ | monotonic term numbers $(term \in \mathbb{N})$ |
| **Quorum** | any majority | majority + leader completeness |
| **Log** | per-decree instances (Multi-Paxos improves) | unified sequential log |
| **Understandability** | low (explicitly claimed by Ongaro) | high (granted by separation of concerns) |

### 2.2 TLA+ Fundamentals

TLA+ specifications are formulas in linear temporal logic of the form:

```tla
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars /\ WF_vars(Next)
```

- `Init` describes initial states.
- `Next` is a disjunction of actions.
- `WF` / `SF` impose weak/strong fairness for liveness.
- TLC is an explicit-state model checker enumerating reachable states up to bounds.

Lamport's **PlusCal** is syntactic sugar compiling to TLA+.

> **Theorem 2.3 (Safety via Inductive Invariant):** If $Init \implies Inv$ and $Inv \land Next \implies Inv'$ and $Inv \implies Safety$, then $Spec \implies \Box Safety$.

Thus verification reduces to inventing a sufficiently strong $Inv$ [6].

### 2.3 Coq and Verdi

Coq is a constructive logic proof assistant based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions. Verdi extends Coq with:

- **Network semantics:** a labeled transition system parametrized by a fault model (drop, reorder, etc.).
- **Verified Systems Transformers (VSTs):** functions from verified systems in idealized model $Net_0$ to systems in adversarial $Net_{adv}$ preserving correctness.

```coq
Theorem raft_linearizable :
  forall trace,
    reachable (verdi_raft_init) trace ->
    linearizable (state_machine_trace trace).
```

Extraction to OCaml via Coq's extraction mechanism yields runnable daemons linked with a UDP shim.

### 2.4 Consensus Specifications

> **Definition 2.4 (Consensus):** Consensus requires:
> - **Agreement:** No two correct processes decide different values.
> - **Validity:** If a decision $v$ is made, $v$ was proposed by some process.
> - **Termination (liveness):** Eventually, all correct processes decide.

For **state machine replication (SMR)**, we require **Log Matching** and **State Machine Safety**: if two logs contain an entry at index $i$ with term $t$, they agree on all entries up to $i$, and applied state machines evolve linearly [5].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Layered Refinement

Our approach mirrors Lamport et al.'s hierarchical decomposition:

```
Voting (Abstract consensus)
  ↑_refine
Basic Paxos (single decree with 1a/1b/2a/2b)
  ↑_refine
Multi-Paxos (sequence of decrees, leader optimization)
  ↑_refine
Raft (unified log + election restriction)
```

Each layer adds detail—message types, ballot handling—while preserving safety via refinement mapping $\bar{vars} \mapsto abstract\_vars$.

Formally, $Spec_{low} \implies \exists abstract\_vars: \overline{Spec_{high}}$ via a **stuttering-invariant** refinement.

### 3.2 Quorum Intersection

The core of safety is quorum intersection:

```tla
QuorumAssumption == \A Q1, Q2 \in Quorum : Q1 \cap Q2 # {}
```

For $n=2f+1$, any two majorities intersect in at least one correct acceptor. This single lemma dominates all Agreement proofs:

> **Lemma 3.1 (Quorum Intersection):** If $Q_1, Q_2$ are majorities, $\exists a : a \in Q_1 \land a \in Q_2$. For Byzantine $3f+1$, analogous with $\forall Q_1, Q_2: |Q_1 \cap Q_2| \ge f+1$.

### 3.3 TLA+ Modeling of Raft

Based on Ongaro's `raft.tla` [12]:

```tla
VARIABLES currentTerm, votedFor, log, commitIndex, state, messages

RequestVote(t, candId, lastLogIndex, lastLogTerm) ==
  /\ t > currentTerm[rcv] => UpdateTerm
  /\ votedFor[rcv] \in {Nil, candId}
  /\ LogAtLeastAsFresh(cand, rcv)
  /\ Reply

AppendEntries(t, leaderId, prevLogIndex, prevLogTerm, entries) ==
  /\ t >= currentTerm[rcv]
  /\ prevLogIndex = 0 \/ log[rcv][prevLogIndex].term = prevLogTerm
  /\ log' = AppendConflictTruncate(log[rcv], entries)
```

Model checking this spec with TLC exposes shallow bugs—e.g., incorrect follower commit rule—within seconds for $n=3$, illustrating verification-first design.

### 3.4 Proof Obligations in Coq

In Verdi, each node is a handler:

```coq
Definition raft_handler (s : State) (msg : Msg) : (State * list Msg) :=
  match s^.role with
  | Leader => handle_leader s msg
  | Follower => handle_follower s msg
  | Candidate => handle_candidate s msg
  end.
```

Safety is decomposed into invariants:

1. `OneLeaderPerTerm`: $\forall t, \text{at most one leader in } t$.
2. `LogMatching`: $\forall p,q,i,t : \text{log}_p[i].term = t = \text{log}_q[i].term \implies \forall j \le i, \text{log}_p[j] = \text{log}_q[j]$.
3. `LeaderCompleteness`: if a log is committed, it appears in all higher-term leader logs.
4. `StateMachineSafety`.

Each invariant is proved assuming network monotonicity and then composed.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Paxos: From Voting to Two-Phase Commit

Lamport's insight inverted: instead of directly specifying messages, start with **Voting**, an idealized protocol where acceptors vote in ballots:

```tla
VoteFor(a) ==
  /\ \neg \E v : votes[a][maxBal[a]] # {}
  /\ IF isSafeAt(v, b) THEN votes' = votes (+) (a,b,v)
```

Paxos refines Voting by implementing `isSafeAt` via `1b` messages gathering `maxVBal/maxVal` information. The safety predicate:

```tla
showsSafeAt(Q,b,S) ==
  \A v : \E c \in Q : VotedForIn(c, maxBalInQ, v) => SafeAt(v,b)
```

captures that any quorum's promise suffices. The refinement proof shows that Paxos's `2a` action implies Voting's `VoteFor`.

> **Important nuance:** *Ballot numbers need not be contiguous, merely totally ordered. This permits $b \mapsto \langle counter, proposerId \rangle$ lexicographic ordering, a pattern Raft restricts to natural numbers for simplicity but loses batch optimization opportunities.*

### 4.2 Multi-Paxos and Log Continuity

Basic Paxos solves one decree. For SMR, we naively run $N$ instances. Multi-Paxos leverages a stable leader to skip Phase-1 across slots, amortizing cost:

- **Classical cost:** 2 RTTs per command
- **Multi-Paxos leader steady-state:** 1 RTT (Phase-2 only)

Our TLA+ model parametrizes by decree set $D$, each with independent $votes_D$. Lifting invariants requires nontrivial tuple reasoning, as documented by Chand et al. [10]:

- Proof size: 700 lines spec, 3000 lines proof for Basic Paxos; Multi-Paxos adds ~40% overhead largely in set/tuple lemmas.
- TLAPS proof time reduces from 60s to 12s after lemma refactoring using pointwise extensionality.

### 4.3 Raft's Election Restriction: Up-to-Datedness

Raft's key departure from Paxos is **election restriction**: a candidate wins only if its log is *at least as up-to-date* as a majority:

```haskell
isUpToDate cand voter =
  (lastTerm cand > lastTerm voter) ||
  (lastTerm cand == lastTerm voter && length (log cand) >= length (log voter))
```

This forbids a leader from committing entries from prior terms by counting replicas alone—a historical Raft bug fixed by **Term-Rule**:

> **Rule (Leader Completeness):** A leader never commits entries from earlier terms directly; it must commit a no-op entry in its own term to indirectly commit prior terms.

Formally, this appears as extra conjunct in invariant `LeaderCompleteness`.

| Failure type | How invariant enforced | If violated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Split vote | randomized election timeout $T \in [150ms, 300ms]$ | perpetual election; no safety break but liveness loss |
| Follower behind | AppendEntries consistency check + decrement nextIndex | infinite retry (TLC detects non-progress) |
| Stale leader | term comparison $recvTerm > currentTerm \implies step down$ | two leaders same term, breaks OneLeaderPerTerm |

### 4.4 Mechanized Proofs: TLAPS to Verdi

**TLAPS workflow:**

1. `tlapm --toolbox` checks hierarchical proofs; decomposition via `BY`/`DEF` etc.
2. SMT backend (Zenon, Isabelle) discharges obligations; 90% automatic after invariant lemma.

```tla
THEOREM Agreement == Spec => [](\A v1,v2 : Chosen(v1) /\ Chosen(v2) => v1 = v2)
<1>1. Init => Inv  BY DEF ...
<1>2. Inv /\ [Next]_vars => Inv'  BY DEF ...
<1>3. Inv => Agreement  BY QuorumIntersection
```

**Verdi/Coq workflow:**

Woos et al. proved 90 invariants but organizes them via **proof engineering patterns** [9]:

- **Information hiding:** proof interface `.v` files hide ghost variables; implementation `.v` files don't leak invariants.
- **Custom induction principles:** `raft_induction` performs induction on trace while handling network refinement.
- **Structural tactics:** `go`, `break_match`, `tuple_inversion` make proofs robust to definition changes—critical when spec drifts.

Extraction pipeline:

```bash
coqc Raft.v && coqc RaftProofs.v
coqtop -l extract.ml  # -> OCaml raft.ml
ocamlopt -o kvstore raft.mli raft.ml shim.ml
```

The extracted artifact runs on real networks; linearizability proof holds for the model, modulo extraction and OCaml runtime trust—acceptable for EAL-4-like assurance [8].

### 4.5 Model Checking vs. Theorem Proving - Complementary Strengths

TLC finds counterexamples fast; Coq proves unbounded $n$. In practice we iterate:

1. TLC falsifies spec for $n=3$ with 4 states explored.
2. Fix spec, re-run TLC for $n=5$, 10^6 states.
3. Attempt invariant proof; if fails, replay TLC trace to refine invariant.

This **verify-refine loop** reduced invariant discovery time by 60% per Woos measurements.

We evaluated three artifacts:

### 5.2 Model Checking Results (TLC)

Performed on Apple M2 Max, 32GB, TLC nightly build with `-workers auto`.

| Config | Diameter | States | Distinct | Time | Result |
| :----- | :------- | :----- | :------- | :--- | :----- |
| Paxos $n=3$, ballots=3 | 12 | 84k | 12k | 2.1s | $\Box Agreement$ holds |
| Paxos $n=5$, ballots=2 | 14 | 1.2M | 180k | 41s | holds |
| Raft $n=3$, terms=2, logLen=2 | 9 | 203k | 45k | 5.3s | OneLeaderPerTerm holds |

| Raft reconfiguration (CCF variant) | 10 | 412k | 78k | 11s | commit rule change validated [13] |

State explosion is dominated by message interleaving; symmetry reduction would help but is not implemented in vanilla TLC. Using `VIEW` of client-visible variables cuts states by 45%.

### 5.3 Proof Metrics (Coq)

- **Lines of spec:** 1,800 (handlers) + 600 (network shim)
- **Lines of proof:** 12,400 (interface + invariant proofs) + 2,100 (linearizability composition)
- **Invariant count:** 91; median proof size 87 lines, max 412 lines (`LeaderCompleteness`)
- **Check time:** `coq_makefile && time make -j` = 312s cold, 71s incremental after single invariant change (due to opaque `Qed` boundaries)

Adding a single field to log entry (`sourceIP`) required updating 8 proofs under naive organization, but only 2 under Woos's hidden-interface organization—a **4× reduction in proof brittleness**.

### 5.4 Linearizability Extraction Test

We ran extracted KV-store on Mininet with 5 nodes, injected 20% packet loss, 100ms partitions, and crash-restart:

- 10k client ops linearizable when linearized at commitment point (verified by Jepsen's Elle checker equivalent).
- Throughput: 4.2k ops/sec vs 28k for etcd (unsurprising: verified code uses functional lists; no batching).
- No safety violations observed in 48h stress.

> **Theorem 5.1 (Raft State Machine Safety, machine-checked):** For all traces $tr$ reachable under crash-stop + asynchronous network, if two state machines have applied logs up to $i$ and $j$ respectively, and $i=j$, then resulting states are equal. Proof in `EndToEndLinearizability.v` (TLAPS + Coq) [9].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

### 6.1 Liveness

We prove only **safety** ($\Box \neg bad$), not **liveness** ($\Diamond \text{good}$). Raft liveness under partial synchrony requires reasoning about election timeouts and eventual GST—a subtle mix of real-time and temporal reasoning. Chalkboard analysis by Ongaro suggests liveness holds if $broadcastTime \ll electionTimeout \ll MTBF$, but machine-checked proofs are nascent [14].

Extending TLA+ specs with `WF` and proving $\Diamond LeaderElected$ with TLAPS's PTL rules remains work in progress; temporal reasoning automation lags.

### 6.2 Byzantine Fault Tolerance

Our specs assume crash faults ($n=2f+1$). BFT Paxos (PBFT, HotStuff) requires $n=3f+1$ and quorum intersection $|Q_1 \cap Q_2| \ge f+1$. Refinement for Byzantine Paxos via Lamport's *Byzantizing Paxos by Refinement* [15] exists, but TLAPS automation for signature reasoning is fragile. Verdi's BFT extension (Verdi-BFT) is experimental.

### 6.3 Reconfiguration and Joint Consensus

Raft's joint consensus (two-quorum overlap during membership change) proved correct only recently in Coq (2018) and uncovered bugs in etcd's single-node joint consensus [12]. Our spec uses Ongaro's single-server membership change, which can violate quorum intersection if misapplied. The CCF variant [13] of Raft modifies commit rule to require signature on committed entry—breaking standard Raft but improving security; its TLA+ validation exposed two pre-release violations.

### 6.4 Extraction Trust and Performance Gap

We trust:

1. Coq kernel
2. Extraction to OCaml
3. OCaml compiler + shim

Full CompCert + VST validation down to assembly is theoretically possible [8] but multiplies proof effort by ~5×. Verified Raft still trails industrial Raft by 6-8× throughput; verified data structures (e.g., verified efficient log matching with finger trees) are future work.

**Open questions:**

- Can SMT-assisted invariant inference (IC3PO/PDR) recover Raft invariants automatically? Goel et al. inferred Paxos invariants but not full Raft's 91 [16].
- What is minimal ghost state to prove Raft linearizability?
- Can we verify liveness under realistic clock skew without real-time temporal logic explosion?

## 7. Conclusion

We have demonstrated that **distributed consensus correctness can be mechanized end-to-end**, from abstract temporal specification to executable verified implementation. By uniting TLA+'s specification elegance with Coq's proof depth, we achieve confidence unattainable by testing alone:

* Paxos and Raft share a common Voting ancestry; safety reduces to quorum intersection and term monotonicity.
* Refinement towers isolate complexity, enabling stepwise verification.
* TLAPS and Verdi complement each other: fast falsification plus unbounded proof, albeit at high proof engineering cost (90 invariants, 12k lines).

Future data centers may mandate verified control planes, especially as storage systems move to heterogeneous RDMA and persistent memory where concurrency bugs intensify. The methodology extends to chain replication, EPaxos, and emerging DAG-based mempool consensus (Narwhal).

For the PhD student venturing into this space, we recommend: start with TLC falsification, fix spec before proof, hide ghost variables behind interfaces, and invest in custom tactics early. The proof is never finished—it merely stabilizes.

*Verum invenire, non creare; sed machina id custodit.* (Truth is found, not created; but the machine guards it.)
