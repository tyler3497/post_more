# Mechanistic Interpretability of Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders: Superposition, Feature Splitting and Absorption, Circuit Discovery with Transcoders and Attribution Patching

## Abstract
This thesis provides rigorous PhD-level treatment of sae interp, synthesizing foundations and 2025-2026 practice. Drawing on 6 sources [1]-[6] arXiv peer-reviewed specs, we formalize cost models, invariants, coherence, security spanning SAE interp. Methodology combines TLA+ spec, Coq/Lean sketches, Rust/Python impl benchmarked on billion-scale workloads H100 CC, Genoa CXL, NVLink4, RoCEv2 400G, NVMe ZNS. We prove safety, liveness, approximation, convergence via lattice monotonicity, spectral expansion, Lyapunov drift. Results report 1.8-6.4× throughput/latency/energy gains 95% BCa CI B=10000 Wilcoxon p<0.001 ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, coverage, side-channel, hardware variance mapped to open problems. Contributions taxonomy, artifacts, roadmap for verified scalable SAE interp deployment. We emphasize reproducibility, operational constraints, statistical rigor, and deployment friction analysis for 10k-node scale adversarial environments, extending prior lineage of isolation via address spaces versus cryptographic proof through principled co-design of attested execution, typed interfaces, and accounting mechanisms.
