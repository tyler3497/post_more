# Federated Byzantine Agreement Revisited: Stellar SCP, MobileCoin, and Asymmetric Trust

![Federated Quorum Topology](/thesis/thesis-fba-scp-20260810-2782-0.webp)

## 1. Introduction

**Federated Byzantine Agreement (FBA)** departs from classical Byzantine agreement by eliminating the requirement of a *unanimously accepted membership list*. In traditional models inspired by Lamport, Shostak, and Pease, safety rests on a global fail-prone system $\mathcal{F} \subseteq 2^{\mathcal{P}}$ where any $F \in \mathcal{F}$ may be Byzantine. In FBA, each node $v \in V$ declares its own *quorum slices* $Q(v) \subseteq 2^{V}$, where each slice $q \in Q(v)$ contains $v$ itself.

This subjectivity captures Internet-scale peering: just as autonomous systems choose transit providers without global coordination, Stellar validators choose trust dependencies that *transitively* bind the system. The Stellar Consensus Protocol (SCP), introduced by Mazières in 2015, is the first construction for FBA that guarantees **optimal safety** in asynchronous networks and *freedom from blocked states* for nodes whose slice dependencies are satisfiable [Mazières 2015].

This thesis synthesizes four threads:

1. The **formal semantics** of quorum slices and quorums in FBAS
2. **Quorum intersection proofs** reframed as *inclusion games* where a prover must defeat bluffing adversaries
3. **MobileCoin Consensus Protocol (MCP)** as a hardened, concrete SCP deployment
4. **Asymmetric trust graphs** and recovery liveness under guild-based models [Cachin-Tackmann 2019; Cachin et al. 2024]

> **Theorem 1 (Quorum Intersection Safety Invariant).** Let $\langle V,Q \rangle$ be an FBAS with quorum system $\mathcal{Q}$. If $\mathcal{Q}$ enjoys quorum intersection, i.e., $\forall Q_1,Q_2 \in \mathcal{Q}: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$, then no two intact nodes can externalize contradictory values for the same slot, even in presence of arbitrary Byzantine behavior outside the intact set.
> *Proof sketch.* By induction on federated voting rounds. Any externalized value requires a quorum $U$ to have accepted it at ballot $b$. Assume two contradictory values $x \neq y$ externalized by $v_1,v_2$. Then there exist quorums $U_1,U_2$ that accepted $x,y$ respectively. $U_1 \cap U_2$ contains an intact node $w$ by intersection. $w$ cannot vote for both $x$ and $y$ at increasing ballots without violating the *never contradict accepted statement* rule of federated voting unless befouled, contradicting intactness. ∎

We will use **bold** for protocol invariants, *italic* for subjective trust roles, and blockquote theorems for formal guarantees.

## 2. Federated Byzantine Agreement System Formalism

### 2.1 FBAS Definition

An **FBAS** $\langle V,Q \rangle$ is:

- $V$: set of nodes
- $Q: V \rightarrow 2^{2^{V}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}$ quorum function, where $\forall v, \forall q \in Q(v): v \in q$

A set $U \subseteq V$ is a **quorum** iff:

- $U \neq \emptyset$
- $\forall v \in U, \exists q \in Q(v): q \subseteq U$

Intuition: *a quorum must contain a slice for each member* — a self-sustaining trust closure.

- **Minimal quorums** are quorums with no proper quorum subset
- **Intact nodes** $I \subseteq V$ are those where (i) all members of $I$ are correct, (ii) $I$ is a quorum, and (iii) $Q^{I}$ (projection of $Q$ onto $I$) enjoys quorum intersection
- **Befouled** nodes are correct nodes not intact; **naive** vs **wise** distinguishes whether a correct node's fail-prone assumption included actual faulty set $F$

Properties:

- *Decentralized control*: Anyone can join, slices chosen locally
- *Flexible trust*: Nonprofit slice can constrain Tier-1 banks
- *Open membership*: No gatekeeper admission control

### 2.2 Tiered Structures and Centralization

Empirical analyses of Stellar's live slice configuration show extreme centralization [Kim et al. 2019 - Is Stellar As Secure As You Think?]:

- Top 2 nodes controlled by Stellar Development Foundation appear in > 80% of slices
- Failure of 2 SDF validators cascades to full system halt in measured topology
- PageRank and NodeRank metrics confirm power-law influence

This motivates formal intersection proofs rather than assuming organic overlap.

## 3. Stellar Consensus Protocol: Federated Voting and Nomination

SCP consists of two subprotocols chained per slot:

1. **Nomination**: Gossip combining candidate values, generating composite *statements* $\langle \text{slot } i, \text{ballot } b, \text{value } x \rangle$
2. **Balloting**: Federated voting on whether to commit $x$ via 3-level abstraction:

### 3.1 Federated Voting Levels

Federated voting generalizes Bracha broadcast to FBAS [García-Pérez & Gotsman 2019]:

- **Level 1 – Vote**: $v$ votes for $a$ if all messages so far consistent
- **Level 2 – Accept**: $v$ accepts $a$ when it observes a *v-blocking* set voting for $a$, or a quorum $U$ where each member either voted for $a$ or accepted $a$
- **Level 3 – Confirm**: $v$ confirms $a$ when quorum $U$ has accepted $a$

*Safety* depends on Level 2-3 propagation: once a quorum accepts, no intact node's future accepting can diverge without containing a slice intersecting that quorum and honoring its containment.

```python
def is_quorum(U, Q, V):
    if not U: return False
    for v in U:
        # node v must have at least one slice fully inside U
        if not any(q.issubset(U) for q in Q[v]):
            return False
    return True

def quorum_intersection(Q_system, V):
    quorums = [U for U in powerset(V) if is_quorum(U, Q, V)]
    for Qi in quorums:
        for Qj in quorums:
            if Qi.isdisjoint(Qj):
                return False, (Qi, Qj)
    return True, None
```

### 3.2 Ballot Bumping and Liveness

SCP never terminates with disagreement but may stall if slices unsatisfiable. Ballot protocol proceeds with increasing ballot numbers $n$:

- $n = \langle \text{counter},\; \text{value }x \rangle$ partially ordered
- Nodes may *bump* ballot when higher ballots seen, preparing previous ballot
- **Prepared** predicate: $p$ prepared $b$ when $p$ has seen quorum accept *prepare $b$*
- Only **intact quorum** can guarantee progress; befouled nodes may depend on failed nodes for liveness

> **Theorem 2 (Guild Liveness and Non-Blocking).** Given FBAS $\langle V,Q \rangle$ with fail-prone mapping $\mathcal{F} = [\mathcal{F}_v]_{v\in V}$, let execution have faulty set $F$. Let $G$ be a guild: $G \subseteq V\setminus F$, $\forall v\in G: F \in \mathcal{F}_v$, and $\exists$ quorum $U\subseteq G$ for each $v\in G$. Then federated voting and SCP balloting are live for $G$; no $v\in G$ gets permanently stuck in nomination or balloting if reconfiguration eventually satisfies slice dependencies.
> *Proof sketch.* Guild closure ensures every member has a quorum inside wise set. Since wise nodes consider $F$ possible, their slices avoid $F$-only quorums. Standard Mostéfaoui et al. randomized binary consensus liveness argument lifted to asymmetric quorums applies: existence of wise quorum implies eventual common coin can be shared among $G$ without blocking on naive or Byzantine nodes. If slices unsatisfiable due to crash, reconfiguration removing failed dependencies restores existence of superset quorum in $G$. ∎

## 4. Quorum Intersection Proofs via Inclusion of Bluff Games

### 4.1 Motivation: Bluffing Adversary

Traditional quorum intersection is *existential*: prove $\forall Q_1,Q_2: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$. In deployed Stellar, slices are declared via `QUORUM_SET` JSON gossiped in `SCPMessage.envelope`. A Byzantine node $b$ may:

- Advertise different slices to different peers (equivocation)
- Claim slice $\{b, v_1, v_2\}$ where $v_1,v_2$ never reciprocate trust, creating illusion of overlap
- Feign intactness while being befouled in victim's view

We model verification as a **Bluff Game** — a 2-player inclusion game between *Challenger* $C$ and *Prover* $P$ (node claiming quorum membership).

**Game Setup**: Given FBAS snapshot $\langle V,Q \rangle$, disputed quorum $U$, Challenger asks Prover to exhibit witness slice closure.

**Rules:**

1. $P$ commits to $U$ and claims $U$ is quorum
2. $C$ selects $v \in U$ adversarially
3. $P$ must reveal $q_v \in Q(v)$ s.t. $q_v \subseteq U$ with signatures from all $u \in q_v$ on their current slice config (inclusion proof)
4. $C$ may recurse into any $u \in q_v$ (depth-limited $d$)
5. $P$ loses if unable to produce inclusion at any step or signatures invalid / stale

Bluff equilibrium: honest $P$ wins by transitivity; dishonest $P$ can bluff only if it forges signatures or hides inconsistency between views — captured by inclusion proof obligation.

This yields **constructive intersection**: to refute intersection failure $Q_1 \cap Q_2 = \emptyset$, we require Challenger to produce two successful Bluff Game transcripts for $Q_1,Q_2$ with disjoint node sets *under same global slice view* anchored in blockchain.

Formal inclusion proof in MobileCoin uses **Merkle membership proofs** over ledger TxOuts; analogously we build **Quorum Membership Merkle Tree** where leaf $\text{leaf}_v = H(Q(v) || \text{seqno})$.

```rust
// Pseudo-Rust: inclusion proof verification for bluff game
fn verify_slice_inclusion(
    quorum: &HashSet<NodeId>,
    slice_proof: &SliceProof,
    root: &MerkleRoot,
) -> bool {
    // 1. Verify leaf for node v is in global quorum config commitment
    if !merkle_verify(slice_proof.leaf, slice_proof.path, root) {
        return false;
    }
    // 2. Check slice subset property
    if !slice_proof.slice.is_subset(quorum) {
        return false;
    }
    // 3. Check v ∈ slice (FBA validity)
    if !slice_proof.slice.contains(&slice_proof.v) {
        return false;
    }
    // 4. Verify signatures of slice members over their own slices
    slice_proof.signatures.iter().all(|sig| sig.verify())
}
```

### 4.2 From Bluff Games to Intersection Verification

**Benefits:**

- *Dynamic discovery*: SCP already exchanges slices inside votes; inclusion proofs make this auditable
- *Deconstructing Stellar*: García-Pérez & Gotsman abstract SCP as reliable broadcast over intact set; Bluff Game shows where broadcast fails if slices non-reciprocal
- *Quantitative safety*: Kim et al.'s NodeRank can be weighted by success probability in bluff game — central nodes with high rank *must* maintain fresh inclusion proofs or lose rank

**Protocol property comparison:**

| Property | PBFT (Threshold) | SCP (FBA) | MobileCoin MCP | Asymmetric Quorums |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Trust assumption | Global $n \geq 3f+1$ | Per-node slices $Q(v)$ | SDF-curated FBAS | Per-process $\mathcal{F}_v$ |
| Quorum definition | Any $2f+1$ nodes | Slice-closed $U$ | MobileCoin quorum set | Local quorum system $Q_v$ |
| Intersection guarantee | By counting | Requires FBQS analysis | Enforced by watchers | Guild-wise $B^3$ condition |
| Safety class | All correct | Intact only | Intact/validator set | Wise only |
| Liveness recovery | View change | Ballot bump + manual slice re-edit | Hard fork / watcher restart | Reconfig via fail-prone update |

## 5. MobileCoin Consensus Protocol as Specialized SCP

MobileCoin (MOB) ledger, designed for *private payments in messaging apps* [Signal integration], chooses **Stellar-style Proof-of-Reputation** over Proof-of-Work due to mobile constraints.

According to *Mechanics of MobileCoin* [koe et al. 2023] and CoinMarketCap summary of MCP:

> MobileCoin Consensus Protocol chooses Stellar's reputation check. It applies Stellar Consensus Protocol to record transactions, CryptoNote and zero-knowledge proofs to hide transaction details, and ring signatures, one-time addresses, RingCT to protect user data. [...] Federated Byzantine agreement is a model [...] The Stellar Consensus Protocol is one example, wherein nodes reach consensus on abstract 'composite statements'. MobileCoin's consensus protocol is a concrete implementation of SCP where the composite statements consensuated are blocks to be added to the blockchain.

### 5.1 Specializations

- **Abstract → Concrete**: SCP consensus on arbitrary externalized values $\mapsto$ blocks $B_i = \{Tx_0..Tx_k, \text prevRand, \text fogHints\}$
- **Validation framework**: Transactions validated inside *Intel SGX secure enclaves* before nomination; enclave attestation ensures only privacy-rule-compliant Tx enter nomination pool — reduces equivocation surface
- **Fixed validator set with reputation**: Unlike fully open Stellar, MobileCoin currently runs 10-?? validators operated by MobileCoin Inc. and third parties, with watcher nodes verifying quorum intersection; this trades *open membership* for *deterministic intersection*
- **Ledger privacy interaction**: Since Tx contents hidden via MLSAG, Pedersen commitments, Bulletproofs, consensus does **not** need to re-validate amounts — only existence of TxOut via Merkle proofs and enclave signature

This architecture mitigates original SCP liveness pitfall where asymmetric slices make quorums hard to find: by curating Tier-0 tightly connected validators (similar to Stellar top tier in Figure 3 of original whitepaper), MCP ensures *intact set = all validators* unless > f crash.

### 5.2 Security Layers and SCP Dependency

MobileCoin security stacks:

1. **Open-Source code + Decentralized Governance** (trust in code)
2. **CryptoNote + RingCT + Bulletproofs** (transaction privacy)
3. **Byzantine Agreement (SCP-based)** (ledger safety)
4. **Secure Enclaves + Remote Attestation + Oblivious RAM** (validator confidentiality)
5. **Fog Threat Model** (user recovery obliviousness)

If SCP quorum intersection fails, layers 1-2 still hide amounts but ledger forks — double-spend possible. Hence MCP watchers continuously run quorum intersection check using TLA+ model similar to §3.1.

## 6. Asymmetric Trust, Guilds, and Fail-Prone Systems

### 6.1 Cachin-Tackmann Formalization

Cachin et al. [Asymmetric Distributed Trust, DISC 2019 / DC 2024] generalize FBQS to asymmetric quorum systems:

- Each $p_i$ specifies $\mathcal{F}_i$: collection of subsets of $\mathcal{P}$ that $p_i$ believes may be jointly faulty
- $Q_i$ is quorum system for $p_i$ where $\forall Q \in Q_i, \forall F \in \mathcal{F}_i: Q \not\subseteq F$ (availability) and $\forall Q_i \in \mathcal{Q}_i, \forall Q_j \in \mathcal{Q}_j, \forall F\in \mathcal{F}_i\cap \mathcal{F}_j: Q_i \cap Q_j \not\subseteq F$ (consistency)

**Distinction:**

- *Correct* vs *faulty* is objective
- *Wise*: correct and $F\in \mathcal{F}_i$ (assumption adequate)
- *Naive*: correct but $F \notin \mathcal{F}_i$ (trust misplaced — friendship pointed at faulty nodes)

Only wise nodes enjoy safety/liveness; naive nodes may diverge.

- **Guild** $G$: set of wise nodes containing a quorum for each member inside $G$ — analogous to intact set but stricter: must be fully wise-closed

![Asymmetric Trust Guild Recovery](/thesis/thesis-fba-scp-20260810-2782-3.webp)

### 6.2 Protocol Lifting

Many symmetric protocols lift by *only* replacing quorum predicate:

- Single-writer multi-reader regular register (Malkhi-Reiter)
- Byzantine consistent broadcast (Bracha variant)
- Randomized binary consensus (Mostéfaoui et al. fix for signature-free common coin)

**Liveness fix insight**: Original Mostéfaoui signature-free consensus suffered liveness violation when Byzantine nodes delay common coin messages. Cachin-Tackmann asymmetric version introduces *kernel system* $K_i$ where each kernel intersects every quorum of $p_i$ outside fail-prone sets, enabling coin finalization without waiting naively.

### 6.3 Relation to Stellar FBQS

- FBQS $\not\equiv$ asymmetric quorum system: FBQS instantiated with uniform trust does *not* collapse to symmetric threshold quorum [Cachin 2024 §2]
- FBQS takes into account *which* nodes are actually Byzantine in defining intersection (execution-dependent), while asymmetric uses *a priori* $\mathcal{F}_i$
- Personal Byzantine Quorum Systems (PBQS) [Losa et al.] drop global intersection entirely, allowing *consensus clusters* — each cluster live internally, but inter-cluster agreement not required

This taxonomy clarifies MobileCoin's choice: by centrally curating slices, MobileCoin *forces* global intersection, collapsing FBQS to near-symmetric system, thereby regaining classic BFT liveness at cost of decentralization.

## 7. Recovery Liveness under Asymmetric Trust Graphs

### 7.1 Why SCP Does Not Self-Recover

SCP paper explicitly notes (§7 limitations): *SCP does not optimize recovery from liveness failures*. If node $v$'s slices all contain a crashed node $c$, $v$ is blocked until operator edits $Q(v)$. Automatic slice editing is dangerous:

- Auto-removal enables eclipse: attacker causes temporary network partition implying $c$ dead → victim auto-slices attacker in → attacker later equivocates
- No protocol-enforced *reconfiguration consensus* in original SCP; slice edits happen out-of-band via `stellar-core` config file

Asymmetric trust formalization offers recovery path:

**Reconfiguration protocol:**

1. **Detection**: Node $v$ in guild $G$ detects non-progress for slot $s$ > timeout $\Delta$, with blocking set $B \subseteq V$ being suspect crashed
2. **Guild proposal**: $v$ proposes $Q'(v) = Q(v)[B \mapsto alternative]$ where alternative is another wise node's quorum
3. **Bluff-game proof of failure**: $v$ must present inclusion proofs that $B$ has not sent `PREPARE` or `CONFIRM` for $b$ within $2\Delta$ across guild signatures — analogous to *failure detector* $\Diamond S(bf)$
4. **Guild agreement**: Existing guild runs binary consensus (asymmetric version) on whether to admit $Q'(v)$ into canonical config commitment
5. **Live handover**: Upon guild agreement, all members update fail-prone system $\mathcal{F}_i' = \mathcal{F}_i \cup \{ \{b\} \cap naive\}$ to mark naive friends as tolerable loss

**Safety during reconfiguration**: Must maintain quorum intersection *throughout* reconfiguration. This requires *two-phase slice update* : first expand slices (add new dependency), wait for guild quorum to externalize $\text{CONFIG}_{new}$, then shrink removing failed node. Expansion-first preserves intersection monotonicity.

### 7.2 Implementation Considerations

Operational checklist for Stellar / MobileCoin validator operators derived from above:

- Maintain *at least* two disjoint guilds? Actually need overlap: ensure your $Q(v)$ contains threshold of top-tier
- Run **watcher** node continuously computing `is_quorum` and `quorum_intersection` using §3 code; alert on disjoint quorums
- Anchor global config Merkle root per ledger block to enable bluff-game verification; MobileCoin already anchors fog keys
- Prefer *stretching* slices (k-of-n thresholds inside slices) over strict lists to increase availability without breaking intersection

Ordered recovery steps under trust graph partition:

1. Identify maximal guilds via graph search on wise nodes
2. For each befouled node, compute minimal befouling set: nodes causing unsatisfiable slice closure
3. Propose slice edits removing befouling set but preserving at least one intact quorum path to original guild
4. Use **binary consensus on reconfiguration** not SCP itself, to avoid circular dependency
5. After externalization of new config, nodes switch active $Q$ only for slots > $s_{reconfig}$

## 8. Evaluation and Attack Surfaces

### 8.1 Attack Matrix

- **Sybil and Slice Poisoning**: FBA resists Sybil because quorums require *trust* not *count*; but attacker spawning many nodes cheaply still can be included if victim naively trusts all newcomers. Mitigation: inclusion proof cost via reputation + enclave attestation in MobileCoin
- **Cascading Failure**: Kim et al. demonstrate 2-node SDF failure halts Stellar. Equivalent to guild disappearance: if guild $G$ cardinality drops below kernel size, liveness lost. Recovery requires out-of-band re-introduction.
- **Equivocating Slices (Bluff)**: Without signed inclusion, Byzantine $b$ tells Alice $Q(b)=\{b,Alice\}$ and Bob $Q(b)=\{b,Bob\}$ creating illusion of intersection that doesn't materialize globally. Bluff Game forces signature-committed $Q(b)$ in global Merkle tree preventing double-statement.
- **Privacy vs Auditability tension in MobileCoin**: Enclave-validated Tx hide amounts but consensus nodes cannot re-validate privacy rules without enclave; compromised SGX can insert invalid Tx that passes consensus (consensus safety ≠ transaction validity). Separation of concerns explicit: SCP ensures *ledger sequence* safety, enclave ensures *semantic* validity.

### 8.2 Quantitative Note on Intersection

Georgiou et al. quantify validator overlap:

> For UNL overlap, Chase-MacBrough conclude under $f<n/5$, overlap should be at least 90%. For Ripple, 99% overlap may be needed to retain liveness [Cachin et al. 2024 related work survey]. Stellar inherits similar: safe top-tier 3-of-4 configuration means any two top-tier quorums intersect in at least 1, but middle-tier $2$-top-tier dependence means middle-tier quorums intersect only via top tier.

Hence *quorum intersection is not transitive by default*; top tier acts as intersection kernel. This explains MobileCoin topology: enforce top tier all-to-all.

## 9. Conclusion

Federated Byzantine Agreement reframes consensus from global thresholds to subjective slice choices, capturing Internet's federated reality yet demanding *verifiable intersection*.

We showed:

- SCP's safety reduces to quorum intersection and federated voting invariants; intactness is maximal achievable safety boundary
- Intersection proofs can be operationalized as **Bluff Games** requiring signed inclusion proofs, turning trust from social statement to cryptographic audit
- **MobileCoin Consensus** is a disciplined SCP instantiation: concrete blocks, enclave validation, reputation-curated slices trading decentralization for predictable intersection; its multi-layer security depends on SCP safety but not vice versa
- **Asymmetric trust** generalizes FBA with wise/naive/guild distinctions, clarifying why naive but correct nodes receive no guarantees
- Recovery liveness requires *explicit reconfiguration consensus* with expansion-first slice updates preserving intersection monotonicity

Future work: *probabilistic Bluff Game* with stake-weighted slice commitments, automatic reconfiguration using reinforcement learning over trust graph embeddings, and formal refinement from abstract SCP broadcast to MobileCoin concrete blocks in TLA+ as Losa et al. began for PBQS.
