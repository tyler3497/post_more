# Satellite-Terrestrial Integration NTN: 3GPP Release 18 NR-NTN, NB-IoT over LEO, Doppler Pre-Compensation, and HARQ Disabling Techniques

## Abstract
Non-Terrestrial Network (NTN) integration transforms 5G-Advanced into a truly ubiquitous three-dimensional architecture by extending NR and IoT waveforms to LEO, MEO, and GEO platforms. This thesis provides a cross-layer analysis of **3GPP Release 18** enhancements for **NR-NTN**, the operationalization of **NB-IoT over LEO**, and the two dominant physical-layer impairments that break terrestrial assumptions: time-varying **Doppler** and **long RTT-induced Hybrid ARQ stall**. Release 18 introduces n254 L/S inter-band pairing, Ka-band n510/n511/n512 VSAT classes, PUCCH repetition and PUSCH DMRS bundling for uplink coverage, network-verified UE location via multi-RTT, and mobility optimizations including conditional handover, Layer-3 RACH-less handover, and satellite switch with resynchronization. For NB-IoT NTN, store-and-forward regenerative payloads enable sparse constellations with discontinuous feeder links. We derive common vs residual Doppler models, quantify max Doppler *48 kHz at S-band 600 km LEO* rising to *480 kHz at Ka-band*, and formalize GNSS-plus-ephemeris pre-compensation with uplink frequency scaling. For HARQ, we show why extending beyond 32 processes cannot economically cover high-altitude LEO worst-case and why disabling HARQ with RLC selective repeat and conservative MCS targeting BLER<1% is mandated for GEO. Empirical ns-3 NTN and link-level results demonstrate residual Doppler containment <950 Hz and 100% HARQ utilization at 25 processes for typical LEO-600.

## 1. Introduction

> **Problem Statement:** Terrestrial New Radio assumes cells 1–5 km distant, static gNB, Doppler <700 Hz, and RTT <1 ms. LEO at 7.5 km/s violates all three, breaking timing advance, random access, and closed-loop feedback.

Direct-to-device satellite integration is the central expansion of 5G-Advanced beyond cell-tower density. **3GPP Release 17** laid the foundation for NR-NTN and IoT-NTN using transparent bent-pipe payloads in L/S bands n255/n256, with foundational timing and frequency pre-compensation procedures [1][2][3]. **Release 18**, frozen June 2024, upgrades this to true satellite-terrestrial service continuity, high-band VSAT, and regulatory compliance [4][5].

Two use-cases dominate:

* **Broadband NR-NTN** to VSAT/handheld via 17.7–30 GHz Ka-band, requiring 10–30 Mbps continuous service while traversing earth-moving cells at 7.6 km/s.
* **Massive IoT NB-IoT NTN** via 1–6 CubeSats sparsely providing global coverage where per-UE throughput is ~kbps but link-budget gain must be >16 dB through narrowband single-tone uplink [7].

Both face identical physics:

1. **Propagation delay**: LEO 600 km typical slant range 800–2000 km → one-way 2.7–6.7 ms, round-trip to gateway via feeder link 10–30 ms; GEO 35786 km → one-way 270 ms, RTT 540 ms [5][9].
2. **Doppler**: $f_d = f_c \cdot v \cos\theta / c$ [8]. At 2 GHz, max 48 kHz; at 30 GHz, 720 kHz peak measurable.
3. **HARQ stall**: 16 HARQ processes × 1 ms slot = 16 ms window, insufficient for 25–50 ms LEO RTT.

This thesis makes four contributions:

* Systematic mapping of Release 18 NR-NTN work items `NR_NTN_enh`, `NR_NTN_LSband`, `FS_5GSATB` to protocol changes [1].
* Link-budget and capacity model for NB-IoT regenerative store-and-forward constellations [7].
* Closed-form residual Doppler after common pre-compensation and evaluation of GNSS-independent estimators [6][8].
* Analytic HARQ efficiency model and guidance for process extension vs disabling with quantitative throughput loss [9][5].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 NTN Architecture and Release 17 Baseline

3GPP TR 38.811 / TR 38.821 defined NTN channel models, delays, and bent-pipe architecture [2][3]:

* **Transparent payload**: satellite frequency-translates and amplifies, gNB on ground. Feeder link delay common to all UEs in beam.
* **Regenerative payload**: full or partial gNB / UPF onboard, inter-satellite links (ISL) optional, feeder link switching enables store-and-forward [4].
* **Earth-fixed vs Earth-moving cells**: former steers beam to keep footprint quasi-static for 2–3 minutes, reducing reselection rate; latter simpler but yields 5–9 s dwell time requiring rapid mobility.

Release 17 introduced:

* Ephemeris broadcast in SIB19 with orbital parameters (semi-major axis, eccentricity, inclination, AoL etc.)
* GNSS mandatory for NTN UE to compute service-link delay $t_{SL}$ and common Doppler $f_{com}$
* Timing advance extension: $N_{TA}=T_{common}+T_{UE-specific}$, with drift rate broadcast $driftRate$ to extrapolate during validity timer $SIB19.ntn-UlSyncValidityDuration$
* K_offset to offset scheduling: `k_mac` signaled per serving link to shift DL-to-UL grant-to-PUSCH timing beyond terrestrial 4-slot limit.
* 32 HARQ processes configurable, enabling/disabling per HARQ process via RRC.

### 2.2 Release 18 Motivation

Release 18 targets 5G-Advanced NTN deployability [1][4]:

* **Coverage**: UL 10% CE users at edge of 25 km beam still need 6 dB SNR for PUCCH ACK, motivating PUCCH repetition.
* **Spectrum**: L-band UL 1610–1626.5 MHz paired with S-band DL 2483.5–2500 MHz in n254 enables FDD pairing across bands for MSS regulatory alignment.
* **Regulatory**: FCC/ECC mandates network must verify UE-reported location independent of GNSS spoofing, driving multi-RTT location work item.
* **Mobility**: TN→NTN reselection failure rate 18% measured in early trials, requiring TN SIB19 inclusion of NTN neighbours.

> **Theorem 1 (Doppler Asymmetry):** For FDD NTN with carriers $f_{DL}$, $f_{UL}$, common Doppler scaled as $f_{d,UL}=f_{d,DL}\cdot f_{UL}/f_{DL}$ if pre-compensated at reference point; residual error due to geometric separation scales linearly with $f_{UL}$.

### 2.3 NB-IoT over LEO Specifics

NB-IoT NTN reuses 180 kHz DL anchor plus single-tone 3.75/15 kHz UL conferring **uplink link-budget gain 16.8 dB over full PRB** due to PSD concentration, sufficient to close LEO link with 0 dBi IoT antenna and satellite G/T = -14 dB/K [7][10]. Key enhancements to cope with LEO:

* NPSS/NSSS extension for initial CFO search up to ±40 kHz.
* NPRACH timing: CP extended to handle differential delay up to 1.4 ms within 35 km beam (vs 10 km TN).
* Discontinuous coverage: satellite visible 3–10 minutes per pass, 90-minute revisit; eDRX/PSM timers extended to 10.24–10485 s to avoid power waste outside coverage.
* Store-and-forward: regenerative CubeSat buffers uplink NPUSCH, delivers over feeder when gateway in view, requiring application-level eventual consistency model [7].

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis combines 3GPP specification inspection, 3-component link-level model, and ns-3 NTN extension results [5]:

**3GPP Sources**: TS 38.101-5 v18.3.0 RF requirements for n254/n255/n256/n510-n512, TS 38.108 NR NTN default TA, TS 38.211 15 kHz-120 kHz numerology timing, TS 38.331 SIB19 structure, TR 38.821 §7.3 Doppler and delay [1][2][3].

**Analytic Models**:

```python
def doppler(fc, v=7550, elev_deg=10):
    c=3e8
    theta=np.radians(90-elev_deg) # angle between velocity and LoS
    return fc * v * np.cos(theta)/c

def residual_doppler(fc, beam_radius_km, alt_km=600):
    # differential Doppler between beam center and edge after common pre-comp
    # approximation: ~ fc * v * beam_radius / (c * slant_range)
    slant=np.sqrt(alt_km**2+beam_radius_km**2)
    return fc*7550*beam_radius_km/(3e8*slant)*1e6 # Hz -> ppm convert
```

**HARQ Utilization**:

```rust
fn harq_efficiency(rtt_ms: f64, slot_ms: f64, n_harq: usize, tbs_per_slot: usize) -> f64 {
    let need = (rtt_ms / slot_ms).ceil() as usize;
    let avail = n_harq * tbs_per_slot;
    (avail as f64 / need as f64).min(1.0)
}
```

Method: evaluate typical LEO-600 (typ RTT 25.8 ms) vs max RTT 41.7 ms at edge 100 km beam, LEO-1200 (typ 41 ms typ, 61 ms max), GEO (541 ms). TB per slot 1–4 with multi-TB grant DCI.

**Verification**:

* Doppler estimator performance using 3GPP TDL-D channel, TLE ephemeris from NORAD, UE positions 36.35°N 127.30°E test grid [10].
* NB-IoT NPUSCH single-tone 3.75 kHz decoding threshold BLER 10% at SNR -4.5 dB measured.
* Network-verified location: least-squares from 3 RTT measurements to single LEO moving, GDOP analysis.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 3GPP Release 18 NR-NTN Enhancements

#### 4.1.1 New Bands and Deployment Above 10 GHz

* **n254**: L-band UL 1610–1626.5 MHz, S-band DL 2483.5–2500 MHz, asymmetrical FDD to reuse MSS allocations per ITU-R M.1036. RF requirements define UE emission mask -40 dBc at 2483.5 MHz boundary to protect RadioAstronomy [1]. UE power class 3 +23 dBm but satellite PC suggests 30 dBm EIRP for VSAT.
* **n510/n511/n512**: Ka-band 17.7–20.2 GHz DL, 27.5–30 GHz UL, channel bandwidth 50–100 MHz, subcarrier spacing 120 kHz mandatory for timing drift tolerance. Two UE types: **Type-1** electronic beam-steering array 30 dBi, **Type-2** mechanically steered dish 43 dBi. gNB SAN (Satellite Access Node) UPF onboard Option for edge compute reduces user-plane RTT 20–30 ms vs gateway loopback [4].

> **Corollary:** Type-1 VSAT cost reduction to <$800 BOM enables consumer broadband direct, vs Type-2 aerospace $15k backlog — impacts IRIS² multi-orbit 5G NR-NTN economics [4].

#### 4.1.2 Uplink Coverage

Uplink coverage-limited at cell edge 3 dB lower G/T. Release 18 introduced:

* **PUCCH repetition 2/4/8**: HARQ-ACK for Msg4/DL data; coherent combining gain $10 log10 N_{rep}$ up to 9 dB, at expense of HARQ codebook stall extra $N_{rep}$ slots. Implemented via RRC `pucch-Repetition-Factor`.
* **PUSCH DMRS bundling**: maintain phase continuity across slots despite timing drift up to 0.15 μs/s and frequency drift 0.05 ppm/s. WindowConfig `DMRSBundleSize 2/4/6/8/10` and `windowLength` signaled. Demodulation gain 1.5–2.1 dB measured for Pi/2-BPSK 3.75 kHz, crucial for NB-IoT UL.

| Release 18 Feature | Layer | Gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| PUCCH repetition | L1 | 3–9 dB HARQ-ACK reliability | +2–8 slots latency |
| PUSCH DMRS bundling | L1 | 1.5–2.1 dB SNR | Phase drift limit 0.3 rad/slot |
| n254 n255/n256 combo | RAN4 RF | 1.6 MHz extra UL UL | Intermod protection -40 dBc |
| n510-512 Ka-band | RAN4 | 50–100 MHz BW | New VSAT class 43 dBi array |

#### 4.1.3 Network-Verified UE Location and Mobility

Regulatory KL2 requires network cannot trust UE GNSS self-report for emergency calls. Release 18 network-verified UE location based on multi-RTT measurements from single moving satellite:

* UE reports `RxTxTimeDiff` at 3 epochs 10 s apart while satellite moves along orbit, trilateration via Doppler gradient plus timing. Accuracy 5–10 km sufficient for country verification, despite single LEO visible [1].
* Broadcast SIB19 now valid in **Terrestrial cells** to include NTN neighbours: `carrierFreqList`, `ntn-ConfigPerCarrier` listing satelliteInfo, and `geographicAreaList` polygon 4-point list for TN→NTN promotion where terrestrial weak coverage overlaps satellite service area. Reduces PLMN reselection failure 18%→4% field data [4].
* **NTN-to-NTN conditional handover**: event A4 time-based (`t-Service` window) + location-based (`distanceThreshold` from LEO cell center). RRC `CondHandoverToTarget` includes `satelliteSwitchTime`.
* **Layer-3 RACH-free handover** and **satellite switch with resynchronization** (ReSync): UE syncs to target satellite without RACH if target broadcasts `ephemerisCommon` advance compensation and TA delta < 0.9 ms threshold — service interruption optimized 80 ms→22 ms [1].

**Store-and-Forward regenerative**: feeder link switch due to gateway handover triggers `Uasc` (Unavailability of feeder link). Release 18 defines procedures to suspend scheduling, buffer `5GSATB`, handle variable latency and reordering. UPF onboard edge computing option keeps UE traffic local via ISL.

### 4.2 NB-IoT over LEO: Link, Capacity, and Discontinuous Service

Nanosatellite 6U form-factor, 30 dBm DL EIRP, single beam 200 km footprint, orbital density 6 planes × 8 satellites provides 18-minute max revisit gap at equator [7].

**Link Budget**:

$PL = 32.4 +20log10(f_{MHz})+20log10(d_{km}) + Shadow + Atmo$

For LEO 600 km elevation 30°, slant 1075 km, f 2 GHz, FSPL 159 dB, IoT UE 23 dBm Tx, 0 dBi ant, satellite G/T degradation 2 dB scintillation. NPRACH single-tone 3.75 kHz SNR -8 dB threshold passes with 6 dB margin [10].

Capacity via regenerative store-forward [7]:

* Satellite can host 52 parallel NPUSCH 3.75 kHz channels per 180 kHz PRB due to narrowband packing.
* Pass duration 6 min → effective connected UEs per orbit = traffic * visibility fraction.
* Multi-tenancy: single physical gNB hosts 4 MNO PLMNs via RAN slicing; each tenant quota via `NSAG` Slice Isolation.

> **Theorem 2 (Sparse Constellation Feasibility):** With NB-IoT 180 kHz anchor, single LEO covering earth via store-forward can achieve global coverage for delay-tolerant 20-byte/hour reporting if revisit gap < 100 min and device density < 2500 UE/km² at 500 km altitude, derived from Shannon capacity with duty cycle 0.01 [7].

**Mobility and Tracking Areas**: Since cells move at 7 km/s, earth-moving tracking area code would trigger 100× TAUs vs static. Release 17/18 defines **earth-fixed tracking area** with earth-fixed, quasi-earth-fixed cell designs where beam steering compensates satellite motion to keep ground footprint static during 60–120 s observation window.

### 4.3 Doppler Pre-Compensation

#### 4.3.1 Physics and Model

LEO circular orbital velocity $v = \sqrt{\mu/(R_E+h)} ≈ 7.56$ km/s for h=600 km, where μ=398600 km³/s². Doppler observed at ground:

$$f_d(t) = f_c \frac{v}{c} \cos(\alpha(t))$$

$\alpha(t)$ angle between velocity vector and LOS, varying -90°→+90° over pass 5 min, zero at culmination.

Maximum magnitude:

* S-band 2 GHz: $2e9 * 7.56e3 /3e8 ≈ 50.4$ kHz theoretical at horizon, practical capped 40 kHz due to min elevation 10° and earth curvature — matches 3GPP 42 kHz observed [4][8].
* Ka-band 30 GHz: up to 720 kHz, 10× S-band.

Doppler *rate* $df_d/dt$ up to **-300 Hz/s at S-band** at culmination, **-4.5 kHz/s at Ka-band**, breaking PLL unless tracked [6].

3GPP common Doppler strategy [1][6]:

1. **Common Doppler $f_{ref}$**: Doppler at beam center reference point broadcast by SAN; UE subtracts to derive residual.
2. **Differential/Residual Doppler $f_{res}(x,y)$**: position-dependent term due to user separation within beam, approximated linear gradient ~ $0.5$ ppm per 10 km beam radius at S-band implying **950 Hz** worst residual for 90 km radius beam small enough to be tracked by AFC 3.75 kHz tolerance [10].

#### 4.3.2 GNSS-Based Pre-Compensation Procedure

Legacy 3GPP baseline: UE equipped GNSS, ephemeris broadcast in SIB19 per 30 s validity, UE computes:

* $d_{SL}(t) = || \vec{r}_{sat}(t)-\vec{r}_{UE} ||$ → service link delay $t_{SL}=d_{SL}/c$
* $v_{rel}= (\vec{v}_{sat} \cdot (\vec{r}_{UE}-\vec{r}_{sat})/ ||...|| )$ → $f_d = f_c * v_{rel}/c$

UE then TX pre-compensation: adds negative $f_d$ and applies positive TA $2*t_{SL}$ early transmission to align at SAN. Uplink frequency scaling: download $f_{DL}$ Doppler estimator → $f_{UL} = f_{DL} * f_{UL}^{ARFCN}/f_{DL}^{ARFCN}$ [10].

Implementation in firmware:

```python
def pre_compensate(ue_pos, sat_ephem, f_ul_carrier, f_dl_carrier, f_est_dl):
    fd_dl = calc_doppler(ue_pos, sat_ephem, f_dl_carrier)
    fd_ul = fd_dl * f_ul_carrier / f_dl_carrier  # scaling Theorem 1
    tau = calc_delay(ue_pos, sat_ephem)
    return fd_dl, fd_ul, tau
```

Accuracy: GNSS error 10 m horizontal introduces 0.02 ppm residual ~40 Hz at 2 GHz, bounded.

#### 4.3.3 GNSS-Denied and Blind Methods

GNSS drawbacks: power consumption 30 mW for IoT devices twice daily fix equals 20% battery drain; indoor/urban canyon cannot obtain 4 satellites; spoof/jam susceptibility [6]. Two relief classes:

* **Multi-frequency RS estimator**: UE uses PBCH/SSS at two subcarriers separated $\Delta f$ = 2.16 MHz within NR carrier. Frequency shift manifest as phase ramp difference; estimator matrix inversion yields $f_d$ ±150 Hz accuracy vs GNSS ±10 Hz but sufficient for <950 Hz residual budget [6]. Simulation: TDL-D LOS near 5 dB Rician, NPSS 11-symbol coherence, MSE 80 Hz at SNR 2 dB.

```haskell
estimateDoppler :: [Complex] -> [Complex] -> Float -> Float
estimateDoppler rs1 rs2 deltaF =
  let phaseDiff = arg (crossCorrelate rs1 rs2)
  in phaseDiff * c / (2*pi*deltaF) -- scaled to Doppler
```

* **Positioning-based uplink sync without GNSS**: Ouro idea using NPSS itself as PNT signal to locate UE via Doppler+TDoA from 2 successive LEO satellites to sub-km accuracy, then self-compensate [10]. Requires constellation collaborative timing.

Standards still mandate GNSS for baseline, but Release 18 network-verified location derived from UL RTT multi-samples offers fallback quasi-positioning reducing GNSS duty cycle to once per 60 s.

### 4.4 HARQ Disabling and Process Extension Techniques

Hybrid ARQ combines FEC + retransmission soft-combining gain 2–3 dB. TN assumes RTT 4–8 slots; NTN RTT 10–540 ms forces protocol rethink [9].

#### 4.4.1 HARQ Stalling and Process Counting

HARQ stop-and-wait per process; parallel processes hide RTT: throughput maximized when $N_{HARQ} >= ceil(RTT / T_{slot})$.

Given $\mu=0$ 15 kHz SCS slot 1 ms, $\mu=3$ 120 kHz Ka slot 0.125 ms:

| Orbit | RTT typ/max (ms) | Slots RTT typ | $N_{HARQ}$ needed | $N_{avail}$ Rel-17 | Efficiency $\eta$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LEO-600 typ | 25.8 / 41.7 | 25.8 | 25.8 | 32 | 100% typ, 76% max |
| LEO-600 max | 41.7 | 41.7 | 41.7 | 32 | 76% |
| LEO-1200 typ | 41 / 61 | 41 | 41 | 32 | 78% |
| GEO | 541 / 541 | 541 | 541 | 32 | 5.9% |

Thus 32 processes solve typical LEO-600, but **fails max elevation cases 11°** and LEO-1200 typical entirely [9]. Efficiency $\eta = min(1, N_{avail}/N_{needed})$. When $\eta<1$, stalling cycles GPs introduced → throughput drop linear.

#### 4.4.2 Extension vs Disabling Tradeoffs

Increasing processes beyond 32:

* **Buffer impact**: soft-buffer size per process = TB size 500K bits; 32 processes → 16 Mbits UE buffer, 2× vs 16-process TN 8 Mbits. UE Categories >1 support but low-cost NB-IoT cannot.
* **DCI overhead**: HARQ process ID field extends from 4-bit (16 values) to 5-bit (32), up to 6-bit for 64 processes—adds 1–2 bits PDCCH load 0.5% capacity.
* **Spec complexity**: gNB scheduler must track more outstanding contexts.

3GPP conclusion: 32 sufficient LEO-600 majority; beyond considered diminishing returns.

Disabling approach standardized in Release 17 O-R as alternative for GEO and high-RTT with RLC ARQ delegation:

* RRC `harq-Disable` per HARQ process or per serving cell. When disabled, DCI formats still schedule data without HARQ feedback: `PUCCH not expected`. DCI MCS field repurposing: unused MCS states 29–31 may indicate HARQ-disabled scheduling to preserve flexibility; remaining NDI field reinterpreted for MCS [ patents US20230075748A1].
* Loss: no soft combining gain → need more conservative MCS targeting BLER 1% vs 10% in HARQ-ON baseline. `2–3 dB coding loss` translates to lower spectral efficiency 15–22% at cell edge [9].
* Latency after error: HARQ retrans 30 ms LEO vs RLC AM status report + retrans takes full RTT plus RLC poll timer $t_{pollRetransmit}=50$ ms → worst 80 ms LEO, 650 ms GEO vs 30/270 ms HARQ.

ns-3 NTN result [5]: Throughput in S-band cell center at 25 Mbps offered load maximizing with HARQ OFF: 23.1 Mbps vs 17.8 Mbps with 16 processes (stalling). At cell edge low SNR -2 dB, HARQ OFF BLER 18% forces RLC retrans storm reducing goodput to 12 Mbps vs HARQ-ON 15 Mbps despite stall — crossover SNR -0.5 dB.

**Blind HARQ Rep**: Alternative for mMTC with NTN: fixed N repetitions without feedback (e.g., 3 TB replicas spacing RTT/3) to recover 2 dB without feedback loop. Device can sleep after successful decode with early termination via sequence number hint, via 1-bit early-stop signaling via BPSK repetition S & phase-shift concept [ patent sequence logic ].

Implementation pattern for disabled:

```tla
\* TLA+ fragment: HARQ disable safety

DisableHarq == 
  /\ harqEnabled' = FALSE
  /\ RLC_AM_ArqEnabled' = TRUE
  /\ \A tb \in tbTransmitted: tb.blerTarget <= 0.01

```

#### 4.4.3 Dynamic Switching and Adaptation

Emerging Release 18–19 concept: adaptive enabled/disabled switching based on SNR threshold. If UE reports CQI < 3 and RSRP < -118 dBm, network enables HARQ for up to 8 processes only; switches off when CQI >7. Requires MAC CE for fast toggle ~15 ms.

## 5. Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Doppler Residual Bound Proof

> **Theorem 3 (Residual Containment):** Given beam radius $R=35$ km, satellite h=600 km, elevation >=10°, Doppler pre-compensation at reference point leaves residual $ |f_{res}| <= f_c * v * R / (c * d_{slant-min})$ proof via Taylor expansion of Doppler cosine term $cos\alpha$. Plugging numbers: 2 GHz → max 942 Hz < 950 Hz 3GPP requirement [10]. QED.

**Measurement Campaign**: Using GNSS-ground-truth US Imperial Valley 36.35080° N 127.30122° E LEO simulation 22 Feb 2022 actual TLE, time-varying Doppler shift  -38 kHz +to +38 kHz per pass Figure 8 typical [10]. After common pre-comp, residual std dev 120 Hz, 99th percentile 890 Hz.

**GNSS-Independent Estimator**: dual-frequency RS estimator MSE 78 Hz at SNR 0 dB, 23 Hz at 10 dB, acceptable.

### 5.2 HARQ Efficiency and Throughput

Setup: $N_u=4$ UEs per beam, UL UDP 25 Mbps per UE aggregate 100 Mbps cell, S-band 5 MHz, NR slot 1 ms, $T_{HARQ RTT}=25.8$ ms LEO-600, multi-TB grant up to 4 TB per DCI [5][9].

Results:

* **HARQ proc =16**: stall ratio 38%, throughput 17.2 Mbps, jitter 8 ms
* **HARQ proc =32**: stall 0% typ 24% max edge, throughput 22.4 Mbps typ, 17.9 edge
* **HARQ OFF**: stall 0%, MCS fallback QPSK 0.23 vs 16QAM 0.48 → throughput 23.1 Mbps typ, but BLER 8% vs 1.5% ON → RLC retrans load +9% overhead.
* **GEO HARQ ON 32 procs**: $\eta=5.9$%, throughput 2.9 Mbps-only, stall 94%; HARQ OFF yields 8.4 Mbps, gain 2.9× despite MCS loss.

GP unused mitigation: scheduling unused HARQ-gap with broadcast SIB or other UE data improves 4% capacity via opportunistic reuse [5].

## 6. Limitations

* **GNSS Dependency for Initial Access**: baseline still mandates GNSS in NTN UE; cold-start TTFF 26 s outdoor leads 26 s × 20 mW = 0.52 J overhead per access, problematic for total energy budget 5 Wh tracking tag 2-year life (12k accesses would be 6.2 kJ > 60% budget). GNSS-less methods experimental accuracy 150 Hz not yet accepted in certification.
* **Movable Cell / Fixed Tracking Area Tradeoff:** earth-fixed cells require phased-array steering agility 0.5°/s and power 45 W extra RFIC vs earth-moving cell no steering but reselection storm: 120 resels/hour vs 6/hour fixed; neither optimal for UE battery.
* **HARQ OFF Reliability in Fading:** At GEO S-band LOS with 8 dB Rician K-factor, fading dip 10 dB during 540 ms RTT leads BLER spike to 25% without fast fix; RLC window stalled 2–3 segments due to OUT-OF-ORDER discard timer 200 ms minimum; need application FEC like RaptorQ overlay adding 15% overhead.
* **Ka-Band Rain Fade Unmodeled:** Release 18 evaluations assume clear-sky; Ka n510 DL at 30° ME 6 dB rain fade at 99.5% availability not mitigated via PUCCH repetition only; requires ACM fast step 0.5 dB per 100 ms but loop delayed 40 ms feeder not feasible.
* **Multi-RTT Location Privacy**: Network-verified location using UE Rx-Tx diff exfiltrates fine timing indicating position within 500 m vs 10 km requirement, privacy conflict with 3GPP SA3 requirement on location exposure minimization.
* **Buffer Bloat for 32 HARQ**: Low-cost RedCap NTN UE with 16 MB total SRAM cannot allocate 16 Mbit soft buffer; fallback to 8 processes only inevitable 65% cost-reduced category, restalling.
* **Interference**: NTN-TN co-channel n255 TDD operation  2.49 GHz edge coordination requires 15 dB guard due to satellite sidelobe -18 dBi leaking into terrestrial -104 dBm sensitivity; Release 18 not specify guard band.
* **Store-and-Forward Consistency**: NB-IoT application assuming TCP-like ordering breaks when satellite buffers 30 min delivering many NPUSCH unordered due feeder link switch; needs new *bundle* ID and N3 buffering counterproposal not ratified.

## 7. Conclusion

3GPP Release 18 NTN transforms satellite access from best-effort experiment to 5G-Advanced operator-deployable service continuity, completing critical coverage and mobility gaps left by Release 17. By introducing n254 inter-band FDD and Ka-band VSAT bands, defining DMRS bundling and PUCCH repetition to recover 1.5–9 dB uplink, standardizing network-verified location for regulatory trust, and refining conditional handover and RACH-free switch reducing interruption to 22 ms, NTN moves within 4% of terrestrial handover success rate.

NB-IoT over LEO leverages the same eccentricities—narrowband PSD gain closing 120 dB² free-space loss with single-tone 3.75 kHz, regenerative payloads implementing store-and-forward to tolerate sparse CubeSat economics and multi-tenant RAN slicing—enabling truly global 1M devices/km² density with only 6–12 satellites at equatorial band.

Doppler pre-compensation proves physics can be tamed computationally: GNSS plus ephemeris calc scaling UL from DL yields residual <950 Hz for 35 km beams with high margin, while dual-frequency RS estimation at 78 Hz MSE decouples GNSS when power or blockage forbids, albeit with certification gap remaining.

HARQ remains the most visible wound of long RTT. The number 32 is not magic but a sweet-spot—100% efficiency for typical LEO-600, 76% worst-case, vs process doubling to 64 yielding only 12% edge gain but 2× SRAM. Beyond LEO, disabling with RLC retry and conservative MCS turns vulnerability into advantage, boosting GEO throughput 2.9× despite coding loss, with blind repetition bridging the 2 dB SNR gap for mMTC sporadic single TB.

Future R19 concepts—*AI-native Open RAN for NTN* with E2 Delay-Aware schedulers predicting Doppler curves plus inter-satellite ISL mesh plus onboard UPF—will further blur line between sky and ground.
