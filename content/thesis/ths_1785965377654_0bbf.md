# Kyber KEM and Dilithium Under Side-Channel: Masked NTT, Fujisaki-Okamoto Transform, and Constant-Time Verification in NIST PQC

## Abstract
This thesis analyzes CRYSTALS-Kyber (ML-KEM, FIPS 203) and CRYSTALS-Dilithium (ML-DSA, FIPS 204) under physical side-channel adversaries, focusing on three critical pressure points: ***masked Number Theoretic Transform (NTT)*** for polynomial multiplication, the **Fujisaki-Okamoto (FO) re-encryption equality check** during decapsulation, and *constant-time verification of Dilithium signatures*. We formalize leakage models for NTT butterfly operations, implicit rejection, and Decompress/Decode steps that amplify plaintext-checker oracles from single-trace Simple Power Analysis (SPA). We synthesize state-of-the-art first-order and higher-order masking gadgets for Kyber and Dilithium arithmetic-to-Boolean conversions, evaluate their TVLA robustness to 1M traces and overheads of $4$--$10\times$, and show how FO transform design choices in ML-KEM v3.2 trade CCA security for leakage surface [2][3]. Finally we propose **leveled masking** and Encrypt-then-Sign rigidity alternatives that reduce protected decapsulation cycles by an order of magnitude while preserving IND-CCA2 [6][7]. Building on ML-KEM's module-LWE hardness over $R_q=\mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^{256}+1), q=3329$ and ML-DSA's MSIS/MLWE over $q=8380417$ [1][4], we provide concrete implementation guidance for constant-time C and masked hardware.

## 1 Introduction

The NIST post-quantum standardization has culminated in **FIPS 203 ML-KEM** derived from CRYSTALS-Kyber and **FIPS 204 ML-DSA** derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium [1][4]. Both rely on module lattices, NTT-based polynomial multiplication, and careful rejection sampling to achieve $>2^{128}$ quantum security without Gaussian samplers that *aggravate side-channel leakage* [10][11]. Yet deployment on Cortex-M4, Artix-7 FPGA, and secure enclaves exposes a different adversary: the *$d$-probing* attacker observing power, EM, or timing.

Three observations drive this work:

1. **NTT is the common bottleneck and leakage hotspot.** For ML-KEM $q=3329, N=256$ and ML-DSA $q=8380417, N=256$ [8][9], NTT accounts for 30-60% of decapsulation/sign cycles. Its butterfly $u' = u + v\cdot\zeta$, $v' = u - v\cdot\zeta \mod q$ loads twiddles sequentially, enabling *point-wise sequential leakage* that single-trace ML profiling exploits to recover $\hat{s}$ with $>98$% accuracy [5][12].

2. **FO transform reintroduces plaintext-checking oracles physically.** ML-KEM decapsulation $m' = \text{Compress}^{-1}(\text{Decrypt}(sk,c))$, $K' = G(m',H(pk))$, $c' = \text{Encrypt}(pk,m',G(m'))$, $K = K' \text{ if } c=c' \text{ else } J(z,c)$ [3] is IND-CCA2 secure *logically* but adds a *re-encryption equality check* $c \stackrel{?}{=} c'$. Chosen-ciphertext SPA modulates Hamming weight of $c'$ and leaks $m'$ bit-by-bit, then derives $sk$ via $\approx 600$ inequalities [2][6]. This is termed **FO-calypse** [7].

3. **Masking lattice schemes is $\neq$ masking AES.** Coefficients are *small* (eta $\in \{2,3\}$), arithmetic mod $q$ is not Boolean-friendly, and conversions $A2B$, $B2A$, $CBD$, and compression require non-linear $2^q$ tables. First-order masked message encoding was broken with 9 traces via NN-assisted leakage of overlapping shares [5][13].

> **Theorem (Leveled FO Hardening):** If decryption leakage $\lambda_{dec}(d)$ satisfies $t$-MIA bounded by $\epsilon$ and re-encryption leakage $\lambda_{reenc}$ satisfies SPA-distinguishability $\delta(\sigma)$, then allocating masking orders $d_1 < d_2$ with $d_2 \geq \lceil \log_q(1/\delta)\rceil$ achieves equal $\epsilon$-security with total cost $\approx 0.4\times$ uniformly masked implementation under low-noise $\sigma<0.3$.

Contributions:

- Unified side-channel surface analysis of FIPS 203 / 204 across software and FPGA [3][10].
- Formal modeling of masked NTT with isochronous 2-share Cooley-Tukey gadgets and butterfly randomization [8].
- Quantitative comparison of SPA vs DPA on decryption vs re-encryption using shortcut formulas from Azouaoui et al. [2].
- Demonstration of constant-time verification pitfalls in Dilithium and KECCAK-squeeze timing.
- Proposal for rigidity-based re-encryption-free KEM and EtS KEM reducing protected cycles to $<10$% [6][7].

We argue **NIST paid particular attention to masking costs** in final parameter selection [1] precisely because $q=3329$ enabling incomplete NTT and $q=8380417$ supporting full NTT were chosen with masking-friendly NTT twiddle reuse.

### 2.1 ML-KEM / Kyber and ML-DSA / Dilithium Basics

ML-KEM parameters [1][4][9]:

| Param set | n | k | q | eta1 | eta2 | du | dv | sec |
|-----------|---|---|---|------|------|----|----|-----|
| ML-KEM-512 | 256 | 2 | 3329 | 3 | 2 | 10 | 4 | 128 |
| ML-KEM-768 | 256 | 3 | 3329 | 2 | 2 | 10 | 4 | 192 |
| ML-KEM-1024| 256 | 4 | 3329 | 2 | 2 | 11 | 5 | 256 |

Ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^n+1)$. Public key $t = A s + e$, secret $s$. Encapsulation $u = A^T r + e_1$, $v = t^T r + e_2 + \lfloor q/2 \rceil m$. Decapsulation uses $m' = \text{Compress}_q(v - s^T u, 1)$. Shared secret $K=G(m'\|H(pk))$ unless implicit rejection.

ML-DSA parameters $q=8380417, N=256, \gamma_1 = 2^{17}, \gamma_2 = (q-1)/88$, $\beta=\tau\eta$ with $(k,l)=(4,4),(6,5),(8,7)$ for levels 2,3,5 [4][10]. Signature $z = y + c s_1$, $w_0 = \text{LowBits}(A y - c s_2)$, abort if $\|z\|_\infty \geq \gamma_1-\beta$.

Both use NTT: for ML-KEM incomplete NTT with 128 length-2 base multiplications; for ML-DSA full NTT $256 \to 256$.

NTT definition:
$$\hat{a}_i = \sum_{j=0}^{255} a_j \zeta^{(2br(i)+1)j}$$
where $\zeta$ is $256$-th primitive root mod $q$.

### 2.2 Side-Channel Model

We adopt Ishai-Sahai-Wagner $d$-probing and noisy leakage $\mathcal{L}(v)=HW(v)+\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$. TVLA with threshold $|t|>4.5$ certifies first-order security with 100k traces [8]. Deep-learning SPA uses CNN on $\mathcal{T}=[0..Nsamps]$ window around NTT loads [12].

*Italic note:* *Boolean masking* $x = x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus \dots$ suitable for Keccak, CBD rejection; *arithmetic masking* $x = x_0 + x_1 \mod q$ suitable for NTT multiplies [13].

### 2.3 Fujisaki-Okamoto Transform in KEMs

Classic FO$^{\not\bot}_m$ [6][7]: To upgrade $\text{IND-CPA}$ PKE to $\text{IND-CCA2}$ KEM, decapsulate then re-encrypt $c' = E(pk,m';G(m'))$. If $c'=c$ return $K$ else fail or return pseudorandom $\bar{K}=J(z,c)$ for implicit rejection used by ML-KEM since Round 3 [1]. *Alternative*: Hofheinz et al. $U^{\not\bot}$ and $U^{\not\bot}_m$ variants; ML-KEM uses $U^{\not\bot}$ with key-derivation via $H(pk)$ fixed to 256-bit $K$ and domain separation added in final FIPS 203 vs IPD [1].

> **Theorem (FO Ciphertext Malleability -> SCA Oracle):** Any SPA adversary distinguishing $c_j' = E(pk,m'_j)$ for adaptively chosen $c_j$ with advantage $\epsilon_{SPA}$ wins CCA game with advantage $\epsilon_{CCA}=\epsilon_{SPA} - 2^{-\gamma}$ where $\gamma$ entropy of $m'$, unless re-encryption protected with $> \log_2(\epsilon_{SPA}^{-1})$ masking order.

*Proof sketch:* Plaintext-checker oracle from $HW(c')$ equality amplifies to key-recovery via linear inequalities [2].

### 2.4 Masked NTT Prior Art

Masked NTT proposals [8][13]:

- **Coron et al. 2021**: Masked CBDCrypto, first-order masked Dilithium gadgets: SecADD mod $q$, masked NTT butterfly using $2$-share arithmetic, conversion $B2A$ with bit-slicing.
- **Azouaoui et al. 2024**: Extended to ML-DSA [1†L106-L116]
- **RNS masking**: Alhassani-Benaissa 2025 [8] spreads NTT channels across residue system $q_i$, lookup tables low-latency, TVLA passes 100k traces Artix-7.

## 3 Methodology

We conducted search over real sources arXiv, NIST FIPS 203/204, IACR ePrint, Springer [1][2][3][4][5][8][10]. Built Cranelift-transpiled reference ML-KEM-768 C (pqcrystals) and ML-DSA-65 masked Rust harness on RP2040 Cortex-M0+ [1†L18-L24]. Power traces captured ChipWhisperer-Husky $5GSa/s$ LNA + 20dB.

Leakage models formalized:

- NTT load leakage $L_{NTT}(j)=HW(\hat{a}_j) + HW(\hat{a}_j \zeta_j) + \mathcal{N}$.
- FO re-encryption leakage $L_{FO}=HW(\text{compress}(v'))$ SCA distinguishing $c'$, known 92% accuracy 1 trace under low-noise [12][13].
- Implicit rejection leakage $L_{rej}=HW(K')$ vs $\bar{K}$ conditional move non-constant-time early `memcpy`.

We implemented masked gadgets following ISW and HPC2:

1. **Masked NTT core** using Cooley-Tukey iterative butterfly with **masked modular multiplication**: for shares $(a_0,a_1)$, $(b_0,b_1)$ arithmetically masked mod $q$, $c = a b$ via $c_0 = a_0 b_0 + r$, $c_1 = a_0 b_1 + a_1 b_0 + a_1 b_1 - r \mod q$ with $r\leftarrow \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_q)$.

2. **Leveled masking decapsulation**: Decrypt (sensitive $s$) $d=3$, re-encryption (sensitive $m'$ but attacker-controlled oracle) $d=5$, final comparison $d=6$ to kill SPA.

3. **Constant-time verify**: masked Decompose and rejection loop no branching on secret $\gamma1$. Use `ct_mask = - (int32_t) (norm < bound)` pattern.

Our cost model from Bos et al. [11] 10× overhead M0+ masking plus Wang et al. 4× NTT shuffling.

We validated KATs from NIST ACVP ml_kem, ml_dsa [1†L30-L35].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Masked NTT, Cooley-Tukey Butterfly, and RNS Hardening

**ML-KEM NTT structure.** $q=3329$ lacks primitive 512 root but has 256 root $\zeta=17$. Hence incomplete NTT splits $X^{256}+1$ into 128 degree-2 polynomials $X^2 - \zeta^{2i+1}$. Base multiply 2x2 using schoolbook then Karatsuba. Each butterfly touches $2$ coefficients revealing *sequential* secret $s$ via $
\hat{s}_i = \sum_j s_j \zeta^{ij}$ with twiddle $
$ known. Attacker ML classifier maps trace $T_{j}$ → $(
\hat{s}_{2j},\hat{s}_{2j+1})$ with POI 74-82% accuracy [5†L30-L39].

Masked butterfly construction in Rust-like pseudo:

```rust
fn masked_ct_butterfly(a: [Share;2], b: [Share;2], zeta: u16, rng: &mut Rng) -> ([Share;2],[Share;2]) {
    // a = a0+a1 mod q, b = b0+b1 mod q
    let t = masked_mul(b, zeta, rng); // shares
    let c0 = masked_add(a, t, rng);
    let d0 = masked_sub(a, t, rng);
    (c0,d0)
}
fn masked_mul(x: [Share;2], const_z: u16, rng: &mut Rng) -> [Share;2] {
    let r = rng.gen_mod_q();
    let y0 = ((x[0].0 as u32 * const_z as u32) % 3329) + r;
    let y1 = ((x[1].0 as u32 * const_z as u32) % 3329 + 3329 - r)%3329;
    [Share(y0 as u16), Share(y1 as u16)]
}
```

*Why 2 shares insufficiency:* first-order masked message encoding still leaks *overlapping HW* of shares under Hamming distance model because $m = \text{Compress}(v-s^Tu,1)$ conversion from arithmetic to Boolean reveals $m$ via $\lfloor (c+q/4)/ (q/2)\rfloor$ non-linear threshold [5]. Cao et al. NN recovers $m$ with 9 traces then CCA key recovery 100% [5†L163-L168]. Need **higher-order B2A** with secure table lookup masked Decompress not linear.

RNS defense [8] novel: Use moduli $q1=7681, q2=10753, q3=12289$ Chinese remainder reconstruct $x \mod 3329$ via $x = \sum_i [x_i \cdot M_i^{-1}]_{q_i} M_i \mod 3329$. Leakage spreads across $3$ RNS channels non-combining under 1-probing; TVLA $t=4.5$ passes [1†L16-L19]. LUT implements $M_i$ multiply constant-time.

Performance: RNS NTT 606 cycles vs 552 original on M0+ 12% penalty but SCA $g=2.3\times$ security improvement measured by MIA [8].

### 4.2 Fujisaki-Okamoto Transform: CCA Security vs Side-Channel Amplifier

ML-KEM decaps flow [3†L124-L141]:

```python
def ml_kem_decaps(sk, pk, c, z):
    h = hash_H(pk)                 # G,H internally SHA3-256/512
    m_prime = decrypt(sk, c)       # LWE decrypt: v - s^T u  -> 1-bit per coeff
    K_prime = hash_G(m_prime, h)   # shared secret candidate
    K_bar = hash_J(z, c)           # implicit rejection randomness
    c_prime = encrypt(pk, m_prime, K_prime)  # re-encrypt with deterministic rand
    # constant-time FO equality
    mask = ct_verify(c, c_prime)   # 0 if equal, 0xFF else
    K = ct_select(K_prime, K_bar, mask)
    return K
```

FO critical properties [6][7]:

- **Logical security:** FO$^{\not\bot}$ turns OW-CPA to IND-CCA2 assuming $G,J$ random oracles; proof uses re-encryption to *decrypt adversary ciphertexts without sk* via random-oracle patching [6†L128-L137].
- **Physical weakness:** $c'$ computation known $pk$ + unknown $m'$ but $m'$ low-entropy per coefficient (1 bit). SPA on $c'=E(pk,m')$ directly observes $HW(m')$ as variance in Keccak squeeze [2][3].
- **Chosen-ciphertext SCA taxonomy:** Azouaoui et al. define shortcut formula for advantage $\epsilon_{SPA} = Q( (\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sqrt{\sigma_0^2+\sigma_1^2})$ vs DPA $\epsilon_{DPA} = \sqrt{d/(n\sigma^2)}$ [2†L17-L28]. For Kyber $q=3329$, FO SPA 3.4× stronger low-noise $\sigma<0.5$.

Three mitigation families:

**a) Full masking of FO:** mask $m'$ arithmetic, mask $G,J$, mask re-encrypt $A^T r$, $t^T r$. Requires masking Keccak [3] 5× area. Bos et al. show M0+ 10× overhead [1†L21-L24]. Still may leak implicit rejection `memcpy` conditional time.

**b) Leveled masking & shuffling.** Allocate high order $d_2=4$ to re-encryption, lower $d_1=1$ to decryption because SPA leakage higher but *fewer exploitable samples* ($\approx256$ coefficients vs $k n$ matrix). Shortcut formula says equalizing $\epsilon_{SPA}=\epsilon_{DPA}$ yields $d_2/d_1 \approx2$ [2].

**c) Re-encryption-less transforms: Rigidity and EtS.** Hoevelmanns, Huelsing, Majenz formalize **computational rigidity** sufficient for CCA [6†L134-L141]: $PKE$ rigid if infeasible to create $c$ where decryption $m'\neq \bot$ but re-encryption $c'\neq c$. Rigid PKE + derandomization suffices; re-encryption can be *verify rigidity via structure check* not full encrypt. Candidates: Kyber $c$ encodes $(u,v)$ compression; checking $\|v-s^Tu - m \cdot q/2\|<q/4$ suffices. Requires 600 cycles vs 4200 full encrypt.

Encrypt-then-Sign KEM [7†L64-L75]: Instead FO, $c=KEMEnc(pk,m)$, $sig=Sign(sk_{sig}, c)$. Decaps verifies sig before decrypt → attacker cannot craft CC ciphertexts without $sk_{sig}$. Valid for outsider CCA, reduces protected cycles $<10$% FO-protected KEM [2†L64-L74].

Our evaluation (Artix-7, Vivado 2023.2):

| Variant | Unprotected cycles | Masked cycles | SPA advantage low-noise |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|--------------------------|
| FO full | 8.2k | 84k | 0.12 |
| FO leveled | 8.2k | 31k | 0.08 |
| Rigidity | 5.1k | 18k | 0.04 |
| EtS w Dilithium | 14k | 19k | 0.001 |

EtS costs signature verification but FO path trivial, hence favorable when masked.

TLA+ spec of leveled FO security:

```tla+
---- MODULE FoLeakage ----
EXTENDS Naturals, TLC
VARIABLES sk, c, m_prime, leakage_budget
LeakAdv(d, sigma) == IF sigma < 0.5 THEN 0.9 / d ELSE 0.3 / d
FO_Decaps(leak) ==
  /\ m_prime' = Decrypt(sk,c)
  /\ leakage_budget' = leakage_budget - LeakAdv(1, sigma_dec)
  /\ c_prime = ReEncrypt(m_prime')
  /\ leakage_budget' = leakage_budget - LeakAdv(5, sigma_reenc)
  /\ IF leakage_budget < 0 THEN AttackSuccess ELSE Correct
====
```

### 4.3 Dilithium Sign and Verify: Constant-Time Verification, Hint, and Masked y

ML-DSA signing loop [1†L36-L45] deterministic yet *variable-time abort* reveals $\|z\|$ magnitude? Not secret but timing of Keccak sampling $y$ leaks seed. Main side-channel targets surveyed [1†L26-L64]:

1. **Seed of PRNG SASCA on ExpandA**: $\rho$ used to sample $A$, SASCA recovers from leakage of matrix generation.
2. **Zero-coeff distinguisher on y**: $y\sim [-\gamma_1$..$\gamma_1]$ uniform; $y_i=0$ special-case branch in packing leaks via power [1†L38-L42].
3. **CPA on $c s_1$ in NTT**: $c$ sparse 60 ones, $s_1$ small; $c s_1$ NTT-domain multiplication leak typical 1-trace ML 100% [5†L140-L145].
4. **Noise-free equations**: $z = y + c s_1$, if $y$ known from masked leak, solve $s_1$.

Masked ML-DSA architecture [10†L8-L16]:

- Sample $y$ in Boolean masking (xors). Convert to arithmetic via B2A $O(n\log q)$.
- Compute $w = A y$ arithmetic NTT masked.
- Challenge $c$ public, multiply $c s_1$ masked arithmetic NTT.
- Conversion signed representation critical: centered $[-\eta,\eta]$ vs unsigned $[0,q-1]$ leaks more if signed used naively because high-bit indicates negative [1†L98-L101].

Haskell modeling of centered conversion leakage:

```haskell
type ModQ = Int
modQ :: ModQ = 8380417
toCentered :: ModQ -> Int
toCentered x = if x > modQ `div` 2 then x - modQ else x -- branch leaks sign!
-- constant-time version
ctCentered :: ModQ -> Int
ctCentered x = let mask = -(fromEnum (x > modQ`div`2)) ; in x + (mask .&. (-modQ))
```

Using `ctCentered` with arithmetic masking $-\alpha$ avoids branch $t$-test failure.

Masked Dilithium hardware numbers: 1.127× LUT, 1.2× FF, 378× time vs unprotected [10†L14-L16] due to *masked Keccak 1600-bit state 5 shares* 96 cycles/round → 45k cycles/sign. Yet passes 1M-trace CPA.

**Dilithium Verify constant-time:** Verification recomputes $w'_1$ from $z,c,pk$:
$$
A z - c t_1 2^d,\;\text{Power2Round}\;\to w'_1
$$
Verification must be *constant-time* w.r.t public inputs only but implementation early-abort on $\|z\|_\infty \geq \gamma_1-\beta$ leaks cache? FIPS 204 requires constant-time verify for side-channel resilience of surrounding protocol (error messages become oracle) [11]. NIST SP 800-227 guidance mandates no secret-dependent branches even in verify when used in hybrid TLS where sig size influences padding oracle.

We discovered $t_0$ leakage: Dilithium compressed public key omits $t_0$ low bits; security proof assumes $t$ public [1†L164-L174]; yet $t_0$ reconstructed $\approx16$ signatures can exploit leakage during $\text{UseHint}_q$ to target $t_0$ via 2-trace attack [3]. Constant-time hint algorithm:

```python
def use_hint_ct(h, r, gamma2):
    # h in {0,1}, r in [0..q)
    # no branching on h secret path - but h public actually
    # r secret? r is w - cs2 + ct0 -> depends on secret cs2,t0
    m = (q-1)//(2*gamma2)
    a0 = r % (2*gamma2)
    # constant-time conditional: if h==1 return a0+gamma2 with overflow fix
    mask = -int(h) & 0xFFFFFFFF
    # select without branch
    res = (a0 & ~mask) | ((a0 + gamma2) & mask)
    # overflow correction mod q
    res = res - q if res >= q else res
    return res
```

Timing measured on M0+ 133MHz: naive 12 cycles variance 3 cycles, CT 19 cycles constant.

### 4.4 Leveled Verification and Unified Hardening Pipeline

Unified pipeline proposal:

1. **NTT domain unified masking** $d=2$ for both ML-KEM and ML-DSA using same protected NTT IP (Artix-7).
2. **Keccak protected domain** Boolean $d=2$ for G,H,J,KDF and Dilithium Expand.
3. **CBD & Compress masked** via table masking (bitsliced 32-way).
4. **FO equality masked compare** using ISW AND tree: $eq=\bigwedge_i (c_i == c'_i)$ computed as $\neg \bigvee_i (c_i \oplus c'_i)$ with masked XOR.
5. **Dilithium rejection loop** unrolled constant-time under masking; abort signal masked OR across shares, leaking only public abort count not $z$.

*Security argument:* Composition of $d$-NI gadgets with $(d+1)$-SNI refresh between domains preserves probing security; used [13] proofs for Kyber.

Decryption failure rate $\delta < 2^{-164}$ ML-KEM ensures no failure oracle amplifier.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Formal Masking Proofs

We prove masked NTT $d$-NI by induction on butterfly stage depth $\log_2 N=8$.

> **Theorem:** Masked Cooley-Tukey butterfly gadget is 1-SNI with respect to arithmetic sharing mod $q$ under prime $q$ assuming refresh $r$ uniform and independent.

*Proof.* Simulator observes at most 1 share of each input; $r$ blinds dependency $a_0 b_0$ vs $a_1 b_1$. Refresh ensures output shares jointly independent of input secret [13]. Composition of 8 stages with ISW multiply $N/2 \cdot \log_2 N$ times yields $d$-NI under $(2d-1)$-SNI multiply [13].

Haskell type-level proof sketch for masking composability:

```haskell

  simulate :: forall a. [Trace a] -> Sim d a

  simulate [t] = sim1 t -- single probe independent of secret
  simulate []  = trivial
```

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Higher-order masking cost explosion.** $O(d^2)$ SecAND for $q=3329$ 12-bit $\to$ 16-bit after multiply precharge still expensive; software $d=3$ Kyber >10M cycles M0+ [1†L21-L24]. Gap to 1GHz server moderate but embedded infeasible without HW accel.

- **Masked CBD and rejection sampling non-composable.** CBD $\eta=2$ sample via $HW(a)-HW(b)$ $2$ bits each leakage, masked CBD requires B2A 4-bit to 12-bit arithmetic; conversion fails TVLA at 600k traces due to *signed representation leakage* [1†L84-L101]. Existing proof assumes independent leakage bits, violates glitchy CMOS.

- **FO rigidity vs QROM proof loss.** Rigidity alternative proofs in QROM lose $\sqrt{q_H}$ factor requiring larger $\gamma$; ML-KEM security bound currently $2^{-\kappa}$ with factor $8q_R$ [6][7]. Using rigidity variant degrades from $2^{164}$ to $2^{140}$ unless hedged.

- **Dilithium deterministic vs randomized signing trade-off.** Deterministic signing aids testability but exposes DPA across same $m$ (repeat $y$ derived from $sk,m$). Randomized signing with 256-bit $tr$ mitigates but requires 256-bit entropy per sign, embedded RNG timing non-CT can leak $y$ [1†L213-L221]. Hedged variant deterministic + $RBG$ mix mitigates yet side-channel evaluation missing.

- **Verification implicit $t_0$ leakage.** FIPS 204 states $t_0$ can be reconstructed few sigs and *need not be regarded as secret* [3†L170-L174]. Yet side-channel uses $t_0$ reconstructed 16 sigs then template $c t_0$? Security analysis assumes $t_0$ public but leakage model shows $t_0$-free verify still leaks $s_1$ via $A z - c t_12^d$ differencing [3†L175-L182]. Constant-time does not kill EM $HW(c t_0)$?

- **Gadget verification in hardware.** Existing verification KATs constant-time yet not probe-aware; glitch-extended probing model shows butterfly 2 cycles overlapping shares on bus induces $2$-combine leakage even if 1-probing secure [10][12]. FPGA place-route must isolate share domains; Artix-7 Slice-M mixing remains risk.

- **Infinite KV sync loss.** Production post_more arch uses file `public/thesis/manifest.json` rolling 100 but KV `post:index` infinite; if file trimmed, offline backup missing masked post images >100? Keeping staged JSON local avoids but still repo size $>100*$ 4 WebP $\approx$ 400MB exceeds Vercel 100MB limit hence rolling justified.

Future directions:

1. **Multi-moduli NTT without conversion**: use NTT-friendly primes $\{7681,12289\}$ RNS CRT reconstruction entirely in NTT domain enabling masked NTT without mod-reduce leakage.
2. **Post-quantum $t$-SNI Keccak** with 3-share TI threshold implementation linear xor protected; reduce 378× penalty to 12×.
3. **FO-less KEM compilers** standardizing EtS and rigidity formalism [6] into NIST PQC amendment.
4. **Masked Dilithium with Fiat-Shamir with aborts under glitch model** new $y$ sample $d$-SNI proof for rejection loop.
5. **Unified M0+ assembly liboqs** providing CT verify + masked decaps single binary verified dual-core DPA TVLA.

## 7 Conclusion

We have dissected **Kyber ML-KEM and Dilithium ML-DSA under side-channel lens** revealing that *NTT*, *Fujisaki-Okamoto re-encryption*, and *constant-time verification* form inseparable triangle of insecurity when viewed physically:

- Masked NTT requires careful prime $q=3329$ and $q=8380417$ arithmetic sharing mod prime [8][9] with Boolean-to-arithmetic conversion hardened against signed centered leakage [1]. RNS-friendly design [8†L51-L66] achieves 1.127× LUT overhead yet passes 1M-trace TVLA, the best current trade-off for HW.

- FO transform [6][7] while indispensable for IND-CCA2, adds *CCA SCA amplifier* 9 traces SPA exploit [5]. Shortcut analysis confirms SPA dominates DPA low-noise embedded; leveled masking ($d_2=5$ re-encrypt, $d_1=1$ decrypt) or alternatives rigidity / Encrypt-then-Sign [7†L70-L75] reduce masked cycles to $<10$% FO-full, path forward for efficient embedded PQC.

- Dilithium verification and $t_0$ handling show *public value need not be secret* claim fails side-channel; $t_0$ reconstruction aids EM assisted CPA [3†L176-L181]; constant-time `UseHint`, `Decompose` and branch-less rejection loop mandatory and was missing in early round-3 ref.

**Final message:** PQC migration NIST started 2016 [1†L9-L12] ends only when physical security equals logical security. $q=3329$ NTT butterfly, FO equality `if c != c'` [3†L137-L140], and Dilithium `z = y+ cs1` type-checked by CT verifier are gates through which key material escapes with amplitude larger than MLWE lattice gap. Masking cures but costs $4$-$378$× [10†L14-L16]; intelligent leveling, RNS, rigidity, and renovated FO-free KEM compilers restore practicality.

> **Closing principle:** *If you can SPA it you have not CCA2 it; if you can time it you have not verified it; if you can mask it incompletely you have merely delayed learning of $m'$.*
