# Univalence, Cubes, and Higher Inductive Types: A Technical Survey of Homotopy Type Theory and Its Cubical Realization

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf type theory through the lens of homotopy: types are spaces, terms are points, and proofs of identity are paths. At its core lies Voevodsky's univalence axiom, which identifies identifications of types in a universe with equivalences between them, collapsing the distinction between isomorphic and equal mathematical structures. This article surveys the development from the classical simplicial model of univalent foundations to the constructive breakthrough of cubical type theory, where univalence becomes a provable theorem with computational content. We examine higher inductive types as the synthetic counterpart of cell complexes, the stratification of types by h-level into n-types, and the program of synthetic homotopy theory culminating in computer-verified computations such as the fundamental group of the circle. We assess proof-theoretic results — including canonicity for cubical type theory — and close with open problems in normalization, coherence, and the unification of cubical and higher-topos semantics.

##1 Intro

Classical foundations of mathematics treat equality as the most rigid of notions: two sets are equal only when they have exactly the same elements, and isomorphic structures are merely *equivalent*, not identical. In everyday mathematical practice this distinction is a fiction — group theorists treat isomorphic groups as "the same" without apology — yet formal systems have historically been unable to express this structuralist intuition without tedious transport lemmas [1]. Homotopy type theory proposes a radical simplification: make the foundational notion of *identity* coincide with the mathematical notion of *equivalence*.

The proposal, due to Voevodsky, begins with an observation about Martin-Löf's dependent type theory. The identity type `x =_A y`, equipped with its induction principle `J`, already has a rich structure: reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and transport all arise from path induction, and iterated identity types form what is recognizably an ∞-groupoid [1]. Rather than suppressing this higher structure as classical type theory does — collapsing each identity type to a subsingleton via uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP) — HoTT *embraces* it, interpreting types as homotopy types (spaces) and identity proofs as paths.

The decisive move is the **univalence axiom**. For a universe `U` and types `A, B : U`, there is a canonical map

> **Theorem:** `idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)`

constructed by path induction, sending `refl_A` to the identity equivalence. Univalence asserts that this map is itself an equivalence:

> **Univalence Axiom:** For all `A, B : U`, `idtoeqv` is an equivalence; equivalently, `(A =_U B) ≃ (A ≃ B)`.

Its consequences are immediate and far-reaching: isomorphic structures can be identified, yielding function extensionality, propositional extensionality, and a structuralist foundation in which "isomorphic structures have identical properties" is a *theorem* rather than a slogan [2]. But the axiom as originally posed is non-constructive in the proof-theoretic sense: nothing computes from it, and its computational content remained opaque until the cubical revolution [3].

This survey traces that trajectory — from the simplicial-set semantics that established consistency, through the constructive interpretation of univalence in cubical type theory, to the emerging practice of synthetic homotopy theory — and evaluates the state of the art as a foundation that is simultaneously mathematical, computational, and mechanizable.

##2 Background

### The homotopy interpretation

The homotopy interpretation of type theory assigns to each type `A` a space (more precisely, an ∞-groupoid), to each term `a : A` a point of that space, and to each proof `p : a =_A b` a path from `a` to `b` [1]. This interpretation is not a metaphor but a precise model: in the simplicial-set model, types are interpreted as Kan complexes and the identity type `Id_A` as the path space [2]. Under this reading:

- *Dependent types* `B : A → U` become fibrations over `A`.
- *Transport* `transport^B : (p : a = a') → B(a) → B(a')` becomes the path-lifting/homotopy-lifting property.
- *Function extensionality* becomes the statement that the function space between spaces has the correct homotopy type — classically false in naive simplicial models but true up to equivalence.

### Equivalences and the univalence axiom

A function `f : A → B` is an **equivalence** when it has contractible fibers:

```haskell
isEquiv f = Π (y : B), isContr (Σ (x : A), f x = y)
```

and we write `A ≃ B` for `Σ (f : A → B), isEquiv f`. This definition — due to Voevodsky — is well-behaved in a way that the naive "quasi-inverse" notion (a function with left and right inverses) is not: in the presence of univalence, the type of equivalences becomes the *correct* object, equivalent to the identity type of the universe [1][2].

Univalence has several equivalent formulations: that `idtoeqv` is an equivalence; that the universe `U` is an object classifier; or that the total space `Σ (B : U), A ≃ B` is contractible. Its consistency was first established by Voevodsky's model in Kan simplicial sets, where the universes are interpreted by a universal Kan fibration, and it was shown in detail by Kapulkin and Lumsdaine that Martin-Löf type theory with one univalent universe is at least as consistent as ZFC with two inaccessible cardinals [2].

### h-Levels and n-types

Voevodsky's stratification of types by **h-level** (homotopy level) organizes the entire subject [1]:

| h-level | Name | Characterization |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Contractible | `Σ (c : A), Π (x : A), c = x` |
| 1 | Mere proposition | `Π (x y : A), x = y` |
| 2 | Set | identity types are propositions |
| 3 | 1-type | identity types are sets |
| n+2 | n-type | identity types are (n−1)-types |
| ∞ | ∞-groupoid | no truncation |

This tower (Figure 4) explains why univalence is so disruptive: in classical mathematics with UIP, *every* type is a set (h-level 2). Univalence forces the universe `U` itself to be a proper ∞-groupoid, since `A =_U B` can have the full structure of `A ≃ B` [1].

### Higher inductive types

Ordinary inductive types are freely generated by *point* constructors. A **higher inductive type** (HIT) additionally admits *path* constructors: generators of identity types, subject to the same universal property. The circle is the canonical example:

```haskell
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base = base
```

The induction principle states that to define a section of `P : S¹ → Type`, it suffices to give `b : P base` and a *path* `l : transport^P loop b = b` — the higher constructor generates data, not just syntax [1][4]. HITs are the synthetic counterpart of cell complexes: spheres, suspensions, pushouts, truncations, quotients, and the torus all admit direct definitions that would classically require point-set topology [1][5].

##3 Methodology

This survey proceeds by **comparative semantic analysis** of three generations of models for univalent foundations, evaluated against four criteria:

1. **Consistency**: does the model validate univalence without classical choice?
2. **Constructivity**: does univalence compute — i.e., does the theory enjoy canonicity (every closed term of `ℕ` reduces to a numeral)?
3. **Higher inductives**: are HITs supported with judgmental computation rules, or merely asserted axiomatically?
4. **Mechanization**: can the theory be implemented in a proof assistant with decidable type checking?

The models compared are the *simplicial* model of Kapulkin–Lumsdaine–Voevodsky [2], the *cubical sets* model of Bezem–Coquand–Huber and its internal type theory of Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg [3], and the *internal topos* axiomatization of Orton–Pitts [6]. For synthetic homotopy theory, we follow the "encode-decode" methodology pioneered by Licata and Shulman [4], in which a type family over a HIT serves as a synthetic universal cover, and path induction computes the total space.

We treat canonicity and normalization results as the empirical core of the subject — the "experiments" of proof theory — and assess open problems by the criterion of what remains unmechanized.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Univalence Axiom and Its Consequences

Univalence is often summarized as "equivalence is equivalent to equality," but its logical force is better understood through its *corollaries*. The most immediate is **function extensionality**: from `ua : (A ≃ B) → (A = B)` and the fact that equivalences are preserved under Π-types, one derives that pointwise-equal functions are equal — a principle independent of Martin-Löf type theory and unprovable without univalence or an equivalent axiom [1][2].

More subtly, univalence implies **propositional extensionality** (logically equivalent propositions are equal) and the principle of **unique choice** in its correct homotopical form. It also yields the **structure identity principle**: for any notion of structure definable in type theory (groups, rings, categories), isomorphic structures are *identified*, so all properties are automatically isomorphism-invariant [1]. This is the formal content of mathematical structuralism.

> **Theorem:** (Structure Identity Principle) Let `Str : U → U` be a standard notion of structure. Then for `A, B : Σ (X : U), Str X`, we have `(A = B) ≃ (A ≅ B)` where `≅` denotes structure-preserving equivalence.

The proof proceeds by characterizing the identity type of Σ-types and applying univalence to the underlying types [1].

A crucial subtlety: univalence is *inconsistent with UIP*. If every identity type were a proposition, then `(A =_U B)` would be a proposition, but `(A ≃ B)` is generally not — e.g., there are two distinct self-equivalences of the two-element type. Hence HoTT *requires* the higher groupoid structure; the axiom cannot be grafted onto extensional type theory [1].

### 4.2 Cubical Type Theory: Making Univalence Compute

The original formulation of univalence is an *axiom*: it asserts the existence of an inverse to `idtoeqv` without providing computational rules. Consequently, in Book HoTT, a closed term like `transport (ua e) 0 : ℕ` is *stuck* — it does not reduce to a numeral, breaking canonicity. The **cubical** approach resolves this by redesigning the identity type itself [3].

In cubical type theory, paths are *functions out of an interval*: a path `p : Path A a b` is a term of an interval-indexed family with `p 0 ≡ a` and `p 1 ≡ b` judgmentally. The theory is interpreted in **cubical sets** — presheaves on the cube category — where the Kan filling condition is built into the notion of *fibrant* type [3][6].

Two innovations carry the computational content:

1. **Composition and transport.** Every type comes with operations `comp` (composition of paths, filling open boxes) and `transp` (transport along lines in the universe). These generalize `J` and are *defined by recursion on the type*, giving them computational behavior at each type former [3].

2. **Glueing.** Voevodsky's insight, internalized: given `A : U`, a partial family `B` defined on a cofibrant proposition `φ`, and an equivalence `e : B ≃ A` on `φ`, the type `Glue [φ ↦ (B, e)] A` extends `B` to a total type equivalent to `A`. Univalence is then *proved* by constructing, for `e : A ≃ B`, a line `ua e : Path U A B` via glueing, with the computation rule that transporting along `ua e` applies `e` [3].

![Kan filling cube](ths_1789029000000_05280-2.webp)

The payoff is **canonicity**: Huber proved that every closed term of type `ℕ` in cubical type theory evaluates to a numeral `suc^k 0` [3]. This was later strengthened to *normalization* results for cubical type theory, giving decidable judgmental equality and a workable proof assistant — Cubical Agda — in which univalence computes silently [5].

The Orton–Pitts analysis [6] clarifies *why* this works: uniform Kan filling can be axiomatized internally in any topos with an interval object satisfying modest axioms, isolating the exact categorical content of the cubical construction from the specifics of any cube category.

### 4.3 Higher Inductive Types, Constructively

In Book HoTT, HITs are specified by *axioms*: the point constructors compute judgmentally, but the path constructors' computation rules are asserted only propositionally [1]. This is unsatisfying both philosophically and practically — the elimination rule for the circle should *compute* on `loop`, not merely transport along an axiom.

Cubical type theory repairs this. Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg give a schema for HITs — covering spheres, the torus, suspensions, propositional truncations, and pushouts — in which **all constructors, including higher ones, have judgmental computation rules**, and universes are closed under the new type formers [5]. The semantics is expressed in a presheaf topos with structure inspired by cubical sets, via initial algebras for the corresponding higher signatures.

The circle illustrates the pattern. Its eliminator in cubical style:

```haskell
elim : (P : S¹ → Type) (b : P base)
     → (l : PathP (λ i → P (loop i)) b b)
     → (x : S¹) → P x
elim P b l base       = b
elim P b l (loop i)   = l i
```

where `PathP` is the *heterogeneous* path type over the line `loop : Path S¹ base base` [5]. The second clause is a genuine computation rule: applying the eliminator to the path constructor reduces definitionally. This is what makes synthetic homotopy theory *run*.

### 4.4 Synthetic Homotopy Theory: The Encode-Decode Method

*Synthetic* homotopy theory does homotopy theory *inside* type theory, taking spaces, points, paths, and homotopies as primitive and deducing consequences logically — in deliberate analogy to synthetic geometry's treatment of points and lines [4]. The flagship result is the computation of the fundamental group of the circle:

> **Theorem:** `π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ`.

The proof by Licata and Shulman [4] is the methodological template for the field. Define a type family `code : S¹ → U` by circle recursion:

- `code base ≡ ℤ` (judgmentally, in the cubical setting),
- `ap code loop = ua (succEquiv)`, where `succEquiv : ℤ ≃ ℤ` is the successor equivalence.

Then `encode : Π (x : S¹), (base = x) → code x` is defined by path induction, and `decode : Π (x : S¹), code x → (base = x)` by circle induction, using the fact that transporting along `loop` acts as successor on `ℤ` [4]. The composite `decode ∘ encode` is the identity by path induction; `encode ∘ decode` by circle induction, exploiting that `ℤ` is a set. Hence `(base =_{S¹} base) ≃ ℤ`, and 0-truncating gives the group isomorphism `π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ` [1][4].

This **encode-decode** pattern generalizes: it computes loop spaces of suspensions, proves the Freudenthal suspension theorem, constructs the Hopf fibration `S¹ → S³ → S²` synthetically, and — in Brunerie's landmark thesis work — shows `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ` by a fully synthetic argument whose *numeral* was later extracted by machine computation in cubical proof assistants. The striking feature is that these are *proofs*, checkable by a kernel of a few thousand lines, of theorems that classically require spectral sequences.

### 4.5 Models in Higher Toposes and the Semantic Frontier

The simplicial model [2] interprets HoTT in the archetypal ∞-topos of spaces. But the semantics has since been vastly generalized. Shulman showed that **every** Grothendieck ∞-topos admits a model of HoTT with strict univalent universes [7], and subsequent work extends this to all elementary ∞-toposes. The univalence axiom thus turns out to be the *internal language* of higher topos theory — the precise sense in which HoTT is a "logic of homotopy."

The cubical and higher-topos stories are converging: cubical sets with the appropriate model structure present the ∞-topos of spaces, and current research aims at a *directed* or *modal* synthesis — cubical type theories internal to arbitrary ∞-toposes, with modalities controlling cohesion, discreteness, and truncation. The frontier is a type theory that is simultaneously a programming language (canonicity, decidable checking), a foundation (univalence, HITs), and the internal logic of geometry.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

The "experiments" of this field are mechanized proofs and metatheorems. We summarize the key results as a ledger of what has been *demonstrated*, not merely conjectured:

1. **Consistency of univalence.** The simplicial model [2] gives relative consistency of MLTT + univalence against ZFC + 2 inaccessibles. This is the bedrock result: univalence does not collapse the theory.

2. **Canonicity for cubical type theory.** Huber's theorem [3]: every closed `n : ℕ` reduces to a numeral. Corollary: univalence *computes*; `transport (ua e)` has operational behavior. This was the experiment that falsified the "axiom" view of univalence.

3. **Normalization and decidability.** Subsequent work (Sterling, Angiuli, Gratzer and others) established normalization for cubical type theory, hence decidable type checking — the prerequisite for Cubical Agda as a usable proof assistant [5][6].

4. **π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ, mechanized.** The Licata–Shulman proof [4] has been formalized in Coq-HoTT, Agda, and Lean; it is the "hello world" regression test of every HoTT implementation.

5. **π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ.** Brunerie's synthetic proof, with the integer `n = 2` extracted by *evaluating* the proof term in a cubical implementation — arguably the first theorem of "experimental synthetic homotopy theory," where the computer both checks the proof and computes the invariant.

6. **All ∞-toposes model HoTT.** Shulman's strict univalent universes in every Grothendieck ∞-topos [7], later extended to elementary ∞-toposes: univalence is not an artifact of spaces but the logic of higher topoi.

> **Theorem:** (Canonicity, cubical) If `⊢ t : ℕ` in cubical type theory, then `t ⇓ suc^k 0` for some `k ∈ ℕ`.

The proof proceeds by a sconing / gluing logical-relations argument over the cubical sets model, showing every closed term is computable [3].

##6 Limitations

The theory is not without genuine limitations, and a survey should state them plainly.

- **Performance of univalence.** Although `ua` computes in cubical implementations, the *constants* are poor: transporting along complex equivalences can be exponentially slower than hand-written code, and Cubical Agda proofs pay a real performance tax versus axiomatic Book-HoTT style. Canonicity is a qualitative, not quantitative, guarantee.

- **HIT schema generality.** The cubical HIT schema [5] covers an important class (spheres, tori, suspensions, truncations, pushouts), but a fully general schema for *higher inductive-inductive* and *higher coinductive* types with judgmental computation rules remains incomplete. Each new schema requires fresh semantic justification.

- **Classical reasoning is unavailable.** Univalence refutes UIP but is compatible with the law of excluded middle only in restricted forms; full classical logic plus univalence collapses the higher structure in ways that trivialize synthetic homotopy theory. Constructive discipline is mandatory, which excludes large bodies of classical argument.

- **The simplicial model is non-constructive.** The Kapulkin–Lumsdaine–Voevodsky model [2] uses classical logic essentially (e.g., in constructing the universal Kan fibration), so it establishes consistency but sheds no light on computation. The two semantics — classical/simplicial and constructive/cubical — have not been fully reconciled at the level of models.

- **Coherence and strictness.** Interpreting the syntax in ∞-categorical models requires solving coherence problems (strictification of weakly stable structure) that are technically formidable; Shulman's universes [7] address this for universes, but a complete coherence theorem for all of Book HoTT in all models is still open.

- **Truncation erases information.** Synthetic proofs frequently 0-truncate to obtain classical statements (e.g., `π₁` as a *group*), discarding the higher structure that univalence provides. Recovering classical theorems often means deliberately forgetting the synthetic content — an awkward fit.

##7 Conclusion

Homotopy type theory began as a philosophical proposal — that equality should mean equivalence — and matured into a threefold achievement: a *foundation* in which structuralism is a theorem [1][2]; a *programming language* in which univalence computes [3][5]; and a *practice* in which homotopy-theoretic proofs are written synthetically and checked by machine [4]. The cubical realization resolved the central tension of the early theory, turning an axiom into a construction with a normalizing operational semantics [3][6]. The ∞-topos semantics [7] revealed univalence as the internal logic of higher geometry rather than an ad hoc postulate.

What remains is consolidation and extension: general HIT schemas with full computation rules, quantitative control of the cost of univalence, directed and modal variants for synthetic (∞,1)-category theory, and the long-term question of whether a univalent proof assistant can rival classical systems in the breadth of formalized mathematics. The evidence so far — from `π₁(S¹)` to `π₄(S³)`, from canonicity to every ∞-topos — suggests the foundations are sound. The task now is to build on them.
