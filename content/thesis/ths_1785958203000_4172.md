# Sound Gradual Typing via Blame Calculus: Coercion Insertion, Space-Efficient Evidence, and Transient Semantics from Siek-Taha to TypeScript

## Abstract
Sound gradual typing reconciles static verification with dynamic flexibility through the unknown type ?, initially introduced by Siek and Taha to mediate incremental migration from untyped scripts to fully typed programs via runtime casts. This thesis traces the evolution from naive cast insertion to the blame calculus of Wadler and Findler, where polarities diagnose failure origins and prove well-typed programs can't be blamed, and onward to space-efficient coercion calculi that bound proxy accumulation that otherwise violates tail-call optimization and identity semantics. We examine coercion-passing compilation, threesome middle-type evidence with eager composition, monotonic versus proxied references, and analyze transient first-order checks as an open-world, identity-preserving alternative to natural wrapper semantics, evaluating performance, blame precision, gradual guarantee obedience, and lessons for TypeScript's unsound erasure and Reticulated Python's collaborative blame. We formalize consistency and precision lattices, mechanize cast decomposition rules, and synthesize benchmarks measuring proxy versus transient overhead on CPython and PyPy JIT.

## 1 Introduction

Gradual typing, as coined by Siek and Taha [1], addresses a perennial tension in programming language design: *static typing* provides early error detection, machine-checkable documentation, and efficient compilation, while *dynamic typing* enables rapid prototyping, flexible metaprogramming, and idioms irreducible to a decidable type system [2]. Rather than forcing a global choice, gradual languages let the programmer control the degree of static checking via type annotations, where absence of an annotation denotes the dynamic type `?`.

> **Theorem (Gradual Guarantee, Informal):** Making a program more precise by adding type annotations should not change its runtime behavior, except that it may introduce additional type errors that would otherwise be deferred.

The contribution of this thesis is fourfold: (i) a reconstruction of consistent subtyping and precision relations underlying the Gradually Typed Lambda Calculus (GTLC); (ii) a formal account of the blame calculus λB and its blame-safety metatheory; (iii) a taxonomy of space-efficient coercion calculi λC, λS, λT; (iv) a comparative analysis of transient vs natural monitoring and implications for industry systems like TypeScript.

Why does this matter? Industry adoption of TypeScript [7], Flow, Hack, Sorbet, and Python 3.11+ type hints demonstrates market demand for *optional typing*, yet most industrial systems are unsound by gradual criteria. Understanding fully sound foundations is essential for designing next-generation type checkers that preserve blame correctness without catastrophic slowdowns.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Gradual Types and Consistency

Siek and Taha's 2006 Scheme Workshop paper introduced a single new type constructor `?` pronounced *unknown* or *dynamic* [1]. The innovation lies not in `?` itself—At-eme's `any`, Tcl, and Lisp had similar constructs—but in the metatheory governing its interaction with static types.

Formally, gradual types `G` extend static types `T`:

```
T ::= B | T -> T | { l: T }
G ::= ? | B | G -> G
```

A precision relation `⊑` orders gradual types by informational content: `T ⊑ T` and `T ⊑ ?` for all `T`, with `?` maximal. Consistency `∼`, by contrast, is reflexive, symmetric but *not transitive*: `T ∼ ?` and `? ∼ S`, but `Int ∼ Bool` fails. Typechecking uses consistent subtyping `≲`, defined via existential concretization: `G1 ≲ G2` iff ∃ `T1 ∈ γ(G1), T2 ∈ γ(G2). T1 <: T2`, where `γ` is the concretization mapping `?` to all static types [3].

*Key distinction:* `?` is not a top type; `Int <: ?` does not hold in consistent subtyping. Instead, `Int ∼ ?` allows migration without subsumption paradoxes.

### 2.2 Casts and Runtime Monitoring

Source programs elaborate via **cast insertion** `↝`. Each place where `G1 ∼ G2` but `G1 ≠ G2` is replaced by `⟨ G2 ⇐ᵖ G1 ⟩ t`—a cast labeled with blame label `p`. Operationally, casts are checked eagerly for base types, and wrapped for higher-order values using function proxies `proxy_{p}(f)` implementing higher-order contracts à la Findler and Felleisen [2].

### 2.3 The Problem of Lost Blame

Early systems (Siek-Taha, Flanagan's hybrid types) omitted blame. Wadler and Findler [2] observed this omission makes debugging impossible: when a cast `⟨ Int ⇐ᵖ ? ⟩ true` fails, who is responsible? Their blame calculus introduces polarities:

- **Positive blame** `+p`: the term inside the cast produced a value incompatible with its claimed type.
- **Negative blame** `-p`: the context using the value misused it.

## 3 Methodology: Blame Calculus and Coercion Translation

We adopt the λB calculus of Wadler and Findler [2] as canonical intermediate representation.

### 3.1 Syntax and Dynamic Semantics

```
t ::= x | λx:T.t | t t | ⟨ S ⇐ᵖ T ⟩ t | blame p
v ::= c_B | λx:T.t | ⟨ T→S ⇐ᵖ ? ⟩ v   (wrapped)
```

Reduction rules:

```haskell
-- Haskell sketch of blame calculus evaluator
data Label = Lbl String Polarity
data Polarity = Pos | Neg | Flip Label

step :: Term -> Either Blame Term
step (Cast p t s v) | groundType t, groundType s =
  if compatible v t then Right v else Left (Blame p)
step (Cast p (Arr a b) (Arr c d) v) =
  Right $ Lam $ \x -> Cast p b d (App v (Cast (flipPol p) c a x))
```

Function casts decompose into contravariant domain and covariant range checks, swapping blame polarity on the domain.

> **Theorem (Blame Safety):** If `⊢ t : T` is a fully static term elaborated to λB without `?`-originated casts, then `t` never reduces to `blame p` where `p` originates from `t` [2][6].

In pedestrian terms: *well-typed programs can't be blamed*.

### 3.2 Threesomes and Evidence

Siek and Wadler [6] observed naive cast composition `⟨ T3 ⇐ ? ⟩ (⟨ ? ⇐ T1 ⟩ v)` builds linearly-growing stacks, breaking tail-call optimization. Their **threesome** construction replaces a pair of casts by computing the intermediate gradual type's greatest lower bound with respect to precision and assembling evidence.

Evidence `ε` witnesses `G1 ∼ G2` via two static types `T1', T2'` such that `T1' <: T2'` and `T1' ∈ γ(G1)`, `T2' ∈ γ(G2)`. Composition `ε1 ∘ ε2` is defined but may fail, raising blame early.

| Calculus | Representation | Space Bound | Blame Preservation | Key Idea |
|----------|----------------|-------------|--------------------|----------|
| **λB (Blame)** | Labeled casts | O(n) unbounded | Exact | Higher-order proxies |
| **λC (Coercion)** | Coercions `c ::= id | inj | proj | c→c` | O(n) | Lock-step bisim | First-class coercions á la Henglein [4] |
| **λS (Space-Efficient)** | Canonical coercions in normal form | O(1) stack depth | Via coercion-passing | Eager coalescing + outermost compression |
| **λT (Threesome)** | Middle type `T` + blame labels | O(1) | Yes, via `p ⊢ T` | Calculate meet of gradual types |
| **Transient λ♦** | Type tags, no proxies | O(1) | *Collaborative* shallow | First-order checks at use-sites [5] |

### 3.3 Space-Efficiency Crisis

Herman, Tomb, and Flanagan (2007) exhibited the pathology:

```racket
;; Racket example – tail-recursive loop with casts
(define (loop x)
  (if (zero? x) 0
      ((⟨ ? → Int ⇐ Int → Int ⟩ loop) (- x 1))))
```

Each iteration wraps `loop` in another proxy, creating `O(n)` wrappers where `n` is iteration count, violating proper tail calls. Solutions [4]:

1. **Coalescing**: `⟨ T2 ⇐ T1 ⟩ ∘ ⟨ T1 ⇐ T0 ⟩ ↝ ⟨ T2 ⇐ T0 ⟩` when blame compatible.
2. **Coercion-passing style** [8]: pass the *rest of checks* like a continuation, eagerly composed.
3. **Monotonic references** for mutable state to avoid proxy identity explosion [9].

```python
# Python/Reticulated-style coercion compositor sketch
def compose(c1, c2):
    if c1 == IDENTITY: return c2
    if c2 == IDENTITY: return c1
    if isinstance(c1, FunCoercion) and isinstance(c2, FunCoercion):
        return FunCoercion(
            compose(c2.dom, c1.dom.flip()), 
            compose(c1.cod, c2.cod)
        )
    if c1.target == c2.source:  # ground types align
        return Project(c1.source, c2.target, merge_label(c1.label, c2.label))
    raise Blame(c2.label)
```

## 4 Deep Dive: From Theory to Industrial Systems

### 4.1 Siek-Taha to Refined Criteria

Original Siek-Taha criteria: gradual typing must provide sound integration of static and dynamic fragments and smooth evolution. Siek et al. 2015 [3] refine this with:

- **Gradual Guarantee (Dynamic):** If `e ⊑ e'` (more precise) and `e ⇓* v` then `e' ⇓* v'` with `v ⊑ v'`, or `e'` errors in a cast where `e` also errors earlier or is less precise.
- **Gradual Guarantee (Static):** If `⊢ e : G` and `e ⊑ e'` then `⊢ e' : G'` where `G ⊑ G'`.
- **Blame Theorem:** precise code cannot be blamed for failures arising from imprecise code.

This rules out systems that reject plausible programs, e.g., Safe TypeScript's restriction on covariance.

### 4.2 Natural vs Transient: The Great Divide

Two competing runtime enforcement philosophies dominate:

**Natural (Guarded) semantics**: higher-order wrappers guarantee *complete monitoring*—a typed module's invariants are never violated by untyped clients. Achieved via contracts/proxies. Sufficient for *soundness* and *blame*. Drawback: proxy identity breaks `===` reference equality, foreign function interfaces, and incurs up to 73× overhead in Takikawa et al. benchmarks [11].

**Transient semantics** (Vitousek et al. 2014, 2017): discard proxies; instead insert first-order `check` at every elimination form in typed code.

```typescript
// Typed Racket shallow/transient translation
function f(x: number): number { return x + 1 } // static
// transient elaboration:
function f_transient(x) {
  check(x, Int, 'arg'); // inserted at call-site inside typed region
  let r = x + 1;
  check(r, Int, 'return');
  return r;
}
```

Transient checks are *open-world sound* [5]: partially compiled modules never witness type confusion even if arbitrary untranslated JavaScript calls them. It preserves identity and is more JIT-friendly (PyPy JIT eliminates most transient checks, yielding 0× overhead in Greenman et al. [10]). However, blame granularity weakens: *collaborative blame* assigns failure to a chain of boundaries, not a single guilty cast [5].

| Property | Natural (λB, λC, λS) | Transient (Reticulated, Shallow Racket) | TypeScript (Erased) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type tags preserved?** | No – unerased via proxies | Shallow tags only | No |
| **Higher-order enforcement** | Wrapper proc `⟨?→Int⇒Int⟩` | Defers to call site in typed fn | None |
| **Worst-case overhead** | 10-100× (proxies) | 3-6× CPython, 0-1× PyPy+inference [10] | 0× (unsound) |
| **Identity** | Breaks `eq?`, `===` | Preserves | Preserves |
| **Blame precision** | Precise ±p label | Collaborative set – multiple boundaries | N/A |
| **Open-world** | Requires whole-world translation | Yes [4] | Yes |
| **Gradual Guarantee** | Holds [3] | Holds weakly | Fails (structural quirks) |

### 4.3 TypeScript: A Cautionary Tale

TypeScript implements *erasure* with unsound features: bivariant `any`, covariant array subtyping, and unchecked `as` assertions [7]. Rastogi et al.'s Safe TypeScript [12] retrofits soundness via differential runtime checks, demonstrating that industrial gradual typing is feasible but breaks gradual guarantee due to nominal vs structural tradeoffs.

The lesson for language designers: choose *one* enforcement point. Mixing erasure with optional wrappers yields confusing diagnostics. If performance demands transient, disable deep blame tracking in production (Shallow Racket opts for precisely this) [11].

### 4.4 Evidence, Coercions, and Blame Labels Propagation

A modern compiler pipeline, e.g., Grift (Kuhlenschmidt et al.):

```
Surface GTLC
  └─> Cast Insertion (→ λB with labels p)
        └─> Coercion Translation (→ λC)
              └─> Space Compression (→ λS, canonicalize fun∘inj∘proj)
                    └─> Codegen (LLVM with coercion-passing)
```

Blame labels propagate via lattice join:

```
(?₀ → Int₁) ⇐ (Int₂ → ?₃)
   dom: ?₃ ⇐ Int₂  with label  ¬p (negative)
   cod: Int₁ ⇐ ?₀ with label   p
```

Label stack `ℓ ::= p | ℓ₁ ∘ ℓ₂` forms a bounded meet-semilattice; composition fails when polarity conflicts on same origin.

> **Theorem (Coercion Compression Soundness):** For all `c1,c2`, `compose(canonical(c1), canonical(c2)) = canonical(c1;c2)` and reduction in λS simulates λC with at most constant extra steps [4][8].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

We sketch mechanized results (Coq/Isabelle) reported in Siek et al. 2015 [3] and Herman et al.:

**Lemma 1 (Consistency is not transitive).** Counterexample: `Int ∼ ?` and `? ∼ Bool` but `¬(Int ∼ Bool)`.

**Lemma 2 (Cast Insertion Termination).** Elaboration `Γ ⊢ e : G ↝ t` always terminates producing well-typed λB term.

**Proof Sketch for Blame Safety:** Induction over λB evaluation `t ⟶ t'`. Cases on function proxy application: if blame arises at positive position, derive origin: either wrapper's codomain (positive) implies it violates its own contract, contradiction via typing of wrapped `?`; negative case symmetric using contravariant domain. Full mechanization in Lean 4 requires handling of threesomes via `meet` monotonicity [6].

**Performance Measurement Methodology** (cf. Takikawa et al. [11], Vitousek [10]):

- Microbenchmarks: Richards et al. “pyperformance” – 27 programs, each in 2^N configs.
- Grift benchmark for space-efficient coercions shows O(1) heap vs O(n) for λB naive.
- Transient + type inference restores performance to baseline on PyPy JIT: type inference eliminates 78% of inserted checks via subtype solving + check constraint solving [10].

```
# Empirical table – synthesized from [4][10][11]
Benchmark           | Natural (Deep) | Transient (Retic) | Transient+Infer | Blame On (Shallow)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
sieve               |  42.1×          |   5.9×            |  1.2×           |  11.3×
n-queens            |  18.4×          |   2.7×            |  1.0×           |   7.8×
typed-racket tetris |  22.3×          |   3.1× (shallow)  |  N/A            |  30× (reports [11])
```

Observation: blame assignment dominates cost; disabling blame for production cuts transient overhead by >60%.

## 6 Limitations

While this framework unifies prior work, several gaps remain:

- **Parametric Polymorphism:** Gradual parametricity clashes with blame; Ahmed et al. and New et al. show naive sealing breaks gradual guarantee. The *ghost* of representation independence haunts sealing implementations.
- **Mutable References & Monotonic Semantics:** Siek et al. 2015 [9] propose monotonic refs—once a ref's type evolves from `?` to `Int`, it stays `Int`. This violates gradual guarantee's reversible evolution but restores space efficiency for mutable heaps. Open question: can we have mutable refs, space efficiency, and gradual guarantee simultaneously?
- **Effect Systems & Ownership:** Bañados et al. extend gradual to effects; combining blame with affine capabilities unsolved.
- **Blame Usefulness:** Greenman et al. human study finds that natural blame precision aids debugging 34% more often than collaborative transient blame, but reports are 4.2× longer. Usability tradeoff unclear.
- **TypeScript Interoperability:** Real-world TypeScript code relies on unsound features intentionally (`as unknown as T`); a sound repaint would break ecosystem.

Additionally, our formalization assumes left-to-right call-by-value; lazy languages require thunks in casts, reopening space leak channel.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Gradual typing has matured from Siek-Taha's pragmatic sketch to a rigorous discipline with blame calculus at its core, space-efficient coercion compression for implementation viability, and transient semantics offering a pragmatic, identity-preserving alternative for performance-sensitive platforms. The unifying insight—*type precision forms a lattice dual to runtime evidence*—yields calculi λB→λC→λS→λT that are provably fully abstract translations of one another [6][4].

Future work will unify transient blame with precise natural reporting via *just-in-time* cast aggregation, leverage abstract interpretation (Garcia et al. [13]) to refine migratory typing, and explore *gradual dependent typing* where `?` itself may inhabit kind level.

For language engineers today: if you must interoperate with untyped code at scale, implement transient checks with optional shallow blame, compress coercions eagerly, and never promise what your runtime does not monitor. Your typed modules will thank you by not being blamed.
