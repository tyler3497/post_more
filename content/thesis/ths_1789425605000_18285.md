# Functoriality and Spectral Geometry: The Langlands Program from Automorphic Forms to the Geometric Correspondence

## Abstract

The Langlands program proposes a deep dictionary between the arithmetic of Galois representations and the harmonic analysis of automorphic forms, mediated by the twin principles of functoriality and reciprocity. This essay traces the program from its classical foundations — the conjectures Robert Langlands communicated to André Weil in 1967 — through the analytic machinery of the Arthur–Selberg trace formula, the theory of endoscopy, and the Beyond Endoscopy strategy for functoriality in full generality, to the geometric Langlands correspondence, which recasts the entire picture as a duality of categories over moduli spaces of bundles on algebraic curves. We explain how automorphic L-functions provide the invariant backbone of functoriality, how Arthur's endoscopic classification settled functoriality for classical groups, how quantization of the Hitchin integrable system produced Hecke eigensheaves, and how the recent proof of the de Rham categorical conjecture reshapes the field. The essay closes with the program's outstanding limitations and its open frontiers.

## 1 Introduction: The Unification Dream of Modern Number Theory

In January 1967, Robert Langlands, then a young professor at Princeton, wrote a seventeen-page letter to André Weil. The letter contained a set of conjectures so sweeping that they reorganized the mental map of twentieth-century mathematics. The **Langlands program**, as it came to be known, proposed a precise correspondence between two apparently unrelated domains: on one side, the Galois theory of number fields, with its representations of absolute Galois groups and their Artin L-functions; on the other, the harmonic analysis of automorphic forms, with its cuspidal representations and their Langlands–Shahidi L-functions. The bridge between them was to be built from two principles — *reciprocity*, matching Galois representations against automorphic ones with identical L-functions, and *functoriality*, asserting that homomorphisms between Langlands dual groups induce transfers of automorphic representations [2][6].

The audacity of the program lay partly in what it subsumed. The Kronecker–Weber theorem, which describes the maximal abelian extension of the rationals, and the whole apparatus of **class field theory** emerge as the special case of the program for the group GL(1): the abelianization of the Galois group corresponds to Hecke characters, and Artin reciprocity becomes an instance of the general dictionary. What class field theory does for one-dimensional representations, the Langlands program promises for representations of arbitrary dimension and for arbitrary reductive groups.

The program's scope has since expanded far beyond its original arithmetic setting. Over function fields of curves over finite fields, the correspondence acquires a geometric flavor; over the complex numbers, it becomes the **geometric Langlands correspondence**, a duality of categories of sheaves on moduli stacks that has drawn in quantum field theory through the work of Kapustin and Witten on S-duality [1]. Three grand themes organize the modern subject:

1. **Reciprocity**: n-dimensional representations of Galois groups correspond to cuspidal automorphic representations of GL(n).
2. **Functoriality**: an L-homomorphism between dual groups transfers automorphic representations from one group to another.
3. **Geometrization**: over curves, the correspondence becomes an equivalence between D-modules on the moduli of bundles and sheaves on the moduli of local systems.

This essay surveys the classical foundations, the analytic engine of the trace formula and its endoscopic refinement, the Beyond Endoscopy strategy aimed at functoriality in full generality, and the geometric program culminating in the recently established categorical conjecture.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The adelic stage

Let F be a number field or a function field, and let A_F be its ring of adeles — the restricted product of all completions of F. For a connected reductive group G over F, the natural home of automorphic forms is the space of functions on the double quotient G(F) \ G(A_F). The Hilbert space L^2(G(F) \ G(A_F)) carries commuting actions of the group at every place, and its spectral decomposition is the central analytic problem of the subject. A **cuspidal automorphic representation** π is an irreducible constituent of the cuspidal subspace; by a theorem of Flath, it factorizes as a restricted tensor product π = ⊗_v π_v of local representations, one at each place v of F.

At almost every place, π_v is *unramified* and is determined by a semisimple conjugacy class in the complex dual group — its **Satake parameter**. The Hecke algebra acts on unramified vectors through these parameters, and the eigenvalues of Hecke operators are the raw spectral data from which L-functions are built.

### 2.2 Dual groups and Weil's Rosetta Stone

To every reductive group G, Langlands attached a complex reductive group ^L G, the **L-group**, constructed by dualizing the root datum of G and forming a semidirect product with the Galois or Weil group of F. The dual group of GL(n) is GL(n, C) itself; the dual of SL(n) is PGL(n, C); the dual of the symplectic group Sp(2n) is the odd orthogonal group SO(2n+1, C). Functoriality is the claim that homomorphisms of L-groups lift to transfers of automorphic representations — a principle of breathtaking economy that would unify base change, automorphic induction, the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence, and the theory of endoscopy under a single heading [5].

André Weil's famous analogy between number fields, function fields, and Riemann surfaces — his *Rosetta Stone* — organizes the three parallel realizations of the program:

| Number fields | Function fields (curves over finite fields) | Complex curves |
|---|---|---|
| Place v of F | Closed point x of X | Point x of a Riemann surface |
| Adele ring A_F | Adeles of the function field | Loop-group data |
| Ideal class group | Jacobian / Picard variety | Jacobian variety |
| Hecke character | Rank-one local system | Flat line bundle |
| Automorphic representation | Hecke eigensheaf (perverse sheaf) | D-module with Hecke property |
| Galois representation | l-adic local system | Flat connection / local system |

Each column speaks the same language with a different accent, and results proved in one column — notably the function-field case — have repeatedly illuminated the others [2][3].

### 2.3 Automorphic L-functions

To an automorphic representation π of G and a finite-dimensional representation r of ^L G, Langlands attached an **L-function** L(s, π, r) defined by an Euler product over the places of F. At unramified places the local factor is det(1 − r(c_v) q_v^{−s})^{−1}, where c_v is the Satake parameter; at ramified and archimedean places the factors are defined through the local Langlands correspondence and the Langlands–Shahidi method. The expected analytic properties — meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane and a functional equation relating s to 1 − s — generalize the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet L-functions. The following snippet illustrates the Euler-product mechanism at the level of the Riemann zeta function:

```python
import sympy as sp
from math import prod, pi

def euler_factor(p, s):
    """Unramified local factor of zeta(s) at the prime p."""
    return 1.0 / (1 - p ** (-s))

def partial_zeta(s, primes):
    return prod(euler_factor(p, s) for p in primes)

primes = list(sp.primerange(2, 500))
print(partial_zeta(2.0, primes))  # converges toward pi^2 / 6
print(pi ** 2 / 6)
```

These L-functions are the program's invariant backbone: two representations related by functoriality must have matching L-functions, and the analytic behavior of L-functions — especially the order of poles — is expected to detect functorial provenance. This is the germ of the Beyond Endoscopy strategy discussed in section 4.2 [5][8].

## 3 Methodology

Mathematicians attack the Langlands program through three interlocking strategies, each with its own characteristic tools:

1. **Analytic methods.** The trace formula compares a spectral expansion (automorphic representations) with a geometric expansion (orbital integrals over conjugacy classes). Converse theorems characterize functorial images by the analytic properties of twisted L-functions. This is the domain of Arthur's work and of Beyond Endoscopy [5][6].
2. **Local and representation-theoretic methods.** The local Langlands correspondence classifies representations of p-adic groups via parameters into the L-group; endoscopy and the stabilization of the trace formula organize global packets. This is where the fundamental lemma and Arthur's endoscopic classification live [5][6].
3. **Geometric methods.** Over curves, automorphic forms are replaced by sheaves on moduli stacks, and the correspondence becomes a statement about categories. The tools are D-modules, perverse sheaves, and the quantization of integrable systems — the route taken by Drinfeld, Beilinson, Frenkel, Gaitsgory, and Vilonen [1][3][4].

The three strategies are not independent. The function-field proof for GL(n) used geometry to prove an arithmetic theorem; the geometric program was in turn inspired by the arithmetic one; and the categorical formulation now feeds back into p-adic geometry through the Fargues–Scholze geometrization of the local correspondence.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Automorphic L-functions and the Principle of Functoriality

Functoriality is the program's central conjecture. Let H and G be reductive groups over F, and let ξ: ^L H → ^L G be an L-homomorphism. Then every automorphic representation σ of H should admit a **functorial transfer** π = ξ_∗(σ), an automorphic representation of G characterized by the identity of L-functions L(s, π, r) = L(s, σ, r ∘ ξ) for all representations r of ^L G. At unramified places this is transparent: the Satake parameter of π_v is simply ξ applied to the Satake parameter of σ_v. The difficulty is entirely global — proving that the naive local transfer assembles into a genuine automorphic representation.

The principle can be expressed in a type-theoretic idiom that makes its functorial character explicit:

```haskell
-- Functoriality as a structure-preserving transfer
data Dual (g :: *)            -- the complex Langlands dual group
data LHom h g                 -- an L-homomorphism ^L H -> ^L G

-- Transfer of an unramified local representation along xi:
-- apply xi to the Satake parameter, then rebuild the representation.
transfer :: LHom h g -> Unramified h -> Unramified g
transfer xi piH = Unramified (xi `applyTo` satakeParameter piH)
```

Many deep theorems are instances of functoriality. The **Jacquet–Langlands correspondence** transfers representations between inner forms of GL(n) — for example, between a quaternion division algebra and GL(2) — and has recently been extended beyond the discrete spectrum via a functorial refinement of the Franke filtration [7]. **Base change** and **automorphic induction** for cyclic extensions, proved by Arthur and Clozel, are instances for restriction and induction of L-parameters. The **symmetric power liftings** — Gelbart and Jacquet's symmetric square, Kim and Shahidi's symmetric cube and fourth power — established cases of functoriality for the maps Sym^k: GL(2, C) → GL(k+1, C), with striking arithmetic consequences such as bounds toward the Ramanujan conjecture [2].

### 4.2 The Trace Formula, Endoscopy, and Beyond Endoscopy

The **Arthur–Selberg trace formula** is an identity between two expansions of the trace of a Hecke operator on L^2(G(F) \ G(A_F)): a *spectral* side, summing over automorphic representations, and a *geometric* side, summing orbital integrals over conjugacy classes. It is the non-abelian generalization of the Poisson summation formula, and nearly every global result in the subject passes through it [6].

To compare trace formulas on different groups — the essential step for functoriality — one must *stabilize* the geometric side. The discrepancy between conjugacy and stable conjugacy is measured by **endoscopic groups**: smaller quasisplit groups H whose dual groups embed into ^L G. The comparison yields transfer identities between orbital integrals, whose keystone is the **fundamental lemma**, proved by Ngô Bảo Châu in 2008. This machinery culminated in Arthur's **endoscopic classification** of the discrete spectrum of classical groups:

> **Theorem (Arthur's endoscopic classification, paraphrased).** The discrete automorphic spectrum of a quasisplit symplectic or special orthogonal group decomposes into global packets indexed by formal Arthur parameters ψ: L_F × SL(2, C) → ^L G, each packet being the functorial image of a packet on a suitable endoscopic group. In particular, functoriality holds for every L-homomorphism arising from classical groups [5][6].

Yet endoscopy handles only a restricted class of L-homomorphisms. For general functoriality, Langlands proposed **Beyond Endoscopy** around the year 2000: refine the *stable* trace formula by weighting each representation π on the spectral side by the order of the pole of L(s, π, r) at s = 1, for a fixed representation r of ^L G. Since that pole order detects which L-homomorphisms the parameter of π factors through, the refined formula would isolate precisely the functorial image of H in G. The difficulties are formidable — the proposal requires analytic control far beyond current technology — but concrete progress exists: Altuğ treated the case of GL(2) with the standard representation using Poisson summation over elliptic conjugacy classes, and recent work on stable transfer operators develops the local harmonic analysis the strategy demands [5][8]. As Arthur emphasizes, the study of Beyond Endoscopy is still in its early stages, and its full development will require new methods from both analytic and algebraic number theory [5].

### 4.3 Geometric Langlands: Hecke Eigensheaves and the Hitchin System

Over a smooth projective curve X over the complex numbers, the adelic double quotient is replaced by the moduli stack Bun_G of principal G-bundles on X, and automorphic functions are replaced by D-modules on this stack. The Hecke operators become **Hecke correspondences** — modifications of a bundle at a point of X — acting on sheaves by integral transforms. A **Hecke eigensheaf** is a D-module Aut_E on Bun_G satisfying H_V(Aut_E) ≃ Aut_E ⊠ E_V for every representation V of ^L G, where E is a ^L G-local system (a flat bundle) on X. This eigen-property is the geometric analogue of an automorphic form being a simultaneous eigenfunction of all Hecke operators, with the local system E playing the role of the Galois representation in the reciprocity dictionary [3][4].

The subject began with Drinfeld's 1983 proof for GL(1), a geometric analogue of class field theory, and was transformed by Beilinson and Drinfeld's **quantization of the Hitchin integrable system**. The Hitchin system describes the moduli of Higgs bundles — pairs of a bundle and a Higgs field — as an algebraically completely integrable system fibered over a base of spectral invariants. Beilinson and Drinfeld showed that quantizing this system produces the commuting differential operators whose joint eigenfunctions are the sought-after Hecke eigensheaves, at least for opers — special local systems with extra structure. For GL(n), the full correspondence was established by Frenkel, Gaitsgory, and Vilonen, as surveyed by Laumon [2][3][4].

A startling physical interpretation arrived with Kapustin and Witten, who derived the geometric Langlands correspondence from **S-duality** of four-dimensional supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory: the duality exchanging electric and magnetic charges in the gauge theory becomes, after compactification on the curve X, the duality exchanging a group G with its Langlands dual ^L G, and Hecke eigensheaves emerge as boundary conditions of the quantum field theory [1].

### 4.4 The Categorical Conjecture and Recent Breakthroughs

The mature form of the geometric program is **categorical**: it predicts an equivalence of triangulated categories

D-mod(Bun_G) ≃ QCoh(LocSys_{^L G}),

between D-modules on the moduli of G-bundles and quasi-coherent sheaves on the moduli of ^L G-local systems, intertwining the Hecke action on the left with tensor product on the right. Refining this statement — specifying the correct subcategories, the role of singular support, and the ramified variants — occupied the subject for two decades.

In 2024, this long arc reached a landmark: Gaitsgory and Raskin announced a proof of the **de Rham geometric Langlands conjecture**, establishing the categorical equivalence for de Rham local systems. The proof synthesizes the entire preceding development — the spectral decomposition of the automorphic category, the theory of factorization algebras, and a delicate analysis of the Hitchin fibration — and represents the deepest theorem yet proved in the geometric program [1][4]. Parallel developments in the Betti and étale settings, and the extension to ramified local systems, remain active frontiers.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

The program's conjectures are theorems in a growing list of cases. The principal milestones are:

1. **Local Langlands for GL(n).** Proved by Harris–Taylor and Henniart: irreducible admissible representations of GL(n) over a p-adic field are in natural bijection with n-dimensional representations of the Weil–Deligne group [2].
2. **Global Langlands for GL(n) over function fields.** Proved by Drinfeld for n = 2 and by Lafforgue for general n, using the geometry of moduli stacks of shtukas [2][3].
3. **The fundamental lemma.** Proved by Ngô Bảo Châu: the identities of orbital integrals required to stabilize the trace formula hold, unlocking endoscopy [5][6].
4. **Endoscopic classification of classical groups.** Proved by Arthur: functoriality and the internal structure of L-packets for quasisplit symplectic and orthogonal groups, conditional on the stabilization of the twisted trace formula [5][6].
5. **Geometric Langlands for GL(n).** Proved by Frenkel, Gaitsgory, and Vilonen: every irreducible rank-n local system on a complex curve admits a Hecke eigensheaf on the moduli of rank-n bundles [3][4]:

> **Theorem (Geometric Langlands for GL(n), paraphrased).** Let X be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and E an irreducible rank-n local system on X. Then there exists a perverse sheaf Aut_E on Bun_n, the moduli stack of rank-n bundles, satisfying the Hecke eigen-property with eigenvalue E [3][4].

6. **The de Rham categorical conjecture.** Proved by Gaitsgory and Raskin: the predicted equivalence between D-modules on Bun_G and sheaves on the moduli of de Rham ^L G-local systems holds [1].
7. **Functoriality beyond the discrete spectrum.** The Jacquet–Langlands correspondence has been extended past the discrete spectrum through a functorial refinement of the Franke filtration, giving the full predicted lift between inner forms of GL(n) [7].

Each of these results was once a conjecture of comparable difficulty to those that remain; their accumulation is the empirical record on which confidence in the full program rests.

## 6 Limitations

Despite this progress, the program's core conjectures remain open, and the obstacles are structural rather than merely technical:

- **General functoriality over number fields.** Beyond classical groups and symmetric powers, functoriality for exceptional groups — or even for the tensor-product maps of GL(n) × GL(m) beyond the known Rankin–Selberg cases — is wide open. Beyond Endoscopy offers a strategy but, as Arthur notes, its study is in its infancy [5][8].
- **The Ramanujan conjecture.** The expected optimal bounds on Hecke eigenvalues (equivalently, the temperedness of cuspidal representations at every place) are known only in special cases; the best general results still come from functoriality via symmetric powers.
- **Local Langlands for general groups.** The Fargues–Scholze geometrization of the local correspondence, using moduli of local shtukas and the stack of L-parameters, promises a uniform construction, but the full local conjecture with its refined desiderata remains incomplete.
- **Effectivity and computation.** The program is largely inexplicit: Satake parameters, functorial transfers, and Hecke eigensheaves are proved to exist far more often than they are computed. Algorithmic aspects — from explicit trace-formula computations to numerical verification of functoriality — lag far behind the theory.
- **The geometric program's remaining settings.** The Betti and étale forms of the categorical conjecture, and the ramified correspondence with wild ramification, are still being built.

These limitations define the research frontier: they are not refutations but invitations, each marking a place where the dictionary is still being written.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Sixty years after Langlands' letter to Weil, the program has grown from a set of conjectures about L-functions into a unifying vision spanning number theory, representation theory, algebraic geometry, and quantum field theory. The trace formula and endoscopy tamed functoriality for classical groups; quantization of the Hitchin system geometrized the correspondence; and the categorical equivalence, now proved in the de Rham setting, exhibits the Langlands duality as a structural feature of the mathematics of moduli spaces rather than a coincidence of L-functions.

What remains — general functoriality, the Ramanujan bounds, the full local correspondence — is correspondingly deeper. The Beyond Endoscopy program, in particular, asks for a genuinely new kind of analytic number theory, one in which L-functions are not merely invariants attached to representations but tools for dissecting the spectrum itself. Whether the next sixty years complete the dictionary or reveal that it was only the first chapter of a larger story, the Langlands program has already demonstrated its central thesis: that the deepest patterns in arithmetic are spectral patterns, and that the right dualities make them visible.
