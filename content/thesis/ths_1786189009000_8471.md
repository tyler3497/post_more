# Causal Representation Learning and Independent Causal Mechanisms: Disentanglement via Interventions, SCM Latent Identifiability

## Abstract
Statistical disentanglement aims to factorize observations into *independent* latent factors, yet **real-world generative factors are causally related**, not independent. Locatello et al. [6] proved unsupervised disentanglement is *impossible* without inductive biases, igniting the search for causal inductive priors. This thesis synthesizes **Causal Representation Learning (CRL)** under the **Independent Causal Mechanisms (ICM)** principle of Schölkopf et al. [1], where the world is modular: $p(z_i | pa_i)$ and $p(z_j | pa_j)$ carry no information about each other. We ask: *when can we recover latent causal variables $Z$ and their SCM $Z_i := f_i(PA_i, U_i)$ from high-dimensional $X = g(Z)$ and interventional data*? We review **CausalVAE** [7] and **ICM-VAE** [2] architectures with learnable flow-based diffeomorphic mechanisms, formalize **causal mechanism equivalence**, and prove **identifiability up to permutation and elementwise reparameterization** under *soft*, *hard multi-node*, and *unknown-target* interventions [3][5][4][8]. We connect sparse mechanism shift, grouping of observational variables, and nonparametric DAG recovery to modern deep generative modeling, providing algorithms and limits for building interventionally robust representations.

---

## 1. Introduction

Deep learning excels at capturing statistical dependence, but *correlation is not causation*. Variational autoencoders factorize $p(z) = \prod_i p(z_i)$, enforcing independence, while physical systems exhibit causal chains: *pendulum angle → shadow length*, *light position → shadow position* [2]. Treating coupled variables as independent yields brittleness under intervention.

**Causal Representation Learning** [1] posits:

$$ X = g(Z), \quad Z_i := f_i(Z_{pa_i}, U_i), \quad U_i \sim \prod P_{U_i}, \quad U_i \perp U_j $$

where $g$ is an unknown mixing function (possibly nonlinear), $\{f_i\}$ are **independent causal mechanisms** obeying ICM, and $U$ are exogenous noise. Goal: from $X$ and environments $E^{(k)}$ arising from $do(Z_I = \tilde{})$ or soft mechanism shifts $\tilde{p}(Z_i | Z_{pa_i})$, recover $(g, \mathcal{G}, \{f_i\})$.

> **Theorem:** Under the ICM Principle [1], the factorization $p(z) = \prod_i p(z_i | z_{pa_i})$ is not arbitrary but reflects physical modularity: intervening on $p(z_i | pa_i)$ does not affect $p(z_j | pa_j)$ for $i \neq j$. Identifiability of $Z$ therefore requires *mechanism-level* equivalence, not joint distributional equivalence.

Contributions of this thesis:

1. **Formal impossibility baseline**: unsupervised nonlinear ICA is unidentifiable [6]; causal supervision or interventions are necessary.
2. **New disentanglement notion**: *causal mechanism permutation equivalence* [2] replaces $\ell_2$ or permutation equivalence, requiring preservation of conditional mechanisms.
3. **Identifiability results**: continuum from linear-mixing + multi-node hard interventions [5] → soft interventions with polynomial mixing [3] → nonparametric mixing with unknown interventions [8] → grouping constraint without interventions [4].
4. **Architectures**: CausalVAE SCM layer [7] and ICM-VAE flow prior [2] with diffeomorphic $f_i$.
5. **Practical algorithms**: autoencoding variational Bayes with deep SCM, sparsity-exploiting recovery, and counterfactual generation via $do$.

We assume *no* temporal structure, *no* known graph necessarily, but *varying* causal mechanisms across domains.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries (with definitions)

### 2.1 Structural Causal Models and ICM

A **Structural Causal Model** $\mathcal{M} = \langle U, Z, F, P_U \rangle$ over DAG $\mathcal{G}$:

- $Z_i := f_i(Z_{pa_i}, U_i)$
- $U_i$ jointly independent
- Induced joint $p(z) = \prod_i p(z_i | pa_i)$ Markov w.r.t $\mathcal{G}$.

**Independent Causal Mechanisms Principle** [1][2]: The conditional $p(z_i | pa_i)$ contains no information about $p(z_j | pa_j)$ and $p(z_i | pa_i)$ algorithmic independence implies autonomy under interventions. Formalized via Kolmogorov complexity: $K(p(z_i|pa_i), p(z_j|pa_j)) \approx K(p(z_i|pa_i)) + K(p(z_j|pa_j))$.

**Sparse Mechanism Shift (SMS)** [1]: Distribution shifts $p^{(e)}$ across environments $e$ affect only sparse subsets of mechanisms.

### 2.2 Why Disentanglement Fails Unsupervised

Locatello et al. [6] proved:

> **Theorem (Impossibility):** For any factorized prior $p(z)=\prod p(z_i)$ and any $d>1$, there exists infinite family of bijective $h: Z \to \hat{Z}$ such that $\hat{Z}$ has identical marginal $p(x)$ under $g \circ h^{-1}$ but entangled coordinates. Hence unsupervised VAE cannot distinguish them from $p(x)$ alone.

This aligns with nonlinear ICA unidentifiability [Hyvärinen & Pajunen 1999].

*Causal fix*: require either **weak supervision** (labels $y \approx z_i$) or **interventional data** from $do$.

### 2.3 Mixing Models

| Mixing $g$ | Assumption | Identifiability enabler |
|---|---|---|
| Linear $X = A Z$, $A$ invertible | Linear ICA | Linear mixing disentangled by multi-node interventions [5] |
| Polynomial $X = Poly(Z)$ degree $p$ | Ahuja et al. 2022 class | Soft interventions + generalized faithfulness [3] |
| Nonparametric diffeomorphic $g$ invertible smooth | Generic $g$ continuous invertible | Unknown interventions + genericity condition [8] or grouping [4] |
| General nonlinear noninvertible | $Z \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $X\in \mathbb{R}^D$, $D>d$ injective | Single-node perfect interventions $K \ge 1$ per node [8] |

### 2.4 Causal Notions of Equivalence

Standard definition: $\theta \sim \hat{\theta}$ if $\exists$ permutation $\pi$ and elementwise diffeomorphism $h_i$ s.t. $p_\theta(x) = p_{\hat{\theta}}(x)$.

*Causal mechanism equivalence* [2] demands:

- **Definition 5 (Causal Mechanism Permutation Equivalence):** $\theta \equiv_{CM} \hat{\theta}$ iff $\exists \pi$ s.t. $\forall i$, $p_\theta(z_i | pa_i^\theta) = p_{\hat{\theta}}(z_{\pi(i)} | pa_{\pi(i)}^{\hat{\theta}}) \circ h_i$ up to diffeomorphism.

Thus $p_\theta(z)=p_{\hat\theta}(z)$ *does not* imply mechanism equivalence when variables causally related. Example: $Z_1 \to Z_2$, $Z_2 = Z_1 + U_2$, swapping $Z_1,Z_2$ preserves joint if covariance adjusted, but $p(z_2|z_1)$ vs $p(z_1|z_2)$ differ — invalid for interventions.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Generative Model with Supervision

ICM-VAE generative process [2] with label $y$ supervision:

```python
# flow-based diffeomorphic mechanism
import torch, torch.nn as nn
class CausalMechanism(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dim_pa):
        super().__init__()
        self.flow = nn.Sequential(
            # affine coupling with PA conditioning
            ConditionalAffine(dim_pa)
        )
    def forward(self, u, pa):
        # z_i = f_i(pa_i, u_i)  diffeomorphic in u_i
        return self.flow(u, cond=pa)  # invertible

def scm_forward(u_vec, graph):
    z = {}
    for i in topological_order(graph):
        pa = torch.cat([z[j] for j in parents[i]], -1)
        z[i] = mechanisms[i](u_vec[i], pa)
    x = decoder(z)  # g ~ invertible CNN
    return x, z
```

**ELBO with causal prior:**

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x,y)}[\log p_\theta(x|z)] - KL(q_\phi(z|x,y) || p_\theta(z|y)) $$

where $p_\theta(z|y) = \prod_i p_\theta(z_i|pa_i, y_i)$ factorized per auxiliary label.

### 3.2 Identifiability Programme

General strategy:

1. **Observational environment** $e_0: p^{(0)}$
2. **Interventional environments** $e_k: p^{(k)} = p^{(0)}$ except $p^{(k)}(z_i|pa_i) \to \tilde{p}^{(k)}(z_i|pa_i)$ (soft) or $do(z_I=v)$ (hard)
3. Contrast: If two models $\theta,\hat\theta$ induce same $p^{(e)}(x) \forall e$, show $\theta \equiv_{CM} \hat\theta$.

Key lemmas tied to source:

- *Lemma (Zhang et al. [3]):* Generalized faithfulness: if $A \not\perp B | C$ under $\mathcal{G}$-induced independence, dependence persists in mixture $p^{(0)}$ vs $p^{(k)}$ detectable via unpaired data.
- *Lemma (Bing et al. [5]):* Sparsity pattern of intervention-induced distribution shifts is sparse in $Z$-space but dense in $\tilde{Z}= ZL$ mixed space, hence $L$ recoverable by minimizing intervened variables.
- *Genericity [8]:* Spurious solutions require fine-tuning where $p^{(0)}(z_i)$ and $\tilde{p}^{(k)}(z_i)$ satisfy polynomial equalities measure zero.

### 3.3 CausalVAE Layer

Haskell sketch for SCM as linear DAG with nonlinearity:

```haskell
type DAG = Map Node [Node]
data SCM f = SCM { mechanisms :: Map Node (f Node), 
                   adj :: Matrix Double }

causalLayer :: SCM (Noise -> Double) -> Vec Noise -> Vec Concept
causalLayer scm eps = go topSort
  where
    go [] acc = acc
    go (i:is) acc =
      let pa = sum [ adj scm!(i,j) * (acc!j) | j <- parents i ]
          zi = activation (pa + eps!i)  -- f_i(PA, U)
      in go is (insert i zi acc)

-- intervention do(z_k = v)
doIntervene :: Node -> Double -> Vec Concept -> Vec Concept
doIntervene k v z = replace k v z  -- cuts incoming edges
```

CausalVAE [7] Proof: Under labels $y_i$ for each concept, model identifiable up to affine transformation $\hat{z}_i = a_i z_i + b_i$.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 ICM-VAE: Flow-Based Mechanisms and Causal Disentanglement Prior

Conventional VAE assumes factorized prior $N(0,I)$. ICM-VAE [2] replaces with:

$$ z_i = f_i(z_{pa_i}, u_i), \quad u_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1), \quad f_i \text{ invertible in } u_i $$

*Why flows?* Diffeomorphism guarantees Jacobian tractable, ensures $p(z_i|pa_i)$ density via change-of-variables:

$$ \log p(z_i|pa_i) = \log p(u_i) - \log |\det \partial f_i / \partial u_i| $$

This *learns mechanism itself*, not just distribution. Identifiability theorem [2]:

> **Theorem (ICM-VAE Identifiability):** Suppose: (a) sufficient label variability: for each $z_i$, auxiliary $y_i$ such that $p(y_i|z_i)$ distinct across values; (b) $f_i$ diffeomorphic and $g$ injective; (c) causal prior $p(z|y)=\prod p(z_i|pa_i,y_i)$ known graph. Then maximizer of ELBO recovers true $z$ and $f_i$ up to permutation and elementwise reparam.

Proof uses iVAE machinery [Khemakhem et al. 2020] extended to conditional factorization. *Essence:* label $y$ acts as *auxiliary variable* breaking symmetry: $p(z|y)$ variation forces $q(z|x,y)$ to align with true $p$.

**Bold distinction:** *Statistical disentanglement* rewards $TC(z)=KL(q(z)||\prod q(z_i))$ small; *causal disentanglement* rewards *mechanism independence*: interventions on one mechanism do not alter others. *Italic nuance:* this permits statistical dependence between $z_i$ and $z_j$ via $z_i \to z_j$ edge, which statistical disentanglement would penalize incorrectly.

Three substeps:

- **(i) Causal prior learning:** $p(z_i|pa_i,y_i)$ via masked MLP respecting DAG; loss $D_{KL}$ regularizes to learn DAG adjacency $A$ via NOTEARS acyclicity constraint $h(A)=tr(e^{A \circ A})-d =0$.
- **(ii) Flow mechanism recovery:** optimizing flow parameters to maximize likelihood recovers diffeomorphism; interventional robustness follows because $f_i$ invariant across environments.
- **(iii) Counterfactual generation:** $do(z_k:=v)$ procedure: cut parent edge, set $z_k=v$, propagate via $f_{descendants}$ generating new $x_{cf}=g(z_{do})$ consistent with causal chain.

Ordered evaluation for pendulum-shadow dataset [2] Fig.1:

1. True SCM: $angle \sim Unif$, $light \sim Unif$ independent, $shadow\_pos = f(angle, light) + \epsilon$, $shadow\_len = g(angle, light)$.
2. Unsupervised $\beta$-VAE learns entangled: changing inferred $angle$ also moves light spuriously.
3. ICM-VAE supervised with labels $(y_{angle}, y_{light})$ learns correct DAG $angle\to shadow$, $light\to shadow$, independent roots, counterfactuals accurate: $do(angle=30^\circ)$ correctly shortens shadow without changing light.

### 4.2 Identifiability from Soft and Multi-Node Interventions

Zhang et al. [3] considers *unpaired* interventional data: each cell observed under single perturbation.

Setup: latent variables unobserved, observed $X = P(Z)$ where $P$ polynomial mixing [Ahuja]. Intervention changes mechanism $p(z_i|pa_i) \to \tilde{p}$. Want to recover $\mathcal{G}$ up to equivalence class and predict **unseen combination** $do(I_1 \cup I_2)$.

> **Key Insight:** Even with soft interventions (mechanism shift, not point), if intervention targets known per dataset but latent identity unknown, DAG identifiable up to *transitive closure* under generalized faithfulness [3] Theorem 2.

Generalized faithfulness vs classical: classical faithfulness violations occur when parameters tuned cancel dependence; generalized version additionally requires interventional distribution shifts be faithful to $\mathcal{G}$'s d-separation.

Bing et al. [5] multi-node hard case: ground truth $Z_1\to Z_2\to Z_3$. Intervene on $\{Z_1,Z_2\}$ simultaneously. Is that fatal for identification? No, if intervention sets *sparse and diverse*:

- Assumption 4.3: For each variable $i$, there exists environment where $i$ *not* intervened but other distribution changes still.
- Signal: In $Z$-space, $Var(Z_i | do)$ sparse change; in $\tilde{Z}=ZL$ dense change because mixing spreads sparse shock densely.

**Algorithm intuition:** Minimize $||\Delta \Sigma||_0$ across environments to recover unmixing $L^{-1}$. Provable for linear mixing, additive Gaussian noise SCM arbitrarily nonlinear.

Rust inner loop for sparse contrast (educational):

```rust
fn intervention_sparsity_score(samples: &[Vec<f64>], envs: &[usize]) -> f64 {
    // compute per-coordinate variance shift across envs
    let mut scores = vec![0.0; samples[0].len()];
    for k in 1..envs.len() {
        let delta = covariance_shift(&samples, 0, k);
        for i in 0..scores.len() {
            if delta[i].abs() > 1e-3 { scores[i] += 1.0; }
        }
    }
    // dense in mixed space => higher score
    scores.iter().sum::<f64>() / scores.len() as f64
}

fn recover_unmixing(candidates: Vec<Matrix>) -> Matrix {
    candidates.into_iter()
        .min_by(|a,b| intervention_sparsity_score(&apply(a), &envs)
                      .partial_cmp(&intervention_sparsity_score(&apply(b), &envs)).unwrap())
        .unwrap()
}
```

### 4.3 Nonparametric Identifiability and Grouping

Most permissive setting: *both* $g$ and SCM nonparametric, intervention targets unknown [8] — *"wild"* CRL.

Result [8] theorem 1 (two variables):

> **Theorem:** With $p^{(0)}(x)$ observational and $p^{(1)}(x)$ from perfect intervention on single unspecified node, under genericity (intervened and observational distributions not finely tuned equal), latents identifiable up to trivial indeterminacies, and causal direction preserved.

Proof sketch: Suppose $X = g(Z_1,Z_2)$ with $Z_1 \to Z_2$. Intervene on $Z_1$ shifts $p(z_1)$ alone, $p(z_2|z_1)$ invariant (ICM). If you wrongly guess $g'$, mixing $Z$, invariant conditional pattern breaks because generic $\tilde{p}$ vs $p$ yield contradiction via independence of mechanisms.

For arbitrary $d$, need at least 1 pair distinct perfect interventions per node ($2d$ datasets). Strength of causal influence $c_{ij} = \partial \mathbb{E}[Z_j|do(Z_i)]/\partial$ preserved up to monotonic transform, hence safe for causal inference from inferred representation.

Grouping of observational variables [4]: *no interventional data at all!*

Assumption: Observed variables $X$ partitioned: $X^{(I)}$ reflects subset of latent causes? Example genomics: genes partitioned into modules each driven by single program $Z_i$. Then identifiability via self-supervised contrast: factorization of conditional $p(X^{(I)} | X^{(\neg I)})$ reveals grouping.

> **Practical takeaway:** When interventions expensive (biology), grouping inductive bias substitutes: assume observation space modularly generated — akin to independent mechanisms but on decoder side.

### 4.4 CausalVAE, DEAR, and Counterfactual Robustness

**CausalVAE architecture** [7] pipeline:

- Encoder $q_\phi(\epsilon|x)$ → exogenous $\epsilon \sim N(0,I)$
- Causal layer: $\epsilon \to z$ via learned SCM adjacency $A$: $z = (I-A^T)^{-1} \epsilon$ (linear case) or nonlinear via MLP: $z_i = f_i(A_i \circ z, \epsilon_i)$
- Decoder $p_\theta(x|z)$
- Supervision loss $||z_i - y_i||^2$ using labels, DAG constraint $h(A)=0$, sparsity $||A||_1$.

DEAR [Shen et al. 2022] improvement: labels $y$ not equal latents but via unknown mapping $y = h(z)+\eta$; learns $h$ via inference network, more realistic.

**Interventional robustness** [Suter et al. 2019 linked in [1]] metric: model trained on $p^{(0)}$ but evaluated on $do(z_i)$. Statistically-disentangled VAE accuracy drops 35-48% (pendulum); causal models drop <8% because causal invariance.

Python interventional evaluation harness:

```python
import torch
def interventional_robustness(model, test_loader, intervened_node, val):
    accs = []
    for x,y in test_loader:
        # do-intervention on latent
        with torch.no_grad():
            z = model.encode(x)
            z_do = do_operation(z, intervened_node, val)
            x_cf = model.decode(z_do)
            y_pred = downstream_classifier(x_cf)
            accs.append((y_pred==y).float().mean())
    return torch.stack(accs).mean()

def do_operation(z, k, v):
    # cut parent dependency
    z_do = z.clone()
    z_do[:,k] = v
    # propagate to children via learned A
    for child in topo_order[ topo_order.index(k)+1: ]:
        pa = (A[child]!=0).float() * z_do
        z_do[:,child] = mechanisms[child](pa)
    return z_do
```

Counterfactual fairness application: $do(Sex)$ shouldn't change $Qualification$-driven hiring score; causal representation enforces invariance via path blocking.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Mechanism vs Joint Equivalence

We formalize counterexample to joint equivalence for causal models.

Let true SCM: $Z_1 \sim N(0,1)$, $Z_2 := Z_1 + U_2$, $U_2 \sim N(0,1)$. Suppose learned $\hat{g}$ yields $\hat{Z}_1 = (Z_1+Z_2)/\sqrt{2}$, $\hat{Z}_2 = (Z_1 - Z_2)/\sqrt{2}$ rotation mixing. Then $p_\theta(z)=N(0,\Sigma)$, $p_{\hat\theta}(\hat z)=N(0,\Sigma)$ identical marginal up to rotation (orth orthogonal). However:

$$ p_\theta(z_2|z_1)=N(z_1,1) $$
$$ p_{\hat\theta}(\hat z_2|\hat z_1)=N(0,2) \neq p_\theta $$

Intervening $do(Z_1=1)$ yields $p(Z_2|do)=N(1,1)$ true vs $N(0,2)$ learned ⇒ predictions diverge. Therefore joint equivalence insufficient.

**Lemma Proof:** $\mathbb{E}_{do(z_i)}[x] = \int g(z) p(z_{-i}|z_i) dz_{-i}$ vs observational $\mathbb{E}[x|z_i]$ differ unless mechanism preserved. Only ICM-aware prior distinguishes.

### 5.3 Statistical Consistency for Soft Interventions [3]

Algorithm reduces to autoencoding variational Bayes:

1. Encode $X\to Z$ via DNN encoder.
2. Decoder composes deep SCM $p(z|y)$ followed by mixing $p(x|z)$.
3. Estimate adjacency $A$ via penalized evidence lower bound + differentiable acyclicity.

Theorem 4 [3]: Under polynomial mixing class $\mathcal{P}$ degree $\le p$, generalized faithfulness, and $n\to \infty$ samples per environment (unpaired), estimated DAG $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$ $\to$ equivalence class of true $\mathcal{G}$ and combinatorial intervention effect predictor consistent.

Implication: Predicting unseen combo $do(Z_i,Z_j)$ feasible without ever observing combo—crucial for genomics combinatorial perturbations 2-gene knockouts from single knockouts.

### 5.4 No-Go for Unsupervised [6] Reproduction Check

Training 12 models $\times$ 6 metrics over dSprites: Locatello et al. found mean variance explained by hyperparameters 2.3× larger than method choice; random seed influences disentanglement 0.4 stddev. Hence hyperparameter tuning on unsupervised objective cannot reliably select disentangled model—corroborates our cause for interventions.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Label supervision bottleneck**: ICM-VAE [2] requires $y_i$ causally related labels for each latent. In natural images, annotating causal factors expensive and incomplete; self-supervised non-label interventions [contrastive multi-view] may replace but trade identifiability strength [4].

- **Genericity condition fragility**: Nonparametric result [8] rules out fine-tuned cancellations where $p(z_i)$ and $\tilde{p}(z_i)$ coincide on measure-zero set satisfying polynomial identity. Near-fine-tuning leads to *weak* identifiability: finite sample separation $O(\sqrt{n})$ fails; requires sample exponential in overlap. Real interventions may be close to observational (weak shift) causing poor conditioning.

- **Mixing invertibility**: Most theory [2][5][8] assumes $g$ diffeomorphic/injective. Real observation from $Z$ often partial occlusion non-invertible; injectivity breaks and multiple $z$ collide to same $x$. No results for non-injective $g$ without temporal structure—open.

- **Acyclicity assumption**: All SCMs assumed DAG. Feedback cycles common in biology and economics. Cyclic SCMs with equilibrium semantics break Markov factorization, ICM definition ambiguous [1]. Recent work on causally disentangled cycles emerging but no latent identifiability yet.

- **Unknown intervention cardinality**: Theory demands at least 1 perfect intervention per node [8]. In practice intervention unknown target unknown number; discovering intervention targets from data itself (as in [5]) requires sparsity heuristic which fails if interventions *dense* (affect many nodes). Real multi-gene perturbations affect hundreds.

- **Computational cost**: Flow-based mechanisms require $O(d^2)$ Jacobian determinants per batch; TCA batch C=512 exhausts GPU memory for CelebA-HQ 1024px. Approximation (diagonal $\Sigma$) introduces bias.

- **Causal sufficiency**: Hidden confounders $H$ between $Z_i$ excluded in all above; grouping paper [4] claims robustness to latent confounders via superset covering but only under grouping-induced conditional independence, not general.

**Ordered future work:**

1. Extend to *temporal* CRL where $Z^t \to Z^{t+1}$ provides extra contrast without explicit interventions — linking to nonlinear ICA with temporal dependencies [Hyvärinen & Morioka 2017].
2. Formalize *optimal* intervention design for CRL: minimal # environments to achieve elementwise identifiability given budget.
3. Integrate SCM learning with *diffusion decoders* $g$ as stochastic (not deterministic) to allow counterfactual diversity.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We traversed **from statistical to causal disentanglement**. The **ICM principle** [1] redefines independent mechanisms as the factorization unit, rendering joint equivalence obsolete. Architectures like **CausalVAE** [7] embedding a causal layer with supervision, and **ICM-VAE** [2] with flow diffeomorphisms and causal prior, elevate VAE disentanglement from independence to mechanism modularity, enabling do-operations and counterfactuals that respect $z_i \to z_j$.

Identifiability escalates with *interventional strength*:

- **Unsupervised impossible** [6].
- **Linear mixing + multi-node sparse hard interventions** suffice [5].
- **Polynomial mixing + unpaired soft interventions** with generalized faithfulness recover DAG and predict unseen combos [3].
- **Nonparametric mixing + unknown perfect interventions** generically identifiable [8].
- **Grouping of observations** dispenses interventions entirely [4].

Causal representation learning thus solves what disentanglement promised but statistical view could not: representations that remain meaningful under *actions* in an imagined space [Lorenz]. True world models are not feature dictionaries but *mechanism collections* — independent, composable, amenable to surgery.

> **Future Ethical Note:** As causally disentangled simulators generate counterfactual faces via $do(Smile)$, ensuring mechanism-level identifiability is not theoretical—it governs fairness audits and out-of-distribution generalization to interventional domains denied to correlatational models.
