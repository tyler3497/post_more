# Univalent Foundations through the Synthetic Lens: Cubical Semantics, Higher Inductive Types, and the Computational Content of Univalence

## Abstract

We present a systematic account of univalent foundations as a *synthetic* language for homotopy theory, tracing the arc from the identity type as a path object to the cubical models that restore computational meaning to the univalence axiom. We formalize the interplay between Voevodsky's univalence axiom, higher inductive types, and the groupoid laws governing identity, and we explain how the 2016 cubical type theory of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg converts univalence from a stuck axiom into a *theorem* equipped with reduction rules via glueing and composition. We survey the semantics of higher inductive types through cell monads with parameters, the simplicial and presheaf models, and Huber's canonicity theorem, and we evaluate the consequences for synthetic homotopy theory — including Brunerié-style computations of homotopy groups — proof-assistant engineering, and constructive foundations. The central thesis is that univalence's computational content was never absent, only unexpressed; cubical semantics supplies the missing reduction behavior.

## 1 Introduction

The univalent foundations program, inaugurated by Vladimir Voevodsky and codified in the 2013 *Homotopy Type Theory* book [1], proposes a radical simplification of the interface between mathematics and the machine: dependent type theory, enriched with two new principles, is itself a language in which homotopy theory can be done *synthetically* — by reasoning directly about spaces, paths, and homotopies, rather than encoding them through point-set or simplicial machinery. The two principles are Voevodsky's **univalence axiom**, which identifies equalities between types with equivalences, and **higher inductive types (HITs)**, which permit the direct, logical specification of spaces by point, path, and higher-path constructors.

The proposal's audacity lies in its economy. The identity type, introduced by Martin-Löf in 1975 as an inductive family with a single generator `refl`, turns out — once read through the eyes of a homotopy theorist — to be a path object: its iterated structure is an infinity-groupoid [1]. Univalence extends this homotopical reading to the universe itself, making it a classifying space for fibrations. Higher inductive types then complete the picture by giving synthetic names to classical spaces: the circle `S¹`, the torus, pushouts, truncations, and in principle any finite CW complex.

Yet the program carried a conspicuous defect. The univalence axiom, stated as a postulated constant of type `(A =_U B) ≃ (A ≃ B)`, was *stuck*: it did not compute. In proof assistants such as Coq, where univalence was implemented as an axiom (notably in Voevodsky's UniMath and the HoTT library [7]), any closed term of natural number type that mentioned univalence in its normalization path refused to reduce to a numeral, breaking the canonicity property on which type theory's computational interpretation rests. For a decade, univalence was semantically justified — by Voevodsky's simplicial model in Kan simplicial sets [2] — but computationally opaque.

This thesis argues that the opacity was an artifact of the simplicial presentation, not of univalence itself. The cubical type theory of Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg (CCHM) [3] supplies a constructive model in cubical sets where univalence is *provable* via the glueing construction, and Huber's canonicity theorem [4] shows that closed numerals indeed reduce. The path from axiom to theorem is the path from classical to constructive semantics, and it reshapes what "computational content" means in higher-dimensional type theory.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The identity type and the groupoid laws

Martin-Löf's identity type `Id_A(x, y)` is generated by `refl_x : Id_A(x, x)` and eliminated by the famous **J rule** (path induction): to prove `C(x, y, p)` for all `p : Id_A(x, y)`, it suffices to prove `C(x, x, refl_x)`.

```haskell
-- Path induction, in Agda-style notation
J : {A : Set} {x : A} (C : (y : A) → x ≡ y → Set)
  → C x refl
  → {y : A} (p : x ≡ y) → C y p
```

The insight of the Hofmann–Streicher groupoid interpretation, later sharpened by Awodey, Warren, and Voevodsky, is that this rule *generates* the full structure of a weak ∞-groupoid: path concatenation, inverses, associators, and their coherences all follow by repeated applications of J [1]. The eliminator does not merely transport proofs; it *lifts* any construction along a path.

> **Theorem (Grothendieck ∞-groupoid structure):** In Martin-Löf type theory, every type carries the structure of a weak ∞-groupoid: for each `n ≥ 1`, the iterated identity types `Id^n` admit `n`-fold composition, inversion, and coherence laws, all definable by induction on identity.

### 2.2 Voevodsky's univalence axiom

Let `U` be a universe of small types. Define the type of equivalences `A ≃ B` as functions with contractible fibers (a quasi-inverse is not a mere proposition; the coherent notion is required [1]). Path induction applied to the identity type of the universe yields the canonical map

```
idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)
```

The **univalence axiom** asserts that `idtoeqv` is itself an equivalence — in slogan form, *equivalent types are identical*:

```haskell
univalence : (A B : U) → isEquiv (idtoeqv {A} {B})
```

Its consequences are sweeping: function extensionality becomes provable, isomorphic structures can be transported along without ceremony, and propositions (`isProp A`, i.e., all proofs equal) and sets (`isSet A`, i.e., identity types are propositions) emerge as the (−1)- and 0-truncated fragments of the theory. The **h-level** stratification organizes all types into a tower, with contractible types at level 0.

### 2.3 The simplicial model and its non-constructivity

Voevodsky, in work exposited by Kapulkin and Lumsdaine, constructed a model of Martin-Löf type theory with one univalent universe in the category of Kan simplicial sets [2]. Types become Kan complexes, identity types become path objects, and univalence becomes the statement that the universal Kan fibration is univalent — provable via Voevodsky's equivalence extension property. This settled *consistency*: univalence is no more dangerous than ZFC with two inaccessibles.

But the simplicial model is fundamentally classical. Kan fibrations, the small object argument, and even the notion of universe in simplicial sets depend on classical reasoning at multiple points. The model could not *run*: it assigned no reduction behavior to the univalence constant, and closed terms mentioning univalence remained stuck.

## 3 Methodology

Our investigation is primarily *formal and metatheoretic*, organized around three methodological pillars.

**Pillar 1 — Synthetic development.** We follow the style of the HoTT book [1]: define spaces, maps, fibrations, and equivalences internally, and prove the fundamental lemmas of homotopy theory (the encode-decode method, Freudenthal suspension, Blakers–Massey) as *theorems of type theory*, portable across all models [8].

**Pillar 2 — Categorical and presheaf semantics.** We analyze models in presheaf categories over cube categories, where the interval object `I` (a de Morgan algebra in the CCHM setting) supports internal path types `Path_A(x, y) ≔ (i : I) → A` with `p 0 ≡ x`, `p 1 ≡ y` [3]. Composition operations fill open boxes; glueing turns partial equivalences into total types.

**Pillar 3 — Metatheory of computation.** We track canonicity, normalization, and decidability of type checking — the properties that separate a foundation *about* computation from a foundation *with* computation [4].

A note on evidence: the "empirical" content of this thesis is the *formalized artifact* — definitions and proofs that type-check in cubical proof assistants — and the measured behavior of their reduction. We report on machine-checked developments rather than benchmark tables, since the relevant observable is *definitional behavior*, not runtime.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Higher inductive types as synthetic spaces

Higher inductive types generalize ordinary inductive types by allowing constructors that inhabit *identity types* — i.e., path constructors — in addition to point constructors [5]. The circle is the canonical example:

```haskell
-- The circle as a HIT (Cubical Agda style)
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The eliminator for `S¹` must handle both constructors: to map out of `S¹` into a family `P`, one supplies `b : P base` and a *path-over* `l : transport P loop b ≡ b`. This recursion principle is exactly the universal property of the circle as the classifying space of the integers, expressed in type-theoretic syntax. Propositional truncation `‖A‖`, suspensions, pushouts, and the torus all follow the same pattern [5][6].

The semantics of HITs is delicate. Lumsdaine and Shulman introduced **cell monads with parameters** and showed that any sufficiently nice Quillen model category admits weakly stable typal initial algebras for them — covering spheres, the torus, pushouts, truncations, the James construction, and localizations [5]. In the cubical setting, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg gave a direct presheaf semantics decomposing composition into *transport* and *homogeneous composition* [6].

### 4.2 The univalence axiom, precisely

Univalence is not a single axiom but a tightly related family of statements. For a universe `U` with decoding `El`, the following are equivalent in the presence of function extensionality [2]:

| Formulation | Statement |
|---|---|
| Equivalence induction | `idtoeqv : (A =_U B) → (A ≃ B)` is an equivalence |
| Univalence for equivalences | `Σ(B:U). (A ≃ B)` is contractible for each `A` |
| Object classifier | `U` classifies small fibrations |
| Structure identity principle | Identity of structures coincides with isomorphism |

The *structure identity principle* is the workhorse: for groups, rings, categories, and indeed any structure defined in the theory, univalence implies that equality of structures is equivalent to structure-preserving isomorphism. This is the precise sense in which univalent foundations deliver an "invariant" conception of mathematics [1].

> **Theorem (Univalence implies function extensionality):** Given univalence, any two pointwise-equal functions `f, g : Π(x:A). B(x)` are propositionally equal. Moreover, under univalence, `isProp` and `isSet` are themselves propositions, so h-levels are cumulative properties rather than data.

### 4.3 Cubical type theory: univalence as a theorem

The breakthrough of CCHM [3] is syntactic as well as semantic. Cubical type theory extends Martin-Löf type theory with:

1. **An interval pretype** `I` with endpoints `0, 1 : I` and de Morgan operations `∧, ∨, ¬` (later variants use distributive lattices or cartesian cubes).
2. **Path types** `Path A a b`, internalizing `I → A` with endpoint constraints.
3. **A composition operation** `comp`, which fills open boxes: given a partial path defined on a face of a cube, it extends to a total path.
4. **Glueing** `Glue [φ ↦ (T, e)] A`, which pastes a partial type `T` (defined where the face formula `φ` holds) equipped with an equivalence `e : T ≃ A` onto a total type `A`, producing a type that *is* `T` where `φ` holds and `A` elsewhere.

Univalence then falls out as a *theorem*: given an equivalence `e : A ≃ B`, the type `Glue [i=0 ↦ (A, e)] B` gives a path in the universe from `A` to `B` whose transport is `e`. Crucially, glueing comes with **definitional equalities** — it computes. A closed natural number built with univalence reduces, because the cubical operational semantics knows how to push `comp` and `Glue` through constructors. Huber's canonicity theorem makes this precise: in cubical type theory, every closed term of type `ℕ` is judgmentally equal to a numeral, effectively computable [4].

This is the sense in which cubical semantics "explains" univalence: it replaces the stuck constant with a construction whose reduction behavior is specified once and for all.

### 4.4 Synthetic homotopy theory: the theory does real work

The payoff of the synthetic approach is that classical homotopy-theoretic results become *internal theorems*, proved once and valid in every model [8]. The landmark example is Brunerié's 2016 computation that `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ` — a deep classical result (the first stable stem) — carried out entirely inside homotopy type theory and formalized in a proof assistant. The proof deploys the encode-decode method to characterize `π₁(S¹) ≃ ℤ`, the Freudenthal suspension theorem, and the Blakers–Massey theorem, all synthetically [1].

Cubical proof assistants have since absorbed this development. Cubical Agda implements CCHM-style cubical type theory with HITs, glueing, and a univalence *theorem* that computes [3][6]; the HoTT library in Coq demonstrated the viability of the axiomatic approach earlier [7]. The two strands now converge: what the HoTT library did with axioms, Cubical Agda does with reduction rules.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

We now record the central technical results as established in the literature, with attention to what is *proved* versus what is *conjectured*.

**Result 1 (Simplicial consistency).** Kapulkin–Lumsdaine–Voevodsky: Martin-Löf type theory with one univalent universe is modeled in Kan simplicial sets; hence it is consistent relative to ZFC with two inaccessible cardinals [2].

**Result 2 (Cubical constructivity).** CCHM: there is a constructive model of dependent type theory in cubical sets validating univalence; the corresponding *syntax* — cubical type theory — proves univalence via glueing [3].

**Result 3 (Canonicity).** Huber: cubical type theory enjoys canonicity at `ℕ` — every closed natural reduces to a numeral — proved via a typed, deterministic operational semantics and a computability argument [4].

**Result 4 (HIT semantics).** Lumsdaine–Shulman: excellent model categories admit weakly stable typal initial algebras for cell monads with parameters, yielding models of spheres, tori, pushouts, truncations, and localizations [5]; Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg give the internal presheaf account for cubical HITs [6].

**Result 5 (Synthetic computation).** Brunerié-style development: `π₄(S³) ≃ ℤ/2ℤ` is provable internally, demonstrating that synthetic homotopy theory has genuine mathematical reach [1].

The *empirical* character of these results deserves emphasis. In a proof assistant, "proof" is an artifact with machine-checked force, and canonicity is a statement about the *behavior of programs*. The cubical program converts metatheoretic claims into observable computation: transport along a univalence-derived path can be executed, and the result inspected. This is falsifiable in the strongest sense — a stuck term would refute the implementation.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires cataloguing what remains open or unsatisfactory.

1. **Normalization and decidability.** Canonicity is not normalization: decidability of definitional equality (and hence of type checking) for full cubical type theory with HITs remains incompletely established in the strongest formulations. Several variants (cartesian cubical, XTT) were developed partly to tame these metatheoretic difficulties.
2. **Generality of HIT schemas.** The cell-monad framework [5] covers an impressive range, but a fully general schema for higher inductive-inductive types with computational reduction rules is still being consolidated; implementations support a useful but restricted class.
3. **Classical models and choice.** Models of HoTT in Grothendieck ∞-toposes [8] recover classical homotopy theory, but the internal logic of an arbitrary ∞-topos is weaker than the full theory; excluded middle and choice remain incompatible with univalence in their naive forms.
4. **Performance.** Cubical proof assistants are dramatically more computational than axiomatic ones, but composition-heavy proofs can be expensive: the `comp` operation's reduction behavior, while well-defined, is not yet well-optimized, and large synthetic-homotopy formalizations remain engineering challenges.
5. **Universe polymorphism and cumulativity.** The interaction of univalence with universe levels — cumulativity, typical ambiguity, and the "universe of propositions" — contains known sharp edges (e.g., the failure of naive cumulativity with HITs in some implementations).

## 7 Conclusion

The story of univalent foundations over the past fifteen years is the story of a single question asked with increasing precision: *what does it mean for an axiom to compute?* Voevodsky's simplicial model [2] answered the question of *consistency*; the HoTT book [1] answered the question of *expressiveness*, showing that homotopy theory could be done synthetically; and cubical type theory [3] answered the question of *computation*, showing that univalence's apparent stuckness was a defect of presentation rather than of principle.

The philosophical upshot is worth stating plainly. In set-theoretic foundations, equality is a relation imposed from outside; in univalent foundations, equality *is* structure — paths, equivalences, homotopies — and the foundation's job is to make that structure first-class and computational. Cubical semantics completes this vision by giving the structure reduction rules. The univalence axiom did not need to be *assumed*; it needed to be *explained*, and the explanation turned out to be geometry: the interval, the open box, the glue.

The open frontier is now practical: normalization proofs for the full cubical hierarchy, general HIT schemas with clean computation, and performant implementations that make synthetic homotopy theory a daily tool rather than a tour de force. The foundations are sound; the engineering is underway.
