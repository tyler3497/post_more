# Programmable Bootstrapping in TFHE: Blind Rotation, Functional Keyswitching, and Encrypted Lookup Tables

## Abstract

Programmable bootstrapping (PBS) is the defining operation of the TFHE cryptosystem: it refreshes the noise of a ciphertext while simultaneously evaluating an arbitrary univariate function through an encrypted lookup table. This thesis develops the full mathematics of the technique — modulus switching of the LWE phase to the negacyclic torus group, the blind rotation of a test polynomial by a bootstrapping key of TGSW encryptions of the secret bits, sample extraction of the rotated constant coefficient, and functional keyswitching to return to the target key and message modulus. We analyze the negacyclic constraint of the polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}_N[X]/(X^N+1)$, the encoding of lookup tables into the test polynomial, and the worst-case noise propagation of the blind rotation external product. We present empirical benchmarks from modern implementations (TFHE-rs / Concrete), discuss precision scaling via multiple and multi-value bootstraps, and formally state correctness and security under the circular-secure ring-LWE assumption. The result is a complete, self-contained treatment of why PBS turns bootstrapping from a pure noise-management primitive into a general-purpose encrypted function-evaluation engine.

## 1 Introduction

Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) was born as a feasibility result: Gentry's 2009 construction showed that arbitrary computation on encrypted data is possible in principle, but the bootstrapping procedure — the noise-refresh operation that makes unlimited depth possible — was for years the scheme's crushing bottleneck [3][6]. The line of work inaugurated by Ducas and Micciancio's FHEW [3] and perfected by Chillotti, Gama, Georgieva, and Izabachène in TFHE [1][2] inverted the economic structure of bootstrapping. Instead of amortizing one slow bootstrap over a deep leveled circuit (as in BGV/BFV/CKKS), TFHE bootstraps *after every gate*, keeping ciphertext noise perpetually small at the cost of performing thousands of fast bootstraps.

The conceptual masterstroke of FHEW/TFHE is that the bootstrap is not merely a noise reset: it is *functional*. Inside the homomorphic accumulator that powers the blind rotation, the encrypted rotation offset is used to index into a **test polynomial** — an RLWE encryption of a polynomial whose coefficients encode the values of an arbitrary function $f$. When the blind rotation completes and the constant coefficient is extracted, the output ciphertext encrypts $f(m)$ rather than merely a refreshed $m$ [3][7]. This is programmable bootstrapping (PBS): one operation simultaneously refreshes noise and evaluates any univariate function over a small domain, from Boolean gates to sign functions, rectifiers, digit extractions, and full 6-to-6-bit lookup tables [5].

This thesis reconstructs PBS from the ground up. We work over the *torus* $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, following the TFHE convention that unifies LWE and its ring variants [2]. We develop the five pipeline stages — modulus switching, blind rotation, test-polynomial encoding, sample extraction, and keyswitching — with exact noise accounting, and we prove a correctness theorem giving the failure probability of a PBS under standard parameter choices. We close with empirical data and the technique's known limitations: the negacyclicity constraint, precision ceilings, and key material costs.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 LWE over the Torus

Let $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ be the real torus. A *TLWE sample* encrypting a message $\mu \in \mathbb{T}$ under secret $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{B}^n$ is a pair $(\mathbf{a}, b) \in \mathbb{T}^n \times \mathbb{T}$ with $b = \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle + \mu + e$, where $e$ is a small Gaussian error [2]. The *phase* $\varphi(\mathbf{a}, b) = b - \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle = \mu + e$ carries the message plus noise. Decryption rounds the phase to the nearest multiple of $1/t$ for a $t$-ary message space.

TFHE lifts this to rings: with $N$ a power of two and $\mathbb{T}_N[X] = \mathbb{T}[X]/(X^N+1)$, a *TRLWE* sample encrypts a polynomial $\mu(X)$ as $(\mathbf{A}, B)$ with $B = \mathbf{A} \cdot S + \mu + E$ under secret $S \in \mathbb{B}_N[X]$ [2]. A *TRGSW* sample encrypts $\mu$ as a matrix of TRLWE samples of $h_i \cdot \mu$ for a gadget vector $\mathbf{h} = (B_g^{-1}, \dots, B_g^{-\ell})$, enabling the *external product* $\boxdot$ between a TRGSW ciphertext and a TRLWE ciphertext:

$$ \text{TRGSW}(\mu_1) \boxdot \text{TRLWE}(\mu_2) = \text{TRLWE}(\mu_1 \cdot \mu_2) + \text{noise}. $$

The noise of the external product grows **linearly** in the decomposition parameters and — crucially — is *independent of the message magnitude*, a property that makes repeated external products in the blind rotation affordable [2][6].

### 2.2 Gadget Decomposition and Approximate Rounding

The external product requires decomposing the TRLWE input into small digits in base $B_g$ with $\ell$ levels. TFHE uses an *approximate* decomposition (rounding-based) rather than exact base-$B_g$ expansion; the approximation error is absorbed into the noise budget, which substantially reduces the decomposition cost while preserving correctness [1][2]. This is one of the optimizations that reduced FHEW's ~1 s bootstrap to TFHE's sub-0.1 s gate bootstrap [1].

### 2.3 The Homomorphic Accumulator

Bootstrapping in FHEW-like systems follows the *homomorphic accumulator* paradigm [3][6]. Given a TLWE ciphertext $(\mathbf{a}, b)$ encrypting $m$ with phase $\varphi = m + e$, one:

1. **Modulus-switches** the phase from the torus to $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}$, obtaining an integer $\bar{b} - \langle \bar{\mathbf{a}}, \mathbf{s} \rangle \approx \frac{2N}{t} m$ (up to rounding noise).
2. Initializes an accumulator $\text{ACC} \in \text{TRLWE}$ encrypting $X^{-\bar{b}} \cdot v(X)$, where $v(X)$ is the **test polynomial**.
3. For each $i$, homomorphically multiplies by $X^{\bar{a}_i s_i}$ using the bootstrapping key $\text{BK}_i = \text{TRGSW}(s_i)$:

$$ \text{ACC} \leftarrow \text{CMux}(\text{BK}_i, X^{\bar{a}_i} \cdot \text{ACC}, \text{ACC}) \quad \text{or equivalently} \quad \text{ACC} \cdot (1 + X^{\bar{a}_i s_i} - X^{\bar{a}_i(1-s_i)}). $$

4. After processing all $n$ secret bits, $\text{ACC}$ encrypts $X^{\varphi \cdot 2N - \bar{b}} \cdot v(X) = X^{\text{phase}} v(X)$ — the test polynomial *rotated* by the (noisy) phase, with the secret bits never revealed [2][3].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology reconstructs the full PBS pipeline as five composable stages, each with a defined input/output ciphertext type, noise behavior, and correctness condition.

### 3.1 Stage 1 — Modulus Switching

The input TLWE ciphertext under key $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{B}^n$ with message $\mu \in \mathbb{T}_t = \{\tfrac{k}{t}\}$ is converted so its phase lands in $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}$. Each torus element is rounded to the nearest multiple of $1/(2N)$; the rounding injects an error bounded by $1/(4N)$ per coefficient, or variance $1/(48N^2)$ under the uniform-rounding model [2]. This discretization is what allows the phase to act as an *exponent* of the monomial $X$ in the blind rotation.

```python
# Modulus switching: torus phase -> Z_{2N} exponent
def modulus_switch(a, b, N):
    """Round TLWE (a, b) in T^{n+1} to integers in Z_{2N}."""
    scale = 2 * N
    a_bar = [int(round(x * scale)) % scale for x in a]
    b_bar = int(round(b * scale)) % scale
    # exponent used in blind rotation: (b_bar - <a_bar, s>) mod 2N
    return a_bar, b_bar
```

### 3.2 Stage 2 — Blind Rotation

The accumulator is initialized as $\text{ACC}_0 = \text{TRLWE}(X^{-\bar{b}} \cdot v)$, where $v(X) = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} v_j X^j$ is the test polynomial encoding the target function. For each $i \in [n]$, with $\bar{a}_i$ the modulus-switched mask coefficient and $\text{BK}_i$ a TRGSW encryption of $s_i$:

$$ \text{ACC}_{i+1} = \text{CMux}(\text{BK}_i, X^{\bar{a}_i} \cdot \text{ACC}_i, \text{ACC}_i), $$

where $\text{CMux}(C, d_1, d_0) = C \boxdot (d_1 - d_0) + d_0$ selects between the two TRLWE inputs according to the encrypted bit [2]. After $n$ iterations, $\text{ACC}_n$ encrypts $X^{\bar{\varphi}} v(X)$ with $\bar{\varphi} = \bar{b} - \langle \bar{\mathbf{a}}, \mathbf{s} \rangle \bmod 2N$. Because the ring is *negacyclic* ($X^N = -1$), rotation wraps with sign flips: $X^{N+k} v$ contributes $-v_k$ at position $k$.

### 3.3 Stage 3 — Test-Polynomial (LUT) Encoding

The function $f: \mathbb{Z}_t \to \mathbb{T}$ is encoded into $v(X)$ so that rotating by $\bar{\varphi} \approx \frac{2N}{t} m$ brings $f(m)$ into the constant coefficient. Concretely, set $v_j = f(\lfloor j t / (2N) \rceil)$ with the negacyclic extension $v_{j+N} = -v_j$; only *negacyclic* functions (satisfying $f(x + t/2) = -f(x)$) are representable directly, and arbitrary functions are handled by restricting to half the message space or by padding tricks [5]. The constant term of $X^{\bar{\varphi}} v$ is then $v_{-\bar{\varphi}} \approx f(m)$.

### 3.4 Stage 4 — Sample Extraction

*SampleExtract* converts the TRLWE accumulator into a TLWE ciphertext encrypting the constant coefficient under the concatenated key $(S_0, \dots, S_{N-1}, \dots)$ [2]. Because the extraction is a linear relabeling of coefficients, it adds *no* noise: the output TLWE encrypts $f(m)$ plus exactly the noise accumulated in the blind rotation.

### 3.5 Stage 5 — Keyswitching (Functional)

The extracted TLWE is under a large, non-binary key. A keyswitching key $\text{KS}_{i,j} = \text{TLWE}_{\mathbf{s}'}(s_i / B_{ks}^j)$ translates the ciphertext back to the original small key $\mathbf{s}'$ (or any target key), simultaneously allowing an affine map on the message — hence *functional* keyswitching [2]. The keyswitch noise is proportional to the decomposition base $B_{ks}$ and levels $t_{ks}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Blind Rotation as an Encrypted Automorphism

The blind rotation deserves emphasis: it evaluates the group action of $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}$ on the ring $\mathbb{T}_N[X]$ *without knowing the group element*. Each CMux step conditionally multiplies the accumulator by $X^{\bar{a}_i}$ — a monomial multiplication that merely permutes coefficients (with sign flips) and therefore costs no noise beyond the external product itself. The $n$ external products dominate the runtime: each costs $O(N \log N)$ via the FFT-based polynomial multiplication in the anticyclic ring, which is why the dedicated FFT for $X^N+1$ in TFHE implementations is performance-critical [1].

```haskell
-- Blind rotation, conceptually: fold over secret bits
blindRotate :: Acc -> [(Int, TRGSW)] -> Acc
blindRotate acc [] = acc
blindRotate acc ((aBar, bk):rest) =
    blindRotate (cmux bk (rotL aBar acc) acc) rest
  where
    -- rotL k: multiply by X^k in the negacyclic ring (coefficient permute + sign)
    cmux c d1 d0 = (c `extProd` (d1 `sub` d0)) `add` d0
```

### 4.2 Encoding Lookup Tables: From Rounding to Arbitrary Functions

The original FHEW bootstrap used the test polynomial to encode a *rounding* function — the bootstrap was purely a noise reset [3]. The programmable generalization observes that the test polynomial is an arbitrary length-$N$ vector of torus elements, so it can encode *any* function constant on the quantization buckets [4][5]. Table 1 summarizes the canonical LUT encodings:

| Function $f$ | Encoding in $v_j$ | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding to $\mathbb{T}_t$ | $\lfloor j t / 2N \rceil / t$ | Plain bootstrap (noise reset) |
| Sign / comparison | $\pm 1/(2t)$ step at $j = N$ | Encrypted comparisons, ReLU |
| Identity on half-domain | $j/(2N)$ for $j < N$ | Message-preserving PBS |
| Arbitrary LUT $[t] \to [t']$ | $f(\lfloor jt/2N\rceil)/t'$ | S-boxes, activations, digit ops |
| Digit extraction | $(j \bmod \text{base})/t$ | Multi-precision decomposition |

Negacyclicity forces $v_{j+N} = -v_j$, i.e. $f(x + t/2) = -f(x)$; non-negacyclic functions sacrifice half the plaintext space (padding with zeros) or use the "full-domain" trick of evaluating on two bootstraps [5].

### 4.3 Functional Keyswitching: Affine Maps for Free

After sample extraction, the ciphertext lives under the TRLWE key. Keyswitching to the TLWE key $\mathbf{s}'$ applies a public affine map $x \mapsto \alpha x + \beta$ to the message at the cost of one keyswitch noise term — this is how TFHE composes PBS output with linear layers (e.g., the multiply-accumulate preceding the next bootstrap) without extra bootstraps [2].

```python
# Functional keyswitch: TLWE_s(m) -> TLWE_s'(alpha*m + beta)
def functional_keyswitch(ct, ks_key, alpha, beta, Bks, tks):
    a, b = ct
    # decompose each mask coefficient in base Bks
    a_dec = [digit_decompose(x, Bks, tks) for x in a]
    # linear combination with keyswitching key
    a_out = -sum(d * k for d, k in zip(a_dec, ks_key))
    b_out = alpha * b + beta - sum(d * k0 for d, k0 in zip(a_dec, ks_key0))
    return (a_out, b_out)
```

### 4.4 Noise Accounting: The Worst-Case Bound

Let $\sigma_{br}^2$ be the variance contributed by one CMux external product (dominated by the TRGSW noise of $\text{BK}_i$ and the approximate decomposition error). The blind rotation performs $n$ sequential external products, so

$$ \mathrm{Var}(\text{ACC}_n) \approx n \cdot \sigma_{br}^2 + \mathrm{Var}_{\text{round}}, $$

with $\mathrm{Var}_{\text{round}} = 1/(48N^2)$ from modulus switching [2]. Sample extraction is free; keyswitching adds $\mathrm{Var}_{ks} \approx n \cdot t_{ks} \cdot B_{ks}^2 \cdot \sigma_{ks}^2 / 12$. Correctness requires the total standard deviation to stay below $1/(4t)$ (half the inter-message distance) with overwhelming probability — a union bound over the Gaussian tail gives the familiar failure probabilities below $2^{-40}$ for standard parameters [1][2].

> **Theorem:** (PBS correctness.) Let $(\mathbf{a}, b)$ be a TLWE ciphertext encrypting $m \in \mathbb{Z}_t$ under binary key $\mathbf{s}$, with phase noise of variance $\sigma_{in}^2$ and $|e| < 1/(4t)$. Let $v$ encode a negacyclic $f$ on the $2N$ buckets, and let parameters $(N, B_g, \ell, B_{ks}, t_{ks})$ satisfy $6\sqrt{n\,\sigma_{br}^2 + \sigma_{ks}^2 + 1/(48N^2)} < 1/(4t')$. Then the PBS pipeline outputs a TLWE ciphertext under the target key encrypting $f(m) \in \mathbb{T}_{t'}$ with failure probability at most $2^{-40}$ under the circular-secure RLWE assumption.

### 4.5 Parameter Selection and Security

Security rests on (circular-secure) LWE/RLWE over the torus with binary secrets; the bootstrapping key publishes TRGSW encryptions of the secret key bits under a *different* key, i.e., a circular-security (KDM) assumption [2][6]. Standard parameter sets target $\geq 110$–$128$ bits of security per the Homomorphic Encryption Security Standard, with $N \in \{1024, 2048\}$, $n \in \{512, 630, 1024\}$, and Gaussian noise $\sigma \approx 2^{-15}$–$2^{-25}$ depending on the stage [1][2]. Increasing precision (larger $t$) forces larger $N$ or more bootstraps: a 6-bit-to-6-bit LUT costs on the order of *seconds* in naive TFHE, motivating multi-value and digit-decomposition techniques that evaluate several LUTs per bootstrap [4][5].

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Arguments

### 5.1 Benchmarks

Table 2 collects representative PBS timings from the literature and modern libraries. The original TFHE gate bootstrap ran in ~13 ms per binary gate on a single core [1]; a full PBS evaluating a nontrivial LUT runs in the tens of milliseconds in TFHE-rs/Concrete on commodity hardware, versus ~690 ms for the FHEW bootstrap it replaced [1][3].

| PBS, 4-bit LUT (TFHE-rs) | tens of ms | Zama / Concrete |

### 5.2 Formal Security Argument

*Correctness* follows from the theorem in §4.4: each stage's noise is bounded, and the composition keeps the phase within the correct decoding bucket except with negligible probability. *Security* reduces to decisional LWE/RLWE: the modulus-switched values are public, the accumulator is always a fresh TRLWE encryption, and the bootstrapping and keyswitching keys are KDM-secure encryptions of key material — simulatable under the circular-security assumption [2][6]. The blind rotation leaks nothing about $\bar{\varphi}$ because each CMux output is a re-randomized TRLWE ciphertext.

### 5.3 Applications Enabled by PBS

Because one PBS evaluates an arbitrary univariate function, entire application classes reduce to "linear layer + PBS" loops: encrypted neural-network inference (each activation is one PBS), private decision trees, encrypted comparisons and sorting networks, homomorphic digit decomposition for multi-precision arithmetic, and S-box evaluation in transciphering [4][5]. This is the sense in which PBS converts TFHE from a gate-bootstrapping curiosity into a general encrypted-computation substrate.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Negacyclicity.** The ring $X^N+1$ forces $f(x+t/2) = -f(x)$; arbitrary functions waste half the message space or need compensating bootstraps [5].
2. **Precision ceiling.** Message spaces beyond ~8 bits per PBS demand large $N$ or digit decomposition into multiple bootstraps; noise grows with $t$, and parameters must scale accordingly [4].
3. **Key material.** The bootstrapping key holds $n$ TRGSW ciphertexts (tens of MB) and the keyswitching key is similarly large; TFHE reduced FHEW's 1 GB key to ~24 MB, but keys remain the dominant memory cost [1].
4. **Latency, not throughput.** PBS optimizes per-operation latency (tens of ms) rather than amortized throughput; batched schemes (BGV/BFV/CKKS) win on bulk SIMD workloads [6].
5. **Circular security.** The bootstrapping key encrypts the secret key under itself — a KDM assumption stronger than plain LWE, shared by all FHEW/TFHE-style systems [2][6].
6. **Univariate only.** A single PBS evaluates $f: \mathbb{Z}_t \to \mathbb{T}$; multivariate functions must be decomposed into univariate steps or evaluated via multiple bootstraps.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Programmable bootstrapping collapses two historically separate operations — noise refresh and function evaluation — into a single homomorphic primitive. The blind rotation turns the secret phase into an encrypted rotation of a test polynomial; the test polynomial turns rotation into table lookup; sample extraction and functional keyswitching turn the lookup result back into a clean, correctly keyed ciphertext. With per-operation latencies in the tens of milliseconds, formal correctness bounds, and security reducible to (circular) ring-LWE, PBS is what makes TFHE a practical engine for encrypted computation rather than merely an asymptotically interesting FHE scheme. Open directions — full-domain functional bootstraps without negacyclic waste [5], multi-value PBS evaluating several LUTs per bootstrap [4], and GPU/ASIC acceleration of the blind rotation's FFT chain — continue to narrow the gap between encrypted and plaintext computation.
