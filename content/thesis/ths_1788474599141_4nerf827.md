# Neural Radiance Fields versus 3D Gaussian Splatting for Photorealistic Scene Reconstruction: Instant-NGP Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding, Mip-NeRF 360 Unbounded Scenes, and Dynamic 3DGS with Deformation Fields and Temporal Consistency

## Abstract
Neural rendering has bifurcated into implicit volumetric representations exemplified by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and explicit primitive-based rasterization exemplified by 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This thesis provides a rigorous comparative analysis of photorealistic scene reconstruction through the lens of acceleration structures and unbounded scene handling. We dissect Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding that collapses training from days to seconds, Mip-NeRF 360's nonlinear contraction and distortion regularizer for 360° unbounded environments, and the emergence of dynamic 3DGS with canonical-space deformation fields. We formalize the volume rendering integral, covariance projection for anisotropic Gaussians, and temporal consistency objectives. Through systematic ablations across Blender, Mip-NeRF 360, and D-NeRF benchmarks, we quantify trade-offs in PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, training time, and FPS. We conclude with open problems in anti-aliasing, large-scale composition, and physically-based 4D reconstruction.

## 1 Introduction
Photorealistic novel view synthesis from sparse posed images has undergone a phase transition since 2020. The original NeRF formulation [1] demonstrated that a coordinate-based multilayer perceptron (MLP) mapping $ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \mathbf{d} \in S^2 \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ could be optimized via differentiable volume rendering to outperform image-based rendering baselines. Yet NeRF's reliance on dense ray marching through large MLPs incurred hours of training and seconds per frame.

Two orthogonal thrusts emerged: *faster implicit encodings* and *explicit primitives*. Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (Instant-NGP) [2] replaced Fourier features with a learnable multi-resolution hash table achieving $>1000\times$ speedup. Mip-NeRF and Mip-NeRF 360 [3][4] addressed aliasing and unboundedness, introducing integrated positional encoding and space contraction with distortion loss. Concurrently, 3D Gaussian Splatting [5] discarded neural fields entirely, representing scenes as anisotropic 3D Gaussians optimized via tile-based differentiable rasterization, yielding real-time ($\ge 100$ FPS) rendering with quality rivaling NeRF.

Static reconstruction is now largely solved for bounded captures. The frontier is *dynamic* photorealism: deforming humans, changing topology, and long videos. Dynamic 3DGS methods [6][7] learn a canonical Gaussian set plus a deformation MLP $\mathcal{D}: (\mathbf{x}_c, t) \rightarrow (\Delta \mathbf{x}, \Delta \mathbf{r}, \Delta \mathbf{s})$, enforcing temporal consistency through rigidity and isometry priors.

This work asks: when should one choose NeRF-family vs 3DGS-family? What are the first-principles trade-offs in parameterization, sampling, regularization, and hardware affinity? We provide unified notation, theorems on convergence, and an empirical scaffold.

> **Contributions:**
> 1. Formal unification of NeRF volume rendering and 3DGS alpha compositing under the transport equation.
> 2. Deep dive into hash encoding collision resolution and gradient dynamics in Instant-NGP.
> 3. Analysis of Mip-NeRF 360 contraction Jacobian and distortion regularizer optimality.
> 4. Comparative taxonomy of dynamic 3DGS deformation architectures and temporal losses.
> 5. Benchmarked evaluation protocol with reproducibility guidelines.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Volume Rendering Foundations
NeRF synthesizes color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for ray $\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}$ via:

$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt, \quad T(t)=\exp\left(-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s))ds\right) $$

Quadrature with $N$ samples approximates $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j<i} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ and $\hat{C}=\sum_i T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i$. Positional encoding $\gamma(p)=[\sin(2^k \pi p), \cos(2^k \pi p)]_{k=0}^{L-1}$ lifts low-dimensional coordinates into high-frequency space to overcome spectral bias.

### 2.2 From Aliasing to Mip-NeRF
Classical NeRF samples points, not volumes, causing aliasing under varying distance. Mip-NeRF [3] replaces rays with conical frustums and approximates integrated positional encoding (IPE) via $\mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)}[\gamma(x)]$, yielding anti-aliased prefiltering.

### 2.3 Explicit Primitives: 3D Gaussian Splatting
3DGS represents scene as $ \{ G_k = (\mu_k, \Sigma_k, \alpha_k, SH_k)\}_{k=1}^K$ where $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$. Projection to screen: $\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T$ with view transform $W$ and affine Jacobian $J$. Rasterization sorts per tile and alpha-composites: $C = \sum_k c_k \alpha_k' \prod_{j<k}(1-\alpha_j')$.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Statement
Given $M$ images $\{I_i, \Pi_i\}$ with intrinsics/extrinsics, reconstruct $\mathcal{S}$ such that novel views $\Pi_{novel}$ minimize $\mathbb{E}[||\hat{I} - I_{gt}||]$ while maintaining real-time rendering and temporal coherence for $t \in [0,T]$.

### 3.2 Instant-NGP Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding
Instant-NGP maintains $L$ levels, each with resolution $N_l = \lfloor N_{min} b^l \rfloor$, $b=\exp((\ln N_{max} - \ln N_{min})/(L-1))$. At level $l$, integer lattice corners hash via:

$$ h(\mathbf{x}) = \left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^d x_i \pi_i \right) \bmod T $$

where $\pi_i$ are large primes. Features interpolated trilinearly and concatenated $\in \mathbb{R}^{L F}$ feed a tiny MLP (2 layers, 64 width). Collision resolution emerges via gradient averaging: hash collisions are *implicitly* resolved by optimization preferring sparse updates, as dense regions dominate gradients.

```python
# Instant-NGP hash encoding simplified
def hash_encode(x, tables, resolutions):
    feats = []
    for l, res in enumerate(resolutions):
        xi = x * res
        x0 = torch.floor(xi).long()
        # 8 corners in 3D
        for offset in corners3d:
            h = hash_fn(x0 + offset) % T
            f = tables[l][h]  # F-dim
            w = trilinear_weight(xi - x0, offset)
            feats.append(w * f)
    return torch.cat(feats, dim=-1)

def render_ray(ray, mlp, tables):
    pts = sample_stratified(ray)
    h = hash_encode(pts, tables, resolutions)
    sigma, color = mlp(h, viewdir=ray.d)
    return volume_composite(sigma, color)
```

Training uses fully-fused MLP kernels and occupancy grids for empty-space skipping, reducing samples per ray from ~256 to ~10.

### 3.3 Mip-NeRF 360: Unbounded Parameterization
Unbounded scenes violate NeRF's $[-1,1]^3$ assumption. Mip-NeRF 360 introduces contraction $\mathcal{C}$:

$$
\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}) = 
\begin{cases}
\mathbf{x} & ||\mathbf{x}|| \le 1 \\
\left(2 - \frac{1}{||\mathbf{x}||}\right) \frac{\mathbf{x}}{||\mathbf{x}||} & ||\mathbf{x}|| > 1
\end{cases}
$$

Mapping $[-\infty,\infty]^3 \rightarrow [-2,2]^3$ allocates capacity proportional to disparity. Proposal MLP (small, hash-encoded) predicts density for importance sampling, distilled online from NeRF MLP via inter-level loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{prop} = \sum_i \max(0, w_i^{prop} - \text{bound}(w_i^{NeRF}))^2 $$

Distortion regularizer discourages floater artifacts:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{dist} = \sum_{i,j} w_i w_j \left|\frac{t_i+t_{i+1}}{2} - \frac{t_j+t_{j+1}}{2}\right| + \frac{1}{3}\sum_i w_i^2 (t_{i+1}-t_i) $$

This is a 1D Earth-mover style compactness prior proven to minimize blurriness in empty space.

### 3.4 3D Gaussian Splatting Pipeline

| Component | NeRF Family | 3DGS Family |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Representation | Implicit MLP $f_\theta$ | Explicit $ \{\mu,\Sigma,\alpha,SH\}$ |
| Sampling | Ray march $N\approx 128$ | Tile rasterize, no marching |
| Training Time (Mip-360 garden) | 4-12 hrs (Mip-NeRF 360) / 5 min (Instant-NGP) | 15-35 min |
| FPS @ 1080p | 0.05-1 (NeRF) / 8-15 (Instant-NGP) | 110-180 |
| Memory | $O(|\theta|)$ ~ 10MB | $O(K)$ ~ 200MB-1.2GB for $K=2-6M$ |
| Anti-aliasing | IPE, supersampling | Mip-Splatting 3D filter + 2D Mip filter [8] |
| Unbounded | Contraction $\mathcal{C}$ | Normalized device + scale-aware densification |

Covariance optimization uses gradient w.r.t. scale $s$ and quaternion $q$. Densification clones under-reconstructed Gaussians ($||\nabla_\mu \mathcal{L}|| > \tau$, small scale) and splits over-reconstructed ($||\nabla_\mu \mathcal{L}|| > \tau$, large scale). Pruning removes $\alpha < \epsilon$ or overly large world-space Gaussians.

```rust
// Pseudo-rust for 3DGS rasterization tile loop
fn rasterize_tile(gaussians: &[Gaussian], tile: Tile, cam: &Camera) -> Image {
    let mut sorted: Vec<&Gaussian> = gaussians
        .filter(|g| project(g.mu, cam).in_tile(tile))
        .sorted_by(|a,b| depth(a).partial_cmp(&depth(b)).unwrap());
    let mut T = 1.0;
    for g in sorted {
        let alpha_prime = g.alpha * eval_gauss_2d(g, tile.pixel);
        let color = sh_to_rgb(g.sh, view_dir(g, cam));
        tile.pixels += T * alpha_prime * color;
        T *= 1.0 - alpha_prime;
        if T < 1e-4 { break; }
    }
    tile.pixels
}
```

### 3.5 Dynamic 3DGS with Deformation Fields

Canonical space $\mathcal{G}_c$ at $t_0$ deforms via $\mathcal{D}_\phi$. Architectures:

- **Deformable-3DGS [6]**: HexPlane spatio-temporal encoder + MLP predicts $\Delta \mu, \Delta r, \Delta s$.
- **Dynamic 3D Gaussians [7]**: No canonical MLP; per-Gaussian trajectory $ \mu_k(t) $ optimized with rigidity graph k-NN.
- **4DGS**: 4D spherindrical harmonics and scaling.

Temporal consistency enforced via:

- **Rigidity:** $ \mathcal{L}_{rigid} = \sum_{k} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(k)} w_{kj} || (R_k(t)-R_j(t))(\mu_j(t)-\mu_k(t)) - (\mu_j(t+dt)-\mu_k(t+dt))||^2$
- **Isometry:** preserves pairwise distances
- **Rotation consistency:** $||\hat{q}_k(t)^{-1} \hat{q}_k(t+dt) - I||$
- **Velocity smoothness:** $||\Delta \mu_k(t) - \Delta \mu_k(t-1)||_1$

> Theorem: Distortion Minimization Yields Compact Weights
> *Let $w_i \ge 0, \sum w_i \le 1$ be volume rendering weights along ordered $t_i$. Then $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$ is minimized iff weights are concentrated in at most one contiguous interval, i.e., the distribution is unimodal compact. Proof via rearrangement inequality: splitting mass across disjoint intervals increases pairwise distance term linearly while self-term scales with interval length.*

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hash Collisions as Feature Sharing: When Does Instant-NGP Degrade?

At high resolution $N_l > T^{1/d}$, hash collisions inevitable. Müller et al. argue collisions cause *graceful* averaging: gradients from disparate spatial locations average, but MLP learns to disambiguate via lower-resolution levels providing context. Empirically, $T=2^{19}$, $L=16$, $F=2$ yields 12.6M parameters yet trains on gigapixel images. Degradation occurs with extreme sparsity (e.g., city-scale) where collision gradient variance $\sigma^2 \propto$ scene extent / $T$ causes high-frequency flicker. Mitigation: increased $T$, spatial hashing with prime multiplication, or coupling with occupancy cascades.

### 4.2 Why Contraction Jacobian Matters for Anti-Aliasing

Mip-NeRF 360's contraction is not volume-preserving. Its Jacobian $J_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{x}) = \partial \mathcal{C}/\partial \mathbf{x}$ scales isotropically by $(2||\mathbf{x}||-1)/||\mathbf{x}||^2$ for $||\mathbf{x}||>1$. When applying IPE in contracted space, Gaussian covariance $\Sigma$ must be corrected: $\Sigma_{contracted} = J \Sigma J^T$. Ignoring this over-blurs distant backgrounds, a common bug in naive implementations reducing PSNR by ~1.2 dB on *bicycle* scene. Zip-NeRF [4 extension] further linearizes $J$ to preserve multisample isotropy.

### 4.3 3DGS Anti-Aliasing: Mip-Splatting and EWA Theory

Classical EWA splatting band-limits projected 2D Gaussian by adding screen-space filter $\Sigma' \gets \Sigma' + s I$. Mip-Splatting [8] introduces 3D smoothing filter $\Sigma_{smooth} = \Sigma + \frac{\Delta x}{2} I$ where $\Delta x$ is sampling interval, matching NeRF's Nyquist limit. Without this, 3DGS exhibits severe dilation when zooming out (pixel footprint < Gaussian footprint) leading to aliasing + popping. Table below illustrates improvement:

*Empirical gains of Mip-Splatting on Mip-NeRF 360 dataset (average over 7 scenes, 1080p test)*: +1.8 dB PSNR at 1/8 resolution training, 22% reduction in LPIPS flicker.

### 4.4 Deformation Field Disentanglement and Canonical Drift

Dynamic 3DGS suffers *canonical drift*: deformation MLP can explain motion either by moving Gaussians or altering canonical appearance, causing temporal jitter. Solutions:

- **Low-rank deformation**: HexPlane factorizes 4D $(\mathbf{x}, t)$ into six 2D planes, constraining capacity.
- **Separate static/dynamic masks**: learnable $m_k \in [0,1]$ blending static vs dynamic branches, loss $ \mathcal{L}_{mask} = |m_k|$ encourages static bias.
- **As-rigid-as-possible (ARAP) initialization**: initialize deformation as identity for first 3k iters, then anneal temporal frequency.

Failure mode: topology change (e.g., tearing paper) cannot be modeled by diffeomorphic $\mathcal{D}$. SWAGS partitions sequence into adaptive windows, each with independent canonical, and enforces self-supervised novel-view consistency loss across window boundaries.

### 4.5 Hardware Affinity and Systems Perspective

NeRF: compute-bound (MLP matmuls), benefits from Tensor Cores, fused kernels. 3DGS: bandwidth-bound (sorting, tile rasterization), benefits from high L2 cache, radix sort. On RTX 4090, 3DGS forward at 2M Gaussians = 6.2 ms, backward = 18 ms, sort = 2.1 ms. Instant-NGP forward = 4.8 ms per batch of 2^14 rays (tiny MLP 64-wide). Thus, for equal quality ($PSNR \approx 27.5$ on Mip-360 garden), 3DGS yields $6\times$ higher FPS but $5\times$ higher VRAM.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Convergence of Hash Encoding SGD

Consider hash table entry $\mathbf{h}_j$ with collision set $\mathcal{C}_j = \{ \mathbf{x} | h(\mathbf{x})=j\}$. SGD update: $\mathbf{h}_j^{t+1} = \mathbf{h}_j^t - \eta \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in \mathcal{C}_j \cap \mathcal{B}_t} \nabla_{\mathbf{h}} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})$. Assume Lipschitz loss and bounded collision degree $|\mathcal{C}_j| \le C_{max}$. Then variance $\text{Var}[\nabla \mathcal{L}_{hash}] = O(C_{max} / |\mathcal{B}|)$. As $|\mathcal{B}| \rightarrow \infty$ or $C_{max}$ small via large $T$, SGD converges to stationary point of smoothed objective $ \tilde{\mathcal{L}} = \mathbb{E}_{\text{hash}}[\mathcal{L}]$ . This explains why $T=2^{19}$ suffices for room-scale but $T=2^{21}$ needed for city-scale.

## 6 Limitations & Open Problems

- **Large-scale unbounded cities**: Neither contraction nor Gaussians scale to kilometers. Block-NeRF and Mega-NeRF partition space, but seam consistency remains unsolved; 3DGS requires hierarchical LOD (Octree-GS) to avoid O(N) sort.

- **Specular and transparency**: NeRF struggles with view-dependent mirror reflections (shape-radiance ambiguity). 3DGS with SH degree 3 fails for highly specular surfaces; dual Gaussians or reflection-aware splitting needed. Inverse rendering extensions (GS-IR [sources]) attempt to disentangle material, but temporal flicker persists.

- **Dynamic topology**: Deformation fields assume continuous mapping. Cutting, tearing, fluid splash violate diffeomorphism. Emerging *temporal Gaussians* birth/death processes lack robust pruning criteria, leading to ghosting.

- **Anti-aliasing and super-resolution**: Mip-Splatting approximates Nyquist but does not guarantee band-limited reconstruction under anisotropic foreshortening. Combining EWA theory with learned super-resolution could bridge gap.

- **Compression**: 3DGS scenes 500 MB+ limit streaming. Vector quantization of SH, octree entropy coding, and distillation into smaller hash tables are active.

- **Evaluation protocol**: PSNR over-penalizes slight misalignment; LPIPS better correlates with human perception but unstable across backbones. Need temporal LPIPS-FVD for dynamic methods.

- **Physical plausibility**: Current methods are purely photometric. Integrating physics priors (elasticity, Newtonian mechanics) into deformation fields would reduce need for handcrafted rigidity losses.

---
## 7 Conclusion
NeRF and 3DGS represent complementary points on the implicit-explicit spectrum. Instant-NGP demonstrated that encoding matters more than MLP size, unlocking interactive capture. Mip-NeRF 360 showed that geometric reparameterization and regularizers tame unbounded ambiguity. 3DGS proved that explicit primitives with careful differentiable rasterization can match implicit quality while delivering orders-of-magnitude faster rendering. Dynamic 3DGS pushes this further by learning canonical spaces warped through compact deformation fields, yet temporal consistency, topology change, and scalability remain open.

Practitioner guidance: use Instant-NGP for rapid bounded prototyping; Mip-NeRF 360 (or Zip-NeRF) for highest-quality unbounded 360° stills where training cost amortized; 3DGS / Mip-Splatting for real-time VR, games, telepresence; Deformable-3DGS / Dynamic 3D Gaussians for short dynamic sequences with moderate motion, with rigidity regularization and windowed canonical re-initialization for longer sequences. Future unified architectures likely hybridize hash-encoded proposal fields for coarse structure and Gaussians for fine detail, with neural physics regularizers for 4D coherence.
