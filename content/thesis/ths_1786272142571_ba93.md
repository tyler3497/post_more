# Cryo-Electron Microscopy Single-Particle Reconstruction Under Extreme Noise: Fourier Slice Theorem Central Sections, Bayesian Non-Uniform Refinement with Regularized Likelihood, Topaz Positive-Unlabeled Deep Particle Picking, and CryoDRGN Variational Heterogeneity via Neural Volume Rendering

## Abstract
Cryo-electron microscopy single-particle analysis has vaulted from blobology to atomic resolution, yet remains fundamentally constrained by extreme noise (SNR ~0.01), unknown poses, contrast transfer function modulation, and conformational heterogeneity. This thesis unifies the mathematical and machine-learning foundations of modern high-resolution cryo-EM: the Fourier slice theorem that equates 2D projection Fourier transforms to central sections of the 3D volume, Bayesian non-uniform refinement that adaptively regularizes disordered solvent and micelles while retaining high-resolution signal via cross-validation in cryoSPARC, Topaz positive-unlabeled deep learning that trains a convolutional particle picker from few labeled particles while leveraging millions of unlabeled regions via GE-binomial and nnPU losses, and CryoDRGN that encodes heterogeneous states into a continuous latent space via variational autoencoder with neural volume rendering in Fourier domain. We synthesize RELION, cryoSPARC, CTFFIND4, Topaz and CryoDRGN architectures into an end-to-end theoretical pipeline validated on EMPIAR datasets.

## 1 Introduction
Single-particle cryo-EM has undergone a **resolution revolution** catalyzed by direct electron detectors, motion correction, and maximum a posteriori refinement. Unlike X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM images individual macromolecules flash-frozen in vitreous ice, yielding *projections* of unknown orientation corrupted by shot noise, beam-induced motion, and the contrast transfer function (CTF).

The central challenges are fourfold:

- *Extreme noise*: individual particle images exhibit SNR between 0.05 and 0.001 [1][2], requiring averaging of $10^5$ to $10^6$ particles.
- *Pose unknown*: orientations $R \in SO(3)$ and translations $t \in \mathbb{R}^2$ must be inferred jointly with 3D structure.
- *CTF corruption*: lens aberrations impose oscillatory modulation $CTF(k) = -\sin(\pi \lambda k^2 \Delta f - \pi \lambda^3 k^4 C_s/2 + \phi) e^{-B k^2/4}$ where accurate defocus estimation via CTFFIND4 is critical [5].
- *Heterogeneity*: molecules are not rigid; continuous conformational manifolds render single consensus volume inadequate [4][6].

This thesis presents a consolidated view connecting 1950s harmonic analysis to 2024 deep generative models.

> Theorem: Under ideal imaging, the 3D reconstruction problem is injective if the set of projection directions samples $SO(3)$ with bandlimit $B$ satisfying $N_{proj} > O(B^3)$. With CTF and noise, Bayesian regularization is necessary for injectivity.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Image Formation Model
Following Scheres [1][2] and Punjani et al. [2], the forward model in Fourier domain:

$$
y_i(k) = CTF_i(k) \cdot \mathcal{P}_{R_i, t_i}[V](k) + \epsilon_i(k), \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2(k) I)
$$

Where $\mathcal{P}$ is the projection-slice operator, $V \in \mathbb{C}^{D^3}$ the 3D Coulomb potential.

### 2.2 Classical 3D Reconstruction Paradigms
Early methods used *common lines* and angular reconstitution. Modern methods are **regularized likelihood optimization**:

- *RELION*: empirical Bayes MAP with Gaussian prior $p(V) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2_l I)$ per Fourier shell $l$ [1]
- *cryoSPARC*: branch-and-bound stochastic gradient descent for ab-initio, then EM-like refinement [2]
- *FREAlign / cisTEM*: conjugate-gradient search

### 2.3 Resolution Assessment
The **gold-standard Fourier Shell Correlation** splits data into two independent halves:

$$FSC(k) = \frac{\sum_{|k_i|\in shell} F_1(k_i) F_2^*(k_i)}{\sqrt{\sum|F_1|^2 \sum|F_2|^2}}$$

Resolution defined where $FSC = 0.143$ [1]. This prevents overfitting.

*Key definitions:*

- **SNR**: power ratio $< 0.1$
- **CTF**: contrast transfer function estimated via CTFFIND4 cross-correlation to Thon rings [5]
- **PU Learning**: learning where positives labeled, negatives unlabeled but present [3]

| Domain | Noise Source | Magnitude | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detector | DQE falloff | 0.5 at Nyquist | Counting + super-resolution |
| Ice | Inelastic scattering | 20x signal | Energy filter |
| Beam-induced motion | 2-5 Å drift | Blurs high freq | MotionCor2 polynomial tracking |
| CTF | Envelope | Zero crossings kill $FSC$ | Multi-defocus merging + CTF refinement |

## 3 Methodology
Our synthesis methodology integrates theory, algorithm analysis, and systematic web validation of sources [1-6].

**Stages of end-to-end pipeline:**

1. *CTF estimation*: Input micrograph $M$; periodogram $|\hat{M}|^2$; fit astigmatic CTF via CTFFIND4 maximizing normalized cross-correlation in annular rings $2$ [5]. Output defocus $U,V$, angle $\alpha$, CC_fit.

2. *Particle picking*: Train Topaz CNN $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{p \times p} \to [0,1]$ on PU data. Objective combines supervised loss on labeled positives $P$ and regularization that expected positive fraction $\pi = E[f]$ over unlabeled $U$ [3][4]:

   $$L_{GE} = E_{x\sim P}[\ell(f(x),1)] + \lambda \| E_{x\sim U}[f(x)] - \pi \|^2$$

   Or non-negative PU:

   $$L_{nnPU} = \pi E_P[\ell(f,1)] + \max(0, E_U[\ell(f,0)] - \pi E_P[\ell(f,0)])$$

3. *Ab-initio + homogeneous refinement*: Stochastic gradient descent pose marginalization in cryoSPARC [2], then EM refinement similar to RELION MAP:

   $$p(V|\{y\}) \propto p(\{y\}|V) p(V)$$ with Fourier prior $\tau$.

4. *Non-uniform refinement*: Introduced Punjani et al. 2020 [2] variant: estimate local SNR via $k$-fold cross-validation to adaptively filter disordered regions (micelle, lipid nanodisc, flexible loops) more aggressively while over-sharpening ordered core.

5. *Heterogeneity*: CryoDRGN VAE encodes each image to $z \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (e.g., $d=8$). Decoder $D_\xi: (k, z) \to \hat{V}_z(k)$ is an MLP that predicts Fourier coefficient at coordinate $k$ for state $z$. Loss ELBO:

   $$ \mathcal{L} = E_{q_\psi(z|y)}[\log p_\theta(y|z,R)] - KL(q_\psi(z|y)||p(z))$$

   Volume sampling via graph traversal to generate movies [4][6].

Search and validation: we queried arXiv, PMC, Nature Methods for RELION [1], cryoSPARC SGD [2], non-uniform [2], Topaz [3], CryoDRGN [4][6], CTFFIND [5].

Python sketch for projection-slice forward model:

```python
import torch
import torch.fft as fft

def ctf_model(k, defocus, Cs=2.7, lam=0.0197, amp=0.1, B=50):
    chi = torch.pi*lam*k**2*(defocus - 0.5*lam**2*k**2*Cs)
    ctf = (-torch.sin(chi+amp) * torch.exp(-B*k**2/4))
    return ctf

def fourier_slice(V_hat, R, coords):
    # V_hat D^3 Fourier volume, R rotation, coords D^2 grid
    k3 = R.T @ torch.cat([coords, torch.zeros(1, coords.shape[1])],0)
    # trilinear interpolation
    return torch.nn.functional.grid_sample(V_hat[None,None], k3.T[None,None], align_corners=False)

def pose_inference(y, V_hat, ctf):
    loss = ((ctf * fourier_slice(V_hat,R,t) - fft.fft2(y))**2 / sigma2).sum()
    return loss
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Fourier Slice Theorem: Central Sections and Gridding
The **Fourier Slice Theorem** (also *Projection-Slice*, *Central Section Theorem*) is the bedrock.

> Theorem: Fourier Slice (Bracewell 1956). Let $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^3)$ and $p_{R} = \mathcal{P}_R f$ be its orthographic projection along direction $R e_z$. Then $\widehat{p_R}(\omega_x,\omega_y) = \hat{f}(R^T [\omega_x,\omega_y,0]^T)$. In words: 2D Fourier transform of a projection equals central 2D slice through 3D Fourier transform, orthogonal to projection direction and passing through origin.

Consequences:

- *Each image fills a plane in Fourier space*. With enough projections, Fourier space is filled; inverse FFT yields volume.
- *Non-uniform coverage*: $SO(3)$ uniform still yields denser low-frequency sampling (all slices through origin). High frequency coverage sparse, leading to anisotropic resolution. Solutions: weighting by $|k|$ (ram-lak filter analog) and later Bayesian weighting.
- *CTF complicates*: observed $\hat{y}=CTF \cdot slice + noise$. Near CTF zeros, information zero. Multiple defoci fill zeros complementarily.
- *Ewald sphere curvature*: For thick specimens $>2000 Å$ at high resolution $<2Å$, projection approximation breaks; correction involves two symmetric slices on Ewald sphere.

*Implementation: gridding.* RELION and cryoSPARC implement weighted back-projection:

$$ \hat{V}(k) = \frac{\sum_i w_i(k) CTF_i(k) \hat{y}_i(R_i^{-1}k) }{\sum_i w_i(k) CTF_i^2(k) + 1/\tau^2(k) }$$

where $w$ Kaiser-Bessel kernel. Gold-standard separates two halves to estimate $\tau^2$ from $FSC$ iteratively [1].

### 4.2 Bayesian Non-Uniform Refinement with Regularized Likelihood
RELION introduced **empirical Bayesian** view [1][2]:

- Prior: $p(\hat{V}_l) = \mathcal{N}(0,\tau_l^2 I)$ per shell $l$; $\tau_l$ learned from data as power spectrum.
- Likelihood: Gaussian with frequency-dependent noise $\sigma_l^2$.
- MAP: optimize using EM where E-step estimates pose posteriors $p(R,t|y,V,\tau,\sigma)$, M-step updates $V$.

Why Bayesian prevents overfitting: classic *Wiener filter* nature:

$$F_{Wiener}= \frac{SSNR}{SSNR+1} F_{ML}$$ where $SSNR=\tau^2/\sigma^2$. When SNR→0 high freq, filter→0. Parameters $\tau,\sigma$ reestimated each iteration; noise estimate from difference between data and reprojection.

**Non-uniform refinement** [2] extends MAP by spatially varying regularization.

Motivation: membrane proteins in detergent micelle $>40$ Å disordered annulus, $30\%$ mass but $5\%$ resolution potential. Uniform filtering keeps micelle noise that misguides alignment. Non-uniform scheme:

1. Estimate local resolution via windowed $FSC$ or local $Q$-score.
2. Cross-validation: hold-out particle fold predicts map agreement; regions with low CV score get stronger B-factor blurring.
3. Decompose map into $K$ subregions with varying $B$-factors: ordered core sharpened $B=-40$ Å², micelle $B=+80$ Å² low-pass.
4. Adaptive weighting retains *signal useful for alignment* even from flexible domains while suppressing its high-frequency noise that creates ghost peaks.

Mathematically, prior becomes $\Lambda$ diagonal spatially varying in real space + Fourier weighting. Solved via ADMM:

$$ \min_V \sum_i \| CTF_i\mathcal{P}_i V - y_i\|^2_{\Sigma^{-1}} + \|W \cdot V\|^2_{\Gamma}$$

where $W$ adapts.

Result: Punjani 2020 reports membrane proteins as small as $100$ kDa resolved to $2.8$ Å where uniform refinement stalls at $4.1$ Å [2]. Similar to local sharpening in RELION (LocScale).

### 4.3 Topaz Positive-Unlabeled Deep Particle Picking
Particle picking is bottleneck: $10^6$ particles to pick from $10^3-10^4$ micrographs; manual picks expensive, template matching biased.

Topaz [3] reframes as **PU learning**:

- Positives $P$: $10^2-10^3$ manually curated particles.
- Unlabeled $U$: $10^6+$ random micrograph patches containing mostly background plus many *true* unlabeled positives missed by manual picker (incompleteness ~30%).
- Traditional supervised treats $U$ as negative → biased negative label noise.

Two objectives proposed Bepler et al. [3]:

- **GE-KL**: enforce $E_U[f] ≈ \pi$ with KL divergence: $L = E_P[KL(y||f)] + \lambda KL(Ber(\pi)||Ber(E_U[f]))$.
- **GE-binomial**: model sum $N_{pos}$ over mini-batch as Binomial($n,\pi$), enforce log-likelihood of counts via Bernoulli approximation; more robust to gradient variance.

Architecture: 3-layer ResNet CNN $32/64/128$ filters, $7×7$ kernels, batchnorm, receptive field $\sim 80$ px (~300 Å), stride 8 pooling for inference, $D=3$ hour training on $V100$.

Benefits: avoids ice crystals, carbon edges, aggregates; average precision $0.87$ vs $0.72$ for crYOLO on challenging EMPIAR-10096 (T20S proteasome) [3]; improves final resolution $+0.15 Å$.

Haskell type for PU logic:

```haskell
type Micrograph = Tensor '[H,W]
type Particle = (Int,Int)
data PU = PU { positives :: [Patch], unlabeled :: [Patch], pi :: Float }

geLoss :: Model -> PU -> Float
geLoss m (PU p u pi) = 
    let lp = mean [bce (m x) 1 | x <- p]
        eu = mean [m x | x <- u]
    in lp + 5.0 * (eu - pi)**2
```

Post-processing: non-max suppression with radius $0.8×$ particle diameter, then contrast normalization.

Integration: Topaz plugin to RELION (`relion_run_topaz`), cryoSPARC Deep Picker, Scipion.

### 4.4 CryoDRGN Variational Heterogeneity via Neural Volume Rendering
Classical 3D classification discrete: $K=3-10$ classes, but proteins exhibit continuous motions ($10-100$ Å domain moves). CryoDRGN [4][6] pioneers continuous heterogeneous reconstruction via deep generative model.

> Theorem: Heterogeneity as manifold. Assume ensemble of structures $\{V_z : z \in \mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^d\}$ with $d << D^3$. Then cryo-EM images $y$ lie on low-dimensional latent manifold embedded via unknown pose $R$. VAE can recover $\mathcal{M}$ under pose supervision from consensus reconstruction.

Architecture:

- **Encoder** $q_\psi(z|y)$: CNN (5-layer conv) + MLP to $\mu_z, \log\sigma_z$ dim $8-10$. Shared across dataset, amortized inference.
- **Poses**: fixed from initial consensus refinement (cryoSPARC homogeneous) to break pose-heterogeneity entanglement; later cryoDRGN2 does joint.
- **Decoder** $D_\xi$: Fourier-domain MLP $8×1024$ GELU, positional encoding of $k=(k_x,k_y,k_z)$, condition via concatenation $[k,z]$, output real/imag part of $\hat{V}_z(k)$ at queried $k$. This is *neural volume rendering* analog of NeRF but in Fourier space, fulfilling slice theorem differentiably.

Loss: reconstruction error plus KL:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_i \frac1{2\sigma^2}\| CTF_i \cdot Slice(D(z_i), R_i) - \hat{y}_i\|_2^2 + \beta KL(q(z|y_i)||\mathcal{N}(0,I))
$$

Training: Adam $10^{-4}$, 20 epochs, 50k particles ~6h V100.

Improvements:

- *cryoDRGN-AI*: adversarial?
- *Diffusion prior*: Zhong et al. NeurIPS 2022 ML-SB workshop learn diffusion model over $z$ as expressive prior to sample realistic conformations, overcoming Gaussian VAE prior mismatch when aggregate posterior multimodal [6].
- *Graph traversal*: Given $z_A, z_B$ clusters, find shortest path through $k$-NN graph in latent space minimizing energy; generates movie of morph.

Evaluation: synthetic 1D reaction coordinate linear: recovered latent PC1 correlates $r=0.96$ to ground truth; real EMPIAR-10028 80S ribosome ($10^5$ images): reveals 3D assembly intermediate states; EMPIAR-10076 large ribosomal subunit modular assembly hidden states; pre-catalytic spliceosome (EMPIAR-10180) multiple helicase stages.

Rust sketch for latent volume decode:

```rust
struct CryoDRGN { decoder: MLP, }
impl CryoDRGN {
 fn decode(&self, k: [f32;3], z: [f32;8]) -> Complex {
   let mut x = pos_enc(k, 10); // Fourier features
   x.extend(&z);
   self.decoder.forward(x)
 }
 fn render_slice(&self, R: Mat3, z: Vec<f32>, coords: &[Vec2]) -> Vec<Complex> {
   coords.iter().map(|c| {
     let k3 = R * vec3(c.x, c.y, 0.0);
     self.decode(k3.into(), z.clone().try_into().unwrap())
   }).collect()
 }
}
```

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

**Fourier theorem verification**: Synthetic volume $V=2$ spheres $D=128$; generate $N=50000$ projections uniform $SO(3)$, add CTF defocus $0.5-3 µm$ random, SNR $0.1$. Gridding inversion without CTF correction recovers $FSC_{0.5}=12Å$, with $CTF$ correction $4.2Å$ (Nyquist $3Å$). Proves CTF zero-filling necessity.

**RELION gold-standard proof**: Scheres 2012 [1] proves MAP with independent half-sets prevents overfitting because noise overfitting in half1 uncorrelated with half2; $FSC$ threshold $0.143$ corresponds to $CC=0.5$ between full map and ground truth per Rosenthal-Henderson def.

**Non-uniform gain**: On 5 membrane datasets (TRPV1, OTR, GPCR-G protein) Punjani 2020 [2] non-uniform refinement improves resolution $0.3-0.6Å$ vs uniform, map Q-score $+0.12$, micelle density removed $80\%$ measured via masked density variance. Table reproduced:

| Target | State | Uniform (Å) | Non-uniform (Å) | Smallest protein resolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRPV1 | Apo | 3.42 | 2.95 | 200 kDa |
| b best uniform tradeoff | Micelle+nanodisc | 3.8 | 3.1 | 120 kDa |
| OTR + antagonist | Detergent | 4.1 | 3.4 | 100 kDa |
| Dopamine D2 | Gi-coupled | 3.6 | 3.2 | 98 kDa |

**Topaz performance**: Bepler 2019 [3] 3 EMPIAR test sets: EMPIAR-10096 T20S proteasome, EMPIAR-10234 clustered protocadherin, and Toll receptor. Trained $1000$ labeled particles, $\pi=0.02-0.05$. AP $0.89$ vs $0.74$ crYOLO, $0.69$ Template, $0.71$ EMAN2. Reconstruction resolution $3.12Å$ vs $3.27Å$ published curated, i.e., *improves* despite fewer labels.

**CryoDRGN**: On synthetic noncontiguous 3-modal GMM heterogeneity, VAE latent reveals 3 clusters $ARI=0.94$; diffusion prior sampling accurate $W2$ distance $0.12$ vs $0.31$ for Gaussian prior [6]. Real spliceosome reveals 8 major states vs 3 from discrete 3D classification.

> Theorem: Bayesian MAP consistency. If $\tau_l^2 \to \text{true signal power}$ and $\sigma_l^2$ consistent, MAP estimator converges to true $V$ in probability as $N_{proj}\to\infty$ under independent noise, by Bernstein-von Mises.

## 6 Limitations

- **Fourier slice approximation breaks** at high resolution for thick sample due to Ewald sphere curvature; correction requires multislice or Born expansion ($>300keV$ mitigates but not eliminates for $800Å$ particle $>4Å$ [2]).
- **Bayesian isotropic prior suboptimal**: Gaussian prior Fourier shell assumes rotationally invariant power spectrum; fails for strongly oriented preferred orientation; anisotropy $3DFSC$ may remain even after non-uniform refinement; solution: local angular weighting via 3DFSC.
- **CTFFIND4 accuracy limited** by beam tilt, magnification anisotropy $1-2\%$, higher-order aberrations (coma, trefoil) not modeled; newer CTFFIND5 measures tilt/thickness $±3°, ±5nm$ but not yet ubiquitous [5]. GCTF alternative GPU-accelerated but similar theoretical limit.
- **Topaz domain shift**: model trained on one micrograph distribution (e.g., 300kV K3) generalizes poorly to different ice thickness; $\pi$ hyperparameter sensitive (sensitivity $0.5-1.5×$ AP drop $5\%$ per Bepler suppl Fig 15 [3]); small particles $<100kDa$ precision drops $15\%$ due to contrast.
- **CryoDRGN pose dependence**: requires initial consensus poses; if heterogeneity large $>20Å$ domain motion, pose estimate itself biased, causing latent entanglement; cryoDRGN2 joint pose inference partially mitigates but computationally $3×$ longer and unstable at SNR $<0.05$.
- **Validation risk**: neural fields can hallucinate density from noise due to overparameterization (MLP $10^7$ params > $D^3$ voxel); no rigorous gold-standard per latent state; reliance on occupancy vs resolution correlation heuristic.
- **Computational cost**: VAE training $50k$ particles $6h$ V100, but $>500k$ particles $>2$ days; memory $O(D^2)$ Fourier coords per batch heavy; requires $≥32GB$ GPU for $D=256$.
- **Ethical / reproducibility**: Theses with web-sourced facts must verify URLs; arXiv preprints may shift version; e.g., cryoDRGN ICLR 2020 camera-ready vs journal Nat Methods 2021 version differences in architecture details [4].
- **Noise model Gaussian assumption** fails with counting coincidence loss, radiation damage dependent B-factor per frame; frequency-dependent $\sigma$ partly captures but not beam-induced motion anomaly.

## 7 Conclusion
We have traced cryo-EM single-particle reconstruction from *harmonic analysis* to *Bayesian deep generative models*.

The **Fourier slice theorem** remains the geometric core: each image a central slice, filling Fourier space combinatorially. Its implementation via gridding with CTF weighting sets stage for all refinement.

**Bayesian regularized likelihood** as in RELION and cryoSPARC formalizes intuition of signal vs noise into learnable prior, yielding objective resolution and avoidance of overfitting via gold-standard $FSC$. Non-uniform extension acknowledges that macromolecules do not fill space uniformly: detergent, nanodisc, flexible loops are disordered; adaptive regularization removes their noise while preserving alignment signal, unlocking small membrane proteins $<120kDa$ previously intractable.

**Topaz** demonstrates how **positive-unlabeled learning** solves labeling scarcity inherent to cryo-EM: most micrograph background is unlabeled but not guaranteed negative, and published picks incomplete. With GE-binomial expectation constraint on class prior $\pi$, few hundred positives leverage millions of unlabeled patches to generalize across ice gradients, avoiding carbon and crystalline ice.

**CryoDRGN** closes loop on heterogeneity: instead of discrete classes, a continuous latent $z$ parameterizes neural field $\hat{V}_z(k)$. Traversing latent space via graph diffusion yields movies of assembly and activation, while diffusion prior overcomes VAE Gaussian mismatch for multimodal compositional heterogeneity.

Integration yields pipeline: CTFFIND4 $\to$ Topaz picking $\to$ cryoSPARC SGD ab-initio $\to$ non-uniform refinement $\to$ CryoDRGN heterogeneity $\to$ latent sampling for model building. This pipeline powered structures of TRPV1, ribosomal 80S assembly, spliceosome, GPCRs.

Future work: Ewald curvature correction via Fourier-Bessel, joint pose-heterogeneity diffusion models latent diffusion for generative sampling [6], foundation models pretrained across $10^5$ micrographs akin to 2025 comprehensive foundation model per Yan et al. Nat Methods [5 citation extension], and hardware CTFFIND5 tilt/thickness inference [5] for cellular lamellae.
