# Differentially Private Synthetic Data: PATE-GAN, DP-SGD with RDP Accounting, and Private Evolution via API

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous, artifact-evaluable treatment of differentially private synthetic data: pate-gan, dp-sgd with rdp accounting, and private evolution via api, synthesizing classical foundations with 2026 systems practice. We formalize threat models, cost semantics, and correctness invariants spanning differential privacy synthetic data generation, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX/ACM, and industry specifications. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale workloads and hardware traces including H100 CC, 96c Genoa, NVLink4, RoCEv2 400G, NVMe ZNS, and Loihi2 neuromorphic. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001, bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, and ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance, mapped to open problems. Contributions include taxonomy, reusable artifacts, and deployment roadmap for verified, efficient, scalable differential privacy synthetic data generation production systems.
