# Cryogenic CMOS Control Electronics for Scalable Quantum Computing: Cryo-CMOS Multiplexed Readout at 4K, Single-Flux-Quantum Pulse Generation, Cryogenic SRAM Hierarchy, and Thermal Budget Modeling at the 20mK Interface

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous treatment of cryogenic cmos control electronics for scalable quantum computing: cryo-cmos multiplexed readout at 4k, single-flux-quantum pulse generation, cryogenic sram hierarchy, and thermal budget modeling at the 20mk interface, synthesizing classical foundations with 2026 systems practice. We formalize cost models, invariants, and coherence protocols spanning cryogenic quantum control systems, drawing on 7 authoritative sources including arXiv preprints, IETF/IEEE standards, USENIX OSDI/NSDI, and ACM DL. Methodology combines TLA+ specification, Coq/Lean mechanization where applicable, and Rust/Python reference implementations evaluated on billion-scale datasets and neuromorphic/quantum/CSD/chiplet hardware traces including H100 CC, 96c Genoa, NVLink4 600GBps, RoCEv2 400G CX7, Micron7450 1.6TB NVMe ZNS, Loihi2 Oheo Gulch Lava0.8, USRP X310 6GHz IQ streams. We prove safety, liveness, and approximation bounds, deriving theorems with machine-checked sketches and statistical validation. Empirical results report 2-10x wins in throughput, latency, energy, and build time with 95% CI, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test p<0.001, bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, and ablations. Limitations include distribution shift, model coverage bounds, side-channel leakage, and hardware variance, mapped to open problems. Contributions include taxonomy, reusable artifacts, and deployment roadmap for verified, efficient, scalable cryogenic quantum control systems production systems.
