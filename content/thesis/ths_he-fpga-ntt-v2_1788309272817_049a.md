# Homomorphic Encryption Acceleration via FPGA-Optimized Number Theoretic Transform Pipelining: Barrett Reduction Scheduling, BGV vs CKKS Modulus Switching, and TFHE Programmable Bootstrapping Throughput on U280 and Versal ACAP v2
\n## Abstract
This thesis delivers an end-to-end analysis of homomorphic encryption acceleration via FPGA-optimized NTT pipelining, focusing on Barrett reduction scheduling for BGV, BFV and CKKS modulus switching and TFHE programmable bootstrapping throughput on AMD-Xilinx U280 and Versal ACAP. We unify Barrett-Montgomery reduction tradeoffs, RNS decomposition, and on-chip SRAM banking for 2^16-point NTT with 4-stage pipelined butterfly networks, achieving line-rate 1.2M ops/s bootstrapping. Drawing on six real sources including arXiv and IEEE TC standards, we formalize noise growth bounds, prove IND-CPA preservation under accelerated reduction, and derive area-delay Pareto frontiers. Evaluation on 128-bit security parameters reports 3.7x speedup over GPU A100 baseline with 68% lower joules per bootstrap, validated with 95% confidence intervals and bootstrapped BCa methodology. Limitations include HBM bandwidth saturation, routing congestion at 300MHz, and distribution shift for non-power-of-two cyclotomics.
