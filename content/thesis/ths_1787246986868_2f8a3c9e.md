# Asymptotically Good Quantum LDPC Codes: Expander Lifted Product, BP-OSD Decoding, Trapping Sets, and Fault-Tolerant Memory Thresholds ## Abstract Quantum low-density parity-check codes achieving constant rate and linear distance resolve the two-decade qLDPC conjecture, with Panteleev–Kalachev lifted products and Leverrier–Zémor quantum Tanner codes delivering asymptotically good [[n, Θ(n), Θ(n)]] families from non-abelian lifts and left-right Cayley square complexes. We synthesize recent decoder evolution from Poulin-Chung belief propagation to belief propagation with ordered-statistics post-processing, BP-LSD, and fully parallel speculative BP-SF, formalizing degeneracy, stabilizer-induced trapping sets, and convergence failure modes that dominate low error floors. Methodology combines expansion analysis of bipartite Ramanujan graphs, quantum expander mixing lemmas, and circuit-level fault-tolerant memory simulations under depth-7 syndrome extraction to bound pseudothresholds near 0.7% with 10× overhead reduction over surface codes. We report Tanner graph parameter trade-offs, logical error rate extrapolations to gigaquop and teraquop regimes, and open barriers to real-time <2 ms GPU decoding for [[288,12]] bivariate bicycle instantiations. ## 1 Introduction ***Asymptotically good quantum LDPC codes*** close a central open problem in quantum error correction: the existence of families of sparse stabilizer codes with *both* constant rate $R = k/n = \Theta(1)$ and linear distance $d = \Theta(n)$ [1][2]. Classical LDPC codes have long enjoyed such families via Gallager and Margulis expanders [3], yet quantum degeneracy and sparse commutation constraints $H_X H_Z^T = 0$ made the quantum analog elusive until 2021–2022 [1][4]. *Why it matters in 2026*: In a fault-tolerant architecture, memory overhead dominates resource estimates. The rotated surface code $[[d^2,1,d]]$ achieves $R\to 0$, requiring $>4000$ physical qubits for 12 logical qubits at $10^{-7}$ target block failure over $10^7$ cycles, while recent bivariate bicycle and lifted-product families achieve the same with $288$ total qubits at $p=0.1\%$ circuit noise [5][6]. However **decoding** remains the bottleneck: degenerate stabilizers create many low-weight codewords in the Tanner graph that trap iterative decoders. > **Theorem 1.1 (Panteleev–Kalachev, Informal).** *For any $\epsilon>0$ there exists $n_0$ such that for all $n>n_0$ there exists a CSS LDPC code with parity-check weight $w\le O(1)$, dimension $k\ge \epsilon n$, and distance $d\ge \epsilon n / \log n$; with two-sided robustly testable lifts $d\ge \epsilon n$.* Proven in [1] via expander lifted products over non-abelian groups with small-set expansion; refined in [4] to linear distance via quantum Tanner codes over left-right Cayley complexes. **Contributions of this thesis:** - *Construction taxonomy* mapping hypergraph product $d=\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ → lifted product $d=\Theta(n/\log n)$ → quantum Tanner $d=\Theta(n)$ with lift size, group order, and local code parameters. - *Decoder analysis* unifying BP, BP-OSD order $o=0..10$, BP-LSD localized Gaussian elimination, and fully parallelizable BP-SF speculative syndrome flips with latency vs logical-error Pareto curves [7][8]. - *Trapping set formalization* for quantum degeneracy: symmetric $(a,b)$ trapping sets, absorbing sets induced by stabilizers, and oscillation dynamics that explain 70% of BP failures on $[[144,12,12]]$ BB codes [7]. - *Fault-tolerant memory evaluation* with concrete syndrome extraction depth-7 CNOT schedules decomposable into two degree-3 planar subgraphs, threshold estimates $p_{th}\approx 0.6$–$0.7\%$ under circuit-level depolarizing noise [5], and extrapolation to gigaquop $10^9$ and teraquop $10^{12}$ regimes [6]. --- ## 2 Background ### 2.1 Quantum LDPC Preliminaries A CSS quantum LDPC code $\mathcal{Q} = \text{CSS}(H_X,H_Z)$ defined by binary matrices $H_X\in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_X \times n}$, $H_Z\in \mathbb{F}_2^{m_Z \times n}$ such that $H_X H_Z^T = 0$. Parameters $[[n,k,d]]$ with $k = n - \text{rank}\,H_X - \text{rank}\,H_Z$ and $d = \min_{c\in \ker H_X \setminus \text{rs}(H_Z)} |c|$. Sparsity requires row and column weight $\le w = O(1)$. *Bounded-degree Tanner graph* $\mathcal{T}=(V\cup C,E)$ where $V=n$ qubit nodes, $C=m_X+m_Z$ checks, $\deg(v)\le d_v$, $\deg(c)\le d_c$. > **Definition 2.1 (Asymptotically Good).** A family $\{\mathcal{Q}_i\}$ is asymptotically good if there exists $R_0>0, \delta_0>0$ such that $k_i/n_i \ge R_0$ and $d_i/n_i \ge \delta_0$ for all $i$. ### 2.2 Before 2020: The $\sqrt{n}$ Barrier | Family | Rate | Distance | Key Limitation | |--------|------|----------|----------------| | Surface / toric $[[O(d^2),1,d]]$ | $\to 0$ | $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ | Vanishing rate | | Hypergraph product $\mathcal{C}_1\times\mathcal{C}_2$ [9] | $\Theta(1)$ | $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ | Commutation forces product distance | | Hyperbolic 4-manifolds (Guth–Lubotzky) | $\Theta(1)$ | $\Theta(\sqrt{n}\log n)$? Actually $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ | Non-LDPC weight? | | Fiber bundle (Hastings–Haah–O'Donnell) | $\tilde\Theta(n^{3/5})$ rate | $\tilde\Theta(n^{3/5})$ | Randomized construction, polylog weight | | Balanced product (Breuckmann-Eberhardt) | $\tilde\Theta(n^{4/5})$ | $\tilde\Theta(n^{3/5})$ | Close but not linear | Informally, tensor-product structure enforced $H_X H_Z^T=0$ at expense of distance [1][3]. ### 2.3 Breakthrough 2021–2022 **Panteleev–Kalachev Lifted Product [1]:** Replace entries of $H_1\otimes I$ with elements of group algebra $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$. Let Tanner code from base expander $G_0=(V_0\cup C_0,E_0)$ with lift $\ell: E_0\to G$. Lifted product $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_1 \otimes_G \mathcal{C}_2$ achieves $k=\Theta(n^{\alpha}\log n)$, $d=\Theta(n^{1-\alpha/2}/\log n)$; for $\alpha\to1$ yields $d=\Theta(n/\log n)$ [1]. Requires two-sided lossless expansion and robust testability of local $A\otimes B$ product codes. **Leverrier–Zémor Quantum Tanner Codes [4]:** Generalize Sipser–Spielman [10] to quantum. Use left-right Cayley complex $\text{Cay}_2(A,G,B)$ with vertices $V_{00}=G$, $V_{10}=G$, $V_{01}=G$, $V_{11}=G$, squares $(g,ag,gb,agb)$. Local codes $C_A\otimes C_B$ and $C_A^\perp \otimes C_B^\perp$ enforce commutation via face-vertex incidence. Expander mixing lemma for squares yields small-set co-boundary expansion, proving $d=\Theta(n)$ [4]. Decoder from [2] (see below) corrects $\Omega(n)$ adversarial errors in linear time with alternating sequential/parallel expansion-fix. *Moderate-length twist*: covering lifts of small base quantum Tanner codes preserve $k$ for odd index $t$ and bound $d'\ge d$, $d'\le t\cdot d$, yielding record $[[96,2,12]]$ with weight-8/half weight-4 checks [11]. --- ## 3 Methodology We adopt *construction–decoding–threshold* pipeline verified against arXiv sources and numerical sanity checks. 1. **Construction validation:** Check CSS commutativity $H_X H_Z^T=0$ symbolically for polynomial pair formalism $H_X=[A|B]$, $H_Z=[B^T|A^T]$ where $A,B\in\mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^l-1)\otimes \mathbb{F}_2[y]/(y^m-1)$ for bivariate bicycle codes $n=2lm$ [5][12]. Group lift condition $G$ non-abelian of size $\ell$ generated from base matrix $W$ over $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$. 2. **Expander proof technique:** Use Ramanujan graphs $d$-regular with second eigenvalue $\lambda_2\le 2\sqrt{d-1}$. Small-set (co)boundary expansion $\ge \epsilon |S|$ for $|S|\le \gamma n$ ensures logical operators cannot be supported on sublinear sparse sets [1][2][4]. Quantitative bound in [1] Lemma 4.7: if $|\partial S|\ge (1-\delta)d|S|$ then code distance $d_{\min}\ge \Omega(n)$. 3. **Decoder specification:** - BP: min-sum $L_{v\to c} = L_v^0 + \sum_{c'\ eq c} L_{c'\to v}$, $L_{c\to v}=(-1)^{s_c} \cdot \min_{v'\ eq v}|L_{v'\to c}| \cdot \prod_{v'\ eq v}\text{sgn}(L_{v'\to c})$. - OSD-0/$w$: sort variable nodes by reliability $|L_v|$, pick information set $I$ of size $k$ linearly independent columns, solve $H_I e_I = s$ via Gaussian elimination, brute force flip $w$ least-reliable bits among $n-k$ [13]. - BP-LSD: partition residual graph after BP failure into overlapping clusters $S_i$ with $|S_i|\le \kappa$, independent GF(2) solves; complexity $O(\kappa^3 \cdot N_{clusters})$ vs $O(n^3)$ global OSD [8]. - BP-SF (fully parallel): monitor oscillation pattern $o_v(t)=\mathbb{I}[\hat e_v^{(t)}\ eq \hat e_v^{(t-1)}]$ over $T=50$ iterations, pick $M$ unreliable bits, generate $2^M$ candidate syndrome flips $\tilde s = s \oplus H \delta$, decode each with 5-iteration BP in parallel [7]. 4. **Trapping set enumeration:** For QLDPC, $(a,b)$-trapping set $\mathcal{T}=(a$ variable nodes, $b$ odd-degree checks in induced subgraph $)$. Quantum-specific: *symmetric stabilizer trapping set* where two equiprobable degenerate errors $e_1, e_2 = e_1 + s$, $s\in S$ have identical syndrome, causing BP posterior $p(e_1|s)\approx p(e_2|s)$ and non-convergence; classified in [14][15]. 5. **Fault-tolerant threshold simulation:** Circuit-level depolarizing model $p$ per gate/SPAM/idling, syndrome extraction schedule depth $\delta=7$ (6 CNOT + measure), ancilla count $n_{anc}=m$. Perform Monte Carlo $N=10^6$ shots per $p$, fit pseudothreshold $p^*$ via $\text{LER} = A (p/p^*)^{d_{eff}/2}$; compute overhead ratio $n_{tot}/k$ vs surface code $d^2/k$. > **Theorem 3.1 (Expansion Implies Distance).** *If the square complex $X$ has $\epsilon$-co-boundary expansion and local codes have $\rho$-robust testability, then $d(\mathcal{Q})\ge \Omega_{\epsilon,\rho}(n)$.* See Leverrier–Zémor [4] Thm 1, Panteleev–Kalachev [1] Thm 5. Choose group $G$, $|G|=\ell$, and voltage assignment $\gamma: E(G_0)\to G$. Lifted Tanner graph $G = G_0\times_\gamma G$ has $n=n_0\ell$ vertices, inherits expansion: if $G_0$ is $(c,\epsilon)$-expander then $G$ is $(c,\epsilon/2)$-expander for random $\gamma$ with high probability when $\ell=\Omega(\log n_0)$ [1] Prop 3.4. *Robust testability* requirement: local product code $A\otimes B$ is $\rho$-robustly testable if for all $x\in A\otimes \mathbb{F}_2^{n_B}+\mathbb{F}_2^{n_A}\otimes B$, $\text{dist}(x, A\otimes B)\le \rho^{-1}(\text{dist}(x, A\otimes \mathbb{F}_2)+\text{dist}(x, \mathbb{F}_2\otimes B))$ . Random linear codes achieve $\rho=\Omega(1)$ with high probability for rates bounded away from 0,1 [1] Lemma 6.2. > **Lemma 4.1 (Product Expansion Boost).** *If $c_A, c_B$ are codes with relative distances $\delta_A,\delta_B$ and $G_0$ has spectral gap $\gamma$, then lifted product code $\mathcal{C}$ has dimension $k=\Theta(n)$ and $d=\Omega(n/\log n)$; if $A\otimes B$ is two-sided robustly testable, $d=\Omega(n)$.* Proof uses spectral bound on size of locally minimal co-chain with small boundary; heavy parity-check count $m=\Theta(n)$ precludes low-weight logical [1][4]. Optimization trick for moderate length: choosing $G=C_\ell$ cyclic lifts only yields quasi-abelian LDPC codes like BB codes $n=2lm$, weight-6 checks, $d\le O(\sqrt{n})$ but excellent practical thresholds; non-abelian $G = PSL(2,q)$ yields better expansion but destroys quasi-cyclic structure useful for layout on reconfigurable arrays [6][11]. ### 4.2 Quantum Tanner Codes, Square Complexes, and Linear-Time Decoding Left-right complex $X$ from groups $G$, generating sets $A,B$, $|A|=|B|=\Delta$. Each vertex $g\in G$ connects to $A$-neighbors $ag$ and $B$-neighbors $gb$. Squares correspond to $(a,b)\in A\times B$: $(g, ag, gb, agb)$. Two graphs $\mathcal{G}_0^\square = (V_{00}\cup V_{11}, E_A\cup E_B)$ and $\mathcal{G}_1^\square = (V_{10}\cup V_{01}, \ldots)$ give Tanner graphs for $C_X$ and $C_Z$. Local code placement: fix $C_A\subset \mathbb{F}_2^A$, $C_B\subset \mathbb{F}_2^B$; at each $V_{00}$ impose $C_A\otimes C_B$ on incident squares (dual $C_A^\perp\otimes C_B^\perp$ at $V_{11}$ for orthogonality). > **Theorem 4.2 (Leverrier–Zémor 2022).** *For random $C_A,C_B$ of rates $\rho_A,\rho_B$ with $\rho_A\rho_B<1/2$, there exists infinite family of quantum Tanner codes with $k=\Theta(n)$, $d=\Theta(n)$, $w=O(1)$, and sequential decoder correcting $\Omega(n)$ errors.* Extends to $\Theta(n)$ adversarial error correction with parallel decoder in $O(\log n)$ iterations [2]. Decoder from [2] alternates: - *Sequential*: while exists vertex with $\ge \alpha$ violated incident checks, flip bits inside its local view to reduce global potential $\Phi = \sum_c |\partial e\cap \text{star}(c)|$. - *Parallel*: when stuck, second type of $A/B$ parallel steps flip many vertices simultaneously using expansion to guarantee progress. Our instantiation: $\Delta=12$, $C_A=[12,4,6]$ extended Hamming, $C_B=[12,8,3]$; product contains 4 logical per square; overall check weight 8–12, matching moderate-weight $[[96,2,12]]$ found by brute force search with bound saturation [11]. ### 4.3 BP-OSD, BP-LSD, and Fully Parallel BP-SF Landscape Iterative BP failure on qLDPC codes traced to *degeneracy-induced splitting*: posterior $p(e|s)\propto \sum_{s'\in S} p(e+s')$ symmetric over stabilizer equivalence class $[e]$, causing oscillation $p\approx 0.5/0.5$ rather than convergence [13][14]. - **BP-OSD**: Poulin–Chung perturbation heuristics (freezing $r$ random variable nodes) vs rigorous OSD. Standard: order $o=0$ OSD corrects up to $p=2\%$ for $[[144,12,12]]$ BB code with logical failure $10^{-4}$; order $10$ combination sweep (flip $\binom{k_{rel}}{10}$ patterns among $k_{rel}=20$ least reliable) closes gap to ML decoder within 0.2 dB but increases latency $10\times$ due to Gaussian elimination $O(n^3)\sim 3M$ ops for $n=144$ [13][8]. - **BP-LSD**: Instead of one $n\times n$ solve, LSD builds residual graph $R$ where unsatisfied checks plus low-magnitude beliefs induce Louvain partition into $\le 50$-node clusters. Each cluster solves independent linear system via bitset GE; parallelism improves GPU utilization, latency 1–2 ms for 320–550 physical qubit codes measured on A100 [6]. Retains $>99\%$ accuracy of OSD-0 [8]. - **BP-SF**: Speculative decoder eliminates GE entirely. Use flipping set $F=\{v: o_v>\tau\}$ where $o_v$ counts belief sign flips over last 10 iterations.
