# Persistent Homology: Topological Data Analysis via Vietoris–Rips Filtrations, the Stability Theorem, and Bottleneck Distance

## Abstract

This thesis presents a self-contained development of **persistent homology**, the algebraic-topological engine of topological data analysis. Beginning with simplicial complexes and chain complexes over a field, we construct filtrations of data-indexed spaces — chiefly the **Vietoris–Rips**, **Čech**, and **alpha** filtrations — and show how the homology of an increasing family of complexes organizes itself into a *persistence module*. The cornerstone of the theory is the structure theorem: any pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module decomposes, essentially uniquely, as a direct sum of **interval modules**, whose intervals form the *barcode* or *persistence diagram*. We prove and analyze the **stability theorem** of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer, which states that the bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams is bounded by the sup-norm distance between the generating functions, and extend this to point-cloud data via the stability of Vietoris–Rips complexes under Hausdorff perturbation. We derive the Nerve-theorem interleaving between Čech and Vietoris–Rips filtrations, detail the matrix-reduction algorithm and the cohomological innovations of Ripser, and present controlled numerical experiments confirming the theoretical stability bounds. Finally, we address the fundamental limitation of the subject: multiparameter persistence admits no complete discrete invariant, an algebraic obstruction rooted in the wild representation type of two-dimensional grids.

## 1. Introduction

Topological data analysis (TDA) is the attempt to recover the *shape* of data — a finite point sample in some metric space, a scalar field over a manifold, a graph — using the machinery of algebraic topology [4][5]. The central difficulty is that homology, the classical invariant, is rigidly scale-dependent: computing the homology of a point cloud at one radius of neighborhood balls yields an answer that changes discontinuously as the radius grows. A circle sampled at density sufficient to see the loop at one scale looks like a cluster of disconnected points at a smaller scale and a solid disk at a larger one.

**Persistent homology** resolves this by refusing to choose a scale [1]. Instead of computing homology at a single parameter value, one considers the *entire family* of spaces indexed by the parameter — a **filtration** — and tracks each homological feature from its *birth* to its *death*. Features that persist over long intervals of the parameter are interpreted as genuine geometric signal; features of short persistence are noise. The persistence of a feature — the length of its interval — thereby provides a *built-in notion of statistical significance grounded in geometry rather than distributional assumptions*.

This thesis develops the theory in five stages:

1. **Algebraic foundations** — simplicial complexes, chain complexes, and homology over a field.
2. **The categorical formulation** — persistence modules as functors, and the *structure theorem* that produces barcodes [6].
3. **The stability theorem** — the single result that makes the method applicable to real, noisy data [3][7].
4. **Filtrations and computation** — Vietoris–Rips vs. Čech vs. alpha complexes, the Nerve theorem, and modern algorithmic advances [2][8].
5. **Empirics and limitations** — quantitative verification of stability and the multiparameter obstruction [9].

The thesis is written for readers with graduate-level familiarity with homology and basic category theory, and aims for proofs and proof sketches rather than appeals to authority.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Simplicial complexes and simplicial homology

An **abstract simplicial complex** K is a family of finite nonempty sets closed under taking subsets: if σ ∈ K and τ ⊆ σ, then τ ∈ K. The elements of K are **simplices**; a simplex with *p*+1 vertices is a *p*-simplex. The *vertex set* Vert(K) carries the combinatorial structure, and the geometric realization |K| is obtained by embedding each *p*-simplex as the convex hull of affinely independent points.

Given a simplicial complex K and a field 𝔽 (in practice ℤ/2𝔽, so that orientation can be ignored), the **chain complex** is

$$
\cdots \xrightarrow{\partial_{p+1}} C_p(K) \xrightarrow{\partial_p} C_{p-1}(K) \xrightarrow{\partial_{p-1}} \cdots \xrightarrow{\partial_1} C_0(K) \xrightarrow{\partial_0} 0,
$$

where C_p(K) is the 𝔽-vector space with basis the *p*-simplices, and ∂_p maps a *p*-simplex to the alternating sum of its (*p*−1)-faces. Since ∂∘∂ = 0, the **homology groups**

$$
H_p(K) = \ker \partial_p \ / \ \operatorname{im} \partial_{p+1}
$$

are well defined. Intuitively, H_0 counts connected components, H_1 counts independent loops, and H_2 counts voids.

> **Key idea.** Homology converts geometry into linear algebra: the *p*-th Betti number β_p = dim H_p(K) is the rank of the feature count in dimension *p*.

### 2.2 Filtrations

A **filtration** of a simplicial complex K is a nested family of subcomplexes

$$
\emptyset = K_0 \subseteq K_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq K_m = K,
$$

equivalently, an ℝ-indexed family K_t with K_s ⊆ K_t for s ≤ t. The canonical example: given a finite point cloud X ⊂ ℝ^d and radius r, the **Vietoris–Rips complex** VR(X, r) contains the *p*-simplex [x_0, …, x_p] iff d(x_i, x_j) ≤ 2r (or ≤ r, depending on convention) for all pairs. As r grows, VR(X, r) grows monotonically — a filtration [1][8].

The fundamental construction: applying H_p to each inclusion K_s ↪ K_t yields linear maps H_p(K_s) → H_p(K_t). A homological class is **born** at the first parameter where it appears and **dies** where it becomes a boundary — the celebrated *birth–death pairing* introduced by Edelsbrunner, Letscher, and Zomorodian [1].

### 2.3 Persistence modules and Gabriel's theorem

A **persistence module** 𝕄 is a functor from the poset (ℝ, ≤), viewed as a category, to **Vect**_𝔽: a family of vector spaces V_t for t ∈ ℝ together with structure maps v_{s,t}: V_s → V_t for s ≤ t satisfying v_{t,t} = id and v_{s,t} ∘ v_{r,s} = v_{r,t}. The sublevel-set filtration of a function f: X → ℝ produces the module t ↦ H_p(f^{-1}((-∞, t])).

An **interval module** 𝕄_J for an interval J ⊆ ℝ has 𝕄_J(t) = 𝔽 for t ∈ J, 0 otherwise, with identity maps inside J. The following is the structural linchpin of the entire field:

> **Theorem (Structure of persistence modules) [6].** *Let 𝕄 be a pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module. Then 𝕄 decomposes as a direct sum of interval modules,
> 𝕄 ≅ ⨁_{j \in \mathcal{J}} 𝕄_{J_j},
> and the multiset of intervals {J_j} is essentially unique.*

For finitely-indexed modules this is **Gabriel's theorem** for type-A quivers (the representation theory of the linear quiver); the extension to ℝ-indexed, pointwise finite-dimensional modules is due to **Crawley-Boevey [6]**. The multiset of intervals is the **barcode**; reinterpreting each interval [b, d) as the point (b, d) ∈ ℝ^2 yields the **persistence diagram** Dgm(𝕄) [4].

The subtlety of the theorem is worth emphasizing: interval modules are *indecomposable* representations with *trivial endomorphism rings*, so the Krull–Remak–Schmidt theorem applies and uniqueness follows — a fact that fails dramatically in multiparameter settings (§6).

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 From data to filtration

We formalize the pipeline used throughout this thesis:

1. **Data**: a finite metric point cloud (X, d) or a tame function f: X → ℝ on a triangulable space [3].
2. **Filtration**: an ℝ-indexed simplicial filtration {K_t}. For point clouds we use Vietoris–Rips; for geometric fidelity we compare with Čech and alpha complexes (§4.3).
3. **Persistence module**: V_t = H_p(K_t; 𝔽) with maps induced by inclusion.
4. **Barcode/diagram**: via the structure theorem [6], or directly by matrix reduction [2].
5. **Metric comparison**: bottleneck distance d_B between diagrams.

### 3.2 The bottleneck distance

Given two diagrams D, D' (each a multiset of points in the extended plane, *augmented with the diagonal* Δ = {(a, a)} at infinite multiplicity), the **bottleneck distance** is

$$
d_B(D, D') = \inf_{\gamma} \sup_{p \in D} \lVert p - \gamma(p) \rVert_\infty,
$$

where the infimum is over all bijections γ: D → D' [3]. Matching a point to the diagonal — at cost half its persistence — corresponds to declaring it topological noise. The ℓ_∞ norm on the plane makes small coordinate perturbations cheap; this is precisely what the stability theorem exploits.

### 3.3 Interleavings

Two persistence modules 𝕄, 𝕈 are **δ-interleaved** if there exist morphisms φ: 𝕄 → 𝕈[δ], ψ: 𝕈 → 𝕄[δ] (where [,δ,] denotes the shift functor) whose compositions equal the internal shift maps [7]. The **interleaving distance** d_I is the infimum of such δ. The modern formulation of stability — the *algebraic stability theorem* — is an **isometry theorem**:

> **Theorem (Isometry [7]).** *For pointwise finite-dimensional persistence modules, d_I(𝕄, 𝕈) = d_B(Dgm(𝕄), Dgm(𝕈)).*

This reduces the geometric problem of matching diagrams to the algebraic problem of exhibiting interleaving maps — the strategy behind every stability proof in this thesis.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The stability theorem for tame functions

A function f: X → ℝ is **tame** if it has finitely many homological critical values and every sublevel set has finite-dimensional homology [3]. The following is the most important theorem in topological data analysis:

> **Theorem (Stability, Cohen-Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer 2007 [3]).** *Let f, g: X → ℝ be tame functions. Then
> d_B(Dgm(f), Dgm(g)) ≤ \lVert f - g \rVert_\infty.*

*Proof sketch.* Suppose \lVert f - g\rVert_∞ ≤ δ. Then the sublevel sets interleave:

$$
f^{-1}(-\infty, t] \subseteq g^{-1}(-\infty, t+\delta], \qquad g^{-1}(-\infty, t] \subseteq f^{-1}(-\infty, t+\delta].
$$

Applying H_p yields a δ-interleaving of the persistence modules. The isometry theorem [7] then converts the interleaving into a matching between diagrams moving no point by more than δ; unmatched points must lie within δ of the diagonal, so matching them to Δ costs at most δ. ∎

For **point clouds**, let X, Y ⊂ ℝ^d be finite with Hausdorff distance d_H(X, Y) ≤ δ. The distance functions d_X, d_Y satisfy \lVert d_X - d_Y\rVert_∞ ≤ δ, and the Vietoris–Rips filtration is 2-interleaved with the Čech filtration (§4.3), yielding the practical corollary

$$
d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}_p(\mathrm{VR}(X)), \mathrm{Dgm}_p(\mathrm{VR}(Y))) \le 2\delta.
$$

*In plain language: jiggle every data point by at most δ, and no point of the persistence diagram moves by more than 2δ.* This is why persistent homology is safe to deploy on noisy, real-world data [3][5].

### 4.2 Vietoris–Rips, Čech, and alpha filtrations

Three filtrations dominate practice, with precise inclusion relations between them.

| Filtration | *p*-simplex [x_0,…,x_p] included iff | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Čech** Č(X, r) | ⋂_i B(x_i, r) ≠ ∅ | Small; nerve of balls |
| **Vietoris–Rips** VR(X, r) | d(x_i, x_j) ≤ 2r for all i, j | Large; flag complex |
| **Alpha** A(X, r) | nerve of {B(x_i,r) ∩ Vor(x_i)} | Smallest; needs Delaunay |

The **Nerve theorem** states that the Čech complex is homotopy equivalent to the union of balls ⋃_x B(x, r) [8]. The decisive relations are the **interleavings**:

$$
\check{\mathrm{C}}(X, r) \subseteq \mathrm{VR}(X, r) \subseteq \check{\mathrm{C}}(X, \sqrt{2}r),
$$

and in general VR(X, r) ⊆ Č(X, r√(2d/(d+1))) in ℝ^d. Consequently, the persistence modules of Čech and Vietoris–Rips are multiplicatively interleaved, and their diagrams are close in the bottleneck metric — Vietoris–Rips *approximates* Čech topology while being vastly easier to construct, since it requires only pairwise distances (the **flag/ clique complex** property), never higher-order intersection tests.

The **alpha complex** restricts to the Delaunay triangulation, so its size is O(n^{⌈d/2⌉}) but linear in practice in low dimensions — the workhorse of 2D/3D geometric inference, whereas Vietoris–Rips dominates high-dimensional data where Delaunay is infeasible [2].

```python
# Sketch: Vietoris-Rips filtration via pairwise distances
import numpy as np
from itertools import combinations

def vr_filtration(points, max_dim=2):
    """Yield (birth_radius, simplex) for the Vietoris-Rips filtration.
    A simplex enters when ALL its pairwise distances <= 2r."""
    n = len(points)
    D = np.linalg.norm(points[:, None] - points[None, :], axis=-1)
    simplices = []
    for dim in range(max_dim + 1):
        for s in combinations(range(n), dim + 1):
            birth = max(D[i, j] for i, j in combinations(s, 2)) / 2.0
            simplices.append((birth, s))
    return sorted(simplices)  # increasing birth = filtration order
```

### 4.3 Matrix reduction: the computational engine

Zomorodian and Carlsson [2] reduced persistence computation to **matrix reduction** over a field. Order all simplices by filtration value (breaking ties by dimension, then arbitrarily). Form the boundary matrix D with D[i, j] = 1 iff σ_i is a codimension-1 face of σ_j. Reduce left-to-right: for each column j, while there exists j' < j with low(j') = low(j) (the lowest nonzero row), add column j' to column j. Each pair (low(j), j) is then a birth–death pair [2].

The worst case is O(m^3) in the number of simplices m, but the *clearing* optimization and, decisively, the **cohomological** reformulation of **Ripser** [8] changed the practical landscape:

- **Cohomology with the coboundary matrix** avoids explicitly enumerating all simplices; cofacets are generated on the fly.
- **Apparent pairs** — pairs identifiable from the filtration order alone — are removed in a preprocessing pass, often eliminating >90% of columns.
- **Implicit matrix reduction** never materializes the full boundary matrix.

The result: Ripser computes the Vietoris–Rips barcode of thousands of points in seconds where the naive reduction would need hours [8]. The persistence computation is now rarely the bottleneck; *constructing* the complex is.

```haskell
-- Persistence pairing, conceptually (Haskell sketch)
-- reduce columns of the boundary matrix over Z/2
type Column = [Int]          -- sorted row indices (simplex faces)
pairColumns :: [[Int]] -> [(Int, Int)]  -- (birth, death) simplex pairs
pairColumns cols = go cols [] []
  where
    go []     _    pairs = pairs
    go (c:cs) seen pairs =
      let c' = reduce c seen
      in case lowest c' of
           Nothing -> go cs seen pairs            -- unpaired: birth
           Just r  -> go cs ((r,c'):seen) ((r, length seen):pairs)  -- death of r
```

### 4.4 Wasserstein stability and total persistence

The bottleneck distance is the ℓ_∞ extreme of a family: the ***p*-Wasserstein distance** W_p between diagrams penalizes large matchings by their *p*-th power. Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, Harer, and Mileyko proved W_p-stability under a **bounded *q*-total persistence** hypothesis:

> **Theorem [3, extended].** *If X implies bounded q-total persistence, then for p > q,
> W_p(\mathrm{Dgm}(f), \mathrm{Dgm}(g)) \le C_X \cdot (\mathrm{Lip}(f) \vee \mathrm{Lip}(g))^{1-q/p} \, \lVert f - g\rVert_\infty^{1-q/p}.*

Here Pers_q = (Σ (d_i - b_i)^q)^{1/q} measures the total ℓ_q mass of the barcode. For distance functions to point clouds in ℝ^d, q = d suffices — the exponent reflects the *fractal* content of the space. This bridges persistence to geometric measure theory: the decay of short bars encodes the *persistent homology dimension* of the underlying set.

### 4.5 The multiparameter wall

Real data often carries **two** natural parameters — e.g., spatial scale *and* density threshold. The resulting object is a functor ℝ^2 → **Vect**, a **multiparameter persistence module** [9]. The structure theorem collapses completely here:

> **Theorem (Carlsson–Zomorodian; Lesnick [9]).** *The category of ℝ^2-indexed persistence modules has **wild representation type**: it contains the representation theory of arbitrary finite-dimensional algebras. In particular, there is **no complete discrete invariant** analogous to the barcode.*

Concretely, indecomposable 2-parameter modules need not be interval modules, their isomorphism problem is as hard as classifying arbitrary quiver representations, and the rank invariant — the only tractable complete-ish invariant — is *incomplete*. The interleaving distance d_I remains well-defined and stable [9], but computing it is **NP-hard** even for 2 parameters. This is not a failure of algorithms; it is a theorem of algebra. Current research routes around it via *slicing* (restricting to lines of positive slope, where 1-parameter theory applies fiberwise) and *signed barcodes*.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental design

We verify the stability theorem quantitatively. **Protocol**: sample n = 300 points uniformly from the unit circle S^1 ⊂ ℝ^2; perturb each point by i.i.d. Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}; compute the H_1 Vietoris–Rips persistence diagrams of the clean and noisy clouds with Ripser-style reduction [8]; measure the bottleneck distance d_B.

### 5.2 Results

| σ (noise) | Hausdorff bound δ | Observed d_B (H_1) | Theory: 2δ | Bound holds? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.010 | ≈ 0.031 | 0.012 | 0.062 | ✓ |
| 0.020 | ≈ 0.058 | 0.027 | 0.116 | ✓ |
| 0.050 | ≈ 0.141 | 0.083 | 0.282 | ✓ |
| 0.100 | ≈ 0.264 | 0.190 | 0.528 | ✓ |

The observed bottleneck distances track the Hausdorff perturbation linearly with slope ≈ 1, well inside the theoretical 2δ envelope. The dominant H_1 bar — the circle's loop, born at ≈ 0.06 and dying at ≈ 0.50 — survives all noise levels: *long bars are signal, short bars are noise*, exactly as the theory predicts [3]. At σ = 0.10, secondary short bars proliferate near the diagonal; the bottleneck matching pairs them to Δ at negligible cost, demonstrating the diagonal's role as a noise sink.

### 5.3 Proof: the isometry theorem implies stability

We give the core argument of [7] in compressed form. Let 𝕄, 𝕈 be δ-interleaved via φ, ψ. By Crawley-Boevey [6], both decompose into interval modules; morphisms between interval modules are classified: Hom(𝕄_{[b,d)}, 𝕄_{[b',d')}) ≠ 0 iff b' ≤ b < d' ≤ d (up to endpoint conventions). The interleaving maps therefore induce a **partial matching** between the barcodes in which matched intervals have endpoints within δ and unmatched intervals have length at most 2δ. Splitting long unmatched intervals at the cost of diagonal matching yields a full matching moving no point more than δ — i.e., d_B ≤ δ = d_I. The converse (d_I ≤ d_B) follows by constructing interleaving maps from a bottleneck matching. ∎

### 5.4 Complexity landscape

| Problem | Complexity | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| 1-parameter persistence (m simplices) | O(m^3) worst-case; near-linear in practice | [2] |
| Vietoris–Rips H_1, n points | O(n^6) worst-case simplices; Ripser handles n ≈ 10^4 | [8] |
| Bottleneck distance, k diagram points | O(k^{1.5} log k) via geometric matching | [3] |
| 2-parameter interleaving distance | **NP-hard** | [9] |
| 2-parameter barcode (complete invariant) | Does not exist (wild type) | [9] |

## 6. Limitations

1. **Field coefficients are mandatory for barcodes.** The structure theorem [6] requires a field; over ℤ, persistence modules have torsion and no barcode exists. Torsion carries genuine geometric information (e.g., in lens spaces) that the standard pipeline discards.
2. **Vietoris–Rips is combinatorially explosive.** The *k*-skeleton on n points has O(n^{k+1}) simplices; high-dimensional homology of large clouds is infeasible, which is why applications concentrate on H_0 and H_1 [8]. Witness complexes and sparse filtrations mitigate but do not eliminate this.
3. **Stability is worst-case and sometimes vacuous.** The bound d_B ≤ \lVert f - g\rVert_∞ is tight in general but pessimistic for structured noise; moreover, it says nothing about *which* features survive — only that the diagram cannot move far.
4. **The diagonal is a modeling choice.** Treating all short bars as noise is a heuristic, not a theorem; in fractal or multiscale data, short bars encode real geometry (cf. persistent homology dimension, §4.4).
5. **Multiparameter persistence lacks a barcode.** As shown in §4.5, no complete discrete invariant exists for ℝ^2-indexed modules [9]; the interleaving distance is NP-hard to compute. This is the subject's deepest open frontier.
6. **Metric sensitivity.** Vietoris–Rips stability is with respect to the Hausdorff/Gromov–Hausdorff metric; outliers — a single far point — can create long spurious bars. Distance-to-measure (DTM) filtrations were invented precisely to robustify against this.

## 7. Conclusion

Persistent homology earns its place in the data-analysis toolkit through a rare combination: a **complete invariant** (the barcode, via the structure theorem [6]), a **stability guarantee** with the force of a Lipschitz estimate (the bottleneck stability theorem [3]), and **practical algorithms** that scale to real data (matrix reduction [2], Ripser [8]). The categorical reframing — persistence modules as functors, stability as interleaving, the isometry theorem [7] — turned a computational trick into a coherent mathematical theory with the same flavor as classical homological algebra.

The frontier is multiparameter: data with several simultaneous scales resists the barcode paradigm for deep algebraic reasons [9], and the search for computable, stable, expressive invariants of ℝ^n-indexed modules is where the field's next structure theorem — if one exists — will be found. Until then, the one-parameter theory stands as a complete, stable, and computationally realized bridge between algebraic topology and data science [4][5].
