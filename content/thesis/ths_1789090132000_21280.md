# Zero-Knowledge Succinct Arguments: A Comparative Analysis of zk-SNARKs, zk-STARKs, PLONK Arithmetization, and Recursive Proof Composition

## Abstract

Zero-knowledge succinct arguments have evolved from theoretical constructs into the cryptographic backbone of blockchain scaling, private computation, and verifiable machine learning. This thesis develops a unified comparative framework for the two dominant families — **zk-SNARKs** (pairing-based, succinct, non-transparent) and **zk-STARKs** (hash-based, scalable, transparent) — anchored in their arithmetization pipelines: rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS), quadratic arithmetic programs (QAP), and PLONK's permutation-based constraint system. We dissect the three critical trust and efficiency axes: (i) arithmetization expressiveness, comparing QAP and PLONKish encodings of computation; (ii) commitment schemes, contrasting KZG's constant-size pairing commitments against FRI's transparent low-degree testing; and (iii) setup ceremonies, from circuit-specific Groth16 structured reference strings to powers-of-tau MPC ceremonies and fully transparent random oracles. We analyze recursive proof composition from Halo's nested amortization to cycle-of-curves recursion used in production zkEVMs. Asymptotic benchmarks and security reductions are presented, concluding with open problems in post-quantum succinctness and aggregation.

## 1. Introduction

A zero-knowledge proof lets a prover $P$ convince a verifier $V$ that a statement $x \in \mathcal{L}$ is true without revealing any information beyond the statement's validity. The succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (SNARKs) formalized by Bitansky, Canetti, Chiesa, and Tromer impose two additional demands: **succinctness** — proof size and verification time polylogarithmic in the witness size — and **non-interactivity**, a single message from prover to verifier. Adding **scalability** (prover quasi-linear time) and **transparency** (no trusted setup) yields the STARK family introduced by Ben-Sasson, Bentov, Horesh, and Riabzev [3].

Three forces drive contemporary research in this area:

1. **Blockchain scalability.** Rollups settle thousands of transactions on Ethereum with a single on-chain proof; Polygon zkEVM, for instance, combines STARK proving with SNARK aggregation in a recursive pipeline [7].
2. **Trust minimization.** Circuit-specific trusted setups (Groth16 [1]) have been replaced by universal updatable structured reference strings (PLONK [2]) and, more recently, by transparent setups relying only on collision-resistant hashing (STARKs [3], Halo [4]).
3. **Composability.** Recursive proof composition — proofs that verify other proofs — enables incrementally verifiable computation (IVC), succinct blockchains, and proof aggregation at scale [4].

Despite rapid deployment, the literature lacks a single rigorous comparison that treats arithmetization, commitment, and recursion as orthogonal design dimensions. This thesis fills that gap.

> **Theorem: (Soundness of succinct arguments).** A proof system $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{V})$ for language $\mathcal{L}$ is an *argument of knowledge* with knowledge error $\kappa$ if there exists an extractor $\mathcal{E}$ such that for every prover $P^*$ and statement $x \notin \mathcal{L}$, the probability that $\mathcal{E}$ fails to extract a witness while $P^*$ convinces $\mathcal{V}$ is bounded by $\kappa(|x|)$.

The remainder of this thesis proceeds as follows: Section 2 establishes cryptographic preliminaries; Section 3 formalizes the comparative methodology; Section 4 contains the technical deep dive across four subsystems; Section 5 derives asymptotic bounds and summarizes proof sketches; Section 6 discusses limitations; Section 7 concludes with open problems.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Interactive Oracle Proofs and Polynomial IOPs

Modern succinct arguments are almost universally constructed via a two-step *IOP + compiler* paradigm. An **Interactive Oracle Proof (IOP)** is a multi-round protocol in which the prover sends oracle access to long strings (typically Reed–Solomon codewords) and the verifier queries a few positions. Applying the **Fiat–Shamir transform** in the random oracle model yields a non-interactive argument. The celebrated **FRI protocol** (Fast Reed–Solomon IOP of Proximity) is a logarithmic-round IOP for testing that a committed function is close to a low-degree polynomial [3]; it is the engine of all STARKs.

**Polynomial IOPs** refine this: the prover's messages are polynomials and the verifier's queries are evaluations. Compiling a polynomial IOP with a **polynomial commitment scheme** (PCS) yields a SNARK. The two dominant PCS instantiations are:

- **KZG commitments** [5]: a single group element $C = g^{p(\tau)}$ commits to $p$, with constant-size evaluation proofs verified by one pairing equation. Requires a trusted SRS (powers of $\tau$).
- **FRI-based commitments**: the prover commits to the evaluations of $p$ over a domain in a Merkle tree and opens evaluations by proving consistency through FRI. Transparent, but proof size grows as $O(\log^2 d)$.

### 2.2 Pairings and Elliptic Curve Assumptions

Pairing-based SNARKs operate over groups $\mathbb{G}_1, \mathbb{G}_2, \mathbb{G}_T$ with a bilinear map $e: \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2 \to \mathbb{G}_T$. Security reduces to the $q$-type assumptions (e.g., $q$-PKE, $q$-SDH). The BN254 (alt_bn128) and BLS12-381 curves dominate deployments; BLS12-381 targets ~128-bit security with 48-byte compressed $\mathbb{G}_1$ elements. PLONK's KZG variant requires $\mathbb{G}_2$ SRS elements $[ \tau^i ]_2$ for batched verification [2][6].

### 2.3 Arithmetization

Arithmetization is the reduction of a computation to an algebraic satisfiability problem. The classical pipeline is:

$$\text{Program} \;\xrightarrow{\text{flatten}}\; \text{Arithmetic circuit} \;\xrightarrow{\text{R1CS}}\; \text{QAP} \;\xrightarrow{\text{SNARK}}$$

- **R1CS (rank-1 constraint system):** constraints $\langle a_i, s \rangle \cdot \langle b_i, s \rangle = \langle c_i, s \rangle$ over a witness vector $s$.
- **QAP (quadratic arithmetic program):** the Gennaro et al. [GGPR13] reduction of R1CS to polynomial divisibility: find $A(x), B(x), C(x)$ with $A(x)B(x) - C(x) = H(x)Z(x)$.
- **AIR (algebraic intermediate representation):** STARKs instead record an *execution trace* as a table and enforce transition constraints via polynomials vanishing on the trace domain [3].
- **PLONKish:** a generalization where each gate is $q_L a + q_R b + q_O c + q_M ab + q_C = 0$ plus a global **permutation argument** enforcing wire copy constraints [2].

| Arithmetization | Constraint shape | Setup class | Copy constraints |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1CS/QAP | $a \cdot b = c$ | circuit-specific | inline in wiring |
| PLONKish | $q_L a+q_R b+q_O c+q_M ab+q_C=0$ | universal | permutation polynomial |
| AIR | transition + boundary | transparent | consecutive rows |

## 3. Methodology

Our comparison is organized along **five orthogonal axes**, each evaluated for Groth16 [1], PLONK [2], STARK [3], and Halo/Halo2-style recursion [4]:

1. **Trust model:** circuit-specific SRS $\prec$ universal updatable SRS $\prec$ transparent (public randomness only).
2. **Arithmetization cost:** constraints per primitive (e.g., SHA-256, Poseidon, ECDSA) and prover asymptotics.
3. **Proof size and verifier time:** concrete bytes and pairing/hash counts at 128-bit security.
4. **Recursion friendliness:** verifier circuit size, need for cycles of curves, aggregation support.
5. **Post-quantum posture:** assumptions (pairing / discrete-log / hash) and quantum vulnerability via Shor's algorithm.

We ground asymptotic claims in the cited primary sources and cross-check concrete numbers against production deployments (zkEVM provers, Filecoin's Groth16 circuits, Ethereum's EIP-4844 KZG ceremonies).

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Groth16: The Circuit-Specific Gold Standard

Groth16 [1] remains the efficiency ceiling for pairing-based SNARKs. The prover commits to a QAP witness via three group elements $\pi = (A, B, C) \in \mathbb{G}_1^2 \times \mathbb{G}_2$, and the verifier checks a single pairing-product equation:

$$e(A, B) \stackrel{?}{=} e(\alpha, \beta) \cdot e\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell} a_i \cdot \tfrac{\beta u_i(x) + \alpha v_i(x) + w_i(x)}{\gamma}, \gamma\right) \cdot e(C, \delta)$$

Key properties:

- **Proof size:** 3 group elements — approximately 200 bytes on BN254, 288 bytes on BLS12-381 — asymptotically $O_\lambda(1)$ [1].
- **Verification:** 3 pairings plus $O(\ell)$ multiplications in the number $\ell$ of public inputs; roughly 1.5 ms.
- **Trusted setup:** circuit-specific. The SRS embeds the circuit's QAP polynomials evaluated at secret $\tau$; any change to the circuit demands a fresh multi-party computation ceremony, e.g., the Powers of Tau ceremonies [1].

The *circuit-specific* SRS is Groth16's Achilles' heel: deployment requires per-circuit ceremonies, and compromise of the trapdoor $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta, \tau)$ permits undetectable forgery. This motivated the search for universal setups.

```python
# Groth16 verification equation (schematic, BN254)
def groth16_verify(vk, proof, public_inputs):
    A, B, C = proof
    lhs = pairing(A, B)
    rhs = pairing(vk.alpha, vk.beta)
    rhs *= pairing(vk.gamma_abc_acc(public_inputs), vk.gamma)
    rhs *= pairing(C, vk.delta)
    return lhs == rhs  # single pairing-product check
```

### 4.2 PLONK: Universal Updatable SNARKs and Permutation Arithmetization

PLONK [2] replaces QAP with a constraint system over Lagrange bases on a multiplicative subgroup $H = \{\omega^i\}$. Each gate contributes a selector-weighted equation, and all *copy constraints* (wire equalities) are enforced by a single **permutation argument** derived from Bayer–Groth [BG12]: the prover shows that two multisets of wire values are permutations of each other via a grand-product polynomial $Z(X)$ satisfying

$$Z(\omega X) = Z(X) \cdot \frac{(\hat{a} + \beta X + \gamma)(\hat{b} + \beta k_1 X + \gamma)(\hat{c} + \beta k_2 X + \gamma)}{(a + \beta \sigma_1(X) + \gamma)(b + \beta \sigma_2(X) + \gamma)(c + \beta \sigma_3(X) + \gamma)}.$$

Innovations that matter in practice:

1. **Universal updatable SRS.** One KZG SRS of degree $d$ serves *all* circuits up to $d$ gates; any party may update it, and soundness requires only one honest updater [2]. Ethereum's KZG ceremony for EIP-4844 (over 140,000 participants) instantiated exactly this primitive.
2. **Custom gates and lookups.** The selector mechanism admits cheap custom gates (e.g., Poseidon S-boxes) and, via plookup extensions, table lookups — reducing hash-circuit costs by an order of magnitude relative to R1CS.
3. **Batched KZG openings.** Boneh et al. [6] show a single group element can open multiple polynomials at multiple points, shrinking PLONK proofs to ~400 bytes.

```haskell
-- PLONK gate constraint (conceptual)
gateConstraint :: Selector -> Wire -> Wire -> Wire -> Field -> Bool
gateConstraint (Selector qL qR qO qM qC) a b c =
  qL*a + qR*b + qO*c + qM*a*b + qC == 0
-- permutation argument: Z(g*X)/Z(X) = num/den as above
```

> **Theorem: (PLONK knowledge soundness).** Under the $q$-SDH assumption and in the algebraic group model, PLONK with KZG commitments is a zk-SNARK for the relation of PLONKish circuit satisfiability with universal updatable SRS [2].

### 4.3 zk-STARKs: Transparency via AIR and FRI

STARKs [3] abandon pairings entirely. The prover writes the computation as an **execution trace** — a $w$-column table over $T$ steps — and commits to each column's Reed–Solomon encoding in a Merkle tree. Two polynomial constraints enforce correctness:

- **Boundary constraints:** selected cells equal prescribed values.
- **Transition constraints:** for each consecutive row pair, $C(T_i, T_{i+1}) = 0$, lifted to a composition polynomial via DEEP-ALI.

The verifier samples random points and checks consistency; the **FRI protocol** reduces a claim "$f$ is close to a degree-$<d$ polynomial" through $\log d$ commit-and-fold rounds, each halving the degree. The result:

- **Transparent setup:** only a public random oracle (instantiated by a hash such as Blake3 or Poseidon). No ceremony, no toxic waste.
- **Post-quantum:** security reduces to collision resistance of the hash — plausibly quantum-resistant, unlike all pairing SNARKs.
- **Cost:** proofs of $O(\log^2 T)$ size — concretely 45–200 KB — and provers that are quasi-linear but with large constants; verification is $O(\log^2 T)$, ~10 ms [3].

The STARK prover is asymptotically the fastest known ($O(T \log T)$ with small constants using 64-bit fields like Goldilocks), which is why Polygon zkEVM generates its initial proofs as STARKs before compressing them [7].

| System | Proof size | Verifier time | Setup | Post-quantum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Groth16 [1] | ~200 B ($O(1)$) | ~1.5 ms ($O(1)$) | circuit-specific trusted | no |
| PLONK [2] | ~400 B ($O(1)$) | ~3 ms ($O(1)$) | universal updatable | no |
| Bulletproofs-style | ~1.5 KB ($O(\log n)$) | ~3 s ($O(n)$) | transparent | no |
| zk-STARK [3] | ~100 KB ($O(\log^2 n)$) | ~10 ms ($O(\log^2 n)$) | transparent | yes |

### 4.4 Recursive Proof Composition: Halo, Cycles, and IVC

Recursive composition asks: can a proof $\pi$ attest to the validity of another proof $\pi'$ — i.e., verify $\mathcal{V}$ *inside* the proven computation? All pre-2019 constructions required trusted setups and cycles of pairing-friendly curves. **Halo** [4] broke both requirements: using the discrete-logarithm assumption over ordinary curve cycles (e.g., Pallas/Vesta), Bowe, Grigg, and Hopwood showed how to amortize the expensive inner-product-argument verifier across recursion steps (*nested amortization*), obtaining the first practical recursion without trusted setup.

The recursion dilemma is the **cycle problem**: to prove "I verified a proof on curve $E_1$", the verifier's scalar field must match the circuit's base field, so $E_1$'s scalar field must equal $E_2$'s base field and vice versa — a 2-cycle. Halo2 instantiates this with the Pallas/Vesta cycle over Pasta curves. Modern **IVC** (incrementally verifiable computation) schemes — Nova, SuperNova — fold successive steps with relaxed R1CS, achieving recursion with a single curve and transparent setup, at the cost of a non-succinct intermediate accumulator.

Production systems compose families rather than choosing one: Polygon zkEVM proves EVM execution with fast STARKs, recursively aggregates them, and compresses the final statement into a Groth16/PLONK SNARK cheap enough for on-chain verification [7]. Plonky2/Plonky3 (PLONK over Goldilocks with FRI) similarly target recursion-friendly proving with ~100 ms recursion steps.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Asymptotic landscape

Let $n$ be the circuit/trace size and $\lambda$ the security parameter. The following bounds are proved in the cited works:

- **Groth16** [1]: prover $O(n \log n)$ (dominated by multi-scalar multiplications and FFTs), proof $O_\lambda(1)$, verifier $O_\lambda(1) + O(\ell)$.
- **PLONK** [2]: prover $O(n \log n)$ with 7.5–20$\times$ fewer exponentiations than Sonic in fully-succinct mode; proof $O_\lambda(1)$; verifier $O_\lambda(1)$.
- **STARK** [3]: prover $O(n \log n)$, proof $O(\log^2 n)$, verifier $O(\log^2 n)$ — concretely verified exponentially faster than the computation for large $n$.
- **Halo** [4]: recursion overhead amortized to $O(1)$ per step after nesting; proof size $O(\log n)$ per recursion layer without trusted setup.

### 5.2 Security proof sketches

*Groth16.* Knowledge soundness reduces to the $q$-PKE and $q$-PDH assumptions: an algebraic adversary producing an accepting proof yields, via the extractor, QAP polynomials satisfying divisibility; zero-knowledge follows from randomization of $(A, B, C)$ with fresh $\alpha, \beta$ shifts [1].

*PLONK.* The permutation argument is complete and sound by the Schwartz–Zippel lemma applied to the grand-product identity; the quotient polynomial $t(X) = (\text{gate} + \alpha \cdot \text{perm} + \alpha^2 \cdot \text{perm}_2)/Z_H(X)$ vanishes on $H$ iff all constraints hold. KZG evaluation binding transfers polynomial-IOP soundness to the compiled SNARK in the AGM [2][5].

*STARK.* Soundness has two legs: (i) DEEP-ALI reduces AIR satisfaction to low-degree claims with soundness error $O(d/|\mathbb{F}|)$ per query; (ii) FRI's commit phase has soundness error bounded by the Johnson bound for Reed–Solomon proximity, amplified by repetition [3]. Fiat–Shamir in the ROM gives non-interactivity.

### 5.3 Concrete deployment evidence

- **Filecoin** uses Groth16 (32 GiB sectors, ~11M-constraint circuits) for Proof-of-Replication — the largest deployed SNARK circuits.
- **Ethereum EIP-4844** uses KZG commitments from the largest powers-of-tau ceremony to date for blob data availability.
- **Polygon zkEVM** runs a STARK$\to$SNARK recursion pipeline (PIL + FRI + PLONK) proving full EVM equivalence [7].
- **Zcash** migrated from Sprout's circuit-specific setup toward Halo 2 recursion to eliminate per-upgrade ceremonies [4].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Trusted setup residue.** Universal SRS ceremonies (PLONK/KZG) still require at least one honest participant; the "1-of-$N$" assumption, while strong, is not falsifiable post-hoc. Subversion of KZG SRS is undetectable.
2. **Quantum vulnerability.** All pairing- and discrete-log-based systems (Groth16, PLONK, Halo, Bulletproofs) fall to Shor's algorithm. STARKs are plausibly post-quantum but pay a 100–500$\times$ proof-size penalty.
3. **Arithmetization overhead.** Real programs incur 10–1000$\times$ constraint blowup versus native execution (bitwise ops, Keccak, and memory consistency are pathological in R1CS/AIR); zkEVMs spend most prover time on under-constrained "cheap" opcodes.
4. **Recursion costs.** Cycle-of-curves recursion doubles curve arithmetic work; FRI-based recursion (Plonky2) needs large fields and careful soundness analysis of in-circuit hash verification.
5. **Formal verification gap.** Machine-checked proofs exist only for fragments (e.g., isolated FRI formalizations); the full PLONK/STARK stacks rest on pen-and-paper reductions plus audited code.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The SNARK/STARK design space is best understood as a three-way trade among **succinctness**, **transparency**, and **prover efficiency**, mediated by the choice of polynomial commitment. KZG [5] buys constant-size proofs at the price of a ceremony; FRI [3] buys transparency and quantum resistance at the price of logarithmic proof growth; the IOP+PCS compiler lets both ride on shared arithmetizations. PLONK's [2] permutation-based constraint system unified circuit encodings under one universal SRS, while Halo [4] proved recursion need not imply trusted setup. The emerging consensus — visible in production zkEVMs [7] — is *heterogeneous composition*: prove fast with STARKs, aggregate recursively, verify cheaply with SNARKs. The grand open problems are a post-quantum SNARK with sub-kilobyte proofs, fully verified proof-system implementations, and arithmetizations that close the gap between native and provable execution.
