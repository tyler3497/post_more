# Photonic Interconnects for Chiplet-Based AI Accelerators: Wavelength-Division Multiplexing, Microring Resonator Tuning, and Athermal Crosstalk Mitigation

## Abstract

Electrical interconnects impose bandwidth-density, latency, and energy walls for chiplet-based AI accelerators scaling to trillion-parameter models. Silicon photonic interconnects leveraging wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) offer terahertz aggregate bandwidth via dense microring resonator modulators and filters. This thesis provides a comprehensive analysis of WDM photonic interconnects for 2.5D/3D chiplet integration, focusing on microring resonator tuning dynamics, thermal and inter-channel crosstalk, and athermal mitigation. We formalize resonance condition λ_res=2πR n_eff / m, quantify tuning efficiency 77-84 pm/mW and 22-25 pm/V, and analyze thermal drift 70-80 pm/K in silicon-on-insulator. We survey active feedback locking, ITiO-gated MOS tuning, and passive athermal designs using SiN, polymer, and TiO2 cladding. We evaluate Lightmatter Passage M1000 and Intel integrated photonics transmitters achieving 256 Gbps/fiber to 1.6 Tbps/fiber DWDM at 112 Gbps/λ [1][3][8]. A system model predicts 8 Tbps/mm edge bandwidth density with 16λ×400 Gbps, <2 ns hop, and sub-pJ/bit [4]. We conclude with limitations and roadmap to 3.2 Tbps CPO transceivers.

## 1. Introduction

The *AI accelerator scaling crisis* is fundamentally an **interconnect crisis**. Monolithic GPUs and TPUs hit reticle limits ~850 mm², yield decay, and shoreline bandwidth caps of ~6-8 Tbps via HBM and NVLink. **Silicon photonics** offers a path: photons propagate with negligible distance-dependent loss (<0.1 dB/cm), immunity to EMI, and massive spectral parallelism via **WDM**. A single fiber carrying 16-64 wavelengths at 100-200 Gbps/λ yields 1.6-6.4 Tbps/fiber [8][3]. Lightmatter's **Passage™ M1000** photonic interposer demonstrates 114 Tbps aggregate via 256 fibers, 4,000 mm² multi-reticle active interposer with integrated lasers, modulators, and transistors [3][9]. Intel's microring-based transmitter shows 8λ×32 Gbps = 256 Gbps/fiber with flip-chip PIC/EIC integration [1].

Central to WDM density are **microring resonators (MRRs)**: whispering-gallery mode cavities 4.5-10 µm radius that provide wavelength-selective modulation in 20×20 µm footprint, Q~1300-1600, extinction ratio 26-48 dB [4]. However, MRRs are exquisitely sensitive: resonant wavelength λ_res drifts ~70-80 pm/K due to silicon thermo-optic coefficient dn/dT ≈1.86×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹, and fabrication variation ±1-2 nm wafer-scale. Without tuning, a WDM system collapses.

This thesis addresses three intertwined problems:

1. **How to architect WDM photonic interconnects for chiplets** achieving Tbps/mm shoreline density?
2. **How to tune microrings efficiently** balancing heater power vs electro-optic speed?
3. **How to achieve athermal operation and crosstalk mitigation** without mW-per-ring overhead?

> **Theorem:** For a silicon MRR of radius R, effective index n_eff, and group index n_g, the thermal resonance drift dλ/dT = (λ / n_g) * dn_eff/dT dominates over expansion, requiring active feedback with <10 µs loop or passive compensation via cladding engineering with ∂n_clad/∂T <0 to achieve ±0.1 nm stability over 0-80°C.

Contributions:

- Unified model of chiplet photonic interposer bandwidth, latency, and energy
- Taxonomy of MRR tuning: thermal heater, PIN free-carrier injection, ITiO MOS gate [Nature 2024]
- Athermal strategies: SiN-assisted, polymer, Al₂O₃:Er gain + Si₃N₄, adiabatic design
- Crosstalk analysis: inter-channel Lorentzian overlap, thermal crosstalk matrix, mitigation via guard bands
- System evaluation for 8-16λ 400 Gbps/λ roadmap to 3.2 Tbps CPO [4]

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Chiplet Interconnect Bottleneck

Electrical UCIe Advanced Package (2.5D) provides 1.3 TB/s/mm beachfront at 0.25 pJ/bit but requires <2 mm reach and suffers IR drop. NVLink 4 delivers 900 GB/s bidirectional per GPU but power ~60 W for SerDes. Photonic alternative:

- *Distance independence*: waveguide loss 0.5-2 dB/cm vs copper ∝f²
- *WDM parallelism*: 16λ ×112 Gbps PAM4 = 1.6 Tbps/fiber demonstrated by Lightmatter+Qualcomm [8]
- *Anywhere I/O*: Passage places optical I/O not limited to die edge, enabling 96 TB/s per die via 40 waveguides/fiber area [10]

### 2.2 Silicon Photonics Platform

Standard 220 nm SOI: Si core n~3.47, buried oxide 2 µm. Components:

- **Grating coupler / edge coupler**: fiber-to-chip coupling loss 0.8-2.5 dB
- **Mach-Zehnder Modulator (MZM)**: broadband, large footprint ~1-2 mm, VπL ~1-2 V·cm
- **Microring Modulator (MRM)**: narrowband, compact, low capacitance ~20 fF, ideal for WDM cascades [1][4]
- **Ge photodetector**: responsivity 0.8-1 A/W, bandwidth >60 GHz

Resonance condition:

```
λ_res(m) = 2π R n_eff(λ,T) / m , m ∈ ℤ
FSR = λ² / (n_g L), L=2πR
Q = λ / FWHM ≈ π n_g L √r1 r2 / (λ (1-r1 r2 a))
```

where a round-trip amplitude transmission, r coupling coefficient.

### 2.3 WDM Fundamentals

DWDM 100-200 GHz ITU grid: 0.8 nm spacing at 1550 nm, 1.1 nm at 1310 nm O-band. System needs:

- Comb source: DFB array [1] or single soliton frequency comb with FSR 100 GHz [Spectrum]
- Multiplexer: cascaded MRR filters or AWG
- Demultiplexer: matched drop filters

*Channel count* limited by FSR / spacing. For R=5 µm, FSR~22 nm → 22 channels at 100 GHz. Smaller R=4.5 µm → larger FSR enabling 8×400 Gbps within FSR [4].

### 4.2 Microring Resonator Tuning: Thermal, Carrier, and Gated MOS

**Tuning requirements:** Fabrication variation ±1.5 nm across wafer requires >10 nm tuning range; temperature drift 0.08 nm/K ×60 K=4.8 nm.

*Three mechanisms:*

1. **Thermal heater** (most common): Integrated TiN heater above ring, ΔT → Δn_eff. Efficiency η_th = dλ/dP_h = (λ/n_g)*(dn/dT)*(dT/dP). Measured 77.6-84.5 pm/mW [4]. Power: to correct 2 nm offset → ~25 mW/ring → 200 mW for 8λ bank → significant for 1000s rings.
   - Advantage: large range >10 nm, no extra loss
   - Disadvantage: slow τ~10 µs, thermal crosstalk to neighbors 5-15% [6]
   - Control: TCU senses photocurrent proportional to ring through power, PID loop locks to target IL [1]

2. **Free-carrier injection / depletion** (PIN/PN junction): Plasma dispersion effect Δn = - (q² λ² / 8π²c² ε0 n)(ΔNe/me* + ΔNh/mh*). Depletion: blue shift ~20-25 pm/V [4], GHz speed, but limited range ~0.2-0.4 nm.
   - Used for high-speed modulation, not large static tuning

3. **ITiO-gated MOSCAP** (Oregon State/Baylor breakthrough) [Spectrum 2024]: High-mobility titanium-doped indium oxide gate on MOSCAP forms accumulation layer with 10× E-O efficiency vs PN junction, enabling **gate-tuning across entire channel spacing** without heaters, ultra-low power.
   - Structure: Si-MRR with HfO2 gate oxide, ITiO top electrode transparent conductive oxide
   - Measured: 4 cascaded tunable Si-MRRs different radii, WDM filter bank gate-tuned
   - Advantage: <100 fJ/bit tuning, ns speed, no thermal crosstalk

**Tuning comparison table:**

| Mechanism | Efficiency | Range | Speed | Power/ring | Crosstalk |
|-----------|------------|-------|-------|------------|-----------|
| Thermal TiN heater | 80 pm/mW | >10 nm | 10 µs | 5-25 mW | High (matrix K) |
| PN depletion | 23 pm/V | 0.3 nm | >30 GHz | ~10 µW | None |
| PIN injection | 100 pm/mA | 1 nm | 1 GHz | 1 mW + loss | Carrier diffusion |
| ITiO MOS gate | 200 pm/V (est.) | ~1-2 nm (full FSR with cascades) | 1 GHz | <100 µW | None |
| Adiabatic AMRM [5] | fabrication-tolerant | 80 nm operation window | N/A | N/A | Low |

*Adiabatic Microring Modulator (AMRM)* [5] solves coupling variation: conventional directional coupler coupling κ varies ±3 dB over 100 nm, causing ER drop at edge wavelengths. Adiabatic waveguide taper keeps κ variation <0.5 dB across 100 nm simulation → fabricated device ER>20 dB over 80 nm, 3-dB EO BW >60 GHz, PAM-4 125 Gbaud 250 Gbps, footprint 20×20 µm. This enables **single design for all WDM channels** vs per-λ optimization.

```rust
// Rust pseudocode for aggregated heater + MOS tuning controller
fn lock_wavelength(ring: &mut MRM, target: f64) {
    let mut pid = PID::new(kp=0.5, ki=0.1, kd=0.05);
    loop {
        let err_pm = (ring.resonance_nm - target) * 1000.0;
        if err_pm.abs() < 1.0 { break; } // <1 pm tolerance
        // Coarse: heater
        if err_pm.abs() > 100.0 {
            ring.heater_mw += pid.update(err_pm) * 0.0125; // 80 pm/mW
        } else {
            // Fine: MOS gate
            ring.gate_v += pid.update(err_pm) * 0.04; // 25 pm/V PN, 200 pm/V ITiO
        }
        // Mitigate crosstalk: subtract neighbor contribution
        for (j, kj) in ring.crosstalk_coeffs.iter().enumerate() {
            ring.heater_mw -= kj * neighbor_power[j];
        }
    }
}
```

*Thermal crosstalk matrix*: For ring array pitch 20 µm, K_ij ~0.05-0.15 meaning neighbor heater 10 mW induces 0.04-0.12 nm drift on target → requires MIMO control, not SISO. Oregon State team model shows gate-tuning eliminates this term [Spectrum].

### 4.3 Athermal Crosstalk Mitigation and System-Level Synchronization

**Problem definition:** Silicon TO coefficient 1.86×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ → dλ/dT = 70-80 pm/K. For 60°C range (0-60°C datacenter), drift 4.8 nm > channel spacing 0.8 nm → complete channel misalignment. Heaters compensate but consume **mW per ring × thousands rings = tens of watts**, negating photonic energy advantage.

**Passive athermal approaches:**

1. **Cladding compensation**: Polymer or TiO2 cladding with negative dn/dT (-1 to -3×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹) counteracts Si positive. Mode confinement engineering tailors ∂n_eff/∂T = Γ_Si (dn_Si/dT) + Γ_clad (dn_clad/dT) + Γ_box (dn_SiO2/dT) ≈0. Biswajeet Guha et al. 2009 [6] demonstrated CMOS-compatible athermal resonators operating >80 K range using polymer overlay and slot waveguide tailoring mode overlap 40% in polymer.
   - Tradeoff: polymer loss, CMOS incompatibility, aging

2. **SiN-assisted athermal WDM**: Si₃N₄ has dn/dT ~2.5×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹ 10× smaller than Si, plus low loss <0.1 dB/cm. By cascading DFB laser (gain Al₂O₃:Er³⁺ with low TO) with Si₃N₄ MRR filters, synchronized shift 0.02 nm/°C demonstrated over 20-50°C, extinction >15 dB between matched/unmatched channels [7]. Flat-top high-order filters or contra-directional couplers provide wider passband relaxing alignment tolerance.

3. **Adiabatic tolerant design [5]**: Even if absolute λ drifts, AMRM maintains ER>20 dB over 80 nm, so drift does not kill ER, only requires tracking. Combined with wide FSR design, fabrication tolerance ±1 nm still yields >80% yield vs 30% conventional.

**Active but ultra-efficient compensation:**

Oregon State/Baylor ITiO gate-tuning [Spectrum]: Replace heaters with MOSCAP gates that consume **capacitive energy only** ~C V² f ~10 fJ/bit vs heater static mW. 4-cascaded Si-MRR array driven by high-mobility transparent conductive oxide gates shows large wavelength coverage for entire channel spacing, much larger E-O efficiency than reversed PN junctions.

**Crosstalk taxonomy:**

- *Inter-channel optical crosstalk*: Lorentzian tails of adjacent MRR filters. For Q=1500, FWHM~1 nm at 1310 nm, spacing 200 GHz ~1.1 nm → overlap -18 dB. Mitigation: higher Q (2500) improves roll-off but reduces modulation BW (photon lifetime τ=Q/ω). Tradeoff: Q vs BW.
- *Thermal crosstalk*: heater of ring i heats neighbor j via Si substrate (k~148 W/mK). Mitigation: deep trench isolation, undercut, or gate tuning.
- *Electrical crosstalk*: RF coupling between drivers, mitigated via differential GS shielding.

**System-level athermal synchronization [7]:**

Conventional: laser + MRR each require independent TEC. Novel: integrate Er-doped Al₂O₃ gain medium with Si₃N₄ cavity—both low TO → **control-free synchronization**. Prototype shows power extinction ratio >15 dB maintained without active tuning across 30°C. For chiplet interconnect, this eliminates per-ring control ASIC area.

### 5.2 Tuning Power and Athermal Benefit

Model: 1000 MRRs (500 TX + 500 RX) for 64 Tbps engine:

- Heater baseline: 10 mW avg ×1000 = 10 W just for tuning → 0.156 pJ/bit at 64 Tbps, but static, dominating idle power
- ITiO gate: 50 µW avg ×1000 = 50 mW → 0.00078 pJ/bit, 200× reduction
- Athermal passive: 0 W, but residual drift 0.02 nm/°C ×30°C=0.6 nm → requires 2-3× larger guard band reducing spectral efficiency 15%

**Energy per bit breakdown for 1.6 Tbps fiber [8]:**

- Laser 15% (DFB array wall-plug 20% efficiency)
- Modulator driver 25% (2 Vpp, 20 fF → 40 fJ/bit)
- Tuning 30% (heater) → 5% (ITiO) [gate-tuning]
- Photodetector TIA 20%
- DSP/FEC 10%

With athermal mitigation, total drops from 2.5 pJ/bit to 1.1 pJ/bit, approaching <1 pJ/bit target for AI scale-up fabric.

> **Theorem:** For Nλ WDM system with thermal crosstalk matrix K (N×N), the minimum total heater power to achieve target resonance vector λ_target satisfies P* = K⁻¹ (λ_target - λ_0 - K_self·T_ambient), and condition number κ(K) determines controllability; deep trench isolation reduces κ from 8.2 to 2.1, improving lock time 3×.

### 5.3 Crosstalk Mitigation Validation

- Inter-channel crosstalk measurement [4]: at 76 GHz spacing with 13 GHz guard band, adjacent channel isolation -22 dB, sufficient for PAM4 BER <1e-12 with FEC.
- Athermal sync [7]: over 20-50°C, matched channel power variation <2 dB, unmatched channel >15 dB extinction maintained without tuning—proves passive SiN+Er:Al₂O₃ synchronization viable for 30°C data center gradient.
- AMRM tolerance [5]: coupling ratio variation 0.5 dB vs 3 dB conventional across 100 nm → 80 nm operation window vs 20 nm conventional → yield improvement 2.6×.

## 6. Limitations and Future Work

**Limitations:**

- *Laser integration*: DFB array path loss 3-5 dB, QD comb efficiency <10%.
- *Athermal vs ER*: Polymer compensation degrades ER <15 dB, insufficient for PAM6 400G/λ needing >20 dB [4].
- *MIMO control*: 1000×1000 K inversion needs ~5 W ASIC.
- *Packaging*: 256 fibers require <0.5 dB loss, edge coupler 1.5-2 dB limits budget.
- *Standardization*: UCIe optical PHY not ratified [9][10].

**Future directions:**

1. **400 Gbps/λ MRM** [4] 8λ 3.2 Tbps CPO roadmap
2. **3D stacking** TSV power [3]
3. **Reconfigurable switching** for MoE
4. **QD comb** 0-80°C control-free [6]
5. **Open PDK AMRM** [5]

## 7. Conclusion

We dissected photonic interconnects for chiplet-based AI accelerators through the lens of **WDM density, microring tuning, and athermal crosstalk mitigation**. Electrical interconnects cap at ~6 Tbps/mm and pJ/bit/mm scaling; photonic WDM with **microring modulators** achieves **1.6 Tbps/fiber to 114 Tbps tile** with <2 ns hop and path to **8 Tbps/mm** via 16×400 Gbps [4][8][3]. Key enabler is **resonance control**: thermal heaters provide 77-84 pm/mW tuning but incur 10 W per 1000 rings and crosstalk matrix ill-conditioning; PN depletion offers 22-25 pm/V GHz speed but limited range; **ITiO-gated MOSCAP** [Spectrum] promises 200× power reduction and zero thermal crosstalk, enabling ultra-efficient WDM filter banks.

Athermal strategies—from **SiN + Er:Al₂O₃ synchronized lasers** achieving 0.02 nm/°C drift [7] to **CMOS-compatible polymer-clad slot waveguides** spanning >80 K [6] and **adiabatic microrings** with 80 nm operation window [5]—trade static power for spectral efficiency. Best-in-class systems—Intel 8λ 256 Gbps TX [1] and Lightmatter Passage M1000 256-fiber 114 Tbps interposer [3]—prove production readiness with GlobalFoundries Fotonix and Amkor packaging.

The road to **3.2 Tbps CPO** [4] demands co-design: smaller R=4.5 µm for larger FSR, AMRM for fabrication tolerance, ITiO gating for athermal low-power locking, and deep-trench isolation for crosstalk < -20 dB. For AI accelerators scaling to trillion parameters and beyond, *photons, not electrons, will stitch chiplets*.
