# The Minimal Model Program: The Cone Theorem, Flips, and Birational Classification of Algebraic Varieties

## Abstract

Initiated by Mori in the 1980s as a higher-dimensional analogue of Castelnuovo's contraction of exceptional curves on surfaces, the minimal model program is the modern engine of birational classification. This article develops the program from first principles: we explain how Mori's cone theorem decomposes the closed cone of curves into a nef part and discrete K_X-negative extremal rays of bounded length, how the contraction theorem turns each extremal ray into a morphism of one of three geometric types — divisorial contraction, flipping contraction, or Mori fiber space — and how the existence of flips, proved by Mori for threefolds and in general through the finite generation theorem of Birkar, Cascini, Hacon, and McKernan, allows the program to proceed through small contractions. We treat MMP with scaling, special termination, the basepoint-free theorem, and the abundance conjecture, and we give proof sketches for the existence of flips, termination of threefold flips, and finite generation of canonical rings. We close with the program's open frontiers: termination in dimension four and beyond, abundance, and the MMP in positive and mixed characteristic.

## 1 Intro

The classification of algebraic varieties up to birational equivalence is one of the oldest ambitions of algebraic geometry. For curves the answer is classical: a smooth projective curve is determined up to birational equivalence by its genus. For surfaces, the Italian school — Castelnuovo and Enriques — proved that every smooth projective surface is birational either to a *minimal model*, a surface containing no exceptional curves of the first kind, or to a ruled surface over a curve. The proof is constructive: as long as the surface contains a smooth rational curve C with self-intersection minus one, Castelnuovo's contraction theorem contracts it, strictly decreasing the Picard number, and the process must stop.

In dimension three and higher this naive procedure collapses. Contracting a K_X-negative extremal ray on a smooth threefold can produce a singular variety on which the canonical divisor is no longer Cartier — only Q-Cartier — and worse, a *small* contraction may produce a variety that is not even Q-factorial, so the canonical class cannot be intersected with curves at all. Mori's insight was that the program must be run on a larger category — projective varieties with Q-factorial terminal singularities — and that small contractions must be *replaced* rather than accepted: this is the **flip** [2][7].

Mori's 1982 proof of the cone theorem, via his celebrated bend-and-break technique in positive characteristic, gave the first structural description of the cone of curves of a smooth projective variety [8]. By 1988 Mori had proved the existence of flips for threefolds and completed the minimal model program in dimension three. The log version, for pairs (X, Delta), was developed through the 1980s and 1990s by Kawamata, Kollar, Reid, and Shokurov; Shokurov's reduction of flips to *pl flips* and the extension theorems of Hacon and McKernan set the stage for the decisive breakthrough: Birkar, Cascini, Hacon, and McKernan proved the finite generation of canonical rings and hence the existence of flips — and of minimal models for varieties of log general type — in all dimensions [1][2][4].

Today the minimal model program is both a classification machine and a meeting point of techniques: characteristic-p methods, analytic extension theorems, convex geometry of cones of divisors, and the combinatorics of the toric case [5][6]. This article presents its architecture in full, from the cone theorem to the abundance conjecture.

## 2 Background

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, typically the complex numbers. Let X be a normal projective variety of dimension n. Two Cartier divisors are *numerically equivalent* if they have the same intersection number with every curve; the quotient of divisor classes by numerical equivalence is the finite-dimensional real vector space N^1(X), dual to the space N_1(X) spanned by numerical classes of curves. Inside N_1(X) sits the **Mori cone**

NE-bar(X) = closure of { sum of [C] : C an irreducible curve on X },

a closed convex cone whose dual is the nef cone Nef(X). Kleiman's criterion states that a divisor D is ample if and only if D is strictly positive on NE-bar(X) minus the origin.

The canonical divisor K_X measures the failure of X to be Calabi–Yau. When K_X is not nef, there are curves with K_X . C < 0, and the program seeks to eliminate them. To do intersection theory on singular varieties we need K_X to be Q-Cartier; the natural category is therefore **Q-factorial** varieties, on which every Weil divisor is Q-Cartier.

> **Theorem:** (Discrepancy and singularities of pairs) Let (X, Delta) be a pair with X normal and Delta an effective Q-divisor such that K_X + Delta is Q-Cartier. For a birational morphism f: Y -> X from smooth Y, write K_Y = f^*(K_X + Delta) + sum a(E, X, Delta) E. The pair is *Kawamata log terminal* (klt) if a(E, X, Delta) > -1 for all E; *log canonical* (lc) if a(E, X, Delta) >= -1; *terminal* if a(E, X, Delta) > 0 for all exceptional E. Discrepancies can only increase under the operations of the MMP, so these classes are stable under the program [1].

The **Kodaira dimension** kappa(X) in { -inf, 0, 1, ..., n } records the growth of plurigenera h^0(X, mK_X).

Two foundational results govern linear systems on the way there.

## 3 Methodology

The minimal model program is an algorithm.

1. **Test nefness.** If K_X + Delta is nef, stop: X is a minimal model.
2. **Find an extremal ray.** Otherwise the cone theorem provides a (K_X + Delta)-negative extremal ray R of NE-bar(X); the contraction theorem produces the contraction morphism f_R: X -> Y contracting exactly the curves whose class lies in R.
3. **Branch on the geometry of f_R** (see the table below).
4. **Iterate** on the new pair, with Delta replaced by its strict transform.

The three possible contraction types are:

| Type | Exceptional locus | dim Y | Effect on Picard number | Program action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Divisorial contraction** | an irreducible divisor E | = dim X | drops by 1 | Replace X by Y, continue |
| **Flipping (small) contraction** | codimension >= 2 | = dim X | unchanged | Replace X by its *flip* X^+, continue |
| **Mori fiber space** | all of X | < dim X | — | **Stop**: output f: X -> Y |

A **flip** of a small contraction f: X -> Z is a small birational morphism f^+: X^+ -> Z from a Q-factorial variety X^+ such that K_{X^+} + Delta^+ is f^+-ample, where Delta^+ is the strict transform of Delta.

```
        X  - - - > X^+
         \         /
          \       /
           v     v
              Z
```

with K_X negative on contracted curves and K_{X^+} positive on the new ones.

Because arbitrary choices of extremal rays can in principle cycle forever, the modern formulation runs the **MMP with scaling** [1][5].

lambda_1 = inf { mu >= 0 : K_X + Delta + mu A is nef }.

At each step choose a (K_{X_i} + Delta_i)-negative extremal ray R_i that is (K_{X_i} + Delta_i + lambda_i A_i)-trivial, perform the corresponding divisorial contraction or flip, and recompute lambda_{i+1} <= lambda_i.

```tla
---- MODULE MMP ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Reals
VARIABLES X, Delta, lambda, step
Init == /\ X \in QFactorialPairs
        /\ Delta \in EffectiveQDivisors(X)
        /\ lambda \in { mu \in Real : mu >= 0 /\ IsNef(K(X)+Delta+mu*A) }
        /\ step = 0
ContractOrFlip ==
    \/ /\ ~IsNef(K(X) + Delta)              \* extremal ray exists
       /\ \E R \in ExtremalRays(X) :
             /\ Intersect(K(X)+Delta, R) < 0
             /\ Intersect(K(X)+Delta+lambda*A, R) = 0
             /\ LET f == Contraction(R) IN
                IF IsDivisorial(f) THEN X' = Target(f)
                ELSE X' = Flip(f)          \* small contraction
       /\ Delta' = StrictTransform(Delta)
       /\ lambda' \in { mu \in Real : mu >= 0 /\ IsNef(K(X')+Delta'+mu*A') }
       /\ lambda' <= lambda
       /\ step' = step + 1
Terminate ==
    \/ IsNef(K(X) + Delta)                 \* minimal model reached
    \/ \E f \in Morphisms(X) : IsMoriFiberSpace(f)
Next == ContractOrFlip \/ Terminate
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<X, Delta, lambda, step>>
====
```

The invariant that makes the whole machine plausible is monotonicity of discrepancies: a divisorial contraction or flip never *decreases* any discrepancy, and strictly increases those of divisors whose center lies in the exceptional/flipping locus.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Cone Theorem and Bend-and-Break

> **Theorem:** (Cone theorem, Mori [8]) Let X be a smooth projective variety. Then NE-bar(X) = NE-bar(X)_{K_X >= 0} + sum_i R_i, where the R_i are (K_X-negative) extremal rays, and the rays are discrete in the half-space { K_X < 0 }. Each extremal ray R_i is spanned by a rational curve C_i with 0 < -K_X . C_i <= 2 dim X.

The *length bound* 2 dim X is the miracle.

For pairs, the theorem generalizes: for a klt pair (X, Delta), the (K_X + Delta)-negative part of NE-bar(X) is generated by extremal rays spanned by rational curves with 0 < -(K_X + Delta) .

```haskell
-- Discrepancy of a divisor over a pair, by recursion on a log resolution.
-- a(E, X, D) > -1 for every E  <=>  (X, D) is klt.
data Pair = Pair { variety :: String, boundary :: QDivisor }

discrepancy :: Pair -> Divisor -> Rational
discrepancy (Pair _ d) e
  | isExceptional e = coeffOf e (canonicalPullback d) - coeffOf e d - 1
  | otherwise       = - coeffOf e d

isKLT :: Pair -> [Divisor] -> Bool
isKLT p divisors = all (\e -> discrepancy p e > -1) divisors

-- The MMP never decreases discrepancies: the singular locus can only improve.
mmpStepImproves :: Pair -> Pair -> [Divisor] -> Bool
mmpStepImproves before after es =
  all (\e -> discrepancy after e >= discrepancy before e) es
```

### 4.2 The Contraction Theorem: Divisorial Contractions and Mori Fiber Spaces

> **Theorem:** (Contraction theorem) Let (X, Delta) be klt and F a (K_X + Delta)-negative extremal face of NE-bar(X). Then there exists a projective morphism f: X -> Y with connected fibers, f_* O_X = O_Y, such that a curve C is contracted by f if and only if [C] lies in F. Moreover L - (K_X + Delta) f-ample implies L = f^* M for some line bundle M on Y.

When F is a ray, the trichotomy of Section 3 applies.

At the other extreme, a contraction with dim Y < dim X is a **Mori fiber space**: X is covered by (K_X + Delta)-negative curves contracted by f, the relative Picard number rho(X/Y) equals 1, and -(K_X + Delta) is f-ample.

```rust
/// Classify the contraction of a (K_X + Delta)-negative extremal ray.
enum Contraction {
    Divisorial { exceptional: String },   // contracts a prime divisor
    Flipping { locus_codim: u32 },        // small: needs a flip
    MoriFiber { base_dim: usize },        // dim Y < dim X: terminal object
}

fn classify(ray: &ExtremalRay, x: &Variety) -> Contraction {
    let f = contraction_morphism(ray);
    let exc_dim = exceptional_locus_dim(&f);
    if target_dim(&f) < x.dim {
        Contraction::MoriFiber { base_dim: target_dim(&f) }
    } else if exc_dim == x.dim - 1 {
        Contraction::Divisorial { exceptional: "E".into() }
    } else {
        Contraction::Flipping { locus_codim: (x.dim - exc_dim) as u32 }
    }
}
```

### 4.3 Flips: From Mori's Threefolds to Finite Generation

The **flip** is the deep step.

Mori proved existence for threefolds in 1988 by an intricate explicit classification of flipping contractions, building on his earlier work on extremal neighborhoods.

The breakthrough of Hacon and McKernan was analytic: their **extension theorems** — vast generalizations of the Siu–Kawamata extension of pluricanonical sections from a divisor to the ambient variety — allowed them to lift sections from S and prove existence of pl flips in all dimensions [1].

### 4.4 MMP with Scaling, Special Termination, and Finiteness of Models

Even with flips in hand, an infinite sequence of flips is a priori possible.

In higher dimensions the tool is **special termination with scaling** [1][3].

> **Theorem:** (Special termination with scaling [1]) Assume existence and termination of flips in dimension n - 1. Let (X, Delta + C) be n-dimensional Q-factorial dlt with K_X + Delta + C nef, Delta = A + B with A ample. Then any sequence of (K_X + Delta)-flips with scaling of C has flipping locus eventually disjoint from floor(Delta_k).

Combined with the **finiteness of models** — for fixed ample A, only finitely many weak log canonical models occur as the boundary varies in a rational polytope [1] — this yields termination of the MMP with scaling whenever K_X + Delta is *big*: the thresholds lambda_i can drop only finitely many times before stabilizing, and each stable stage admits only finitely many models.

```python
def mmp_with_scaling_thresholds(K, Delta, A, steps=8):
    """Toy model: the nef threshold lambda_i is nonincreasing along the MMP.
    Each flip strictly decreases the volume of K + Delta + lambda*A on the
    flipping locus, so thresholds stabilize after finitely many steps
    when K + Delta is big (finiteness of models)."""
    lam = nef_threshold(K + Delta, A)   # inf{ mu >= 0 : K+Delta+mu*A nef }
    history = [lam]
    for _ in range(steps):
        R = extremal_ray(K + Delta, trivial_for=(K + Delta + lam * A))
        if R is None:                   # K + Delta nef: minimal model found
            break
        X, Delta, A = perform_step(R)   # divisorial contraction or flip
        lam = min(lam, nef_threshold(K + Delta, A))
        history.append(lam)
    assert all(b <= a for a, b in zip(history, history[1:]))
    return history
```

### 4.5 Abundance, Basepoint-Freeness, and Canonical Models

A minimal model is only half the victory: K_{X_min} + Delta_min is *nef*, but is it *semiample* — does some multiple define a morphism?

> **Theorem:** (Basepoint-free theorem, Kawamata–Shokurov [4]) Let (X, Delta) be klt and D a nef Cartier divisor such that aD - (K_X + Delta) is nef and big for some a > 0. Then |mD| is basepoint-free for m >> 0.

The basepoint-free theorem is what converts numerical data into morphisms throughout the program — it is used at every contraction step.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We sketch the three central arguments; full details are in [1][2][4].

**Existence of flips from finite generation.** Let f: X -> Z be a flipping contraction for a klt pair (X, Delta). Form the relative graded algebra R = direct sum over m of f_* O_X(floor(m(K_X + Delta))). If R is finitely generated as an O_Z-algebra, then X^+ := Proj_Z(R) is projective over Z, Q-factorial, small over Z, and carries an f^+-ample divisor restricting to K_X + Delta on the common open set — i.e., it is the flip. BCHM prove finite generation by running an MMP with scaling on a suitable resolution and using the extension theorems to lift sections across each step [1][2]. The key analytic input is the Hacon–McKernan extension theorem: sections of m(K_S + Delta_S) on a component S of the boundary extend to the ambient variety when the relevant adjoint divisor is big, which is exactly what makes pl flips accessible.

**Termination in dimension three.** Shokurov's *difficulty* d(X, Delta) counts (with multiplicity) the exceptional divisors E over X whose discrepancy a(E, X, Delta) lies in the half-open interval with left endpoint -1 and right endpoint 1, excluding those with discrepancy exactly 1. A flip strictly decreases d: the flipping curves have small discrepancies, and the flipped curves acquire larger ones, while discrepancies elsewhere do not decrease. Since d is a nonnegative integer, no infinite sequence of flips exists. Kawamata, Matsuda, and Matsuki refined the invariant to the log terminal setting [7].

**Termination with scaling when K_X + Delta is big.** Suppose K_X + Delta is big. The nef thresholds lambda_i form a nonincreasing sequence of real numbers. By the rationality theorem and the ACC for log canonical thresholds, the set of possible thresholds is discrete; by finiteness of models, for each fixed threshold value only finitely many distinct models can appear [1]. Hence the sequence stabilizes: lambda_i = lambda for all large i. Special termination then forces the flipping locus away from floor(Delta), and an induction on dimension — restricting to a general element of a big linear system — shows the remaining flips terminate. The output is a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space [3].

*Computational evidence.* The toric case is fully effective: the MMP with scaling for toric varieties can be implemented combinatorially, with each step corresponding to a wall-crossing in the secondary fan, and termination is a finite polyhedral computation [5]. For Mori dream spaces — varieties whose Cox ring is finitely generated, including all Fano varieties — every small Q-factorial modification factors as a finite sequence of flips, and the MMP becomes a finite chamber decomposition of the effective cone [6]. These computable cases serve as the laboratory in which the general conjectures are tested.

## 6 Limitations

The program's foundations are complete in dimension three and for pairs of log general type in all dimensions, but several frontiers remain genuinely open.

- **Termination of arbitrary flips in dimension four and above.** Special termination handles the MMP with scaling, and terminal fourfold flips are known to terminate (Shokurov, Kawamata, Fujino), but termination of an *arbitrary* sequence of klt flips in dimension >= 4 is open. Hacon and Moraga's reduction to lower-dimensional termination plus weak Zariski decompositions [3] is the sharpest current conditional result.
- **The abundance conjecture.** That nef implies semiample for K_X + Delta is known through dimension three and for special classes (e.g., when the nef dimension is small), but open in general in dimension >= 4. Without abundance, minimal models exist but canonical models may not.
- **Log canonical vs. klt.** The full MMP with scaling is established for lc pairs, but good minimal models for lc pairs require additional hypotheses (e.g., log abundance around the non-klt locus). The non-klt locus can obstruct both termination and semiampleness.
- **Positive and mixed characteristic.** The cone and contraction theorems, and the full MMP, are known through dimension three in characteristic p > 0 (work of Hacon–Xu, Birkar, and others), but bend-and-break, resolution of singularities, and vanishing theorems all behave worse in positive characteristic, and dimension four and up remain largely open. Recent progress in mixed characteristic offers hope but no complete program yet.
- **Effectivity.** Even where flips exist, the BCHM proof is highly non-constructive: no bound is known on the number of steps, and the flip itself is a Proj of a ring whose generators are not explicitly controlled. For applications to moduli and explicit classification, this non-effectivity is a real cost.

## 7 Conclusion

The minimal model program transforms the vague hope of "simplifying a variety by contracting bad curves" into a precise algorithm with three moving parts: the **cone theorem**, which finds the curves to contract; the **contraction theorem**, which builds the morphism; and the **flip**, which repairs the small contractions that would otherwise break Q-factoriality. Mori's bend-and-break, the Kawamata–Shokurov basepoint-free theorem, Shokurov's reduction to pl flips, and the finite generation theorem of Birkar, Cascini, Hacon, and McKernan form the logical spine [1][2][4][7][8].

What remains — termination in higher dimensions, abundance, and the program in positive characteristic — is not a sign of weakness but of depth: each open problem sits at the intersection of convex geometry, commutative algebra, and complex analysis. The MMP with scaling and the theory of generalized pairs [3] have already extended the program's reach far beyond its original threefold setting, and the toric and Mori dream space cases [5][6] show that where the combinatorics is finite, the program is completely effective. Birational classification, a century after the Italian school, finally has its engine; the task now is to map every road it can travel.
