# Prime Editing Off-Target Mitigation: Nickase Engineering, Engineered pegRNA Architectures, and Whole-Genome Validation of Precision Genome Editors

## Abstract

Prime editing (PE) writes arbitrary sequence changes into genomic DNA through a Cas9(H840A) nickase fused to an engineered Moloney murine leukemia virus reverse transcriptase, programmed by a prime editing guide RNA (pegRNA) that simultaneously specifies the target locus and encodes the desired edit [1]. Because productive editing demands sequential passage through spacer hybridization, primer-binding-site annealing, reverse transcription, and 3′ flap resolution — four semi-independent molecular checkpoints — PE exhibits intrinsically lower off-target activity than Cas9 nuclease at matched loci [1]. Residual off-target editing nevertheless persists, concentrated in PE3 architectures whose second nick reintroduces double-strand-break-adjacent hazards. This thesis develops a unified mitigation doctrine along three axes: *(i)* nickase engineering, from H840A through temporally gated PE3b nicking, the PEmax architecture, and mismatch-repair suppression in PE4/PE5 [4][5]; *(ii)* engineered pegRNA architectures, including tevopreQ1-stabilized epegRNAs and paired twinPE designs that suppress off-target probability quadratically [2][3]; and *(iii)* whole-genome validation assays adapted to nickase editors, for which DSB-capture methods such as GUIDE-seq are structurally blind [6][7]. We formalize a thermodynamic specificity model, prove checkpoint-multiplication and quadratic-suppression theorems, and compile empirical off-target rates across PE generations.

## 1 Introduction

Up to 89% of known pathogenic human variants are, in principle, correctable by the edit classes prime editing supports — all twelve base-to-base conversions, small insertions, deletions, and combinations — without double-strand breaks (DSBs) or donor DNA [1]. The 2019 demonstration of >175 distinct edits in human cells, including correction of the sickle-cell *HBB* transversion and the Tay–Sachs *HEXA* deletion, repositioned editing from a nuclease-centric paradigm to a *write-centric* one: the editor synthesizes the desired sequence directly into the genome instead of breaking DNA and hoping for repair [1].

Precision, however, is a claim about the *whole genome*, not the target locus. Any therapeutic editor must satisfy two inequalities: on-target efficiency above a clinical threshold, and genome-wide off-target burden below a quantifiable safety threshold. Cas9 nuclease fails the second at many loci: GUIDE-seq revealed off-target DSBs at sites bearing up to five mismatches [6]. Base editors trade DSBs for deaminase-dependent off-target deamination of DNA and RNA. Prime editing's wager is architectural — *specificity through sequential checkpoints* — and the record supports it: Anzalone and colleagues reported "much lower off-target editing than Cas9 nuclease at known Cas9 off-target sites" [1], and epegRNAs preserved that property while raising efficiency 3–4-fold [3].

This thesis argues that residual off-target risk in PE is three coupled phenomena, each demanding its own engineering response:

1. **Nickase-mediated risk.** PE3's second nick on the unedited strand can coincide with the first to produce a *de facto* DSB, resurrecting indel hazards the system was designed to avoid [1].
2. **Guide-mediated risk.** Exonucleolytically truncated pegRNAs occupy target sites as dominant-negative inhibitors — and, at homologous off-target loci, as potential editors carrying corrupted templates [3].
3. **Validation risk.** Assays built for nucleases detect DSBs; applied naively to nickase editors they report comforting zeros that are *assay* artifacts rather than biological facts [6][7].

Our contribution is a unified mitigation doctrine: engineer the nickase in *time* (PE3b) and in *protein space* (PEmax, high-fidelity nickases), engineer the guide in *structure* (epegRNAs) and in *multiplicity* (twinPE), and validate with assays whose detection physics matches nickase biology [2][3][4][6][7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From nucleases to writers

CRISPR–Cas9 nuclease editing couples target recognition to concerted cleavage of both DNA strands; the cell's DSB repair machinery then determines the outcome, stochastically. Off-target DSBs are therefore off-target *mutations* with probability approaching one [6][7]. Base editing replaced cleavage with deamination but retained a stoichiometric hazard: the deaminase acts on any accessible substrate, producing guide-dependent and guide-independent off-target edits in DNA and RNA.

Prime editing breaks this coupling. The effector fuses *S. pyogenes* Cas9(H840A) — HNH inactivated, so only the RuvC domain nicks the PAM-containing (edited) strand — to an engineered M-MLV reverse transcriptase [1]. The pegRNA extends a standard sgRNA with a 3′ extension comprising a primer binding site (PBS) and a reverse transcription template (RTT) encoding the edit. The nicked 3′ flap anneals to the PBS, RT polymerizes the RTT into DNA, and flap-resolution pathways install the edit — no DSB, no donor DNA.

### 2.2 Generations of the prime editor

| System | Protein | Guide logic | Key advance | Off-target posture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PE1 | nCas9(H840A)–WT M-MLV RT | pegRNA | Proof of concept | Baseline (low) [1] |
| PE2 | nCas9(H840A)–RT pentamutant (D200N/L603W/T330P/T306K/W313F) | pegRNA | ~5× over PE1 | Baseline (low) [1] |
| PE3 | PE2 protein | pegRNA + nicking sgRNA (unedited strand) | Repair bias → higher efficiency | Elevated indels; nick-pair DSB risk [1] |
| PE3b | PE2 protein | pegRNA + nicking sgRNA matching *edited* sequence | Nick gated on prior edit | PE3 efficiency, near-PE2 byproducts [1] |
| PE4 | PE2 + MLH1dn | pegRNA | MMR suppression | Retained precision, higher purity [4] |
| PE5 | PE3 + MLH1dn | pegRNA + nicking sgRNA | MMR suppression + repair bias | Highest efficiency; PE3-like indel watch [4] |
| PEmax | Codon-optimized RT, Cas9 R221K/N394K, improved NLS | Any of above | Broad efficiency gain | Architecture-neutral [4] |
| twinPE | PE2/PEmax protein | Two pegRNAs, complementary RTT flaps | Large insertions/deletions/inversions | Quadratic suppression [2] |

### 2.3 Mismatch repair as the silent antagonist

Two 2021–2022 studies revealed that mismatch repair (MMR) actively antagonizes prime editing: the heteroduplex formed after flap resolution is recognized as a mismatch and excised, erasing the edit [4][5]. Chen *et al.* showed transient dominant-negative MLH1 (MLH1dn) co-expression yields PE4/PE5 with improved efficiency and product purity [4]; Ferreira da Silva *et al.* independently confirmed enhanced editing in MMR-deficient backgrounds [5]. Because MMR suppression raises the survival probability of *any* incorporated flap — on- or off-target — PE4/PE5 demand stricter validation than their efficiency gains might suggest.

## 3 Methodology

We evaluate off-target mitigation through a triangulation protocol for nickase editors, applied uniformly across PE generations:

1. **In silico enumeration and thermodynamic scoring.** Protospacers within Hamming distance ≤ 6 of the spacer are enumerated and scored by a nearest-neighbor free-energy model over spacer hybridization, PBS annealing, and flap incorporation (§4.3), ranked by predicted editing probability.
2. **Biochemical profiling.** In vitro nickase-adapted assays (nDigenome-seq analogues) find sites where the editor binds and nicks purified genomic DNA, bounding biochemically accessible off-targets [7].
3. **Cellular validation.** Top candidates are assayed by targeted deep sequencing (~0.1% floor); edited single-cell clones undergo 30–60× WGS with matched parental controls; RNA-seq screens for guide-independent perturbation, since PE expresses an active RT [7].

**Metrics.** For each system we report the *on:off ratio*, the *indel fraction* at the target, and the *byproduct spectrum* (partial RTT incorporations, scaffold insertions). A therapeutic candidate must demonstrate on:off > 10³ with indel fraction < 1%.

**Assumptions.** SpCas9 (NGG) PAM availability, diploid human genome context, and transient editor expression. Stable or long-term expression changes the risk calculus (§6).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Nickase Engineering: From H840A to Temporal Gating and PEmax

The H840A lesion is the foundation of PE specificity. Wild-type Cas9 requires only R-loop formation plus concerted cleavage — two checkpoints. The prime editor requires R-loop formation, nicking, flap–PBS annealing, reverse transcription, and flap resolution — five checkpoints.

> **Theorem 1 (Checkpoint multiplication).** *Let p₁…p₅ be the per-checkpoint probabilities that an off-target locus passes each successive PE checkpoint, with pᵢ ≤ qᵢ where qᵢ is the corresponding Cas9-nuclease checkpoint probability. Then the off-target editing probability satisfies P_PE(off) = ∏ᵢpᵢ ≤ ∏ᵢqᵢ = P_Cas9(off), with strict inequality whenever any downstream checkpoint (PBS annealing, RT processivity, flap resolution) discriminates beyond R-loop formation.*

*Proof sketch.* The joint probability is the product of conditional checkpoint probabilities. The nuclease pathway ends after checkpoint 2 while PE requires checkpoints 3–5 — each involving sequence-specific hybridization or polymerization — so the product acquires additional factors strictly below one wherever the PBS or RTT mismatches the off-target flap. ∎

**PE3 and its discontents.** PE3 adds a nicking sgRNA against the unedited strand to bias repair toward the edited product [1]. But two nicks on opposite strands within ~100 bp form a staggered DSB, and the consequence is exactly what theory predicts: elevated indels. The mitigation is *temporal*: **PE3b** designs the nicking spacer to complement the *edited* sequence, so the second nick can only occur after flap incorporation has rewritten the locus — kinetic proofreading in guide sequence [1].

**PEmax and high-fidelity nickases.** Chen *et al.* re-architected the protein: human codon-optimized RT, SpCas9 R221K/N394K, and improved nuclear localization, with gains composing multiplicatively with PE4/PE5 and epegRNAs (10–100-fold combined) [4]. Orthogonally, high-fidelity Cas9 nickase domains with reduced off-target R-loop stability can replace the wild-type nickase module; by Theorem 1, tightening checkpoint 1 tightens the whole product.

### 4.2 Engineered pegRNA Architectures: epegRNAs and Paired Designs

Nelson *et al.* showed that appending structured RNA motifs — the trimmed prequeosine₁₋₁ aptamer (**tevopreQ1**) or a frameshifting pseudoknot (**mpknot**) — to the 3′ terminus blocks exonucleolytic decay, improving editing 3–4-fold across cell types and delivery modalities *without* increasing off-target editing at assayed sites [3]. Zhang and colleagues independently validated the principle with xrRNA motifs, reporting up to 9.1-fold gains in PE3 frameworks with retained specificity [7].

**Paired pegRNAs and twinPE.** In twin prime editing, two pegRNAs program nicks on opposite strands while their RTTs encode complementary 3′ flaps overlapping by ≥ 20 nt; the flaps hybridize to each other rather than to the genome, and the intervening unedited segment is excised [2]. Anzalone *et al.* replaced a 90-bp *HEK3* segment with *attB* at ~80% efficiency, installed *attP* at ~58%, and — with Bxb1 recombinase — integrated 5.6-kb cargo at up to 6.8% and inverted 40-kb segments at 7.7–9.6%, with fewer byproducts than paired-nuclease strategies [2].

> **Theorem 2 (Quadratic suppression by paired nicking).** *Consider an off-target locus where a single pegRNA induces an edit with probability p ≪ 1. A twinPE-class design requiring two independent pegRNA-specified nicks with complementary flap hybridization at the same locus induces an edit with probability O(p²), provided the two off-target binding events are approximately independent.*

*Proof sketch.* Productive twinPE editing requires nick 1 *and* nick 2 *and* flap–flap hybridization within one spatiotemporal window. Under approximate independence of the two binding events, P(both) ≈ p², and the hybridization requirement contributes a further factor below one unless the RTTs are mutually complementary *and* the nicks are correctly phased — not satisfied by random co-occurrence. ∎

Practical consequences:

- *Specificity budget.* A marginal single-pegRNA off-target (p ~ 10⁻³) becomes negligible under twinPE (p² ~ 10⁻⁶).
- *Design rule.* Paired RTTs must be mutually complementary *and* lack homology to the reference genome; genome-homologous flaps revert the mechanism to single-pegRNA statistics.
- *Cost.* Two guides double the guide-dependent search space; validation must enumerate off-targets for *both* spacers and their joint occurrences.

### 4.3 A Thermodynamic Specificity Model

Sequence similarity is a poor proxy for PE off-target propensity because checkpoints 3–5 are thermodynamic. We model the productive complex as

ΔG_total = ΔG_R-loop + ΔG_PBS + ΔG_RT + ΔG_flap

with ΔG_R-loop from Cas9 mismatch energetics, ΔG_PBS the nearest-neighbor duplex energy of flap–PBS annealing, ΔG_RT penalizing RTT secondary structure against polymerase processivity, and ΔG_flap the edited-vs-unedited flap equilibration energy. Off-target probability at locus *j* follows a Boltzmann factor

P_j ∝ exp(−ΔG_total(j) / RT) / Σ_k exp(−ΔG_total(k) / RT),

normalized over candidates. Reference implementation:

```python
import math
from itertools import product

# Nearest-neighbor DNA duplex parameters (kcal/mol at 37 C), SantaLucia 1998, excerpt
NN = {('A','T'): -1.00, ('T','A'): -0.88, ('C','G'): -1.44, ('G','C'): -1.30,
      ('A','A'): -0.73, ('T','T'): -0.73, ('C','C'): -0.92, ('G','G'): -0.92}
INIT, RT = 1.96, 0.616  # kcal/mol

def duplex_dg(seq5to3: str) -> float:
    """Nearest-neighbor Delta-G of a perfect duplex for seq (5'->3')."""
    s = seq5to3.upper()
    dg = INIT
    for a, b in zip(s, s[1:]):
        dg += NN.get((a, b), -0.5)  # fallback for mixed steps
    return dg

def mismatch_penalty(flap: str, pbs: str) -> float:
    """+1.5 kcal/mol per mismatch destabilizing PBS annealing."""
    return 1.5 * sum(1 for a, b in zip(flap, pbs) if a != b)

def score_offtarget(flap_seq: str, pbs_seq: str, rloop_dg: float) -> float:
    """Boltzmann weight of productive editing at a candidate locus."""
    dg = rloop_dg + duplex_dg(pbs_seq) + mismatch_penalty(flap_seq, pbs_seq)
    return math.exp(-dg / RT)

# Example: rank three candidate PBS lengths at a fixed locus
flap = "GATTACAGATTACA"
for L in (10, 13, 16):
    pbs = flap[:L]  # perfect-match control
    print(L, round(score_offtarget(flap, pbs, rloop_dg=-18.2), 4))
```

The same scoring composes as a *monoid* over mismatch penalties:

```haskell
-- Specificity scoring as a monoid over per-position penalties
newtype Penalty = Penalty { getPenalty :: Double } deriving (Show, Eq)
instance Semigroup Penalty where
  Penalty a <> Penalty b = Penalty (a + b)

instance Monoid Penalty where
  mempty = Penalty 0.0

-- | Position-weighted mismatch penalty: PAM-proximal mismatches cost more.
positionalPenalty :: Int -> Char -> Char -> Penalty
positionalPenalty i a b
  | a == b    = mempty
  | otherwise = Penalty (1.5 * exp (-fromIntegral i / 8.0))

scoreLocus :: String -> String -> Penalty
scoreLocus guide target =
  mconcat [positionalPenalty i a b | (i, (a, b)) <- zip [0..] (zip guide target)]
```

The model's qualitative predictions match experiment: lengthening the PBS raises on-target efficiency until stable PBS secondary structure or off-target PBS annealing dominates — the classic inverted-U that epegRNA stabilization shifts rightward [3].

### 4.4 Whole-Genome Validation for Nickase Editors

The central validation insight is *detection-physics matching*: an assay that detects DSBs cannot validate a nickase. GUIDE-seq integrates a 34-bp dsODN at DSBs and maps integration sites at ~0.1% sensitivity [6] — but PE2 generates no DSB, so a clean GUIDE-seq result for PE2 is uninformative rather than reassuring. PE3's nick pair *can* form DSBs and is partially visible, which makes cross-generation comparisons using GUIDE-seq alone systematically biased in PE2's favor [7].

| Assay | Detects | Nickase-visible? | Sensitivity | Role in PE validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GUIDE-seq [6] | DSBs (dsODN capture) | PE3 only; PE2 blind | ~0.1% | Screens PE3 nick-pair DSBs |
| CIRCLE-seq | In vitro DSBs | PE3 only; high FP rate [7] | High (in vitro) | Hypothesis generation, needs validation |
| nDigenome-seq analogue | In vitro nicks | Yes | High (in vitro) | Upper bound on accessible sites |
| Targeted deep seq (rhAmpSeq) | Edits at nominated sites | Yes | ~0.1% | Validates top in silico candidates |
| Clone WGS (30–60×) | All clonal variants | Yes | Clonal only | Unbiased gold standard [7] |
| RNA-seq | Transcriptomic perturbation | Yes (indirect) | — | Guide-independent screen [7] |

Candidate enumeration for targeted validation must be genome-wide and mismatch-tolerant; a minimal Rust kernel:

```rust
/// Enumerate candidate off-target protospacers within `max_mm` mismatches.
pub fn enumerate_candidates(genome: &[u8], spacer: &[u8], max_mm: u8) -> Vec<(usize, u8)> {
    let k = spacer.len();
    let mut hits = Vec::new();
    for (i, window) in genome.windows(k).enumerate() {
        let mm: u8 = window.iter().zip(spacer.iter())
            .map(|(a, b)| (a != b) as u8).sum();
        if mm <= max_mm {
            hits.push((i, mm));
        }
    }
    hits
}
```

Two further considerations complete the validation picture. First, *guide-independent* off-target activity: PE carries no deaminase, and Gao *et al.* found minimal guide-independent genomic or transcriptomic perturbation — consistent with RT acting only on a correctly primed 3′ flap [7]. Second, *long-read* validation: short-read WGS is insensitive to large structural variants, yet PE3 nick pairs and twinPE excisions can in principle generate deletions and translocations — UDiTaS/Tn5-tagmentation assays anchored at the target site close this gap [7].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

Compiling reported measurements across PE generations (human cell lines, transient delivery):

| System | On-target (representative) | Max validated off-target | Indel fraction | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PE2 | 20–50% (HEK3 ins) | < 0.1% at 4 Cas9 off-target sites | < 0.5% | [1] |
| PE3 | 2–4× PE2 | < 0.1% at nominated sites | 1–10% (locus-dependent) | [1] |
| PE3b | ≈ PE3 | Undetected above 0.1% | ≈ PE2 | [1] |
| epegRNA (PE2/PE3) | 3–4× over pegRNA | Similar to pegRNA controls | Unchanged | [3] |
| PE5 | Up to ~10× PE2 (combined w/ epegRNA, PEmax) | Retained precision | PE3-like watch | [4] |
| twinPE | 58–80% (90-bp replacement) | Fewer byproducts than paired Cas9 | Minimal | [2] |

Three regularities emerge. **First**, PE2's off-target rate at validated Cas9 off-target sites sits at or below deep-sequencing detection limits — Theorem 1 realized [1]. **Second**, PE3's efficiency gain is purchased with indels, and PE3b's temporal gate recovers PE2-like purity at PE3-like efficiency [1]. **Third**, every efficiency-boosting modification (epegRNAs [3], MLH1dn [4][5], PEmax [4]) preserves its parent architecture's off-target posture — but MMR suppression must raise the survival probability of any incorporated off-target flap, so the absence of a detected increase reflects assay sensitivity, not a theorem.

We formalize the flap-resolution race in TLA+:

```tla+
---- MODULE FlapResolution ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES flap5, state
(* state \in {"annealed", "ligated_edited", "ligated_unedited", "excised"} *)
Init == /\ flap5 = "present"
        /\ state = "annealed"
LigateEdited   == /\ state = "annealed" /\ state' = "ligated_edited"
                  /\ UNCHANGED flap5
LigateUnedited == /\ state = "annealed" /\ state' = "ligated_unedited"
                  /\ UNCHANGED flap5
Excise5        == /\ state = "annealed" /\ flap5' = "excised"
                  /\ state' = "excised"
Next == LigateEdited \/ LigateUnedited \/ Excise5
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<flap5, state>>
THEOREM Spec => <>(state = "ligated_edited")
====
```

> **Theorem 3 (PE3b gating reduces byproduct probability).** *In the flap-resolution race above, let the PE3 nick on the unedited strand occur only in states where the edited flap has been ligated (the PE3b condition). Then every execution containing the second nick also contains prior edit installation, so nick-induced indels cannot precede editing — the byproduct class "nick-pair DSB without edit" is unreachable.*

*Proof sketch.* Under the PE3b condition the second nick is guarded on `state = "ligated_edited"`, so any trace exhibiting the nick has a prefix ending in `LigateEdited`. Nick-pair DSBs are thus confined to post-edit states, where end joining acts on already-edited alleles — the byproduct class "nick-pair DSB without edit" is unreachable. ∎

## 6 Limitations

1. **MLH1dn mutagenesis.** Safety claims for PE4/PE5 rest on MLH1dn expression being transient; prolonged or leaky expression risks microsatellite instability and a mutator phenotype — an assumption the delivery modality can violate [4][5].
2. **RT mispriming.** M-MLV RT extends any annealed 3′ OH. Endogenous nicks genome-wide are potential pegRNA-independent priming sites, a hazard with no high-sensitivity assay; current evidence is reassuring but absence-of-evidence [7].
3. **Assay blind spots.** GUIDE-seq is blind to PE2 nicks; short-read WGS misses large structural variants; targeted sequencing only tests nominated sites. No single assay combines genome-wide scope with nick sensitivity.
4. **Cell-type and delivery variance.** Rates measured in HEK293T do not transfer quantitatively to primary cells, zygotes, or *in vivo* delivery, where chromatin context and editor persistence differ [1][3].
5. **PAM and edit-size constraints.** NGG PAM requirement and declining efficiency beyond ~100-bp edits bound the addressable variant space; twinPE extends but does not eliminate this bound [2].
6. **Truncated-guide hazard.** epegRNAs mitigate but do not abolish 3′ degradation; the residual truncated fraction remains a dominant-negative and potential off-target editor [3].

## 7 Conclusion

Prime editing's off-target profile is the product of an architecture: a nickase that cannot cut twice, a guide that must survive to its 3′ end, a polymerase that must be primed, and a flap that must win a resolution race. Each mitigation here — temporal gating of the second nick (PE3b), guide stabilization (epegRNAs), quadratic suppression through paired designs (twinPE), protein re-architecture (PEmax), and MMR modulation (PE4/PE5) — tightens one factor in the specificity product of Theorem 1, and their combination is multiplicative [1][2][3][4]. Validation must keep pace: assays whose detection physics matches nickase biology, applied knowing that a clean GUIDE-seq result for a nickase editor is a statement about the assay, not the editor [6][7]. The path to therapeutic prime editing runs through this discipline — engineer every checkpoint, then measure with instruments that can actually see them.
