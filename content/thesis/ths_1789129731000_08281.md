# Neuromorphic Signal Processing on Intel Loihi 2: the Lava Framework, Programmable Plasticity, and the Energy Economics of Event-Driven Inference

## Abstract

Neuromorphic computing replaces clocked, dense matrix algebra with sparse, asynchronous spike events exchanged between colocalized memory and compute. Intel's Loihi 2 processor — fabricated on a pre-production Intel 4 process node and fielded at billion-neuron scale in the Hala Point system — is the most ambitious empirical test of this thesis to date, and the open-source Lava framework is its programming model. This article gives a technically grounded account of the Loihi 2/Lava stack: the leaky integrate-and-fire dynamics and compartmental neuron models realized in silicon; spike-timing-dependent plasticity and the three-factor learning rules enabled by the chip's programmable learning engine; Lava's communicating-sequential-processes architecture with its Magma compiler and heterogeneous backends; and event-based vision workloads, including sigma–delta neural networks, that exploit temporal sparsity. We quantify energy-per-inference using published measurements — up to 15 TOPS/W at 8-bit precision on Hala Point-class silicon versus roughly 3–6 TOPS/W for contemporary GPUs — and identify precisely the sparsity regimes where the neuromorphic advantage holds, and where it collapses.

## 1 Introduction

The dominant paradigm of deep learning is energetically profligate.

Neuromorphic computing proposes a different contract.

This article is a technical survey of that stack.

1. A precise formulation of the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron model as implemented in Loihi's compartmental neurocores, with executable reference implementations.
2. A treatment of spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) from its neurobiological characterization [5][6] through the three-factor rules Loihi 2's learning engine supports [9].
3. An exposition of the Lava software framework — its process/channel/message-passing architecture, Magma compiler, and hardware backends [4].
4. A quantitative comparison of energy-per-inference between neuromorphic and GPU baselines, grounded in published measurements and explicit about the conditions under which each wins [2][7][9].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From artificial neurons to spiking neurons

A conventional artificial neuron computes a static nonlinear function of a weighted sum, $y = \sigma(\sum_j w_j x_j + b)$. A spiking neuron instead maintains an internal *state* that evolves in continuous time and emits discrete events. The canonical abstraction is the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) model:

$$\tau_m \frac{dv}{dt} = -(v - v_{\mathrm{rest}}) + R\,I(t), \qquad \text{emit spike when } v \ge v_{\mathrm{th}}, \text{ then } v \leftarrow v_{\mathrm{reset}}.$$

The membrane time constant $\tau_m$ sets the memory of the neuron; the leak term implements a soft forgetting of stale input; and the all-or-none spike provides a binary, temporally precise communication primitive. Information can be carried in *rates* (Poisson-like spike counts), in *timing* (time-to-first-spike), or in *population* codes — a strictly richer representational palette than the scalar activations of ANNs, purchased at the cost of nondifferentiability: the spike mechanism has zero gradient almost everywhere, which is why spiking networks historically resisted gradient-based training until the advent of *surrogate gradients* [2].

### 2.2 The neuromorphic hardware landscape

Several architectural families pursue event-driven computation.

The first-generation Loihi integrated 128 neuromorphic cores, three embedded x86 cores for management, and over 33 MB of on-chip SRAM on a 60 mm² die in Intel's 14 nm process, supporting up to 131,072 compartmental neurons and roughly 130 million synapses [1].

Loihi 2, unveiled in 2021 and fabricated on a pre-production Intel 4 process, advanced the design on nearly every axis [8]:

| Feature | Loihi 1 | Loihi 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Process node | Intel 14 nm | Intel 4 (pre-production) |
| Neuromorphic cores | 128 | 128 (redesigned) |
| Max neurons per chip | ~131,000 | ~1,000,000 |
| Spike semantics | Binary spikes | Spikes with integer payloads |
| Neuron model | Microcoded LIF + compartments | Fully programmable, broader model class |
| Learning | Programmable two-factor rules | Three-factor rules, per-synapse third factor |
| Timestep | — | < 200 ns chip-wide; ~5,000× biological rate |
| Speed | Baseline | Up to 10× faster at circuit level |

Two departures from biological fidelity are deliberate.

## 3 Methodology

This survey is constructed from primary sources: Intel Labs publications and technical disclosures [1][2][3][7][8][9], the open-source Lava codebase [4], and the foundational STDP literature [5][6].

**Notation.** We denote presynaptic spike times by $\{t_i^f\}$, postsynaptic spike times by $\{t_j^f\}$, the membrane potential by $v(t)$, the firing threshold by $v_{\mathrm{th}}$, and a synapse's weight by $w_{ij}$. The spike-timing difference for a pre/post pair is $\Delta t = t_{\mathrm{post}} - t_{\mathrm{pre}}$.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $v(t)$ | Membrane potential at time $t$ |
| $\tau_m$ | Membrane leak time constant |
| $v_{\mathrm{th}}$, $v_{\mathrm{reset}}$ | Firing threshold, reset potential |
| $w_{ij}$ | Synaptic weight, neuron $i \to j$ |
| $\Delta t$ | $t_{\mathrm{post}} - t_{\mathrm{pre}}$, spike-timing difference |
| $A_+, A_-, \tau_+, \tau_-$ | STDP potentiation/depression amplitudes and windows |
| $e_{ij}(t)$ | Eligibility trace (three-factor learning) |
| $M(t)$ | Third-factor modulatory signal |

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The leaky integrate-and-fire neuron and its silicon realization

Loihi implements a current-based-synapse (CUBA) variant of LIF with two state variables per compartment: a synaptic response current $u(t)$ (the weighted sum of input spikes plus bias, itself filtered) and the membrane voltage $v(t)$. Discretized at the chip's algorithmic timestep, the update is:

$$u[t+1] = u[t]\cdot(1 - \delta_u) + \sum_j w_j s_j[t] + b, \qquad v[t+1] = v[t]\cdot(1 - \delta_v) + u[t+1],$$

with a spike emitted when $v[t+1] \ge v_{\mathrm{th}}$, followed by reset and an optional refractory period. Compartments assemble into tree-structured dendrites so that a single logical neuron can integrate nonlinear sub-threshold interactions across branches — a meaningful generalization of the point neuron, and one that the Loihi 2 microcode exposes to the programmer rather than hard-wiring [1][8].

The following Python sketch mirrors the discrete update; the Lava-native version is given in §4.3.

```python
import numpy as np

class LIFLayer:
    """Discrete-time CUBA LIF layer (reference model, not hardware code)."""
    def __init__(self, n, du=0.9, dv=0.95, vth=1.0, w=None, b=0.0):
        self.u = np.zeros(n); self.v = np.zeros(n)
        self.du, self.dv, self.vth = du, dv, vth
        self.w = np.random.randn(n, n) * 0.1 if w is None else w
        self.b = b

    def step(self, spikes_in: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
        self.u = self.u * self.du + self.w @ spikes_in + self.b
        self.v = self.v * self.dv + self.u
        spikes = (self.v >= self.vth).astype(float)
        self.v = np.where(spikes > 0, 0.0, self.v)  # reset
        return spikes
```

The same dynamics admit a purely functional reading, which is useful for equational reasoning about network behavior:

```haskell
-- A leaky integrate-and-fire neuron as a Mealy machine.
data Neuron = Neuron { v :: Double, vth :: Double, leak :: Double }

stepLIF :: Neuron -> Double -> (Neuron, Bool)
stepLIF n i =
  let v'   = leak n * v n + i
      fire = v' >= vth n
  in (n { v = if fire then 0.0 else v' }, fire)

-- A layer is a synchronous product of Mealy machines.
stepLayer :: [Neuron] -> [Double] -> ([Neuron], [Bool])
stepLayer ns is = unzip (zipWith stepLIF ns is)
```

Purity buys something concrete here: because `stepLIF` is referentially transparent, the entire network trajectory is a pure function of the input spike train, which makes property-based testing of quantized fixed-point implementations (the kind Loihi actually runs) straightforward — the same input must produce bit-identical outputs in simulation and on silicon.

### 4.2 From two-factor STDP to three-factor learning rules

In 1998, Bi and Poo mapped the dependence of synaptic modification on the precise relative timing of pre- and postsynaptic spikes in cultured hippocampal neurons [5].

$$\Delta w_{ij} = \begin{cases} A_+ e^{-\Delta t / \tau_+} & \Delta t > 0 \ (\text{LTP}) \\ -A_- e^{\Delta t / \tau_-} & \Delta t < 0 \ (\text{LTD}) \end{cases}$$

> **Theorem 1 (Competitive selection under STDP).** Under additive pair-based STDP driven by Poisson spike trains, initially homogeneous synaptic weights develop a *bimodal* stationary distribution: individual synapses are driven toward either the upper or the lower bound. Synapses whose presynaptic activity is *causally correlated* with postsynaptic firing are selectively potentiated, while uncorrelated inputs are depressed, yielding unsupervised feature selection without an explicit objective function [5][6].

This is the sense in which "neurons that fire together, wire together" acquires mathematical teeth: STDP implements a *causal* Hebbian rule, sensitive not merely to correlation but to temporal order. The phenomenon is robust across preparations and has been reviewed extensively [6]; it is also the primitive that Loihi's learning engine was built to execute. The original chip allowed researchers to microcode arbitrary two-factor rules — functions of the pre- and postsynaptic spike trains alone [1]. Loihi 2 extends this to *three-factor* rules: the weight update becomes a product of a local Hebbian term and a modulatory third factor $M(t)$ (reward, error, or surprise signal) mapped precisely to individual synapses [9]:

$$\frac{dw_{ij}}{dt} = M(t) \cdot e_{ij}(t), \qquad \frac{de_{ij}}{dt} = -\frac{e_{ij}}{\tau_e} + f_{\mathrm{Hebb}}(s_i, s_j).$$

The *eligibility trace* $e_{ij}$ accumulates the Hebbian coincidence signal with a decay $\tau_e$, and the third factor gates its consolidation into the weight — the standard mathematical form of reward-modulated STDP and, in the limit, of biologically plausible approximations to backpropagation through time. Intel's Mike Davies has explicitly framed Loihi 2's expanded learning engine as the vehicle for "cutting-edge approximations of backpropagation algorithms for SNNs" [9].

A compact reference implementation of the pair-based rule:

```rust
/// Pair-based STDP weight update. dt = t_post - t_pre (ms).
fn stdp_update(w: f64, dt: f64, a_plus: f64, a_minus: f64,
               tau_plus: f64, tau_minus: f64,
               w_min: f64, w_max: f64) -> f64 {
    let dw = if dt > 0.0 {
        a_plus * (-dt / tau_plus).exp()      // causal: potentiate
    } else {
        -a_minus * (dt / tau_minus).exp()   // acausal: depress
    };
    (w + dw).clamp(w_min, w_max)
}
```

Three properties of on-chip STDP matter for practitioners:

1. **Locality.** The rule uses only information available at the synapse — no global error transport — so it scales without a backward pass.
2. **Continual adaptation.** Because plasticity is always on, the network can track nonstationary statistics, a prerequisite for lifelong learning at the edge.
3. **Stability demands.** Naive STDP is unstable without compensating mechanisms (weight bounds, heterosynaptic competition, homeostasis); the bimodality of Theorem 1 is a feature for selection but a bug for graded representation, which is why Loihi exposes the rule's full parameterization to the programmer.

### 4.3 Lava: a CSP-inspired software stack for heterogeneous neuromorphic execution

Hardware without a programming model is a museum piece.

The architecture has three strata:

- **Process layer.** User-facing abstractions: `LIF` neurons, dense/sparse connectivity, I/O processes, learning-rule processes. A process declares *what* it computes.
- **Process-model layer.** Each process carries implementations for different backends — floating-point CPU simulation, fixed-point CPU, Loihi 1/2 bit-accurate models. A process model declares *how* it computes on a given substrate.
- **Magma (compiler + runtime).** Lowers the process graph onto the chosen execution platform, mapping channels to shared memory, NoC messages, or inter-chip links as appropriate [4].

A minimal Lava program instantiates a recurrent LIF population, drives it with an event source, and executes it on the Loihi 2 simulator backend:

```python
import numpy as np
from lava.proc.lif.process import LIF
from lava.proc.io.source import RingBuffer
from lava.proc.monitor.process import Monitor
from lava.magma.core.run_configs import Loihi2SimCfg
from lava.magma.core.run_conditions import RunSteps

# 128 LIF neurons, recurrent excitation, Poisson-ish drive
neurons = LIF(shape=(128,), du=0.9, dv=0.95, vth=10.0,
              bias_mant=2.0, bias_exp=6)
drive = RingBuffer(data=np.random.poisson(0.05, (200, 128)))
drive.s_out.connect(neurons.a_in)

mon = Monitor()
mon.probe(neurons.s_out, num_steps=200)

neurons.run(RunSteps(num_steps=200),
            run_cfg=Loihi2SimCfg(select_tag="floating_pt"))
spikes = mon.get_data()["s_out"]
neurons.stop()
print("mean firing rate:", spikes.mean())
```

The channel contract that makes this composition sound can be stated as a safety invariant.

```tla
---------------------------- MODULE LavaChannel ----------------------------
EXTENDS Integers, Naturals, Sequences
CONSTANT Capacity, Producers, Consumers
VARIABLES buf, phase
TypeOK == /\ buf \in Seq(Nat)
          /\ Len(buf) <= Capacity
          /\ phase \in {"idle", "send", "recv"}
Safety == [][
    /\ TypeOK
    /\ (phase = "send" => Len(buf) < Capacity)
    /\ (phase = "recv" => Len(buf) > 0)
  ]_<<buf, phase>>
=============================================================================
```

*The invariant says:* a send is enabled only when the channel has capacity, a receive only when it is nonempty, and the buffer length never exceeds capacity. Model-checking this against the Magma runtime's channel implementation is what lets Lava compose thousands of processes without deadlock or message loss — the CSP discipline made executable.

Lava's ecosystem extends beyond the core: `lava-lib-dl` provides SLAYER-based gradient training of deep SNNs for deployment to Loihi, `lava-lib-optimization` maps QUBO and constraint-satisfaction problems onto neurodynamical solvers, and `lava-lib-dnf` builds dynamic neural fields [4].

### 4.4 Event-based vision and sigma–delta neural networks on Loihi 2

The workload class that best matches neuromorphic hardware is *event-based vision*. A dynamic vision sensor (DVS) does not produce frames; each pixel independently emits an event $(x, y, t, p)$ when its log-intensity changes by more than a threshold, with polarity $p \in \{+1,-1\}$ indicating the direction of change. The output is a sparse, asynchronous spatiotemporal point process — precisely the native language of a spiking chip, and a terrible fit for a GPU that wants dense batched tensors [3].

On Loihi, this pipeline has been demonstrated end to end: gesture recognition with on-chip few-shot learning, optic-flow-based landing control for micro air vehicles, and event-driven automotive perception (CarSNN), all surveyed in [2].

$$e[t] = \mathcal{Q}\big(x[t] - \hat{x}[t-1]\big), \qquad \hat{x}[t] = \hat{x}[t-1] + e[t].$$

When the input is temporally redundant — as video overwhelmingly is — the event rate collapses while the computed function is preserved up to the quantization error.

| Workload | Mapping | Reported character |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse coding (LASSO) | Loihi 1 vs. CPU/GPU | >5,000× better energy–delay product [1] |
| Gesture recognition | On-chip few-shot STDP, Loihi | Milliwatt-class, online adaptation [2] |
| Optic-flow MAV landing | Spiking PID on Loihi | Closed-loop neuromorphic control [2] |
| Event-based driving (CarSNN) | SNN on Loihi | Competitive accuracy, sparse compute [2] |
| Signal processing / SDNN | Loihi 2 | DNN accuracy, event-driven efficiency [3] |

The pattern is consistent: wherever the *input* is sparse in time and the *computation* can be kept local, the neuromorphic mapping wins decisively.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

### 5.1 What the measurements say

Three classes of published evidence bear on the energy question:

1. **Kernel-level.** On a large convolutional sparse-coding problem, pre-silicon Loihi benchmarks demonstrated over three orders of magnitude better energy–delay product than conventional solutions [1]. Sparse coding is the ideal case — iterative, recurrent, sparse — and should be read as an upper bound on the advantage, not a typical one.
2. **Application-level.** The Davies et al. survey catalogues Loihi results across deep-learning inference, attractor networks, and combinatorial optimization, finding consistent efficiency advantages on event-driven and recurrent workloads, with the magnitude strongly workload-dependent [2].
3. **System-level.** Hala Point packages 1,152 Loihi 2 chips in a 6U chassis: 1.15 billion neurons, 128 billion synapses, 140,544 neuromorphic cores, 2,300 embedded x86 cores, 16 PB/s of memory bandwidth, 3.5 PB/s of inter-core communication bandwidth, all within a 2,600 W envelope [7]. Intel reports deep-learning inference efficiencies up to **15 TOPS/W at 8-bit precision** on this class of silicon, against roughly **6 TOPS/W (INT8)** for NVIDIA's Blackwell GB200 NVL72 and **3.1 TOPS/W** for DGX H100 systems [9].

> **Theorem 2 (Event-driven energy scaling, informal).** For a spiking layer with $N$ synapses, mean firing rate $f$ spikes/neuron/s, and energy $E_{\mathrm{spike}}$ per synaptic event, the dynamic energy per inference scales as $\Theta(f \cdot N \cdot E_{\mathrm{spike}})$, *independent of the layer's nominal size*. The neuromorphic advantage over a dense accelerator is therefore proportional to the *sparsity factor* $1/f$: it is maximal for rare-event processing and vanishes as $f \to 1$ (every neuron firing every timestep), at which point the dense engine's superior arithmetic efficiency dominates.

This is the central quantitative insight of the field, and it explains both the spectacular wins and the disappointing draws in the literature.

### 5.2 The Hala Point scale argument

Hala Point's headline numbers deserve careful reading [7]:

| Quantity | Hala Point |
|---|---|
| Loihi 2 processors | 1,152 |
| Neurons | 1.15 × 10⁹ |
| Synapses | 128 × 10⁹ |
| Neuromorphic cores | 140,544 |
| Embedded x86 cores | ~2,300 |
| Peak power | 2,600 W |
| Memory bandwidth | 16 PB/s |
| Synaptic operations | >380 × 10¹² 8-bit synops/s |
| Neuron operations | >240 × 10¹² /s |

At full capacity the system can execute its 1.15 billion neurons roughly 20× faster than biological real time — a neuron count Intel compares to an owl's brain or a capuchin monkey's cortex [7].

## 6 Limitations

A clear-eyed assessment must register where the paradigm currently fails:

1. **Training remains the hard problem.** Surrogate-gradient methods train SNNs off-chip and deploy them; on-chip plasticity handles adaptation, not from-scratch learning of deep hierarchies. The three-factor machinery of Loihi 2 is a substrate for *research into* biologically plausible credit assignment, not a solved training pipeline [2][9].
2. **The software bottleneck.** The quote that best summarizes the field's status comes from Davies: the software stack — not the silicon — is the limiting factor, and there is little point building larger machines until programming models catch up. Lava is the serious attempt to close this gap, but it remains a research framework, not a production toolchain [4][7].
3. **Access and openness.** Loihi hardware is available only through the INRC; there is no commercial part, no cloud SKU, no PyTorch-level onboarding ramp. This throttles independent replication of energy claims.
4. **Precision and encoding overhead.** Fixed-point weights, quantized states, and the cost of *converting* dense inputs into spike trains can erase the theoretical advantage. SDNNs mitigate this for video-like signals [3], but for static images the encoding tax is real.
5. **Benchmarking fairness.** Comparing a sparse event-driven chip against a dense GPU on the GPU's home turf (batched ImageNet) measures the wrong thing; comparing on the neuromorphic chip's home turf (sparse streaming) measures the right thing but flatters it. Honest evaluation requires *workload-matched* metrics — energy per *task-relevant* inference under realistic duty cycles — which the field is still standardizing [2].

## 7 Conclusion

Intel's Loihi 2 and the Lava framework constitute the most complete realization of the neuromorphic program to date: programmable spiking dynamics with integer-payload events, a learning engine that spans two- and three-factor plasticity rules, a CSP-based software stack with a real compiler, and a demonstrated path from milliwatt edge inference to the billion-neuron Hala Point system. The energy economics are now legible: up to 15 TOPS/W at 8-bit precision on suitable workloads, against 3–6 TOPS/W for contemporary GPUs, with the advantage governed by a single parameter — temporal sparsity.

The open problems are commensurately clear. *Learning*: can three-factor rules, eligibility traces, and surrogate gradients converge into a training methodology that rivals backpropagation on hard tasks? *Software*: can Lava (or its successors) make event-driven programming as productive as tensor programming? *Scale*: does the efficiency advantage survive the journey from 1,152 chips to wafer-scale systems, or do communication costs reassert the von Neumann tax in new form? The silicon has answered what it can answer. The rest is algorithms — which is to say, the rest is the actual research.
