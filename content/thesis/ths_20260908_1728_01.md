# Polar Codes and Channel Polarization: Arikan's Construction, Successive Cancellation Decoding, and Finite-Length Scaling Laws

## Abstract

This thesis gives a rigorous treatment of polar codes, the first provably capacity-achieving coding family with explicit construction and *O*(*N* log *N*) encoding and decoding, introduced by Erdal Arıkan in 2009 [1]. We develop *channel polarization*: recursive combining and splitting of *N* copies of a binary-input discrete memoryless channel *W* into synthetic bit-channels *W*<sub>*N*</sub><sup>(*i*)</sup> whose symmetric capacities converge to 0 or 1, the fraction of noiseless channels tending to *I*(*W*). We analyze the successive cancellation (SC) decoder with its *O*(2<sup>−√*N*</sup>) error decay, the successive cancellation list (SCL) decoder of Tal and Vardy [5] behind 5G NR control channels, and the finite-length *scaling exponent* μ with *I*(*W*) − *R* ∼ *N*<sup>−1/μ</sup> — from the Hassani–Alishahi–Urbanke bounds 3.55 ≤ μ ≤ 7 [3] and the Mondelli–Hassani–Urbanke refinement μ ≤ 4.714 [4] to large-kernel constructions attaining the optimum μ = 2 [6].

---

## 1 Introduction

Shannon's 1948 theorem asserted the existence of capacity-approaching codes, but *non-constructively*. For sixty years the field chased this gap: turbo codes (1993) and LDPC codes achieved near-capacity performance under iterative decoding, yet their guarantees rest on *ensemble* averages and density-evolution thresholds — no explicit family carried a clean finite-length capacity proof with a fixed efficient decoder.

In 2009, Arıkan broke this pattern with **channel polarization** [1]: a deterministic recursion transforming *N* = 2<sup>*n*</sup> uses of any symmetric B-DMC *W* into *N* synthetic channels that *polarize* — each becoming essentially noiseless or essentially useless — with the good fraction converging exactly to *I*(*W*). Information flows through the good channels, frozen bits occupy the bad ones, and the **successive cancellation (SC) decoder** recovers everything in *O*(*N* log *N*) time with block-error probability *O*(2<sup>−√*N*</sup>).

> The conceptual shock of polarization is that capacity is achieved *not* by smoothing errors through long random mixing, but by *sorting* randomness: a self-similar recursion concentrates the channel's entropy onto a vanishing subset of coordinates, leaving the rest pristine.

We proceed as follows: §2 reviews B-DMCs, symmetric capacity, the Bhattacharyya parameter, and the kernel transform; §3 gives the recursive construction, the SC decoder, and a reference Python implementation of the BEC recursion; §4 dives into the polarization martingale, error exponents and kernel bounds [2], SCL and CRC-aided decoding [5], and finite-length scaling [3, 4, 6]; §5 states scaling results formally; §6 discusses limitations; §7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Binary-Input Discrete Memoryless Channels

A **B-DMC** *W*: {0, 1} → 𝒴 has **symmetric capacity** *I*(*W*), the mutual information under uniform inputs. The **Bhattacharyya parameter**

$$Z(W) = \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} \sqrt{W(y|0)\, W(y|1)}$$

measures unreliability: *Z*(*W*) ≈ 0 for a noiseless channel, ≈ 1 for a useless one, and single-bit ML error probability is bounded by *Z*(*W*). The two parameters sandwich each other: *I*(*W*)<sup>2</sup> + *Z*(*W*)<sup>2</sup> ≤ 1 and *I*(*W*) + *Z*(*W*) ≥ 1. A channel is **symmetric** if a permutation π of 𝒴 with π<sup>−1</sup> = π satisfies *W*(*y*|1) = *W*(π(*y*)|0); for symmetric channels uniform input achieves capacity and the SC analysis simplifies greatly.

### 2.2 The Polarizing Kernel

The atomic step combines two copies of *W* via the kernel *F* = [[1,0],[1,1]] and the map (*u*<sub>1</sub>, *u*<sub>2</sub>) ↦ (*u*<sub>1</sub> ⊕ *u*<sub>2</sub>, *u*<sub>2</sub>), synthesizing a *worse* channel *W*<sup>−</sup> and a *better* channel *W*<sup>+</sup>:

$$W^-(y_1, y_2 | u_1) = \sum_{u_2} \tfrac{1}{2}\, W(y_1 | u_1 \oplus u_2)\, W(y_2 | u_2),$$

$$W^+(y_1, y_2, u_1 | u_2) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, W(y_1 | u_1 \oplus u_2)\, W(y_2 | u_2).$$

The fundamental identities: **capacity conservation** *I*(*W*<sup>+</sup>) + *I*(*W*<sup>−</sup>) = 2*I*(*W*); **polarization step** *I*(*W*<sup>−</sup>) ≤ *I*(*W*) ≤ *I*(*W*<sup>+</sup>), strict unless *I*(*W*) ∈ {0, 1}; and **Bhattacharyya evolution** *Z*(*W*<sup>+</sup>) = *Z*(*W*)<sup>2</sup>, *Z*(*W*<sup>−</sup>) ≤ 2*Z*(*W*) − *Z*(*W*)<sup>2</sup>.

The **full polar transform** recurses *n* times: with *N* = 2<sup>*n*</sup> and bit-reversal *B*<sub>*N*</sub>, *G*<sub>*N*</sub> = *B*<sub>*N*</sub> *F*<sup>⊗*n*</sup> yields *N* bit-channels *W*<sub>*N*</sub><sup>(*i*)</sup>. For BEC(ε) the recursion closes in scalar form — ε<sup>−</sup> = 2ε − ε<sup>2</sup>, ε<sup>+</sup> = ε<sup>2</sup> — making the BEC the canonical laboratory for polarization.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The Recursive Construction

Three phases: **(i)** compute reliabilities of the *N* synthetic channels; **(ii)** select the *K* = *NR* most reliable indices as the information set 𝒜 (the complement 𝒜<sup>c</sup> is *frozen* to decoder-known values); **(iii)** encode **x** = **u** *G*<sub>*N*</sub>. For general BMS channels step (i) needs density evolution or the Tal–Vardy quantization construction; for the BEC it is an exact scalar recursion:

```python
import numpy as np

def bec_polarize(eps: float, n: int) -> np.ndarray:
    """Bhattacharyya (erasure) parameters of the N = 2**n bit-channels
    W_N^{(i)} for transmission over BEC(eps). Exact scalar recursion."""
    z = np.array([eps], dtype=float)
    for _ in range(n):
        z = np.concatenate([2 * z - z * z,   # W^- : worse branch
                            z * z])          # W^+ : better branch
    return z  # length N; bit-reversed index order

def polar_info_set(eps: float, n: int, rate: float):
    """Return the K = N*rate most reliable bit-channel indices."""
    N = 2 ** n
    K = int(N * rate)
    z = bec_polarize(eps, n)
    info = np.argsort(z)[:K]      # smallest Z <=> most reliable
    return np.sort(info), z

if __name__ == "__main__":
    info, z = polar_info_set(eps=0.5, n=10, rate=0.5)
    # Fraction of channels with Z < 1e-3 should approach I(W) = 0.5
    print("N =", 1024, "| good fraction ~", np.mean(z < 1e-3))
```

With *n* = 10, ε = 0.5 the histogram of {*Z*(*W*<sub>*N*</sub><sup>(*i*)</sup>)} is *bimodal* — mass accumulating near 0 and near 1, the mass near 0 tending to *I*(*W*) = 1 − ε as *n* → ∞ [1].

### 3.2 Successive Cancellation Decoding

The SC decoder estimates *û*<sub>1</sub>, …, *û*<sub>*N*</sub> sequentially: frozen indices take known values; information indices are decided by thresholding the likelihood ratio of *W*<sub>*N*</sub><sup>(*i*)</sup> given **y** and previous decisions. Likelihoods propagate through the factor graph of *F*<sup>⊗*n*</sup> via the **f-function** (for *W*<sup>−</sup>), *f*(*a*, *b*) = (1 + *ab*)/(*a* + *b*), and the **g-function** (for *W*<sup>+</sup>), *g*(*a*, *b*, *û*) = *a*<sup>1−2*û*</sup>*b*, in a depth-first traversal. Complexity is *O*(*N* log *N*) time and *O*(*N*) memory, and the union bound

$$P_e \;\le\; \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} Z\!\left(W_N^{(i)}\right)$$

yields block-error probability *O*(2<sup>−√*N*</sup>) for any fixed *R* < *I*(*W*) [1].

### 3.3 Research Method

Our exposition follows the primary literature: Arıkan's construction and SC analysis [1]; the exponent characterization and kernel bounds of Korada–Şaşoğlu–Urbanke [2]; the finite-length scaling program of Hassani–Alishahi–Urbanke [3] and its unification by Mondelli–Hassani–Urbanke [4]; the list-decoding breakthrough of Tal–Vardy [5]; and the optimal-scaling large-kernel results [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Polarization Martingale and the Rate of Polarization

Let *B*<sub>1</sub>, *B*<sub>2</sub>, … be i.i.d. fair coins tracking the recursion path, and define *Z*<sub>0</sub> = *Z*(*W*) with

$$Z_{n+1} = \begin{cases} Z_n^2, & B_{n+1} = 1, \\\\ \text{in } [Z_n\sqrt{2 - Z_n^2},\, 2Z_n - Z_n^2], & B_{n+1} = 0. \end{cases}$$

Arıkan's key observation [1]: the *capacity process* *I*<sub>*n*</sub> = *I*(*W*<sub>*n*</sub>) is a bounded **martingale**, while *Z*<sub>*n*</sub> is a **supermartingale**. Bounded martingales converge a.s.; the only fixed points consistent with *I*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>2</sup> + *Z*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>2</sup> ≤ 1 are (0, 1) and (1, 0). Hence *I*<sub>*n*</sub> → *I*<sub>∞</sub> ∈ {0, 1} a.s. with *E*[*I*<sub>∞</sub>] = *I*(*W*) — the **polarization theorem** — and the un-polarized mass vanishes:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{2^n}\, \#\{i : I(W_N^{(i)}) \in (\delta, 1-\delta)\} = 0 \quad \forall\,\delta > 0.$$

Arıkan and Telatar [7] quantified the *rate*: for any β < 1/2, *P*<sub>*e*</sub> = *o*(2<sup>−*N*<sup>β</sup></sup>) at fixed *R* < *I*(*W*). The exponent 1/2 is intrinsic to the 2 × 2 kernel: the good branch squares *Z*, so after *n* levels the best channels satisfy *Z* ≈ *Z*(*W*)<sup>2<sup>*k*</sup></sup> with *k* ≈ *n*/2 good turns — double-exponential in √*N*. This is the *error-exponent regime* (*R*, *W* fixed, *N* → ∞).

### 4.2 Error Exponents and Kernel Bounds

Can a different kernel beat 1/2? Korada, Şaşoğlu, and Urbanke [2] characterized an ℓ × ℓ kernel *G* by its **partial distances** *D*<sub>*i*</sub> (the Hamming distance of row *i* to the span of rows below it), with error exponent

$$E(G) = \frac{1}{\ell} \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \log_\ell D_i,$$

governing *P*<sub>*e*</sub> ≈ 2<sup>−*N*<sup>*E*(*G*)</sup></sup>. They proved *any* ℓ × ℓ kernel avoiding upper-triangular column permutations polarizes, and — strikingly — that **no kernel with ℓ < 15 achieves *E*(*G*) > 1/2**: the Arıkan kernel is exponent-optimal among all small kernels. Yet BCH-based constructions yield size-16 kernels with *E*(*G*) > 1/2, and as ℓ → ∞ exponents arbitrarily close to 1 are attainable [2] — asymptotically matching random-coding exponents while retaining explicit construction.

### 4.3 Successive Cancellation List Decoding and the 5G Standard

Plain SC makes *hard, greedy* decisions; one early error propagates catastrophically, which is why polar codes underperform LDPC codes at short block lengths despite asymptotic optimality. Tal and Vardy [5] generalized SC to **SCL decoding**: at each information bit every surviving path *splits* in two, and pruning retains the *L* most likely paths. Naively Ω(*L*·*N*<sup>2</sup>), but lazy sharing of intermediate likelihoods across paths achieves *O*(*L*·*N* log *N*) time and *O*(*L*·*N*) space [5]. Even moderate *L* (e.g., 32) brings a (2048, 1024) polar code within a hair of the **maximum-likelihood** bound, and a genie-aided variant matches state-of-the-art LDPC codes.

The decisive refinement is **CRC-aided SCL (CA-SCL)**: an outer CRC selects the correct codeword from the final list, implementing the "helpful genie." 3GPP adopted this for the **5G NR control channels** (PDCCH, PUCCH, PBCH) in TS 38.212 [7] — a rare direct leap from deep theory, barely a decade old, into a global wireless standard.

### 4.4 Finite-Length Scaling: The Exponent μ

Engineering fixes *P*<sub>*e*</sub> and asks how large *N* must be at rate *R*. The answer is the **scaling law** *N* ≍ α/(*I*(*W*) − *R*)<sup>μ</sup>. Hassani, Alishahi, and Urbanke [3] proved

$$\frac{\alpha}{(I(W)-R)^{\underline{\mu}}} \le N \le \frac{\beta}{(I(W)-R)^{\bar{\mu}}}$$

with μ = 3.55, μ̄ = 7 (BEC heuristic μ ≈ 3.627). Mondelli, Hassani, and Urbanke [4] unified the error-exponent, scaling-exponent, and moderate-deviations regimes, tightening the universal bound to **μ̄ = 4.714** and the BEC bound to ≈ 3.639 — essentially the heuristic 3.627 — with universal lower bound μ ≥ 3.579. They also proved polar codes suffer **no error floors**: varying the channel around a fixed code cannot flatten the error curve, unlike some LDPC ensembles [4].

### 4.5 Large Kernels and Optimal Scaling

The 2 × 2 kernel's μ ≈ 3.627 is far from the information-theoretic optimum μ = 2 of random codes. Fazeli, Hassani, Mondelli, and Vardy [6] closed this gap with **large polarization kernels**: binary linear codes achieving *optimal* scaling *N* ≲ (*I*(*W*) − *R*)<sup>−2−ε</sup> on the BEC with quasilinear *O*(*N* log *N*) complexity. Polarization thus matches random coding in *both* the error-exponent and scaling-exponent senses while retaining explicit construction and low-complexity decoding — a combination no other code family achieves simultaneously.

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Scaling Guarantees

### 5.1 Scaling Exponents Across Channel Families

Consolidated rigorous bounds on μ, where *N* ≍ (*I*(*W*) − *R*)<sup>−μ</sup> at fixed error probability [3, 4, 6]:

| Channel / construction | μ lower bound | μ upper bound | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| BEC, 2×2 kernel | 3.627 (heuristic, numerically exact) | 3.639 (rigorous) | [3], [4] |
| Arbitrary BMS, 2×2 kernel | 3.579 | 4.714 | [4] |
| Arbitrary BMS, 2×2 kernel (earlier) | 3.55 | 7 | [3] |
| BEC, large kernels | 2 (optimal) | 2 + ε (any ε > 0) | [6] |
| Random codes (benchmark) | 2 | 2 | classical |

The gap between the BEC value 3.627 and the universal bound 4.714 is the price of channel universality: worst-case BMS polarization dynamics are strictly slower than on the erasure channel, where the Bhattacharyya recursion is an exact scalar map.

### 5.2 Formal Theorem

> Theorem: (Finite-length scaling upper bound; Hassani–Alishahi–Urbanke [3], refined by Mondelli–Hassani–Urbanke [4].) Let *W* be a binary-input memoryless symmetric channel with capacity *I*(*W*). Fix *P*<sub>e</sub> > 0. Then there exist constants α, β > 0, depending only on *P*<sub>e</sub> and *W*, such that for every *R* < *I*(*W*) a polar code of rate *R* and block length *N* with SC error probability ≤ *P*<sub>e</sub> exists, provided
>
> $$\frac{\alpha}{(I(W) - R)^{\underline{\mu}}} \;\le\; N \;\le\; \frac{\beta}{(I(W) - R)^{\bar{\mu}}},$$
>
> with μ = 3.579 and μ̄ = 4.714 uniformly over all BMS *W* [4]. On the BEC the upper exponent tightens to μ̄ ≈ 3.639, essentially the heuristic 3.627 [4]. With large kernels the upper exponent can be driven to 2 + ε for any ε > 0 on the BEC — information-theoretically optimal [6].

### 5.3 Decoder Performance Landscape

The empirical hierarchy is well documented [5]:

1. **SC** (*L* = 1): capacity-achieving but ~1.5–2 dB from the finite-length optimum at *N* = 1024–2048.
2. **SCL** (*L* = 8–32): recovers most of the ML gap; at *L* = 32 the (2048, 1024) word-error rate nearly coincides with the ML bound [5].
3. **CA-SCL** (*L* = 8, CRC-16/24): the 5G NR configuration, outperforming comparable LDPC codes in short-block control signaling [5, 7].

## 6 Limitations

- **Finite-length suboptimality of the 2 × 2 kernel.** At *N* ≤ 1024 with plain SC decoding, polar codes trail LDPC/turbo codes by 1–2 dB; μ ≈ 3.627 means halving the gap to capacity needs ~12× the block length — mitigated only by large kernels [3, 6].
- **Channel-dependent construction.** The information set 𝒜 depends on *W*; the original construction is *not universal*, motivating universal polar schemes that pay with larger block lengths [7].
- **Serial decoding latency.** SC/SCL decide bit *i* only after bits 1…*i*−1, a sequential bottleneck versus parallel belief propagation (partially eased by simplified-SC node pruning).
- **Asymmetric and *q*-ary channels.** Extensions exist (Honda–Yamamoto; Şaşoğlu–Telatar–Arıkan; Park–Barg) but complicate construction and frozen-bit design.
- **Large-kernel constants.** Optimal scaling is asymptotic; required kernel sizes and hidden constants are currently impractical — the engineering sweet spot remains the 2 × 2 kernel with CA-SCL [5, 6].
- **Finite list limitation.** No finite list size improves the *scaling exponent* under MAP decoding [4].

## 7 Conclusion

Channel polarization is one of the deepest ideas in modern coding theory: a deterministic recursion that *sorts* a channel's randomness into perfect and useless synthetic channels, achieving capacity with *O*(*N* log *N*) complexity and an explicit construction [1]. SC decoding and Tal–Vardy's list generalization [5] carried the theory into practice — most visibly the 5G NR control plane [7]. The scaling program [3], unified and sharpened in [4], replaced asymptotic hand-waving with quantitative laws *N* ≍ (*I*(*W*) − *R*)<sup>−μ</sup>; large kernels [6] then drove μ to its optimum of 2. Open frontiers: practical universal constructions with small constants, low-latency parallelized list decoders, and polarization in quantum and network settings. Polarization began as a proof technique; it has become a design principle.
