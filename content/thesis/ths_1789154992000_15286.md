# Hardware Transactional Memory at the ISA Boundary: Intel TSX and IBM POWER8 TM, Hybrid Fallback Paths, Abort Taxonomies, and Verification under the C++ Memory Model

## Abstract

Hardware transactional memory (HTM) brought the Herlihy–Moss transactional abstraction into shipping silicon: Intel's Transactional Synchronization Extensions (TSX), with Hardware Lock Elision (HLE) and Restricted Transactional Memory (RTM) [2], and IBM's POWER8 transactional-memory facility built on `tbegin.`/`tend.`/`tabort.` with suspend/resume support [5]. Both are *best-effort* systems: they abort transactions for data conflicts, capacity overflow, interrupts, and privileged operations, guaranteeing progress only through programmer-supplied fallback paths. This thesis compares the two instruction sets, formalizes the abort taxonomy that governs retry-versus-fallback policy, develops hybrid TM (HyTM) fallback tiers with explicit contention management, and reduces TM correctness to verification conditions over the C++ memory model. We prove that retrying capacity aborts is strictly wasteful, that fallback serializability follows from a lock-word protocol across hardware, software, and lock tiers, and that abort-cause misclassification costs performance but never correctness given a sound fallback. A TLA⁺ HyTM controller specification is model-checked, synthetic experiments reproduce the asymmetric Haswell/POWER8 capacity budgets, and we close with the hard limits: fixed capacity walls, the TSX microcode disablement [7], and the RTL-to-memory-model verification gap.

## 1. Introduction

Transactional memory was introduced by Herlihy and Moss as architectural support for lock-free data structures: extend load-linked/store-conditional so a processor may update a collection of independently chosen memory words atomically, implemented by modest extensions to any cache-coherence protocol [1]. The idea waited nearly two decades for commercial silicon while software transactional memory carried the research agenda. Between 2011 and 2014 it returned: IBM Blue Gene/Q (2011), IBM zEC12 (2012) with *constrained transactions* guaranteed to eventually commit [8], Intel Haswell with TSX (2013), and IBM POWER8 (2014) with a full TM facility in the Power ISA [5].

This thesis studies the *instruction-set boundary* of that revival — what hardware promises, what it merely attempts, and how software is organized around the attempt — along three themes:

1. **ISA comparison.** TSX's HLE is a legacy-compatible *hint* whose retry policy is baked into hardware; RTM is flexible but forces the programmer to write the fallback. POWER8 exposes richer failure classification plus *suspend/resume* semantics enabling qualitatively different system software [2][5].
2. **Abort taxonomies and hybrid fallback.** Best-effort HTM aborts for many reasons, and the optimal response differs per cause: capacity aborts are *persistent* (retry is futile), conflict aborts *transient* (bounded retry pays). Hybrid TM — hardware fast path, software slow path, lock-based fallback of last resort — is the engineering answer, but only with disciplined contention management.
3. **Verification under the C++ memory model.** The C++ TM Technical Specification (N4513) defines transaction semantics against the C++11 memory model [3]. We formalize what correctness means for best-effort HTM with a lock fallback: committed transactions must appear atomic at serialization points, aborted ones must not leak observable effects (*opacity*), and non-transactional accesses remain governed by happens-before and coherence.

Section 2 surveys the ISAs; Section 3 the methodology; Section 4 the four deep dives; Section 5 results and proofs; Sections 6–7 limitations and conclusions.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The best-effort contract

All commercial HTM is *best-effort*: hardware attempts transactional execution and may fail for implementation-defined reasons, with no guarantee that any transaction ever commits in hardware. Progress is software's responsibility. This departs fundamentally from the Herlihy–Moss vision, where TM *is* the synchronization primitive. In commercial HTM, TM is an *optimization* layered over a conventional fallback — usually a lock. Hence: every transactional region needs a correct non-transactional alternate path; abort causes must be *classified* (transient vs. persistent) to choose retry vs. fallback; and the fallback path must coordinate with in-flight hardware transactions.

### 2.2 Intel TSX: HLE and RTM

Intel TSX [2] provides two interfaces. *Hardware Lock Elision* (HLE) reuses the `REPNE`/`REPE` opcode space as `XACQUIRE`/`XRELEASE` prefixes on lock-prefixed read-modify-write instructions. On legacy hardware the prefixes are ignored (binary compatibility); on TSX hardware the lock write is elided, the critical section executes transactionally, and on abort execution restarts from the `XACQUIRE` instruction as an ordinary lock acquire. Retry policy is entirely hardware-defined.

*Restricted Transactional Memory* (RTM) is the flexible interface: `XBEGIN` (with fallback-address operand), `XEND`, `XABORT` (8-bit immediate delivered to software), and `XTEST`. On abort, control transfers to the fallback address with abort status in `EAX`; the programmer owns retry policy, fallback, and the correctness argument. RTM raises `#UD` where unsupported (`CPUID.07H.EBX.RTM`, bit 11).

### 2.3 IBM POWER8 TM

Power ISA v2.07 (2013) added TM: `tbegin.` (the `.` sets CR0 with the outcome), `tend.`, `tabort.`, plus `tsuspend.`/`tresume.` for suspending a transaction around system calls or lock acquisition, and `trechkpt.`/`treclaim.` for kernel context-switch support [5]. On failure, the TFHAR directs control to the software handler with a failure code distinguishing *transient* from *persistent* failures. POWER8 tracks footprints in 64-entry content-addressable TM directories (TMCAM) at L2/L3: 8 KB read/write capacity per thread, 128-byte conflict granularity, versus Haswell's 64-byte lines and ~4 MB read / ~22 KB write budgets [9].

| Feature | Intel TSX (Haswell) | IBM POWER8 TM |
|---|---|---|
| Begin / end | `XBEGIN rel32`, `XEND` | `tbegin.`, `tend.` |
| Explicit abort | `XABORT imm8` | `tabort.` |
| Lock elision | HLE prefixes (legacy-compatible) | Explicit `tbegin.` around lock region |
| Suspend / resume | Not supported | `tsuspend.` / `tresume.` |
| Conflict granularity | 64-byte cache line | 128-byte |
| Read / write capacity | ~4 MB / ~22 KB | 8 KB / 8 KB |
| Failure classification | `EAX` abort status bits | Transient/persistent codes + TFHAR |
| Nesting | Flat (depth counter) | Nested; inner failure fails outer |

### 2.4 The C++ TM Technical Specification

The WG21 TM TS (N4513, published as ISO/IEC TS 19841) [3] adds `transaction_safe` annotations and `__transaction_atomic` / `__transaction_relaxed` blocks. Key decisions:

- Transactions are *atomic blocks*: a committed `__transaction_atomic` block's effects appear atomic, with sequentially-consistent ordering at transaction boundaries.
- *Unsafe* operations (I/O, certain atomics, volatile access) inside atomic transactions are ill-formed or implementation-defined; relaxed transactions permit them with weaker guarantees.
- The TS is implementation-agnostic — realizable over HTM, STM, or HyTM — which is why best-effort fallback discipline matters: the language guarantee must hold even when the hardware path is abandoned.

## 3. Methodology

1. **Documentary ISA analysis.** Reconstructed programmer-visible contracts from Intel's TSX documentation [2], IBM's AIX TM documentation [5], the zEC12 transactional-execution paper [8], and the TM TS draft [3], cross-checking abort-cause enumerations across vendors.
2. **Synthetic capacity probing.** A Python harness generates parameterized read/write footprints and measures commit probability against capacity models calibrated to published Haswell and POWER8 budgets, reproducing read/write capacity asymmetry without physical access to both machines.
3. **Formal modeling.** A TLA⁺ specification of a HyTM controller (Section 5.3) captures the hardware-fast-path / software-fallback / global-lock protocol; the opacity-relevant safety invariant is model-checked for bounded configurations.
4. **Proof sketches.** Correctness of the canonical lock-fallback discipline is reduced to a serialization argument (Section 5.4), identifying exactly which abort-cause misclassifications would break it.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Intel TSX: lock elision versus best-effort HTM



**HLE: elision as hint.** Under HLE the lock is never written transactionally; the processor adds the lock's cache line to the transaction's *read set* and suppresses the store, so another thread acquiring the lock aborts every eliding transaction via read-set invalidation. *Elided critical sections are thus mutually atomic with lock-holding sections.* HLE is pure *lock elision* — it cannot express a transaction that was not already a lock-protected region — and its retry policy is fixed in microcode: Haswell retries a small fixed number of times [6], optimal for transient conflicts but pathological for persistent capacity aborts.

**RTM: best-effort HTM.** RTM drops the lock. The canonical pattern:

```rust
fn elided(lock: &AtomicBool, retries: &mut u32) {          // RTM elision skeleton
    loop {
        let status: u32;
        unsafe { core::arch::asm!("xbegin 1f", "jmp 2f", "1:",
                     out("eax") status, options(nostack)); }
        if status == u32::MAX {                            // _XBEGIN_STARTED
            if lock.load(Ordering::Relaxed) {
                unsafe { core::arch::asm!("xabort $0xff") }; }
            critical_section_body();
            unsafe { core::arch::asm!("xend") };
            return;
        }
        *retries += 1;
        if should_retry(status, *retries) { continue; }
        lock.store(true, Ordering::SeqCst);                 // fallback: real lock
        critical_section_body();
        lock.store(false, Ordering::SeqCst);
        return;
    }
}
```

The `EAX` abort status *is* the visible abort taxonomy: bit 0 explicit `XABORT`, bit 1 retry-possible, bit 2 data conflict, bit 3 capacity overflow, bit 4 debug, bit 5 nested abort, bits 24–31 the `XABORT` immediate:

| Abort class | `EAX` | Persistence | Correct response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit `XABORT` | bit 0 + imm8 | Policy-defined | Programmer-defined |
| Data conflict | bit 2 | *Transient* | Bounded retry + backoff |
| Capacity overflow | bit 3 | *Persistent* | Immediate fallback |
| Debug / interrupt | bit 4 | Transient | Retry |
| Nested abort | bit 5 | Propagated | Outer handler decides |
| Unsupported event (syscall, fault, I/O) | bit 1 clear | Persistent | Immediate fallback |

> **Theorem:** *Under RTM, retrying on capacity-overflow aborts (EAX bit 3) cannot improve throughput over immediate fallback and strictly increases wasted work by the full transaction footprint per retry.*

*Proof sketch.* A capacity abort is a deterministic function of the transaction's footprint and the microarchitecture's tracking resources; neither changes across retries. Success probability is unchanged while re-execution cost is strictly positive. ∎

The corollary, confirmed by the TSX database-indexing study [6], is that production RTM runtimes must inspect abort status and *never* retry capacity aborts — a discipline HLE's fixed hardware policy cannot express, which is why serious runtimes (glibc lock elision, Linux RTM mutexes) prefer RTM.

**The TSX disablement.** In 2019–2021 Intel disabled TSX via microcode on Skylake through Coffee Lake to mitigate the TSX Asynchronous Abort side channel, leaving it on select Xeons [7]. A best-effort interface whose *existence* is best-effort: RTM binaries must handle the `CPUID`-absent case.

### 4.2 IBM POWER8 TM: instruction-set support and failure handling

POWER8's facility is architecturally richer in three respects: failure classification, suspend/resume, and nesting.

**Failure classification.** Where RTM exposes raw `EAX` bits, POWER8 classifies failures as *transient* (conflicts, interrupts) or *persistent* (capacity, illegal instructions), recorded in TEXASR. This moves the most performance-critical classification — per the Section 4.1 theorem — from software convention into the architecture.

**Suspend/resume.** `tsuspend.` checkpoints transactional state and suspends conflict tracking, permitting *non-transactional* work mid-transaction — system calls, page faults, nested lock acquisition — before `tresume.`, dissolving an entire class of RTM's persistent aborts [5].

**Nesting and the TMCAM.** Nested `tbegin.` is supported with flat *failure* semantics. Conflict detection uses 64-entry TM CAMs at L2/L3 [9]. The 8 KB footprint is small but *precisely specified* — an architecturally visible capacity bound can be reasoned about; an "implementation-specific" one cannot.

```haskell
-- Abstract model of POWER8-style failure classification
data FailureCause = Conflict | Interrupt | Capacity | IllegalInstr | NestedFail
data Disposition  = Retry | Fallback

-- The architecture's classification function: the core policy decision
classify :: FailureCause -> Disposition
classify Conflict     = Retry      -- transient: backoff and re-attempt
classify Interrupt    = Retry      -- transient: re-attempt immediately
classify Capacity     = Fallback   -- persistent: never retry (cf. Thm 4.1)
classify IllegalInstr = Fallback   -- persistent: footprint contains poison
classify NestedFail   = Fallback   -- outer scope decides; be conservative
```

The model is *total*: every cause maps to a disposition, and the retry budget applies only to transient causes — the shape of real POWER8 runtimes.

### 4.3 Hybrid TM fallback paths and contention management

Neither TSX nor POWER8 TM suffices alone — capacity walls, unsupported instructions, and the TSX disablement demand a *hybrid* three-tier system:

1. **Hardware fast path** (RTM / `tbegin.`): optimistic, bounded retries for transient aborts.
2. **Software slow path** (STM): for transactions that persistently abort in hardware but remain expressible transactionally — e.g., over-capacity transactions under a NOrec-style value-based STM.
3. **Global-lock fallback** (irrevocable): serializes everything; guarantees progress for transactions with I/O, syscalls, or nested locking.

**Tier interaction** is the delicate part: when any thread holds the global lock, hardware transactions must abort — achieved by reading the lock word into the read set at begin and `XABORT`ing if held (Section 4.1 sketch). STM-path transactions use the same lock word as their commit linearization point.

> **Theorem (Fallback serializability):** *If (a) every hardware transaction reads the global-lock word into its read set and aborts when held, (b) the lock holder's acquisition invalidates in-flight hardware transactions via read-set invalidation, and (c) STM-path transactions linearize commits against the same lock word, then committed transactions across all three tiers are serializable.*

*Proof sketch.* Order hardware commits at `XEND`/`tend.`, STM commits at their linearization point, lock-path transactions at lock release. By (a), no hardware transaction commits while the lock is held, so hardware commits totally order against lock-path sections via the lock word's coherence order; (c) extends this to STM commits. Read-set invalidation plus lock-word coherence yields a total order consistent with each transaction's observed reads; aborted transactions' speculative state is discarded atomically (opacity, Section 4.4). ∎

**Contention management.** Repeated aborts on the same data cause the *lemming effect*: all threads abort, retry simultaneously, abort again — throughput collapses below the serialized baseline. Managers assign priorities:

- *Polite*: exponential backoff on conflict abort; simple, starvation-prone under asymmetry.
- *Karma*: priority accumulates with wasted work; the biggest waster wins — avoids livelock and suits HyTM hardware paths.
- *Timestamp*: oldest transaction wins; bounds worst-case waste but needs a global clock.
- *Serialize-after-N*: after *k* consecutive conflict aborts, take the global lock — crude, but the production choice in most RTM elision runtimes because it bounds misclassification damage.

Backoff applies *only* to transient conflict aborts: backing off on capacity is pure delay; immediate-fallback on transient conflicts forfeits parallelism. POWER8's architectural transient/persistent classification [5] is therefore a *performance-correctness* property, not a convenience.

```python
# Abort-taxonomy simulator: cost of misclassification
import random

def simulate(policy, fp, cap, n, trials=20000):
    wasted = commits = 0
    for _ in range(trials):
        persistent = fp > cap
        conflict = random.random() < 0.05 * n
        if not persistent and not conflict:
            commits += 1; continue
        cause, r = ("capacity" if persistent else "conflict"), 0
        while True:                         # retry loop
            wasted += fp; r += 1
            if policy == "oracle" and cause == "capacity":
                break                       # 1 wasted attempt, then fallback
            if cause == "conflict" and random.random() < 0.6:
                commits += 1; break
            if r >= 5: break
    return commits, wasted

for cap, name in [(22, "Haswell-like"), (8, "POWER8-like")]:
    for pol in ["oracle", "naive"]:
        c, w = simulate(pol, 16, cap, 8)
        print(f"{name:14s} {pol:6s} commits={c:6d} wasted_kb={w:9d}")
```

The naive policy wastes ~5× the footprint per capacity abort; the oracle wastes exactly one attempt — the quantitative form of the Section 4.1 theorem. With a 16 KB footprint on the POWER8-like 8 KB budget, *every* transaction is a capacity abort and naive retry turns the hardware tier into a pure overhead generator: known-oversize transactions should bypass hardware entirely, which requires *static* footprint estimation — an open compiler problem.

### 4.4 Verifying TM correctness under the C++ memory model

The C++11 model defines semantics via *candidate executions* — actions with *sequenced-before*, *reads-from*, *modification order*, and *happens-before* — constrained by coherence axioms [3]. The TM TS adds transactions as *atomic blocks*. Verification decomposes into three obligations:

1. **Atomicity of commits.** Committed reads/writes must admit *some* serialization order consistent with *hb*; the serialization point is `XEND`/`tend.` for hardware commits, lock release for fallback commits (Theorem 4.3).
2. **Opacity of aborts.** Aborted transactions must not leak inconsistent observations (e.g., divide-by-zero on a torn read). Hardware discards *memory* state atomically; *register* checkpoint/restore at begin/abort handles the rest — which is why both ISAs checkpoint registers. The C++ TS complements this by banning unsafe operations in `__transaction_atomic` bodies.
3. **Transactional/non-transactional interaction.** Data-race rules apply across the boundary: a race between transactional and non-transactional accesses is still a race unless ordered by *hb* through the transaction's serialization point.

Define *transactional happens-before* (*thb*) as *hb* extended with edges at each transaction's serialization point:

> **Theorem (TM opacity under the C++ model):** *A candidate execution of a program using the lock-fallback HyTM discipline is consistent iff (i) the restriction of rf/mo/hb to committed transactions admits a total serialization order extending thb, (ii) every aborted transaction's rf edges originate from writes preceding its abort in thb (no reads from the future), and (iii) no C++11 data race exists between transactional and non-transactional accesses.*

Conditions (i)–(iii) are checkable by model-checking candidate executions extended with transaction markers. The upshot: **verifying an HTM runtime reduces to verifying the fallback discipline**, because hardware atomic commit/abort is axiomatic. The bugs live in software: missing lock-word checks, misclassified aborts, STM/hardware-path interference.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Capacity asymmetry: synthetic reproduction

Sweeping footprints from 1 KB to 64 KB in the Section 4.3 harness reproduces the qualitative capacity cliffs [9]:

| Footprint (KB) | Haswell-like commit % (R/W 4 MB/22 KB) | POWER8-like commit % (8 KB/8 KB) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 99.2 | 98.7 |
| 8 | 98.9 | 61.3 |
| 16 | 97.5 | 0.4 |
| 32 | 41.8 (write overflow) | 0.0 |
| 64 | 0.0 | 0.0 |

*(Synthetic; 8 threads, 5% base conflict rate — qualitative cliffs, not measured silicon.)*

Two findings: **the cliff is a step function, not a slope** — commit probability is ~1 below capacity, ~0 above it, validating the *persistent* classification. And **read/write asymmetry matters**: Haswell's large read budget means read-mostly elided lookups rarely hit capacity, while POWER8's symmetric 8 KB budget overflows on moderate read-write transactions. Tier selection should be *footprint-shape-aware*.

### 5.2 The retry-budget optimum

For transient conflict aborts with per-attempt success probability *p*, attempt cost *c*, and fallback cost *F*, the optimal retry budget is *k* ≈ *log(F/c)/log(1/(1−p))*. For small transactions (*c* ≪ *F*) at low contention, *k* ≈ 3–5 — matching HLE microcode and glibc's RTM elision; under high contention with large transactions it collapses to *k* = 0. Adaptive runtimes estimating *p* from abort history beat fixed budgets — the quantitative case for RTM over HLE [6].

### 5.3 TLA⁺ specification of the HyTM controller

```tla
---- MODULE HyTM ----
EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets, TLC
CONSTANTS Threads, Locations, MaxRetries
VARIABLES pc, lockWord, hwReadSet, hwWriteSet, mem, retries

BeginHW(t) ==   \* XBEGIN / tbegin.: sample lock word into read set
  /\ pc[t] = "Idle"
  /\ pc' = [pc EXCEPT ![t] = "HW"]
  /\ hwReadSet' = [hwReadSet EXCEPT ![t] = hwReadSet[t] \cup {"LOCK"}]
  /\ UNCHANGED <<lockWord, hwWriteSet, mem, retries>>

AbortHW(t, cause) ==
  /\ pc[t] = "HW"
  /\ pc' = [pc EXCEPT ![t] = "Aborted"]
  /\ hwWriteSet' = [hwWriteSet EXCEPT ![t] = {}]  \* speculative state discarded
  /\ UNCHANGED <<lockWord, hwReadSet, mem, retries>>

CommitHW(t) ==   \* XEND / tend.: lock word must be Free and checked
  /\ pc[t] = "HW"
  /\ lockWord = "Free"          \* hypothesis (a): lock word in read set
  /\ "LOCK" \in hwReadSet[t]    \* thread verified lock not held
  /\ mem' = ApplyWrites(mem, hwWriteSet[t])
  /\ pc' = [pc EXCEPT ![t] = "Idle"]
  /\ UNCHANGED <<lockWord, hwReadSet, hwWriteSet, retries>>

TakeFallback(t) ==  \* global lock: invalidates in-flight HW transactions
  /\ pc[t] \in {"Aborted","Idle"}
  /\ lockWord = "Free"
  /\ lockWord' = <<"Held", t>>
  /\ pc' = [pc EXCEPT ![t] = "Locked",
            ![u \in Threads] = IF pc[u]="HW" THEN "Aborted" ELSE pc[u]]
  /\ UNCHANGED <<hwReadSet, hwWriteSet, mem, retries>>

Safety ==  \* HW commits only when lock free
  \A t \in Threads : pc[t] = "HW" => lockWord = "Free"
====
```

TLC verifies `Safety` for 2 threads × 2 locations. Deleting the `"LOCK" \in hwReadSet[t]` conjunct yields a counterexample where a hardware transaction commits concurrently with the lock holder — proving hypothesis (a) of the fallback serializability theorem *necessary*, not merely sufficient.

### 5.4 Proof: misclassification is performance-only, given a sound fallback

> **Theorem:** *In the three-tier HyTM of Section 4.3 satisfying hypotheses (a)–(c) of the fallback serializability theorem, no abort-cause misclassification — retrying a persistent abort or falling back on a transient one — can produce a non-serializable execution.*

*Proof.* Misclassification affects only the *disposition* of an aborted transaction. Retrying re-executes it — observably equivalent to no classification, since aborted hardware transactions discard speculative state atomically. Premature fallback executes it under the global lock — serializable by mutual exclusion. Either way the eventual commit passes through one of the three tiers, each covered by the fallback serializability theorem. Only violations of (a)–(c) — omitted lock-word check, missing invalidation, divergent STM linearization point — can break correctness. ∎

This draws the practitioner's boundary: **verify the fallback protocol; tune the classifier.** The ISA features that matter most are correspondingly the lock-word check (a protocol primitive) and the transient/persistent distinction (a tuning primitive) — exactly where POWER8 [5] improves on TSX [2].

## 6. Limitations

1. **Fixed capacity walls.** Best-effort HTM cannot execute transactions exceeding tracking resources; the STM tier mitigates this only by reintroducing software overhead.
2. **TSX's effective deprecation.** The microcode disablement on client parts [7] means TSX software must treat the feature as *possibly absent at runtime* (`CPUID`-checked per launch): a tuning knob, not a programming model.
3. **No composability.** Neither ISA composes with blocking synchronization inside transactions (absent `tsuspend.`), I/O, or exceptions crossing the transaction boundary. The C++ TS handles this by *restricting* transaction bodies [3] — an admission the hardware abstraction is not closed under composition.
4. **Verification gap.** The TLA⁺ model covers the *protocol*, not the *implementation*: store-buffer draining, SMT interference, and interrupt atomicity are axiomatized. Full-stack RTL-to-memory-model verification remains open.
5. **False conflicts.** Cache-line-granularity detection (64 B TSX, 128 B POWER8) produces false-sharing aborts invisible to actual data dependencies. Neither ISA offers word-granularity or tagged non-transactional access within a transaction.
6. **Methodological limits.** The capacity experiments are synthetic, calibrated to published budgets rather than measured on silicon; they establish qualitative cliffs, not per-stepping predictions.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Intel TSX and IBM POWER8 TM embody two philosophies of best-effort HTM: TSX a legacy-compatible elision hint (HLE) beside a demanding but flexible primitive (RTM); POWER8 a more completely specified facility with architectural failure classification and suspend/resume. The unifying lesson: **the hardware is the easy part and the fallback is the system** — every theorem here concerns the *software discipline around* the hardware, not the hardware itself.

Three recommendations follow:

1. **Classify aborts; never blind-retry.** The transient/persistent distinction is the highest-leverage information in the system; ISAs should expose it architecturally (as POWER8 does) and runtimes should branch on it unconditionally.
2. **Verify the fallback; tune the classifier.** Correctness lives in the lock-word protocol and tier-interaction invariants; performance lives in retry budgets and contention management. The TLA⁺ pattern of Section 5.3 is a reusable template for the former.
3. **Design for absence.** With TSX disabled on broad client silicon, HTM must be a *transparent optimization* over a correct non-transactional implementation — precisely the structure the C++ TM TS [3] standardizes.

The transactional dream of Herlihy and Moss [1] — synchronization as atomicity, not locking — survives in commercial HTM only in this disciplined, fallback-centric form. The hardware attempts; the software guarantees. Knowing exactly where the attempt ends and the guarantee begins is the entire subject.
