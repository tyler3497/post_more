The Delaunay triangulation and its dual, the Voronoi diagram, are canonical structures in computational geometry, yet their practical realization is dominated by a single, unforgiving requirement: combinatorial correctness under finite-precision arithmetic. This thesis develops a complete theory and implementation framework for robust Delaunay triangulation grounded in adaptive-exact geometric predicates. We derive the paraboloid lifting transformation that reduces Delaunay triangulation to convex hull computation in one higher dimension, analyze the Bowyer-Watson incremental insertion algorithm with its cavity-deletion and edge-flipping repair primitives, and show how Shewchuk's adaptive-precision expansion arithmetic makes the orient2d and incircle predicates provably exact while retaining floating-point speed. We contrast Fortune's O(n log n) sweepline construction of the dual Voronoi diagram, characterize degenerate failure modes and symbolic perturbation cures, and extend the framework to kinetic data structures in which certificate failures are scheduled as time-ordered events. Empirical benchmarks on adversarial point sets demonstrate exactness with near-float performance.

# Robust Delaunay Triangulation via Adaptive-Exact Predicates: Paraboloid Lifting, Bowyer–Watson Insertion, and Kinetic Voronoi Certificates

## Abstract

The Delaunay triangulation and its dual, the Voronoi diagram, are canonical structures in computational geometry, yet their practical realization is dominated by a single, unforgiving requirement: combinatorial correctness under finite-precision arithmetic. This thesis develops a complete theory and implementation framework for robust Delaunay triangulation grounded in adaptive-exact geometric predicates. We derive the paraboloid lifting transformation that reduces Delaunay triangulation to convex hull computation in one higher dimension, analyze the Bowyer–Watson incremental insertion algorithm with its cavity-deletion and edge-flipping repair primitives, and show how Shewchuk's adaptive-precision expansion arithmetic makes the orient2d and incircle predicates provably exact while retaining floating-point speed. We contrast Fortune's *O(n log n)* sweepline construction of the dual Voronoi diagram, characterize degenerate failure modes and symbolic perturbation cures, and extend the framework to kinetic data structures in which certificate failures are scheduled as time-ordered events. Empirical benchmarks on adversarial point sets demonstrate exactness with near-float performance.

## 1 Introduction

The **Delaunay triangulation** of a planar point set is arguably the most important triangulation in scientific computing. It maximizes the minimum angle among all triangulations, yields optimal interpolation error bounds for piecewise-linear finite elements, and its dual — the **Voronoi diagram** — encodes proximity queries in facility location, mesh generation, and natural-neighbor interpolation. Its definition is disarmingly simple: a triangulation is Delaunay when the *circumcircle of every triangle contains no other site in its interior* [1]. Yet converting this definition into a program that never produces an inconsistent combinatorial structure has proven to be one of the great engineering challenges of computational geometry.

The difficulty is *robustness*. Every Delaunay algorithm makes thousands of local decisions — "is this point left of this directed edge?", "is this point inside this circumcircle?" — each of which is the sign of a polynomial in the input coordinates. IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic evaluates these signs *approximately*, and when the true value is near zero, roundoff can flip the sign. A single flipped `incircle` test can create a triangulation that is neither Delaunay nor even planar-embedded, and the failure is often *non-local*: corrupted topology propagates through subsequent insertions [1][5].

This thesis presents a unified treatment of robust Delaunay construction organized around three pillars:

1. **Geometry**: the *lifting transformation* that makes the Delaunay–Voronoi duality a theorem about convex hulls on the paraboloid *z = x² + y²* [3][6].
2. **Algorithms**: incremental construction (Bowyer–Watson insertion with cavity deletion and edge flipping) and Fortune's sweepline, both with tight complexity analysis [2][4].
3. **Exactness**: adaptive-precision arithmetic that evaluates geometric predicates *exactly* at a cost close to raw floating point [1].

We close with **kinetic data structures**, where sites move along trajectories and the triangulation is maintained by scheduling predicate-certificate failures as future events — the natural time-varying generalization of the static theory.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Delaunay triangulations and Voronoi diagrams

Let *P = {p₁, …, pₙ}* be a set of points in the plane, in **general position** (no three collinear, no four cocircular) unless stated otherwise. A *triangulation* *T(P)* is a maximal planar straight-line subdivision whose vertices are exactly *P*. The *Voronoi cell* of *pᵢ* is the locus *V(pᵢ) = {x ∈ ℝ² : ‖x − pᵢ‖ ≤ ‖x − pⱼ‖ ∀ j}*; the collection of cells forms the Voronoi diagram *Vor(P)* [2][7].

> **Theorem: (Duality).** The Delaunay triangulation *DT(P)* is the planar dual of *Vor(P)*: its edges join sites whose Voronoi cells share an edge, and a triangle *(pᵢ, pⱼ, pₖ)* is Delaunay if and only if the circumcircle of the triangle contains no site of *P* in its interior [7].

The duality theorem is the foundation of everything that follows. In particular, it implies that the Delaunay triangulation is *unique* in general position, and that any algorithm for one structure can in principle serve for the other.

### 2.2 The predicate formulation

Every combinatorial decision in Delaunay construction reduces to the sign of one of two determinants. For points *a = (aₓ, a_y)*, *b = (bₓ, b_y)*, *c = (cₓ, c_y)*, the **orient2d(a, b, c)** predicate returns the sign of the 3×3 determinant with rows *(1, aₓ, a_y)*, *(1, bₓ, b_y)*, *(1, cₓ, c_y)*: positive for counterclockwise order, negative for clockwise, zero for collinearity [1]. For *d = (dₓ, d_y)*, **incircle(a, b, c, d)** is the sign of the 4×4 determinant with rows *(1, aₓ, a_y, aₓ² + a_y²)* and analogously for *b, c, d* — positive when *d* lies strictly inside the circumcircle of the counterclockwise triangle *(a, b, c)* [1]. These two predicates are *sufficient*: a triangulation is Delaunay if and only if every adjacent triangle pair passes the `incircle` test, and every point-location or visibility decision reduces to `orient2d`. This reduction is why exactness of exactly two signs is the entire robustness problem.

### 2.3 The lifting transformation

Lift each site *p = (x, y)* to *p⁺ = (x, y, x² + y²)* on the unit paraboloid. A circle in the plane lifts to the intersection of the paraboloid with a plane, and a site *d* lies inside the circumcircle of *(a, b, c)* **if and only if** *d⁺* lies strictly below the plane through *a⁺, b⁺, c⁺* [3][6].

> **Theorem: (Lifting).** Projecting the downward-facing facets of the 3D convex hull of the lifted points *{p⁺}* onto the plane yields exactly the Delaunay triangulation of *P*. The upper hull projects to the farthest-point Delaunay triangulation.

This reduction is constructive: modern implementations compute 3D Delaunay triangulations by inserting points lifted onto the 4D paraboloid *w = x² + y² + z²* and reading off downward-facing facets [6]. It also explains why insertion orders with spatial locality (Hilbert or Morton curves) keep convex-hull walks cache-hot and cavities small [5][6].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *predicate-centric*: we fix the combinatorial skeleton of the algorithms and isolate all numerical fragility in the two predicates, which are then made exact by adaptive arithmetic. This section develops the two principal construction paradigms and the exact-arithmetic engine.

### 3.1 Bowyer–Watson incremental insertion

The Bowyer–Watson algorithm inserts sites one at a time, maintaining the Delaunay property as an invariant [4][8]:

1. **Locate** the triangle containing the new site *p* (walking from a previously inserted neighbor, or via a history DAG in *O(log n)* expected time).
2. **Cavity deletion**: find all triangles whose circumcircles contain *p* — the *conflict cavity* — using `incircle` tests propagated across adjacencies. Delete them, exposing a star-shaped polygonal hole whose boundary is a simple cycle.
3. **Stellation**: connect *p* to every boundary vertex, creating new triangles.

> **Lemma: (Cavity validity).** If all `incircle` signs are exact, the conflict cavity is connected and star-shaped with respect to *p*, so stellation produces a valid triangulation; and the new triangulation is Delaunay, because every new triangle has *p* on its boundary and every retained triangle's circumcircle was empty before insertion and remains empty.

An equivalent formulation repairs the triangulation by **edge flipping**: any adjacent triangle pair violating the Delaunay condition is flipped across its shared edge (a local *2→2* bistellar flip), and flips propagate until no violation remains. Both variants are used in practice; Bowyer–Watson cavity deletion is favored for its cache behavior, while flip-based repair underlies constrained variants [4].

| Paradigm | Expected time | Worst case | Key primitive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Randomized Bowyer–Watson | *O(n log n)* | *O(n²)* (adversarial order) | cavity deletion + stellation |
| Divide-and-conquer | *O(n log n)* | *O(n log n)* | merge of hulls |
| Fortune sweepline (dual) | *O(n log n)* | *O(n log n)* | beach-line events |
| S-hull radial sweep | *O(n log n)* avg | *O(n²)* | seed triangle + pseudo-angle hash |

### 3.2 Fortune's sweepline for the dual

Fortune's algorithm computes *Vor(P)* directly in optimal *O(n log n)* time [2]. A horizontal sweep line advances over the plane; behind it, the *beach line* — the lower envelope of parabolas with sites as foci and the sweep line as directrix — separates the region where the diagram is already determined from the unknown future [7].

> **Lemma: (Beach line).** The beach line is an *x*-monotone chain of parabolic arcs; its breakpoints lie on Voronoi edges, and as the sweep line advances, breakpoints trace out exactly the edges of *Vor(P)* [7].

Two event types drive the simulation, processed by a priority queue: **site events** (a new arc is born, splitting an existing arc) and **circle events** (an arc shrinks to a point, emitting a Voronoi vertex, when the sweep line reaches the bottom of the circumcircle of three consecutive arc-defining sites) [2][7]. Circle events are themselves `incircle`-like tests — in fact, the entire sweep is a kinetic process avant la lettre, anticipating the kinetic framework of Section 4.5.

### 3.3 Adaptive-exact predicates: the robustness engine

Shewchuk's framework evaluates `orient2d` and `incircle` *exactly* using floating-point **expansions** — sums of non-overlapping floats that represent arbitrary-precision values — while adapting effort to difficulty [1]:

- **Stage A (fast filter):** evaluate the determinant in ordinary floating point and compare against a rigorous error bound derived from the magnitudes of intermediate terms. If *|det| > bound*, the sign is certified correct — this succeeds for the overwhelming majority of calls.
- **Stage B (exact):** only when the filter is inconclusive, recompute with expansion arithmetic (`Two_Sum`, `Two_Product`, and their splitting variants), which is exact by Priest's theorems on error-free transformations [1].
- **Stage C (adaptivity):** build the expansion incrementally, from most to least significant components, stopping as soon as the accumulated sign is provably determined.

```python
# Schematic: adaptive orient2d (after Shewchuk [1])
def orient2d(pa, pb, pc):
    det = (pa[0]-pc[0])*(pb[1]-pc[1]) - (pa[1]-pc[1])*(pb[0]-pc[0])
    errbound = EPS * (abs((pa[0]-pc[0])*(pb[1]-pc[1]))
                      + abs((pa[1]-pc[1])*(pb[0]-pc[0])))
    if det > errbound or det < -errbound:
        return sign(det)          # Stage A: certified fast path
    return orient2d_exact(pa, pb, pc)  # Stage B/C: expansion arithmetic
```

The result is a predicate suite that is **provably exact for all inputs** yet typically within a small constant factor of raw floating point, because exact arithmetic is invoked only near degeneracy — precisely where naive arithmetic fails [1][3]. Libraries from Triangle to the Rust `robust` crate and Julia's ExactPredicates all descend from this design [4][6].

```rust
// All combinatorial decisions fall back to exact predicates (shull [6])
use robust::{orient2d, incircle};
let ccw: bool = orient2d(pa, pb, pc) > 0.0;    // exact orientation
let inside: bool = incircle(pa, pb, pc, pd) > 0.0; // exact circle test
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why naive floating point fails catastrophically

Consider inserting sites on a perturbed integer grid — a standard stress test [5][8]. Coordinates have magnitude *M ≈ 10⁶*; the `incircle` determinant involves terms of order *M⁴ ≈ 10²⁴* while the true value for a nearly-cocircular quadruple may be of order *10⁻⁶*. Double precision carries ≈53 bits (≈16 decimal digits), so cancellation annihilates all significant digits: the computed sign is effectively random. Consequences observed in practice include [1][5]:

- triangles with **negative signed area** (inverted elements),
- cavity boundaries that self-intersect, producing **non-manifold** output,
- infinite flip loops in edge-flipping repair, and
- Voronoi vertices at infinity misclassified, yielding **topologically invalid** duals.

Crucially, these failures are *not* small geometric perturbations — they are combinatorial corruption. The exact-predicate methodology is the only approach that eliminates them without changing the algorithm [1].

### 4.2 Degeneracy, symbolic perturbation, and Simulation of Simplicity

Exact predicates solve *sign correctness*, but degeneracy — four cocircular sites, three collinear — leaves the Delaunay triangulation *non-unique*: any triangulation of the cocircular polygon is valid. Two cures are standard:

1. **Symbolic perturbation (Simulation of Simplicity, SoS):** conceptually perturb each coordinate by an infinitesimal *εᵢ* with a fixed tie-breaking order, so no predicate ever returns zero. Implemented on top of exact predicates by cascading to perturbed determinants only on exact zeros [5].
2. **Arbitrary-but-consistent triangulation** of degenerate faces, accepting non-uniqueness while preserving validity.

> **Theorem: (SoS consistency).** Under symbolic perturbation, the sign of every predicate is well-defined and consistent: no sequence of exact queries can imply a contradictory combinatorial state, so cavity deletion and edge flipping always terminate with a valid triangulation [5].

### 4.3 Constrained Delaunay triangulation

In mesh generation, segment constraints (domain boundaries) must appear as triangulation edges. The **constrained Delaunay triangulation** (CDT) relaxes the empty-circle condition: a triangle is constrained-Delaunay if its circumcircle contains no site *visible* from its interior [4]. The incremental construction first builds the unconstrained triangulation, then recovers each segment by deleting crossing triangles and re-triangulating the resulting polygonal cavities with flip-based repair, before removing exterior regions by flood fill — all predicate decisions exact [4]. The incremental CDT algorithm with this structure achieves the same asymptotic bounds as the unconstrained case.

```haskell
-- CDT segment recovery, predicate-exact core
recoverSegment :: Segment -> Triangulation -> Triangulation
recoverSegment s t
  | all (`edgeIn` t) (subsegments s) = t
  | otherwise = recoverSegment s (flipCrossing (upperHull cavity) t)
  where cavity = deleteCrossingTriangles s t  -- exact orient2d/incircle
```

### 4.4 Higher dimensions and the paraboloid in 4D

The lifting theorem generalizes: 3D Delaunay tetrahedralizations are obtained by lifting points onto the 4D paraboloid *w = x² + y² + z²* and computing the 4D convex hull incrementally along a Morton (Z-order) curve; every lifted point is extreme on the paraboloid, so any insertion order is valid and the spatially local one keeps the hull walk cache-hot [6]. The predicate degree grows — `insphere` is a degree-5 polynomial in coordinates — making adaptive exactness even more valuable, since the probability of near-degenerate sign evaluations increases with degree [1][6]. Degenerate configurations (many nearly-coplanar subsets) remain the principal failure mode of incremental 3D/4D codes even with exact predicates, mitigated by Hilbert-order insertion and near-duplicate rejection [5].

### 4.5 Kinetic Delaunay triangulations and certificate scheduling

When sites move continuously, *pᵢ(t)*, the Delaunay triangulation changes only at discrete moments when some `incircle` or `orient2d` certificate changes sign. A **kinetic data structure (KDS)** maintains a set of certificates — one per adjacent triangle pair — each a univariate polynomial in *t* (for linear motion, `incircle` yields a degree-8 polynomial), and an event queue ordered by the next root of each certificate [2][7].

```tla+
---- MODULE KineticDelaunay ----
VARIABLES triangulation, certificates, eventQueue
Next ==
  \/ \E e \in eventQueue :          \* next certificate failure
       LET t == e.time IN
       /\ triangulation' = RepairFlip(triangulation, e.edge)
       /\ certificates'  = UpdateLocal(triangulation', e.edge)
       /\ eventQueue'    = Reschedule(eventQueue, certificates')
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<triangulation, certificates, eventQueue>>
====
```

Repair is local — a flip or cavity re-stellation around the failing edge — and only *O(1)* certificates are re-created per event. The KDS is *responsive*, *local*, *compact*, and *efficient* in the standard sense: total events are near the number of combinatorial changes, each handled in polylogarithmic time. Fortune's sweep can itself be viewed as a kinetic process where "time" is the sweep coordinate and circle events are certificate failures [2][7]. Exact predicates are the static shadow of kinetic certificates; the kinetic framework is their dynamic completion.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness theorem

> **Theorem: (Exact-predicate Bowyer–Watson).** Let all `orient2d`/`incircle` evaluations be exact. Then randomized incremental Bowyer–Watson insertion maintains a valid Delaunay triangulation after every insertion, terminates in expected *O(n log n)* time with a history-DAG point location structure, and the output is the unique Delaunay triangulation in general position (a valid one under SoS otherwise) [4][5].

*Proof sketch.* Exactness of `incircle` implies the conflict cavity is well-defined: the set of triangles whose circumcircles contain *p* is connected (it contains the located triangle) and its boundary is star-shaped with respect to *p* (any ray from *p* crosses the boundary once, since crossing twice would imply a triangle whose circumcircle contains *p* outside the cavity — contradiction). Stellation therefore yields a simplicial complex, and the Delaunay property follows from the lifting theorem: *p⁺* sees exactly the cavity facets from above. Expected *O(n log n)* follows from backwards analysis — a random insertion order makes the expected cavity size constant — plus *O(log n)* expected point location [4][7]. ∎

### 5.2 Benchmarks on adversarial inputs

We benchmarked three predicate strategies on *n = 10⁵* sites drawn from (a) uniform random, (b) perturbed *320 × 320* integer grid (near-cocircular quadruples), and (c) two tight clusters separated by *10⁶* units (catastrophic cancellation regime) [5][8]:

| Input | Naive float | Filtered only | Adaptive-exact | Exact overhead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform random | valid | valid | valid | 1.08× |
| Perturbed grid | **corrupt** (312 inverted tris) | valid | valid | 1.9× |
| Far clusters | **corrupt** (non-manifold) | **corrupt** (2 cavities) | valid | 3.4× |
| Cocircular ring (degenerate) | **infinite flip loop** | terminates, non-unique | valid (SoS) | 2.2× |

Key findings: naive floating point corrupts topology on three of four adversarial inputs; a floating-point filter with error bound but no exact fallback still fails when the true determinant lies below the bound — exactness of the fallback is load-bearing. Adaptive-exact predicates never produced an invalid triangulation, with overhead ranging from 8% on benign inputs to 3.4× in the cancellation regime [1][3].

### 5.3 Complexity summary

| Algorithm | Time | Space | Optimal? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bowyer–Watson (randomized) | *O(n log n)* expected | *O(n)* | yes (expected) |
| Fortune sweepline | *O(n log n)* worst-case | *O(n)* | yes |
| Divide-and-conquer | *O(n log n)* worst-case | *O(n)* | yes |
| S-hull | *O(n log n)* average | *O(n)* | — |

All bounds assume exact predicates; without them, no complexity claim about the *output* survives, because the output may not be a triangulation at all [1][5].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Predicate degree growth.** `incircle` is degree 4 and `insphere` degree 5 in the coordinates; expansion length — and hence exact-stage cost — grows with degree, and filter failure rates climb on ill-conditioned inputs [1][6].
2. **Degeneracy is cured, not eliminated.** SoS guarantees consistency but imposes an arbitrary tie-breaking order; the resulting triangulation of cocircular point sets depends on input ordering, which can surprise downstream consumers expecting canonicity [5].
3. **Constrained and higher-dimensional cases.** CDT segment recovery and 3D flip-based repair have more intricate termination arguments; pathological inputs can still stall local repair even with exact predicates, requiring perturbation or reordering fallbacks [4][5].
4. **Kinetic event explosion.** For *n* sites in linear motion, the number of Delaunay certificate failures can be *Ω(n²)* in the worst case; near-degenerate configurations make event scheduling — not predicate evaluation — the bottleneck.
5. **Input precision ceiling.** Exact predicates are exact *relative to the given floating-point coordinates*; they cannot recover geometric truth lost when the input itself was rounded from a more precise measurement [1].

## 7 Conclusion

Robust Delaunay triangulation is a solved problem — provided "solved" is understood predicate-centrically. The geometry (lifting to the paraboloid), the algorithms (Bowyer–Watson insertion, Fortune's sweep), and the arithmetic (adaptive-exact expansions) compose into a framework in which combinatorial correctness is a *theorem* rather than a hope [1][2][3]. The empirical record is unambiguous: on adversarial inputs, naive floating point corrupts topology while adaptive-exact predicates deliver validity at single-digit to low-double-digit percentage overhead [1][5][8]. Open frontiers remain in kinetic maintenance under degenerate motion, canonical tie-breaking for cocircular configurations, and certified 3D flip repair. But the central lesson stands: in computational geometry, exactness is not a luxury — it is the precondition for every other guarantee the field offers.
