# Maximum Satisfiability: Core-Guided Search, Implicit Hitting Sets, Branch and Bound, and Industrial Applications

## Abstract

Maximum satisfiability (MaxSAT) asks for an assignment minimizing the weight of violated clauses of a propositional formula, generalizing SAT to optimization and subsuming the partial, weighted, and weighted partial variants. This thesis develops the modern algorithmic theory of complete MaxSAT solving around three pillars: core-guided search, which turns unsatisfiable cores returned by a SAT oracle into lower bounds through clause relaxation and cardinality constraints, from Fu–Malik through WPM and OLL; implicit hitting sets (IHS), which recast MaxSAT as a duality between minimum-cost hitting sets over accumulated cores and SAT-guided feasibility checks, as realized in MaxHS; and branch-and-bound with lower bounding driven by MaxSAT resolution and unit propagation. We contrast reductions to iterated SAT with native solvers, survey the MaxSAT Evaluations as the field's empirical engine, and ground the theory in industrial applications — timetabling, design debugging, and software package upgradeability — closing with correctness arguments, empirical results, and open problems in proof complexity and certification.

## 1. Introduction

The satisfiability problem (SAT) asks whether a propositional formula in conjunctive normal form (CNF) admits a satisfying assignment. Its optimization counterpart, *maximum satisfiability* (MaxSAT), relaxes the all-or-nothing requirement: given hard clauses that must be satisfied and soft clauses carrying penalties when falsified, MaxSAT minimizes the total weight of falsified soft clauses [1]. The decision version is NP-complete; the optimization version is FP^NP-complete, solvable in polynomial time with an NP oracle — and a SAT solver plays precisely that oracle role in virtually every modern implementation [12].

MaxSAT is not merely a theoretical curiosity. It is the natural target language for a growing family of industrial optimization problems whose constraints decompose into "must hold" and "would like to hold" requirements: course timetables with mandatory curriculum constraints and preferred scheduling slots, digital circuits with specified behavior and candidate fault sites, and software distributions with dependency/conflict structure and desirable package versions. The last two decades have seen MaxSAT solvers evolve from straightforward branch-and-bound engines into a sophisticated ecosystem of core-guided, hitting-set, and hybrid architectures that now routinely outperform generic integer-programming systems such as CPLEX on structured benchmarks [12].

Three algorithmic families structure this thesis. **Core-guided search**, initiated by Fu and Malik, extracts an *unsatisfiable core* from a failing SAT call and relaxes the soft clauses it touches with fresh blocking variables under a cardinality constraint, lifting a provable lower bound one core at a time [1][2][10]. **Implicit hitting sets (IHS)**, pioneered in MaxHS, dualize the problem: an optimal solution corresponds to a minimum-cost hitting set over the collection of all cores, and because enumerating all cores is infeasible, an IP solver iteratively hits only the cores a SAT solver has revealed [5][6][7]. **Branch-and-bound**, embodied in solvers such as MINIMAXSAT, descends a search tree while computing lower bounds from disjoint inconsistent subformulas discovered by unit propagation [13].

This thesis presents each family formally, traces their lineage from the 2006 breakthroughs to the OLL and WPM refinements, compares encodings-to-SAT against native solvers, reports the empirical landscape of the annual MaxSAT Evaluations, and grounds the theory in three industrial case studies, closing with correctness arguments and open problems.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Problem Definition and Variants

Let *X* = {*x*₁, …, *x*ₙ} be Boolean variables. A *literal* is a variable or its negation; a *clause* is a disjunction of literals; a formula is in CNF when it is a conjunction of clauses. A weighted partial MaxSAT instance is a triple *F* = (*F*ₕ, *F*ₛ, *c*), where *F*ₕ is a set of hard clauses, *F*ₛ a set of soft clauses, and *c*: *F*ₛ → ℝ⁺ a cost function. An assignment *τ* is a *solution* if it satisfies every clause of *F*ₕ, and its *cost* is cost(*F*, *τ*) = Σ_{*C*∈*F*ₛ, *τ*⊭*C*} *c*(*C*) [6]. A solution is *optimal* if its cost is minimal among all solutions; the *weighted partial MaxSAT* problem asks for such an optimum. This single schema subsumes several classical variants:

| Variant | Hard clauses | Soft weights | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain MaxSAT | none | unit | Maximize number of satisfied clauses |
| Partial MaxSAT | present | unit | Satisfy all hard clauses, maximize satisfied soft clauses |
| Weighted MaxSAT | none | integer | Minimize total weight of falsified clauses |
| Weighted Partial MaxSAT | present | integer | The general case; subsumes the rest |

The unweighted partial variant, targeted by the original Fu–Malik algorithm, remains the conceptual core of core-guided search [1][2].

### 2.2 Unsatisfiable Cores and Blocking Variables

A *core* of a formula is an unsatisfiable subset of its clauses [10]. Assumption-based SAT solvers expose cores as the selector literals responsible for a conflict. A *blocking* variable *b_C* attached to a soft clause *C* yields *C* ∨ *b_C*; setting *b_C* = 1 "pays" for the clause. Optimization becomes minimizing the weighted sum of blocking variables set to 1.

### 2.3 A Timeline of Complete Solvers

The following chronology anchors the exposition [4]:

| Year | Solver / Algorithm | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 2006 | Fu–Malik (PM1) | First core-guided lower-bound search for partial MaxSAT [1] |
| 2007 | MSU3 | Blocking variables on disjoint cores; refined lower bound [10] |
| 2009 | WPM1 / WMSU1 | Weighted extension via minimum-weight core splitting [2] |
| 2010 | WPM2 | One relaxation per clause; disjoint core covers |
| 2013 | MaxHS | Implicit hitting sets: SAT + MIP duality [6] |
| 2014 | Open-WBO | Incremental, industrial-grade core-guided platform |
| 2014 | OLL | One relaxation per soft clause with structural transformation |
| 2018 | RC2 | PySAT's relaxation-based core-guided solver |
| 2019–20 | EvalMaxSAT | Native MaxSAT reasoning inside the SAT solver |

Each entry reuses the same raw material — a SAT oracle, a core, a relaxation — but arranges the *accounting* of the lower bound differently, and that accounting is where the deep ideas live.

## 3. Methodology / Formal Framework

### 3.1 The Working Formula

Core-guided algorithms maintain a *working formula* *φ*_W that evolves across iterations. Initially *φ*_W = *F*ₕ ∪ {*C* | (*C*, *w*) ∈ *F*ₛ} for the partial case, with soft clauses activated by selector assumptions. Each iteration queries the SAT oracle; on SAT the current lower bound *k* is optimal, and on UNSAT with core *κ*, every soft clause in *κ* is relaxed with a fresh blocking variable and a hard cardinality constraint CNF(Σ_{*C*∈*κ*} *b_C* ≤ 1) is added — at least one clause of the core must be violated (that is what the core tells us), and at most one, preventing the bound from overshooting the optimum [1]. The lower bound increments by the core's minimum weight.

> **Theorem (Fu–Malik correctness).** The algorithm terminates with the minimum cost of falsified soft clauses. Each core *κ* forces cost ≥ bound + minw(*κ*); relaxation with Σ*b* ≤ 1 discards no optimal solution; the integral, strictly increasing bound is capped by the total soft weight, so the first SAT call certifies optimality [1][2]. ∎

### 3.2 Weighted Cores: Splitting

Weights complicate the picture: a core *κ* = {(*C*₁, *w*₁), …, (*C_k*, *w_k*)} with unequal weights cannot be uniformly relaxed. The WPM1/WMSU1 scheme computes *m* = min *w_i*, splits each clause with *w_i* > *m* into (*C_i*, *m*) and a residual (*C_i*, *w_i* − *m*) with a fresh selector, and applies the unweighted relaxation to the weight-*m* slice [2]. The cost accumulates *m*, and the residuals re-enter the pool for future cores — a single conceptual iteration collapses what would otherwise be *m* unit steps.

### 3.3 The Hitting-Set Characterization

The IHS viewpoint begins with a simple observation: an optimal solution must falsify at least one clause from *every* core, i.e., the set *H* of falsified soft clauses is a *hitting set* over the collection of all cores. Conversely, any minimum-cost hitting set over *all* cores extends to an optimal solution. Because enumerating all cores is infeasible, IHS algorithms maintain a subset K of discovered cores, compute a minimum-cost hitting set *H* over K with an IP solver (a tractable subproblem of the full instance), and ask a SAT solver whether *F*ₕ ∪ (*F*ₛ ∖ *H*) is satisfiable; if not, the new core joins K and the loop repeats [5][6].

> **Proposition (IHS lower bound).** Let *H* be a minimum-cost hitting set over any collection K of cores of *F*. Then cost(*F*, *H*) ≤ cost(*F*), the true optimum. If the SAT solver returns a model for *F*ₕ ∪ (*F*ₛ ∖ *H*), that model is optimal. ∎

The proof is immediate from the hitting-set property, yet the architecture it enables — *best of both worlds*, in the tutorial's phrase — is profound: the SAT solver proves unsatisfiability and explains it with cores, while the IP solver handles the cost function, and neither needs to know the other's internals [4][6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Core-Guided Search: From Fu–Malik to OLL

The original Fu–Malik (PM1) algorithm is elegant but suffers on overlapping cores: a soft clause participating in several cores accumulates multiple blocking variables, introducing *symmetries* — assignments that differ only in which redundant blocking variable is set — that the SAT solver must expensively refute [4]. Three generations of refinements address this:

1. **WPM1/WPM2 (2009–2010).** WPM2 assigns *one* relaxation variable per soft clause globally and groups intersecting cores into *disjoint covers*, adding one cardinality constraint per cover rather than per core [4][10].
2. **OLL (2014).** The *one literal per level* transformation restructures the relaxation so that each soft clause receives exactly one blocking variable, and the cardinality constraints are organized so that symmetries are structurally eliminated. OLL-based solvers (e.g., Open-WBO's OLL variant) became dominant in the weighted tracks of the MaxSAT Evaluations.
3. **RC2 (2018).** Implemented in PySAT, RC2 refines core-guided search with stratification, unsatisfiable-core trimming, and incremental cardinality encodings, remaining the reference pedagogical implementation [1].

A PySAT-style sketch of the core loop (following `pysat.examples.fm`) is:

```python
from pysat.examples.fm import FMPlus
from pysat.formula import WCNF

wcnf = WCNF()
# ... populate wcnf with hard clauses (wcnf.append)
# ... and soft clauses: wcnf.append(clause, weight=w)
fm = FMPlus(wcnf, verbose=0)
opt_model = fm.compute()          # iterate: SAT call -> UNSAT core
print('optimum cost:', fm.cost)    # sum of minw over processed cores
for clause in fm.get_model():     # assignment achieving the optimum
    print(clause)
```

Internally, `treat_core()` extracts the core via `get_core()`, computes `minw`, splits overweight clauses (`split_core()`), relaxes each core clause with a fresh relaxation literal, and posts Σ*r* ≤ 1 — exactly the algorithm of Section 3.1, with unit cores handled by `remove_unit_core()` [1].

### 4.2 The Implicit Hitting Set Duality

Where core-guided search pushes a lower bound upward through syntactic relaxation, IHS maintains a genuine *duality gap*. Consider the loop:

1. SAT solver tests *F*ₕ ∪ (*F*ₛ ∖ *H*) under assumptions ¬*b* for blocking variables outside *H*.
2. On UNSAT, extract core *κ*, add to K; on SAT, extract model *τ* and tighten the *upper* bound.
3. IP solver recomputes *H* = MCHS(K), tightening the *lower* bound.
4. Terminate when lower and upper bounds meet.

MaxHS instantiates this with a MIP solver for the hitting-set step; LMHS replaces the monolithic IP call with a SAT-IP hybrid that reuses learned structure across iterations [5][6]. Recent work revisits the MaxHS architecture with modern SAT backends and new characterizations of when hitting-set bounds dominate core-guided bounds [7]. Incremental IHS under *changing weights* — recomputing optima as the cost function shifts, crucial for interactive applications — has been formalized as a CP problem with dedicated propagation [8].

The duality also yields an *anytime* profile for free: every SAT model is a feasible solution with a certified gap, so IHS solvers double as strong incomplete solvers, a property exploited in the anytime tracks of the MaxSAT Evaluations [9].

### 4.3 Branch-and-Bound and Lower Bounding via MaxSAT Resolution

Branch-and-bound (B&B) predates core-guided search and remains competitive through solvers like MINIMAXSAT [13]. The schema is DPLL-style search augmented with optimization bookkeeping:

- Maintain global upper bound *UB* (best solution found) and, at each node, a lower bound *LB* on the best achievable cost below it.
- Compute *LB* from *disjoint inconsistent subformulas*: if unit propagation derives *t* pairwise clause-disjoint contradictions, at least *t* soft clauses must be falsified, so *LB* = current cost + *t* [13].
- Prune when *LB* ≥ *UB*; branch on an unassigned variable otherwise.

The engine's proof-theoretic soul is *MaxSAT resolution*, an inference rule that transforms the formula while preserving the optimum: from (*x* ∨ *A*, *u*) and (¬*x* ∨ *B*, *v*) derive (*A* ∨ *B*, min(*u*,*v*)) plus compensation clauses, with the empty clause (□, *w*) recording *w* units of unavoidable cost [3]. MaxSAT resolution generalizes classical resolution to weighted optimization and underlies both lower-bounding in B&B and the correctness of core-guided transformations, which can be viewed as resolution proofs in disguise.

> **Theorem (Soundness of MaxSAT resolution).** Applying the weighted MaxSAT resolution rule preserves the optimal cost of the instance. *Proof sketch.* The resolvent and compensation clauses are satisfied exactly when one of the premises' weight contributions is; a case analysis on the assignment of *x* shows cost is conserved across the transformation [3]. ∎

Recent proof-complexity work extends this picture with *redundancy rules* for MaxSAT — cost-preserving clause additions that admit polynomial-size proofs of weak pigeonhole optima where MaxSAT resolution alone is exponential, delineating the boundary of what core-guided calculi can efficiently certify [14].

### 4.4 Encodings to SAT versus Native Solvers

Two philosophies compete for implementing MaxSAT on SAT technology. **Encodings to SAT** (the core-guided mainstream) compile each optimization step into CNF: relaxation variables, cardinality networks (sequential counters, sorting networks, totalizers), and assumption interfaces. The encoding cost is real — cardinality constraints can dominate formula size — but the approach inherits every CDCL advance for free, including incrementality across iterations [1][4].

**Native solvers** reason about optimization inside the solver. EvalMaxSAT integrates MaxSAT-aware preprocessing and lower-bound computation into the CDCL loop; stochastic local search (SLS) solvers abandon completeness for anytime performance, with clause-weighting schemes tuned separately for partial and weighted partial structure [13]. Empirically, native engines win where encoding overhead bites, while core-guided encodings dominate the weighted partial tracks where core quality is high.

### 4.5 Industrial Applications

MaxSAT's industrial footprint is the strongest argument for the field's maturity [12]:

- **Timetabling.** Curriculum and room constraints become hard clauses; preferences — teacher slots, room contiguity — become soft. Among the hardest industrial benchmarks, where core-guided solvers routinely beat generic MIP [2].
- **Design debugging.** Given a circuit and a failing test vector, hard clauses encode correct behavior while soft clauses mark candidate faulty gates; the optimum is a minimum-cardinality suspect set.
- **Software package upgradeability.** In the Mancoosi competition's *upgradeability* instances, dependencies and conflicts are hard clauses and each installable package a weighted soft clause (e.g., "keep *p* at its newest version"). The optimum is the best achievable installation, and core-guided solvers clear all 100 competition instances [2][12].
- **Combinatorial testing.** Covering-array-number minimization encoded as MaxSAT lets weighted RC2 perform top-down search and beat dedicated greedy algorithms on dozens of instances [11].
- **Data analysis.** Correlation clustering, haplotype inference, and preference-based planning round out the portfolio of standard MaxSAT formulations.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The MaxSAT Evaluations

Since 2006, the annual **MaxSAT Evaluations** (MSE) have provided the field's shared empirical substrate: complete tracks (unweighted and weighted) and incomplete/anytime tracks, plus a special incremental track [9]. Recent editions show three trends: core-guided solvers (Open-WBO variants, RC2, MaxHS) sweep the complete tracks; hybrid SLS+complete portfolios beat pure incomplete solvers; industrial categories — timetabling, upgradeability, haplotyping — separate the field more sharply than crafted random instances [2][9].

Representative weighted-industrial results (solved instances, from the PMRes study's benchmark set) illustrate the gaps [3]:

| Benchmark family | eva (core-guided) | wpm1 | wpm2 | msunc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| haplotyping (97) | 97 | 97 | 81 | 52 |
| packupwpms (95) | 95 | 90 | 46 | 27 |
| timetabling (11) | 11 | 10 | 7 | 8 |
| upgradeability (100) | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| **Total (360)** | **360** | **354** | **258** | **236** |

The lesson is consistent across a decade of editions: no single architecture dominates every family, and portfolios that combine core-guided lower bounds with SLS upper bounds are the state of the art [4][9][11].

### 5.2 Correctness Arguments

The three families' correctness rests on three invariants:

1. **Core-guided:** the working formula *φ*_W at bound *k* is satisfiable iff the original instance has a solution of cost ≤ *k*; each core raises *k* by exactly the unavoidable additional cost [1][2][10].
2. **IHS:** the MCHS cost over discovered cores is always a lower bound; the first model extracted for *F*ₕ ∪ (*F*ₛ ∖ *H*) is optimal because its cost equals the bound [5][6].
3. **B&B:** disjoint inconsistent subformulas certify unavoidable cost; pruning *LB* ≥ *UB* discards only suboptimal subtrees; MaxSAT resolution steps preserve the optimum [3][13].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**Encoding blowup.** Cardinality constraints from large cores can dwarf the original formula; totalizer and sorting-network encodings are asymptotically efficient but constant-heavy, and incremental encodings remain an active engineering frontier [4].

**Hitting-set hardness.** The MCHS subproblem in IHS is NP-hard; on instances with many small cores the IP solver becomes the bottleneck. Characterizing the core-size distributions favoring each family is open [6][7].

**Proof complexity gaps.** There are families with short IHS-style arguments but exponentially long core-guided proofs; the recent redundancy rules [14] are a first step toward closing the gap, and certified MaxSAT lags far behind certified SAT.

**Incomplete solving theory.** SLS solvers dominate anytime tracks empirically, but their clause-weighting dynamics lack the convergence theory of complete methods; the 2025 DeepDist results suggest deep differentiation may finally supply one [9].

**Incremental and dynamic MaxSAT.** Real deployments solve *sequences* of related instances; incremental IHS under changing weights [8] is promising but immature, and warm-starting core-guided search across instance edits is largely unexplored.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Maximum satisfiability has matured from a theoretical curiosity into an industrial optimization workhorse, and the journey is written in three algorithmic ideas. Core-guided search taught the field to *listen to the SAT solver's explanations*: each unsatisfiable core is a certificate of unavoidable cost [1][2][10]. Implicit hitting sets taught it *duality*: cores and hitting sets are two faces of the same optimum, and a SAT solver plus an IP solver, each doing what it does best, can find it together [5][6][7]. Branch-and-bound with MaxSAT resolution taught it *propagation*: lower bounds can be harvested from the search tree itself, with proof rules preserving cost exactly [3][13]. The MaxSAT Evaluations turned these ideas into an empirical science [9], and applications from timetabling to package upgradeability turned the science into infrastructure [12].
