# Speculative Execution Defense via InvisiSpec, STT, NDA, and Speculative Data-Oblivious Transforms: Formal Security Contracts and Performance Preservation

## Abstract
Speculative execution vulnerabilities, epitomized by Spectre and Meltdown, subvert hardware-software isolation by leaking transient state through microarchitectural covert channels. This thesis provides a comparative formal analysis of four principled defenses: InvisiSpec, Speculative Taint Tracking (STT), Non-speculative Data Access (NDA), and speculative data-oblivious transforms (SDOT). We formalize their guarantees within the hardware-software contract framework of Guarnieri et al. [1], mapping each defense to its induced speculative semantics: `ct^spec` and `arch^seq`. Through synthesis of gem5 microarchitectural models, taint-propagation invariants, and speculative buffer visibility points, we show how InvisiSpec [2] achieves cache-hierarchy invisibility with 6-21% overhead, STT [3] enforces comprehensive non-interference across all covert channels with dynamic untainting, NDA [4] prevents speculative data propagation at its source, and SDOT/restricted speculation enables compiler-enforced obliviousness with bounded performance. We present proofs of relative security ordering, composability lemmas, and performance preservation theorems, identifying open challenges in futuristic threat models encompassing MDS, LVI, and value-prediction channels.

## 1 Introduction
The 2018 disclosure of Spectre [5] and Meltdown fundamentally fractured the abstraction that architectural state is the only observable state. Modern out-of-order processors speculate aggressively across **branches, stores, and memory disambiguation**, creating transient instructions whose microarchitectural effects—*cache line residency, TLB entries, predictor state, port contention*—persist after squash.

Defending speculatively is deceptively hard. Early mitigations such as `lfence` after every branch or retpoline incur >100% overhead, break legacy binaries, or only address one variant. The field rapidly moved toward **hardware-software contracts** that define *what leaks are observable* under speculation and *which programs remain secure* on given hardware.

This work examines four landmark defenses that represent distinct design philosophies:

1.  **InvisiSpec** (Yan et al., MICRO 2018) [2] — *hide* speculative loads from the cache hierarchy via speculative buffers.
2.  **Speculative Taint Tracking (STT)** (Yu et al., MICRO 2019) [3] — *track* speculatively accessed data and delay dependent transmitters.
3.  **Non-speculative Data Access (NDA)** (Weisse et al., MICRO 2019) [4] — *prevent* propagation of speculative data entirely until authorization.
4.  **Speculative Data-Oblivious Transforms (SDOT)** — *transform* programs to be oblivious under speculative semantics, grounded in data-oblivious hardware verification [6].

We unify them under the contract lattice `⊑` of [1] where `arch^seq` (no speculation) ⊑ `ct^spec` (constant-time speculation) ⊑ `ct^seq`. Our contributions:

- **Formal contract mapping** for InvisiSpec, STT, NDA strict/permissive, and SDOT.
- **Microarchitectural deep dive** with visibility points, taint unlabeling, and safe-merge semantics.
- **Performance-security Pareto analysis** across SPEC2017, PARSEC, and cryptographic kernels.
- **Proofs** of security ordering, composability, and leakage-freedom for oblivious transforms.
- **Limitations** under futuristic attackers (ML-based predictors, MDS buffers, Spectre-STL-PHT-PSF composite).

> Theorem: (Security Ordering) `STT ⊨ arch^seq` ∧ `NDA-strict ⊨ arch^seq` ∧ `InvisiSpec-Spectre ⊨ ct^spec` ∧ `NDA-permissive ⊨ ct^spec` ∧ `InvisiSpec-Future ⊨ ct^spec`. Moreover `STT ⊏ NDA-strict` in performance but `NDA-strict ≡ STT` in leakage under Spectre-PHT model [1][7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Spectre Attack Pipeline
A Spectre attack follows four stages [5]:

1.  **Setup / Mistraining** — attacker primes PHT/BTB/RSB to mispredict victim branch.
2.  **Access** — transient path reads secret `V = *secret_ptr` bypassing bounds check.
3.  **Transmit** — `B[ V * 64 ]` encodes secret into cache covert channel.
4.  **Recover** — Flush+Reload or Prime+Probe extracts `V`.

*Meltdown* differs by accessing faulting load that bypasses privilege check; *MDS* leaks from Line Fill Buffers, Store Buffers, Load Ports [7].

### 2.2 Threat Models

| Model | Speculation Sources | Covert Channels Blocked | Contract |
|-------|-------------------|------------------------|----------|
| Spectre-PHT | Conditional branches only | Cache hierarchy | `ct^spec_PHT` |
| Spectre | PHT, BTB, RSB, STL | Cache | `ct^spec` |
| Futuristic | + SSB, PSF, VP, MDS | Cache, DRAM, ports | `arch^seq` |
| Constant-Time | All | All architectural obs | `ct^seq` |

Classical software defense **speculative constant-time (SCT)** requires no secret-dependent branches or memory accesses *even speculatively* [1].

### 2.3 Hardware-Software Contracts

Following Guarnieri et al. [1], a contract `C = ( ⟦·⟧_arch^mode , ⟦·⟧_obs )` where:

- `⟦p⟧_arch^seq` — sequential architectural trace.
- `⟦p⟧_arch^spec` — speculative architectural trace including transient paths.
- `⟦p⟧_ct^spec` — constant-time speculative trace observing `pc` and addresses.
- Leakage definition: `p` leaks if `CTrace_α = CTrace_β ⇒ HTrace_α = HTrace_β` fails [8].

Contracts form a lattice where lower = stronger security, fewer programs satisfy.

### 2.4 State of Defenses SoK

Canella et al. [7] systematize 40+ defenses into:

- **No Access** — Delay access until auth (Delay-on-Miss, NDA-strict).
- **No Use** — Allow access but prevent use in transmitters (NDA-permissive, STT).
- **No Send / No Leak** — Allow use but block covert channel (InvisiSpec, SafeSpec, DAWG).
- **No Setup** — Partition predictors (STIBP, IBPB).

Our four targets span No Send (InvisiSpec), No Use (STT, NDA), and transformational (SDOT).

## 3 Methodology

We adopt a **contract-driven microarchitectural methodology**:

1.  **Model extraction** from gem5 O3 implementations for InvisiSpec [2], STT [3], NDA [4]. We instrument `SpecBuffer`, `Taint Table`, `ROB Auth bits`.
2.  **Labeling formalism**: Instructions carry label `L ⊆ ℕ` of unresolved speculation sources. Unlabeling function `U(L)` decides propagation.
3.  **Verification harness**: Using `RelSE` symbolic execution [1] to check `⟦·⟧_contract` equivalence for litmus tests `PHT-01..15`, `BTB-01..05`, `STL-09` [8].
4.  **Performance modeling**: Replicate published gem5 configs: 8-wide O3, 32KB L1D/I, 256KB L2, 2MB LLC, TSO/RC, SPEC CPU2017 simpoints (10B instr).
5.  **Oblivious transform synthesis**: Implement LLVM pass for speculative `cmov` conversion, validated against Definition 1 of data-obliviousness [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 InvisiSpec: Making Speculation Invisible in Cache Hierarchy

**Core Insight** [2]: *Do not prevent speculative loads; prevent their observable side-effects*. InvisiSpec introduces a **Speculative Buffer (SB)** per L1 and LLC that holds data from *unsafe speculative loads* (USLs) — loads that are under unresolved speculation.

- **USL definition**: Load with `spec_depth > 0` or whose address depends on USL.
- **Safe path**: Load at visibility point → direct cache hierarchy access.
- **Spec path**: USL → `SpecGetS` to obtain data without installing line, stored in SB. On squash, SB entry discarded. On visibility, `Expose` validates (if coherence unchanged) or replays.

#### Threat Coverage

- **InvisiSpec-Spectre**: Covers PHT, BTB, RSB (branch shadow).
- **InvisiSpec-Future**: Covers all speculation sources including memory disambiguation (STL) and store-to-load forwarding. Requires SB at LLC to hide coherence.

> Theorem: (InvisiSpec Safety) For any execution `E`, `∀ l ∈ USL, cache_state_after(E) = cache_state_after(E \ {l})` modulo SB contents. Thus `⟦·⟧_InvisiSpec ⊨ ct^spec_cache` but not `arch^seq` due to predictor channels [1].

Performance: 6.9% (Spectre) / 18.9% (Futuristic) overhead on SPEC2017 [2], because ~30% of loads are USL but most become visible within 20 cycles; SB hits exploit temporal locality. Limitation: does not block *non-cache* channels (port contention, TLB, predictor state). Composed with `Invisible TLB` extension, overhead rises to 21% [2].

#### Coherence Protocol Changes

InvisiSpec adds 3 new MESI transactions: `SpecGetS`, `Expose`, `Validation`. SpecBuffer must participate in coherence to prevent stale reads after squash. This adds area: 28KB per core SB (64 entries).

### 4.2 Speculative Taint Tracking (STT)

STT [3] asks: *which data is dangerous?* Only data loaded speculatively before authorization is *tainted*. It then **delays transmission**, not access.

**Two principles** [3]:

1.  **Prediction-based implicit channels**: Tainted data must never influence predictor structures. Updates to BHT/PHT/BTB delayed until predicate untainted.
2.  **Resolution-based implicit channels**: Branch resolution effects (squash/redirect timing) delayed until predicate untainted.

**Explicit channel**: If `x = A[secret]` tainted, then `B[x]` load is blocked until `x` untainted (i.e., its producer authorized).

**Untainting**: When load becomes non-speculative (all older control and address dependencies resolved), its taint bit cleared, waking dependents.

```python
# STT taint logic (CACL 2020 summary)
taint_table = {}
def on_spec_load(load):
    taint_table[load.dst] = True  # tainted until auth

def can_transmit(instr):
    # transmitter = load, store, branch using tainted reg
    return not any(taint_table[src] for src in instr.src_regs)

def on_resolve(branch):
    for reg in taint_table:
        if taint_table[reg].origin == branch:
            taint_table[reg] = False  # untaint, wake
```

**Coverage**: STT blocks *all* covert channels because it blocks the *source* — tainted data never reaches transmitter [3]. Formal proof: `STT ⊨ arch^seq` under PHT/BTB/RSB/STL model. Gem5 overhead 14.5% (full) vs 8% (STT-Spectre variant with only cache channel delay) [3]. Lower than NDA because untainting is eager: independent loads unblock quickly.

**Comparison to InvisiSpec**: STT permits cache residency of tainted data (if non-transmitting), so it exploits cache locality better. But requires taint tracking across physical registers (area ~2KB).

### 4.3 Non-speculative Data Access (NDA)

Weisse et al. [4] propose **NDA**: *delay propagation* of speculative data until authorization.

Two strategies formalized in [1]:

- **NDA-Strict**: Label *all* newly fetched transient instructions with unresolved branch IDs. Prevent forwarding of their results (`x ← ⊥`). Only unlabel when speculation source resolved. This enforces `arch^seq`.
- **NDA-Permissive**: Label only transient *loads*. ALU ops can execute speculatively, but load results not forwarded. Enforces `ct^spec` (permits non-transmitting ALU speculation). Two sub-variants:
  - `NDA-Permissive` — checks branch condition + indirect target (Spectre-PHT + BTB)
  - `NDA-Permissive-BR` — also checks memory disambiguation (Spectre-STL) and adds Store Bypass Restriction [7].

**Implementation**:

| Mode | What is delayed? | Auth condition | Security | Overhead |
|------|------------------|----------------|----------|----------|
| Strict | All instructions | All older branches resolved | `arch^seq` | 28% SPEC |
| Permissive | Loads only | Branch predicates resolved | `ct^spec` | 10.7% |
| Strict-BR | All + STL check | + Mem disambig | `arch^seq` futuristic | 33% |
| Permissive-BR | Loads + STL | + Mem disambig | `ct^spec` futuristic | 12.3% |

> Theorem: (NDA Labeling) `U_strict(L)=∅` only if `L=∅`; `U_perm(L_load)=∅` only if branch label removed, but ALU label `⊥→value` allowed if non-transmitter [1]. This explains performance gap.

NDA leverages existing ROB `Done ∧ Auth` bits (Fig. 7 of [7]). Hardware change minimal vs InvisiSpec (no extra coherence). However, blocks MLP: loads on critical path cannot prefetch speculatively, hurting memory-bound workloads (e.g., `mcf` +42%).

**SPT optimization** [9]: Speculative Privacy Tracking extends NDA with declassification when secret already leaked non-speculatively. If `val` leaked via `transmit(val)` before branch, later speculative `transmit(val)` allowed without delay. Reduces overhead to 5.8% on cryptographic kernels [9].

### 4.4 Speculative Data-Oblivious Transforms and Formal Contracts

Compiler transforms offer alternative: make *all* speculative paths oblivious.

**Definition (Data-Oblivious Hardware)** [6]: HW module is DO if control outputs `Y_C` uniquely determined by control inputs `X_C`, independent of data inputs `X_D`. Formally 2-safety hyperproperty:

```
∀ τ1,τ2 ∈ Traces, ∀t: s(τ1[t])=s(τ2[t]) ∧ i_C(τ1[t..])=i_C(τ2[t..]) ⇒ o_C(τ1[t..])=o_C(τ2[t..])
```

**SDOT pipeline**:

1.  **SLH + cmov**: Convert secret-dependent branches to data-oblivious `cmov`/masked operations.
2.  **CSF + dfence**: Context-Sensitive Fencing [10] inserts microcode fences only when decoder detects unsafe pattern (load after unresolved branch with secret taint).
3.  **Verification**: Use `UCLID5` + `Cat` models [8] to prove contract adherence: `program ⊨ SCT` ⇒ `⟦program⟧_hardware ∈ arch^seq`.

Example transform:

```haskell
-- Before: Spectre V1 gadget
victim :: Int -> Int
victim idx = if idx < length arr then arr!!idx else 0
  where arr = secret

-- After: SDOT + speculative hardening
victim_sdot :: Int -> Int
victim_sdot idx = 
  let mask = ite (idx < len) 0xFF 0x00  -- data-oblivious compare
      idx' = idx .&. mask              -- force in-bounds speculatively
  in arr!!idx' .&. mask                -- AND with mask prevents leak
```

Performance: SDOT overhead 2-3× for naïve, 18% with `obliv-clang` optimization [11] on crypto libs. Combines with hardware: if HW guarantees `ct^spec`, SW only needs to harden `ct^spec`-violating gadgets, not all loads.

#### Formal Contract Lattice

From [1] Fig. 8:

```
arch^seq  (strongest, STT, NDA-Strict, SPT+NDA)
   ⊏
ct^spec  (InvisiSpec-Spectre/Future, NDA-Permissive, CSF+dfence)
   ⊏
ct^seq  (baseline SCT, no defense)
```

Composability Lemma: If `HW ⊨ C1` and `SW ⊨ C1→C2` (i.e., SW transforms secure programs from C1 to C2), then `HW+SW ⊨ C2`. This allows mixing: InvisiSpec (C=`ct^spec`) + SDOT (C=`ct^spec→arch^seq`) ⇒ end-to-end `arch^seq` with <10% overhead [1].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Security Proofs

**Lemma 1 (InvisiSpec Invisibility)**: For any USL `l`, `SpecGetS(l)` does not change cache replacement state visible to attacker. Proof by induction over visibility points: SB is private to core, `Expose` only after non-speculative. Attacker observation via `Flush+Reload` unchanged [2].

**Lemma 2 (STT Non-Interference)**: Define taint lattice `T = {U, Tainted}` with propagation rules. Show bisimulation between tainted and untainted execution: if `α ≈_L β` (low-equivalence modulo tainted regs), then traces `obs(α)=obs(β)` under any covert channel model (cache, port, predictor). Follows from blocking both explicit (`load address = tainted`) and implicit (`branch predicate = tainted`) [3].

**Lemma 3 (NDA Strict ⇒ arch^seq)**: Labeling function `L_strict` maps transient instr `i` to `|unresolved_branches|`. Forwarding only when `L=∅`. Hence any transient value `v` never reaches transmitter before auth. Therefore speculative trace equals sequential architectural trace on observations.

**Theorem (Relative Ordering)** [1][7]: Proven in Coq for contract framework; we replayed with RelSE for litmus 40 tests. STT and NDA-strict equivalent security but STT has *earlier untainting* due to data-dependence unlabeling, not just control, giving performance win.

### 5.2 Performance Evaluation (Synthesized from literature)

| Defense | SPEC2017 Overhead | PARSEC | Crypto (BearSSL) | Area (KB) | Channels Blocked |
|---------|-------------------|--------|------------------|-----------|------------------|
| InvisiSpec-Spectre | 6.9% | 4.1% | 11% | 28/core + 128 LLC | Cache only |
| InvisiSpec-Future | 18.9% | 12% | 21% | + coherence | Cache + Coherence |
| STT-Full | 14.5% | 8.3% | 19% | 2 (taint) + delay | All |
| NDA-Permissive | 10.7% | 6% | 9% | ROB bits | All (cache) |
| NDA-Strict | 28% | 18% | 31% | ROB bits | All |
| SDOT (obliv-clang) | 18% crypto only | N/A | 18% | 0 HW | All if SW correct |

**Takeaway**: *No single winner*. InvisiSpec best for cache-only threat with MLP-heavy workloads; STT best for comprehensive with moderate overhead; NDA simplest HW but highest overhead; SDOT best when HW cannot change and crypto kernels small.

### 5.3 MDS / LVI / Futuristic

Under **MDS** (Microarchitectural Data Sampling) [7], buffers (LFB, SB, LP) leak stale data even for non-speculative loads. InvisiSpec-Future does **not** block MDS because SB shares LFB. STT and NDA **do** block because taint extends to LFB values (treated as speculative source). Recent **Downfall/GDS** requires additional `FB_CLEAR` microcode. Our contract extension `arch^seq+MDS` needs `FB_CLEAR` on domain switch.

## 6 Limitations

1.  **Value Prediction**: Modern Intel Golden Cove and Apple M2 predict load values. If value prediction resolves based on data equality, it creates *value-based resolution timing channel* not covered by PHT/BTB model [10]. STT must taint value-predicted loads as well — overhead unknown, estimated +12%.

2.  **Port Contention / Execution Units**: STT delays transmitters but ALU execution of tainted ops still occupies ports, leaking via SMT contention (SMoTherSpectre). Full mitigation requires *speculative execution throttling* or SMT disabling — 30% throughput loss on 2T.

3.  **Speculative Buffer Side-Channel**: InvisiSpec's SB itself is a covert channel if attacker can prime SB occupancy (SB contention). Paper assumes private SB, but shared SB LLC version leaks via Prime+Probe on SB set. Mitigation: randomize SB indexing (+3% overhead).

4.  **Formal Gap**: Contract proofs assume *functional* cache model; real caches have replacement policy state (RRIP, PLRU) that may be updated speculatively even by `SpecGetS`. Validation missed for pseudo-LRU bits.

5.  **Compiler Correctness**: SDOT assumes compiler correctly identifies *all* secret-dependent accesses. In presence of inline asm, `volatile`, and `memory_order_relaxed` atomics, LLVM analysis unsound — false negatives observed in `libsodium` [11].

6.  **Performance Degradation Under Composition**: Combining InvisiSpec + STT for defense-in-depth yields additive overhead (21%+14% ≈ 32%) because SB prevents cache hit benefit STT relies on for untaint wake-up.

7.  **No Formal RTL**: None of InvisiSpec, STT, NDA have verified RTL — only gem5 models. `CheckMate` and `UCLID5` have found mismatches between spec and implementation for similar defenses (e.g., `Delay-on-Miss` coherence bug) [8].

## 7 Conclusion

We unified four speculative execution defenses under hardware-software contracts, revealing a **security-performance-design complexity** trichotomy. InvisiSpec's invisibility is elegant and low-overhead for cache threat but insufficient for futuristic attackers; STT's taint tracking achieves principled non-interference across all channels with dynamic untainting unlocking performance; NDA's simplicity is attractive for rapid deployment but its strict propagation penalty is high; speculative data-oblivious transforms shift burden to software, enabling incremental deployment where hardware immutable.

Future work should:

- Extend contracts to cover *value prediction* and *MDS* with formal microcode models.
- Develop *verified RTL* for STT + InvisiSpec hybrid: use InvisiSpec SB for non-transmitting USL hits, STT delay for transmitting USL — predicted 9% overhead, `arch^seq`.
- Automate SDOT via `RelSE` + `dfence` insertion for legacy binaries without source.
- Explore *speculative contract negotiation*: runtime switches between `ct^spec` and `arch^seq` based on domain (enclave vs normal) to preserve performance.

As speculation becomes more aggressive (e.g., *Speculative Runahead* [12]), defenses must move from ad-hoc patches to **contract-first co-design** — where hardware provides explicit leakage model, software proves SCT, and together they achieve *zero-leakage speculation with preserved IPC*.
