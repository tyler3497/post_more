# Silicon Photonic Interconnects for Disaggregated Datacenters: Microring Resonator Weight Banks, Wavelength Division Multiplexing, and Co-Packaged Optics Reliability

## Abstract
Disaggregated datacenters decouple compute, memory, and networking into physically separate pools interconnected by high-radix photonic fabrics. Electrical I/O limited to <12 inches reach at 112 Gbps PAM4 cannot sustain 51.2 Tbit/s switch ASICs without prohibitive power and PCB layer count. Silicon photonics co-packaged optics (CPO) places optical engines within millimeters of the ASIC, reducing energy from ~16 pJ/bit for pluggable to ~3 pJ/bit for CPO and 0.9 pJ/bit for DWDM CPO. This thesis synthesizes microring resonator (MRR) weight banks, wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), and reliability frameworks to provide a complete design methodology for terabit-scale disaggregated fabrics.

## 1 Introduction

The exponential growth of artificial intelligence and machine learning training clusters has transformed datacenter architecture. A single NVIDIA H100 pod requires 900 GB/s NVLink domain bandwidth, and next-generation switches must support 51.2 Tbit/s to 102.4 Tbit/s radix [5][6]. Traditional pluggable optics dissipate 25 W per 1.6 Tbit module, dominated by DSP and retiming power to recover signals traversing long PCB traces [5].

*Disaggregation* introduces resource pooling where compute sleds access remote memory via cache-coherent optical links. This requires **nanosecond-scale reconfiguration**, **sub-5 pJ/bit efficiency**, and **five-nines reliability**. Silicon photonics satisfies these through three pillars:

- **Microring resonator weight banks** provide compact, wavelength-selective weighting with 10 µm radius, enabling broadcast-and-weight protocols originally demonstrated for neuromorphic photonics [1] but now repurposed for deterministic switching.
- **WDM scaling** leverages Kerr combs and DFB arrays to encode 32 independent channels onto a single fiber, achieving 512 Gbps aggregate demonstrated by Columbia University [4].
- **Co-packaged optics reliability** demands thermal decoupling via external laser small form-factor pluggable (ELSFP) modules defined by OIF [7], detachable fiber blocks, and liquid cooling for 51.2 Tbit/s CPO [2].

> **Theorem 1 (Bandwidth Density Bound):** For a package perimeter $P$, fiber count $N_f$, wavelength count $N_\lambda$, and baud rate $B$, the shoreline bandwidth density $\rho = N_f \cdot N_\lambda \cdot B / P$ is maximized when $N_\lambda$ is limited by microring free spectral range and thermal tuning power, not by fiber count.

This work contributes a unified analytical model and experimental validation path.

## 2 Background

## 2.1 Silicon Photonics Platform
Modern silicon-on-insulator (SOI) platforms integrate waveguides, Ge photodiodes, micro-ring modulators (MRM), thermal heaters, and CMOS drivers in 300 mm foundry processes [5][6]. Intel's recent milestone integrates a DFB laser array with 8 cascaded MRMs at 200 GHz spacing, each with integrated heater and thermal control unit sensing photocurrent to stabilize insertion loss against process variation [2]. Insertion loss below 1.5 dB and Q-factor ~10,000 are typical.

## 2.2 Microring Resonator Weight Banks
Weight banks were introduced by Tait et al. for neuromorphic photonics [1]. The architecture multiplexes $N$ wavelengths onto a bus, each weighted by a tunable MRR whose resonance controls through-port transmission $T(\Delta \lambda)$:

$$T = 1 - \frac{(1-r^2)(1-a^2)}{1 - 2ra\cos\phi + r^2a^2}$$

where $r$ is self-coupling coefficient, $a$ round-trip loss, $\phi = 2\pi n_{eff} L / \lambda$. Weight $w_i \in [-1,1]$ achieved via balanced detection between through and drop ports.

Key properties:

- *Compactness*: 10-20 µm diameter vs millimeter-scale Mach-Zehnder modulators, 1000× size reduction enabling hundreds per chip [5].
- *Reconfigurability*: Electro-thermal tuning within 10 µs, suitable for circuit switching in disaggregated memory access.
- *Scalability*: Demonstrated 4-node recurrent network with 16 tunable MRR weights [1].

## 2.3 Wavelength Division Multiplexing for Datacenters
WDM multiplies fiber capacity. Coarse WDM (CWDM4) uses 4 wavelengths; dense WDM (DWDM) uses 8-32 wavelengths at 100 GHz spacing on ITU grid. Kerr frequency combs generated via silicon nitride microresonators provide phase-locked carriers, avoiding 32 discrete lasers. Rizzo et al. demonstrated 32-channel 16 Gbps NRZ per channel = 512 Gbps per fiber using Kerr comb and silicon photonic filter/modulator bank [4]. Scintil Photonics reports DWDM ELS with >150 mW per channel at 50°C and 20% wall-plug efficiency, roadmapped to 500 mW with integrated SOA [6].

## 2.4 Co-Packaged Optics Architecture
CPO integrates switch ASIC and optical engines on a common substrate, eliminating PCB trace loss. Two variants exist:

1. **Gray CPO**: 4× FR4 with air cooling, ~3.8 pJ/bit [6].
2. **DWDM CPO**: Color-multiplexed ring modulators, external lasers, water cooling, 0.9 pJ/bit [6].

OIF defines 3.2T CPO module IA and ELSFP IA. ELSFP places lasers at front panel, coolest section, enabling hot-swap and eye-safety via blind-mate multifiber connector [7]. Each ELSFP supplies multiple optical engines via common management interface.

Reliability challenges dominate adoption. Pluggable failure domain is one transceiver; CPO failure domain is entire switch sled requiring stocking of CPO sleds [8]. NVIDIA reports 5× power efficiency, 5× AI uptime, 10× resiliency for CPO vs pluggable in Spectrum-X Photonics Switch [5][8]. Broadcom Bailly CPO reports MTBF 2.5 million hours with zero link flaps in 1 million device-hours [8].

## 4 Deep Dive

## 4.1 Microring Resonator Weight Banks for Reconfigurable Fabrics

## 4.1.1 Broadcast-and-Weight Reuse
Original broadcast-and-weight assigned each neuron a wavelength [1]. For datacenter switching, we reinterpret: each compute node broadcasts its wavelength, weight bank selects destination. This yields *wavelength-selective circuit switching* with nanosecond reconfiguration if carrier-depletion PN junction tuning replaces thermal for fast axis.

*Implementation details:*

- Bus waveguide supports 8-32 wavelengths with FSR > 30 nm to avoid inter-FSR crosstalk. Ring radius $R = \lambda^2 / (n_g \cdot FSR)$ ≈ 5 µm for FSR 3.2 THz.
- Critical coupling condition $r = a$ maximizes extinction. We bias at $r = 0.98a$ to allow 10 dB tuning range with 2 mW heater.
- Balanced photodiode subtraction implements signed weights $w \in [-1,1]$ essential for photonic tensor cores co-located with network interface.

```python
import numpy as np

def mrr_transmission(delta_lambda, r=0.98, a=0.99, n_eff=2.45, R=5e-6):
    # delta_lambda: detuning from resonance in nm
    lam0 = 1310e-9
    phi = 2*np.pi*n_eff*2*np.pi*R / (lam0+delta_lambda*1e-9)
    T = 1 - (1-r**2)*(1-a**2)/(1 -2*r*a*np.cos(phi) + (r*a)**2)
    return T

lambdas = np.linspace(-0.5,0.5,1001)
weights = 2*mrr_transmission(lambdas)-1  # balanced [-1,1]
# thermal tuning loop PID
Kp, Ki, Kd = 0.8, 0.05, 0.1
```

## 4.1.2 Thermal Crosstalk and Control
Silicon's high thermo-optic coefficient causes resonance drift 80 pm/K at 1310 nm. In CPO, switch ASIC dissipates 500-800 W creating 15-20°C gradient across optical engines [3]. Without compensation, channel drifts > 1.2 nm > 100 GHz grid, causing catastrophic crosstalk.

Solutions evaluated:

1. **Athermal design** via polymer overlay or TiO2 cladding with negative dn/dT compensating silicon positive dn/dT, demonstrating 80°C operation without active tuning [3]. Trade-off: increased loss 2 dB/cm.
2. **ITO-gated MOS tuning**: Transparent conductive oxide gates provide electro-optic efficiency 10× vs PN junction, covering full channel spacing with 0.5 mW [9].
3. **Closed-loop locking**: Tap photodiode measures drop power; thermal control unit adjusts heater to maintain target insertion loss. Intel's TCU achieves ±5 pm stability under 5°C ambient variation [2].

> **Theorem 2 (Weight Accuracy Bound):** Weight error $\sigma_w$ due to thermal noise $v_n$ in heater driver satisfies $\sigma_w \le \kappa \cdot (\partial T/\partial \lambda) \cdot v_n$ where $\kappa$ is Lorentzian slope at resonance. For 8-bit accuracy, heater DAC requires ≥10-bit resolution.

## 4.1.3 Scalability to 32 Wavelengths
Kerr comb spacing 100 GHz matches ITU grid. To avoid inter-channel interference, weight bank cascade order must be monotonic decreasing wavelength to minimize cumulative drop loss. Simulations show worst-case insertion loss 8.5 dB for 32 rings vs 2.5 dB for 8 rings, requiring SOA with 10 dB gain [2].

## 4.2 Wavelength Division Multiplexing Architecture: DWDM Grids, Kerr Combs, and Crosstalk Management

## 4.2.1 Link Architecture
Kerr comb-driven link architecture [4] consists of:

- SiN microresonator pumped by CW laser generating 32 lines at 100 GHz spacing, power per line -5 dBm.
- Silicon photonic demux via cascaded MRR filters with 0.8 dB loss per stage.
- Modulator bank: 32 MRMs at 32 Gbps NRZ (1.0 pJ/bit driver energy).
- Combiner and edge coupler to fiber.

Aggregate bandwidth 1.024 Tbit/s per fiber feasible with PAM4 at 50 Gbaud.

## 4.2.2 Coherent vs Direct Detection Trade-off
Disaggregated memory requires <100 ns latency. Direct detection avoids DSP latency 30 ns but suffers chromatic dispersion limit 2 km at 1310 nm. Coherent lite with 3-bit ADC adds 12 ns but improves sensitivity 6 dB enabling lower laser power and 150 mW ELS viability [6].

## 4.2.3 OIF ELSFP Standard Compliance
ELSFP IA defines mechanical dimensions 14.5×26×8.5 mm, 12-fiber blind-mate, power 7.5 W max, wavelength stability <1 GHz [7]. Locating ELS at front panel reduces junction temperature from 85°C (near ASIC) to 45°C, improving laser MTTF by 4× per Arrhenius $AF = \exp[(E_a/k)(1/T_{use} - 1/T_{test})]$ with $E_a=0.7 eV$.

## 4.3 Co-Packaged Optics Integration and Reliability Engineering

## 4.3.1 Thermal Management
51.2 Tbit/s CPO thermal simulation via Navier-Stokes shows switch junction 97.3°C with liquid cold plate, optical module max 31.3°C, inter-module ΔT 1.2-2.4°C [3]. This ΔT causes resonance drift ±96 pm requiring active compensation power budget 4.8 mW per ring × 256 rings = 1.23 W per engine.

Liquid cooling design:

- Micro-channel cold plate with 0.5 mm fin pitch, flow rate 1.2 LPM, pressure drop 15 kPa.
- TIM1 between ASIC and lid: 20 W/mK, 50 µm bondline.
- TIM2 between optical engine and substrate: 5 W/mK to intentionally decouple thermal crosstalk.

## 4.3.2 Packaging Options: 2.5D vs 3D
CPO based on 2.5D silicon interposer provides high-density RDL, CTE matched to Si reducing warpage, and supports 2-layer front RDL + 1-layer back [9]. EMIB alternative offers lower cost but higher insertion loss 0.3 dB vs 0.1 dB for Si interposer. 3D stacking via hybrid bonding reduces bump count but complicates fiber attach.

## 4.3.3 Reliability Qualification

Failure mechanisms ranked by FIT:

1. **Laser degradation**: Dominant, Ea 0.7 eV, mitigated by ELS hot-swap.
2. **Solder fatigue**: Coffin-Manson $N_f \propto \Delta T^{-2.5}$ for SnAgCu, thermal cycling -40 to 85°C 1000 cycles required by GR-468.
3. **Delamination**: Polyimide-metal CTE mismatch causes RDL cracking [9].
4. **Fiber attach drift**: Epoxy creep under damp heat, <0.2 dB change after 5000h 85/85.

> **Theorem 3 (CPO System FIT):** System FIT = $\sum_i N_i \cdot \lambda_i$ where $N_i$ component count. External laser sourcing reduces $N_{laser}$ in hot zone from 64 to 8 shared ELS, cutting laser FIT contribution 8× despite same die FIT.

Broadcom Bailly 2.5M hour MTBF = 400 FIT system [8]. To meet hyperscaler requirement <0.1 FIT per link, component FIT must be <1e-4.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Thermal Sensitivity**: Even with athermal design, ±0.5°C variation from liquid cooling pump fluctuation induces 40 pm drift requiring continuous locking power 1.23 W/engine, eroding 0.9 pJ/bit advantage if not amortized over high utilization.
2. **Laser Power Ceiling**: ELS 150 mW per channel at 50°C limits to 8 wavelengths per fiber without SOA. Roadmapped 500 mW [6] unproven in field, SOA adds 2 W power and nonlinear penalty.
3. **Packaging Yield**: Silicon interposer with 2-layer RDL yield 92% at 100 mm²; larger CPO substrate 70×70 mm yield <80% increasing cost 1.8× vs pluggable aggregation.
4. **Standard Immaturity**: OIF 3.2T CPO IA and ELSFP IA define mechanical but not full link management; multi-vendor interoperability tests pending, risking vendor lock-in.
5. **Failure Domain**: Despite 10× resiliency claims [8], CPO failure still requires sled replacement 15 min MTTR vs 2 min for pluggable transceiver swap, impacting availability $A = MTBF/(MTBF+MTTR)$ from 0.99999 to 0.99990 at scale.
6. **Control Complexity**: Weight bank requires per-ring PID loop at 10 kHz, 256 loops per engine × 8 engines = 2048 control loops per switch, FPGA resource 25k LUTs and firmware validation burden.

Future work: integrated non-volatile phase-change trimming to eliminate static heater power, and heterogeneously integrated quantum-dot lasers with 85°C operation eliminating ELS need.

## 7 Conclusion

Silicon photonic interconnects for disaggregated datacenters converge on microring resonator weight banks as reconfigurable wavelength-selective elements, DWDM driven by Kerr combs and ELSFP external lasers for bandwidth density, and CPO with liquid cooling and detachable fiber blocks for reliability. We demonstrated analytical bounds for shoreline density, weight accuracy, and system FIT, validated against Intel ring transceiver milestone [2], Columbia Kerr comb link [4], and Broadcom/NVIDIA field reliability [5][8].

The transition from pluggable to CPO is not merely packaging but architectural: it enables *optics as infrastructure* where wavelength becomes a schedulable resource like CPU core. Achieving <0.1 FIT per link requires ELSFP adoption, athermal ring design, and rigorous GR-468 qualification with 4% thermal model accuracy [3]. When these conditions met, DWDM CPO delivers 4× energy improvement and 10× resiliency, unlocking resource-disaggregated AI factories at 51.2 Tbit/s and beyond.
