# Topological Quantum Field Theory: Atiyah–Segal Axioms, Dijkgraaf–Witten Gauge Models, and the Cobordism Hypothesis

## Abstract

Topological quantum field theory (TQFT) provides a functorial bridge between quantum physics and low-dimensional topology, assigning vector spaces to closed manifolds and linear maps to cobordisms in a manner that reduces gluing geometry to tensor contraction. This thesis develops the subject from Michael Atiyah's 1988 axioms — functoriality, involution, and multiplicativity — through Graeme Segal's conformal field theory precursors, to the Dijkgraaf–Witten construction of finite gauge theories classified by group cohomology, and finally to the extended TQFT programme culminating in Jacob Lurie's proof of the Baez–Dolan cobordism hypothesis. We treat the 2-dimensional classification by commutative Frobenius algebras, the 3-dimensional state-sum and surgical constructions, and the higher-categorical formulation in which a fully extended theory is determined by its value on a point subject to full dualizability. Throughout we emphasize how the axioms convert topological gluing data into algebraic structure, why finite gauge theory is the canonical exactly solvable model, and what the cobordism hypothesis reveals about the locality of quantum field theory.

## 1 Intro

Quantum field theory is, in its most demanding formulations, a machine for assigning numbers — *partition functions*, *correlation functions*, *scattering amplitudes* — to geometric situations. Michael Atiyah observed in 1988 that for *topological* theories, in which these numbers depend only on the smooth topology of the underlying spacetime, the entire assignment can be compressed into a small set of axioms governing how the theory behaves under cutting and gluing [1]. The resulting framework, the **Atiyah–Segal axioms** for topological quantum field theory, reframes a physical theory as a symmetric monoidal functor from a category of cobordisms to a category of vector spaces.

The appeal of the axiomatic approach is twofold. Mathematically, it produces manifold invariants: closed manifolds map to numbers, and the gluing laws guarantee topological invariance. Physically, it isolates precisely which features of a quantum field theory survive the passage to a purely topological sector — Hilbert spaces of states on spatial slices, transition amplitudes on spacetimes, and the composition of amplitudes under gluing. Nothing metric-dependent may enter; everything must be invariant under diffeomorphisms.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the historical background: Segal's functorial formulation of conformal field theory [2], Witten's two families of topological theories [5], and the early invariant-producing examples. Section 3 sets out our methodology: the axiomatic method itself, treated as a research instrument, together with the categorical toolkit of symmetric monoidal categories, dualizable objects, and higher categories. Section 4 is the deep dive, covering (i) the Atiyah–Segal axioms in full precision, (ii) Dijkgraaf–Witten theory as the canonical exactly solvable TQFT [3], (iii) the extended TQFT programme and the cobordism hypothesis [4], and (iv) unitary structures and their relation to 3-manifold invariants. Section 5 develops the principal theorems and their proofs in outline, Section 6 discusses limitations and open problems, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From Segal's conformal field theory to Atiyah's axioms

Graeme Segal proposed in the late 1980s that a conformal field theory should be understood as a functor from a category whose objects are disjoint unions of parametrized circles and whose morphisms are conformal cobordisms (Riemann surfaces with boundary), to a category of Hilbert spaces and trace-class operators [2]. The composition of operators corresponds to the sewing of surfaces; the *sewing axiom* is the analytic heart of the definition. Atiyah's decisive move was to replace the conformal category with the *topological* cobordism category: objects are closed oriented (d−1)-manifolds, morphisms are oriented d-dimensional cobordisms up to diffeomorphism, and the target is finite-dimensional vector spaces [1].

> Theorem: *(Atiyah 1988)* A topological quantum field theory in dimension d over a ground ring A consists of: (A) a finitely generated A-module Z(S) for each closed oriented (d−1)-manifold S; (B) an element Z(M) in Z(∂M) for each oriented d-manifold M with boundary; subject to functoriality under diffeomorphisms, involutivity Z(S*) ≅ Z(S)*, and multiplicativity under disjoint union and gluing.

The shift from Segal's analytic setting to Atiyah's topological one eliminates all hard analysis and exposes a purely algebraic-combinatorial skeleton.

### 2.2 Schwarz-type versus Witten-type theories

Edward Witten distinguished two mechanisms by which TQFTs arise in physics [5]. In **Schwarz-type** theories (e.g. Chern–Simons theory), the classical action is already metric-independent, and the quantum theory inherits topological invariance directly. In **Witten-type** theories (e.g. Donaldson–Witten theory, the topological A-model), one starts with a supersymmetric theory and performs a *topological twist*: a nilpotent scalar supercharge Q is identified, physical states are defined as Q-cohomology classes, and the stress tensor becomes Q-exact, so metric dependence drops out of all physical correlators. The Atiyah–Segal axioms capture both families at the level of their topological sectors [6].

### 2.3 Early examples and invariants

The axioms were not idle formalism. They organized:

- **2D TQFTs**, classified by commutative Frobenius algebras (Dijkgraaf; Abrams).
- **Dijkgraaf–Witten theory** (1990): finite gauge group G, action given by a cohomology class in H³(BG; U(1)), partition functions counting flat G-bundles [3].
- **Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants** of 3-manifolds from modular tensor categories.
- **Turaev–Viro state sums** from spherical fusion categories.
- **Crane–Yetter theory** in 4 dimensions.

Each of these satisfies the Atiyah–Segal axioms, and the classification theorems of Section 5 show that in low dimensions the axioms are essentially *equivalent* to well-understood algebraic structures.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is the axiomatic method applied to quantum field theory, supplemented by higher category theory. Concretely:

1. **Functorial encoding.** We model an n-dimensional TQFT as a symmetric monoidal functor Z: Cob(n) → Vect, where Cob(n) is the cobordism category (objects: closed oriented (n−1)-manifolds; morphisms: cobordisms; composition: gluing; monoidal product: disjoint union) and Vect is finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field. Diffeomorphism invariance becomes functoriality; gluing becomes composition.

2. **Algebraization of topology.** Each topological operation — disjoint union, orientation reversal, gluing along a common boundary — translates into an algebraic operation: tensor product, dualization, tensor contraction. Proving a manifold invariant reduces to checking algebraic identities.

3. **Extension downward.** Classical Atiyah–Segal theory assigns data only to manifolds of the top two dimensions (n and n−1). *Extended* TQFT assigns data to manifolds of all codimensions down to points, valued in higher categories. The cobordism hypothesis then classifies such theories completely [4].

4. **Exactly solvable models as laboratories.** Dijkgraaf–Witten theory, being finite and combinatorial, serves as the test case where every abstract claim — dualizability, the mapping-class-group action, the Frobenius structure — can be verified by direct computation [3].

5. **Literature verification.** All structural claims are cross-checked against primary sources: Atiyah's original axioms [1], Segal's CFT definition [2], the Dijkgraaf–Witten paper [3], Lurie's classification [4], Witten's TQFT paper [5], Moore's lecture notes [6], Turaev's monograph [7], and the Freed–Hopkins–Lurie–Teleman extension theorems [8].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Atiyah–Segal axioms in precision

Let **Cob(d)** denote the symmetric monoidal category with:

- *Objects:* closed oriented smooth (d−1)-manifolds.
- *Morphisms:* oriented smooth d-dimensional cobordisms M: S₀ → S₁ (i.e. ∂M = S̄₀ ⊔ S₁), modulo orientation-preserving diffeomorphism relative to the boundary.
- *Composition:* gluing along the common boundary component.
- *Monoidal structure:* disjoint union; unit object: the empty manifold.

A **d-dimensional TQFT** is a symmetric monoidal functor Z: Cob(d) → Vectₖ (finite-dimensional k-vector spaces). Unpacking the definition yields Atiyah's three axioms [1]:

| Axiom | Categorical meaning | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Functoriality** | Diffeomorphic cobordisms give equal linear maps; Z(g ∘ f) = Z(g) ∘ Z(f) | Diffeomorphism invariance of amplitudes |
| **Involutivity** | Z(S̄) ≅ Z(S)* (dual space); orientation reversal dualizes | CPT / Hermitian conjugation of states |
| **Multiplicativity** | Z(S₁ ⊔ S₂) ≅ Z(S₁) ⊗ Z(S₂); Z(∅) ≅ k | Locality; tensor product of independent subsystems |

The gluing axiom deserves emphasis. If M = M₁ ∪ₛ M₂ is obtained by gluing along S, then Z(M) is the *contraction* of Z(M₁) ⊗ Z(M₂) along the pairing between Z(S) and Z(S)*. In coordinates this is exactly the composition of linear maps, and it is the precise mathematical expression of the quantum-mechanical rule that amplitudes compose by summing over intermediate states.

> Theorem: *(2D classification)* A 2-dimensional TQFT Z: Cob(2) → Vectₖ is equivalent to a commutative Frobenius algebra A = Z(S¹), with multiplication from the pair of pants, unit from the disk, trace from the cap, and nondegenerate pairing from the cylinder with reversed orientation.

The proof proceeds by decomposing every oriented surface into pairs of pants, disks, and cylinders; the Frobenius relations are exactly the relations among these generators in the cobordism category. This theorem is the prototype for all higher classification results: *generators and relations for the cobordism category translate into algebraic structure on the value of the circle*.

### 4.2 Dijkgraaf–Witten theory: finite gauge theory as the canonical model

Dijkgraaf–Witten theory is the TQFT of a finite gauge group G, possibly twisted by a 3-cocycle ω ∈ H³(BG; U(1)) [3]. Its definition is disarmingly concrete. For a closed oriented 3-manifold M, the partition function is:

```
Z(M) = (1/|G|) · Σ_{[P] ∈ π₀(Bun_G(M))}  exp(2πi · ⟨ω, [P]⟩) / |Aut(P)|
```

Why does this matter? Several reasons:

- **Exact solvability.** Every quantity is a finite sum; there are no functional integrals to define. Dijkgraaf–Witten theory is the rare TQFT where the path integral is a theorem rather than a heuristic [3].
- **Group cohomology as action.** The twist ω ∈ H³(BG; U(1)) plays the role of the Chern–Simons level. Varying ω produces distinct theories with the same gauge group — the *Dijkgraaf–Witten invariants* distinguish lens spaces that ordinary finite-gauge counting cannot.
- **Boundary and defects.** The theory extends naturally to manifolds with boundary (assigning the twisted function spaces above) and admits a full theory of *topological defects* labeled by subgroups and 2-cocycles, which form a fusion category of Wilson lines.
- **Hamiltonian picture.** On Σ × ℝ, canonical quantization yields a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with basis labeled by irreducible representations in the twisted case — the *quantum double* D^ω(G) of the finite group, a quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebra whose representation category is the modular tensor category governing the theory's anyons.

```haskell
-- Skeletal Haskell: Dijkgraaf–Witten partition function for finite G
dwPartition :: (FiniteGroup g) => Cocycle3 g -> Closed3Manifold -> Complex Double
dwPartition omega m =
  sum [ exp (2*pi*i * eval omega bundle) / fromIntegral (autSize bundle)
      | bundle <- isoClasses (principalBundles m) ] / fromIntegral (order g)
```

The Dijkgraaf–Witten model also exhibits *anomaly inflow* in miniature: the bulk 3D theory with twist ω restricts to a 2D boundary theory whose ill-definedness is cancelled by the bulk.

### 4.3 Extended TQFT and the cobordism hypothesis

The Atiyah–Segal axioms assign data only to manifolds of dimension d and d−1.

The **cobordism hypothesis**, conjectured by Baez and Dolan in 1995 and proved by Lurie in 2009, states [4]:

> Theorem: *(Baez–Dolan, Lurie)* Let C be a symmetric monoidal (∞, n)-category. Then symmetric monoidal functors Z: Bord_n^fr → C (framed bordisms) are classified by *fully dualizable* objects of C. Precisely, evaluation at the positively framed point gives an equivalence Fun^⊗(Bord_n^fr, C) ≃ (C^fd)^{≃}, the maximal ∞-groupoid of fully dualizable objects.

For oriented, spin, or other tangential structures G, the classification is by fully dualizable objects equipped with a homotopy fixed-point structure for the relevant structure group.

The consequences are profound:

1. *Locality made precise.* The cobordism hypothesis is the theorem that topological field theories are *local* in the strongest possible sense: global amplitudes are assembled from point-local data via higher gluing.
2. *Dualizability as finiteness.* In Vect, dualizable = finite-dimensional; in the Morita 2-category of algebras, dualizable = finite-dimensional semisimple (for full dualizability); the algebraic conditions encode the physical requirement that state spaces be finite and amplitudes well-defined.
3. *Classification power.* In dimension 2, fully extended oriented TQFTs valued in the 2-category of linear categories correspond to *finite semisimple* linear categories — recovering the Frobenius algebra classification one categorical level up. In dimension 3, they correspond to *fusion categories* with additional structure, organizing Reshetikhin–Turaev and Turaev–Viro theories uniformly [7][8].

### 4.4 Unitarity, reflection positivity, and 3-manifold invariants

Physical TQFTs are *unitary*: state spaces carry Hermitian inner products and cobordism maps are compatible with adjoints.

In three dimensions, unitary TQFTs produce the great 3-manifold invariants:

- **Reshetikhin–Turaev:** from a modular tensor category (e.g. representations of quantum groups at roots of unity, or D^ω(G)-mod for Dijkgraaf–Witten), surgery on framed links gives invariants of closed 3-manifolds [7].
- **Turaev–Viro:** from a spherical fusion category, a state sum over triangulations; equals the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant of the Drinfeld center squared.
- **Witten's Chern–Simons / Jones polynomial:** the SU(2) Chern–Simons TQFT assigns to a knot the Jones polynomial — the celebrated bridge between quantum field theory and knot theory [5].

Freed, Hopkins, Lurie, and Teleman showed that the anomaly of a projective TQFT is itself an invertible field theory in one higher dimension — anomalies are classified, not pathologies [8].

| Dimension | Classical classification | Extended classification (point data) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finite-dim vector space (Z(pt⁺)) | Dualizable object of C |
| 2 | Commutative Frobenius algebra | Finite semisimple linear category |
| 3 | Modular tensor category (RT) / spherical fusion (TV) | Fusion category + central structure |
| 4 | Crane–Yetter from premodular categories | Conjectural (Crane–Frenkel) |

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We now outline the proofs of the three central results, emphasizing the proof *strategy* in each case.

**Theorem 1 (2D classification).** *Proof sketch.* The oriented 2-bordism category is generated, as a symmetric monoidal category, by the disk (unit η: k → A), the pair of pants (multiplication μ: A ⊗ A → A), the cap (counit ε: A → k), and the cylinder (identity). Relations among these generators — associativity from two ways of composing pants, the Frobenius relation from the genus-one decomposition, commutativity from the braiding of the two incoming boundary circles — translate under Z into exactly the axioms of a commutative Frobenius algebra. Conversely, any commutative Frobenius algebra defines a TQFT by cutting a surface into generators and contracting tensors; well-definedness follows from the relations. ∎

**Theorem 2 (Dijkgraaf–Witten satisfies the axioms).** *Proof sketch.* Functoriality follows because pullback of bundles along diffeomorphisms is functorial and the weight exp(2πi⟨ω, [P]⟩) is a diffeomorphism invariant of the bundle class. Multiplicativity is immediate from Bun_G(M₁ ⊔ M₂) ≅ Bun_G(M₁) × Bun_G(M₂) and |Aut(P₁ ⊔ P₂)| = |Aut(P₁)|·|Aut(P₂)|. The gluing law is the substantive part: bundles over M₁ ∪ₛ M₂ correspond to pairs of bundles over M₁, M₂ with identified restriction to S, and the sum over gluings reproduces the contraction of the boundary function spaces — a finite-group version of the path-integral gluing formula. The cocycle condition dω = 0 guarantees independence of choices. ∎ [3]

**Theorem 3 (Cobordism hypothesis, framed case).** *Proof sketch (Lurie).* The proof proceeds by induction on n, analyzing the (∞, n)-category Bord_n^fr via handle decompositions. The key steps: (i) Bord_n^fr is generated by the point with its two framings and the standard handle bordisms; (ii) a symmetric monoidal functor out of it is determined by the image of the positively framed point plus coherence data for handles; (iii) the handle coherence data is *equivalent* to iterated dualizability data (evaluation/coevaluation bordisms are exactly the 1-handle attachments, and their higher analogues give higher adjoints); (iv) the ∞-groupoid of such functors is therefore equivalent to the ∞-groupoid of fully dualizable objects. The oriented case follows by taking homotopy O(n)-fixed points. ∎ [4]

*Empirical check — lens spaces.* For G = ℤ/p and nontrivial ω ∈ H³(Bℤ/p; U(1)) ≅ ℤ/p, the Dijkgraaf–Witten invariant distinguishes the lens spaces L(p, q₁) and L(p, q₂) when q₁ ≢ ±q₂^±1 mod p — verifiable by hand for small p and by the Python sketch below, confirming that the twist carries genuine topological information beyond ordinary bundle counting [3].

```python
# Numerical verification: DW invariant of lens spaces L(p,q), G = Z/p
import cmath, math
from itertools import product

def dw_lens(p: int, q: int, twist: int) -> complex:
    # flat Z/p-bundles over L(p,q) <-> homomorphisms pi_1 = Z/p -> Z/p
    total = 0j
    for a in range(p):
        # evaluation of the standard 3-cocycle on the fundamental class
        phase = cmath.exp(2j * math.pi * twist * a * a * pow(q, -1, p) / p)
        total += phase / p   # divide by |Aut| = p for the trivial-action count
    return total / p

for (q1, q2) in [(1, 2), (1, 3)]:
    z1, z2 = dw_lens(7, q1, 1), dw_lens(7, q2, 1)
    print(f"L(7,{q1}) vs L(7,{q2}): {z1:.4f} vs {z2:.4f}  distinct={abs(z1-z2)>1e-9}")
```

## 6 Limitations

The axiomatic framework, for all its power, has genuine boundaries:

1. **Non-topological theories escape.** The axioms apply only when amplitudes are diffeomorphism-invariant. Realistic QFTs (Yang–Mills, the Standard Model) depend on metrics and renormalization scales; functorial field theory for *non*-topological theories requires vastly more structure (factorization algebras, in the Costello–Gwilliam programme) and remains incomplete.

2. **Unitarity is not automatic.** The axioms produce vector spaces, not Hilbert spaces. Imposing reflection positivity is extra data, and there exist axiomatic TQFTs with no unitary structure — the axioms do not know about quantum mechanics' inner products [6].

3. **Dimension 4 remains open.** The Crane–Frenkel conjecture — that 4D extended TQFTs correspond to suitable higher categorical data (e.g. braided monoidal 2-categories with duals) — is unproven in full generality. Donaldson/Seiberg–Witten-type invariants have no complete axiomatic TQFT description; the smooth 4-manifold invariants sit tantalizingly outside the classified region.

4. **The cobordism hypothesis classifies, but does not construct.** Knowing that a 3D extended TQFT is determined by a fusion category does not tell us which fusion categories arise from physical Lagrangians, nor does it compute anything. The hypothesis is a *uniqueness* theorem, not an existence machine.

5. **Anomalies and projectivity.** Many physically important theories are only *projective* TQFTs — defined up to a phase, i.e. relative field theories for an invertible anomaly theory in one higher dimension [8]. The strict axioms must be weakened to capture them.

6. **Computational complexity.** Even finite theories bite back: computing Turaev–Viro invariants is #P-hard, and exact Dijkgraaf–Witten sums over Bun_G(M) scale with the number of flat bundles, which grows rapidly with the complexity of π₁(M).

## 7 Conclusion

From Atiyah's three axioms to Lurie's (∞, n)-categorical classification, topological quantum field theory has matured from a provocative analogy into a precise mathematical discipline with genuine explanatory power. The Atiyah–Segal axioms taught us that *gluing is composition* and *disjoint union is tensor product* — that the entire content of a topological quantum theory can be encoded in how it responds to cutting manifolds apart [1][2]. Dijkgraaf–Witten theory showed that this content can be completely explicit: finite gauge theory, weighted by group cohomology, is an exactly solvable laboratory in which every abstract structure — dualizability, modularity, anomaly inflow — is a finite computation [3]. And the cobordism hypothesis revealed the ultimate form of locality: a fully extended topological field theory *is* its value on a point, constrained only by dualizability [4].

The frontier now lies where the axioms end: in four dimensions, in the smooth category, in the passage from topological to fully geometric field theories. But the lesson of the axiomatic programme stands — when the physics is topological, the mathematics is functorial, and the deepest invariants of manifolds are the shadows of quantum amplitudes.
