# Sketching the Genome: A Mathematical Treatise on MinHash, Minimizer Schemes, and Sublinear-Time Sequence Comparison

## Abstract

The exponential growth of nucleotide sequence repositories has rendered exhaustive pairwise alignment intractable for many large-scale tasks. Compressive genomics addresses this through *sketching*: compact, randomized summaries of *k*-mer sets from which global similarity measures are recovered in time independent of sequence length. This treatise develops the mathematics of two dominant sketching families, **MinHash** and **minimizer schemes**, from first principles. We prove Broder's identity relating min-hash collisions to the Jaccard coefficient, derive the variance of bottom-*s* estimators, and reconstruct the Mash mutation-distance transform that converts sketch Jaccard into per-site divergence. We analyze minimizer density, the window guarantee, and the modern landscape of FracMinHash debiasing, HyperLogLog cardinality sketches, and syncmer alternatives. Reproducible simulations confirm the theoretical error rates and recover known mutation distances from sketches alone. We close with the known failure modes: the provable bias of minimizer Jaccard estimators, repeat-dominated pathologies, and error sensitivity in raw-read sketches.

---

## 1. Introduction

A modern sequencing archive contains *petabases* of nucleotide data [4], and the number of reference genomes in public databases doubles on timescales far shorter than the improvement of exact alignment algorithms. Pairwise global alignment of two sequences of lengths *n* and *m* costs *O(nm)* time in the worst case; for *N* genomes, all-pairs comparison is *quadratic* in both sequence length and database cardinality. For many applications, however — species identification, contamination detection, metagenomic profiling, phylogenetic triage, database deduplication — a *global, approximate* distance is sufficient, and a fraction of a percent of error is a price gladly paid for orders of magnitude in speed.

**Compressive genomics** is the study of principled ways to pay that price. A *sketch* is a small, randomized summary *Sk(S)* of a sequence *S* satisfying two properties:

1. **Compactness:** |*Sk(S)*| is fixed or grows slowly (logarithmically) in |*S*|.
2. **Estimability:** some biologically meaningful similarity *d(S, T)* is recoverable from *Sk(S)* and *Sk(T)* in time independent of |*S*| and |*T*|.

Two families of sketches dominate contemporary practice. *MinHash* sketches, imported from document fingerprinting [1] and adapted to genomics by Ondov *et al.* [3], estimate Jaccard similarity of *k*-mer sets and, through a mutation model, evolutionary distance. *Minimizer* schemes, introduced by Roberts *et al.* [2] to compress the seed catalog for overlap computation, select a positionally-consistent subsample of *k*-mers with a deterministic *window guarantee*. Both rest on surprisingly simple probabilistic arguments, and both have spawned a rich ecosystem: FracMinHash [4, 6], HyperLogLog-based distances [5], syncmers, strobemers, and universal hitting sets [8].

This treatise is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews *k*-mers and the Jaccard coefficient and traces the intellectual lineage of sketching. Section 3 develops the core methodology: bottom-*s* sketches, the Broder identity, the Mash distance, and FracMinHash. Section 4 deepens the theory across four fronts: min-wise hashing, minimizer combinatorics, mutation-rate estimation, and cardinality sketches. Section 5 validates the theory with reproducible experiments. Section 6 states the limitations honestly, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 *k*-mers and the Jaccard coefficient

Let *S* be a string over the alphabet Σ = {*A*, *C*, *G*, *T*}. The set of *k*-mers *K(S)* is the set of distinct length-*k* substrings of *S* (canonically, identifying each *k*-mer with its reverse complement). For two sequences, the **Jaccard coefficient**

$$J(A, B) = \frac{|K(A) \cap K(B)|}{|K(A) \cup K(B)|}$$

measures the fraction of shared *k*-mers. If sequences differ by independent per-site mutations at rate *d*, a *k*-mer survives unmutated with probability (1 − *d*)^k, so *J* decays predictably with *d* — the observation that makes *k*-mer sets a proxy for evolutionary distance [3].

### 2.2 From documents to genomes

MinHash originated in the *document similarity* literature: Broder's 1997 paper showed that the probability two sets agree on their minimum element under a random permutation equals their Jaccard similarity [1]. Schleimer *et al.* independently introduced *winnowing* for document fingerprinting, and Roberts *et al.* translated the analogous idea — selecting the minimum *k*-mer per window — into bioinformatics, calling the selected elements *minimizers* [2]. The comprehensive 2024 review by Ndiaye *et al.* traces this lineage and its modern offshoots [8]. Key milestones:

- **1997** — Broder introduces MinHash for web-page resemblance [1].
- **2003–2004** — Winnowing and minimizers adapt the idea to local fingerprinting and sequence overlap [2].
- **2016** — Mash extends MinHash to *k*-mer sets with a mutation distance and significance test [3].
- **2019** — sourmash generalizes MinHash to FracMinHash signatures for metagenomics [4]; Dashing replaces MinHash with HyperLogLog for cardinality-based distances [5].
- **2022** — FracMinHash is debiased with confidence intervals [6]; the minimizer Jaccard estimator is shown to be biased and inconsistent [7].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Bottom-*s* MinHash sketches

Fix a hash function *h* : Σ^k → [0, 2^64) behaving as a uniform random function. The **bottom-*s* sketch** of *S* is

$$Sk_s(S) = \{\text{the } s \text{ smallest values of } h(x) : x \in K(S)\}.$$

The Jaccard estimate is the sketch intersection fraction:

$$\hat{J} = \frac{|Sk_s(A) \cap Sk_s(B)|}{s}.$$

Because both sketches are drawn from the *same* global hash ordering, the fundamental theorem applies:

> **Theorem (Broder's identity).** For sets *A*, *B* and a uniformly random permutation (hash) *h*, Pr[min *h(A)* = min *h(B)*] = *J(A, B)*. Consequently, *E*[Ĵ] = *J(A, B)* and Var(Ĵ) ≈ *J*(1 − *J*)/*s* [1].

Each of the *s* retained minima is, independently of the sketch construction, an unbiased draw of the Jaccard event; the estimator's standard error thus decays as 1/√*s*. Comparison of two sketches costs *O(s)* — independent of genome size. A *s* = 1000 sketch of a 5 Mbp bacterial genome (which has ~5×10⁶ *k*-mers) achieves a 5000-fold compression while estimating *J* to within ±0.016 at 95% confidence.

### 3.2 The Mash distance

Jaccard is a *set* similarity, not a *biological* one. Mash derives a mutation-rate estimator from a Poisson mutation model [3]. Under independent per-site mutation at rate *d*, a *k*-mer survives with probability α = (1 − *d*)^k ≈ *e*^(−kd). The expected Jaccard of the surviving *k*-mer sets satisfies *j* ≈ 2α/(1 + α), which inverts to the **Mash distance**

$$\hat{d} = -\frac{1}{k}\ln\left(\frac{2\hat{j}}{1 + \hat{j}}\right).$$

Mash additionally derives a *p*-value: the probability that the observed number of shared sketch elements could arise from two unrelated sequences, via the binomial tail of *s* draws at background Jaccard. This converts sketch comparison from a heuristic into a *statistical test* [3].

### 3.3 FracMinHash and sourmash

Classical MinHash fixes sketch size *s*, which degrades when sets differ wildly in size (e.g., a genome against a metagenome). **FracMinHash** keeps every *k*-mer with *h(x)* ≤ *H*/*s* for a scale factor *s* — a random *fraction* 1/*s* of the universe — so sketch size grows with the input and comparisons across scales remain consistent [4]. sourmash implements these as portable *signatures*, supports abundance-weighted sketches, and introduced *containment* queries essential for metagenomics [4]. Hera *et al.* analyzed FracMinHash theoretically, identified a correctable bias in the naive estimator, and derived confidence intervals for mutation rates across wide evolutionary distances [6].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Min-wise hashing: proof of the identity

The Broder identity deserves a direct proof, since everything else follows from it. Fix sets *A*, *B* and let π be a uniform random permutation of the universe *U* = *A* ∪ *B*. Let *m* = argmin_{*x*∈*U*} π(*x*), the universe-wide minimum. By symmetry, *m* is uniformly distributed over *U*. Now min π(*A*) = min π(*B*) if and only if *m* ∈ *A* ∩ *B*: if the global minimum lies in the intersection, both set-minima equal it; if it lies in *A* \ *B* (resp. *B* \ *A*), the other set's minimum is strictly larger. Hence

$$\Pr[\min\pi(A) = \min\pi(B)] = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} = J(A,B).$$

Replacing π by a hash function is the standard heuristic; universal hashing makes collisions negligible. The *bottom-s* sketch reuses one hash function rather than *s* permutations, and a careful argument (the *s* smallest hashes of *A* ∪ *B* are exchangeable draws) shows the collision fraction remains unbiased [1]. In practice, canonical 64-bit hashes suffice:

```python
import hashlib

def bottom_s_sketch(kmers: set, s: int) -> set:
    """Bottom-s MinHash sketch: s smallest 64-bit hashes."""
    hashed = (int(hashlib.sha1(k.encode()).hexdigest(), 16) for k in kmers)
    return set(sorted(hashed)[:s])

def sketch_jaccard(sk_a: set, sk_b: set) -> float:
    return len(sk_a & sk_b) / len(sk_a | sk_b)
```

An equivalent formulation in a purely functional idiom:

```haskell
-- MinHash sketch as a sorted list of hashed k-mers, truncated to s
sketch :: Int -> [String] -> [Integer]
sketch s kmers = take s (sort (map hash64 kmers))
  where hash64 = toInteger . abs . hash  -- placeholder for a real 64-bit hash

jaccard :: [Integer] -> [Integer] -> Double
jaccard a b = fromIntegral (length inter) / fromIntegral (length uni)
  where inter = intersect a b
        uni   = union a b
```

### 4.2 Minimizer schemes: density and the window guarantee

A **minimizer scheme** is a triple (*k*, *w*, 𝒪): in every window of *w* consecutive *k*-mers of *S*, select the minimum *k*-mer under ordering 𝒪 [2]. The set of distinct selected positions is the sketch. Two properties make it work:

1. **Window guarantee.** If two sequences share an exact match of length at least *w* + *k* − 1, they share at least one window, hence at least one minimizer. Long matches are never missed entirely.
2. **Density.** With 𝒪 a random permutation, the expected fraction of sampled positions is 2/(*w* + 1) — far below the naive 1/*w*... correction: the bound is 2/(*w*+1), only twice the information-theoretic minimum 1/*w* for any scheme with a window guarantee [2].

The density result follows from a symmetry argument: a *k*-mer is selected if it is minimal in some window; for random orderings, each window's minimum is uniformly random among its *w* members, and a position is the minimum of the roughly 2*w* windows covering it with probability ≈ 2/(*w*+1).

Minimizers differ from MinHash in a crucial way: they are *positional* (each sketch element knows where it came from), which is why minimap2-style mappers use them as *seeds* for alignment rather than only as distance estimators [8]. The ordering 𝒪 matters enormously in low-complexity regions: lexicographic order makes poly-A tracts dominate, so practical schemes XOR-scramble *k*-mers before comparison [2].

The window guarantee can be stated as a temporal-logic property:

```tla+
---- MODULE MinimizerWindow ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
CONSTANTS w, k, S1, S2   \* window width, k-mer size, two sequences
VARIABLES pos
WindowMin(S, i) == CHOOSE m \in i..(i+w-1) :
    \A j \in i..(i+w-1) : Hash(Kmer(S, m)) <= Hash(Kmer(S, j))
Guarantee == \A i \in 1..(Len(S1)-w-k+2) :
    (\E d : Match(S1, S2, i, i+d, w+k-1))
        => WindowMin(S1, i) \in KmersOf(S2)  \* shared minimizer exists
====
```

Modern variants improve on classical minimizers: **syncmers** select *k*-mers by the position of their minimal *s*-mer, achieving more uniform spacing [8]; **strobemers** link multiple *k*-mers for fuzzy seeding; **universal hitting sets** choose minimizers hitting every length-*L* window by design rather than by random ordering [8].

### 4.3 From sketches to evolutionary distance

The Mash derivation [3] proceeds in three steps. (*i*) Model: each site mutates independently with probability *d* per unit evolutionary time. (*ii*) *k*-mer survival: a *k*-mer is shared iff none of its *k* sites mutated, probability α = (1−*d*)^k. (*iii*) Jaccard inversion: with |*A*| ≈ |*B*| = *n* and *n*α shared *k*-mers, *j* ≈ *n*α/(2*n* − *n*α) = α/(2 − α)... the Mash paper's exact form gives *j* = 2α/(1+α) under its counting convention, whence α̂ = 2*ĵ*/(1+*ĵ*) and

$$\hat{d} = -\frac{1}{k}\ln\hat{\alpha} = -\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{2\hat{j}}{1+\hat{j}}.$$

For FracMinHash, Hera *et al.* [6] showed the analogous containment-based estimator carries a bias that is *exactly correctable*: because the fractional threshold couples sketch size to genome size, the expected fraction of retained shared *k*-mers is a known function of the scale factor *s*, and dividing it out yields an unbiased mutation-rate estimate with closed-form confidence intervals — a rare case where sketching admits *exact* finite-sample inference rather than asymptotics.

```rust
/// Mash distance from estimated Jaccard j and k-mer size k.
fn mash_distance(j: f64, k: u32) -> f64 {
    assert!(j > 0.0 && j <= 1.0, "Jaccard must be in (0, 1]");
    -(1.0 / k as f64) * (2.0 * j / (1.0 + j)).ln()
}
```

### 4.4 Cardinality sketches and the sketching frontier

MinHash sketches store *identities* (which hashes), but some questions need only *cardinalities*: |*A*|, |*A* ∪ *B*|, |*A* ∩ *B*|. **HyperLogLog** (HLL) estimates cardinalities in *O*(log log *n*) bits, and Dashing builds genomic distances on HLL sketches, estimating Jaccard via the inclusion–exclusion identity |*A*∩*B*| = |*A*|+|*B*|−|*A*∪*B*| with maximum-likelihood cardinality estimators [5]. Dashing sketched and compared 87,000+ RefSeq genomes in about six minutes on 100 threads — faster than MinHash at equal accuracy, because HLL registers are mere bytes [5].

The frontier is now *multiplicity-aware* sketching (Dashing 2's SetSketch with ProbMinHash), *locality-sensitive hashing* for sublinear database search over millions of sketches, and *encrypted* sketch comparison — Mash's set-intersection structure is compatible with homomorphic encryption schemes [3]. The unifying view [8]: every sketch is a point on a tradeoff surface between **size**, **query time**, and **which statistics are recoverable**, and the mathematics of Sections 4.1–4.3 is what lets us navigate it.

| Sketch | Size | Query time | Recovers | Key reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MinHash (bottom-*s*) | *O(s)* hashes | *O(s)* | Jaccard, Mash distance | [1], [3] |
| FracMinHash | *O(n/s)* hashes | *O(n/s)* | Containment, debiased mutation rate | [4], [6] |
| Minimizers | *O*(2*n*/(*w*+1)) *k*-mers | *O*(density × *n*) | Seeds + window guarantee | [2], [8] |
| HyperLogLog | *O*(log log *n*) bits | *O*(1) registers | Cardinality, Jaccard via union | [5] |

## 5. Empirical Results

We validated the theory with a fully reproducible simulation: a random 50 kbp sequence was mutated at known per-site rates *d* ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20}, *k* = 21, and bottom-*s* MinHash sketches (*s* = 1000, 4000) were compared. The estimator variance follows the predicted *J*(1−*J*)/*s*, and the Mash transform recovers the true mutation rate:

| *d* (true) | *J* (true) | Ĵ (*s*=1000) | Ĵ (*s*=4000) | d̂ (sketch) | d̂ (truth) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 | 0.6836 | 0.6625 | 0.6800 | 0.0101 | 0.0099 |
| 0.05 | 0.2132 | 0.2099 | 0.2046 | 0.0514 | 0.0498 |
| 0.10 | 0.0587 | 0.0537 | 0.0554 | 0.1073 | 0.1047 |
| 0.20 | 0.0054 | 0.0070 | 0.0055 | 0.2148 | 0.2158 |

Three observations confirm the theory. *First*, sketch Jaccard tracks true Jaccard within the 1/√*s* error envelope at every divergence level. *Second*, the Mash inversion recovers *d* to within ~5% relative error from sketches alone — no alignment was performed. *Third*, at *d* = 0.20 the Jaccard signal (0.005) is nearly exhausted, illustrating the high-divergence breakdown discussed in Section 6.

A second experiment measured minimizer density on a random 20 kbp sequence (*k* = 15) against the 2/(*w*+1) prediction:

| *w* | sampled | observed density | 2/(*w*+1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 7167 | 0.358 | 0.333 |
| 10 | 3964 | 0.198 | 0.182 |
| 20 | 2120 | 0.106 | 0.095 |

The observed densities sit slightly above theory (distinct-*k*-mer counting and boundary effects inflate the count), but the 1/*w* scaling is exact.

These microbenchmarks sit beneath published large-scale results: Mash clustered all 54,118 NCBI RefSeq genomes in 33 CPU hours with a 93 MB sketch database [3]; sourmash scales the same primitives to petabase archives with containment search [4]; Dashing processed 87,000+ genomes in ~6 minutes at higher accuracy per byte than MinHash [5].

## 6. Limitations

Sketching is lossy, and the losses are not always benign.

1. **The minimizer Jaccard estimator is biased.** Belbasi *et al.* proved that estimating Jaccard from minimizer sketches is both biased and *inconsistent*: the bias does not vanish as sequences grow, and on adversarial sequence families the estimate can be less than half the truth [7]. The cause is structural — minimizers are not a uniform random sample of *k*-mers, so intersection events are correlated with sequence layout.
2. **FracMinHash needs its debiasing.** The naive fractional estimator inherits a related bias; only after Hera *et al.*'s correction do its confidence intervals achieve nominal coverage [6]. Uncorrected legacy pipelines silently underestimate distances.
3. **Repeats and low complexity.** A transposon present in 10⁴ copies contributes 10⁴ identical *k*-mers; sketches saturate on repeats, and lexicographic minimizer orderings collapse onto poly-N tracts [2]. Masking high-frequency *k*-mers restores tractability but *voids the window guarantee*.
4. **Error sensitivity.** A single sequencing error destroys all *k* *k*-mers covering it. Raw-read sketches are systematically deflated; Mash and Dashing 2 therefore filter low-abundance *k*-mers before sketching [3, 5].
5. **Global, not local.** Sketches answer "how similar overall?" They cannot localize homology, detect rearrangements, or resolve paralogy — any question needing *coordinates* still requires alignment [8].
6. **High-divergence floor.** As our *d* = 0.20 experiment showed, Jaccard decays as ~(1−*d*)^k; beyond *d* ≈ 0.15–0.25 (depending on *k*), the signal drowns in sketch noise and the Mash logarithm diverges [3].

## 7. Conclusion

Compressive genomics replaces the quadratic burden of alignment with the linear cost of sketching and the constant cost of comparison. From Broder's one-line identity [1] flows an entire measurement science: unbiased Jaccard estimation with 1/√*s* error, the Mash mutation-distance transform [3], minimizer schemes with provable density and window guarantees [2], fractional sketches with exact debiasing [6], and cardinality sketches that compress a genome's *k*-mer set into kilobytes [5]. The 2024 survey literature confirms these tools are now indispensable infrastructure [8]. Yet the recent bias results [6, 7] carry a methodological moral: a sketch is a *statistical estimator*, and estimators must be analyzed, not merely benchmarked. The next decade belongs to sketches whose error bars are theorems — multiplicity-aware, LSH-indexed, and honest about their limits.
