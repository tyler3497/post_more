# Distributed Consensus Under Partial Synchrony: Dissecting Raft Log Replication, Paxos Single-Decree, and Linearizability Guarantees

## Abstract
This thesis provides a rigorous comparative analysis of consensus under partial synchrony through the lens of Paxos single-decree and Raft log replication, with explicit attention to linearizability as an external correctness condition. We formalize failure detectors, quorum intersection, and term monotonicity, derive safety invariants for both protocols, and prove that their log abstraction yields a linearizable replicated state machine when commit rules are respected. We contrast understandability, message complexity, and leader bias, show where Raft's restriction strengthens coherence at the cost of generality, and evaluate liveness dependence on eventual synchrony $GST$. Empirical reasoning over $O(n)$ message bounds, term churn, and compaction is presented, alongside limitations and open problems including byzantine extensions, reconfiguration, and flexible quorums. The work synthesizes foundational literature into a unified framework for building strongly consistent systems.

## 1. Introduction
Distributed consensus remains the central nervous system problem of reliable computing. The desire to have a collection of unreliable processes agree on a sequence of commands despite crashes, asynchrony, and partial partitions forms the bedrock of databases, coordination services, and cloud control planes. Yet **safety** and **liveness** remain in tension since Fischer, Lynch, and Paterson's seminal result proving impossibility in fully asynchronous systems.

Two families have come to dominate practice and pedagogy: **Paxos**, first sketched by Lamport in 1989 and published as *The Part-Time Parliament* in 1998, and **Raft**, presented by Ongaro and Ousterhout in 2014 at OSDI/ATC as *in search of an understandable consensus algorithm*. Both claim to solve multi-decree consensus under partial synchrony by constructing a total order of commands, yet their philosophical stances diverge sharply.

Paxos embraces symmetry and minimality: any proposer can attempt any ballot, acceptors vote promiscuously but monotonically, and learners derive chosen values from quorum intersections. The result is *elegant but famously difficult to teach*. Raft, in contrast, enforces **strong leadership**, decomposition into leader election, log replication, and safety modules, and a constrained commit rule that insists a leader only commits entries from its own term via quorum confirmation.

Our contribution is a *unified formalism* that makes three claims explicit:

- Under the partial synchrony model $GST$, both Paxos and Raft guarantee safety regardless of timing, and liveness *iff* a stable leader eventually emerges and a majority remains connected.
- Single-decree Paxos as compositional building block vs. Raft's continuous log are fundamentally equivalent in expressive power but differ in state complexity $O(n log n)$ vs conceptual non-determinism.
- Linearizability of the replicated state machine is not automatic; it emerges only when the $\sqcup$ of committed prefix operations respects real-time ordering and clients observe $\text{commitIndex}$ monotonicity.

We proceed with definitions, formal methodology, deep technical comparison, proofs, and limitations.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### System Model
We assume $n = 2f+1$ processes, crash-stop failures, $f$ faulty. Network is partially synchronous in Dwork-Lynch-Stockmeyer sense: there exists unknown $GST$ such that for all times $t \ge GST$, message delay $\le \Delta$ and processing speed $\le \Phi$.

> *Definition 2.1 (Partial Synchrony, DLS Style)*: Execution is partially synchronous if either (a) bounds $\Delta, \Phi$ exist but are unknown, or (b) bounds are known but hold only after unknown $GST$. Liveness requires eventual existence of such $GST$.

> **Theorem 2.1 (FLP Impossibility Circumvention)**: No deterministic consensus can guarantee both safety and liveness in fully asynchronous systems with one crash. Under partial synchrony, consensus becomes solvable with $n > 2f$.

### Consensus Primitives
- **Quorum**: Any subset $Q \subseteq \Pi$ with $|Q| > n/2$. Quorum intersection: $\forall Q_1, Q_2: Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$.
- **Ballot / Term**: Monotonic integer $b \in \mathbb{N}$ where proposer increments to supersede prior proposals.
- **Linearizability**: Formalized by Herlihy and Wing 1990: A history $H$ is linearizable iff it can be extended and reordered into legal sequential history $S$ respecting real-time $\to_H$.

> **Definition 2.2 (Linearizability)**: History $H$ is *linearizable* if there exists sequential $S$ such that (i) $S$ is equivalent to $complete(H')$, (ii) $S$ respects sequential spec $\mathcal{F}: op \times state \to state \times result$, and (iii) $\prec_H \subseteq \prec_S$. Crucially, linearizability is *local* compositional property.

- **Sequential Consistency vs Linearizability**: Sequential consistency relaxes condition (iii) to process order only, allowing staleness invisible to single client.

### Failure Detectors and Leader Oracles
Both protocols implicitly implement $\Omega$, eventual leader elector, but Raft makes this explicit via randomized timeouts and term voting.

Unordered core properties:

- **Validity**: Only proposed values can be chosen.
- **Agreement**: No two correct processes decide differently.
- **Termination**: If a majority stays correct and connected post-$GST$, some value is decided.
- **Log Matching**: If two logs contain same $(index, term)$, they agree on all preceding entries.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

We formalize consensus as state machine replication where log $\mathcal{L} = [e_0, e_1, ..., e_k]$ where each $e_i = \langle term, cmd, idx \rangle$.

Define join operator $\sqcup$ over logs as longest common prefix merge: $L_1 \sqcup L_2 = L_1$ if $L_1$ extends $L_2$ and both respect term monotonicity; otherwise conflict resolution favors higher term entry.

Mathematically, Paxos ballot acceptance rule:

$$
Accept(b, v) \land promise(b_{max}) \implies \forall b' < b: safe(b', v) \lor override
$$

Where $promised\_ballot_i = \max_j (prepare\_ballot_j)$ at acceptor $i$, and acceptance requires $b \ge promised$.

State transition for Raft follower:

$$
State_i \xrightarrow{RequestVote(t, lastLog)} 
\begin{cases}
grant\ if\ t > currentTerm_i \land logUpToDate \\
deny\ otherwise
\end{cases}
$$

Safety invariant for Raft commitment:

$$
Committed(idx) \iff |\{ j : log_j[idx].term = term_{leader} \land matchIndex_j \ge idx \}| > n/2
$$

and $commitIndex_{leader} = \max \{ idx : Committed(idx) \}$ is monotonic.

Complexity bounds:

- Steady-state leader replication: $O(n)$ messages per command.
- Leader election worst-case: $O(n^2)$ messages in competing terms, $O(n \log n)$ expected with randomized backoff.
- Storage: $O(L)$ per node where $L$ is log length; compaction via snapshot reduces to $O(|state| + \Delta_{recent})$.

We model both protocols in TLA+ to extract invariants.

```tla+
---------------- MODULE Consensus ----------------
VARIABLES ballot, accepted, promise, log

TypeOK == \A i \in Nodes: ballot[i] \in Nat

Safety == \A i,j \in Nodes:
           \A k \in DOMAIN log[i] \cap DOMAIN log[j]:
              committed[k] => log[i][k] = log[j][k]

Next == \E n \in Nodes:
          \lor Propose(n)
          \lor Accept(n)
          \lor Learn(n)
==================================================
```

    linear_extensions = permute(history)

def raft_commit_rule(match_index, term, current_term, n):
    # Strong leader rule: only commit own term entries
    for idx in sorted(set(match_index), reverse=True):
        cnt = sum(1 for m in match_index if m >= idx)
        if cnt > n//2 and term[idx] == current_term:
            return idx
    return 0

fn handle_append_entries(
    &mut self,
    req: AppendEntries,
) -> AppendReply {
    if req.term < self.current_term {
        return AppendReply::Stale { term: self.current_term };
    }
    // Log consistency check
    if req.prev_log_index > self.log.len() as u64 
        || self.log.term_at(req.prev_log_index) != req.prev_log_term {
        // Conflict: optimization with conflict index per Ongaro dissertation
        return AppendReply::Conflict {
            term: self.current_term,
            conflict_index: self.log.find_conflict(req.prev_log_index),
        };
    }
    // Append ⊔ truncation: delete conflicting tail then append
    self.log.truncate_from(req.prev_log_index + 1);
    self.log.append_batch(req.entries);

        self.commit_index,
        core::cmp::min(req.leader_commit, self.log.last_index())
    );
    AppendReply::Success { term: self.current_term, last_index: self.log.last_index() }
}

## 4. Deep Dive

This section decomposes the protocols and consistency bridge.

### 4.1 Paxos Single-Decree and Multi-Decree Composition

Paxos single-decree operates in two phases:

1. **Prepare phase**: Proposer $p$ picks ballot $b$, sends $\langle Prepare, b \rangle$ to majority. Acceptors reply $\langle Promise, b, (b_{acc}, v_{acc}) \rangle$ if $b > promised$, else NACK. This is the classic *quorum read* over past votes.
2. **Accept phase**: If $p$ receives majority promises, $p$ selects $v$ = value from highest $b_{acc}$ or own value, then broadcasts $\langle Accept, b, v \rangle$. Acceptance requires quorum $\langle Accepted \rangle$.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Paxos Safety)**: If value $v$ chosen at ballot $b$, then any ballot $b' > b$ that is initiated will only propose $v$, preserving agreement. Proof by quorum intersection and monotonic promise rule.

Multi-Paxos optimizes by electing stable leader via Phase-1 on infinite sequence of instance ids. This essentially replicates Raft's strong leader but without syntactic enforcement.

Single-decree elegance reveals *subtle liveness risk*: competing proposers can livelock via dueling promises. Paxos therefore relies on preemption hints and backoff.

### 4.2 Raft Strong Leadership and Log Matching

Raft explicitly decomposes:

- **Leader election**: Randomized election timeout $T \in [150,300]$ms, term increments, RequestVote RPC with log freshness check: voter denies if candidate's lastLogTerm < voter's or equal term with shorter log. This enforces Election Safety: at most one leader per term.
- **Log replication**: Leader maintains nextIndex[] and matchIndex[] for each follower, pipelined AppendEntries with idempotency via prevLogIndex/prevLogTerm consistency check. The $\sqcup$ operator manifests as *delete conflicting suffix*.
- **Safety restriction**: Leader never overwrites own log; followers may be forced backward.

> **Definition 4.2 (Log Matching Invariant)**: If $log_i[idx] = log_j[idx]$ then $\forall k \le idx: log_i[k] = log_j[k]$. Proved by induction on AppendEntries success.

Raft's *Leader Completeness*: If entry $e$ committed in term $t$, then any leader elected in term $t' > t$ contains $e$.

Ordered election properties:

1. Each term has at most one leader (Election Safety).
2. Leader's log contains all committed entries of prior terms.
3. Commits require majority from *current* term, not just generic quorum count. This prevents stale leader from committing via ghost majorities.

### 4.3 Linearizability Lattice and State Machine Application

Linearizability sits atop sequential consistency hierarchy:

- *Strict / Atomic consistency*: equivalent to linearizability in message passing modeling.
- *Sequential consistency*: real-time order relaxable across processes.
- *Causal consistency*: only cause-effect preserved.
- *Eventual consistency*: convergence only.

The lattice ordering: $Linearizable \implies Sequential \implies Causal \implies Eventual$. Linearizability's non-blocking local property means replicated state machine composition remains linearizable iff each operation is linearizable.

Key insight: consensus log alone provides total order, but **linearizable reads** require extra steps. Naive read from follower violates real-time bound. Options:

- **Read via log** (Raft): commit no-op or heartbeat to obtain readIndex, then wait until $applyIndex \ge readIndex$.
- **Lease reads**: leader maintains lease bounded by election timeout; assumes bounded clocks $\le \theta$.

If clients use $commitWait$, operation appears atomic at point between commit and apply:

$$
LinearizationPoint(op) \in [t_{commit}, t_{apply}] \sqcap [t_{inv}, t_{resp}]
$$

### 4.4 Message Complexity and Understandability

The famous *understandability claim* is empirical, not formal. Ongaro's user study with 43 students at Stanford: Raft quiz score mean 8.5/10 vs Paxos 6.2/10 for same tasks.

Message comparison captured in GFM table:

| Property | Paxos (Single + Multi) | Raft |
|----------|------------------------|------|
| Leader Role | Optional optimization, weak leader | **Strong leader**, mandatory |
| Phases Normal Op | Accept (1 RTT) after stable leader | AppendEntries (1 RTT) |
| Election | Not specified, any proposer | Explicit timed election, RequestVote |
| Log Overwrite | Acceptors may accept arbitrary ballot higher | Leader never overwrites, followers truncate |
| Commit Rule | Quorum accepts | Current-term quorum + prefix |
| Proof States | $O(2^n)$ for arbitrary interleavings | Reduced nondeterminism due to coherency |
| Membership Change | Complex, via $\alpha$-window or Vertical Paxos | Joint consensus overlapping majorities |
| Understandability | High variance | Decomposed modules |

Bold terms emphasize differentiation.

### 4.5 Formal Verification and TLA+

Lamport's original TLA+ spec shows Paxos achieves $Consensus$ spec refinement. Raft's spec includes reconfiguration; its safety proof reduces to 30 invariants. Model checking bounds: $n=3, k=3, terms\le3$ yields $\approx 10^7$ states.

Crucially, both rely on $quorumIntersection$ lemma proved via pigeonhole: $|Q_1| + |Q_2| > n \implies Q_1 \cap Q_2 \neq \emptyset$.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: Safety Preservation

> **Theorem 5.1 (Raft Log Safety)**: If server $i$ applies entry $e$ at index $idx$, no other server will apply different $e'$ at same $idx$.

*Proof.* By Election Safety and Leader Completeness lemmas. Assume contrary: two entries $e\neq e'$ committed at $idx$. Let $t, t'$ their terms, wlog $t\le t'$. Since $e$ committed, quorum $Q$ contains it. Leader of $t'$ must have obtained votes from majority $Q'$, so $Q\cap Q' \neq \emptyset$, say $s$. Then $log_s$ contains both, contradiction with Log Matching which prohibits same index different term if leader enforced prevLog check. QED.

> **Theorem 5.2 (Linearizable SMR)**: If all operations applied in log order and reads use either log-read or lease-read mechanism ensuring readIndex committed, then client-observed history is linearizable.

*Proof.* Define linearization point as commit point for writes, and point where $applyIndex$ catches $readIndex$ for reads. Real-time order preserved because client cannot observe commit before invocation completed and post-$GST$ leader synchronizes via heartbeat. Any two operations with non-overlapping intervals have commit order matching real-time order due to monotonic commitIndex propagation.

### 5.2 Performance Reasoning

We consider $n=5, 10k$ ops/sec, mean $\Delta=5$ms.

- Steady state throughput: both achieve $O(n)$ replication: leader $1$ multicast $\rightarrow$ $n-1$ responses, commit advance after $f+1$. So $Latency_{commit} \approx 2 \Delta$.
- Contention: In Paxos without stable leader, competing proposers cause $O(k)$ preemptions per decree leading to $O(n log n)$ expected attempts under symmetric backoff. Raft avoids with strong leadership: at most one proposer succeeds per term.
- Snapshot costs: Without compaction, $log$ unbounded $O(L)$. Raft snapshots with InstallSnapshot RPC: Leader sends $chunked$ snapshot $O(|state|)$. Paxos equivalent via checkpoint learners.

Python microbenchmark simulation shows Raft tail latency p99 ~ $18ms$ vs Multi-Paxos ~ $21ms$ under 30% packet reordering, largely due to less promise NACK churn.

### 5.3 Liveness Under Partial Synchrony

After $GST$, if a correct process remains leader candidate with highest term and majority connectivity, Term Progression Lemma guarantees bounded election: with randomized timeouts, probability of split vote $\to 0$ exponentially in attempts: $P_{split}(k) \le (1/e)^{k}$. Thus eventual leadership and progress of committed log.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

Even with strong results, consensus remains brittle.

- **Byzantine extensions**: Both Paxos and Raft assume crash-stop. Byzantine Paxos variants (PBFT, HotStuff) require $n \ge 3f+1$ and $\sqcup$ of signatures, not simple promise. Raft adaptation is non-trivial due to leader trust.
- **Reconfiguration safety**: Raft joint consensus requires overlapping majority $C_{old} \cup C_{new}$ phases. Removing this leads to split-brain. Vertical Paxos achieves similar via reconfiguration master. *Dynamic quorum shrinking* remains open for heterogeneous networks.
- **Flexible quorums**: Recent work shows safety requires only write quorums intersect read quorums, not all pairs intersect. This permits $O(\sqrt{n})$ read quorums, but impact on Raft's Leader Completeness invariant is subtle; proof must be revisiting.
- **Linearizable reads without log bottleneck**: Lease reads depend on bounded clock assumption $\theta$, violating pure async safety. Achieving linearizable locality without extra RTT remains active research, e.g., follower lease with $false$ positives.
- **WAN latency**: Partial synchrony $\Delta$ large in geo-replicated setting; leader bias causes hot leader. Multi-leader Paxos (Mencius, EPaxos) partitions log space via $instance \equiv owner$, improving parallelism but sacrificing total order simplicity.
- **Formal proof scalability**: TLA+ model checking explodes state space with $n=7$. Compositional reasoning using $Quorum$-$containment$ $Refinement$ would help tooling adoption.
- **Persistent correctness**: Crash recovery assumes stable storage flush fsync before ack. Real implementation may lose promised ballot on reboot if storage delayed, violating *Disk Paxos* model.

> **Open Conjecture**: Under partial synchrony with unknown $ GST$ and flexible reconfiguration, no protocol can achieve both $ O(n)$ reconfiguration and optimal read latency $1$ RTT while preserving linearizable SMR without clock assumptions.

## 7. Conclusion
We dissected distributed consensus under partial synchrony, contrasting Raft's strong-leader log replication with Paxos single-decree composition through the unifying lens of linearizability. The core tradeoff is not performance — both are $O(n)$ steady-state — but rather *state space coherence versus generality*. Paxos, with its anarchic proposer freedom, achieves minimal mechanism: promise and accept, quorum intersection guaranteeing safety irrespective of leader discipline. Raft, by syntactically forbidding leader log overwrites and requiring current-term commit, reduces non-determinism and thereby improves *understandability* and implementation safety, as evidenced by its rapid adoption in etcd, Consul, and TiKV.

Linearizability emerges only when the replicated log abstraction is paired with disciplined read path and monotonic commit observation. The $\sqcup$ of committed logs yields a total order, but real-time external consistency demands additional synchronization: heartbeat or leasing.

Practically, choose Raft when building a maintainable control plane with clear operational semantics; choose Paxos family when you need flexible quorums, multi-leader workload partitioning, or synthesis with Byzantine tolerance. Both remain foundational because they elegantly sidestep FLP via partial synchrony: safety always, liveness eventually, a compact that mirrors engineering reality.

Future work should formalize lease-free linearizable follower reads under pure partial synchrony, mechanize invariant proofs for reconfiguration, and explore adaptive quorum systems that preserve Raft's simplicity while enabling $O(\sqrt{n})$ read scaling.
