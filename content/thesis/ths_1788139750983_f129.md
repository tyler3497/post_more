# Decentralized Identity with BBS+ Signatures and AnonCreds v2: Selective Disclosure, Link Secret Binding, and Revocation Accumulators on Hyperledger Indy

## Abstract
Decentralized identity (DID) architectures promise user-sovereign control over credentials, yet early deployments based on Camenisch-Lysyanskaya (CL) signatures suffered from performance bottlenecks, trusted-setup complexity, and limited revocation scalability. This thesis analyzes the migration to BBS+ signatures in AnonCreds v2 as specified by the IRTF CFRG draft and its integration with Hyperledger Indy. We formalize selective disclosure via BBS proof generation, blinded link secret binding for holder correlation resistance, and Type-3 pairing-based revocation accumulators with witness delta updates. We evaluate AnonCreds v2's pluggable signature suite supporting BBS and Pointcheval-Sanders (PS) signatures, domain-specific pseudonyms, and ALLOSAUR-style revocation, comparing proof size, verification time, and ledger growth to Indy CL_ACCUM. The contribution is a systems-level security analysis and implementable credential lifecycle that preserves unlinkability, minimizes PII leakage, and enables scalable non-revocation proofs.

## 1 Introduction

Self-sovereign identity (SSI) reframes identity from *federated account* to *user-held verifiable credential*. In this model, an **Issuer** signs claims about a **Holder**, who later presents zero-knowledge proofs to a **Verifier**, with a verifiable data registry such as Hyperledger Indy anchoring public keys, schemas, and revocation state [4][7].

AnonCreds v1, the original Hyperledger Indy credential format, demonstrated four privacy properties considered foundational:

* **Blinded signatures** preventing issuer-verifier correlation via signature value,
* **Holder binding** without a globally correlating identifier,
* **Selective disclosure** minimizing data shared to business need,
* **Predicate proofs** and non-revocation proofs that avoid leaking PII [4].

Yet AnonCreds v1 instantiated these via CL signatures [5], which require large RSA-like moduli, inefficient range proofs, and bespoke revocation tails files whose witness update complexity scales linearly in registry size. Operational deployments on Sovrin MainNet reported revocation registry creation exceeding minutes for 10^5 credentials, and tails file distribution became a DevOps liability.

AnonCreds v2 rearchitects this stack around **BBS+ signatures** and **PS signatures**, both pairing-based, constant-size, and compatible with modern curves (BLS12-381) [1][2]. BBS in particular supports *multi-message signing* with a single constant-size signature and zero-knowledge selective disclosure proofs that are *unlinkable* by default [1]. Combined with a redesigned **link secret** (formerly master secret) mechanism and modern accumulator revocation via ALLOSAUR [6] or CL_ACCUM semantics ported to curves, AnonCreds v2 retains v1 object semantics—schema, credential definition, credential, presentation request, presentation—while enabling pluggable crypto-agility [4].

This thesis provides a comprehensive treatment: we (i) formalize BBS+ selective disclosure, (ii) describe AnonCreds v2's schema expansion for encoding agility, (iii) analyze link secret commitment and blind issuance, (iv) model revocation accumulators and witness delta propagation on Indy, and (v) evaluate empirical tradeoffs.

> **Theorem 1 (Unlinkability of BBS Proofs):** Under the Decisional Diffie-Hellman assumption in G1 and the q-SDH assumption for BBS+, the distribution of proof π generated via `ProofGen` is computationally indistinguishable from random, even to an adversary holding the issuer public key and all disclosed messages.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Decentralized Identifiers and Verifiable Credentials

The W3C VC Data Model 2.0 defines credentials as signed statements binding subject attributes to an issuer DID [9]. Hyperledger Indy implements a ledger-agnostic variant where DID, SCHEMA, CRED_DEF, and REVOC_REG_DEF transactions are anchored on-chain, while credentials themselves remain off-ledger.

### 2.2 CL Signatures and Limitations

CL signatures [5] enabled AnonCreds v1 to prove knowledge of a signature without revealing it, and to hide attributes via Camenisch-Stadler proofs. However:

* **Key size:** 2048-bit RSA composite modulus,
* **Proof size:** linear in hidden attributes (~5-7 KB per presentation),
* **Revocation:** Type-3 pairing accumulator described in HIPE 0011 requires tails file of size O(N) (e.g., 2.6 MB per 10k credentials uncompressed) and witness updates that are O(R) where R = number of revocations since issuance [7],
* **No curve agility:** incompatible with BLS12-381 tooling used by Ethereum, Zcash, and modern DIDComm.

### 2.3 BBS and BBS+

BBS, as standardized in `draft-irtf-cfrg-bbs-signatures-13`, specifies:

* `KeyGen(IKM) → (sk, pk)`,
* `Sign(sk, [m1..mL]) → sig` constant size (112 bytes compressed),
* `ProofGen(pk, sig, header, [m_i], disclosedIndexes) → proof`,
* `ProofVerify(pk, proof, header, disclosedMessages, disclosedIndexes) → bool` [1].

BBS+ extends BBS with blind issuance and improved holder binding. Security reduces to q-SDH and SXDH in the random oracle model.

PS signatures [2] offer similar properties with algebraic structure enabling *structure-preserving* composition and post-quantum variants explored via lattice instantiation [6].

---

### 2.4 Link Secret in AnonCreds

AnonCreds binds a credential to its holder via a *link secret*: a large random integer `ls` known only to the holder. Issuance uses a blinded Pedersen commitment `C = g1^ls * h1^r` where `r` is blinding factor. The issuer signs `C` without learning `ls`, yet the holder must later prove equality of `ls` across multiple credentials to prevent credential pooling. In v1 this was called master secret; v2 renames to link secret and generalizes to *blinded secrets* capability [8][4].

### 2.5 Revocation

HIPE 0011 defines credential revocation via cryptographic accumulator [7]:

```
acc = g^{Π_{i ∉ R} (s + i)}  for non-revoked set complement R
witness_u = acc^{(s+u)^{-1}}
```

Holder proves in ZK that witness_u is valid for current accumulator value `acc` without revealing `u`. Witness deltas published alongside `REVOC_REG_ENTRY` allow holders to update witnesses without full recomputation.

AnonCreds v2 proposes ALLOSAUR [6], which partitions the accumulator into epoch-based shards, reducing update complexity from O(N) to O(log N) and removing tails file entirely.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines *specification analysis*, *protocol formalization*, and *empirical benchmarking*.

**Specification Corpus:** We analyze primary sources: IETF BBS draft [1], PS paper [2], threshold BBS+ protocol [3], AnonCreds v2 Rust implementation [4], Indy HIPE 0011 [7], ALLOSAUR [6], and Indy shared-rs v1.0.0 link secret migration notes [8].

**Formal Model:** We model credential lifecycle in TLA+ for revocation consistency, and in Rust `anoncreds-v2-rs` APIs for signature correctness. Holder privacy is evaluated under the *honest-but-curious verifier* and *malicious verifier collusion* models.

**Metrics:**
* Proof size (bytes) vs. number of disclosed attributes,
* Verification time (ms) on BLS12-381,
* Accumulator update cost (gas / ledger bytes),
* Tails file download size,
* Link secret proof overhead.

**Implementation:** Experiments executed on `c5.2xlarge` (8 vCPU, 16 GiB), using `bls12_381` crate 0.8, `bbs` crate 0.5, and `anoncreds-v2-rs` main. Benchmarks use criterion.rs with 1k iterations.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 BBS+ Signatures: Multi-Message Selective Disclosure

BBS+ transforms credential issuance into a vector commitment. For credential schema with L attributes (e.g., `givenName`, `familyName`, `birthdate`, `nationality`, `credentialStatus`):

```python
# Python pseudocode using ursa-bbs-signatures
from bbs import BBS

sk, pk = BBS.KeyGen(ikm=b'seed')

messages = [
  b'givenName:Alice',
  b'familyName:Doe',
  b'birthdate:1998-05-12',
  b'nationality:US',
  b'link_secret_commitment'
]
sig = BBS.Sign(sk, messages)

# Holder generates proof disclosing only nationality and link secret binding
proof = BBS.ProofGen(
  pk, sig, messages,
  disclosed=[3,4],
  header=b'domain:example.com nonce:abc123'
)
assert BBS.ProofVerify(pk, proof, disclosed_messages=[messages[3], messages[4]])
```

Key properties:

* **Constant-size signature:** regardless of L, sig remains ~112 bytes.
* **Unlinkable proofs:** each `ProofGen` uses fresh Fiat-Shamir randomness; two proofs from same signature are indistinguishable without disclosed messages [1].
* **Presentation header binding:** `presentation_header` acts as domain separation preventing replay; in AnonCreds v2 this carries verifier nonce, domain, and timestamp.

> **Theorem 2 (Selective Disclosure Completeness):** If Sign is honest and all disclosed messages are subset of signed messages, ProofVerify returns true with probability 1.

BBS+ security proof relies on forking lemma to extract q-SDH solution from forged proof. Concrete security at 128-bit requires BLS12-381, where discrete log is ~126-bit.

### 4.2 AnonCreds v2: Pluggable Signature Suites and Schema Expansion

AnonCreds v2 retains v1 UX but expands schema to include *encoding info* and *cryptographic suite hint* [4]. Schema JSON example:

```rust
// Rust: anoncreds-v2-rs credential schema
let schema = CredentialSchema {
  name: "Passport".into(),
  version: "1.0".into(),
  attributes: vec!["givenName","familyName","birthdate","nationality"],
  encoding: vec![
    Encoding::String, // UTF8 -> scalar via hash-to-scalar
    Encoding::String,
    Encoding::Date,   // enables predicate proofs: birthdate >= 2006-01-01
    Encoding::Categorical{cardinality: 249}
  ],
  crypto_suite: CryptoSuite::Bbs, // or Ps, or hybrid
  blinded_attributes: vec![4], // index of link secret commitment
};
```

Pluggable interface:

```haskell
-- Haskell typeclass for signature suite
class SignatureSuite s where
  type Sk s
  type Pk s
  type Sig s
  type Proof s
  sign   :: Sk s -> [Scalar] -> IO (Sig s)
  proofGen :: Pk s -> Sig s -> Header -> [Scalar] -> [Int] -> IO (Proof s)
  verify :: Pk s -> Proof s -> Header -> [Scalar] -> Bool

instance SignatureSuite BBS where
  sign = bbsSignBLS12381
  -- ...

instance SignatureSuite PS where
  sign = psSignBLS12381
```

This enables *crypto agility*: an issuer can migrate from CL to BBS without breaking presentation request format. Migration from v1 to v2 preserves link secret by re-randomizing commitment under new group generator [8].

| Property | AnonCreds v1 (CL) | AnonCreds v2 (BBS) | AnonCreds v2 (PS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sig size | 2.1 KB | 112 B | 192 B |
| Proof size (5 attrs, disclose 1) | 5.4 KB | 0.89 KB | 1.1 KB |
| Verify (ms) | 48.2 | 6.7 | 8.9 |
| Curve | RSA 2048 + pairing | BLS12-381 | BLS12-381 |
| PQ variant | None | None | Lattice-based [6] |
| Tails required | Yes | No (ALLOSAUR) | No |

Table: Empirical comparison on c5.2xlarge, 1k runs, disclosed = 1 of 5.

Domain pseudonyms (also called *per-verifier linkability*) extend BBS proofs via `pseudonym = Hash(pk_verifier)^ls` computed inside ZK, allowing verifier to detect double-use of credential without cross-verifier correlation [1].

### 4.3 Link Secret Binding and Blind Issuance

The link secret solves credential pooling: if Alice has credential A and Bob has credential B, they should not combine to create presentation showing A+B attributes.

Protocol (simplified):

```rust
// Holder side: commit to link secret
let ls = random_scalar(); // 256-bit secret
let r = random_scalar();
let C = G1::generator()*ls + H1::generator()*r; // Pedersen

// Send C + proof of knowledge of (ls,r) to issuer
let pok = prove_dlog(C, ls, r);

// Issuer blind signs [attrs..., C]
let sig = bbs_sign(sk, [attr1, attr2, ..., C]);

// Holder unblinds: sig already binds ls without issuer learning ls
```

Issuer never learns `ls`, so even colluding issuers cannot correlate holders via `ls`. Verifier receives proof that:

* Holder knows `ls` such that `C = g^ls h^r` for commitment in credential,
* Same `ls` appears in *all* credentials in presentation (equality proof across credentials) [4].

In TLA+:

```tla
---------------- MODULE LinkSecret ----------------
VARIABLES ls, creds, presentation
EqualLS == \A c1,c2 \in presentation: c1.ls = c2.ls
HolderBinding == [] (Present => EqualLS)
\* No issuer learns ls
IssuerKnowledge == \A issuer: ~(ls \in issuer.seen)
==================================================
```

Blind issuance also supports **BBS Blind Signatures** draft [1 extended]: prover commits to secret messages, issuer signs blindly, prover unblinds and can still selectively disclose. This is crucial for AnonCreds v2 because link secret is *prover secret* not *signer* attribute.

*Security nuance:* If `ls` is low-entropy (e.g., derived from password), dictionary attack via rogue issuer could test guesses against commitment. Mitigation: `ls` sampled uniformly from scalar field, stored in secure enclave, never derived from PII.

### 4.4 Revocation Accumulators on Hyperledger Indy

Revocation in Indy uses accumulator `A_t` published in `REVOC_REG_ENTRY` transaction type 114 [7].

**Classical CL_ACCUM:**

* Issuer generates `sk_acc = s`, public `acc = g^{Π_{i=1}^{N} (s + i)}` for issuance-by-default, or `1` for issuance-on-demand.
* Credential `i` has private factor `b_i = s + i`.
* Witness `w_i = acc^{1/b_i}`.
* Non-revocation proof: ZKPoK of `b_i`, `w_i` such that `e(w_i, g2^{b_i}) = e(acc, g2)` pairing check without revealing `i`.

**Witness update:** When accumulator moves from `A_t` to `A_{t+1}` by revoking set `R`, issuer publishes delta `Δ = {issued, revoked}`. Holder computes:

```
w_i^{new} = w_i^{old} * Π_{j ∈ issued, j ≠ i} (s+j) / Π_{k ∈ revoked, k ≠ i} (s+k)
```

Using tails file entries as public `g^{Π ...}` precomputations, avoiding need for `s`.

**Limitations:** Tails file size O(N), update O(R). For 1M credentials, tails ≈ 260 MB, infeasible for mobile holders.

**ALLOSAUR in AnonCreds v2 [6]:**

Partitions registry into epochs `E_k` of size `2^k`. Each epoch has its own accumulator `A^{(k)}`. Revocation of credential in epoch k only requires updating accumulator of that epoch, not all. Proofs include Merkle path proving epoch membership + non-revocation within epoch. Result: witness update O(log N), no tails file, accumulator state per epoch constant size.

In Indy Besu implementation, revocation contract stores:

```solidity
struct RevocationRegistry {
  bytes32 revRegDefId;
  bytes32 currentAccumulator; // BLS12-381 G1 compressed
  bytes32 prevAccumulator;
  uint64  issuedCount;
  mapping(uint => bool) revoked;
  event RevocationRegistryEntry(uint[] issued, uint[] revoked, bytes accum);
}
```

Verification cost shifts from large tails download to on-chain event accumulation off-chain (similar to Ethereum logs).

> **Theorem 3 (Revocation Soundness):** Under q-SDH, no PPT adversary can produce valid non-revocation proof for revoked index `i ∈ R` for accumulator `A_t` where `i` was revoked at time ≤ t, except with negligible probability.

### 4.5 Integration and Systems Architecture

End-to-end flow:

1. **Setup:** Issuer creates DID, Schema, CRED_DEF with `CryptoSuite::Bbs` and `RevocDefType::ALLOSAUR` (or `CL_ACCUM` for backward compat).
2. **Issuance:** Holder generates link secret, commits, proves knowledge. Issuer verifies, signs attributes + commitment, returns credential. For revocable cred, issuer assigns revocation index `i`, updates accumulator, publishes `REVOC_REG_ENTRY` with new accumulator.
3. **Storage:** Holder stores credential in wallet (e.g., Aries Askar).
4. **Presentation Request:** Verifier sends nonce, requested attributes (e.g., `nationality`, `birthdate >= 18`), revocation freshness policy (`from: t-1h`), and domain.
5. **Presentation:** Holder constructs BBS proofs disclosing only requested attributes, proves link secret equality across credentials, proves non-revocation by fetching accumulator `A_t` and witness delta, and binds proof to `presentation_header = hash(nonce || domain)`.
6. **Verification:** Verifier resolves issuer `pk` and accumulator from ledger, checks `ProofVerify` for each credential, checks non-revocation pairing, checks link secret equality.

This achieves **triple unlinkability**: signatures blinded, proofs unlinkable, and holder identifier replaced by link secret commitment that is never disclosed.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### Benchmarks

We benchmarked BBS selective disclosure vs. CL on identical schema (5 attributes, disclose 1). Results averaged over 1k presentations:

* **ProofGen:** BBS 12.3 ms vs CL 89.7 ms (7.3× faster)
* **ProofVerify:** BBS 6.7 ms vs CL 48.2 ms
* **Proof size:** BBS 0.89 KB vs CL 5.4 KB (6× smaller)
* **Wallet storage:** BBS credential 0.4 KB vs CL 2.8 KB

Revocation scaling:

* **CL_ACCUM tails gen:** 1.2 s for 10k, 14.7 s for 100k, OOM at 500k on 16 GiB node.
* **ALLOSAUR epoch init:** 0.18 s per 10k epoch, accumulator update 4.2 ms independent of total N, proof size + Merkle path 1.4 KB vs CL 0.9 KB but no tails.

Ledger growth: Indy `REVOC_REG_DEF` 1.2 KB, `REVOC_REG_ENTRY` with delta 0.3 KB + 32 bytes per revoked index (batchable). Sovrin MainNet analysis shows average 12 revocations per batch, leading to ~0.7 KB per update.

### Security Proofs Sketch

**Lemma 1 (Link Secret Hiding):** Commitment `C = g^ls h^r` is perfectly hiding under discrete log assumption in G1; issuer view contains no information about `ls`.

*Proof.* Pedersen commitment with random `r` independent of `ls`. ∎

**Lemma 2 (Non-transferability):** If two holders collude to pool credentials with different link secrets `ls1 ≠ ls2`, equality proof fails unless they solve discrete log relation between commitments.

*Proof.* Equality proof requires proving same `ls` satisfies `C1 = g^{ls}h^{r1}` and `C2 = g^{ls}h^{r2}`. If `ls1 ≠ ls2`, adversary must produce `r1', r2'` breaking binding, reducible to discrete log. ∎

**Theorem 4 (Full Presentation Unforgeability):** Under q-SDH for BBS+, SXDH for PS, and hiding of link secret, no PPT adversary can forge a verifiable presentation for attributes not signed by honest issuer for same link secret, even after seeing polynomially many presentations.

*Proof sketch.* Reduce to BBS+ existential unforgeability via forking lemma extraction of signature from proof; link secret equality reduces to Pedersen binding. ∎

### Formal Verification

TLA+ model checking of revocation consistency found no violation of safety (`revoked ⇒ never verified as non-revoked`) for 3 epochs, 2 issuers, 10 credentials, under eventual ledger consistency. Liveness holds assuming holder eventually fetches latest witness delta.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Curve monoculture risk:** BBS+ standardizes on BLS12-381. Break of BLS12-381 (e.g., via TNFS improvements) would require re-issuance. AnonCreds v2 mitigates via suite agility to PS and future lattice PS variant [2][6], but migration still requires holder interaction.

2. **Link secret storage:** If holder device is compromised and `ls` exfiltrated, all credentials bound to that `ls` become transferable. Hardware binding via secure enclave and biometric gating mitigates but is not mandated by spec. User UX for `ls` backup is unresolved: exporting `ls` enables correlation if backup leaks.

3. **Revocation privacy vs. freshness:** Non-revocation proofs reveal *time of proof* via accumulator version used. An observer seeing multiple presentations with same `acc` version can infer holder updated witness at same time, providing coarse correlation. ALLOSAUR epochs exacerbate this by revealing epoch membership. Mitigation: verifier should accept any `acc` within freshness window, and holder should pre-fetch deltas proactively.

4. **No post-quantum security:** BBS+ and PS are pairing-based, broken by Shor. Post-quantum PS variant [6] has keys ~1.2 MB, signatures ~2.8 KB, proofs ~6 KB—impractical for mobile. AnonCreds v2 spec allows PQ suite but no production implementation yet.

5. **Ledger anchoring cost:** Indy requires endorsers for transactions; revocation updates incur ledger write fees. At 10 revocations/hour, annual ledger cost ≈ 87k transactions per issuer. Besu EVM deployment shifts cost to gas, but accumulator updates still O(R) calldata.

6. **Predicate proof expressiveness:** AnonCreds v1 supported only positive integer range proofs. v2 expands to signed integers, range, set membership, equality across credentials, but still lacks arbitrary circuit proofs (e.g., proving `hash(passportNumber) ∈ allowlist` without revealing passportNumber). Integration with general-purpose zk-SNARKs (Circom, Halo2) is future work.

7. **Threshold issuance:** Threshold BBS+ [3] enables distributed issuance without single point of failure, but introduces new trust assumption: threshold of issuers must be honest. Implementation in Rust lacks audit as of 2024.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a systems analysis of decentralized identity using BBS+ signatures in AnonCreds v2 on Hyperledger Indy, covering selective disclosure, link secret binding, and revocation accumulators. BBS+ provides constant-size signatures, unlinkable zero-knowledge proofs, and presentation header binding, reducing proof size 6× and verification 7× over CL while enabling modern curve tooling [1][3]. AnonCreds v2's pluggable suite and expanded schema preserve v1 semantics yet add crypto agility, predicate expressiveness, and ALLOSAUR revocation that eliminates tails files and scales to millions of credentials [4][6].

Link secret commitments prevent credential pooling without introducing correlating identifiers, provided holders sample uniformly and protect storage. Revocation via CL_ACCUM remains viable for small registries, but ALLOSAUR's epoch-partitioned accumulators offer superior scalability at modest proof size increase.

Future work includes: (i) formalizing per-verifier pseudonyms for abuse detection without cross-verifier tracking [1], (ii) integrating BBS blind signatures for fully blind issuance of arbitrary attributes, (iii) post-quantum PS instantiation benchmarking on mobile wallets, and (iv) standardizing domain separation for presentation header across DIDComm v2.

For production SSI, we recommend: BBS+ as default suite, link secret generated in secure enclave with encrypted backup, ALLOSAUR for any registry >10k credentials, and verifiers accepting accumulator freshness window ≥1 hour to minimize timing correlation.
