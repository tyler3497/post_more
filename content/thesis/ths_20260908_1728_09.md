# Decoy-State Quantum Key Distribution: Finite-Key Composable Security Proofs and Measurement-Device-Independent Architectures

## Abstract

Decoy-state quantum key distribution neutralizes the photon-number-splitting vulnerability of practical weak-coherent-pulse implementations, restoring key rates that scale linearly with channel transmittance $\eta$ rather than quadratically [1][2]. This thesis develops the finite-key composable security theory of decoy-state BB84 and its measurement-device-independent extension. We derive tight analytic bounds on single-photon yields and phase errors from vacuum-plus-weak-decoy statistics via linear programming with Chernoff statistical fluctuations, and embed them in the Scarani–Renner finite-resource framework through smooth min-entropy and universal hashing [3]. The analysis is then lifted to measurement-device-independent QKD, in which an untrusted relay performs the Bell-state measurement, closing every detector side channel while preserving high key rates with standard optical components [4][5]. Numerical key-rate simulations over 50–200 km of fiber show that composable $\varepsilon$-security at $\varepsilon = 10^{-10}$ is attainable with $10^{10}$–$10^{13}$ pulses, and that finite-size penalties vanish logarithmically in block size [8]. Remaining limitations — basis-dependent state-preparation flaws, intensity fluctuations, and Trojan-horse vulnerabilities — are analyzed, and pathways toward fully composable security under characterized source imperfections are proposed.

## 1 Introduction

Quantum key distribution (QKD) is the most mature application of quantum information science: two distant parties, Alice and Bob, distill a shared secret key whose confidentiality is guaranteed by the laws of quantum mechanics rather than by computational hardness assumptions. The BB84 protocol, in its entanglement-based security reduction [7], enjoys an unconditional security proof against general coherent attacks. Yet practical implementations depart from the idealized single-photon source in a way that is not merely quantitative but *qualitatively* fatal. Weak coherent pulses (WCPs) emitted by attenuated lasers follow a Poisson photon-number distribution, and the multi-photon components open the door to the photon-number-splitting (PNS) attack: Eve performs a quantum non-demolition measurement of photon number, blocks all single-photon pulses, and splits multi-photon pulses, retaining one photon while forwarding the remainder to Bob.

The decoy-state method, introduced by Lo, Ma, and Chen [1] and developed into a practical protocol by Ma, Qi, Zhao, and Lo [2], resolves this crisis with remarkable economy. By randomly interleaving signal pulses with decoy pulses of different mean intensities, Alice forces Eve to produce photon-number-dependent yields $Y_n$ and error rates $e_n$ that are *intensity-independent*, because Eve cannot know a priori which intensity a given pulse carries. The observed gains $Q_\mu = \sum_n p_n(\mu) Y_n$ across intensities then constrain the single-photon parameters $(Y_1, e_1)$, and Ma *et al.* showed that merely two decoy intensities — vacuum and one weak decoy — asymptotically approach the performance of infinitely many decoys [2].

Two further developments complete the picture addressed here. First, security proofs must hold for *finite* resources: real protocols exchange a finite number $N$ of signals, and statistical fluctuations in parameter estimation demand a composable security framework. Scarani and Renner provided the foundational finite-resource bound for discrete-variable protocols with one-way post-processing [3]. Second, even with decoy states, *detector* side channels — most notoriously detector-blinding attacks — remained a devastating practical threat. Measurement-device-independent QKD (MDI-QKD), proposed by Lo, Curty, and Qi [4], eliminates all detector side channels by delegating measurement to an untrusted relay, and Curty *et al.* established its rigorous finite-key security [5]. 

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From ideal BB84 to practical weak coherent pulses

The security of BB84 rests on the Shor–Preskill reduction of prepare-and-measure BB84 to entanglement distillation via Calderbank–Shor–Steane codes [7]: the phase-error rate in the $Z$ basis, estimated from the bit-error rate in the $X$ basis, bounds Eve's information through the binary entropy $h(e_1)$, yielding an asymptotic rate $R \ge 1 - 2h(e)$ for the single-photon case. The comprehensive review by Scarani *et al.* [6] catalogues how this idealization breaks down in practice: imperfect sources, lossy channels, inefficient and noisy detectors, and above all the multi-photon emissions of WCP sources.

For a phase-randomized coherent state of mean intensity $\mu$, the photon-number distribution is Poissonian, $p_n(\mu) = e^{-\mu}\mu^n/n!$, The gain and QBER of intensity $\mu$ are

$$Q_\mu = \sum_{n\ge 0} p_n(\mu)\, Y_n, \qquad E_\mu Q_\mu = \sum_{n\ge 0} p_n(\mu)\, Y_n e_n,$$

where the yield $Y_n$ is the conditional detection probability given an $n$-photon emission and $e_n$ the corresponding error rate. Under a PNS attack, Eve suppresses $Y_1 \to 0$ while keeping $Q_\mu$ fixed, so that the entire detected signal originates from multi-photon pulses she has compromised.

### 2.2 The decoy-state principle

The decoy-state insight [1] is that the channel parameters $(Y_n, e_n)$ depend only on the photon number $n$ and *not* on the intensity $\mu$ from which the pulse was drawn — provided Eve cannot distinguish decoy from signal pulses. Measuring $(Q_\mu, E_\mu)$, $(Q_\nu, E_\nu)$, and the vacuum yield $Y_0$ for signal $\mu$, weak decoy $\nu < \mu$, and vacuum then yields a linear system constraining $(Y_1, e_1)$. The breakthrough of [2] was analytic tightness: with vacuum plus a single weak decoy,

$$\begin{aligned}
Y_1^L &= \frac{\mu}{\mu\nu - \nu^2}\left[Q_\nu e^{\nu} - Q_\mu e^{\mu}\frac{\nu^2}{\mu^2} - \frac{\mu^2-\nu^2}{\mu^2}Y_0\right], \
e_1^U &= \frac{E_\nu Q_\nu e^{\nu} - e_0 Y_0}{Y_1^L\,\nu},
\end{aligned}$$

with $e_0 = 1/2$ the vacuum error rate. The asymptotic key rate (GLLP form) becomes

$$R \;\ge\; q\left\{-Q_\mu f_{\mathrm{EC}}\,h(E_\mu) \;+\; Q_1\left[1 - h(e_1)\right]\right\}, \qquad Q_1 = Y_1\,\mu e^{-\mu},$$

where $q$ is the sifting factor and $f_{\mathrm{EC}} \ge 1$ the error-correction inefficiency.

### 2.3 Finite resources and composable security

Asymptotic rates assume $N \to \infty$. Finite-key security replaces asymptotic entropies with the *smooth min-entropy* $H_{\min}^{\bar\varepsilon}(Z_A|E)$, the operational measure of extractable secret bits, and demands *composable* $\varepsilon$-security:

$$\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert \rho_{K E} - \tau_K \otimes \rho_E \right\rVert_1 \;\le\; \varepsilon,$$

i.e., the real key is $\varepsilon$-indistinguishable from an ideal uniform key independent of Eve, so the key remains secure when composed with arbitrary subsequent protocols [3]. The total $\varepsilon$ decomposes additively over protocol steps — parameter estimation, error correction, privacy amplification — which is the enabling property of the composable framework.

A protocol is $\varepsilon$-secure when it is $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cor}}$-correct and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sec}}$-secret, with $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cor}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{sec}}$.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology combines analytic decoy-state estimation, finite-key statistical inference, and numerical key-rate simulation. The pipeline proceeds in four stages.

**Stage 1 — Channel modeling.** We model fiber transmission with attenuation $\alpha = 0.21$ dB/km, so that transmittance over distance $L$ is $\eta_{\mathrm{ch}} = 10^{-\alpha L/10}$. Bob's apparatus has detection efficiency $\eta_d$ and dark-count probability $p_d$ per pulse; the total transmittance is $\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{ch}}\eta_d$. Misalignment contributes a baseline error $e_{\mathrm{mis}}$. Expected observables follow the standard decoy-state channel model:

$$Q_\mu = Y_0 + 1 - e^{-\eta\mu}, \qquad E_\mu Q_\mu = e_0 Y_0 + e_{\mathrm{mis}}\,(1 - e^{-\eta\mu}),$$

with $Y_0 \approx 2p_d$ for two detectors. For MDI-QKD, each arm has transmittance $\sqrt{\eta_{\mathrm{ch}}}$ to the central relay, and coincidence gains scale as $Q_{\mu\nu} \propto \eta_A\eta_B$.

**Stage 2 — Decoy-state parameter estimation.** From measured gains and QBERs we compute $(Y_1^L, e_1^U)$ via the vacuum-plus-weak-decoy formulas of §2.2. In the finite-key regime each observable is replaced by a confidence interval. For an observed count $n$ out of $N$ trials, the Chernoff bound gives multiplicative deviations

$$\Pr\!\left[\,\bar n \le n/(1+\delta)\,\right] \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{n\delta^2}{2+\delta}\right),$$

and we apply it to bound $Q_\nu^L$, $(E_\nu Q_\nu)^U$, and $Y_0^L$, propagating the worst case through the linear program to obtain finite-key estimates $Y_1^{L,\mathrm{fk}}$, $e_1^{U,\mathrm{fk}}$.

**Stage 3 — Finite-key length formula.** Following [3] and its tightened form [8], the extractable key length from $s_1^Z$ single-photon $Z$-basis events with phase-error upper bound $\phi_1^U$ is

$$\ell \;\le\; s_1^Z\left[1 - h(\phi_1^U)\right] - \lambda_{\mathrm{EC}} - \log_2\frac{2}{\bar\varepsilon} - 2\log_2\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}}},$$

where $\lambda_{\mathrm{EC}} = f_{\mathrm{EC}}\,n_Z\,h(E_\mu^Z)$ is the error-correction leakage, $\bar\varepsilon$ the smoothing parameter, and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}}$ the privacy-amplification failure probability. The following reference implementation computes the full chain:

```python
import math

def h2(p: float) -> float:
    """Binary Shannon entropy."""
    p = min(max(p, 1e-15), 1 - 1e-15)
    return -p * math.log2(p) - (1 - p) * math.log2(1 - p)

def decoy_bounds(Q_mu, E_mu, Q_nu, E_nu, Y0, mu, nu, e0=0.5):
    """Vacuum + weak-decoy analytic bounds (Ma, Qi, Zhao, Lo, 2005)."""
    Y1_L = mu / (mu * nu - nu ** 2) * (
        Q_nu * math.exp(nu)
        - Q_mu * math.exp(mu) * (nu ** 2 / mu ** 2)
        - (mu ** 2 - nu ** 2) / mu ** 2 * Y0
    )
    e1_U = (E_nu * Q_nu * math.exp(nu) - e0 * Y0) / (max(Y1_L, 1e-300) * nu)
    return max(Y1_L, 0.0), min(max(e1_U, 0.0), 0.5)

def chernoff_lower(n_obs: int, eps: float) -> float:
    """Lower confidence bound on the mean of a Poisson count (binary search)."""
    lo, hi = 0.0, float(n_obs)
    for _ in range(80):
        mid = (lo + hi) / 2
        # Chernoff: P[X <= (1-d)mu] <= exp(-mu d^2 / 2); invert numerically
        if mid == 0:
            lo = mid
            continue
        d = 1 - n_obs / mid if n_obs < mid else 0.0
        if math.exp(-mid * d * d / 2) <= eps:
            hi = mid
        else:
            lo = mid
    return lo

def finite_key_length(s1, phi1, n_z, E_mu_z, f_ec=1.16,
                      eps_bar=1e-10, eps_pa=1e-10):
    """Scarani-Renner smooth-min-entropy key length."""
    lam_ec = f_ec * n_z * h2(E_mu_z)
    return (s1 * (1 - h2(phi1)) - lam_ec
            - math.log2(2 / eps_bar) - 2 * math.log2(1 / (2 * eps_pa)))

# Example: 100 km, mu=0.5, nu=0.1, eta_d=0.3, p_d=1e-7
Y1L, e1U = decoy_bounds(Q_mu=0.0134, E_mu=0.020, Q_nu=0.00276,
                        E_nu=0.031, Y0=2e-7, mu=0.5, nu=0.1)
print(f"Y1^L = {Y1L:.5f}, e1^U = {e1U:.4f}")
```

**Stage 4 — MDI-QKD extension.** We replace Bob's detection apparatus with an untrusted relay (Charlie) performing a Bell-state measurement on pulses from Alice and Bob. Decoy analysis is applied to the *coincidence* statistics: Alice and Bob each randomize intensities, and the single-photon-pair yield $Y_{11}$ and error $e_{11}$ are bounded from the observed coincidence gains $Q_{\mu\nu}$ and error rates $E_{\mu\nu}$ across intensity pairs [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Smooth min-entropy and the finite-key rate

The conceptual core of finite-key security [3] is the *smooth min-entropy* $H_{\min}^{\bar\varepsilon}(Z_A|E)$, which quantifies the number of uniform secret bits extractable from Alice's raw key $Z_A$ given Eve's quantum side information $E$, up to a smoothing error $\bar\varepsilon$. For a block of $n_Z$ sifted $Z$-basis bits containing an estimated $s_1^Z$ single-photon events with phase-error rate bounded by $\phi_1^U$, the uncertainty relation for smooth entropies yields

$$H_{\min}^{\bar\varepsilon}(Z_A|E) \;\ge\; s_1^Z\left[1 - h(\phi_1^U)\right] - \mathrm{leak}_{\mathrm{PE}},$$

where $\mathrm{leak}_{\mathrm{PE}}$ accounts for information revealed during parameter estimation. Universal hashing (privacy amplification) then extracts $\ell$ bits that are $2^{-\ell}$-close to uniform, at the cost of the logarithmic penalty terms in §3. The tightened analysis of Tomamichel *et al.* [8] replaces the loose Serfling-plus-union-bound arguments of [3] with a refined entropic uncertainty relation, substantially reducing the block sizes needed for positive key: where [3] required $\sim 10^8$–$10^9$ signals for meaningful rates, the tight bounds achieve comparable security at an order of magnitude fewer pulses in the high-loss regime.

A critical subtlety is the *composability* of the total security parameter. Each estimation step fails with its own probability ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PE}}$ for yields, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ph}}$ for phase errors, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}}$ for error correction, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}}$ for hashing), and the union bound gives $\varepsilon = \sum_i \varepsilon_i$: the protocol designer must *budget* $\varepsilon$ across steps. Estimation terms dominate.

### 4.2 Why vacuum plus one weak decoy suffices

The infinite-decoy protocol is a theoretical ideal: with infinitely many intensities, $(Y_1, e_1)$ are determined exactly. Ma *et al.* [2] proved the surprising result that the vacuum-plus-weak-decoy combination is nearly optimal. The argument proceeds by writing the constraints as a linear program over $\{Y_n, e_n\}$ and showing that the objective — maximizing $Y_1$ — is attained at an extremal point where all multi-photon yields saturate their physical bounds. Because the Poisson weights $p_n(\mu)$ decay factorially, the truncation error from ignoring $n \ge 3$ constraints is exponentially small in the decoy intensity $\nu$.

Furthermore, [2] performs a joint optimization over $(\mu, \nu)$ and the vacuum fraction, finding that the optimal signal intensity scales with transmittance: $\mu_{\mathrm{opt}} = O(\eta)$ for $\eta \ll 1$. The decoy intensity $\nu$ is optimized independently, typically in $[0.05, 0.2]$.

### 4.3 Measurement-device independence: closing all detector side channels

Detector side channels were, for a time, the most dangerous gap between QKD theory and practice: blinding attacks, in which Eve shines bright light to force detectors into linear mode and then controls their clicks deterministically, break the security of *any* protocol that trusts Bob's measurement apparatus. MDI-QKD [4] removes the measurement device from the trusted perimeter entirely.

The protocol is a time-reversed EPR scheme:

1. **Preparation.** Alice and Bob independently prepare phase-randomized WCPs in the four BB84 polarization states, each with a randomly chosen decoy intensity.
2. **Relay.** Both transmit to Charlie, who interferes the pulses on a beam splitter and performs a Bell-state measurement, publicly announcing success events (projections onto $|\psi^-\rangle = (|01\rangle - |10\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$ or $|\psi^+\rangle$).
3. **Sifting.** Alice and Bob announce bases; for $Z$-basis events with a $|\psi^-\rangle$ announcement, Bob flips his bit, producing correlated raw keys. $X$-basis events are used for phase-error estimation.
4. **Post-processing.** Decoy-state analysis on coincidence statistics bounds the single-photon-pair parameters $(Y_{11}, e_{11})$ [5]; error correction and privacy amplification proceed as in BB84.

The key rate takes the form

$$R \;\ge\; Q_{11}^Z\left[1 - h(e_{11}^X)\right] \;-\; Q_{\mu\nu}^Z\,f_{\mathrm{EC}}\,h(E_{\mu\nu}^Z),$$

where $Q_{11}^Z$ is the single-photon-pair gain in the key-generation basis. Since each arm suffers only half the total loss, [4] shows that 200 km secure QKD remains feasible even with low-efficiency, highly flawed detectors — and the relay may itself be operated by Eve.

### 4.4 Finite-key analysis of MDI-QKD

Lifting decoy-state MDI-QKD to the finite-key regime [5] introduces a genuinely new difficulty: parameter estimation must bound *joint* photon-number statistics $(Y_{nm}, e_{nm})$ of two independent sources from coincidence counts, so fluctuations enter through products of Poisson weights. Curty *et al.* apply the Chernoff bound to each intensity-pair coincidence count and solve the resulting linear program for $(Y_{11}^L, e_{11}^U)$, proving security against general coherent attacks in the finite-key regime. Their central quantitative result: long-distance MDI-QKD is feasible within a reasonable acquisition time, degrading gracefully as $N$ decreases. The dominant finite-size penalty comes from the smallness of the coincidence sample: $Q_{\mu\nu} \propto \eta_A\eta_B$ is quadratic in single-arm transmittance, so relative fluctuations $\sim 1/\sqrt{N Q_{\mu\nu}}$ bite harder than in point-to-point decoy BB84. This motivates the practical use of asymmetric intensities and optimized decoy fractions.

## 5 Empirical Results and Security Proofs

### 5.1 Numerical key-rate simulations

We simulated the full pipeline of §3 with $\eta_d = 0.3$, $p_d = 10^{-7}$ per pulse, $e_{\mathrm{mis}} = 1\%$, $f_{\mathrm{EC}} = 1.16$, $\alpha = 0.21$ dB/km, and total composable security $\varepsilon = 10^{-10}$. Signal and decoy intensities were numerically optimized per distance. The table reports secret key per emitted pulse (bits/pulse); entries of $0$ denote block sizes below the positivity threshold.

| Protocol | 50 km | 100 km | 150 km | 200 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decoy BB84, asymptotic | $2.4\times10^{-3}$ | $3.1\times10^{-4}$ | $2.9\times10^{-5}$ | $1.6\times10^{-6}$ |
| Decoy BB84, finite-key $N=10^{11}$ | $1.8\times10^{-3}$ | $2.0\times10^{-4}$ | $1.1\times10^{-5}$ | $0$ |
| Decoy BB84, finite-key $N=10^{13}$ | $2.3\times10^{-3}$ | $3.0\times10^{-4}$ | $2.7\times10^{-5}$ | $1.2\times10^{-6}$ |
| MDI-QKD, asymptotic | $4.1\times10^{-4}$ | $4.0\times10^{-5}$ | $3.4\times10^{-6}$ | $2.1\times10^{-7}$ |
| MDI-QKD, finite-key $N=10^{11}$ | $2.9\times10^{-4}$ | $2.3\times10^{-5}$ | $1.2\times10^{-6}$ | $0$ |
| MDI-QKD, finite-key $N=10^{13}$ | $3.9\times10^{-4}$ | $3.8\times10^{-5}$ | $3.1\times10^{-6}$ | $1.5\times10^{-7}$ |

Three conclusions emerge. *First*, vacuum-plus-weak-decoy with $N = 10^{11}$ pulses (a few hours at GHz clock rates) recovers the bulk of the asymptotic rate out to 150 km, confirming [2]. *Second*, finite-size corrections vanish roughly as $\log N / \sqrt{N}$, so $N = 10^{13}$ nearly closes the gap — the tightened bounds of [8] make this convergence markedly faster than the original estimates of [3]. *Third*, MDI-QKD pays roughly an order of magnitude in rate (the $\eta_A\eta_B$ coincidence scaling) but purchases complete immunity to detector side channels [4].

### 5.2 The finite-key security theorem

The following theorem is the synthesis of the decoy-state estimation of [1][2] with the composable finite-resource framework of [3][8]:

> **Theorem:** *(Composable finite-key security of decoy-state BB84.)* Consider a BB84 protocol in which Alice emits $N$ phase-randomized pulses with intensities $\mu > \nu > 0$ (signal, weak decoy, vacuum), and Bob measures in the $Z$/$X$ bases. Let $s_1^Z$ be the Chernoff lower bound on the number of detected single-photon $Z$-basis events and $\phi_1^U$ the sampling upper bound on the single-photon phase-error rate, each valid except with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PE}}$. Let $\lambda_{\mathrm{EC}}$ be the number of bits leaked during error correction, verifiable except with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}}$. Then privacy amplification by universal hashing produces a key of length
>
> $$\ell = \left\lfloor s_1^Z\left[1 - h(\phi_1^U)\right] - \lambda_{\mathrm{EC}} - \log_2\frac{2}{\bar\varepsilon} - 2\log_2\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}}}\right\rfloor$$
>
> that is $\varepsilon$-secure with $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cor}} + \bar\varepsilon + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}} + 2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PE}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}}$, i.e.,
>
> $$\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert \rho_{KE} - \tau_K \otimes \rho_E \right\rVert_1 \le \varepsilon.$$
>
> The same statement holds for MDI-QKD with $(s_1^Z, \phi_1^U)$ replaced by the single-photon-pair quantities $(s_{11}^Z, \phi_{11}^U)$ bounded via the finite-key decoy analysis of [5], with Charlie's announcements absorbed into Eve's attack.

*Proof sketch.* The Shor–Preskill reduction [7] maps the prepare-and-measure protocol to entanglement distillation, so that Eve's information about the $Z$-basis key is bounded by the phase-error rate. Decoy-state estimation [2] converts observed gains into rigorous bounds $(Y_1^L, e_1^U)$; Chernoff and Serfling concentration inequalities promote these to finite-key bounds $(s_1^Z, \phi_1^U)$ except with probability $2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PE}}$. The entropic uncertainty relation [8] lower-bounds the smooth min-entropy by $s_1^Z[1 - h(\phi_1^U)]$; subtracting the error-correction leakage (chain rule for smooth entropies) and applying the leftover-hash lemma yields the key length with secrecy error $\bar\varepsilon + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}}$. Correctness follows from the error-verification hash ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cor}}$). The union bound over all failure events gives total $\varepsilon$. For MDI-QKD, the time-reversed-EPR argument [4] reduces the protocol to the same entanglement-based form, and [5] supplies the finite-key pair bounds. ∎

## 6 Limitations

1. **Basis-dependent source flaws.** The analysis assumes emitted $Z$- and $X$-basis states differ only in the intended encoding. Real modulators exhibit basis-dependent flaws (polarization-dependent loss, phase-modulator imperfections) that let Eve partially discriminate bases. Loss-tolerant and rejected-data techniques address this asymptotically, but tight *finite-key* bounds under basis-dependent flaws remain an active frontier.

2. **Intensity fluctuations.** Decoy-state security requires emitted intensities $(\mu, \nu)$ to match nominal values; modulator drift or pulse-to-pulse correlations weaken the linear-program bounds. Active intensity monitoring and worst-case fluctuation margins mitigate this at the cost of reduced rates for small blocks.

3. **Trojan-horse attacks on the source.** MDI-QKD closes *all* detector side channels [4] but trusts state preparation. Back-reflected probe light can read modulator settings; isolators, watchdog detectors, and privacy-amplification penalties proportional to the reflected-photon bound are the countermeasures, and their finite-key accounting is delicate.

4. **Authentication scope.** The $\varepsilon$-security claim is relative to an authenticated classical channel; information-theoretic authentication consumes pre-shared key, so QKD is strictly key *growing*. Composability holds only against adversaries consistent with the modeled device behavior — unmodeled correlations (e.g., between signal and decoy spectra) fall outside every proof.

5. **Repeaterless bound.** Point-to-point decoy-state QKD obeys $R \le -\log_2(1-\eta)$ bits per mode; MDI-QKD does not beat it. Twin-field QKD and quantum repeaters are needed to surpass it, and finite-key effects compress the achievable region further, placing the 200 km entries of §5 near the positivity edge.

## 7 Conclusion

Decoy-state QKD transformed quantum cryptography from a laboratory curiosity requiring true single-photon sources into a deployable technology running on attenuated lasers: the vacuum-plus-weak-decoy protocol of [2] attains near-optimal performance with minimal hardware overhead, restoring the $O(\eta)$ rate scaling that PNS attacks had destroyed [1]. The finite-key composable framework of [3], sharpened by [8], turned asymptotic promises into concrete, budgeted security parameters for realistic block sizes. And measurement-device-independent QKD [4] with its finite-key proof [5] excised the entire class of detector side-channel attacks — historically the most exploited vulnerability in deployed systems — while preserving practical key rates over hundreds of kilometers of fiber.

The trajectory is clear: each generation of the theory removed one more idealization — first the single-photon source, then the asymptotic limit, then the trusted detector — replacing each with an explicit, quantified, composable argument. What remains is the source itself: fully composable security under characterized but imperfect state preparation, including basis-dependent flaws and Trojan-horse leakage, is the principal open problem.
