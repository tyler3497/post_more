# De Novo Genome Assembly: de Bruijn Graphs, Long-Read Technologies, and the Telomere-to-Telomere Era of Complete Genomes

## Abstract

Reconstructing a complete genome sequence from millions of sequencing fragments — *de novo* assembly — is one of the foundational computational problems of genomics. This thesis traces the arc from the overlap-layout-consensus paradigm of the Human Genome Project era, through the reduction of assembly to the Eulerian path problem on de Bruijn graphs [1], to the string-graph formulation that eliminated k-mer decomposition [2]. We develop the graph-theoretic machinery — Eulerian trails, transitive reduction, tip and bubble simplification — and show how sequencing technology redefined the problem: noisy long reads demanded correction-first hierarchical pipelines such as Canu [7], while PacBio circular-consensus (HiFi) reads [3] made accurate all-vs-all overlap graphs practical again, enabling haplotype-resolved phased assembly with hifiasm [4]. The culmination is the Telomere-to-Telomere Consortium's complete 3.055-gigabase human genome T2T-CHM13 [5]. We formalize quality metrics — N50, NG50, k-mer-based quality values — derive the Lander-Waterman coverage statistics, and close with the open problems of diploid-complete, polyploid, and metagenomic assembly.

## 1 Intro

In 1995, the 1.8-megabase genome of *Haemophilus influenzae* was assembled from random shotgun fragments, proving whole-genome shotgun sequencing viable [1]. By 2001, two competing drafts of the 3-gigabase human genome had been published. Every milestone confronted the same mathematical object: a multiset of short strings (reads) sampled at random from an unknown long string (the genome), to be reconstructed without a reference template. This is *de novo* assembly, and it remains the hardest inference problem in sequence bioinformatics.

The difficulty is not data volume but genome structure. Roughly half of the human genome is repetitive: transposable elements, segmental duplications, tandem arrays, centromeric satellites. A repeat longer than the read length is *fundamentally unresolvable* — the reads contain zero information distinguishing its copies. This is the **repeat problem**, and the history of assembly is the history of weapons built against it: longer reads, more accurate reads, smarter graphs.

Three eras define the field. The **Sanger era** used overlap-layout-consensus (OLC): pairwise overlaps, Hamiltonian layout, consensus. The **short-read era** shattered reads into k-mers and assembled de Bruijn graphs via Eulerian paths, trading read coherence for computational elegance [1]. The **long-read era** resurrected OLC as string graphs [2][7], then fused length with accuracy through HiFi sequencing [3] and phased assembly graphs [4], culminating in the first truly complete human genome [5].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The shotgun sampling model

Reads of length *L* are sampled uniformly from a genome of length *G*, giving *N* reads at coverage *c = NL / G*. Lander and Waterman modeled read starts as a Poisson process:

> **Theorem:** (Lander–Waterman coverage.) The fraction of the genome covered by at least one read is *1 − e^(−c)*, and the expected number of gaps is approximately *N·e^(−c)*.

At *c = 30*, the expected uncovered fraction is *e^(−30) ≈ 10^(−13)* — coverage is not the problem. The hidden assumption is that every position is *distinguishable*. Repeats break distinguishability, and no coverage repairs it.

### 2.2 Overlap-layout-consensus

The classical OLC pipeline:

1. **Overlap** — all significant pairwise read overlaps (originally Smith-Waterman-like alignment, later seed-and-extend heuristics [6]).
2. **Layout** — order reads into a consistent path: the *shortest common superstring* problem, NP-hard, equivalent to a Hamiltonian path through the overlap graph.
3. **Consensus** — multiple alignment of laid-out reads; errors corrected by majority vote.

OLC assemblers (Celera, Arachne) powered the Human Genome Project. But all-vs-all overlap scales quadratically and Hamiltonian layout is intractable in the worst case — greedy heuristics fail precisely in repeats.

### 2.3 Eulerian versus Hamiltonian

Pevzner, Tang, and Waterman observed the hardness is an artifact of representation [1]. Shred reads into k-mers and build the **de Bruijn graph** — nodes are *(k−1)*-mers, directed edges are k-mers — and assembly becomes finding a path using *every edge exactly once*: an Eulerian path.

| Problem | Graph | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| OLC layout | Overlap graph (reads as nodes) | Hamiltonian path — NP-hard |
| de Bruijn assembly | de Bruijn graph (k-mers as edges) | Eulerian path — linear time |

> **Theorem:** (Euler–Hierholzer.) A directed graph admits an Eulerian circuit iff every nonzero-degree vertex lies in one weakly connected component and in-degree equals out-degree everywhere. An Eulerian trail exists iff at most one vertex has out-degree exceeding in-degree by one, at most one has the reverse imbalance, and the rest are balanced.

Hierholzer's algorithm finds such trails in linear time. The NP-hard layout problem dissolves — at the cost of losing long-range read coherence, the wound of the short-read era.

### 2.4 Sequencing technologies

| Technology | Read length | Accuracy | Error profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sanger | ~800 bp | >99.99% | Substitutions |
| Illumina | 100–300 bp | >99.9% | Substitutions |
| PacBio CLR | 10–100 kb | ~85–90% | Indels, random |
| PacBio HiFi (CCS) | 10–25 kb | >99.9% | Rare, random |
| Oxford Nanopore | 10 kb – 2 Mb+ | ~98–99%+ | Indels, homopolymers |

PacBio circular consensus sequencing reads each template molecule repeatedly, yielding HiFi reads above 99.8% accuracy at 13.5 kb mean length [3]. Nanopore threads DNA through a protein pore, producing ultra-long reads that span entire centromeres. The field's central realization: **length resolves repeats, accuracy resolves graphs** — the winning technology supplies both.

### 2.5 Quality metrics

- **Contig**: gapless assembled sequence. **Scaffold**: ordered contigs with estimated gaps.
- **N50**: length *L* such that contigs of length ≥ *L* cover half the *assembly*. **NG50** uses the known *genome* size instead, penalizing incompleteness.
- **QV**: *QV = −10·log10(P_error)*, Phred-scaled per-base error; estimable reference-free from k-mer survival spectra.
- **Completeness**: conserved single-copy gene recovery (BUSCO) or k-mer completeness.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Constructing the de Bruijn graph

Each k-mer becomes a directed edge from its *(k−1)*-mer prefix to its *(k−1)*-mer suffix; parallel edges record coverage. Assembly is then Hierholzer's algorithm:

```python
from collections import defaultdict

def debruijn(reads, k):
    """Nodes are (k-1)-mers, edges are k-mers."""
    graph = defaultdict(list)
    indeg, outdeg = defaultdict(int), defaultdict(int)
    for read in reads:
        for i in range(len(read) - k + 1):
            u, v = read[i:i+k-1], read[i+1:i+k]
            graph[u].append(v)
            outdeg[u] += 1; indeg[v] += 1
    return graph, indeg, outdeg

def eulerian_trail(graph, indeg, outdeg):
    """Hierholzer's algorithm: linear-time Eulerian trail."""
    g = {u: list(vs) for u, vs in graph.items()}
    start = next((u for u in g if outdeg[u] - indeg[u] == 1), next(iter(g)))
    stack, trail = [start], []
    while stack:
        u = stack[-1]
        if g.get(u):
            stack.append(g[u].pop())
        else:
            trail.append(stack.pop())
    return trail[::-1]
```

Error-free, with every genomic k-mer present, spelling the trail recovers the genome exactly. Sequencing errors inject false k-mers creating characteristic artifacts.

### 3.2 Graph simplification

- **Tips** — dead-end branches from errors near read ends; pruned when short and low-coverage.
- **Bubbles** — alternative paths between one node pair, from errors or heterozygosity. Error bubbles are *popped* (keep high-coverage path); heterozygous bubbles must be *preserved* for diploid assembly (Section 4.3).
- **Bulges** — short detours removed by topological simplification.

True k-mers appear at depth ≈ *c*; error k-mers are rare. This statistical separation is what makes short-read assembly possible.

### 3.3 The string graph

Myers showed k-mer shredding is unnecessary [2]. In the **string graph**, reads are nodes and edges are overlaps — minus *transitive* edges: if *a→b→c* is consistent, the direct *a→c* edge is redundant. **Transitive reduction** runs in linear expected time, collapsing the quadratic overlap graph to a sparse graph preserving exactly the inferable adjacencies. Contigs are maximal non-branching paths (*unitigs*). With 15-kb reads, overlaps are long and informative, and transitive reduction is devastatingly effective — the foundation of Canu [7] and repeat-graph methods [8].

### 3.4 The hierarchical long-read pipeline

Noisy long reads (~10–15% error) cannot be overlapped at k-mer resolution. The hierarchical strategy [7]:

1. **Overlap** — all-vs-all alignment with adaptive tf-idf MinHash k-mer weighting to find overlaps despite errors.
2. **Correct** — consensus of each read's overlap pile; random errors average out.
3. **Trim** — remove chimeric or unsupported read ends.
4. **Assemble** — string graph from corrected reads; extract unitigs.
5. **Polish** — re-align raw reads with minimap2 [6]; correct residuals (Arrow, Racon, Medaka).

HiFi reads short-circuit this pipeline: above 99.9% accuracy, correction is nearly unnecessary, and hifiasm [4] builds overlap graphs directly from raw reads — the elegance of the de Bruijn era with the coherence of OLC.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The repeat problem, formally

A repeat *R* of length *r* with *m* copies collapses in the de Bruijn graph onto one path of coverage *m·c*, flanked by *m* entry and *m* exit branches. Pairing entries to correct exits requires information spanning the repeat. Hence the fundamental law: **a repeat is resolvable iff some read or read pair spans it entirely**. Contiguity is limited not by coverage but by the *read-length vs. repeat-length* race.

Repeat graphs [8] generalize this for long noisy reads: approximate disjointigs replace exact k-mers, then reads are threaded through the graph to *unfold* repeats — using coverage and long-range connectivity to disambiguate copies, the graph equivalent of scaffolding with unbounded insert size.

### 4.2 From CLR to HiFi

The 2017–2019 transition is the hinge of modern assembly. Continuous long reads (CLR) gave length at 85–90% accuracy, forcing the expensive correct-then-assemble hierarchy [7]. Circular consensus sequencing changed the trade: HiFi reads of 10–25 kb at >99.9% accuracy [3]. Wenger et al. showed HiFi-only human assembly reaching contig N50 above 15 megabases with 99.997% concordance to the Genome-in-a-Bottle benchmark — *substantially outperforming assembly with less-accurate long reads* [3].

Accuracy collapses two hard problems. Overlap detection becomes near-exact: minimap2 [6] seed-chain-align finds true overlaps with negligible false positives. Error correction — the slowest, most heuristic CLR stage — shrinks to light polishing. And graph bubbles become overwhelmingly *biological* (heterozygous variants) rather than technical noise — which is precisely what enables phasing.

### 4.3 Phased assembly graphs

A diploid human genome differs between haplotypes at roughly one position per kilobase; each heterozygous site forms a graph bubble. A *collapsed* assembly pops all bubbles into a mosaic haploid consensus; a *phased* assembly preserves both paths.

hifiasm [4] builds a **phased assembly graph** preserving the contiguity of *all* haplotypes:

1. **Informative-position correction** — a position with exactly two base types, each supported by ≥3 reads, marks haplotype differences; overlaps inconsistent across informative positions are discarded, separating haplotypes during correction.
2. **Haplotype-aware cleaning** — bubble popping is restricted so heterozygous bubbles survive as parallel paths.
3. **Graph trio binning** — parental k-mers assign unitigs (longer and less ambiguous than reads) to haplotypes, far surpassing read-level trio binning.

On human — and even hexaploid, ~30-Gb California redwood — datasets, hifiasm delivered haplotype-resolved assemblies where prior tools produced collapsed mosaics [4]. The bubble becomes signal instead of artifact.

### 4.4 Case study: the telomere-to-telomere human genome

The T2T Consortium targeted CHM13, a complete hydatidiform mole cell line that is effectively haploid, isolating the repeat problem in pure form [5]. The data: 30× PacBio HiFi, 120× Nanopore ultra-long, 100× Illumina PCR-free, 70× Hi-C, BioNano optical maps, Strand-seq. The backbone was a **high-resolution string graph built directly from HiFi reads** [5] — Myers's 2005 formalism [2], vindicated at genome scale.

T2T-CHM13: 3.055 billion base pairs, gapless on every chromosome except Y, adding ~200 megabases absent from GRCh38 — all centromeric satellite arrays, recent segmental duplications, the short arms of the five acrocentric chromosomes [5]. The new sequence held 1,956 gene predictions, 99 protein-coding. Centromeres — megabase arrays of ~171-bp alpha-satellite monomers in higher-order repeats — had resisted every prior technology; ultra-long reads finally spanned them. Estimated consensus accuracy exceeded Q70: under one error per ten megabases [5].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Correctness of Eulerian assembly

> **Theorem:** (Idealized correctness.) With no exact repeat of length ≥ *k* and every genomic k-mer present error-free, the de Bruijn graph has a unique Eulerian trail (up to rotation), and spelling it recovers the genome exactly.

*Proof sketch.* Without length-*k* repeats, each k-mer has a unique genomic position, so the genome spells an Eulerian trail using each edge once. Any Eulerian trail must follow genomic adjacency — successors are forced by k-mer uniqueness — and balance conditions make the trail unique. ∎

Repeats violate the hypothesis: the graph branches and multiple Eulerian trails exist. This is not algorithmic failure but *non-identifiability* — the data underdetermine the genome, exactly as Section 4.1 formalizes.

### 5.2 Coverage statistics

Under the Poisson model, a base is uncovered with probability *e^(−c)*; expected contig count is *N·e^(−c)* and expected contig length grows as *(e^c − 1)·L / c* — exponential in coverage. Beyond ~30×, coverage-completeness gains are negligible; remaining gaps are repeat-induced, not sampling-induced.

### 5.3 Assembler benchmarks

| Assembly | Technology | Contig N50 | Gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRCh38 (2013) | Sanger + mixed | ~56 Mb | ~900+ |
| Canu human (2017) [7] | PacBio CLR | >21 Mb (NG50) | hundreds |
| HiFi human (2019) [3] | PacBio HiFi | >15 Mb | dozens |
| hifiasm human (2021) [4] | PacBio HiFi | tens of Mb | few, phased |
| T2T-CHM13 (2022) [5] | HiFi + ONT UL + Hi-C | chromosome-length | 0 (excl. Y) |

Contiguity rises monotonically from megabases to whole chromosomes; accuracy from Q40 to above Q70.

### 5.4 Eulerian traversal in Haskell

Hierholzer's algorithm as a functional fold over an edge multiset:

```haskell
-- Hierholzer's algorithm: linear-time Eulerian trail
hierholzer :: Ord v => v -> [(v, v)] -> [v]
hierholzer start edges = go [start] (buildAdj edges) []
  where
    buildAdj = foldr (\(u,v) m -> insertWith (++) u [v] m) empty
    go [] _ trail = trail
    go (u:stack) adj trail =
      case lookup u adj of
        Just (v:vs) -> go (v:u:stack) (insert u vs adj) trail
        _           -> go stack adj (u:trail)
```

The invariant — stack vertices retain unused outgoing edges, trail vertices are exhausted — consumes each edge exactly once: *O(E)* time.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Diploid-complete assembly.** CHM13 exploited effective haploidy. Assembling *both* haplotypes telomere-to-telomere in a normal diploid — separating near-identical homologs across centromeres — remains unsolved at scale, though phased graphs [4] lead the way.
2. **Segmental duplications.** Recent duplications above 99% identity over hundreds of kilobases defeat HiFi; only ultra-long reads spanning whole duplication blocks resolve them, and some arrays stay ambiguous.
3. **Centromere polymorphism.** Higher-order repeat arrays vary wildly between individuals and homologs; one reference cannot capture this diversity, motivating pangenome graphs.
4. **Metagenomic assembly.** Strain mixtures collapse into chimeric consensus; strain-level resolution from short reads is provably limited, and long-read metagenomics is still maturing.
5. **Computational cost.** All-vs-all overlap remains the bottleneck — minimap2 [6] and MinHash sketching [7] make it practical but not cheap: hundreds of CPU-hours and large memory per human genome.
6. **Validation without ground truth.** For novel species there is no reference; k-mer QV and read concordance are proxies, and repeat misassemblies can be invisible to all of them.
7. **Telomeres and rDNA.** Terminal (TTAGGG)n tracts and ribosomal DNA arrays on acrocentric short arms sit at the extreme of resolvability; T2T required targeted manual curation there [5].

---

## 7 Conclusion

Assembly has traveled from NP-hard Hamiltonian layout to linear-time Eulerian traversal [1], from k-mer shredding back to read-coherent string graphs [2], from error-dominated heuristics to near-exact overlap graphs on HiFi reads [3][4][7]. Each transition was forced by the same function: the repeat problem yields only to information spanning repeats, and sequencing technology finally supplies it. T2T-CHM13 [5] is capstone and beginning at once — proof that complete genomes are computable, and a challenge to extend completeness to diploids, pangenomes, and ecosystems. The graph remains the central object; what changed is that, for the first time, the graph tells the truth.
