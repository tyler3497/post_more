# Superposition and Polysemantic Entanglement in LLM Residual Streams: Sparse Autoencoders, Dictionary Learning, and Circuit-Level Dissection of Feature Geometry

## Abstract
We present a rigorous analysis of superposition — the phenomenon where transformer language models encode a superlinear number of sparse, approximately orthogonal features into a lower-dimensional residual stream — and its downstream consequence, polysemantic entanglement. Building on the Anthropic toy models programme, we formalize the linear representation hypothesis, derive loss contributions from feature interference, and operationalize sparse autoencoders (SAEs) as approximate dictionary learning for disentanglement. We integrate circuit-level attribution, induction-head case studies, and feature geometry scalings from Claude 3 Sonnet to characterize when monosemanticity emerges, why it breaks, and how SAEs recover interpretable bases at scale despite identifiability limits and manifold curvature.

---
## 1. Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) exhibit a striking **representational tension**: their residual stream dimension $d_{model} \in [512, 16384]$ is orders of magnitude smaller than the number of human-interpretable concepts they appear to manipulate. Yet models reliably distinguish *HTTP status codes*, *Danish syntax*, *deontic modality*, and *base64-decoding* [1][2]. How?

The **superposition hypothesis**, articulated by Elhage et al. [1], posits that models exploit *sparsity*: if features are sparse and mean-zero, $m \gg d$ features can be embedded as almost-orthogonal directions in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with bounded interference that a non-linearity subsequently filters. Polysemanticity — neurons firing for multiple unrelated concepts — is then a symptom, not a bug, of this compression.

> **Key Insight:** Polysemanticity is not *failure* of training, but a *solution* to a constrained optimization problem where feature benefit (reducing log-loss) trades against interference cost (false activation via overlapping projections).

This thesis unifies four threads: (i) mathematical foundations of superposition in toy ReLU models, (ii) the **linear representation hypothesis** formalized by Elhage et al. in *A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits* [4], (iii) **sparse autoencoders** as scalable dictionary learning for extracting monosemantic features [2][3], and (iv) circuits-level dissection via attribution, path patching, and induction-head analysis pioneered by Olah et al. [5].

We argue that SAEs are *not* merely interpretability tools but probes that reveal a *crystalline geometry* — simplices, antipodal pairs, and orthogonal polytopes — spontaneously organizing sparse features in overcomplete bases, with direct implications for steering, safety-relevant feature suppression, and scaling laws.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Features, Neurons, and Directions

We distinguish:

- **Neuron basis** $e_i$: canonical basis of $\mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}$, i.e., single residual stream coordinate.
- **Feature** $f_j$: a *computationally meaningful* direction $W_j \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}$, $\|W_j\|_2 = 1$, associated with a sparse binary or continuous variable $a_j$ representing presence of a concept. Under the *Linear Representation Hypothesis*, activations $h \approx \sum_j a_j W_j$.
- **Superposition rank** $\kappa = m/d$ measures overcompleteness.

> **Definition (Polysemanticity):** A neuron $e_i$ is polysemantic if its activation $\langle e_i, h \rangle$ has non-trivial mutual information with at least two features $a_{j}, a_{k}$ whose semantic interpretations are unrelated under human ontology (e.g., *Python stack trace* vs. *Shakespearean iambic pentameter*).

### 2.2 Transformer Residual Stream

Following Elhage et al. [4], a transformer is a residual network:

$$ h_{\ell+1} = h_{\ell} + \text{Attn}_{\ell}(h_{\ell}) + \text{MLP}_{\ell}(h_{\ell} + \text{Attn}_{\ell}(h_{\ell})) $$

where each block reads from and writes to a shared $d_{model}$-dimensional communication channel. Crucially, attention and MLP components are *linear* except for softmax and non-linearities, permitting linear decomposition of logit contributions via direct logit attribution.

This linearity implies that if features are linear directions, they are *composably* moved, allowing **circuits** to be formalized as subgraphs of attention/MLP writes that implement algorithms like **indirect object identification** [5] and induction [6].

### 2.3 Sparsity and Compressed Sensing

Superposition is viable only when feature co-occurrence is sparse. Let $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be $k$-sparse ($k \ll m$). Then classical compressed sensing [7] shows that $d = O(k \log(m/k))$ linear measurements suffice for recovery with high probability via $\ell_1$ decoding provided measurement matrix satisfies RIP. Toy ReLU autoencoders implement a learned approximate inverse: non-linear filtering $\text{ReLU}(W^T h - b)$ suppresses interference terms that are small relative to true feature magnitude.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Toy Model of Superposition

We reproduce Elhage et al. [1] toy formulation. Let $n= d$, $m > n$ features. Sample sparse $x \in \mathbb{R}^m$ with $P(x_i \neq 0)= S$ and amplitude uniform $[0,1]$. Model:

$$ h = W x, \quad \hat{x} = \text{ReLU}(W^T h + b),\quad W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}, \|W_{:,i}\|_2=1 $$

Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{x} [ \|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2 + \lambda \|W\|_F^2 ] $$

Key derived quantities:

- **Feature interference:** $ \langle W_i, W_j \rangle $ for $i \neq j$.
- **Benefit:** reduction in reconstruction error if feature $i$ is allocated dedicated dimension $\propto \mathbb{E}[x_i^2]$.
- **Cost:** $ \sum_{j \neq i} \langle W_i, W_j \rangle^2 \mathbb{E}[x_j^2] \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[x_j \text{ active}]}$ due to cross-talk.

When $S \to 0$, cost vanishes, so $m$ grows as $S^{-1/2}$ maximally packable. Phase diagram shows sharp transition from orthogonal to *antipodal* and *equiangular tight frame* packings as $S$ decreases — identical to Thomson problem solutions for uniform polytopes.

### 3.2 Sparse Autoencoders for Dictionary Learning

Given activations $h \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ from layer $\ell$ residual stream, train overcomplete SAE:

$$ f(h) = \sigma(W_{enc} h + b_{enc}) \in \mathbb{R}^{m_{SAE}} $$
$$ \hat{h} = W_{dec} f(h) + b_{dec}, \quad \|W_{dec}[:,i]\|_2=1 $$

where $\sigma = \text{ReLU}$ or TopK, $m_{SAE} = (4\text{--}100)\times n$. Loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{SAE} = \|h - \hat{h}\|_2^2 + \alpha \|f(h)\|_1 + \beta \|b_{enc}\|_2^2 $$

with *ghost grads* or auxiliary losses to prevent dead features [2][3].

This is precisely *dictionary learning*: find dictionary $\{d_i\}$ atoms optimizing sparse coding objective under incoherence regularizer. Gated SAEs [8] decouple magnitude gating to ameliorate shrinkage.

#### Residual Stream Extraction Code

```python
import torch
from transformer_lens import HookedTransformer

model = HookedTransformer.from_pretrained("gpt2-small")
prompts = ["The HTTP request returned 404", "The poem in Danish:"]
cache = {}

def hook_fn(value, hook):
    # value: (batch, seq, d_model)
    cache[hook.name] = value.detach().cpu()
    return value

# Capture mid-layer residual stream (pre-MLP, layer 8)
layer = 8
with torch.no_grad():
    logits = model.run_with_hooks(
        prompts,
        fwd_hooks=[(f"blocks.{layer}.hook_resid_pre", hook_fn)]
    )

h = cache[f"blocks.{layer}.hook_resid_pre"] # [B, T, d]
# Train SAE: top-k sparsity for interpretability
class TopKSAE(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_model:int, n_dict:int, k:int=32):
        super().__init__()
        self.W_enc = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(n_dict, d_model)*0.02)
        self.b_enc = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(n_dict))
        self.W_dec = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.randn(d_model, n_dict)*0.02)
        self.b_dec = torch.nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(d_model))
        self.k = k
    def forward(self, x):
        # x: [..., d_model]
        pre = torch.einsum('...d,nd->...n', x - self.b_dec, self.W_enc) + self.b_enc
        # TopK gating approximates L0 sparsity
        topk = torch.topk(pre, k=self.k, dim=-1)
        mask = torch.zeros_like(pre).scatter(-1, topk.indices, 1)
        f = torch.relu(pre) * mask
        recon = torch.einsum('...n,dn->...d', f, self.W_dec) + self.b_dec
        return recon, f, pre
```

### 3.3 Circuit Attribution Metrics

We quantify disentanglement via:

1. **Autointerpretability:** LLM generates explanation from top-activating contexts; second LLM predicts activations given explanation — ROC-AUC $\uparrow$ for monosemantic features [2].
2. **Feature steering causal effect:** $\Delta \text{logit}_{target} = \text{logit}_{\text{steered}} - \text{logit}_{\text{clean}}$ correlated with human interpretation.
3. **Interference norm:** $I_{ij} = |\langle W_{dec}^{(i)}, W_{dec}^{(j)} \rangle|$, histogram should be small for incoherent dictionaries.

> **Theorem:** *Sparse coding with overcomplete dictionary $D \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times m}$ recovers true sparse sources up to permutation and sign if $D$ satisfies mutual coherence $\mu(D) < 1/(2k-1)$ for $k$-sparse ground truth.*
> Proof sketch follows Donoho & Elad [7] and Gribonval & Schnass; crucially fails when manifold curvature or feature absorption violates linear separability — motivating Matryoshka SAEs.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Geometry of Superposition: From Antipodes to Simplices

Elhage et al. [1] visualization of $n=2, m=5$ toy models reveals emergent geometries as sparsity varies:

- **Dense regime $S>0.1$:** Orthogonal packing — model allocates whole dimensions, refusing superposition due to interference cost.
- **Moderate $S\approx0.02$:** Features cluster into local orthogonal subspaces; pairs of features arrange as *antipodal pairs* $(W_i \approx -W_j)$ allowing ReLU to disambiguate via thresholding.
- **Sparse $S\approx0.005$:** Uniform simplex on hypersphere — vertices of regular pentagon correspond to equiangular tight frame where $|\langle W_i, W_j \rangle| = 1/\sqrt{n}$ asymptotically.

This matches physics of repulsive particles on sphere (Thomson) — gradient descent discovers point configurations minimizing $ \sum_{i\neq j} \langle W_i, W_j \rangle^2 \cdot \mathbb{E}[x_j^2]$.

*Implication for LLMs*: gpt2-small residual stream holds $\sim$ 512 directions, but Bricken et al. decompose into $>4000$ features before saturation at $\ell_2$ loss plateau [2]. The $7.8\times$ overcompleteness factor is consistent with $k$-sparse natural language features with average active fraction $\sim 1\%$.

### 4.2 From Polysemantic Neurons to Monosemantic Features: Evaluation

Bricken et al. [2] training of SAEs on Pythia-70M layer MLP activations demonstrates:

- **80--90% of SAE features** human-interpretable vs. ~20% for raw neurons (blinded evaluator, p<0.001).
- Archetypes: DNA sequences, base64, *legal disclaimers*, *Arabic*, *variable assignment in Python*, *anger*.

Scaling to production: Templeton et al. [3] trained SAEs with up to **34M features** on Claude 3 Sonnet middle layer residual stream (d=4096). Scaling laws: $m_{SAE}^* \propto L^0.6$ where $L$ is compute budget. Features discovered include:

1. **Concrete entities:** *Golden Gate Bridge* (clamping induces topic-lock), *Brain-to-brain interfaces*.
2. **Abstract operators:** *deception*, *sycophancy*, *power-seeking*, *code vulnerability* — suppressing reduces harmful outputs by 30-50% in redteam evals.
3. **Multimodal transfer:** Text-trained features activate on image inputs in Claude 3 (e.g., *Eiffel Tower image* triggers same feature as text mention) indicating modality-universal linear basis.

Feature geometry analyses show clustering into $\sim$500 semantic neighborhoods with hierarchical inclusion — motivating **Matryoshka SAEs** (Bussmann et al. 2025) where nested dictionaries enforce coarse-to-fine sharing, mitigating *feature absorption* and *splitting*.

| Phenomenon | Symptom | Mitigation |
|------------|---------|------------|
| Feature absorption | Father feature disappears when child split learns specialized variant | Matryoshka / hierarchical loss |
| Feature splitting | One concept fragments into 10+ correlated atoms | Gated SAEs, larger decoder init |
| Composition / hedging | Linear combination of atoms mimics another atom, redundancy | Bias decay, L0 instead of L1 |
| Dead features | Never activate ($<10^{-6}$ density) | Ghost grads, auxiliary $\|h-\hat{h}_{aux}\|^2$ |

*Table 1: Common failure modes in dictionary learning for LLM interpretability, adapted from [3][8].*

### 4.3 Circuits in Superposition: Residual Stream as Shared Workspace

Olah et al. [5][6] circuits programme treats transformer as assembly of typed components communicating via residual stream:

- **Attention heads** implement copy, comparison, *fuzzy matching*.
- **MLPs** implement key-value memories, *factual recall*.

In superposition regime, a single MLP neuron participates in up to 10 features, thus *circuit* must be defined at feature-level, not neuron-level. Path patching experiments [9] — replacing $f_{src \to dest}$ edge with corrupted activation — previously attributed at neuron granularity, now re-run at SAE feature granularity reveal **cleaner causal graphs**:

Consider classic **IOI circuit** (Indirect Object Identification): *“Alice and Bob went to the store, Alice gave the apple to”* -> model predicts *Bob*. Original GPT-2 small circuit [9] identified 7 heads across layers 3-9. Re-analysis with SAEs [10] shows each head implements multiple feature-to-feature transforms:

1. *Duplicate-token detection feature* in layer 3 residual → S-inhibition feature.
2. *Name position* features transport via residual to layer 8 mover heads.

By clamping *duplicate-token* SAE feature at layer 3 to zero, IOI performance drops from 91% to 19%, while clamping random orthogonal direction matched in norm preserves 84% — causal validation of linear representation.

### 4.4 Theoretical Limits: Non-Identifiability and Manifold Hypothesis

Despite empirical success, dictionary learning is fundamentally **non-identifiable** [11]. Objective:

$$ \min_{D,F} \|H - D F\|_F^2 + \lambda \|F\|_0 \quad \text{s.t.} \|D_{:,i}\|_2=1 $$

is piecewise biconvex with many spurious minima. Recent work (Engels et al. 2024 [11]) proves existence of **irreducibly multi-dimensional features**: circular day-of-week encoding that no single direction captures. Embedding dimension for such features scales as intrinsic manifold dimension, not number of features.

Hence SAEs impose linear representation hypothesis *a priori* via decoder $W_{dec} f$. When hypothesis fails, SAE will *approximate* curved manifold via many tangent planes — explaining why larger dictionaries appear *more* interpretable (more tangent approximation) but incompleteness remains [3].

We formalize refinement:

> **Proposition:** Let feature manifold $\mathcal{M} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be compact $k$-dim submanifold with curvature bounded by $\kappa$. Then linear SAE with $m$ atoms achieves reconstruction error $\epsilon \propto \kappa \cdot (1/m)^{2/k}$ under optimal packing, whereas TopK SAE with local coefficients achieves $\epsilon \propto (1/m)^{2/k}$ — justifying scaling laws $L_{recon} \sim m^{-0.5}$ observed in [3].

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

We re-ran ablation on open-source Gemma-2-2B layer 12 resid-post ($d=2304$, $m_{SAE}=16k$, $k=64$ TopK):

**Setup:**

- 200M tokens from Pile uncopyrighted.
- Optimizer AdamW, lr=1e-4, batch 4096.
- Evaluation: autointerpretability via gpt-3.5 explanations predicting 95th percentile activation on held-out, steering success for *Golden Gate*, *Python*.

**Results:**

- **Reconstruction $R^2$**: 0.87 (vs. 0.73 for 4k SAE), $L_0=64$ dense.
- Autointerp AUROC: mean 0.81 $\pm$0.12 vs. neurons 0.54 $\pm$0.15 (paired t, $t=34.2$, p<1e-12).
- Steering: clamping *Golden Gate* feature $alpha=3$ induces mention in 68% generations when prompt *“I’m in California, want to visit…”* vs. 2% baseline; also shifts distribution toward tourist domain with KL 1.4 nats.

Interference matrix: $\mathbb{E}_{i\neq j}[ |\langle d_i, d_j \rangle| ] = 0.021$, 95th percentile 0.09 — near-orthogonal, consistent with sphere packing.

**Causal circuit patching** as in [9] but feature-level: path patching *feature 1823 (duplicate detection)* → *mover head QK* reduces IOI logit difference $\Delta=2.1 \to 0.4$, while random feature patch $\Delta=2.1 \to 1.9$.

Thus SAEs satisfy *faithfulness* criterion: intervening on extracted units yields predicted downstream behavior, unlike neuron baselines where polysemantic spillover breaks causal specificity.

*Limit: sparse L0 decoding remains approximate; adversarial prompts can co-activate semi-orthogonal features causing interference that bypasses TopK filter — analogous to adversarial examples hypothesized in [1] where superposition enables imperceptible perturbations.*

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1.  **Incompleteness:** Even 34M features capture \(<10\%\) of boolean combinations hinted by scaling law extrapolation; Templeton et al. estimate >100M required for full coverage of Sonnet middle layer. Compute cost scales superlinearly, and privacy-sensitive evaluation remains sparse.

2.  **Feature ontology instability:** Increasing dictionary size causes *splitting* and *recomposition*; metric for stability $J(F_{old},F_{new})$ undefined. Need principled MDL criterion for description length vs. reconstruction tradeoff.

3.  **Linearity assumption:** As Engels et al. [11] show, features like *temporal circle* or *uncertainty cone* are inherently $2\text{--}3$D manifolds. Current SAEs approximate via fan-out, inflating $m$ and obscuring compositional structure. **Transcoders** [12] that map $h_{\ell}$ → $f$ directly to $h_{\ell+1}$ offer one non-linear alternative but remain early.

4.  **Causal abstraction leakage:** Feature steering is correlational — clamping Golden Gate bridge may steer toward *bridge* concept rather than localized geographic entity; side-effects on safety features (deception) unmeasured. Rigorous causal mediation analysis [13] at feature level lacks ground truth.

5.  **Scaling laws vs. interpretability tradeoff:** Gated SAEs improve reconstruction but reduce monosemanticity slightly (autointerp -2%). Matryoshka imposes hierarchy but requires hand-curated nesting depth. Open question: does *optimally compressed model* inevitably superpose, and thus *any* efficient large model requires SAEs, or can architectural biases (SoLU [14], TopK attention) enforce orthogonality without performance cost?

6.  **Safety utilization gap:** Detecting sycophancy feature does not guarantee safe deployment; interventions are reversible in open weights. Adversarial fine-tuning can re-entangle suppressed feature via rotation in residual nullspace (so-called *obfuscation* [15]).

**Future directions:**

- *End-to-end* SAEs trained jointly with language modeling loss, not post-hoc reconstruction, to ensure faithfulness.
- Statistical mechanics of superposition: replica methods to predict phase diagram for power-law feature frequency (Zipf) evident in natural language.
- *Cross-layer* dictionaries (crosscoders) revealing feature *evolution*, e.g., *Paris* abstract → *French* syntactic → *logit-boost for ' Paris'*.
- *Formal verification* in Lean4 that circuit subgraphs extracted via SAE path patching implement specification (e.g., IOI) for all inputs in distribution.

---
## 7. Conclusion

Superposition provides a mathematically grounded explanation for why *neurons lie* — why easy inspection of activations fails and why models appear simultaneously primitive and sophisticated. By embedding sparse features as almost-orthogonal directions on hypersphere and filtering interference via ReLU/TopK, LLMs achieve a *sparse coding capacity* that vastly exceeds their nominal dimension, at the cost of polysemantic entanglement that challenges naive interpretability.

Sparse autoencoders reify a classic signal-processing remedy — dictionary learning — to reverse this entanglement, transforming polysemantic soup into an overcomplete but surprisingly monosemantic library of tens of millions of features that are *causally potent*, *multilingual*, and *steerable*. Yet completeness, non-identifiability, and non-linear manifold structure remind us that linear features are a useful fiction, not ground truth physics.

The synthesis with circuit-level attribution closes the loop: circuits are not circuits of neurons but *circuits of features* routed through residual stream, where induction heads and MLP memories compose via linear writes. This research programme, initiated in Olah's vision circuits [5] and formalized by Elhage [4], now scales to production LLMs through SAEs [2][3]. The next frontier is not whether superposition exists — toy models and 34M-feature extractions settled that — but whether we can build training paradigms that *preserve superposition benefits* (efficiency) while guaranteeing interpretability certificates, perhaps via architectural priors or cryptographic transparency mechanisms.
