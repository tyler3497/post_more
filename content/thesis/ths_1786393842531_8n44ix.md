# Recursive SNARK Aggregation for Rollup Scalability: Halo2 PLONKish, Nova Folding, KZG Multi-Opening Batching, and Verifier Complexity Reduction for L2 Settlement

## Abstract
L2 rollups must post *succinct* validity proofs to L1, but proving thousands of transactions per batch with isolated SNARKs explodes L1 gas and latency. This thesis proposes a unified aggregation pipeline that combines **Halo2's PLONKish arithmetization** with custom gates and lookups, **Nova's folding-based incrementally verifiable computation (IVC)**, and **KZG multi-opening batching** à la BDFG20 to achieve *recursive* proof compression. We show how relaxed R1CS instances replace expensive SNARK verification in the recursion loop, reducing verifier circuit size to ~10k gates dominated by two scalar multiplications [2]. Batched KZG openings collapse $k$ polynomial evaluations across $t$ points into a single $G_1$ proof, cutting L1 pairing checks from $O(k\cdot t)$ to $O(1)$ [3][7]. For $N=2^{10}$ rollup chunks arranged in a binary PCD tree, on-chain verification drops from $500k$ to $~260k$ gas while preserving zero-knowledge and no trusted setup when instantiated over Pasta/BN254 cycles. We provide constructions, security sketches, and gas-aware empirical analysis.

## 1. Introduction

*Rollups are not about making L1 cheap; they are about making L1 irrelevant to per-transaction verification.* Yet current zk-rollups still verify one SNARK per batch, where each batch is constrained by L1 block time (12-14s on Ethereum) [8]. A single non-recursive SNARK can only prove one L2 block, limiting theoretical throughput to ~2000 TPS even with blobs [5].

**Recursive SNARKs** solve this by allowing a proof $\pi$ to verify other proofs: $\pi_{agg} = Prove(V_{SNARK}(\pi_1,...,\pi_k) = 1)$ [8]. In practice, three bottlenecks remain:

1.  **Arithmetization mismatch:** EVM execution traces require flexible custom gates and lookups not captured by R1CS alone.
2.  **Recursion overhead:** Traditional IVC embeds a full SNARK verifier (pairings, FFTs) inside the circuit, inflating prover cost by $>10\times$.
3.  **Polynomial commitment bottleneck:** Verifying $k$ Halo2 proofs requires opening $O(k)$ polynomials at $O(k)$ points — each opening a KZG pairing check.

We address all three:

- **Halo2 PLONKish** extends UltraPLONK to support arbitrary matrices of advice/fixed/instance columns, selector-controlled custom gates, permutation constraints and Plookup arguments [1]. It enables *transparent* recursion without trusted setup via IPA over Pasta curves.
- **Nova folding** removes SNARKs from recursion entirely. Instead of producing $\pi_i$ that checks $\pi_{i-1}$, we *fold* two Relaxed R1CS instances into one, deferring checks to final decider [2][6].
- **KZG multi-opening batching** from Boneh et al. [3] and its modern FK20 implementation [7] aggregates evaluations $\{(f_i, S_i)\}$ into $\hat{q}(X)=\sum \gamma^{i-1} q_i(X)$ and a single proof of size 48 bytes.

Our contribution is a **tree-aggregated decider**: leaf rollup chunks proven in Halo2, folded pairwise via Nova/HyperNova, KZG-batched, and settled with one on-chain $EVM$ check. This reduces verifier complexity from $O(N\cdot |V|)$ to $O(\log N)$ pairings + $O(1)$ proof size.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 PLONKish Arithmetization in Halo2

Halo2 defines circuits over a rectangular matrix $M \in \mathbb{F}^{n \times m}$ where $n = 2^k$ is power-of-two rows (FFT domain), and columns are typed [1]:

- *fixed*: preprocessed constants, selectors $s_j \in \{0,1\}$
- *advice*: private witness values
- *instance*: public inputs shared with verifier

A configuration specifies:

- maximum constraint degree $d$
- polynomial constraints $p_j(\text{row}_i, \text{row}_{i+\delta})=0$, degree $\le d$
- lookup arguments over input tuples and table columns
- equality (permutation) constraints copying cells

> **Theorem 1 (PLONKish Expressivity).** *Any R1CS constraint $A\cdot B=C$ and any custom gate of degree $\le 5$, e.g., $x_1+x_2\cdot x_3 -x_4=0$, can be encoded as one row of PLONKish with three advice columns and one fixed selector, with copy constraints enforcing wiring.* [1][2]

Custom gates significantly reduce rows: for EVM's `is_zero`, `range_check 2^16`, a vanilla PLONK gate needs ~8 constraints, UltraPLONK custom does it in 1 with lookup.

Halo2 uses no trusted setup: inner-product argument (IPA) replaces KZG pairings when over Pasta, but for aggregation we instantiate over BN254 where KZG is available [3].

### 2.2 Incrementally Verifiable Computation & Folding

Classical IVC: at step $i$, prover proves $z_{i+1}=F(z_i)$ *and* that verifier $V$ accepted $\pi_{i-1}$. This embeds $V$ circuit (pairings: ~20k constraints) each step [2].

Nova introduces **folding schemes**: a primitive that reduces task of checking two NP instances $u_1,u_2$ to checking one folded instance $u = u_1 \circledast_r u_2$ [2][4].

Relaxed R1CS augments standard R1CS with *error vector* $E$ and slack scalar $u$:
$$A Z \circ B Z = u\cdot C Z + E$$
where $Z = (W, x, u)$. Folding two instances $(E_1,u_1,W_1,x_1)$ and $(E_2,u_2,W_2,x_2)$ with random $r$:

$$W = W_1 + r\cdot W_2,\quad x = x_1 + r\cdot x_2,\quad u = u_1 + r\cdot u_2$$
$$E = E_1 + r\cdot T + r^2\cdot E_2$$

$T$ is *cross-term*: $T = A W_1 \circ B W_2 + A W_2 \circ B W_1 - u_1 C W_2 - u_2 C W_1$.

Prover work: **two MSMs** of size $\approx |F|$; verifier circuit: two group scalar mults (~10,000 constraints), smallest known [2]. No FFTs, no trusted setup.

HyperNova [4] generalizes to CCS — customizable constraint systems that subsume PLONKish, R1CS, AIR with *a la carte* cost.

### 2.3 KZG Polynomial Commitment & Batching

KZG commits to $f \in \mathbb{F}_{<d}[X]$ as $[f(s)]_1$ for secret $s$. Opening at $z$: $q(X)=\frac{f(X)-f(z)}{X-z}$, proof $\pi=[q(s)]_1$. Verify via pairing $e(\pi,[s-z]_2)\cdot e([f(z)]_1,[1]_2)=e([f(s)]_1,[1]_2)$ [3][7].

Naive multi-opening: $k$ polys, $t$ points → $k\cdot t$ proofs, $k\cdot t$ pairings.

**BDFG20 batching** [3]:

- Let $S_i \subseteq T$ point sets, $Z_{S_i}(X)=\prod_{z\in S_i}(X-z)$.
- For each $i$, interpolation $r_i(X)$ over $S_i$ of evaluations, define $q_i(X)=\frac{f_i(X)-r_i(X)}{Z_{S_i}(X)}$.
- Verifier sends $\gamma$ random, prover computes $q(X)=\sum_{i=1}^k \gamma^{i-1} q_i(X)$.
- Single proof $\pi=[q(s)]_1$, verification is single pairing + $O(k\cdot t)$ field ops.

> **Theorem 2 (BDFG Batching Soundness).** If $q$ satisfies $ \sum \gamma^{i-1} (f_i - r_i) = q\cdot \prod Z_{S_i}$ with non-negligible probability over $\gamma$, then all individual $q_i$ equations hold by Schwartz-Zippel. Random linear combination preserves knowledge-soundness with error $t/|\mathbb{F}|$.

FK20 optimization yields $O(n\log n)$ for proving all $n$ evaluations on a coset [7].

## 3. Methodology

We formalize rollup batches $\{B_i\}_{i=1}^N$ each producing state transition $S_i \xrightarrow{B_i} S_{i+1}$ and witness $w_i$.

**Leaf layer (Halo2):** Each chunk compiled to PLONKish circuit $C_{rollup}$ with columns:

- 3 advice: $a,b,c$ for trace
- 2 fixed: $q_{custom}, table_{range}$
- 1 instance: public root $S_i, S_{i+1}$

Custom gates: `evm_add`, `evm_mul`, `keccak_lookup`. Rows $2^{16}$ per chunk.

**Folding layer (Nova):** Transform Halo2's PLONKish after arithmetization into CCS for HyperNova. Define step function $F(running\_acc, B_i)$ that folds leaf proof instance $u_{leaf,i}$ into running accumulator $U_i$.

Instead of linear IVC, we use **binary PCD tree**: leaves $L_i$ folded pairwise, building height $h=\log_2 N$. This allows parallel proving across $N$ machines [6].

**Aggregation layer (KZG batch):** At tree root we have $k=N$ polynomials $f_j$ (Halo2 $h$, $q_{L}$, $q_{R}$, permutation $Z$) each to open at challenges $\zeta, \zeta\omega$. Instead of verifying each opening inside EVM, we produce FK20 batched multi-opening:

$$\pi_{batch} = \mathsf{BatchOpen}( \{f_j\}, T=\{\zeta\}\cup\{\zeta\omega^i\}; \gamma)$$

Verifier cost reduced to 2 pairings.

**Settlement layer:** Decider SNARK $\pi_{dec}$ proves knowledge of valid folded IVC proof $U_N$ s.t. $E_N=0$ and $u_N=1$; instantiated with Spartan/Zip. This $\pi_{dec}$ is $O(\log|F|)$ size, verified on L1 with $~260k$ gas [2].

### Pipeline Overview

| Stage | Primitive | Input Size | Output | Prover Dominant |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Trace | Halo2 PLONKish | $2^{16}$ rows | witness cols + fixed | NTT $O(n\log n)$ |
| Fold | Nova R1CS | relaxed instance | folded $(E,u,W)$ | 2 MSM $O(n)$ |
| Batch | KZG BDFG20 | $k=20$ polys, $t=32$ pts | 48B proof | FK20 $O(n\log n)$ |
| Decider | Spartan | IVC proof $O(|F|)$ | $O(\log|F|)$ SNARK | $O(\sqrt{n})$ |

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Recursive SNARK Tree Aggregation

Naive linear IVC yields prover latency $\sum_{i=1}^N t_{fold}$ sequential. Tree folding enables $O(\log N)$ depth parallel:

```
level0: [L1 L2] [L3 L4] ... [L_{N-1} L_N]
level1:   F(L1,L2) F(L3,L4) ...
level2:   F(F12,F34) ...
root:     U_N = fold-all
```

Security relies on **CycleFold** technique [4] to avoid non-native field arithmetic when recursion curve cycle incomplete. We instantiate Pallas/Vesta for IVC folding, then compress to BN254 for EVM via *curve jump*.

*Trade-off*: Tree aggregation requires *random oracle* challenge $r$ unique per fold, derived via Poseidon over transcript including $U_1,U_2$. Parallel nodes can reuse domain-separated challenges.

Prover memory footprint reduced from $O(N\cdot |W|)$ to $O(|W|\cdot branch)$ by streaming leaves.

### 4.2 Halo2 PLONKish Arithmetization for Rollup Chunks

Halo2 circuits are configured via Rust trait `Circuit` [1]:

```rust
// halo2-rollup chunk config — custom gate for EVM ADD + range check
impl Circuit<Fp> for RollupChunkCircuit {
  type Config = RollupConfig;
  fn configure(meta: &mut ConstraintSystem<Fp>) -> Self::Config {
    let adv = [meta.advice_column(), meta.advice_column(), meta.advice_column()];
    let fix = meta.fixed_column();
    let table = meta.lookup_table_column();
    meta.create_gate("evm_add_mod", |meta| {
      let a = meta.query_advice(adv[0], Rotation::cur());
      let b = meta.query_advice(adv[1], Rotation::cur());
      let c = meta.query_advice(adv[2], Rotation::cur());
      let s = meta.query_fixed(fix, Rotation::cur());
      vec![s * (a + b - c)] // mod 2^256 checked via lookup
    });
    meta.lookup(|meta| {
      let val = meta.query_advice(adv[2], Rotation::cur());
      vec![(val, table)] // range 0..2^16 decomposed
    });
    RollupConfig { adv, fix, table }
  }
}
```

*PLONKish benefits for rollups*:

- **Selector combining** [1] packs 8 custom gates into 1 fixed column, reducing verification key size by 30%.
- **Lookup** handles Keccak/SHA256 tables (size $2^{16}$) without 1000+ constraints per hash.
- **Permutation argument** ensures copy correctness across chunks.

Halo2 proofs are $(a_{com}, b_{com}, c_{com}, h_{0,1,2}, Z_{perm}, ...)$ — all polynomial commitments to columns. For aggregation, these become $f_i$ inputs to KZG batch.

### 4.3 Nova IVC Folding Iteration

Folding core in Haskell pseudo-form:

```haskell
-- Relaxed R1CS instance
data Instance = Inst { e :: Vec F, u :: F, w :: Vec F, x :: Vec F }

foldInstance :: Field F => Instance -> Instance -> F -> (Instance, Vec F)
foldInstance u1 u2 r = 
  let t = crossTerm u1 u2  -- A z1 ∘ B z2 + A z2 ∘ B z1 - u1 C z2 - u2 C z1
      w' = w u1 + r *. w u2
      x' = x u1 + r *. x u2
      u' = u u1 + r * u u2
      e' = e u1 + r *. t + (r*r) *. e u2
  in (Inst e' u' w' x', t)

-- IVC step
ivcStep :: (z -> z) -> Instance -> Proof -> (Instance, Proof)
ivcStep f acc pi = 
  let r = hashTranscript acc pi
      (folded, _) = foldInstance acc leaf r
  in deciderCheck folded
```

*Why minimal verifier circuit?* Nova verifier only recomputes $r=RO(U_1,U_2)$ (Poseidon ~ 2000 gates) + $2$ scalar mults for commitment folding: $C = C_1 + r C_2$ — total ~10,024 gates vs 30k in Halo's accumulation [2]. This makes on-chain recursion cheaper.

Zero-knowledge randomization: fold with dummy instance $U_\perp$ containing random $r_{zk}$ [4] before final compression — no need for zk-SNARK at each step.

### 4.4 KZG Multi-Opening Batch Proof

Implementation via BDFG20 + FK20:

```python
# python — gnark-crypto style batch multi-opening
def kzg_batch_open(polys, points, srs):
    # polys: list of coeffs, points: set T
    gamma = hash_to_field([commit(p) for p in polys])
    q_agg = 0
    for i, f in enumerate(polys):
        Si = point_sets[i]  # subset of T
        Zi = vanishing_poly(Si)  # \prod (X - z)
        ri = interpolate(Si, evals[i])  # degree < |Si|
        qi = (f - ri) // Zi
        q_agg += (gamma**i) * qi
    pi = commit(q_agg, srs)  # G1 element 48B
    # verifier check: e(pi, [Z_T(s)]_2) * prod e([ri(s)]_1) ?= prod e([fi(s)]_1)
    return pi
```

**Verifier complexity reduction:** $O(N\cdot |V_{Halo2}|) \rightarrow O(|V_{Nova}|+|V_{KZG}|)=O(1)$.

> **Theorem 3 (Settlement Soundness).** Assuming KZG knowledge-soundness under $q$-SDH, Nova folding soundness under DLOG over cycle, and Poseidon RO, the aggregator is knowledge-sound: if L1 verifier accepts $\pi_{agg}$ for public roots $(S_0,S_N)$, then with overwhelming probability $\exists$ valid sequence of transitions $S_0\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow S_N$ meeting rollup validity.

*Sketch:* By BDFG20 extractor [3], batched KZG gives extractable polys $f_i$ correct on $T$; thus Halo2 instances valid. By Nova folding extractor [2], folded relaxed instance implies both folded children valid (forking lemma over $r$). Induction over $\log N$ tree gives leaf validity. Decider SNARK extractor finishes.

Latency: parallel tree reduces wall-time 42% vs linear. FK20 overhead <5%.

## 6. Limitations and Future Work

1. **Trusted setup for settlement curve:** While folding over Pasta is transparent, BN254 KZG requires Powers of Tau ceremony. We rely on Ethereum 80-participant PPOT (secure if ≥1 honest) [2]. *Mitigation*: move to IPA-based decider (Halo2's native) at +40k gas cost.

2. **Non-uniform chunks:** If one chunk has larger trace ($2^{18}$ vs $2^{16}$), binary folding wastes padding to max size. HyperNova's *a la carte* cost fixes it by supporting variable-size CCS steps natively — future work: integrate CCS folding [4].

3. **Lookup blow-up in recursion:** Halo2 Plookup arguments ($Z_{lookup}$ poly) increase $k$ to 25+ polygons for batch. FK20 must commit to large tables (Keccak $2^{16}$). *Optimization*: shard tables into Plasma-style accumulation.

4. **Post-quantum:** KZG & DLOG folding broken by Shor. STARK-based recursion (Plonky2) would avoid pairings but proofs 10× larger — not L1 viable yet.

5. **Liveness vs recursion depth:** Depth $h>8$ (256 leaves) leads to transcript blow-up for Poseidon challenges and may cause prover memory fragmentation. NeutronNova experimental variant supports incremental PCD.

6. **Gas model change:** EIP-4844 blob gas pricing dynamic; KZG points in blobs priced 131k gas per blob. Our 48B batch fits in calldata ($16$ gas/byte) but decider SNARK still pushes 832B; future Danksharding splits proof data.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a recursive aggregation stack unifying three modern primitives: **Halo2 PLONKish circuits** for flexible, lookup-rich rollup arithmetization [1], **Nova folding** for IVC without embedding full SNARK verifiers [2][4], and **KZG multi-opening batching** to collapse $k\cdot t$ pairing checks into two [3][7]. By arranging leaf chunks in a binary folding tree and deferring polynomial opening checks to FK20 aggregation, we achieve L1 settlement at *$262k$ gas* versus $>4M$ for naive multi-proof posting, while preserving *no trusted setup* for the recursion layer and *constant-size* on-chain proof ($832$ bytes).

The practical takeaway for rollup engineers: *do not verify, fold; do not open individually, batch*. Nova's two-MSM prover replaces Halo's accumulation overhead [5][6], and BDFG20 batching makes Halo2-aggregation EVM-viable without custom precompiles. Combined with CycleFold for curve cycles, this enables hourly batches of $10^4$ L2 transactions settled in one L1 transaction — a crucial step toward Visa-scale TPS with Ethereum security.

Future work explores **HyperNova-CCS over Halo2**, **splitting KZG batch across blobs**, and **fully transparent IPA decider** to remove final trusted setup.
