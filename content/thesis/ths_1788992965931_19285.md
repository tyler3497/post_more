# Identifiable Latent Structure: A Rigorous Account of Nonlinear ICA, Interventional Identifiability, and Causal Representation Learning

## Abstract

The unsupervised recovery of latent causal variables from high-dimensional observations is the central program of *causal representation learning* [2]. Plain nonlinear independent component analysis (ICA) is unidentifiable in general: infinitely many distinct solutions fit the same observed distribution, with no canonical relation among them [6]. This thesis develops a unified, rigorous account of how identifiability is restored. We survey three modern remedies: (i) **auxiliary-variable identifiability** — conditioning on an observed auxiliary variable such as a time-segment index, class label, or domain index recovers sources up to permutation and component-wise transforms via time-contrastive learning and the identifiable VAE (iVAE) [1]; (ii) **causal disentanglement** — the impossibility theorems of Locatello et al. [3] on why unsupervised disentanglement cannot be identified, and how independent-mechanism assumptions and interventional data repair the theory; and (iii) **invariance-based learning** — invariant risk minimization (IRM) [4], which recovers causally stable predictors across environments. We state the identifiability theorems, discuss estimation, compare methods in a unified table, and delimit the open theoretical frontier.

## 1. Introduction

Modern machine learning learns *representations*: mappings from raw observations $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ to latent codes $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ that capture "meaningful" factors of variation. Classical statistics begins the other way around, from a *generative model*

$$\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z}), \qquad p_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{z}) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p_{z_i}(z_i),$$

where $\mathbf{f}$ is a smooth mixing function and the latent sources $z_i$ are statistically independent. This is the nonlinear ICA model. The question of *identifiability* asks: given only samples from $p_{\mathbf{x}}$, can we recover $\mathbf{f}$ and the distribution of $\mathbf{z}$ — uniquely, or at least up to an interpretable equivalence class? It is the epistemic license for representation learning: without identifiability, disentanglement has no ground truth to converge to.

For *linear* ICA, $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}$, the answer is reassuring: under non-Gaussianity of at most one source, $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{z}$ are identifiable up to permutation and scaling [5]. For *nonlinear* ICA, the answer is negative — catastrophically so. Hyvärinen and Pajunen (1999) showed that without further structure, nonlinear ICA admits infinitely many solutions that fit the data distribution exactly yet recover entirely different "sources" [6]. The rotated-Gaussian measure-preserving automorphism is the canonical counterexample: map data to an isotropic Gaussian, rotate, map back; the observed distribution is unchanged while the recovered components are arbitrary.

Two broad resolutions emerged. The *structural* resolution constrains the mixing function itself — sparsity, mechanism independence, or algorithmic independence of the conditionals. The *auxiliary-variable* resolution assumes the sources are independent only *conditional* on an additionally observed variable $\mathbf{u}$, such as a segment index in nonstationary time series, a class label, or a domain index. Under mild regularity, this single extra observable restores identifiability — and with it, a principled notion of disentanglement, causal discovery from high-dimensional data, and invariant prediction across environments.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The nonlinear ICA generative model

We formalize the setting. Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be observed, generated by an unknown *smooth injective mixing function* $\mathbf{f}: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathcal{X}$ from latent sources $\mathbf{z} = (z_1, \dots, z_n)^\top$ with independent components:

$$\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z}), \qquad p(\mathbf{z}) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p_i(z_i). \tag{1}$$

A model is *identifiable* if equality of the observed distributions implies the parameters coincide up to a known equivalence relation. The natural equivalence for nonlinear ICA is **$\sim_A$-identifiability**: there exists a permutation $\pi$ and invertible component-wise functions $q_i$ with $\hat{z}_i = q_i(z_{\pi(i)})$ a.s. — the same indeterminacies as linear ICA (ordering and nonlinear rescaling), yielding a *principled disentanglement*.

### 2.2 The impossibility result

The canonical negative result is due to Hyvärinen and Pajunen [6]: for general smooth $\mathbf{f}$ and general $p_{\mathbf{z}}$, nonlinear ICA is *unidentifiable*. The proof exhibits a measure-preserving automorphism of any absolutely continuous distribution — e.g., the *rotated-Gaussian* map (normalizing flow to an isotropic Gaussian, a rotation, the inverse map) — which preserves the observed distribution exactly while scrambling the sources arbitrarily. Without auxiliary assumptions, "disentanglement" has no ground truth to converge to [3].

### 2.3 Auxiliary variables: conditional independence

The breakthrough framework assumes an *additionally observed* auxiliary variable $\mathbf{u}$ — a time-segment index, class label, domain indicator, or the past history $\mathbf{x}_{t-1}$ — such that

$$p(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{u}) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p_i(z_i \mid \mathbf{u}). \tag{2}$$

The sources are *marginally dependent* but *conditionally independent* given $\mathbf{u}$. The key insight: if the conditional distribution of each source *varies sufficiently* with $\mathbf{u}$, the mixing $\mathbf{f}$ is pinned down. Time-contrastive learning (TCL; Hyvärinen & Morioka 2016) trains a classifier to distinguish true $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u})$ pairs from pairs with permuted $\mathbf{u}$; permutation-contrastive learning (PCL; Hyvärinen & Morioka 2017) uses temporal dependence; and the identifiable VAE (iVAE; Khemakhem et al. 2020) [1] integrates the theory into a maximum-likelihood VAE framework handling noise, undercomplete observations, and discrete data.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The conditionally-factorized exponential-family prior

The iVAE [1] assumes each source's conditional density belongs to an exponential family of fixed order $k$:

$$p_{T, \lambda}(z_i \mid \mathbf{u}) = \frac{Q_i(z_i)}{Z_i(\mathbf{u})} \exp\!\left( \sum_{j=1}^{k} T_{i,j}(z_i)\, \lambda_{i,j}(\mathbf{u}) \right), \tag{3}$$

where $T_i = (T_{i,1}, \dots, T_{i,k})$ are sufficient statistics, $\lambda_i(\mathbf{u})$ are parameters output by an arbitrary function of $\mathbf{u}$, and $Z_i$ normalizes.

### 3.2 Estimation by variational inference

The iVAE maximizes the evidence lower bound with the *conditional* prior:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u})}\!\left[ \log p_\theta(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z}) \right] - D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left( q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) \, \| \, p_{T,\lambda}(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{u}) \right). \tag{4}$$

Under the identifiability conditions of Section 5, optimizing (4) recovers the true $\mathbf{f}$ and the true sources up to $\sim_A$ equivalence — the VAE posterior $q_\phi$ approximates the *true* posterior, a statement unattainable for vanilla VAEs [1][3]. Identifiable normalizing flows arise as a noiseless special case.

A minimal implementation sketch illustrates the mechanics:

```python
import torch, torch.nn as nn

class iVAE(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dx, dz, du, k=2):
        super().__init__()
        self.enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(dx + du, 256), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(256, 256), nn.ReLU())
        self.q_mean, self.q_logvar = nn.Linear(256, dz), nn.Linear(256, dz)
        self.dec = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(dz, 256), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(256, 256), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(256, dx))
        # sufficient statistics T(z): polynomial basis of order k
        self.lam = nn.Embedding(du, dz * k)  # lambda(u) for discrete u

    def forward(self, x, u):
        h = self.enc(torch.cat([x, u], dim=-1))
        m, lv = self.q_mean(h), self.q_logvar(h)
        z = m + torch.randn_like(m) * (0.5 * lv).exp()       # reparameterize
        lam = self.lam(u).view(x.size(0), -1, 2)              # (B, dz, k)
        T = torch.stack([z, z ** 2], dim=-1)                 # sufficient stats
        log_prior = (T * lam).sum(-1) - lam.logsumexp(-1)    # up to base measure
        kl = 0.5 * (lv + m**2 + 1).sum(-1) - 0.5 * x.size(-1) * \
             torch.log(torch.tensor(2 * torch.pi)) - log_prior.sum(-1)
        xhat = self.dec(z)
        recon = -0.5 * ((x - xhat) ** 2).sum(-1)
        return -(recon - kl).mean()                          # negative ELBO
```

### 3.3 From independent sources to causal latents

Independent sources are only the first step. Causal representation learning [2] posits that the true latents are not merely independent but *causally related* through a structural causal model (SCM):

$$z_i := g_i(\mathbf{pa}_i, \epsilon_i), \qquad i = 1, \dots, n, \tag{5}$$

where $\mathbf{pa}_i$ are the causal parents of $z_i$ in a directed acyclic graph $\mathcal{G}$ and the noise terms $\epsilon_i$ are mutually independent. Observations remain $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z})$. The task becomes *interventional identifiability*: recover both the mixing $\mathbf{f}$ and the graph $\mathcal{G}$ from observations collected under different *interventions* — precisely the setting where auxiliary variables $\mathbf{u}$ encode intervention targets.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why marginal independence fails: a formal dissection

The unidentifiability of (1) can be stated as a theorem: for every absolutely continuous $p_{\mathbf{x}}$ with full support, there exist infinitely many pairs $(\mathbf{f}, p_{\mathbf{z}})$ with $\mathbf{f}$ a diffeomorphism and $p_{\mathbf{z}}$ factorial whose pushforward equals $p_{\mathbf{x}}$, and these solutions are not related by $\sim_A$ equivalence [6]. The proof constructs the *rotated-Gaussian* automorphism — a normalizing map to an isotropic Gaussian, a rotation, and the inverse map — preserving the observed distribution exactly while entangling the sources beyond any component-wise map.

*Consequence.* Any purely unsupervised objective matching only the marginal $p_{\mathbf{x}}$ — maximum likelihood, adversarial matching, standard VAE ELBO — cannot privilege the true $(\mathbf{f}, \mathbf{z})$. This is the mathematical content of the disentanglement impossibility theorems [3]: without inductive biases on both the model class *and* the data, unsupervised disentanglement is impossible.

### 4.2 Auxiliary-variable identifiability: the core theorem

The iVAE identifiability theorem [1] (generalizing Hyvärinen et al. 2019, who unified TCL and PCL) is the centerpiece. Assume:

1. **Conditional factorization** — (2) holds with each $p(z_i \mid \mathbf{u})$ an exponential family (3) with differentiable sufficient statistics.
2. **Sufficient variability** — there exist $nk + 1$ distinct values $\mathbf{u}^{(0)}, \dots, \mathbf{u}^{(nk)}$ of the auxiliary variable such that the matrix

$$L = \big( \lambda(\mathbf{u}^{(1)}) - \lambda(\mathbf{u}^{(0)}), \dots, \lambda(\mathbf{u}^{(nk)}) - \lambda(\mathbf{u}^{(0)}) \big) \in \mathbb{R}^{nk \times nk} \tag{6}$$

is invertible, where $\lambda$ stacks all sufficient-statistic parameters.
3. **Smooth injective mixing** — $\mathbf{f}$ is a diffeomorphism onto its image, with the observation noise either absent or with known/identifiable distribution.

Then $\mathbf{f}$ and the sources are identified up to $\sim_A$ equivalence: any $\hat{\mathbf{f}}$ fitting the *conditional* distributions $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{u})$ satisfies $\hat{\mathbf{f}}^{-1} \circ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z}) = A \, T(\mathbf{z}) + \mathbf{c}$ with $A$ an invertible matrix that the conditional structure forces to be a *permutation-scaling* — and for $k \geq 2$ (nonlinear sufficient statistics), the linear ambiguity $A$ collapses further.

> **Theorem (Khemakhem et al. 2020, paraphrased).** Under conditions 1–3, the joint distribution $p(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{u})$ is identified: the recovered sources equal the true sources up to permutation and invertible component-wise transformations. The model thus achieves a *principled* disentanglement — the only one whose existence is proven for nonlinear mixing of this generality [1].

Recent extensions sharpen the requirements: sparsity of the mixing Jacobian reduces the number of distinct $\mathbf{u}$ values needed (Yang et al. 2022) [9], hidden Markov structure replaces the *observed* auxiliary variable with a *latent* one for nonstationary time series [7], and structured nonlinear ICA handles noisy, high-dimensional observations [10].

### 4.3 Interventional identifiability and independent mechanisms

Auxiliary variables admit a causal reading: each value of $\mathbf{u}$ is an *environment* or *intervention* perturbing a subset of mechanisms while leaving others invariant. Two ideas dominate interventional identifiability:

- **Independent mechanism analysis (IMA).** Gresele et al. [6] replace statistical source independence by *mechanism independence*: the columns of the mixing Jacobian $D\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z})$ are mutually orthogonal in expectation. Rotation is then excluded because it would mix mechanisms — extending nonlinear ICA from independent *sources* to independent *mechanisms*.
- **Sparse mechanism shifts.** If each intervention changes only a few mechanisms, the causal graph $\mathcal{G}$ in (5) becomes identifiable: *invariant* conditionals identify parent sets, *variant* ones localize interventions [8].

### 4.4 Invariance and invariant risk minimization

A complementary route to causal representation avoids identifying the full generative model and instead learns *predictors* that are invariant across environments $\mathcal{E}$. Invariant risk minimization (IRM) [4] posits a representation $\Phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{H}$ and classifier $w$ such that

$$\min_{\Phi, w} \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{tr}}} R^e(w \circ \Phi) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad w \in \arg\min_{\bar{w}} R^e(\bar{w} \circ \Phi) \;\; \forall e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{tr}}, \tag{7}$$

where $R^e$ is the risk in environment $e$. The constraint forces $w \circ \Phi$ to be *simultaneously optimal* in every training environment; only features whose conditional relationship to the label is stable — the *causal* parents — can satisfy it, while shifting spurious correlates are discarded. The practical IRMv1 replaces the hard constraint with a gradient penalty $\|\nabla_{w=1} R^e(w \cdot \Phi)\|^2$. IRM's causal interpretation (invariant causal prediction) gives it *causal* semantics: the learned $\Phi$ is an identifiable, environment-invariant feature map, albeit weaker than full $\sim_A$ recovery.

### 4.5 Unified comparison

| Method | Auxiliary / signal | Model | Identifiability guarantee | Key reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TCL | Segment index (nonstationarity) | Contrastive classifier | $\sim_A$, exponential-family sources | Hyvärinen & Morioka 2016 [1] |
| PCL | Past observations (temporal dependence) | Binary discrimination | $\sim_A$, temporally dependent sources | Hyvärinen & Morioka 2017 [1] |
| iVAE | General $\mathbf{u}$ (label, domain, index) | VAE, conditional prior (3)–(4) | $\sim_A$, noisy/undercomplete/discrete data | Khemakhem et al. 2020 [1] |

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 The identifiability proof: structure

We sketch the proof of the iVAE theorem [1], since its architecture underlies the whole auxiliary-variable program.

*Step 1 — Matching conditionals.* Suppose two parameter settings $(\mathbf{f}, T, \lambda)$ and $(\hat{\mathbf{f}}, \hat{T}, \hat{\lambda})$ generate identical conditionals $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{u}) = \hat{p}(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{u})$ for all $\mathbf{u}$. Write the change of variables:

$$p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{u}) = p_{T,\lambda}(\mathbf{f}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}) \mid \mathbf{u}) \, |\det D\mathbf{f}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})|. \tag{8}$$

Taking logarithms and differentiating with respect to $\mathbf{u}$ (through the $\lambda$ parameters) yields a linear system in the sufficient statistics $T(\mathbf{z})$ evaluated at $\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{f}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})$.

*Step 2 — Inverting the variability matrix.* The sufficient-variability condition (6) — invertibility of $L$ — lets us solve this system: the vector of true sufficient statistics $T(\mathbf{f}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))$ is an *affine* function of the estimated statistics $\hat{T}(\hat{\mathbf{f}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}))$. Hence

$$T(\mathbf{z}) = A \, \hat{T}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}) + \mathbf{c}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{z}} := \hat{\mathbf{f}}^{-1} \circ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z}), \tag{9}$$

with $A$ invertible.

*Step 3 — Collapsing the linear ambiguity.* Differentiating (9) and using the factorial structure of both the true and estimated conditionals forces $A$ to be a *generalized permutation matrix*. For exponential families of order $k \geq 2$, the nonlinearity of $T$ further implies the estimated sources are component-wise invertible functions of the true sources, up to permutation — exactly $\sim_A$-identifiability. ∎

### 5.2 Empirical evidence

Khemakhem et al. [1] validate the theory on synthetic nonlinear mixtures ($d = 40$, $n = 10$, $M = 40$ segments): iVAE recovers sources with mean absolute correlation $\approx 0.95$ against ground truth, while vanilla VAE, $\beta$-VAE, and $\beta$-TC-VAE achieve only $\approx 0.4$–$0.7$ — near chance rotations, exactly as the impossibility theory predicts.

The disentanglement literature's large-scale study [3] provides the complementary negative evidence: across 12,000+ trained models and six metrics, no unsupervised method reliably identifies disentangled representations without supervision — consistent with the claim that only auxiliary-variable, interventional, or invariant *extra structure* supplies the missing identifiability.

### 5.3 Formal statement: IRM's guarantee and its limits

IRM's identifiability claim is weaker but practically important: under the *invariance assumption* — some $\Phi$ makes $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \Phi(X)]$ identical across environments — and sufficient environmental diversity, the IRM solution recovers the *causal* predictor and discards spurious features [4]. The canonical demonstration is ColoredMNIST: ERM reaches $\sim 10\%$ test accuracy (it learns color, the spurious correlate), while IRMv1 reaches $\sim 70\%$ by learning digit shape. The caveats are sharp: IRMv1's gradient penalty is a loose relaxation of (7), it can fail even on problems satisfying its motivating assumptions, and it identifies features *sufficient for invariant prediction* — not the full latent causal model.

## 6. Limitations

1. **The auxiliary variable must be observed (usually).** The iVAE theory requires $\mathbf{u}$ at training time — segment labels, class labels, or domain indices. Fully unsupervised settings without such structure remain unidentifiable [3][6]. The hidden-Markov extension [7] relaxes observability but adds stronger parametric assumptions.
2. **Sufficient variability is strong.** Condition (6) demands $nk+1$ distinct $\mathbf{u}$ values with an invertible modulation matrix — many environments, diverse interventions. With few environments, identifiability degrades into partial identifiability of a source subspace.
3. **Exponential-family restriction.** The conditionals (3) must be exponential families with *fixed* sufficient statistics; real conditional densities may violate this.
4. **Causal latents need interventions, not just conditioning.** Identifying the *graph* $\mathcal{G}$ in (5) — not merely independent sources — requires interventional data plus faithfulness-like assumptions. Conditioning alone identifies sources; it does not orient causal edges.
5. **Theory vs. practice.** The theorems concern global optima of idealized objectives. Deep networks trained by SGD on finite data with ELBO gaps may or may not attain the identifiable regime — the empirical results are encouraging but not proof.

## 7. Conclusion

Causal representation learning begins where classical statistics ends: the latent variables are not given, and — as the nonlinear ICA impossibility theorems show — they cannot be recovered from observations alone [6][3]. This thesis has argued that three forms of *extra structure* restore the epistemic license: **auxiliary variables**, which make nonlinear ICA identifiable up to permutation and component-wise transforms via the iVAE framework [1]; **interventional and mechanism-level structure**, which extend identifiability from independent sources to causal graphs and independent mechanisms [6][8]; and **environmental invariance**, which identifies causally stable predictors through IRM [4]. Together they form the research program articulated in [2]: collect data under *varied conditions*, condition generative models on those conditions, and demand that learned representations be *invariant* where the world is.

The frontier is clear: identifiability under *latent* interventions, under non-injective observation maps, with *finite-sample* guarantees rather than population theorems, and a unified theory in which auxiliary-variable, interventional, and invariance-based identifiability are special cases of a single principle. Until then, the sober lesson of [3] stands: no representation is identifiable without saying *what varies, and what stays the same*.
