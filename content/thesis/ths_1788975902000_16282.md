# Weak Memory Models and Formal Concurrency Semantics: The C++11/C++20 Memory Model, RC11, herd7 Litmus Testing, and Verified Compilation to Hardware

## Abstract

The memory models of C and C++ constitute one of the most technically demanding interfaces in all of computer science: they must simultaneously legitimize aggressive compiler optimization, expose exploitable hardware parallelism, and preserve the sanity of programmers reasoning about concurrent code. This thesis traces the arc from the axiomatic formalization of C++11 concurrency through its discovered pathologies — broken sequential consistency for atomics, the out-of-thin-air problem, and unsound compilation schemes — to the modern repaired landscape: RC11, the C++20 revisions, promising and event-structure semantics, mechanized litmus testing with herd7, and verified compilation to POWER, ARMv7, and ARMv8. We present the axiomatic machinery in full, prove why key program transformations were invalid under C11 and valid under its repairs, and evaluate the empirical evidence from herd7-based validation campaigns that underpin the current standard.

## 1. Introduction

In 2011, C and C++ acquired their first formally specified memory models. For the first time, a concurrent program's *observable behaviors* were defined not by the whims of a particular compiler or processor but by a mathematical document: a set of axioms over *candidate executions* — graphs whose nodes are memory events and whose edges are the relations `po` (program order), `rf` (reads-from), `mo` (modification order), and derived relations such as `hb` (happens-before) [11]. This was a landmark achievement, and almost immediately it began to crack. The discovery that the published compilation schemes from C++11 atomics to IBM POWER were *unsound* [2], that commonplace compiler optimizations were *invalid* under the model [3], and that the axiomatic definition admitted values appearing "out of thin air" [9], launched a decade-long repair program that remains the most ambitious formal verification effort ever applied to a mainstream programming language standard.

> **Thesis:** The C++11 memory model was simultaneously one of the great formal artifacts of programming-language theory and a specification riddled with subtle soundness holes; its repair — through RC11, the C++20 revisions of P0668R5 [1], promising semantics [4], and mechanized litmus testing with herd7 [6] — demonstrates that only machine-checked, empirically anchored formalization can produce a memory model that is simultaneously optimizable, compilable, and intelligible.

This thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 introduces the axiomatic method and the C11 model of Batty *et al.* Section 3 describes our methodology: axiomatic reconstruction, litmus-test archaeology via herd7, and compilation-scheme proof sketches. Section 4 is the deep dive: the C11 axioms, the RC11 repair, the out-of-thin-air problem and its proposed resolutions, herd7-based empirical validation, and verified compilation mappings to hardware. Section 5 presents theorems and empirical results; Section 6 is candid about limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Why sequential consistency is not enough

Leslie Lamport's *sequential consistency* (SC) — every execution behaves as if the operations of all processors were interleaved in some total order respecting program order — is the gold standard of intelligibility. It is also, for modern systems, unaffordable. Store buffers, out-of-order execution, speculative loads, and non-multi-copy-atomic interconnects all violate SC routinely [10]. Hardware architects therefore expose *weak* (relaxed) memory models: x86-TSO (total store order), IBM POWER, ARMv7, and ARMv8-A, each weaker than the last. A language memory model must then answer the question: *which of the hardware's exotic behaviors may a programmer observe, and which may a compiler introduce?*

### 2.2 The axiomatic style

The modern answer is *axiomatic*. Rather than describing an operational machine (store buffers, queues), one defines a candidate execution `X = ⟨E, po, rf, mo⟩` [7]:

| Relation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `po` (program order) | Sequenced-before order within each thread |
| `rf` (reads-from) | Which write each read observes |
| `mo` (modification order) | Per-location total order over writes |
| `hb` (happens-before) | `(po ∪ sw)⁺`, where `sw` is synchronizes-with |
| `fr` (from-reads) | Read `r` → writes `mo`-after the write `r` read from |
| `eco` (extended coherence) | `(rf ∪ mo ∪ fr)⁺` |
| `sc` | Total order over sequentially-consistent operations |

A model is a set of *axioms* — typically irreflexivity or acyclicity constraints on compositions of these relations. An execution is *consistent* if it satisfies all axioms; the program's allowed behaviors are the consistent executions of its candidate event structures. This style, pioneered for C++11 and systematized by the herd7 tool [6], turns memory-model questions into *graph* questions, amenable to exhaustive enumeration.

### 2.3 The synchronization vocabulary of C++

C++ exposes six *memory orders*: `relaxed`, `consume`, `acquire`, `release`, `acq_rel`, and `seq_cst`. The design intent is a performance portability ladder: `relaxed` compiles to plain loads/stores (free on every architecture), acquire/release to barrier-free mappings on x86-TSO and cheap barriers (`lwsync`) on POWER [2], and `seq_cst` to the expensive full barriers. The central derived relation is *synchronizes-with* (`sw`): a release store `W` synchronizes with an acquire load `R` when `R` reads (possibly through a *release sequence*) from `W`, and *happens-before* (`hb`) is the transitive closure of `po` and `sw`. The *data-race-freedom (DRF) guarantee* — programs free of data races behave sequentially consistently — is the contract that makes the model usable: ordinary code need never think about any of this [11].

## 3. Methodology

Our investigation combines three methods. **First**, axiomatic reconstruction: we re-derive the C11, RC11, and C++20 coherence axioms from primary sources [2][1] and prove, by hand, the inclusion relationships between the models. **Second**, litmus-test archaeology: we analyze the canonical litmus tests (store buffering `SB`, message passing `MP`, load buffering `LB`, independent reads of independent writes `IRIW`) as enumerated by herd7's `.cat` model files [6], and we cross-check published verdict tables (allowed/forbidden) against the axioms. **Third**, compilation-scheme analysis: we examine the standard mappings from C++ atomics to POWER and ARMv8 assembly and the proof techniques used to establish their soundness, drawing on the RC11 and promising-compilation literature [2][4].

A note on evidence: where we claim "hardware exhibits behavior X," the claim rests on published litmus campaigns (notably the Cambridge POWER/ARM studies underlying [6]) and on the herd7 simulation infrastructure that encodes those observations as executable `.cat` specifications. Where we claim "the model permits/forbids behavior X," the claim is checkable by running the corresponding litmus test through herd7 with the appropriate model file.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The C11 axiomatic core and its elegance

The C++11 model, as mathematized by Batty *et al.* [11], defines consistency through a handful of axioms. The *coherence* axioms constrain each location's `mo` against `hb` and `rf` (write-write, read-read, read-write, and write-read coherence); the *SC axioms* constrain the total order `sc` over sequentially consistent operations, requiring roughly that `sc` be consistent with `hb` and `mo`; and the *atomicity axiom* requires that read-modify-write operations read from the immediate `mo`-predecessor of the write they produce. The elegance is real: from these few constraints flow the DRF guarantee, the validity of the message-passing idiom under release/acquire, and a precise characterization of which compiler reorderings are observable.

The model's operational intuition is the *reads-from* relation: a read may read from any write not forbidden by coherence, and the axioms carve the forbidden set. Consider message passing:

```cpp
// initially x = y = 0
// Thread 1:            // Thread 2:
x.store(1, relaxed);    while (y.load(acquire) == 0) {}
y.store(1, release);    r = x.load(relaxed); // must be 1
```

The release store to `y` synchronizes with the acquire load; `hb` then spans the threads, and read-write coherence forces `r == 1` [11]. This is the model working as intended — and it is genuinely beautiful.

### 4.2 The cracks: SC too strong, SC too weak, compilation unsound

Lahav, Vafeiadis, Kang, Hur, and Dreyer demonstrated that the C++11 treatment of `seq_cst` was broken in *both directions* [2]:

1. **SC semantics too strong.** C11 forbade certain reorderings of `seq_cst` accesses that real compilers perform and that hardware permits, making the standard compilation schemes to POWER *unsound* — they admitted executions the model forbade. Concretely, both published mappings of C++11 `seq_cst` to POWER were shown incorrect, a result that invalidated years of assumed-correct compilation [2].
2. **SC semantics too weak.** The C11 SC axioms failed to forbid the `IRIW` (independent reads of independent writes) shape for `seq_cst` accesses in the way programmers expected: two threads could observe two `seq_cst` writes in opposite orders, contradicting the informal promise that `seq_cst` operations appear in a single global order.

The proposed repair, **RC11** (for *Repaired C11*), restructured the SC axioms around a new derived order, the *SC-before* relation, and a strengthened coherence condition. The key technical moves [2]:

> **Theorem (RC11 SC repair, informal):** By replacing C11's SC axioms with (i) a coherence axiom `irreflexive(hb; eco?)` for the release/acquire fragment and (ii) SC axioms formulated over a partial *psc* (partial SC) order rather than demanding consistency with a total `sc` order on all executions, RC11 admits exactly the POWER-compilable behaviors while restoring the expected SC guarantees for `seq_cst` programs.

Crucially, RC11 was proved to *validate the standard compilation schemes* to POWER and x86-TSO — the first model in the C11 lineage for which this held [2]. The RC11 `.cat` model, maintained in the herd7 ecosystem [6], remains the reference executable specification of repaired C++ concurrency. The release/acquire fragment itself was characterized separately by Lahav, Giannarakis, and Vafeiadis, whose taming of release-acquire consistency supplied the per-location simulation and coherence-decomposition proof techniques on which RC11's coherence axiom builds [5].

A parallel thread of repair concerned *release sequences*. P0982R1 (adopted into C++20) weakened the definition so that `relaxed` stores by the releasing thread no longer extend the release sequence [1] — closing a hole through which irrelevant relaxed operations could interfere with synchronizes-with. And P0668R5 [1] — the paper that drove the C++20 memory model revisions — additionally strengthened `seq_cst` fences, whose C11 definition was "too weak… caused by historical assumptions that have since been disproved."

### 4.3 The out-of-thin-air problem and the quest for a constructive semantics

The deepest pathology of C11 was *out-of-thin-air* (OOTA) reads. Consider load buffering with a data dependency:

```cpp
// initially x = y = 0, all relaxed
// Thread 1:        // Thread 2:
r1 = x.load();     r2 = y.load();
y.store(r1);       x.store(r2);
```

Can `r1 == r2 == 42`? There is no write of 42 anywhere in the program; the value appears from nowhere. C11's axioms *permit* this execution: nothing forbids the cycle, because the axiomatic style constrains *which* values reads may observe but provides no account of *how values come into existence* [9][3]. Yet forbidding it naively — by requiring `hb ∪ rf` acyclicity, as some tools do [8] — also forbids *load buffering*, the `r1 == r2 == 1` shape that POWER and ARM hardware genuinely exhibit:

```cpp
// Thread 1:        // Thread 2:
r1 = x.load();     r2 = y.load();
y.store(1);        x.store(1);   // r1 == r2 == 1 allowed on HW
```

Distinguishing the speculatively-justified `1` from the conjured `42` is the OOTA problem, and it resisted solution for years. The C++ committee's approach, from C++14 onward, was an *informal note* prohibiting out-of-thin-air results without defining them — formally useless for verification [1][9].

The breakthrough came from *operational, promise-based* semantics. Kang, Hur, Lahav, Vafeiadis, and Dreyer proposed the **promising semantics** [4]: threads may *promise* future writes, but every promise must be *certifiable* — the thread must be able to execute, from the current state, a sequence of steps fulfilling the promise without relying on other threads' unfulfilled promises. Values can be speculated (legitimizing load buffering and compiler reorderings) but never conjured (the certification requirement blocks OOTA). The promising semantics was proved to validate essentially all desired compiler optimizations — a property the axiomatic C11 model famously lacked [3] — while admitting a program logic [4]. Subsequent work ("grounding thin-air reads with event structures") refined the construction further [4].

### 4.4 herd7: mechanizing the model

Theory without tooling is speculation. **herd7**, part of the herdtools7 suite by Alglave and Maranget [6], is the instrument that turned weak-memory axiomatics into an empirical science. Its architecture:

1. A *litmus test* (a small concurrent program, in C or assembly) is parsed.
2. herd7 enumerates *all candidate executions* — all choices of `rf` and `mo` consistent with `po`.
3. A `.cat` file — an executable relational specification of a memory model (C++11, RC11, POWER, ARMv8, x86-TSO, RISC-V, and dozens more) — filters candidates by checking the model's axioms, typically *acyclicity* or *irreflexivity* constraints over relation compositions.
4. Each execution is labeled **Allowed** or **Forbidden**; the set of allowed outcomes is the model's prediction for the test.

The `.cat` language is the great unifying artifact: the *same* litmus test can be run against the C++ model, the POWER model, and the ARMv8 model, and the verdict tables compared. This is how the community discovered, for instance, that C11's `seq_cst` axioms disagreed with POWER compilation [2], and how RC11's repairs were validated against thousands of litmus tests [6]. The herd7 repository further ships the RC11 `.cat` model and regression suites derived from the PLDI'17 and POPL'15 papers [6]. Modern consistency-checking research — e.g., optimal reads-from consistency checking for C11-style models [7] — builds directly on this axiomatic, herd7-compatible formulation, where an execution is the tuple `⟨E, po, rf, mo⟩` and consistency is a property of derived relations.

### 4.5 Verified compilation: from language model to silicon

A language memory model is only as good as its compilation schemes. The gold standard is a *verified compilation theorem*:

> **Theorem (Compilation correctness, schema):** For compilation scheme `C` from language model `L` to hardware model `H`, every behavior of `C(P)` observable under `H` is a behavior of `P` allowed by `L`.

Proving this requires *both* models to be formal — which is why the axiomatization of POWER (Sarkar *et al.*), ARMv8, and x86-TSO in herd7's `.cat` corpus [6] was a prerequisite. The results form a clear narrative [2][4][10]:

| Source model | Target | Key mapping insight | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| C11 release/acquire | POWER | `lwsync` before release stores; `ctrl+isync` after acquire loads | Proved (Batty *et al.*) |
| C11 `seq_cst` | POWER | `sync` barriers; leading/trailing variants | **Unsound under C11**; sound under RC11 [2] |
| RC11 | x86-TSO | Mostly direct; `MFENCE`/`XCHG` for `seq_cst` | Proved [2] |
| Promising semantics | ARMv8 | Promise certification matches ARMv8 operational model | Proved (Podkopaev *et al.*) [4] |
| C++20 (P0668R5) | POWER/ARMv8 | Strengthened SC fences; weakened release sequences | Schemes preserved [1] |

The LLVM perspective adds the compiler middle-end: the 2017 LLVM Developers' Meeting analysis showed how the OOTA problem infects *optimization validity* — transformations like load-store forwarding and dead-store elimination are unsound under a naive reading of C11, which is why LLVM's atomics support tracked the promising-semantics literature closely [10].

A cautionary sub-result deserves emphasis: Vafeiadis *et al.* proved that *common, previously-assumed-valid compiler optimizations* — expression linearization, "roach motel" reorderings moving operations into critical sections — were **invalid** in C11, with machine-checked Coq proofs of both the invalidity and the validity under proposed fixes [3]. The memory model is not merely descriptive; it is *prescriptive* of the optimizer's legal moves, and getting it wrong silently miscompiles programs.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

We now state the chapter's central technical claims precisely.

> **Theorem 1 (RC11 repairs SC, Lahav *et al.* [2]):** RC11's SC axioms forbid precisely the executions that violate the programmer's SC expectation for `seq_cst` atomics (including IRIW-shaped anomalies), while admitting all executions producible by the standard compilation schemes to POWER and x86-TSO. Under C11, the corresponding schemes were unsound.

> **Theorem 2 (RC11 validates compilation [2]):** The standard mappings — C++ `seq_cst` to POWER `sync`-based sequences, release/acquire to `lwsync`/`ctrl+isync`, and the x86-TSO mappings — are sound with respect to RC11. This was proved by simulation between the axiomatic source executions and the operational hardware models.

> **Theorem 3 (Promising semantics admits optimizations, Kang *et al.* [4]):** The promising semantics validates the standard suite of sequential compiler optimizations (including reorderings forbidden by axiomatic C11 [3]) while provably excluding out-of-thin-air executions via the promise-certification requirement.

> **Theorem 4 (Coherence characterization [7]):** For the RA (release/acquire) fragment of RC11, consistency reduces to `irreflexive(hb; eco?)` — a forbidden cycle must consist of a happens-before path followed by at most one extended-coherence step. This yields optimal reads-from consistency checking algorithms and is the basis of modern model checkers for C11-style models [7][8].

**Empirical anchor.** The herd7 litmus corpus provides the experimental counterpart: running the SB, MP, LB, IRIW, and WRC families against the `rc11.cat`, `power.cat`, and `aarch64.cat` models reproduces exactly the allowed/forbidden tables predicted by Theorems 1–2, and the diy7 generator [6] produces these tests systematically from concise specifications. The C11Tester project [8] complements herd7's static enumeration with *dynamic* race detection over the C/C++20 model fragment, enforcing `hb ∪ sc ∪ rf` acyclicity to rule out OOTA in practice — a pragmatic echo of the theoretical certification requirement [4].

## 6. Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating what the repaired edifice still lacks.

1. **No sanctioned OOTA solution in the standard.** C++20 adopted the RC11-inspired SC repairs, the weakened release sequences, and strengthened SC fences [1] — but the out-of-thin-air prohibition remains an informal note [9]. Formal verification of C++ programs using relaxed atomics remains, strictly speaking, without a standard-blessed semantic foundation; the promising semantics [4] is the community's best answer but is not the standard.
2. **`consume` is vestigial.** No production compiler implements `memory_order_consume` as specified; all strengthen it to `acquire` [8]. The dependency-ordering machinery (`dependency-ordered-before`) survives in the standard as dead specification weight.
3. **Axiomatic vs. operational tension persists.** The axiomatic style excels at *forbidding* (cycle detection over `⟨E, po, rf, mo⟩` [7]) but cannot naturally express *how* values arise — the root of the OOTA problem. Operational models (promising semantics, hardware operational models) excel at construction but complicate compositional reasoning. No single formalism yet dominates both.
4. **Compilation proofs cover fragments.** Verified compilation results [2][4] target carefully delimited language fragments; full C++ — with its object model, mixed-size accesses, and `volatile` — remains beyond any proved scheme.
5. **Empirical coverage is litmus-bound.** herd7's strength is exhaustive enumeration of *small* tests [6]; whether litmus-test agreement implies agreement on large programs is an extrapolation, mitigated but not eliminated by tools like C11Tester [8].

## 7. Conclusion

The decade from C++11 to C++20 transformed the memory model from a flawed axiomatic sketch into the most rigorously validated informal specification in programming-language history — and simultaneously revealed how much work remains. RC11 repaired sequential consistency and restored sound compilation [2]; P0668R5 carried the repairs into the standard [1]; promising semantics gave the first convincing constructive account of speculation without conjuration [4]; and herd7 turned the entire subject into a checkable, empirical discipline [6]. The through-line is methodological: *a memory model is a scientific theory*, and like any theory it must survive confrontation with both proof (compilation correctness, optimization validity) and experiment (litmus campaigns, model checking). The C++ memory model saga is the clearest demonstration our field has that formal methods, mechanized and empirically grounded, can discipline even the most performance-critical interface in computing. The out-of-thin-air problem's final resolution — a standard-blessed, constructive, compilable semantics — remains the open problem that will define the next decade.
