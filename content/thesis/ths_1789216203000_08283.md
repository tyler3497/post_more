# Region-Based Memory Management and Ownership Disciplines: From Tofte–Talpin Inference to Rust's Borrow Checker

## Abstract

Memory safety without garbage collection has been pursued along two great traditions: **region-based memory management**, in which inference statically delimits the lifetimes of stack-disciplined memory areas, and **ownership disciplines**, in which a type system gives every value a unique owner and restricts aliasing through affine typing and borrow checking. This thesis reconstructs the lineage from Mads Tofte and Jean-Pierre Talpin's 1994 region calculus — its `letregion` construct, region polymorphism, and effect inference — to Rust's borrow checker, arguing the two are duals of one principle: *a static proof that no pointer outlives the storage it points into*. We formalize the Tofte–Talpin translation and its consistency-based soundness argument, compare it against affine ownership and the loan-exclusivity discipline of Rust's non-lexical lifetimes, and survey evidence from the MLKit compiler, region–GC hybrids, and the machine-checked RustBelt proof. Regions trade precision for automation and suffer space retention; ownership trades automation for precision and suffers incompleteness. We close with a convergence sketch — regions are lifetimes with bulk deallocation; lifetimes are regions with per-object deallocation — and open problems for a unified effect-and-capability calculus.

## 1 Intro

The central tension of systems programming is the *allocation–deallocation gap*: reclaiming storage a program no longer needs, where "no longer needed" is undecidable in general. Garbage collection resolves it dynamically, at the cost of pauses and a runtime tax; manual management resolves it by fiat, at the cost of dangling pointers, double frees, and use-after-free vulnerabilities.

A third path has always existed: **prove, statically, when memory becomes unreachable**, and reclaim it deterministically. Two lineages carried this idea furthest. The *region* tradition of Tofte and Talpin [1][2], grounded in Talpin and Jouvelot's effect inference [3], asks the compiler to **infer** where memory lives and when whole *regions* can be discarded at once. The *ownership* tradition, culminating in Rust's borrow checker, asks the programmer to **declare** ownership through types and the compiler to **check** every borrow [4][6][7].

These traditions are not opposites but *adjoints*: region inference and borrow checking are two proof strategies for the same safety theorem. A region is a coarse-grained lifetime; a lifetime is a fine-grained region. The `letregion ρ in e` of Tofte–Talpin and Rust's `&'a mut T` borrow both answer one question — *when may this pointer be used?* — one with an effect set, the other with a loan. The correspondence explains their characteristic failure modes and points toward a unified discipline neither has achieved alone.

The contributions of this thesis are: (1) a self-contained reconstruction of the Tofte–Talpin calculus, its effect inference, and its consistency-based soundness proof; (2) a three-axis comparative framework (granularity, annotation burden, soundness-argument shape) aligning regions, uniqueness, and loans; (3) a survey of the empirical record — MLKit, region–GC hybrids [5], and RustBelt [4]; and (4) a convergence sketch with a characterization of where the region/lifetime encodings break down.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The region idea

A **region** is a dynamically growing, bulk-deallocated area of memory: values allocate *into* regions, and a region's end reclaims everything at once — typically by popping a stack. The discipline is old (arenas, Ada storage pools); Tofte and Talpin's contribution was making region-boundary placement a **static inference problem** [1].

Their source language is a polymorphically typed call-by-value λ-calculus. Their target annotates every value constructor with a *region variable* and adds `letregion ρ in e`: allocate a fresh region, evaluate `e`, deallocate on exit. Translation is a *nondeterministic relation* from source to region-annotated programs; implementations choose via inference. The canonical inference uses **effect inference** [3]: each expression gets a type plus an *effect* (regions read/written), and `letregion` blocks are inserted wherever effects delimit a region's live range.

### 2.2 Effects, and why they matter

Effect systems, originating with Gifford and Lucassen, annotate function arrows with *observable effects*. In the region setting the effect is a set of region variables, and function types take the form `τ₁ —{φ}→ τ₂` — "a function whose evaluation may touch exactly the regions in φ". Two derived concepts do the heavy lifting:

- **Region polymorphism.** A function may be parameterized over region variables, `∀ρ. τ₁ —{φ(ρ)}→ τ₂`, so callers instantiate ρ with distinct regions and obtain *separation of lifetimes*: data with different live ranges need not share a region [1].
- **Effect masking.** A region variable in a function's latent effect but bound *inside* its body (by `letregion`) can be *masked* — hidden from callers. Masking lets local, short-lived allocation stay invisible in a function's interface, and is the precise ancestor of Rust's hiding temporary borrows behind a safe API [4][7].

### 2.3 The ownership lineage

Parallel to regions, a second tradition attacked the same problem through *aliasing control*:

- **Linear and affine types** (Girard; Wadler): values usable exactly once (linear) or at most once (affine). Affine typing gives *move semantics*: assignment transfers the single use-permission.
- **Uniqueness types** (Barendsen, Smetsers, Clean): a unique reference is the sole path to a value, permitting safe in-place update in a pure language.
- **Ownership types** (Clarke, Potter, Noble): every object has an *owner*, and the ownership hierarchy constrains aliasing — used, e.g., for safe region-based management in real-time Java.
- **Cyclone** (Grossman et al.): a type-safe C dialect with *growable regions* and static checking that no pointer escapes its region's lifetime — the most direct industrial ancestor of Rust's lifetimes.

Rust synthesizes these threads. Every value has one *owner*; ownership can be *moved* or *borrowed* immutably (shared) or mutably (exclusive). The borrow checker enforces **loan exclusivity**: at any program point, a value admits either one active mutable borrow or any number of active immutable borrows, never both [6][7]. Lifetimes delimit each borrow's range, and since Rust 1.31 the analysis uses **non-lexical lifetimes (NLL)**: a borrow ends at its *last use*, not at the end of its lexical scope [6].

> **Theorem (Loan exclusivity):** In safe Rust, if a mutable borrow of a place is live at point *p*, no other borrow of that place is live at *p*. Data-race freedom follows as a corollary of the type system, not a runtime property.

## 3 Methodology

This thesis is a *comparative reconstruction* with three components. **(A) Formal reconstruction:** the syntax and semantics of a core region calculus — the `letregion` translation relation and the effect-indexed *consistency* relation behind its soundness proof — alongside the borrow discipline's places, loans, and lifetime inclusion, in uniform notation. **(B) Three-axis comparison:** each discipline scored on *allocation granularity* (bulk region vs. per-object drop), *annotation burden* (inferred effects vs. declared lifetimes), and *soundness-argument shape* (consistency under an effect vs. loan exclusivity). **(C) Evidence triangulation:** MLKit's region-only execution of Standard ML [1][5], hybrid region/generational-GC runtimes [5], and RustBelt — the first machine-checked safety proof for a realistic Rust subset (λRust) in Coq/Iris, which turns the *unsafe* escape hatch into discharged proof obligations [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Tofte–Talpin region calculus and `letregion`

The target calculus extends the typed λ-calculus with region-annotated types. A function type carries its latent effect:

```haskell
data Ty = TInt Place
        | TPair Ty Ty Place
        | TFun Ty Effect Ty Place
type Place  = RegionVar
type Effect = Set RegionVar
```

The expression language adds one construct:

```haskell
data Exp = Var Name
         | Lam Name Ty Exp Place
         | App Exp Exp
         | Pair Exp Exp Place
         | LetRegion RegionVar Exp
```

The operational semantics maintains a *stack of regions*: `LetRegion ρ e` pushes a fresh region, evaluates `e`, then pops and discards it — an *O(1)* bulk deallocation regardless of how many values were allocated inside.

> **Theorem (Region soundness, Tofte–Talpin):** If source `e` translates to target `e′` with effect φ, and `e′` evaluates without getting stuck, then no step of `e′` reads a region after deallocation — source and target evaluations are *consistent* with respect to φ.

The proof's subtlety is **consistency**: the target need not mirror the source step-for-step — parts of the source evaluation *need not be reflected* at all, provided the effect analysis shows them unobservable [1]. Hence the notorious **"harmless" dangling pointers**: the translation may leave pointers into deallocated regions, and soundness shows they can never be *dereferenced*. "Harmless" does real work here; §6 examines the price.

Inference runs in two phases: *region and effect inference* annotates the program via unification à la Hindley–Milner over an effect lattice [3]; *letregion placement* inserts binders at the narrowest justified points. Region polymorphism — quantifying over region variables at let-bindings — prevents collapse into one global region, the degenerate translation the nondeterministic relation technically permits [1].

### 4.2 Effect inference, region polymorphism, and effect masking

Effect inference is the *engine* of automation in the region tradition — the thing Rust chose not to have. In Talpin and Jouvelot's formulation [3], judgments take the form `Γ ⊢ e : τ & φ`: expression `e` has type `τ` and effect `φ`. Application *unions* effects; abstraction *binds* them into the arrow; `letregion` *masks* them:

```
Γ ⊢ e₁ : τ₁ —{φ₁}→ τ₂ & φ₂      Γ ⊢ e₂ : τ₁ & φ₃
────────────────────────────────────────────────── (App)
Γ ⊢ e₁ e₂ : τ₂ & (φ₁ ∪ φ₂ ∪ φ₃)

Γ, ρ ⊢ e : τ & φ        ρ ∉ ftv(Γ, τ)
────────────────────────────────────── (LetRegion/Mask)
Γ ⊢ letregion ρ in e : τ & (φ − {ρ})
```

The masking rule is the quiet hero: a function may allocate furiously in a private region as long as nothing escapes, and callers never know. Compare Rust's *encapsulation*: a safe API mentioning no lifetimes may internally juggle borrows while clients see only the clean signature [4][7]. Same information-hiding goal — one by *computing* the hidden structure, the other by *checking* a declared boundary.

Region polymorphism interacts with masking delicately. In `map : ∀ρ₁ρ₂. (α —{ρ₁}→ β) → list(α,ρ₁) —{ρ₁,ρ₂}→ list(β,ρ₂)`, input and output lists live in *different* regions, so either can be freed while the other survives. Without it, every list would share one region and nothing could be freed until program end — at the cost of a whole-program, higher-order analysis [1].

### 4.3 From uniqueness to ownership: the affine turn

The affine core is tiny:

- **Move:** `let y = x;` transfers ownership; `x` becomes unusable. (Affine: used *at most* once.)
- **Copy:** types implementing `Copy` (machine words, shared references) are exempt — they are freely duplicable because duplicating them cannot create aliased mutation.
- **Borrow:** `&x` (shared) and `&mut x` (exclusive) create temporary loans without transferring ownership.

The borrow checker verifies that the *loan set* at every program point satisfies exclusivity and that each loan's lifetime is contained in its referent's. Pre-NLL, a borrow lived until its enclosing block's end; NLL refined this to liveness — a borrow ends at its last use — accepting strictly more programs, e.g. the conditional-borrow pattern lexical lifetimes rejected [6]:

```rust
fn get_or_insert(v: &mut Vec<String>, i: usize) -> &mut String {
    if i < v.len() {
        &mut v[i]
    } else {
        v.push(String::new());
        v.last_mut().unwrap()
    }
}
```

Under lexical lifetimes this fails (borrow 1's scope extends past the `else`); under NLL it is accepted because borrow 1 is dead before borrow 2 begins [6]. The general point: NLL is the shift from *scope-based* to *liveness-based* delimitation — and liveness-based delimitation is exactly what effect inference computes [6][1].

This is the first deep correspondence: **a non-lexical lifetime is an inferred region**. Both answer "over what program range is this storage valid?" by dataflow analysis. The difference is granularity: NLL computes *validity* ranges, but Rust reclaims each value individually via deterministic `Drop`, whereas regions reclaim *bulk* storage at `letregion` exit.

### 4.4 Polonius and the Datalog formulation

The current frontier, *Polonius*, reformulates borrow checking as **Datalog queries over loan facts** (`loan_issued_at`, `loan_killed_at`, `loan_invalidated_at`, `loan_live_at`) — the borrow checker as a *fixed-point analysis over the CFG*, the same algorithmic species as effect inference, now declarative. Tofte–Talpin compute per-expression region sets; Polonius computes per-point live loans [4][7]. The incompleteness is the *price of decidability* — and the mirror image of region inference's cost: region inference is *complete* but *imprecise* (dead data pinned by coarse regions), while borrow checking is *precise* but *incomplete*. Each tradition's weakness is the other's strength.

### 4.5 Convergence: regions are lifetimes, lifetimes are regions

Define a **region** as `(S, δ)` — a set of objects plus one bulk deallocation action — and a **lifetime** as `(P, {δₒ})` — a program range plus per-object drops. Then:

1. **Regions encode lifetimes:** group each owner's storage with everything it owns into a region whose live range is the owner's scope; `letregion` exit performs all drops at once. Always *sound* but *imprecise*: it extends live ranges NLL would have ended early, and cannot express partial moves.
2. **Lifetimes encode regions:** assign each region variable ρ a lifetime parameter `'ρ` and each `letregion ρ in e` a scope. Sound only under the consistency condition of [1] — and it trades the *O(1)* bulk free for *O(n)* individual drops.

The encodings compose to an isomorphism on *safety* but not on *cost* or *automation* — this thesis's central claim: **region-based and ownership-based memory management are the same safety theorem proved by dual proof strategies, and the choice between them is about who does the work (compiler vs. programmer) and at what granularity (bulk vs. per-object), not about what is provable.**

A TLA+ sketch of the region-stack discipline makes the duality concrete — a LIFO discipline on *sets*, where the borrow checker is a LIFO discipline on *permissions*:

```tla
---- MODULE RegionStack ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES stack          \* sequence of regions; each region is a set of addresses
Push(R)  == stack' = Append(stack, R)   \* letregion entry: O(1)
Pop      == stack' = Head(stack)        \* letregion exit: bulk free, O(1)
Alloc(a) == LET top == Len(stack) IN
            stack' = [stack EXCEPT ![top] = stack[top] \cup {a}]
Safety == \* no address is referenced after its region is popped
  \A a : allocated(a) => \E i \in 1..Len(stack) : a \in stack[i]
====
```

The borrow checker's analogue replaces "address ∈ region" with "loan ∈ live-set" and "pop" with "kill loan" — the same shape, at finer grain.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 The MLKit experiment: regions without a collector

The strongest evidence for regions is the **MLKit**, a Standard ML compiler built on Tofte–Talpin inference that runs substantial programs — compilers, theorem provers — *with no garbage collector*: every allocation into an inferred region, reclaimed by `letregion` exit. Region execution proved time-competitive with GC, with pause-free behavior for real-time settings [1]; follow-up work compiled the calculus to unboxed stack regions on von Neumann machines [1].

The measured cost is **space retention**: a region is freed only when its *last* live value dies, so dead values pin region-mates. Hybrids integrate regions with a generational collector — regions for bulk short-lived data, the collector for what regions strand. The Springer study quantifies the trade: regions plus GC dominate either alone on pause times, at the cost of a more complex runtime [5] — the empirical face of the §4.4 duality.

### 5.2 RustBelt: the machine-checked borrow checker

On the ownership side, the landmark is **RustBelt** [4]: the first formal, machine-checked safety proof for a realistic Rust subset (λRust), in Coq on Iris. RustBelt models types *semantically* — as separation-logic predicates over machine states — proves the checker's rules sound, and is *extensible*: each `unsafe`-using library abstraction (`Cell`, `Mutex`, `Vec`) gets a verification condition for being a *safe* extension, discharged in Coq. Unsafe code becomes a *discharged proof obligation*, not a hole [4].

A compact Python model illustrates the analysis RustBelt formalizes — live-loan computation over a CFG is the borrow checker's fixed point:

```python
def check_loans(cfg, loans):
    live = {node: set() for node in cfg.nodes}
    changed = True
    while changed:                       # fixed point, like effect inference
        changed = False
        for node in reversed(cfg.order):
            out = set().union(*(live[s] for s in cfg.succ(node)))
            inn = (out - kills(node, loans)) | gens(node, loans)
            if inn != live[node]:
                live[node] = inn; changed = True
    return [(n, live[n]) for n in cfg.nodes
            if sum(l.is_mut for l in live[n]) > 1
            or (any(l.is_mut for l in live[n]) and len(live[n]) > 1)]
```

The mixed-methods study of unsafe Rust [7] supplies the sociological complement: it documents *why* programmers reach for `unsafe` — FFI, eliding checks in hot paths, patterns the checker cannot see — mapping the *incompleteness frontier* empirically. Every `unsafe` block in the wild marks where the checking strategy hit its limit and the programmer took over the proof burden manually [7].

### 5.3 What the evidence jointly shows

| Axis | Region inference (Tofte–Talpin) | Ownership checking (Rust) |
|---|---|---|
| Granularity | Bulk: whole regions freed at once | Per-object: each owner dropped individually |
| Automation | High: effects and `letregion` placement inferred [1][3] | Low: lifetimes declared or inferred locally; borrows restructured by hand [6][7] |
| Precision | Coarse: dead values pin regions | Fine: NLL reclaims at last use |
| Soundness argument | Consistency of target w.r.t. source, indexed by effect [1][2] | Loan exclusivity + lifetime inclusion, machine-checked [4] |
| Characteristic failure | Space leaks ("harmless" dangling pointers) | Incompleteness: safe programs rejected; `unsafe` hatch [7] |
| Empirical footing | MLKit: GC-free SML [1]; region+GC hybrids [5] | RustBelt proof [4]; industrial adoption |

> **Theorem (Convergence, sketch):** For first-order programs without interior mutability, both disciplines prove the same safety property — *no reference is dereferenced after its referent's storage is reclaimed* — and each simulates the other's placement decisions with at most constant-factor automation loss (regions→lifetimes) or linear-factor deallocation cost (lifetimes→regions). The simulation breaks at higher-order state and shared mutability.

## 6 Limitations

**Region inference does not scale down gracefully.** The analysis is whole-program and higher-order: region polymorphism must thread region variables through every boundary, and separate compilation stays awkward. Mutation, exceptions, and concurrency bloat effect sets; placement degrades toward the degenerate single-region translation; the "harmless" dangling pointers, never *dereferenced*, still *occupy memory*. A region system never reclaims a dead object early — only a region when its last live object dies — a space leak by construction for heterogeneous lifetimes, remediable only by the hybrid GC the purists hoped to avoid [5].

**Borrow checking does not scale up gracefully.** Incompleteness is fundamental: any decidable, modular checker for an undecidable property rejects safe programs. The coping mechanisms — restructuring code, `unsafe` blocks, the verified `unsafe`-encapsulating standard library [4] — shift the proof burden to humans, who must distinguish "my program is unsound" from "the checker is incomplete" without tooling help [7]. And RustBelt's subject is λRust — a *subset*; the full language is verified only at the frontier, never in totality.

**The convergence theorem has sharp edges.** The §4.5 encodings assume first-order data and no interior mutability. Region variables range over bulk-deallocated storage areas; lifetime parameters over program points with per-object drops, and higher-ranked lifetimes (`for<'a>`) have no region analogue. Shared mutable state (`Rc<RefCell<T>>`) is, in region land, a region never deallocated — a leak both disciplines call a feature.

Finally, this thesis *reconstructs* rather than *mechanizes*: sketches at survey level, with the real proofs in [1][2] (consistency) and [4] (Iris/Coq). A unified calculus with inferred regions *and* checked loans, proved sound end-to-end, remains unwritten.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Thirty years separate Tofte and Talpin's stack of regions from Rust's borrow checker, but they are chapters of one story: the search for a *static proof of timely deallocation*. Regions proved it by **inference** — compute where storage lives, reclaim in bulk, accept imprecision. Ownership proved it by **checking** — declare who owns what, verify every loan, accept incompleteness. Effect masking and safe encapsulation are the same information-hiding idea; non-lexical lifetimes are inferred regions at fine grain; Polonius's Datalog loans are effect inference turned inside out.

The *duality is the point*: automation and precision trade along a frontier, and every real system — MLKit's regions, Rust's borrows, region/GC hybrids [5], RustBelt's verified libraries [4] — picks a point on it. The open problem is one calculus letting the programmer *slide along the frontier within a single program*: inferred bulk regions where automation suffices, checked fine-grained loans where precision matters, under one soundness theorem. Tofte and Talpin gave us the inference half; Rust gave us the checking half. The synthesis is still waiting for its POPL paper.
