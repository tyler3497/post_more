# Causal Inference from Observational Data with Double Machine Learning: Neyman Orthogonality, R-Learner, EconML, and Sensitivity Analysis under Unmeasured Confounding

## Abstract
This thesis develops a unified treatment of causal inference from observational data when nuisance functions are high-dimensional and must be estimated with machine learning. We study the partially linear model and interactive regression model under unconfoundedness, focusing on double/debiased machine learning (DML) with Neyman orthogonal scores, cross-fitting, and quasi-oracle R-Learner for heterogeneous treatment effects. We connect Robinson's residualization, Chernozhukov et al.'s DML framework, and Nie & Wager's R-Learner, and show how the EconML library operationalizes orthogonal learners with flexible nuisance estimation and valid inference. We prove √n-consistency and asymptotic normality under n^{1/4}-rate nuisance estimation, derive orthogonality conditions via Gateaux derivatives, and discuss honest forests, lasso, and deep nets as nuisance learners. Finally, we address violations of unconfoundedness through modern sensitivity analysis — Rosenbaum Γ-bounds, marginal sensitivity models, and Oster/Cinelli-Hazlett benchmarking — and provide sharp partial identification bounds via the B-Learner. Empirical illustrations on synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks demonstrate double robustness, variance reduction, and robustness to hidden confounding.

## 1 Introduction

Estimating causal effects from *observational* data is central to economics, medicine, and policy evaluation [1][2]. Unlike randomized controlled trials, observational studies suffer from **confounding**: treatment assignment $T$ correlates with covariates $X$ that also affect outcome $Y$. Under the *unconfoundedness* or *ignorability* assumption $Y(0),Y(1) \perp T \mid X$, causal parameters are identified, yet classical parametric adjustments fail when $X$ is high-dimensional and nuisance functions $m(X)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]$ and $e(X)=\mathbb{P}(T=1\mid X)$ are complex.

This thesis synthesizes three breakthroughs:

- **Double Machine Learning (DML)** [1][2]: Neyman orthogonal scores + cross-fitting to obtain $\sqrt{n}$-consistent, asymptotically normal estimates even when nuisances are estimated at $n^{-1/4}$ rates via arbitrary ML.
- **R-Learner** [3]: a *quasi-oracle* meta-learner for conditional average treatment effects (CATE) $\tau(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$ based on Robinson's transformation.
- **EconML** [4]: a unified Python toolkit implementing DML, DR-Learner, R-Learner, and orthogonal forests with valid confidence intervals.

We also confront the fragility of unconfoundedness. *No test* can verify $Y(t) \perp T \mid X$. We therefore develop sensitivity analysis [5][6][7] that quantifies how strong unmeasured confounding $U$ must be to overturn conclusions.

> Theorem: If $\psi$ is Neyman orthogonal, first-order errors in $\hat{\eta}$ do not affect first-order asymptotic distribution of $\hat{\theta}$. Formally, $\partial_{\eta}\mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)][\eta-\eta_0]=0$.

Contributions:

- Rigorous derivation of Neyman orthogonality and its model-free construction via influence functions
- Connection between partially linear, interactive, and partially linear IV models
- R-Learner excess risk bounds and equivalence to DML for CATE
- Practical guide to EconML's `DML`, `DRLearner`, `RLearner`, `CausalForestDML`
- Modern sensitivity frameworks: Rosenbaum $\Gamma$, Tan's marginal sensitivity model, Dorn et al. sharp bounds, Cinelli-Hazlett benchmarking

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Potential Outcomes and Identification

Let $Y(t)$ denote potential outcome under $t\in\{0,1\}$, $T$ observed treatment, $X\in\mathbb{R}^p$ covariates. Observed outcome $Y = T Y(1)+(1-T)Y(0)$ under SUTVA. Target parameters:

- **ATE**: $\theta_0 = \mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)]$
- **CATE**: $\tau_0(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]$
- **ATET**: $\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid T=1]$

*Assumptions* [1][5]:

1. Unconfoundedness: $\{Y(0),Y(1)\} \perp T \mid X$
2. Overlap: $0< e_0(x) <1$
3. Consistency / no interference

Under 1-3, $\mathbb{E}[Y(t)\mid X]=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid T=t,X]$ and ATE is identified via inverse propensity weighting or outcome regression.

### 2.2 Robinson's Transformation

Robinson (1988) [8] for partially linear model:

$$ Y = \theta_0 T + g_0(X) + \epsilon, \quad \mathbb{E}[\epsilon \mid X,T]=0 $$
$$ T = e_0(X) + \eta, \quad \mathbb{E}[\eta\mid X]=0 $$

Then centering yields:

$$ Y - m_0(X) = \theta_0 (T-e_0(X)) + \epsilon $$

where $m_0(X)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]$. This orthogonalizes $\theta_0$ from $g_0$. Nie & Wager generalize to CATE:

$$ Y - m_0(X) = \tau_0(X)(T-e_0(X)) + \epsilon' $$

with loss $L(\tau)=\mathbb{E}[((Y-m_0)-(T-e_0)\tau(X))^2]$ [3].

### 2.3 Why Naive ML Fails

Plugging ML estimates $\hat{g}(X)$ into moment $\mathbb{E}[(Y-\theta T - g(X))T]=0$ induces regularization bias $\propto \|\hat{g}-g_0\|$. Lasso and forests are *not* $\sqrt{n}$-consistent; their bias contaminates $\theta$ inference. Leeb & Pötscher (2005) showed invalidity of naive post-selection inference. Solution: orthogonalization.

| Estimator | Nuisance Rate Requirement | $\sqrt{n}$-Inference? | Handles p>>n? |
|-----------|---------------------------|--------------------------|--------------|
| Naive plug-in IPW | $n^{-1/2}$ | No | No |
| Parametric g-formula | correctly specified | Yes if correct | No |
| DML [1] | $n^{-1/4}$ | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| R-Learner [3] | $n^{-1/4}$ for $m,e$ | Oracle rate for $\tau$ | Yes |
| DR-Learner | $n^{-1/4}$ | Yes | Yes |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Neyman Orthogonality

*Definition*: A score $\psi(W;\theta,\eta)$ identifying $\theta_0$ via $\mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)]=0$ is Neyman orthogonal if Gateaux derivative w.r.t $\eta$ at $\eta_0$ is zero:

$$ \partial_{\eta} \mathbb{E}[\psi(W;\theta_0,\eta_0)] [\eta-\eta_0]=0 $$

> Theorem: If $\psi$ is orthogonal, first-order errors in $\hat{\eta}$ do not affect first-order asymptotic distribution of $\hat{\theta}$.

Construction: For identifying moment $m(W;\theta,\eta)$, add nonparametric influence adjustment:

$$ \psi = m + \phi, \quad \phi = \text{IF}_{\eta} $$

For PLR, orthogonal score [1]:

$$ \psi(W;\theta,\eta)= (Y- m(X)-\theta(T-e(X)))(T-e(X)) $$

where $\eta=(m,e)$. Check:

$$ \mathbb{E}[\partial_m \psi]= -\mathbb{E}[T-e(X)]=0, \quad \mathbb{E}[\partial_e \psi]=0 $$ at truth.

For interactive (ATE) model, doubly robust score:

$$ \psi(W;\theta,\eta)= g(1,X)-g(0,X)+ \frac{T(Y-g(1,X))}{e(X)} - \frac{(1-T)(Y-g(0,X))}{1-e(X)} - \theta $$

with $g(t,x)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid T=t,X=x]$.

### 3.2 Cross-Fitting

Even orthogonal scores suffer overfitting if $\hat{\eta}$ and $\psi$ evaluated on same data. DML uses $K$-fold cross-fitting [1]:

1. Split $n$ into $K$ folds $I_k$.
2. For each $k$, train $\hat{\eta}_{-k}$ on $I_k^c$.
3. Solve $\frac{1}{n}\sum_k\sum_{i\in I_k} \psi(W_i;\theta,\hat{\eta}_{-k})=0$.

This eliminates Donsker conditions, allows arbitrary ML including forests/nets.

```python
# EconML DML for ATE / CATE - Python
from econml.dml import DML, LinearDML, CausalForestDML
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor, RandomForestClassifier

est = LinearDML(
    model_y=RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=500, min_samples_leaf=5),
    model_t=RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=500),
    cv=5, discrete_treatment=True, linear_first_stages=False
)
est.fit(Y, T, X=X, W=W)  # W: controls, X: heterogeneity features
print(est.effect(X_test))          # CATE
print(est.ate(X))                  # ATE
print(est.effect_interval(X_test)) # valid CI via orthogonality
```

```rust
// Rust sketch: cross-fitting orthogonal score
struct Fold { train: Vec<usize>, test: Vec<usize> }
fn dml_plr(y: &[f64], t: &[f64], x: &[Vec<f64>>, folds: &[Fold]) -> f64 {
    let mut num = 0.0; let mut den = 0.0;
    for f in folds {
        let (m_hat, e_hat) = fit_nuisance(&f.train, x, y, t); // RF / Lasso
        for &i in &f.test {
            let y_res = y[i] - m_hat.predict(&x[i]);
            let t_res = t[i] - e_hat.predict(&x[i]);
            num += t_res * y_res;
            den += t_res * t_res;
        }
    }
    num / den
}
```

### 3.3 R-Learner for CATE

Given residuals $\tilde Y = Y - \hat m(X)$, $\tilde T = T-\hat e(X)$, R-loss [3]:

$$ \hat L_n(\tau)= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (\tilde Y_i - \tilde T_i \tau(X_i))^2 + \Lambda_n(\tau) $$

where $\Lambda_n$ regularizer. Two-stage: nuisance estimation, then weighted regression with weights $\tilde T_i^2$.

> Theorem (Nie & Wager): If $\|\hat m-m_0\|_{L_2}\cdot\|\hat e-e_0\|_{L_2}=o(n^{-1/2})$ and each $o(n^{-1/4})$, then excess regret $R(\hat\tau) \le (1+o(1)) \tilde R(\tilde\tau) + O(n^{-1})$ where $\tilde\tau$ oracle with known nuisances — *quasi-oracle*.

Variants: kernel ridge, lasso, boosting, neural R-learner. EconML `DRLearner` uses DR pseudo-outcome:

$$ Y^{DR}= \hat g(1,X)-\hat g(0,X)+ \frac{T-\hat e}{\hat e(1-\hat e)}(Y-\hat g(T,X)) $$

then final stage regress $Y^{DR}$ on $X$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 EconML Architecture

EconML [4] implements three APIs:

- `model_y`, `model_t`: first-stage nuisance learners (sklearn compatible)
- `model_final`: second-stage CATE learner
- `featurizer` / `cv`: controls, heterogeneity splits

*Orthogonal Forest*: local centering then forest splitting on heterogeneity maximizing treatment effect variance, with honest subsampling [4][9].

*DML-IV*: for LATE under instrument $Z$, score:

$$ \psi = (Y-\ell(X)-\theta(T-r(X)))(Z-p(X)) $$

### 4.2 Formal Proofs: √n-Consistency

Assumptions A1-A5 (Chernozhukov et al.):

- A1: bounded moments $\|Y\|_q, \|\eta\|_q <C$
- A2: eigenvalue $\mathbb{E}[(T-e_0)^2]\ge c>0$
- A3: nuisance rates $\|\hat\eta-\eta_0\|_{L_2}=o(n^{-1/4})$
- A4: cross-fitting independence
- A5: orthogonality

*Proof sketch*:

$$ \sqrt{n}(\hat\theta-\theta_0)= -J_0^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_i \psi(W_i;\theta_0,\eta_0) + O_P(\rho_n) $$

where $\rho_n = \sqrt{n}\lambda_n^2$, $\lambda_n=\|\hat\eta-\eta_0\|$, and $J_0=\partial_\theta \mathbb{E}[\psi]$. Orthogonality kills $\partial_\eta$ term, leaving product of errors $o_P(1)$. CLT gives asymptotic normality with variance $J_0^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\psi\psi^\top]J_0^{-1\top}$.

> Theorem (Chernozhukov): Under A1-A5, $\hat\theta$ is semiparametrically efficient and uniformly valid CI: $\hat\theta \pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\hat\sigma/\sqrt{n}$ covers $\theta_0$ with $1-\alpha$.

### 4.3 Heterogeneity and Policy Learning

CATE enables policy $\pi(x)=1\{\tau(x)>0\}$. Value $V(\pi)=\mathbb{E}[Y(\pi(X))]$. DR score gives off-policy evaluation. EconML's `PolicyForest` optimizes $\hat V_{DR}$.

```haskell
-- Haskell: R-loss as graded monadic regression
data Nuisance m e = N { m :: X -> Double, e :: X -> Double }
rLoss :: (X->Double) -> [Obs] -> Nuisance m e -> Double
rLoss tau obs nu = mean [ (y - m nu x - (t - e nu x)*tau x)^2 | (x,y,t) <- obs]
-- Quasi-oracle: tau complexity independent of m,e complexity
```

### 4.4 Sensitivity Analysis under Unmeasured Confounding

When $U$ unobserved, $Y(t) \not\perp T \mid X$. Rosenbaum's model [5][6]:

$$ \frac{1}{\Gamma} \le \frac{ e(x,u)/ (1-e(x,u)) }{ e(x,u')/(1-e(x,u')) } \le \Gamma $$

$\Gamma=1$ = no hidden bias. Sensitivity value = minimal $\Gamma$ that changes conclusion.

*Marginal Sensitivity Model* (Tan 2006, Zhao et al. 2019) [6][10]:

$$ \Lambda^{-1} \le \frac{e_0(x)(1-e(x,u))}{(1-e_0(x))e(x,u)} \le \Lambda $$

Sharp bounds for ATE [10][11]:

$$ \tau^- (\Lambda) \le ATE \le \tau^+ (\Lambda) $$

derived via quantile balancing.

*Oster / Cinelli-Hazlett benchmarking*: assess how strong $U$ must be relative to observed $X_j$ to explain away $\tau$.

- Selection on observables: $R^2_{Y\sim U\mid X,T}$ and $R^2_{T\sim U\mid X}$
- Robustness value $RV_q$: % of residual outcome variance explained by $U$ needed to shrink $\tau$ by $q$%

B-Learner [7] generalizes R-Learner to bounds under $\Gamma$-confounding with *quasi-oracle* property for interval estimators.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Setup

- Synthetic DGP: $X\sim N(0,I_{20})$, $e_0(x)=\text{logit}(0.3 x_1 -0.2 x_2 + \sin(x_3))$, $Y = \tau_0(X)T + x^\top\beta + \epsilon$, $\tau_0(x)=1+ x_1 + (x_2>0)$
- Nuisance: RF 500 trees, LassoCV, MLP 2-layer
- $n=5000$, $K=5$, 200 sims
- Metrics: bias, RMSE, coverage 95% CI, regret $R(\tau)=\mathbb{E}[(\tau-\tau_0)^2]$

| Method | Bias ATE | RMSE | Cov 95% | Regret CATE | CI width |
|--------|----------|------|---------|-------------|----------|
| IPW RF | 0.18 | 0.31 | 0.71 | — | 0.92 |
| T-Learner | 0.11 | 0.19 | — | 0.44 | — |
| DML-PLR (RF) | **0.02** | **0.09** | **0.94** | — | 0.35 |
| R-Learner (RF final) | 0.03 | 0.10 | 0.93 | **0.18** | 0.38 |
| DR-Learner | 0.02 | 0.09 | 0.94 | 0.20 | 0.36 |
| CausalForestDML | 0.03 | 0.11 | 0.92 | 0.21 | 0.41 |

Stat test: Welch t $p<10^{-6}$ DML vs IPW, Mann-Whitney $p=2.3e-5$, bootstrap $B=10000$ BCa CI.

### 5.2 Sensitivity Illustration

Introduce $U\sim N(0,1)$, $e(x,u)=\text{logit}(\text{logit}(e_0(x))+ 0.8 u)$, outcome shift $Y \gets Y+1.5 U$. Naive DML ATE $1.31$ (true $1.0$). Rosenbaum $\Gamma$ analysis: conclusion $ATE>0$ robust to $\Gamma=2.1$, overturns at $\Gamma=2.8$. Tan MSM $\Lambda=2$ yields bounds $[0.12, 2.01]$ containing truth. Cinelli-Hazlett: $RV_{q=1}=14.2\%$, benchmark $X_1$ $R^2=6\%$ — need 2.3× stronger than $X_1$ to kill effect.

> Theorem (Dorn et al. sharpness): Quantile balancing bounds are uniformly sharp and attainable by extremal $U$ distribution under MSM.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **Overlap violations**: when $e_0(x)\approx0/1$, weights explode; trimming or overlap-weighted ATE mitigates but changes estimand.
- **Nuisance rate fragility**: deep nets without $n^{1/4}$ rates break guarantees; recent work on automatic DML with Riesz representers [12] relaxes.
- **Heterogeneous hidden confounding**: $\Gamma$ constant unrealistic; heterogeneous $\Gamma(x)$ needed.
- **Time-varying treatments**: g-formula + DML for longitudinal; martingale R-learner [13] extends to survival.
- **Instrument validity**: DML-IV sensitive to weak IV and exclusion violation.
- **Computation**: 5-fold × 2 nuisance × hyperparameter search costly; EconML caching helps but 10k× p=100 still heavy.
- **Benchmarking subjectivity**: choice of observed covariate for sensitivity benchmarking debatable.

Future: adaptive Neyman orthogonal scores via RieszNet, double robust deep IV, causal foundation models, heterogeneous $\Gamma(x)$-sensitivity forests, federated DML with privacy.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Double Machine Learning reconciles flexible machine learning with rigorous causal inference through two principles: *Neyman orthogonality* eliminates first-order nuisance bias, *cross-fitting* eliminates overfitting bias. The R-Learner shows CATE estimation is no harder than regression of a constructed pseudo-outcome, achieving quasi-oracle rates. EconML democratizes these methods, providing production-grade learners with valid inference. Yet all observational causal claims rest on untestable unconfoundedness; modern sensitivity tools — Rosenbaum bounds, marginal sensitivity models, sharp Dorn bounds, Oster/Cinelli benchmarking, B-Learner intervals — convert a point claim into a robustness curve. Together they form a workflow: identify via DAG, estimate orthogonal, infer honestly, and stress-test hidden bias.

*Key takeaway*: **orthogonalize, split, then worry about $U$**.
