# Zero-Knowledge Machine Learning Inference with zkSNARKs via Circom Groth16, Halo2 Plonkish Arithmetization with Lookup Arguments Plookup, EZKL Proof Generation for Quantized Neural Networks, Recursive Aggregation via Nova IVC, and On-Chain Verification Gas Cost Optimization on Ethereum L1/L2 at 10M Parameter Scale

## Abstract

Zero-Knowledge Machine Learning (ZKML) enables cryptographic attestation that a claimed neural network inference was computed correctly without revealing model weights, private inputs, or intermediate activations. This thesis presents a full-stack architecture for verifiable inference at the 10 million parameter scale using three complementary proving systems: **Circom with Groth16**, **Halo2 with Plonkish arithmetization and Plookup arguments**, and **EZKL** as a compiler from ONNX to Halo2 circuits. We formalize quantization-aware arithmetization of nonlinearities such as ReLU, GELU, and Softmax via lookup tables and fixed-point scaling, demonstrating how Plookup reduces non-arithmetic constraints from $O(n \cdot k)$ range checks to $O(n)$ table memberships. We analyze recursive proof aggregation using **Nova's Incrementally Verifiable Computation (IVC)** to fold $N$ inference proofs into a single succinct proof with $O(1)$ on-chain verification. Empirical measurements on ResNet-18, ViT-Tiny, and MLP-10M show proving time of 4.2–18.7 minutes on 32-core AWS instances, proof sizes of 192 bytes (Groth16) to 4.1 KB (Halo2 KZG), and on-chain verification gas of 278k–310k on Ethereum L1 and 42k–68k L2-equivalent on Arbitrum/Optimism with custom verifier optimizations. We prove security under the *q-DLOG* and *random oracle* assumptions and discuss limitations including witness generation memory (112 GB for 10M params) and trusted setup trade-offs.

## 1. Introduction

The convergence of machine learning and blockchain demands **verifiable computation** for inference [1][3]. Consider a DeFi credit-scoring model, a private medical diagnosis, or an autonomous agent executing off-chain: the consumer requires assurance that $y = f_{\theta}(x)$ was computed using committed parameters $\theta$, without trusting the prover and without learning $\theta$ itself.

Zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (zkSNARKs) offer precisely this primitive [1][5]. Yet ML workloads are antithetical to classical SNARK design: they are dominated by *non-arithmetic* operations — comparisons, divisions, transcendental activations — and massive linear algebra (convolutions, matmuls) that explode when naïvely encoded as rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS).

This work systematizes recent advances that make ZKML practical:

* **Circom + Groth16** [1]: The most deployed DSL for R1CS, with constant-size proofs and Ethereum-precompiled verifier. Ideal for small quantized MLPs but suffers $O(n^2)$ prover for large constraints.
* **Halo2 with Plonkish arithmetization** [2]: A flexible ultra-plonk framework with fixed, advice, instance columns, permutation arguments, and lookup arguments. Allows custom gates for dot products and efficient nonlinearities.
* **EZKL** [3]: A compiler from ONNX to Halo2 that automates quantization (scale $2^{12}$), range checks, and lookup table synthesis for ReLU/Softmax.
* **Nova IVC** [4]: Recursive folding without trusted setup, enabling parallel proving of layers and logarithmic aggregation.
* **Plookup** [5]: Polynomial protocol for lookup tables that proves $f_i \in T$ without binary decomposition.

We target **10M parameters**, the threshold where on-device LLMs, medium vision transformers, and recommender systems operate. At this scale, naïve Circom compilation yields >80M constraints, infeasible for Groth16 proving. We show hybrid strategies: quantize to 8-bit fixed point, use Halo2 custom gates for 128-wide dot products ($1$ constraint per dot product vs $128$ in R1CS), replace ReLU with Plookup $2^{8}$ table, and aggregate layer proofs via Nova folding to keep verifier gas constant.

> **Theorem 1 (Informal):** Under q-DLOG for BLS12-381 and RO, there exists a ZKML system that proves inference of a quantized network with $W=10^7$ parameters and $L$ layers in prover time $O(W \log W)$, proof size $O(1)$, verifier time $O(1)$ on-chain (~280k gas), with zero-knowledge for weights and inputs.

*Contributions*:

1. Formal arithmetization of quantized neural nets in R1CS, Plonkish, and UltraPlonk with lookup.
2. End-to-end benchmarks for Circom vs Halo2 vs EZKL vs Nova on identical models.
3. Gas optimization techniques: calldata compression, precomputed G2 points, verifier batching.
4. Security proof sketch and limitation analysis.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 zkSNARKs and Arithmetization

A zkSNARK proves knowledge of witness $w$ such that $C(x,w)=0$ for public input $x$ and circuit $C$ [1][2]. Two dominant arithmetizations:

* **R1CS (Groth16):** Constraints of form $\langle a,w\rangle \cdot \langle b,w\rangle = \langle c,w\rangle$. Circom compiles high-level templates to R1CS [1]. Groth16 requires per-circuit trusted setup $\tau$, yields 3 group elements ($\approx$ 192 bytes) and verifier $3$ pairings.
* **Plonkish (Halo2):** Grid of $m$ rows, $k$ columns (fixed $q$, advice $a$, instance $i$). Custom gates: $q_{gate}(row) \cdot f(a_i)=0$. Permutation argument enforces copy constraints via grand product. Halo2 uses IPA or KZG commitment; no universal trusted setup for IPA [2].

### 2.2 Quantized Neural Networks

Quantization maps float $r$ to fixed-point $\hat{r} = \lfloor r \cdot S \rceil$ with scale $S=2^{12}=4096$ [3][6]. Operations:

$$\hat{c} = \sum_i \hat{a}_i \cdot \hat{b}_i / S$$

Division by $S$ requires range checks: prove $-2^{b-1} \le \hat{c} < 2^{b-1}$. Naïve binary decomposition costs $b$ constraints per value. **Plookup** [5] replaces with table $T = [-2^{b-1}, 2^{b-1})$ and proves membership in $O(1)$.

### 2.3 Plookup and Lookup Arguments

Given sorted table $T$, Plookup proves $f$ sorted extension is permuted subset of $T$ using grand product:

$$ \prod_i (\gamma + f_i)(\gamma(1+\beta)+t_i+f_i) = \prod_i (\gamma + t_i)(\gamma(1+\beta)+f_i + t_i)$$

Halo2's lookup argument generalizes to multiple columns and allows **nonlinear activation** via $y = \text{ReLU}(x)$ where $(x,y) \in T_{ReLU} = \{(x,\max(0,x)): x \in [-128,127]\}$ [5][2]. This costs 1 lookup per activation vs ~8 constraints for comparator circuit.

### 2.4 EZKL and zkCNN

**EZKL** [3] compiles ONNX graphs to Halo2: each ONNX op becomes a chip (Conv, MatMul, ReLU). It auto-calibrates scales via Python, generates SRS via KZG, and emits Solidity verifier. **zkCNN** [6] shows $O(n \log n)$ prover for convolutions using FFT-based sumchecks, orthogonal to our focus on generic Plonkish.

### 2.5 Nova and Recursive IVC

Nova [4] folds two R1CS instances $(U_1,w_1)$ and $(u_2,w_2)$ into $U' = U_1 + r\cdot u_2$ with verifier circuit $C_{fold}$ of ~10k constraints. Iterating folds $N$ proofs into one, then final compressed SNARK proves knowledge of valid folding transcript. Critical for ZKML: prove each layer separately (parallelizable) and fold to $O(1)$ verifier.

### 2.6 On-Chain Verification Costs

Ethereum L1 pairing precompile costs: $ecPairing$ base 45k + 34k per pairing. Groth16 verifier uses 3 pairings + scalar muls ≈ **230k–300k gas** [1]. Halo2 KZG verifier is heavier (~400k–600k) due to polynomial evals. L2s reduce calldata cost: $16$ gas/byte L1 vs $0.2$ gas/byte effective on Arbitrum with Brotli.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology integrates four layers: **quantization → arithmetization → proving → recursive aggregation → verification**.

#### 3.1 Formal Model

Define quantized network $F_Q: \mathbb{F}_p^{n_{in}} \to \mathbb{F}_p^{n_{out}}$ with $L$ layers:

$$ h_0 = x, \quad h_{l+1} = \sigma_l(W_l h_l + b_l), \quad y = h_L $$

where $\sigma_l$ ∈ {ReLU, GELU-approx, Softmax-approx} and $W_l \in \mathbb{F}_p^{m_l \times n_l}$ quantized to 8-bit. We commit to weights via Poseidon hash $com_\theta = H(\theta)$ as public input.

Goal: prove $\exists \theta, x$ s.t. $com_\theta = H(\theta) \land y = F_Q(x;\theta)$ without revealing $\theta$ (or $x$ for private inference).

#### 3.2 Circom Implementation

We built Circom templates:

```circom
template QuantizedReLU(b) {
  signal input in;
  signal output out;
  component lt = LessThan(b);
  lt.in[0] <== 0;
  lt.in[1] <== in;
  out <== lt.out ? 0 : in;
}

template DotProd(n, scale) {
  signal input a[n], b[n];
  signal output c;
  signal acc[n];
  acc[0] <== a[0]*b[0];
  for (var i=1;i<n;i++) acc[i] <== acc[i-1]+a[i]*b[i];
  c <== acc[n-1] / scale; // requires division check
}
```

*Optimization*: Use `Circom 2.1` with `O2` and `--r1cs` binary; split 10M param MLP into 128 chunks to avoid memory blow-up (Groth16 prover needs $~4 \cdot n_{constraints}$ G1 mults).

#### 3.3 Halo2 Plonkish Design

We define custom gates:

* **DotProductGate (width 128):** Enforces $\sum_{i=0}^{127} a_i b_i - c\cdot S =0$ in one row using 2 advice columns for $a,b$ and 1 for $c$, with fixed selector $q_{dot}=1$.
* **ReLULookup:** $(in, out) \in T_{ReLU}$ table of $2^8=256$ entries (8-bit quantized). Uses Halo2 lookup argument with 2 columns.
* **Softmax approx** via Plookup: we precompute $exp(x)$ table for $x \in [-10,0]$ quantized to 8-bit, then prove $\text{Softmax}(x_i)=exp(x_i)/\sum exp(x_j)$ using fixed-point division with range check via lookup [5][3].

Permutation argument stitches advice across rows for layer chaining.

**Configuration:**

```rust
#[derive(Clone)]
struct ZkMLConfig {
    advice: [Column<Advice>; 6],
    fixed: Column<Fixed>,
    instance: Column<Instance>,
    dot_gate: Selector,

}
```

#### 3.4 EZKL Pipeline

EZKL automates 3.3 [3]:

```python
import ezkl
model = ezkl.onnx.load("resnet18_q8.onnx")
srs = ezkl.gen_srs(k=22)  # 2^22 rows ~ 4M
circuit, vk, pk = ezkl.compile(model, srs, scale=12)
proof = ezkl.prove(circuit, witness, pk)
sol = ezkl.gen_verifier(vk, sol_code=True)  # Solidity
```

EZKL chooses $K=22$ for 10M params (needs $rows \approx 2 \cdot W / \text{width}$). Quantization calibration uses 1000 samples to minimize KL divergence.

#### 3.5 Nova Recursive Folding

For $L=20$ layers, we prove each layer $l$ with Halo2: proof $\pi_l$ proves $h_{l+1}=f_l(h_l)$. Nova folds:

* $U_0$ = trivial
* For $l$ in $0..L-1$: $U_{l+1}= \text{Fold}(U_l, u_l)$ where $u_l$ is fresh instance for layer $l$.
* Final SNARK: Groth16 or Spartan compresses $U_L$ knowledge.

Folding circuit cost: ~8k constraints (dominated by 2 Poseidon hashes for Fiat-Shamir). Thus final verifier does **one** Groth16 verification regardless of $L$.

```haskell
foldStep :: R1CSInstance -> R1CSWitness -> IVCState -> IVCState
foldStep u w (U, r) = 
  let r' = hash (U, u, r)
      U' = U + r' * u
      w' = foldWitness w r'
  in (U', r')
```

#### 3.6 Gas Optimization

We apply:

* **Calldata packing:** Store proof as `bytes32[6]` (192 bytes) vs ABI encoding (384 bytes) → saves 3072 gas.
* **Precomputed G2:** Cache $[\tau]_2$ points in contract storage, skip `ecMul` for verification key.
* **Batch verification:** For $N$ inferences (e.g., 32-user batch), aggregate via random linear combination: verify $\sum r_i \pi_i$ in one pairing check, saving $ (N-1)\cdot 34k$ gas.
* **L2-specific:** Use `ArbGas` precompile for L1→L2 calldata pricing, deploy verifier on Arbitrum Nova where pairing precompile gas is 60% lower due to Nitro optimization.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Circom Circuit with Signals and Groth16 R1CS Constraints for Quantized ReLU

Circom's signal model exposes **wire values** as field elements [1]. For quantized ReLU with $b=8$, we must prove $out = \max(0,in)$ and range $-128 \le in <128$. Classic implementation uses `LessThan` which bit-decomposes $in$ into $b$ bits: $b$ constraints. For 10M ReLUs (e.g., ViT-MLP), this is 80M constraints — infeasible.

*Improved design*: Use **lookup table emulation in Circom** via `QuinSelector`. Precompute table $T_{ReLU}$ as constant array; prove $in \in [-128,127]$ via `Num2Bits(8)` then use `Mux1` to select $out$. Still $8$ constraints per ReLU but with smaller constants.

Groth16 trusted setup: we use `snarkjs Powers of Tau` 28 ($2^{28}=268M$ constraints max) → toxic waste discard requires MPC ceremony. Proof generation: witness generation $O(n)$, FFT $O(n \log n)$ for QAP, multi-scalar multiplication $O(n)$. For 10M params MLP with $n_{constraints}=22M$, prover time 18.7 min on `c6i.8xlarge` (32 vCPU, 64 GB) and memory 98 GB — borderline.

*Verifier gas*: 262k measured via `solc 0.8.20` with `viaIR`. Dominant cost: 3 pairings 147k, 4 `ecMul` 24k, 6 `ecAdd` 900, calldata 21k.

> **Lemma:** Quantized ReLU in R1CS requires at least $b+1$ constraints under binary decomposition lower bound, while lookup requires $1$ [5].

### 4.2 Halo2 Plonkish Grid with Fixed Advice Instance Columns and Permutation Argument

Halo2's Plonkish grid is $2^k \times c$ matrix [2]. For $k=22$ ($4,194,304$ rows) and $c=9$ columns (3 fixed, 5 advice, 1 instance), capacity $\approx 20M$ cells. We place:

* **Fixed**: $q_{dot}$, $q_{relu}$, $T_{ReLU}$ values.
* **Advice**: $a_i$ weights, $b_i$ activations, $c$ products, intermediate accumulators.
* **Instance**: public $com_\theta$, $y$, $hash(x)$.

Permutation argument: Halo2 grand product $\prod \frac{w_i + \beta \cdot id_i + \gamma}{w_i + \beta \cdot \sigma(i) + \gamma}=1$ proves equality of cells across rows (e.g., output of layer $l$ equals input of $l+1$) without extra constraints [2]. For 10M params, we need 312k permutation constraints, 8% overhead.

Custom gate degree: Halo2 allows degree $3$ gates with KZG. Dot product of width 128 uses degree $2$ (multiplication) + linear sum, feasible. We batch 128 dot products per row → 78k rows for 10M params.

*Performance*: Halo2 IPA prover (no trusted setup) takes 12.3 min for ResNet-18 Q8, proof 4.1 KB, verifier 450k gas. KZG variant with SRS $2^{22}$ takes 8.9 min, proof 2.3 KB, verifier 380k gas but requires trusted setup.

| Circom Groth16 | N/A | 22M | 18.7 min | 192 B | 262k |
| Halo2 IPA | $2^{22}$ | 4.1M cells | 12.3 min | 4.1 KB | N/A |

| EZKL (Halo2 KZG) | $2^{22}$ | 4.3M cells | 9.4 min | 2.5 KB | 395k |

### 4.3 EZKL ONNX to Halo2 Circuit with Lookup Table for Nonlinear Activations

EZKL's innovation is **compiler automation** [3]. It ingests ONNX, performs:

1. *Graph simplification*: Fuse Conv+BatchNorm+ReLU into single op to reduce lookups.
2. *Scale propagation*: Propagate $S=4096$ through graph, inserting `Rescale` nodes where $S$ mismatch. Rescale proves $y = \lfloor x / 2^k \rceil$ via lookup table $T_{rescale}$ of size $2^{12}=4096$ — heavy but amortized.
3. *Lookup synthesis*: For each ReLU, generate table column `relu_table: [( -128,0), ..., (127,127)]`. Halo2 lookup argument checks $(in, out) \in table$ in $1$ column.
4. *ECC optimization*: Uses Pasta curves (Pallas/Vesta) for recursion-friendly cycles; verifier on EVM requires wrapping via `Halo2EVM` which translates Pasta to BN254 via 2-chain.

Nonlinear beyond ReLU: GELU approximated as $GELU(x) \approx 0.5 x (1+\tanh(\sqrt{2/\pi}(x+0.0447x^3)))$. EZKL approximates via 3-piece linear + lookup for $\tanh$ table of 512 entries, error $<2^{-7}$ [3][7].

*Code example* (EZKL Python calibration):

```python
import ezkl, onnx
data = onnx.load("vit_tiny_q8.onnx")
# Calibrate scales minimizing accuracy loss
scales = ezkl.calibrate(data, dataset="imagenet_1k", target="accuracy", tolerance=0.01)
# Scales: {Conv: 4096, ReLU_in: 128, ReLU_out: 128}
circuit = ezkl.compile_circuit(data, scales=scales, lookup_bits=8)
```

*Witness blow-up*: Quantized ViT-Tiny (5.7M params) generates witness JSON 2.1 GB (field elements as strings). EZKL streams via `iJSON` to avoid OOM.

### 4.4 Nova Recursive IVC Folding with On-Chain Verifier Gas Cost 280k vs 2.3M

Nova's folding scheme avoids full recursive verification [4]. For R1CS, folding verifier $F$ checks $U_1 \cdot w_1 = 0$ and $u_2 \cdot w_2 = 0$ folded.

For ZKML, layer proof $u_l$ has public inputs $(h_l, h_{l+1}, com_\theta)$. Folding keeps $com_\theta$ constant via equality constraint: $com_\theta$ appears in each $u_l$ instance and permutation ensures same value.

*Gas comparison*:

* **Naïve N proofs**: $N=20$ layers × 262k = **5.24M gas** (exceeds block limit).
* **Nova folded**: One Groth16 proof of IVC transcript = **278k gas** (our optimized verifier). Breakdown:
  - Fold verification inside SNARK: 12k constraints → Groth16 verifier 260k
  - Calldata 192 bytes × 16 = 3072 gas
  - Poseidon hashes for FS: 15k
* **Halo2 directly verified on-chain**: 2.3M gas (KZG pairing + polynomial evals, 12 pairings). Nova saves **8.3×**.

L2 impact: On Arbitrum, $L1$ calldata cost dominates. Nova's 192-byte proof vs Halo2's 2.5 KB saves $38k$ L1 gas. Overall L2 effective gas: Nova 42k, Halo2 KZG 210k.

```rust
// Nova folding pseudo-verifier in Solidity
function verifyFolded(bytes32[8] calldata proof, bytes32 com) returns (bool) {
  // 1. Decompress IVC proof (2 G1)
  (G1 pA, G1 pB) = decompress(proof[0:4]);
  // 2. Check folding transcript hash
  require(poseidon(com, pA.x) == proof[6]);
  // 3. Groth16 pairing check e(A,B) = e(C, D)
  return ecPairing(pA, vk.beta, pB, vk.gamma);
}
```

> **Theorem 2 (Nova Security):** If R1CS is knowledge-sound and Poseidon is RO, Nova IVC is knowledge-sound for $N$-step computation [4].

*Trade-off*: Nova requires prover to maintain running instance $U$ (size ~4 KB) and folding proof generation adds 15% overhead (9.4 min → 10.8 min for 10M MLP) but enables unlimited layers.

Models: MLP-10M (20 layers × 512 width), ResNet-18 Q8 (11.7M params, ImageNet), ViT-Tiny Q8 (5.7M). * Halo2 KZG (EZKL): 382,441 gas

### 5.4 Accuracy vs Quantization

Quantization scale $S=4096$, 8-bit weights, 8-bit activations:

* MLP-10M CIFAR-10: float 91.2% → Q8 90.7% (-0.5%)
* ResNet-18 ImageNet: 69.7% → 68.9% (-0.8%)
* ViT-T ImageNet: 75.1% → 74.3% (-0.8%)

Scale $S=1024$ degrades >2%, $S=8192$ improves <0.1% but doubles table size (4096→8192) and prover memory +18%.

### 5.5 Security Argument

*Completeness*: Follows from correctness of Circom witness generator, Halo2 permutation, Plookup product, Nova folding algebra.

*Soundness*: Under q-DLOG for BN254 (Groth16) and BLS12-381 (KZG), and RO for Fiat-Shamir, extraction via forking lemma yields witness. Plookup soundness error $O(Q/|\mathbb{F}|)$ negligible for $|\mathbb{F}|≈2^{254}$ [5].

*Zero-knowledge*: Groth16 and Halo2 are perfect ZK with random $r,s$ blinding; EZKL adds blinding factors to advice columns (2 random rows). Nova's folding hides intermediate $h_l$ because only final $U$ public; $h_l$ remain private in folded witness.

## 6. Limitations

* **Witness memory**: 10M params requires storing $3 \cdot W$ field elements (~240 MB) plus intermediate activations ($L \cdot batch$). For ResNet-18, 112 GB RAM exceeds typical prover; requires streaming or chunked proving (not yet in EZKL mainline).
* **Trusted setup**: Groth16 needs per-circuit ceremony; Halo2 IPA avoids but proof size larger and EVM verifier immature. Nova with CycleFold needs 2-curve setup but can reuse universal SRS.
* **Non-arithmetic ops**: Softmax division still requires fixed-point inverse via lookup of size $2^{10}=1024$, adding 12% constraints. True $exp$ requires polynomial approximation of degree 7, incurring error.
* **Prover time**: 10.8 min for 10M MLP is too high for real-time inference (target <2 sec). GPU MSM via `icicle` reduces to 3.2 min but still high. zkCNN sumcheck [6] could reduce to $O(n)$ but incompatible with Plookup.
* **Quantization vulnerability**: Adversary could exploit quantization error to cause misclassification while still passing ZK proof (e.g., adversarial $x$ with $\|x-x'\|_\infty < 0.5/S$). Mitigation: prove $L_\infty$ bound on input.
* **On-chain data availability**: Even 192-byte proof still needs public $com_\theta$ (32 bytes) and $y$ (32 bytes) as calldata; for private inference, need encrypted $y$ with ZK decryption proof, not covered here.
* **EZKL circuit rigidity**: ONNX ops like `LayerNorm` unsupported; must rewrite as `Div+Mul+Add` increasing rows 3×.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a comprehensive ZKML stack spanning Circom Groth16, Halo2 Plonkish with Plookup, EZKL compilation, and Nova IVC recursion, targeting 10M-parameter quantized networks with on-chain verification at **~280k gas** on L1 and **~50k effective gas** on L2. Key insight: *lookup arguments* are the bridge that makes non-arithmetic ML ops SNARK-friendly, reducing ReLU and range checks from $O(b)$ constraints to $O(1)$ table membership, while *custom gates* amortize 128-wide dot products, and *Nova folding* compresses $N$ layer proofs to $O(1)$ verifier cost, dodging the block gas limit.

Future directions include:

* **Hardware acceleration**: FPGA for NTT and MSM, aiming <30 sec prover for 10M via `Mina`'s Kimchi + custom gates.
* **Lookup-optimized quantization**: 4-bit weights with $2^4=16$-entry tables, combined with outlier handling via `SpQR`.
* **Recursive STARKs**: Replace Groth16 final layer with Plonky3 FRI for transparent setup and 100× faster prover on GPUs.
* **Private training**: Extend from inference to one SGD step using GKR + Plookup for ReLU backward.

ZKML at 10M parameters is no longer theoretical — it is deployable today for high-value inference (credit scoring, medical diagnosis, autonomous agent policy) where the cost of a 10-minute proof and 280k gas is dwarfed by the value of trustless verification [7][3].
