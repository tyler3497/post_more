# Neutrino Oscillations and the Seesaw Mechanism: The PMNS Matrix, Leptogenesis, and the DUNE/Hyper-Kamiokande Program

## Abstract

The discovery that neutrinos oscillate between flavor eigenstates — established by Super-Kamiokande's atmospheric deficit in 1998 and the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory's solar-flavor transformation in 2001–2002 — proved that neutrinos carry nonzero mass and reshaped the Standard Model. This thesis surveys the theoretical and experimental frontier that discovery opened. We present the Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata (PMNS) matrix as the leptonic counterpart of the quark CKM matrix, with best-fit oscillation parameters from the NuFIT 6.0 global analysis, and develop the matter-enhanced oscillation formalism that governs long-baseline experiments. We derive the type I, II, and III seesaw mechanisms from the dimension-five Weinberg operator, showing how sub-electronvolt active masses emerge from physics at scales as high as ten to the fourteenth gigaelectronvolts, and we formulate thermal leptogenesis as the leading explanation of the cosmic baryon asymmetry, constrained by the Davidson–Ibarra bound. Finally, we assess the DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande programs — 1300 km and 295 km baselines respectively — whose combined program will resolve the neutrino mass ordering and measure leptonic CP violation, completing the PMNS picture within the coming decade.

##2 Background

### The three-flavor oscillation formalism

In the three-neutrino paradigm, the flavor eigenstates νe, νμ, ντ that couple to the W boson are coherent superpositions of three mass eigenstates ν1, ν2, ν3:

> **Definition.** The PMNS matrix *U* is the unitary transformation ν_α = Σ_i U_{αi} ν_i, where α ranges over flavors and i over mass eigenstates.

The standard parametrization decomposes *U* into three rotations and a phase,

U = R23(θ23) · Γδ · R13(θ13) · Γδ† · R12(θ12),

with Γδ = diag(1, 1, e^{iδ}), where δ ≡ δCP is the Dirac CP-violating phase. If neutrinos are Majorana particles, two additional phases appear in a diagonal matrix on the right, but these cancel identically in all oscillation probabilities and are accessible only through lepton-number-violating processes. The six oscillation parameters — three angles, two independent mass-squared differences Δm²21 = m²2 − m²1 and Δm²31 = m²3 − m²1, and δCP — constitute the complete low-energy description [1].

In vacuum, the amplitude for a neutrino produced as flavor α to be detected as flavor β after traveling distance *L* at energy *E* is

A(α→β) = Σ_i U_{αi}* U_{βi} exp(−i m²i L / 2E),

so that the probability P(α→β) = |A|² exhibits sinusoidal dependence on the kinematic phases Δm²ij L / 4E. The famous two-flavor approximation, P = sin²2θ sin²(1.267 Δm² L / E) with Δm² in eV², *L* in km, and *E* in GeV, captures the dominant physics whenever one mass splitting dominates the baseline.

Matter changes everything. Coherent forward scattering on electrons endows electron neutrinos with an extra potential V = √2 GF Ne, where Ne is the electron number density, while muon and tau neutrinos feel only the flavor-universal neutral-current potential. The Mikheyev–Smirnov–Wolfenstein (MSW) effect — the adiabatic level crossing this potential induces — explains the solar neutrino deficit quantitatively and, crucially for long-baseline experiments, generates a *fake* CP asymmetry that must be disentangled from the genuine δCP signal using the very different baselines of DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande.

### Experimental status of the parameters

The NuFIT 6.0 global analysis of 2024 combines solar, atmospheric, reactor, and accelerator data into the world's most precise determination of the oscillation parameters [1]. For the normal mass ordering (m1 < m2 < m3), the best-fit values are:

| Parameter | Best fit (NO) | 3σ range |
|---|---|---|
| sin²θ12 | 0.307 | 0.275 – 0.345 |
| sin²θ13 | 0.02203 | 0.0203 – 0.0239 |
| sin²θ23 | 0.561 | 0.430 – 0.612 |
| δCP | 197° | 108° – 312° |
| Δm²21 (10⁻⁵ eV²) | 7.41 | 6.92 – 7.95 |
| Δm²31 (10⁻³ eV²) | 2.507 | 2.427 – 2.590 |

Equivalent determinations by the Particle Data Group agree within quoted uncertainties [2]. Three qualitative facts stand out. First, leptonic mixing is *large* — θ23 is near-maximal and θ12 large — in stark contrast to the small CKM angles of the quark sector, a disparity that cries out for a flavor-symmetry explanation. Second, θ13, though the smallest angle at roughly 8.5°, is large enough that the CP-violating terms in the oscillation probability are experimentally accessible; had θ13 been an order of magnitude smaller, the DUNE/Hyper-Kamiokande program would be unfeasible. Third, the ordering of the spectrum — whether m3 is the heaviest (normal) or lightest (inverted) state — remains unknown, as does the octant of θ23 and the value of δCP, though global fits mildly favor normal ordering and a phase near 197°, intriguingly close to maximal CP violation.

### Absolute mass scale and Majorana versus Dirac

Oscillations measure only mass-*squared differences*, leaving the absolute scale undetermined. Three complementary probes constrain it: tritium beta-decay kinematics (KATRIN), which bounds the effective electron-neutrino mass below 0.45 eV; cosmological structure formation, which bounds the sum of masses below roughly 0.12 eV in standard ΛCDM; and neutrinoless double-beta decay, which — if neutrinos are Majorana — constrains the effective Majorana mass mββ. Whether the neutrino mass term violates lepton number by two units is arguably the single most consequential open question in the field: a Majorana mass is precisely what the seesaw mechanism most naturally produces.

##3 Methodology


### Effective-field-theory starting point

The unique dimension-five operator constructible from Standard Model fields is the Weinberg operator [5]:

> **Theorem (Weinberg, 1979).** Up to flavor indices, there exists exactly one gauge-invariant operator of mass dimension five built from Standard Model fields: O5 = (L̄ᶜ H̃*)(H̃† L) / Λ, where *L* is the lepton doublet, *H* the Higgs doublet, and Λ a heavy scale.

*Proof sketch.* Any dimension-five operator must contain an even number of fermion fields (Lorentz invariance) and be an SU(2) × U(1) singlet. With two lepton doublets and two Higgs doublets, the contraction above is the only possibility; adding quarks or gauge fields raises the dimension or breaks the symmetries. ∎

After electroweak symmetry breaking, O5 becomes a Majorana mass term mν ∼ v²/Λ with v ≈ 174 GeV. The *seesaw* is the statement that any ultraviolet completion of this operator — any heavy states integrated out to produce it — imprints its scale on Λ, so that sub-eV neutrino masses point to Λ ∼ 10¹⁴ GeV for order-one couplings. Our methodology derives each seesaw variant by writing the renormalizable Lagrangian, integrating out the heavy fields, and matching onto the Weinberg operator.

### Numerical oscillation engine

We compute exact three-flavor probabilities by direct diagonalization of the Hamiltonian in matter of constant density, H = (1/2E)[U diag(0, Δm²21, Δm²31) U† + diag(A, 0, 0)], with A = 2√2 GF Ne E. A reference Python implementation is given in §5. Sensitivity projections quoted for DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande are taken from the collaborations' technical design reports [3][4], cross-checked against independent GLoBES-based studies in the literature.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The seesaw mechanism: types I, II, and III

The type I seesaw introduces right-handed neutrino singlets NR with a Majorana mass matrix MR and Yukawa couplings Yν to the lepton doublet. The full neutrino mass matrix in the (νL, NᶜR) basis is a block matrix with a zero upper-left block, the Dirac matrix mD = Yνv off-diagonal, and MR in the lower-right. For MR ≫ mD, block diagonalization yields the celebrated formula

mν ≃ −mD MR⁻¹ mDᵀ,

so that the light eigenvalues scale as mν ∼ mD²/MR [7]. With Dirac masses at the electroweak scale and MR ∼ 10¹⁴ GeV, one obtains mν ∼ 0.1 eV — the atmospheric scale — with no small parameters anywhere. This is the seesaw's conceptual triumph: the *smallness* of the neutrino mass is explained by the *largeness* of a new scale, plausibly the grand-unification scale.

![Figure 2 — The seesaw: light active masses as the reciprocal shadow of heavy right-handed states](ths_1789479002000_09282-1.webp)

The type II seesaw instead adds a scalar SU(2) triplet Δ with hypercharge two, whose small induced vacuum expectation value vΔ generates mν = YΔ vΔ directly. The type III seesaw adds fermionic SU(2) triplets Σ with zero hypercharge, giving mν ≃ −mD MΣ⁻¹ mDᵀ in close analogy to type I but with the heavy states carrying electroweak quantum numbers — and therefore, in principle, producible at colliders if their masses lie near the TeV scale.

| Variant | Heavy mediator | Mass formula | Collider testability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type I | RH neutrino singlet NR | mν ≃ −mD MR⁻¹ mDᵀ | Essentially none at high scale |
| Type II | Scalar triplet Δ | mν = YΔ vΔ | Doubly-charged Higgs signatures |
| Type III | Fermion triplet Σ | mν ≃ −mD MΣ⁻¹ mDᵀ | Triplet lepton pair production |

A crucial counting argument tempers enthusiasm: the type I seesaw with three right-handed neutrinos carries eighteen physical parameters (nine moduli and nine phases, minus rephasings), while low-energy experiments access only nine (six oscillation parameters plus three masses, of which two splittings and two Majorana phases). The Casas–Ibarra parametrization makes this explicit, writing the Yukawa matrix as Yν = (i/v) √MR R √mν U† with *R* an arbitrary complex orthogonal matrix encoding the nine unobservable high-energy parameters. The seesaw *explains* small masses beautifully but *predicts* almost nothing uniquely — its high-energy parameters are hidden behind the orthogonal-matrix veil. This underdetermination is the central frustration of neutrino model-building, and the reason leptogenesis, which depends on those very hidden parameters, is both compelling and maddeningly hard to test.

Low-scale variants evade part of this criticism. In the *inverse* seesaw, an approximate lepton-number symmetry suppresses the light masses by a small symmetry-breaking parameter μ while keeping the heavy pseudo-Dirac states at the TeV scale, within reach of the LHC and future colliders. *Resonant* leptogenesis exploits nearly-degenerate heavy neutrinos to resonantly enhance the CP asymmetry, lowering the viable scale to the TeV range. These constructions trade the seesaw's pristine minimality for testability — a bargain many phenomenologists consider worthwhile.

### 4.2 Flavor symmetries, texture zeros, and the fall of tri-bimaximal mixing

The stark contrast between large leptonic mixing and small quark mixing motivated an industry of flavor-symmetry models. The most celebrated was tri-bimaximal mixing (TBM), predicting sin²θ12 = 1/3, sin²θ23 = 1/2, and θ13 = 0, elegantly derived from a discrete A4 symmetry with specific vacuum alignment. TBM fit the pre-2012 data beautifully — and was then falsified within months by Daya Bay's measurement of θ13 ≈ 8.5°, a nonzero value excluded by exact TBM at overwhelming significance.

The field's response is instructive. Rather than abandoning discrete symmetries, model-builders demoted TBM to a *leading-order* ansatz, with charged-lepton corrections or next-to-leading-order operators generating the observed θ13 while preserving the successful predictions approximately. The lesson cuts deeper than one model: the PMNS matrix shows no obvious simple pattern, and its large angles may reflect anarchy — random order-one entries in the underlying mass matrices — rather than symmetry. Distinguishing symmetry from anarchy requires precisely the precision measurements of δCP and the θ23 octant that DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande will deliver, since different symmetry-breaking patterns predict sharp correlations between these observables.

Texture-zero models take a complementary, bottom-up approach: assume specific entries of the neutrino mass matrix vanish in the flavor basis (motivated by Abelian symmetries) and derive the resulting correlations among observables. Of the fifteen possible two-zero textures, only a handful survive current data, and each makes falsifiable predictions — for instance, several viable textures *require* inverted ordering or *forbid* maximal CP violation. These models will face a day of reckoning within the decade.

### 4.3 Leptogenesis: from heavy-neutrino decays to the baryon asymmetry

The universe contains essentially no primordial antimatter: the baryon-to-photon ratio ηB ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ demands a dynamical origin. Sakharov's three conditions — baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium — are all satisfied, remarkably, by the type I seesaw's heavy neutrinos. In thermal leptogenesis, the lightest right-handed neutrino N1 is produced thermally after inflation; as the universe cools below its mass, it decays out of equilibrium via N1 → ℓH and N1 → ℓ̄H̄ [6]. CP violation in the interference of tree-level and one-loop decay amplitudes generates a lepton asymmetry, quantified by

ε1 ≃ (3/16π) (M1/v²) (m3 − m1) sin φ,

and electroweak sphalerons — nonperturbative Standard Model processes active above roughly 100 GeV — partially convert this lepton asymmetry into a baryon asymmetry, since sphalerons violate B + L while conserving B − L.

> **Lemma (Davidson–Ibarra bound).** For hierarchical right-handed neutrinos in thermal leptogenesis, the CP asymmetry satisfies |ε1| ≲ (3/16π)(M1/v²)(m3 − m1), implying M1 ≳ 10⁹ GeV to reproduce the observed ηB [8].

*Proof sketch.* The asymmetry is bounded by the light-neutrino mass scale via the seesaw relation; requiring ε1 times the efficiency factor κ ≲ 0.1 to yield ηB ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ forces M1 above approximately 10⁹ GeV, with mild dependence on the initial N1 abundance. ∎

The bound has teeth: it excludes electroweak-scale thermal leptogenesis in the minimal hierarchical scenario and pushes the relevant physics far beyond collider reach — the *testability problem* of leptogenesis. Two escape routes exist. Resonant leptogenesis, with quasi-degenerate heavy neutrinos, enhances ε1 by orders of magnitude and works at the TeV scale. Alternatively, nonthermal production or flavored effects (where the individual lepton-flavor asymmetries, washed out differently, evade the single-flavor bound) can lower the scale substantially.

The most tantalizing connection is also the most fragile: does the low-energy CP phase δCP measured in oscillations relate to the high-energy phases driving leptogenesis? In full generality, no — the Casas–Ibarra *R* matrix carries independent phases. But in constrained models (minimal textures, specific flavor symmetries), correlations emerge, and a measured δCP near 197° — close to maximal violation — would be circumstantial evidence favoring a CP-violating high-energy sector. Leptogenesis thus motivates the CP program without being strictly falsifiable by it, an asymmetry of logic the field must handle with honesty.

### 4.4 The absolute mass scale: double-beta decay, KATRIN, and cosmology

Oscillations fix two mass-squared differences but leave three qualitatively distinct spectra: normal ordering with the lightest mass near zero, inverted ordering, and the quasi-degenerate regime where all three masses far exceed the splittings. The effective Majorana mass probed in neutrinoless double-beta decay,

mββ = |Σi U²ei mi|,

spans a *band* for inverted ordering (roughly 15–50 meV) that next-generation ton-scale experiments — LEGEND-1000, nEXO, CUPID — will fully cover, while for normal ordering it may vanish by cancellation. A null result at the ton scale would therefore not exclude Majorana neutrinos but would disfavor inverted ordering under standard assumptions; a positive signal would be revolutionary twice over, establishing both the Majorana nature and an absolute mass scale.

KATRIN's tritium endpoint measurement, currently bounding the kinematic mass below 0.45 eV, and cosmological surveys bounding the sum of masses near 0.1 eV, squeeze the quasi-degenerate regime from above. Within standard ΛCDM, cosmology already disfavors the fully degenerate spectrum, and upcoming surveys will probe the minimal normal-ordering sum near 0.06 eV. The convergence of laboratory and cosmological probes on the absolute scale within this decade is one of the quiet triumphs-in-progress of the field.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### The oscillation evidence chain

The empirical case for three-flavor oscillation rests on five pillars: (i) Super-Kamiokande's atmospheric νμ disappearance with the characteristic L/E dip; (ii) SNO's model-independent demonstration of solar flavor conversion via charged-current, neutral-current, and elastic-scattering rates; (iii) KamLAND's reactor ν̄e disappearance at the solar splitting, confirming the LMA-MSW solution; (iv) Daya Bay, RENO, and Double Chooz's precision θ13; and (v) the appearance signals νμ → νe at T2K and NOvA that carry the δCP information. Each pillar has been confirmed by independent experiments; the three-flavor paradigm is as secure as any in particle physics.

### Reference numerical implementation

The following self-contained Python code computes exact three-flavor oscillation probabilities in vacuum from the standard parametrization, suitable for validating analytic approximations and exploring parameter dependence:

```python
import numpy as np

def pmns_matrix(t12, t13, t23, delta):
    """Standard parametrization of the PMNS matrix."""
    c12, s12 = np.cos(t12), np.sin(t12)
    c13, s13 = np.cos(t13), np.sin(t13)
    c23, s23 = np.cos(t23), np.sin(t23)
    eid = np.exp(1j * delta)
    U = np.array([
        [c12 * c13, s12 * c13, s13 * np.conj(eid)],
        [-s12 * c23 - c12 * s23 * s13 * eid,
          c12 * c23 - s12 * s23 * s13 * eid, s23 * c13],
        [s12 * s23 - c12 * c23 * s13 * eid,
         -c12 * s23 - s12 * c23 * s13 * eid, c23 * c13],
    ])
    return U

def osc_probability(U, alpha, beta, L_km, E_GeV, dm21, dm31):
    """Exact 3-flavor P(nu_alpha -> nu_beta) in vacuum."""
    masses_sq = np.array([0.0, dm21, dm31])
    phase = 1.267 * masses_sq * L_km / E_GeV
    amp = np.sum(
        np.conj(U[alpha, :]) * U[beta, :] * np.exp(-1j * phase)
    )
    return float(np.abs(amp) ** 2)
```

##6 Limitations

Honesty requires enumerating what the coming program cannot do. **Degeneracies persist**: the octant of θ23, the mass ordering, and δCP enter the appearance probability in correlated combinations, and while DUNE plus Hyper-Kamiokande break the leading degeneracies, subleading ones involving nonstandard interactions can mimic the standard signals. **Matter-density uncertainties** along the 1300 km chord, at the few-percent level, feed directly into the ordering determination. **The seesaw remains underdetermined**: eighteen high-energy parameters collapse to nine observables, so no oscillation measurement can reconstruct the heavy sector or falsify minimal leptogenesis; the Davidson–Ibarra bound's reliance on hierarchical, thermal assumptions means the viable parameter space is far larger than the textbook bound suggests. **Majorana phases are invisible** in oscillations, leaving double-beta decay as the only realistic probe — and one hostage to nuclear matrix-element uncertainties spanning factors of two to three. **Cosmological mass bounds** assume ΛCDM; extensions with dynamical dark energy or extra radiation reopen the degenerate regime. Finally, **non-unitarity** of the effective 3×3 mixing matrix — the generic signature of heavy sterile mixing — is constrained only at the percent level, and a light sterile neutrino at the eV scale, though disfavored by cosmology, is not definitively excluded by the reactor and gallium anomalies' contested status. The precision era will be an era of shrinking, not vanishing, uncertainties.

##7 Conclusion

Neutrino physics has traveled from Pauli's "desperate remedy" to a precision science in less than a century. The PMNS matrix is now measured with percent-level precision on its angles [1][2]; the seesaw mechanism provides the compelling dynamical explanation of small masses through the dimension-five Weinberg operator [5][7]; and leptogenesis elevates that mechanism into a quantitative theory of the cosmic baryon asymmetry, bounded by the Davidson–Ibarra scale [6][8]. What remains — the mass ordering, the CP phase, the absolute scale, the Majorana question — is precisely what DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande are built to answer [3][4]. Within a decade, we will know whether the lepton sector violates CP, whether the neutrino spectrum is normally or invertedly ordered, and whether the hints from T2K and NOvA crystallize into discovery. Whatever the outcomes, they will either vindicate the seesaw-leptogenesis paradigm as the bridge between the smallest masses and the largest cosmic structures, or force the field toward more radical explanations. The ghost particle is about to speak clearly; the field's task is to be ready to listen.
