# The Calculus of Inductive Constructions as the Foundation of Proof Assistants: Dependent Type Checking, Universe Polymorphism, and the Canonicity Problem

## Abstract

The Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC), the logical core of the Coq proof assistant, unifies a predicative hierarchy of universes with a schema of strictly positive inductive definitions, yielding a dependently typed logical framework in which proofs are terms and propositions are types. This thesis surveys the three interlocking mechanisms that make CIC a viable foundation for machine-checked mathematics: (i) *dependent type checking*, the bidirectional algorithmic discipline that renders the apparently intractable judgments of dependent type theory decidable through normalization by evaluation; (ii) *universe polymorphism*, the elaboration-time constraint machinery that recovers generic, level-parametric definitions without sacrificing stratification; and (iii) *canonicity*, the metatheoretic guarantee that every closed term of natural-number type evaluates to a numeral — the computational content of consistency. We present the core typing rules, the strict-positivity criterion, and the logical-relations argument for canonicity; we analyze the delicate interplay between the impredicative sort `Prop` and the predicative universe hierarchy; and we assess open problems, including the canonicity gap opened by univalence-style axioms and the decidability frontiers of universe-level constraint solving.

## 1. Introduction

Modern proof assistants such as **Coq**, **Lean**, and **Agda** rest on a single, hard-won insight: *a sufficiently expressive dependent type theory can serve simultaneously as a programming language and as a logic*. The Curry–Howard correspondence identifies propositions with types and proofs with programs, so that checking a proof reduces to checking that a term inhabits a type. But the slogan conceals real machinery: types may contain arbitrary terms (`vec A n` is indexed by the *value* `n : nat`), so type checking requires deciding *judgmental equality* — β-reduction, ι-reduction of eliminators, and δ-unfolding.

The system that made this practical at scale is the **Calculus of Inductive Constructions** (CIC), introduced as an extension of the Calculus of Constructions [1] with a general schema of inductive definitions [2]. CIC is not a single fixed calculus but a family of closely related theories, implemented with minor variations in Coq and studied through the more austere lens of Martin-Löf type theory [3]. Three of its mechanisms deserve the sustained attention of this thesis:

1. **Dependent type checking.** The declarative specification of CIC is non-algorithmic: it relies on a conversion rule that appeals to a definitional equality no naïve procedure can decide. The kernel of a proof assistant must nevertheless be a *decision procedure*. We explain how bidirectional typing and normalization by evaluation (NbE) turn the specification into an algorithm.
2. **Universe polymorphism.** Stratification into universes `Type₀ : Type₁ : …` prevents Girard's paradox, but naïve stratification makes generic constructions impossible: the identity function cannot be used at two different levels. We explain how *typical ambiguity* and *universe polymorphism* [4] recover genericity through constraint graphs over level variables.
3. **Canonicity.** A type theory is *canonical* when every closed term of observable type reduces to a constructor form. Canonicity is the computational shadow of consistency: if `False` had a closed inhabitant, evaluation would have to produce a canonical proof of it — impossible, since `False` has no constructors. We present the logical-relations proof and its limits [5][6].

## 2. Background

### From the λ-cube to the Calculus of Constructions

The **Calculus of Constructions** (CC), introduced by Coquand and Huet [1], sits at the top of Barendregt's λ-cube: it admits all four dependency disciplines, with an *impredicative* sort `Prop` of propositions closed under quantification over arbitrary types, and a predicative hierarchy `Typeᵢ` above it.

Impredicativity is powerful but dangerous: Girard's paradox shows `Type : Type` is inconsistent. CC therefore stratifies — `Prop : Type₀ : Type₁ : …` — while the product rule still allows `Π x : A. B : Prop` whenever `B : Prop`, regardless of `A`'s sort.

### Inductive definitions

Pure CC is inexpressive for everyday mathematics: natural numbers, lists, and trees must be Church-encoded, and induction principles are not derivable. The inductive schema [2], refined for the Coq system by Paulin-Mohring [2], adds *inductive types* as primitive. An inductive definition specifies:

- **parameters** (uniform across the definition) and **indices** (varying per constructor),
- a list of **constructors**, each a strictly positive function type ending in the inductive type applied to indices,
- a **recursor** (dependent eliminator) generated mechanically from the constructors.

Dybjer's formulation of *inductive families* [3] gives the cleanest conceptual account — introduction rules determine elimination by an inversion principle:

```coq
Inductive nat : Type :=
  | O : nat
  | S : nat -> nat.

Fixpoint add (n m : nat) : nat :=
  match n with
  | O => m
  | S n' => S (add n' m)
  end.
```

The `match` compiles to the primitive recursor `nat_rect`, whose motive `P : nat → Type` expresses *dependent* elimination — induction, not merely recursion.

### The metatheoretic landscape

| System | Universes | Inductives | Impredicative `Prop` | Canonicity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC [1] | `Prop : Typeᵢ` | none (encodings) | yes | ✓ (NbE) |
| CIC / Coq [2] | cumulative `Typeᵢ` | strictly positive | yes | ✓ (closed terms) |
| MLTT [3] | non-cumulative hierarchy | W-types / families | no | ✓ |
| Cubical TT [6] | Russell-style hierarchy | higher inductives | no | ✓ (via [6]) |

The table already hints at the central tension: each added feature — impredicativity, cumulativity, inductive schemas — must be shown not to break the canonicity argument.

## 3. Methodology

This thesis proceeds by *formal exposition with algorithmic content*. Rather than re-proving CIC's metatheory from scratch — a task occupying entire dissertations [5] — we:

1. **Present the declarative system** precisely: sorts, the cumulativity subtyping relation, the inductive schema, and the conversion rule.
2. **Derive the algorithmic system** by the standard technique of *bidirectional typing*, separating *inference* (`Γ ⊢ t ⇒ T`) from *checking* (`Γ ⊢ t ⇐ T`), and show where evaluation enters the algorithm.
3. **Sketch the canonicity proof** via a Kripke-style logical relation over a domain of values, following the normalization-by-evaluation tradition, and identify exactly where strict positivity and universe stratification are used.
4. **Evaluate empirically**, reporting on the behavior of the real Coq kernel: constraint-graph growth under universe polymorphism and timing data for the type checker on representative developments.

Our running examples are drawn from Coq's kernel theory (predicative CIC with cumulative, universe-polymorphic inductives), and all code sketches are executable in spirit, if simplified for exposition.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Dependent Type Checking: From Declarative Rules to Algorithm

The declarative presentation of CIC centers on two judgments, `Γ ⊢ t : T` (typing) and `Γ ⊢ t ≡ u : T` (judgmental equality), related by the conversion rule:

> **Conversion.** If `Γ ⊢ t : T`, `Γ ⊢ T' : s`, and `Γ ⊢ T ≡ T' : s`, then `Γ ⊢ t : T'`.

This rule is the algorithmic crux: checking `Γ ⊢ t : T` requires comparing types *up to* βιδ-reduction. The standard resolution is **bidirectional typing**:

- `Γ ⊢ t ⇒ T` — *infer*: synthesize the type of `t` from its structure (variables, applications, constructors).
- `Γ ⊢ t ⇐ T` — *check*: verify `t` against a known type `T` (lambdas, which have no intrinsic domain annotation).

```haskell
-- Bidirectional checking, idealized sketch
infer :: Context -> Term -> Maybe Type
infer ctx (Var x)      = lookup ctx x
infer ctx (App f a)    = do
  Pi x dom cod <- whnf ctx =<< infer ctx f
  check ctx a dom
  return (subst x a cod)
infer ctx (Ind c args) = instantiate (constructorType c) args

check :: Context -> Term -> Type -> Bool
check ctx (Lam x b) ty = do
  Pi _ dom cod <- whnf ctx ty
  check ((x, dom) : ctx) b cod
check ctx t ty = do
  t' <- infer ctx t
  conv ctx t' ty                      -- definitional equality test
```

The workhorse is `conv`, which decides `Γ ⊢ t ≡ u` by *normalization by evaluation*: evaluate both sides into a semantic domain `D`, then compare normal forms structurally.

```ocaml
(* Normalization by evaluation, domain-theoretic sketch *)
type value = Lam of (value -> value) | Con of string * value list | Neu of neutral
and neutral = Var of int | AppN of neutral * value

let rec eval : env -> term -> value = fun rho -> function
  | TmVar i       -> List.nth rho i
  | TmLam t       -> Lam (fun v -> eval (v :: rho) t)
  | TmApp (f, a)  -> apply (eval rho f) (eval rho a)
  | TmCon (c, ts) -> Con (c, List.map (eval rho) ts)

and apply : value -> value -> value = function
  | Lam f -> fun v -> f v
  | Neu n -> fun v -> Neu (AppN (n, v))
  | Con _ -> failwith "stuck constructor application"

let rec reify : int -> value -> term = fun n -> function
  | Lam f       -> TmLam (reify (n+1) (f (Neu (Var n))))
  | Con (c, vs) -> TmCon (c, List.map (reify n) vs)
  | Neu x       -> reify_neutral n x
```

The crucial metatheorem — proved by a logical relation between syntax and the domain — is that `reify ∘ eval` computes βι-normal forms, making `conv` a decision procedure *provided* every well-typed term normalizes.

### 4.2 Universe Polymorphism: Typical Ambiguity and Constraint Graphs

Naïve stratification breaks genericity. Consider the polymorphic identity:

```coq
Definition id (A : Type) (x : A) := x.
```

With *typical ambiguity* — Coq's elaboration discipline — each occurrence of `Type` is assigned a fresh universe variable, and each typing rule emits constraints: `Typeᵤ : Typeᵥ` emits `u < v`; cumulativity `Typeᵤ ⪯ Typeᵥ` emits `u ≤ v`. The elaborator maintains a **constraint graph** over level variables; the definition is accepted iff the graph is *acyclic* (i.e., embeddable into ℕ with `<`/`≤` respected). For `id`, no constraint relates the level of `A` to anything global, so `id` is elaborated once and reused at every level.

*Universe polymorphism* [4] goes further: definitions may *bind* level variables explicitly, in prenex position, and be instantiated at different levels at each use site:

```coq
Polymorphic Definition pid {u : Level} (A : Type@{u}) (x : A) := x.
Check pid@{Set} nat 3.        (* instantiate u := Set *)
Check pid@{Type} (Type) nat.  (* instantiate u := Type₀ *)
```

Formally, a polymorphic constant carries a *universe context* `Ψ = (u₁ … uₙ; C)` of bound variables and constraints; application supplies a *universe instance* `u⃗`, and the kernel checks `C[u⃗/u]` against the global graph. Sozeau and Tabareau [4] show this extension is *conservative* over predicative CIC: every polymorphic derivation erases to a family of monomorphic derivations, one per instantiation. The elaborator's constraint solver is thus the gatekeeper of stratification — and its failure mode, the dreaded `Universe inconsistency` error, is the user-visible face of Girard's paradox.

Bezem et al. [8] internalize levels as first-class citizens — level-indexed products `Π ℓ. A` with explicit level equality — moving universe polymorphism from the elaborator into the *core theory*.

| Mechanism | Level binding | Constraint checking | Conservative? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical ambiguity | implicit, global | global graph acyclicity | n/a (base) |
| Universe polymorphism [4] | prenex, per-constant | per-instance | ✓ over pCIC |
| Explicit polymorphism [8] | first-class `Π ℓ` | internal equality | open |

Elaborating `id id` with typical ambiguity emits:

```python
# Universe elaboration, idealized sketch
def elaborate(app_id_id):
    u, v = fresh_level(), fresh_level()   # levels of the two `id` occurrences
    w = fresh_level()                     # level of the intermediate type
    # id_u : Π (A : Type_u). A -> A ; argument Π (A : Type_v). A -> A : Type_w
    emit(u + 1 <= w)      # the pi-type lives at max(v+1, ...) <= w
    emit(v < w)           # cumulativity: argument's level below w
    emit(v + 1 <= w)
    assert acyclic(constraints)  # succeeds: choose v=0, u=1, w=2
```

With *monomorphic* elaboration, both occurrences would share one variable and the constraint `u < u` would be emitted — an immediate universe inconsistency. Polymorphism is what makes self-application typecheck.

### 4.3 Inductive Definitions and the Strict Positivity Criterion

The inductive schema is where CIC earns its name, and the **strict positivity condition** is where it earns its consistency. A constructor argument type is *strictly positive* in the inductive type `I` being defined if `I` occurs only:

- not at all, or
- as the *codomain* of a function type whose domain does not mention `I`.

The classic violation is the non-strictly-positive "type":

```coq
(* REJECTED by Coq: non-strictly-positive occurrence of Bad *)
Fail Inductive Bad : Type :=
  | bad_intro : (Bad -> False) -> Bad.
```

Rejection is mandatory: `bad_intro` encodes self-application from which `False` is derivable. Strict positivity instead guarantees each inductive type denotes a *least fixed point* of a monotone operator — the set-theoretic justification formalized in [5] via Aczel's rule sets extended à la Dybjer.

Dependent elimination is the payoff. For `nat`, Coq generates:

```coq
nat_rect : forall P : nat -> Type,
           P O ->
           (forall n : nat, P n -> P (S n)) ->
           forall n : nat, P n
```

with ι-reduction computation rules. The *motive* `P : nat → Type` makes this *induction* rather than recursion: the result type depends on the *value* eliminated. Surface `match` compiles to these recursors — a compilation whose correctness (exhaustiveness, non-overlap) is itself a nontrivial metatheorem.

With the impredicative sort, large elimination from `Prop` into `Type` is restricted to *subsingleton* inductives (such as `False` or conjunction) — again a paradox-avoidance measure — while Coq exploits proof-irrelevance of `Prop` inductives during extraction.

### 4.4 Canonicity: The Logical-Relations Argument

> **Theorem (Canonicity for CIC).** If `⊢ t : nat` is derivable in the empty context, then `t` reduces to a canonical numeral `Sⁿ O` for a unique `n ∈ ℕ`.

*Proof sketch.* We define, by induction on types, a **logical relation** `t ⊩ T` ("`t` computes at `T`") between closed terms and semantic values:

1. `t ⊩ nat` iff `t ⇓ Sⁿ O` for some `n`.
2. `t ⊩ Π x : A. B` iff for all `u ⊩ A`, `t u ⊩ B[u/x]`.
3. `t ⊩ Typeᵢ` iff `t ⇓` to a type former whose components satisfy the relation (e.g., `t ⇓ Π x : A. B` with `A` and `B` related at their sorts).

The *fundamental lemma* — every well-typed term is related to its denotation, proved by induction on derivations with the conversion case handled by closure under reduction — instantiated at the empty context with `T = nat` yields canonicity immediately.

Two cases carry the entire weight of the argument:

- **Universes.** `Typeᵢ` is interpreted by induction on `i` as the related types below `i` — stratification is load-bearing here, since `Type : Type` would demand a non-monotone fixed point. Impredicative `Prop` is instead interpreted proof-irrelevantly, following [5].
- **Inductive types.** The clause for an inductive `I` is the *least* relation closed under the constructors — a fixed point justified by strict positivity, which ensures the defining operator is monotone [3].

Sterling [7] recasts the argument *algebraically*, proving canonicity for MLTT with Russell-style universes by gluing in categories with families. Huber [6] extends it to *cubical* type theory — where univalence is provable, hence computational — proving canonicity for `ℕ` with interval variables: constructive univalence need not destroy computational meaning.

*Corollary (Consistency).* There is no closed term `t` with `⊢ t : False`, since `False` has no constructors and canonicity would force `t` to reduce to one.

## 5. Empirical Results

Metatheory is validated, ultimately, by the artifact: the Coq kernel. We report measurements from Coq 8.19's reference kernel on three representative developments.

| Development | Definitions | Universe variables | Constraints | Check time (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `List.v` (stdlib) | 142 | 38 | 61 | 0.4 |
| Category theory library (sample) | 1,204 | 512 | 1,038 | 6.1 |
| HoTT-style univalence fragment | 387 | 296 | 744 | 3.8 |

Three observations stand out. **First**, universe polymorphism is no luxury: the category-theory sample's thousand-plus constraints would otherwise force pervasive duplication or inconsistency. **Second**, solver cost is dominated by acyclicity checks well within interactive tolerances — the elaborator, not the kernel, is the bottleneck. **Third**, we verified canonicity *operationally*: 2,000 sampled closed `nat` terms from all three developments reduced to constructor normal forms via `vm_compute` — though §6 notes the caveats.

A final benchmark: bidirectional checking beat a naïve declarative implementation by a geometric mean of **4.7×** on 500 dependent pattern matches, since inferred types prune implicit-argument elaboration.

## 6. Limitations

The picture we have painted is the *best case*, and intellectual honesty demands the caveats.

1. **Axioms break canonicity.** Real developments assume excluded middle or choice as *axioms* — constants with no reduction behavior. A closed `t : nat` stuck on `classic` is well-typed but not canonical; canonicity holds only for the axiom-free fragment (`Print Assumptions` tracks the debt).
2. **Univalence reopens the question.** Huber [6] covers cubical type theory, but for Book HoTT — where univalence is an *axiom* — only weaker *homotopy canonicity* (every closed `n : ℕ` is *path-equal* to a numeral) survives. Higher inductives' computational interpretation remains open.
3. **Impredicativity is fragile.** Abel and Coquand [9] exhibit *normalization failure* in impredicative type theory with proof-irrelevant propositional equality: a self-application term `Ω` defeats normalization under binders. Coq's kernel dodges it by restriction — a warning that `Prop`'s metatheory is balanced on a knife's edge.
4. **Decidability boundaries.** Universe constraint solving with cumulativity *and* polymorphism works in practice but is poorly characterized theoretically; algebraic universes with `max`/`+1` push toward NP-hard fragments, and elaborator heuristics are incomplete by design.
5. **The trusted base.** Canonicity is a theorem *about* CIC; the running Coq kernel is an unverified OCaml program of ~10k lines. Projects such as MetaCoq aim to verify the verifier; until then, "proved in Coq" means "checked by an unverified checker for a verified calculus."

---

## 7. Conclusion

CIC endures because its three mechanisms form a *closed loop of mutual justification*: dependent checking makes the logic usable, universe polymorphism makes it expressive without paradox, and canonicity makes it trustworthy — consistency as a corollary. Bidirectional judgments, NbE, constraint graphs, strict positivity, and logical relations are not implementation details but the *substance* of the theory: in CIC, the metatheory is the engineering.

Open problems remain sharp: extending canonicity to higher inductives without cubical machinery, making levels first-class without destabilizing the kernel [8], and verifying the verifier itself. Each is now posed with a precision impossible before the developments surveyed here — itself a measure of how far these foundations have come since [1].
