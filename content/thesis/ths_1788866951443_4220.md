# Conflict-Driven Clause Learning in Modern SAT Solvers: VSIDS Branching, Restarts, Clause Database Management, and DRAT Proof Checking

## Abstract

Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) is the dominant complete search paradigm for propositional satisfiability, powering industrial-strength solvers that decide million-variable formulas arising in hardware verification, planning, and cryptography. This thesis gives a rigorous account of the modern CDCL architecture. We trace the evolution from the Davis-Putnam-Logemann-Loveland (DPLL) procedure through the clause-learning revolution, formalizing conflict analysis as resolution over the implication graph with the first unique implication point (1UIP) scheme. We analyze branching heuristics from Variable State Independent Decaying Sum (VSIDS) through exponential VSIDS (EVSIDS) to Learning Rate Branching (LRB), treat restarts - the Luby universal sequence and Glucose-style adaptive restarts - and dissect clause database management via the Literal Block Distance (LBD) metric and the glue-clause phenomenon. We survey inprocessing (vivification, blocked clause elimination, bounded variable elimination) and examine DRAT proof logging, drat-trim, and the verified LRAT pipeline terminating in the CakeML-verified cake_lpr checker.
## 1. Introduction

The Boolean satisfiability problem (SAT) occupies a paradoxical position: it is the canonical NP-complete problem, yet modern solvers routinely decide industrial instances with millions of variables and tens of millions of clauses within minutes [10]. The engine behind this paradox is **Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL)**, a search architecture that augments the classical DPLL backtracking procedure with *learning from failure*. Every conflict yields a new clause — a theorem of the input formula in the resolution proof system — that prunes not only the current subtree but an entire family of symmetric dead ends.

CDCL's power decomposes into four cooperating subsystems: **conflict analysis** (1UIP resolution on the implication graph), **branching heuristics** (exponentially decaying statistics over conflict participation), **restarts and clause management** (Luby/Glucose policies, LBD-scored databases), and **proof certification** (DRAT logging, `drat-trim`, verified `cake_lpr`) [8].

> **Thesis claim:** Modern CDCL solvers succeed because cheap local statistics (VSIDS activities, LBD, moving averages of clause quality) approximate a global adaptive strategy that proof-complexity theory only partially explains — and DRAT-based verification makes that strategy trustworthy without trusting its implementation.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Propositional Satisfiability and Resolution

A SAT instance is a CNF formula $F$: a conjunction of clauses, each a disjunction of literals. The **resolution rule** — from $(C \lor x)$ and $(D \lor \lnot x)$ derive $C \lor D$ — is refutationally complete: unsatisfiable formulas have refutations ending in $\Box$. Resolution is the proof system against which CDCL is measured [6].

### 2.2 DPLL and Unit Propagation

DPLL searches by *deciding* unassigned variables and applying **Boolean Constraint Propagation (BCP)** — *unit propagation*: a clause with all but one literal falsified forces the remaining literal true. A fully falsified clause is a conflict, and DPLL *chronologically backtracks* past the most recent decision. Its weakness is amnesia: nothing is learned from a conflict except the single failed branch.

### 2.3 The CDCL Main Loop

CDCL extends DPLL with clause learning and non-chronological backtracking [1]:

```
CDCL(F):
    dl := 0
    loop:
        conflict := BCP(F, trail)
        if conflict != none:
            if dl == 0: return UNSAT
            C := AnalyzeConflict(conflict)      # 1UIP learning
            backtrack to assertion level of C
            add C to clause database; enqueue its asserting literal
        else if all variables assigned:
            return SAT
        else:
            if should_restart(): restart()
            v := PickBranchVariable()           # VSIDS / LRB
            dl := dl + 1
            enqueue v with saved phase          # phase saving
```

**Two-watched-literal** propagation [1] makes BCP nearly amortized-constant time, and the assignment *trail* records the order and reason of every assignment. BCP consumes 80–90% of CPU time [5], so every other subsystem must be nearly free.

### 2.4 Implication Graphs

Each BCP assignment has a *reason clause* $C$ whose other literals were falsified at assignment time. The **implication graph** is a DAG of assigned literals at their decision levels, with edges from each falsifying literal of a reason clause to the implied literal; a conflict vertex $\kappa$ collects edges from the falsified conflict clause. Conflict analysis is *resolution* along this graph: resolve the conflict clause with reason clauses of current-level literals, backwards from $\kappa$, until a unique implication point appears.

> **Theorem:** Every clause learned by 1UIP conflict analysis is a logical consequence of the input formula (soundness), and is *asserting*: after backjumping it unit-propagates its asserting literal. [3][6]

> **Proof:** Each resolvent of entailed clauses is entailed. The 1UIP literal is the clause's only current-level literal; backjumping to the second-highest level leaves the rest falsified, making it unit on the 1UIP literal. ∎

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 From DPLL to CDCL: The Learning Revolution

The leap from DPLL to CDCL, pioneered by GRASP and industrialized by Chaff [1], replaces *chronological backtracking* with *conflict analysis plus backjumping*: a learned clause $C$ lets the solver backjump to the *assertion level* (second-highest decision level in $C$), skipping dozens of irrelevant decisions at once. This is sound because $C$ is a logical consequence of the formula.

The second leap is *persistence*: learned clauses join the formula and propagate in future, so reasoning power grows monotonically. Proof-theoretically, a restart-free CDCL run is a *trivial resolution* derivation; with restarts the connection to resolution is subtler, and whether restarts add proof-theoretic power remains an open problem [6].

### 4.2 Conflict Analysis: Implication Graphs and the 1UIP Scheme

Conflict analysis is the heart of CDCL: given the falsified conflict clause $K$, the solver repeatedly resolves $K$ with the reason clause of the most recently assigned current-level literal. A **unique implication point (UIP)** is a current-level literal through which every path from the decision literal to the conflict vertex passes; the **first UIP (1UIP)** is the one closest to the conflict.

The standard 1UIP algorithm maintains a counter `pathC` of current-level literals [2]:

```python
def analyze_conflict(conflict_clause, trail, level):
    seen, learnt, pathC = set(), [], 0
    for lit in conflict_clause:                       # seed from conflict
        seen.add(var(lit))
        if level[var(lit)] == current_level: pathC += 1
        else: learnt.append(lit)
    idx = len(trail) - 1
    while True:                                       # resolve backwards
        while var(trail[idx]) not in seen: idx -= 1
        p, idx = trail[idx], idx - 1
        for lit in reason_clause(p):
            v = var(lit)
            if v not in seen:
                seen.add(v)
                if level[v] == current_level: pathC += 1
                else: learnt.append(lit)
        pathC -= 1
        if pathC == 0: break                          # p is the 1UIP
    learnt.insert(0, negate(p))                        # asserting literal
    return learnt
```

**Example** [6]: with $a,b,c$ true at earlier levels, decision $x$ at the last level, and 1UIP $y$, resolving backwards from the conflict yields the asserting clause $a \lor b \lor c \lor y$, with $y$ propagated at the backjump level. The 1UIP scheme produces the smallest learned clauses among the standard schemes and is universally adopted [2][5].

Cheap refinements exploit information in the implication graph: **clause minimization** (*self-subsuming resolution*, *recursive minimization*) shrinks learned clauses by ~15–20%, and **on-the-fly subsumption** strengthens existing clauses.

### 4.3 Branching Heuristics: VSIDS, EVSIDS, and Learning Rate Branching

Branching is the most influential heuristic choice in CDCL. The lineage begins with **VSIDS (Variable State Independent Decaying Sum)** [1]:

- Each variable $v$ carries an *activity* $a(v)$, initialized to its occurrence count.
- On each conflict, variables on the conflict side of the implication graph (in MiniSat's variant, variables in the learned clause) are *bumped*: $a(v) \mathrel{+}= \mathit{inc}$.
- Periodically all activities are *decayed*: $a(v) \mathrel{\times}= d$ with $0 < d < 1$ (classically $0.95$), or equivalently $\mathit{inc}$ is rescaled upward.
- Branch on the unassigned variable of maximum activity (maintained in a binary heap).

The decay is an **exponential moving average** over conflict participation, so VSIDS tracks *recently* conflict-active variables [2]. "State independent" is a necessity: two-watched literals deliberately forget which clauses are satisfied, so the heuristic cannot cheaply consult the current assignment.

**EVSIDS** makes the exponential weighting explicit — the bump increment grows geometrically ($\mathit{inc} \leftarrow \mathit{inc}/d$ per conflict) instead of rescaling all activities — avoiding floating-point underflow. This is the MiniSat 2.2 variant.

**LRB (Learning Rate Branching)** [4] makes the objective explicit: each variable maintains $\mathrm{lr}(v) = \frac{\text{participations}}{\text{conflicts since last assignment}}$ as an EMA ($\alpha \approx 0.4$), and the solver branches on max $\mathrm{lr}(v)$. Liang *et al.* showed VSIDS approximates this quantity; the explicit form powered the MapleSAT solvers dominating 2016–2018 competitions [11], with later *distance*-based refinements weighting variables by graph distance to the conflict for better early search [11].

**Phase saving** [Pipatsrisawat & Darwiche, 2007]: each variable remembers its last assigned polarity, reused at decision time. Worth an order of magnitude on structured instances, it turns restarts from amnesiac re-searches into *diversification operators* preserving the good parts of the previous assignment.

| Heuristic | Statistic | Update rule | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| VSIDS | activity $a(v)$ | bump $+1$, decay $\times 0.95$ | Chaff, DAC 2001 [1] |
| EVSIDS | activity $a(v)$ | bump $+\mathit{inc}$, $\mathit{inc}\leftarrow \mathit{inc}/d$ | MiniSat 2.2 [2] |
| LRB | learning rate $\mathrm{lr}(v)$ | EMA, $\alpha=0.4$ | Liang *et al.*, SAT 2016 [4] |
| Phase saving | last polarity | overwrite on assignment | Pipatsrisawat & Darwiche, 2007 |

### 4.4 Restarts and Clause Database Management: Luby Sequences, LBD, and Glue Clauses

A *restart* discards the partial assignment (keeping learned clauses and scores) and resumes from level 0. Heavy-tailed runtime distributions trap solvers in exponentially large unproductive subtrees; fresh decisions guided by accumulated activities and saved phases escape the trap. Huang's 2007 study showed restarts separate solving from timing out on many structured instances.

The **Luby sequence** [Luby, Sinclair & Zuckerman, 1993] $1,1,2,1,1,2,4,1,1,2,1,1,2,4,8,\dots$ (restart after $\mathit{base}\times\mathrm{luby}(i)$ conflicts, $\mathit{base}=100$) is *universally optimal* for Las Vegas algorithms with unknown runtime distributions: no fixed schedule is more than logarithmically worse on any distribution.

**Glucose-style adaptive restarts** [3] monitor search *quality* via learned-clause LBD: when the short-term LBD average (window 50) exceeds $K=0.8$ times the global average (with trail-length blocking $R=1.4$), restart. Rising LBD means poor recent clauses — the search is stuck; falling LBD means progress. This is the restart strategy in Kissat, CaDiCaL, and MapleSAT [12].

Whether CDCL *with* restarts can be polynomially simulated by CDCL *without* remains open [6]. Practically, no competitive solver runs without restarts.

A solver learns thousands to millions of clauses; keeping all explodes memory and slows BCP, keeping too few discards hard-won knowledge. The breakthrough metric is **LBD — Literal Block Distance** [3]: $\mathrm{LBD}(C)$ is the number of *distinct decision levels* among $C$'s literals. A clause with $\mathrm{LBD} \le 2$ is a **glue clause**: it glues together at most two decision blocks, expressing a near-binary dependency propagation can exploit repeatedly.

Strikingly [3]: glue clauses, a minority of learned clauses, drive a disproportionate share of future propagations; high-LBD clauses are rarely useful again. Glucose (i) *never deletes* glue clauses, (ii) periodically halves the learned database keeping the lowest-LBD half, and (iii) *dynamically updates* LBD when a clause propagates. This $O(|C|)$ metric turned clause management into a principled, nearly parameter-free policy; every competitive solver since is LBD-based at its core.

### 4.5 Inprocessing and Proof Certification: Vivification, DRAT, and cake_lpr

**Inprocessing** interleaves simplification *during* search. The CaDiCaL/Kissat line [9] derives much of its edge from an aggressive portfolio scheduled by conflict counters:

- **Vivification** [Piette, Hamadi & Saïs, 2008]: for $C = \ell_1 \lor \dots \lor \ell_k$, tentatively assign $\lnot\ell_1 \land \dots \land \lnot\ell_{i-1}$ and propagate; if propagation derives $\ell_i$ or a conflict, literals can be dropped or the clause strengthened. Modern vivification reuses the solver's own propagation engine — 2–3× cheaper per clause.
- **Blocked clause elimination (BCE)**: a clause $C$ containing $\ell$ is *blocked* if every resolvent of $C$ on $\ell$ is a tautology; blocked clauses can be removed preserving satisfiability. Generalizations (CCE) and **bounded variable addition (BVA)**/SBVA expose new blocked structure by re-encoding [9].
- **Bounded variable elimination (BVE)** [Eén & Biere, 2005]: eliminate $x$ by resolving all clauses containing $x$ against all containing $\lnot x$, keeping the result only if the formula stays within a size bound.
- **Probing**, **equivalent-literal substitution**, and **subsumption/self-subsuming resolution** round out the portfolio.

The subtlety is *proof logging*: every inprocessing step must emit DRAT-checkable justifications, even for satisfiability-preserving steps like BCE — recent CaDiCaL versions produce LRAT proofs with antecedents for the full stack [9].

A CDCL solver is tens of thousands of lines of subtle C; trusting its "UNSATISFIABLE" outright is untenable. The answer is **proof logging**: the solver emits every learned clause and inprocessing step in **DRAT (Delete Reverse Asymmetric Tautology)** format [Wetzler, Heule & Hunt, 2014], each addition satisfying the **RAT (Resolution Asymmetric Tautology)** property [Järvisalo, Heule & Biere, 2012] — a generalization of reverse unit propagation capturing exactly this reasoning.

Checking is a three-stage pipeline:

1. **Emission.** The solver logs additions (`<lits> 0`) and deletions (`d <lits> 0`) at single-digit percentage overhead.
2. **Trimming.** `drat-trim` [Wetzler *et al.*, 2014] checks the proof *backwards* from $\Box$, following only participating clauses — validating and *trimming* away the usually vast majority of irrelevant learned clauses — and emits **LRAT**, where each addition carries *hints*: the IDs of clauses used in its check [Heule *et al.*].
3. **Verified checking.** The LRAT proof is checked by `cake_lpr`, formally verified in CakeML/HOL4 [8]: its machine-checked soundness theorem states acceptance implies unsatisfiability. The trusted base shrinks from "50 KLOC of solver C" to "the LRAT checker plus the logic kernel", and compositional extensions stitch per-cube DRAT proofs into one verified verdict [8].

> **Theorem (informal, [8]):** If `cake_lpr` accepts an LRAT proof $\pi$ for formula $F$, then $F$ is unsatisfiable — with the proof of this implication itself machine-checked.

Checking can exceed solving time, but the SAT competition has required DRAT proofs for UNSAT results since 2013, and the drat-trim → LRAT → cake_lpr pipeline is the gold standard for high-assurance unsatisfiability [8].

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

On structured industrial benchmarks CDCL outperforms learning-free DPLL by orders of magnitude; learned clauses act as *lemmas* BCP reuses across the search [10]. On uniform random 3-SAT near the phase transition ($m/n \approx 4.26$) the advantage narrows — conflicts share little structure — as resolution lower bounds predict [6].

**1UIP dominance.** Ablations rank 1UIP first among UIP schemes; every competition winner since MiniSat uses it [2][5], with clause minimization removing ~15–20% of learned literals on top.

**VSIDS → LRB.** MapleSAT solved strictly more competition instances than its VSIDS baseline [4]; the 2016–2018 podiums (MapleCOMSPS, Maple_LCM) and distance-based early-search refinements corroborate the impact [11].

**LBD management.** Deleting by LBD while protecting glue clauses cuts memory by an order of magnitude with no loss — often a gain — in solving power [3], and LBD-keyed adaptive restarts made the policy the industry default [12].

**Proof overhead.** DRAT logging adds single-digit overhead; `drat-trim` backward checking is within a small factor of solving time; verified `cake_lpr` checking has been demonstrated on competition-scale and compositional certificates [8].

**Formal analysis.** Proof complexity models restart-free CDCL as *trivial resolution with lemmas* [6]; *pool resolution* captures it under a regular depth-first discipline. The flagship open question — whether restarts add superpolynomial power — remains unresolved: industrial practice decisively outruns theory.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **The restart power question.** Does CDCL *with* restarts polynomially simulate CDCL *without*? Buss *et al.* [6] list this as the flagship open problem; a resolution would clarify whether restarts are a proof-theoretic resource or merely a search heuristic.
2. **Heuristic theory gap.** No nontrivial bounds exist for VSIDS, EVSIDS, LRB, or phase saving as proof-search strategies [6] — the entire branching literature is empirical.
3. **Inprocessing vs. proof complexity.** BVE and BCE are satisfiability-preserving but not equivalence-preserving; their status as proof-system extensions is understood operationally (via RAT) but not cleanly.
4. **Verified checking performance.** `cake_lpr` closes the trust gap at a performance cost precluding default use; narrowing the verified-vs-unverified checking gap is active work [8].
5. **Parallel solvers.** Clause-sharing solvers (e.g., Gimsatul [9]) need globally consistent clause IDs; certified parallel solving with compositional LRAT assembly remains frontier work.
6. **Beyond CDCL.** Chronological backtracking with restarts, DRAT-style SMT certificates, and neural branching heuristics all challenge the orthodoxy — none has displaced CDCL on the competition main track.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Four decades after DPLL, the CDCL architecture — 1UIP conflict analysis, exponentially decaying branching statistics from VSIDS to LRB, Luby and LBD-adaptive restarts, LBD-scored clause databases, aggressive inprocessing, DRAT-based verified certification — remains the undisputed engine of practical satisfiability solving. The methodological lesson: maintain cheap, local, exponentially decaying statistics about *where the solver has been failing*, restart before failure modes compound, remember only the failures that generalize (glue clauses), and log everything so nobody must trust the implementation. Until proof complexity catches up, the verified DRAT/LRAT/`cake_lpr` pipeline is the bridge between a solver we can run and a verdict we can believe.
