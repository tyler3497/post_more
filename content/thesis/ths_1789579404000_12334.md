# The Coend Calculus: Ends, Coends, Kan Extensions, and the Integral Notation of Universal Constructions

## Abstract

Ends and coends are the universal quantifiers of category theory: the end of a bifunctor *T*, written in integral notation as ∫_c *T(c, c)*, is the universal dinatural wedge into *T*, while the coend ∫^c *T(c, c)* is the universal dinatural cowedge out of *T*. Isolated by Yoneda and systematized by Eilenberg–Kelly and Mac Lane, these two constructions absorb an astonishing range of universal phenomena — natural transformations, limits and colimits, tensor products of modules, Kan extensions, weighted colimits, and Day convolution all emerge as special ends or coends. Loregian's treatise codified the resulting coend calculus as a quasi-deductive system with formal manipulation rules: the Fubini rule for iterated coends, the Yoneda reduction lemmas, and the nerve–realization paradigm. This post develops the calculus from dinaturality to enriched ends, proves its central identities, and works the nerve–realization adjunction, the pointwise Kan extension formulas, and the coend presentation of geometric realization as extended case studies. It closes with a frank account of the formalism's limits: size constraints, the non-composability of dinatural transformations, and the passage to homotopy and infinity-coends.

## 1 Introduction

Category theory is, at bottom, the study of *universal* constructions: objects characterized not by their internal constitution but by the maps into or out of them. Limits, colimits, adjunctions, and Kan extensions are all instances of this pattern. Ends and coends push the pattern one level deeper. Where a limit quantifies universally over the *objects* of a diagram, an end quantifies universally over the objects of a category *simultaneously in a covariant and a contravariant position* — and this single idea turns out to subsume nearly every universal construction in the subject.

The history is instructive. Coends first appeared in Yoneda's work on homological algebra, where the tensor product of functors demanded a construction dual to the end. Eilenberg and Kelly isolated *dinaturality* as the governing notion of morphism between bifunctors, and Mac Lane devoted the chapter "Special limits" of *Categories for the Working Mathematician* to ends and coends, treating them as the natural completion of the theory of limits [2]. What Mac Lane sketched, Loregian systematized: his *Coend calculus* [1] presents the manipulation of ends and coends as a formal game with explicit rules, in which nontrivial theorems — the Yoneda lemma, the existence of pointwise Kan extensions, the nerve–realization adjunction — fall out of a few lines of integral manipulation.

The integral notation is not mere decoration. Writing an end as ∫_c *T(c, c)* records a genuine analogy: just as integration is linear, monotone, and subject to Fubini's theorem on exchanging the order of integration, coends commute with colimits and satisfy their own Fubini rule. The thesis of this post is that this analogy can be made *rigorous and productive*: we develop the calculus from first principles, derive its core identities, and deploy it on three substantial targets — Kan extensions, weighted colimits with Day convolution, and the geometric realization of simplicial sets. We then examine where the formalism breaks down.

## 2 Background

Let **C** be a small category and *T* : **C**^op × **C** → **D** a bifunctor — contravariant in its first argument, covariant in its second. A **wedge** for *T* with vertex *d* ∈ **D** is a family of maps *τ_c* : *d* → *T(c, c)*, one for each object *c* of **C**, that is *dinatural* in *c*: for every morphism *f* : *c* → *c′* in **C**, the two evident composites

*d* → *T(c, c)* → *T(c, c′)* and *d* → *T(c′, c′)* → *T(c, c′)*

coincide, where the first uses the covariant action *T(c, f)* and the second the contravariant action *T(f, c′)*. Dually, a **cowedge** is a dinatural family *σ_c* : *T(c, c)* → *d* satisfying the mirror condition. An **end** of *T*, written ∫_c *T(c, c)*, is a universal wedge: a wedge through which every other wedge factors uniquely. A **coend**, written ∫^c *T(c, c)*, is a universal cowedge [1][2].

This universal property immediately yields concrete presentations. Because universality can be expressed equationally, the end is an *equalizer* and the coend a *coequalizer*:

- ∫_c *T(c, c)* is the equalizer of the two canonical maps ∏_c *T(c, c)* ⇉ ∏_{f : c→c′} *T(c, c′)* induced by the two actions of *T*;
- ∫^c *T(c, c)* is the coequalizer of ∐_{f : c→c′} *T(c′, c)* ⇉ ∐_c *T(c, c)*.

These formulas demand that **C** be small and that **D** possess the relevant products or coproducts — the first of several size hypotheses that will shadow the theory.

The following instances show how much ordinary mathematics is already (co)end calculus in disguise:

- **Natural transformations.** For functors *F*, *G* : **C** → **D**, the set of natural transformations is an end: Nat(*F*, *G*) ≅ ∫_c **D**(*Fc*, *Gc*). Dinaturality here collapses to ordinary naturality, and the universal wedge is the family of components.
- **Limits and colimits.** A bifunctor constant in one variable, *T(c, c′)* = *F(c′)*, has end equal to the limit of *F* and coend equal to the colimit of *F*. Ordinary (co)limits are thus degenerate (co)ends.
- **Functor tensor products.** For *A* : **C**^op → **Set** and *B* : **C** → **Set**, the coend ∫^c *Ac* × *Bc* is the tensor product *A* ⊗_**C** *B*, generalizing *M* ⊗_R *N* for modules over a ring [1].
- **The hom bifunctor.** The end of **C**(−, −) weighted appropriately recovers the Yoneda embedding's full faithfulness, a first hint of the Yoneda reduction rules of the next section.

*Extranaturality* extends dinaturality to families depending on additional parameters held fixed; it is the technical device that makes the Fubini rule and iterated (co)ends legitimate [1]. With these definitions in place, the calculus proper can begin.

## 3 Methodology

The methodological claim of the coend calculus [1] is that proofs about universal constructions can be conducted as *formal manipulations of integrals*, governed by a small set of rules. The core rulebook is summarized below.

| Rule | Statement (in integral notation) |
|---|---|
| Fubini | ∫^c ∫^d *T(c, c, d, d)* ≅ ∫^{c,d} *T* ≅ ∫^d ∫^c *T(c, c, d, d)*, and dually for ends |
| Yoneda reduction (coend) | ∫^c **C**(c, −) × *Fc* ≅ *F* |
| Yoneda reduction (end) | ∫_c **C**(−, c) → *Gc* ≅ *G* |
| Density | *F* ≅ ∫^c *Fc* · よ*c* (every presheaf is a canonical colimit of representables) |
| Continuity | Hom-functors send coends in the first variable to ends: **D**(∫^c *T(c,c)*, d) ≅ ∫_c **D**(*T(c,c)*, d) |
| (Co)ends preserve (co)limits | Coends commute with colimits; ends commute with limits |

Here よ*c* = **C**(−, *c*) is the representable presheaf, and *X* · *d* denotes the copower of *d* by the set *X*. The *Yoneda reduction* lemmas — sometimes called the "ninja Yoneda lemma" — are the workhorses: they say that integrating a representable against a functor evaluates the functor, exactly as the Dirac delta integrates against a test function [1].

A typical coend-calculus proof follows a fixed choreography:

1. **Encode** the construction at hand as an end or coend (a Kan extension, a weighted colimit, a tensor product).
2. **Manipulate** the resulting integral using Fubini, Yoneda reduction, and continuity of hom.
3. **Recognize** the simplified integral as a known construction, reading the theorem off the final line.

That this three-step procedure succeeds as often as it does is the empirical content of the calculus. To make the encoding step concrete, here is the standard higher-rank encoding of ends and coends in Haskell, in the style popularized by Milewski's categorical programming expositions:

```haskell
{-# LANGUAGE RankNTypes #-}

-- The end of f is the type of dinatural families out of the unit:
-- in Haskell, parametricity stands in for dinaturality.
newtype End f = End { runEnd :: forall c. f c c }

-- The coend of f is the existential hiding of the integrating object.
data Coend f = forall c. Coend (f c c)

-- Yoneda reduction, covariant form: integrating the hom-functor
-- against f evaluates f.  Dinaturality of the witness is enforced
-- by the universal quantification in the continuation.
yonedaReduce :: (forall c. (c -> a) -> f c c -> r) -> f a a -> r
yonedaReduce k faa = k id faa
```

The encoding is honest about its own limits: Haskell's parametricity *approximates* dinaturality but does not enforce the wedge equations, which is precisely why the equalizer presentation of the end matters mathematically. The code is nevertheless a faithful laboratory for the *formal* moves of the calculus.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The End as Universal Wedge: Dinaturality and the Equalizer Formula

Unpacking the definition, a wedge *τ* : *d* ⇛ *T* is a cone-like structure in which the two legs over each morphism *f* : *c* → *c′* — one pushing forward along the covariant slot, one pulling back along the contravariant slot — agree. The resulting *dinaturality hexagon* is the characteristic diagram of the theory. The end ∫_c *T(c, c)* is then terminal among wedges, and the equalizer formula of Section 2 exhibits it whenever **D** has the needed products [2].

The paradigmatic computation is the natural-transformations-as-end identity. Given *F*, *G* : **C** → **D**, a wedge from the terminal object 1 into **D**(*F*−, *G*−) is exactly a family *α_c* : 1 → **D**(*Fc*, *Gc*) — i.e., a family of components *α_c* : *Fc* → *Gc* — satisfying dinaturality, which for a bifunctor of the form **D**(*F*−, *G*−) is *equivalent* to ordinary naturality. Hence Nat(*F*, *G*) ≅ ∫_c **D**(*Fc*, *Gc*), and the end formula turns the set of natural transformations into a limit — a fact with real force in enriched settings, where the end *defines* the hom-object of the functor category [3].

### 4.2 The Coend as Universal Cowedge: Tensors of Modules and Functors

Dualizing, the coend ∫^c *T(c, c)* is initial among cowedges, presented as a coequalizer that identifies, for each *f* : *c* → *c′*, the two ways of moving an element of *T(c′, c)* into the coproduct ∐_c *T(c, c)*. This quotienting behavior is what makes coends the categorical home of *tensor products*.

Let *R* be a ring, viewed as a one-object category, *M* a right *R*-module and *N* a left *R*-module — i.e., functors *M* : **R**^op → **Ab** and *N* : **R** → **Ab**. The coend ∫^{∗} *M* ⊗ *N* is the coequalizer identifying *m*·*r* ⊗ *n* with *m* ⊗ *r*·*n* for every *r* ∈ *R*: precisely the defining relations of the tensor product *M* ⊗_R *N*. The familiar bilinearity relations of the tensor product are thus *literally* the cowedge identifications [1]. The same pattern, with **Set** in place of **Ab**, yields the tensor product of a presheaf with a copresheaf, *A* ⊗_**C** *B* = ∫^c *Ac* × *Bc* — the construction underlying geometric realization, as we shall see in Section 5.

### 4.3 Pointwise Kan Extensions as Coends

Kan extensions are the crown jewels of the calculus. Given *K* : **A** → **B** and *F* : **A** → **D**, the left Kan extension Lan_K *F* : **B** → **D**, when it exists pointwise, is computed by the coend formula

Lan_K *F*(*b*) ≅ ∫^{a : **A**} **B**(*Ka*, *b*) · *Fa*,

and dually Ran_K *F*(*b*) ≅ ∫_{a : **A**} *Fa*^{**B**(b, Ka)} [1][2]. The intuition is transparent in integral notation: to extend *F* along *K*, integrate over all *a* mapping into *b*, weighting each value *Fa* by the hom-set **B**(*Ka*, *b*) that measures how *Ka* approximates *b*. Yoneda reduction then gives the sanity check Lan_id *F* ≅ *F* in a single line.

> **Theorem (Density).** Every presheaf *F* : **C**^op → **Set** is canonically a colimit of representables: *F* ≅ ∫^c *Fc* · よ*c*.

*Proof sketch.* The right-hand side is Lan_よ よ evaluated at *F* under the identification of presheaves with their own left Kan extension along Yoneda; Yoneda reduction applied to the defining coend collapses it to *F* itself. The full argument is a two-line integral manipulation in [1].

The density theorem is the engine of the nerve–realization paradigm developed in Section 5: it says that presheaf categories are *freely* generated under colimits by the representables, so a cocontinuous functor out of a presheaf category is determined by its values on representables — and the coend formula is exactly the extension.

### 4.4 Weighted Colimits and Day Convolution

Ordinary colimits weight every object of the diagram equally. *Weighted* colimits allow a presheaf-shaped weight *W* : **J**^op → **V** to specify the "shape" of the colimit of *F* : **J** → **C** enriched over a cosmos **V**. The coend calculus absorbs weights effortlessly [3]:

colim^W *F* ≅ ∫^{j : **J**} *Wj* · *Fj*,      lim^W *F* ≅ ∫_{j : **J**} *Fj*^{*Wj*}.

Every weighted colimit is thus a coend, and the enriched Yoneda lemma, enriched Kan extensions, and tensors/cotensors all drop out as specializations. This is the setting in which the calculus earns its keep in homotopy theory and higher algebra.

A spectacular application is **Day convolution**. If (**C**, ⊗) is a small monoidal category, the presheaf category [**C**^op, **Set**] inherits a monoidal structure — the *Day convolution product* — defined by the coend

(*F* ⋆ *G*)(*x*) ≅ ∫^{c, d : **C**} **C**(*c* ⊗ *d*, *x*) × *Fc* × *Gd*  [7].

Associativity of ⋆ follows from associativity of ⊗ *via* Fubini and Yoneda reduction, in a computation that would be forbidding in elementary terms but is routine in integral notation. The same coend pattern defines *profunctor composition*: for profunctors *P* : **A** ⇸ **B** and *Q* : **B** ⇸ **C**, the composite is (*Q* ◇ *P*)(*a*, *c*) = ∫^{b : **B**} *P(a, b)* × *Q(b, c)*, making profunctors into a bicategory whose 1-cell composition is literally coend calculus [1].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

We now put the calculus to work on three substantial derivations, presented as the "experimental" confirmation of the method.

> **Theorem (Ninja Yoneda).** For *F* : **C** → **Set**, there is a natural isomorphism ∫^c **C**(c, −) × *Fc* ≅ *F*.

*Proof.* A cowedge from the bifunctor (*c*, *c′*) ↦ **C**(c, *c′*) × *Fc* to a set *X* consists of maps **C**(c, *c′*) × *Fc* → *X* dinatural in *c*. By the ordinary Yoneda lemma, such a family is determined by its values on identity morphisms, giving maps *Fc* → *X* natural in *c* — i.e., a cocone under *F*. Universality of the coend then identifies it with the colimit presentation of *F* as a colimit of representables, which *is* *F* by density. ∎

The proof is characteristic: one line of wedge-chasing plus the density theorem, both of which are themselves coend computations [1].

> **Theorem (Nerve–realization).** Let よ : **C** → [**C**^op, **Set**] be Yoneda and *F* : **C** → **D** with **D** cocomplete. Then Lan_よ *F* exists pointwise by *R(X)* = ∫^c *Xc* · *Fc*, and *R* is left adjoint to the nerve *N_F*(*d*) = **D**(*F*−, *d*).

*Proof.* Pointwise existence is the coend formula of Section 4.3. For the adjunction, compute **D**(*R(X)*, *d*) ≅ **D**(∫^c *Xc* · *Fc*, *d*) ≅ ∫_c **D**(*Xc* · *Fc*, *d*) by continuity of hom, then ≅ ∫_c **Set**(*Xc*, **D**(*Fc*, *d*)) by the tensor–hom adjunction, and finally ≅ [**C**^op, **Set**](*X*, *N_F*(*d*)) by natural-transformations-as-end. Each step is a calculus rule. ∎

The payoff is **geometric realization**. Take **C** = Δ, the simplex category, and *F* = Δ^• : Δ → **Top** sending each ordinal to the topological simplex. The nerve–realization adjunction becomes |−| ⊣ Sing, where the *geometric realization* of a simplicial set *X* is the coend

|*X*| ≅ ∫^{n : Δ} Δ^n × *X_n*,

gluing topological simplices along the face and degeneracy maps exactly as the cowedge identifications dictate [4]. The singular simplicial set Sing(*Y*)_n = **Top**(Δ^n, *Y*) is the nerve. That |−| preserves colimits — the geometrically essential fact that realization commutes with gluing — is now a one-line corollary: coends commute with colimits. Riehl's development of this coend presentation in the context of simplicial homotopy theory [4] demonstrates the calculus operating at full power inside a live research area.

A compact table of further "notable integrals," in the spirit of Loregian's appendix of integral tables [1], records the calculus's empirical coverage:

| Integral | Evaluation |
|---|---|
| ∫^c *Fc* (coend of a covariant functor) | colimit of *F* |
| ∫_c *Fc* (end of a covariant functor) | limit of *F* |
| ∫^c **C**(c, −) × *Fc* | *F* (Yoneda reduction) |
| ∫_c **D**(*Fc*, *Gc*) | Nat(*F*, *G*) |
| ∫^c *Fc* · よ*c* | *F* (density) |
| ∫^n Δ^n × *X_n* | geometric realization \|*X*\| |

## 6 Limitations

The calculus is a formalism, not a panacea, and its boundaries are worth stating precisely.

1. **Size.** Ends and coends over large categories are generally illegitimate: the equalizer and coequalizer presentations require small indexing categories, and extending the calculus to large **C** demands universes or careful smallness hypotheses [2]. Many "formal" coend computations in the literature quietly assume smallness that the application does not provide.
2. **Completeness and enrichment.** The (co)end formulas presuppose that **D** has the requisite (co)limits; the enriched versions further require tensors and cotensors over the base cosmos, and the enriched end is a genuinely subtler object than its unenriched shadow [3]. The nLab's account of enriched categories stresses how much structure the base must carry before the calculus even typechecks [6].
3. **Dinaturality does not compose.** Unlike natural transformations, dinatural transformations cannot in general be composed; extranaturality restores composability only under side conditions. Proofs that casually "compose" dinatural families are the most common source of gaps in coend arguments [1].
4. **Higher dimensions.** Strict 2-categorical and (∞, 1)-categorical analogues of coends require homotopy-coherent versions of the universal property; the Fubini rule itself needs a nontrivial proof in the ∞-setting [5]. The formal rules of the 1-categorical calculus do not transfer naively.
5. **Computational opacity.** A coend formula delivers a universal property, not an algorithm. The integral notation can conceal non-constructive choices — a coequalizer whose quotient is uncomputable, a colimit over an intractable index — behind an elegant symbol.

These are not refutations but *delimitations*: they mark where the calculus ends and genuine mathematical work begins.

## 7 Conclusion

Ends and coends earn their integral notation. From a single universal property — the universal (co)wedge — the calculus derives natural transformations, (co)limits, tensor products, pointwise Kan extensions, weighted colimits, Day convolution, profunctor composition, and geometric realization, each in a line or two of formal manipulation. The Fubini rule, Yoneda reduction, and the nerve–realization paradigm form a deductive system in which the classical theorems of category theory become computations.

The research frontier lies in *formalizing the formalism*: making the "rules of the game" precise as a deductive system in their own right — work connected with directed type theory and dinaturality-as-equality — and in extending the calculus coherently to higher and enriched settings where strict (co)ends no longer suffice [1][5]. For the working mathematician, the practical moral is simpler: when a universal construction resists direct attack, write it as a (co)end and let the integrals do the work.
