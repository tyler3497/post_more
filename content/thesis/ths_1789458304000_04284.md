# Varifolds and Integer Rectifiable Currents: Allard Regularity, Singular Structure, and the Almgren–Pitts Min–Max Theory of Minimal Surfaces

## Abstract

Geometric measure theory recasts the calculus of variations for area as measure theory, replacing smooth parametric surfaces with generalized objects — varifolds and integer rectifiable currents — for which compactness, first variation, and regularity can be developed with analytical precision. This treatise develops the two foundational frameworks in tandem: varifolds as Radon measures on the Grassmann bundle, equipped with the first variation formula, generalized mean curvature, and the monotonicity formula; and integral currents, whose duality with differential forms preserves orientation and boundary data for the Plateau problem. We present Allard's interior ε-regularity theorem in its full analytical context — tilt excess decay, Lipschitz approximation, and harmonic approximation — alongside the singular structure theory of Federer, Schoen–Simon, and Naber–Valtorta. Finally we expose the Almgren–Pitts min–max machinery for the area functional and its modern culmination in the Marques–Neves volume spectrum and the resolution of Yau's conjecture on infinitely many minimal hypersurfaces.

---

##1 Intro

The classical theory of minimal surfaces confronts a foundational obstruction: the class of smooth embedded surfaces is not closed under the weak limits that arise from minimizing or min–max sequences for the area functional. A sequence of surfaces with uniformly bounded area can develop necks that pinch off, sheets that collide and cancel, or oscillations that vanish in the limit, and the limit object need not be a classical surface at all. The Douglas–Radó solution of the Plateau problem in parametric form [1] sidesteps this difficulty by fixing the conformal type of the domain, but critical points of area — unstable minimal surfaces, which in general exist in far greater abundance than minimizers — cannot be captured this way.

Two measure-theoretic generalizations, introduced in the mid-twentieth century, resolve the compactness problem from complementary directions. **Varifolds**, introduced in the minimal-surface context by Almgren following ideas of L. C. Young, are Radon measures on the Grassmann bundle of unoriented tangent planes; they record where a surface is and in which direction it points, deliberately forgetting orientation and boundary data [3]. **Currents**, in the Federer–Fleming theory, are linear functionals on differential forms; they retain orientation and admit a boundary operator dual to the exterior derivative, making them the natural setting for the Plateau problem [2].

This treatise has four aims. First, to give precise definitions of rectifiable varifolds, integral varifolds, and integer multiplicity rectifiable currents, with the compactness theorems that justify them. Second, to develop the first variation formula, generalized mean curvature, and the monotonicity formula — the analytical engine of the entire theory. Third, to present **Allard's ε-regularity theorem**, the cornerstone result that promotes weakly flat generalized surfaces to classical smooth graphs, together with the stratification of the singular set. Fourth, to expose the **Almgren–Pitts min–max theory**, from the pull-tight construction to its recent triumphs: the volume spectrum of Marques–Neves and the resolution of Yau's conjecture [6][7][8].

##2 Background

### 2.1 Rectifiable sets and approximate tangent planes

The atomic notion of geometric measure theory is *rectifiability*. A set $R \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is **countably $k$-rectifiable** if it can be covered, up to a set of $k$-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero, by countably many Lipschitz images of subsets of $\mathbb{R}^k$. Such sets possess an *approximate tangent $k$-plane* $T_x R$ at $\mathcal{H}^k$-almost every point $x$, meaning that blow-ups of the set converge weakly to a $k$-plane with a well-defined multiplicity:

> **Definition (Rectifiability):** A set $R$ is countably $k$-rectifiable when it is the union of a $\mathcal{H}^k$-null set and countably many $C^1$ $k$-dimensional submanifolds. The approximate tangent plane $T_x R$ then exists for $\mathcal{H}^k$-a.e. $x \in R$ [2].

### 2.2 Varifolds as measures on the Grassmann bundle

Let $G(n,k)$ denote the Grassmannian of unoriented $k$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^n$. A **$k$-varifold** $V$ in an open set $U \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a Radon measure on the bundle $G_k(U) = U \times G(n,k)$. The projection onto $U$ yields the **weight measure** $\|V\|$, and for $\|V\|$-a.e. $x$ there is a well-defined approximate tangent plane $T_x V$.

A varifold is **rectifiable** if there exist a countably $k$-rectifiable set $R$ and a Borel density function $\theta : R \to (0,\infty)$ such that

$$V = v(R, \theta), \qquad v(R,\theta)(\varphi) = \int_R \varphi(x, T_x R)\, \theta(x)\, d\mathcal{H}^k(x)$$

for all test functions $\varphi$ on the Grassmann bundle. When $\theta$ takes values in the positive integers $\mathbb{N}$, the varifold is called **integral**, denoted $IV_k(U)$ [1].

The $k$-dimensional **density** of $V$ at $x$ is the normalized mass ratio

$$\Theta^k(\|V\|, x) = \lim_{r \downarrow 0} \frac{\|V\|(B_r(x))}{\omega_k r^k},$$

when the limit exists; for integral varifolds it exists $\|V\|$-a.e. and equals $\theta(x)$ a.e.

### 2.3 Currents and integer multiplicity

A $k$-**current** $T$ is a continuous linear functional on compactly supported smooth $k$-forms. The **mass** $\mathbf{M}(T)$ is the supremum of $T(\omega)$ over forms with comass at most one. A current is **rectifiable** if it integrates forms against an oriented rectifiable set with a density, and **integral** (an *integer multiplicity rectifiable current*) if additionally $T$ and its boundary $\partial T$ have finite mass — the celebrated **boundary rectifiability theorem** of Federer–Fleming [2].

The two frameworks are compared below:

| Feature | Varifolds | Currents |
|---|---|---|
| Basic object | Radon measure on $G_k(U)$ | Linear functional on $k$-forms |
| Orientation | Forgotten (unoriented planes) | Retained (oriented tangent $k$-vectors) |
| Boundary | No boundary operator | Boundary via Stokes duality, $\partial T$ |
| Cancellation | None — mass is additive | Possible — oppositely oriented sheets cancel |
| Natural problem | Min–max / critical points | Plateau / minimization with boundary |
| Compactness | Allard's integral compactness | Federer–Fleming compactness |

The decisive point for min–max theory is that **currents fail the Palais–Smale property**: a sequence of minimal surfaces can converge as currents to the zero current (oppositely oriented parallel sheets annihilate), while the corresponding varifolds converge to a nonzero stationary varifold [3]. This is why Almgren built min–max on varifolds.

##3 Methodology

### 3.1 First variation and generalized mean curvature

For a varifold $V$ and a compactly supported $C^1$ vector field $X$ on $U$, the **first variation** is the rate of change of mass under the flow of $X$:

$$\delta V(X) = \int_{G_k(U)} \operatorname{div}_S X(x)\, dV(x,S),$$

where $\operatorname{div}_S X = \sum_{i=1}^k \langle D_{e_i} X, e_i \rangle$ for an orthonormal basis of the plane $S$. A varifold is **stationary** when $\delta V = 0$. More generally, if $|\delta V(X)| \leq C \sup |X|$ then Riesz representation yields a $\|V\|$-measurable vector field $\mathbf{H}$, the **generalized mean curvature**, satisfying

$$\delta V(X) = -\int_U X \cdot \mathbf{H}\, d\|V\|.$$ Allard's foundational 1972 paper established that $\mathbf{H} \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ always exists under this boundedness hypothesis [5].

### 3.2 Monotonicity and tangent cones

The monotonicity formula is the analytical heart of the theory. For a stationary integral $k$-varifold in $\mathbb{R}^n$:

> **Theorem (Monotonicity):** For $x \in \operatorname{spt}\|V\|$, the density ratio $\Phi(r) = \|V\|(B_r(x)) / (\omega_k r^k)$ is nondecreasing in $r$, and the limit $\Theta^k(\|V\|,x) = \lim_{r\downarrow 0} \Phi(r)$ exists [1].

*Method of proof.* Differentiating $\Phi$ and inserting the radial vector field into the first variation yields a nonnegative derivative: the excess of the tangential gradient of the radial function integrates to the defect from conicality. A quantitative version holds with mean curvature in $L^p$, $p > k$, adding a controlled error term [5].

Consequences are profound:

1. **Upper semicontinuity** of the density $\Theta^k(\|V\|, \cdot)$.
2. **Existence of tangent cones:** for blow-ups $\eta_{x,r}(y) = (y-x)/r$, any varifold limit $C$ of $(\eta_{x,r})_\# V$ as $r \downarrow 0$ is a *stationary cone* — invariant under dilations about the origin.
3. **Stratification:** the singular set stratifies by the maximal dimension of translation invariance of tangent cones, $\mathcal{S} = \bigcup_j \mathcal{S}_j$, with $\dim_{\mathcal{H}} \mathcal{S}_j \leq j$ (Federer dimension reduction).

### 3.3 The regularity program: excess decay

Allard's ε-regularity theorem proceeds by an excess-decay iteration [5]:

1. **Tilt excess.** For a ball $B_\rho(\xi)$ and a $k$-plane $T$, define $E(\xi,\rho,T) = \rho^{-k} \int_{B_\rho(\xi)} \|p_{T_x M} - p_T\|^2\, d\mu$, the $L^2$ deviation of tangent planes from $T$.
2. **Lipschitz approximation.** Small excess implies the varifold is close in measure to a Lipschitz graph over $T$, except on a small exceptional set.
3. **Harmonic approximation.** The approximating Lipschitz function is close in $L^2$ to a harmonic function, because stationarity linearizes to the minimal surface equation's linearization — the Laplace equation.
4. **Excess improvement.** Harmonic decay yields $E(\theta\rho) \leq \theta^{2\alpha} E(\rho)$ for some fixed scale factor $\theta \in (0,1)$, iterating to $C^{1,\alpha}$ regularity.

A numerical illustration of the monotonicity principle is straightforward to implement:

```python
import math

def density_ratio(mass_in_ball, radius, k):
    omega_k = math.pi ** (k / 2.0) / math.gamma(k / 2.0 + 1.0)
    return mass_in_ball / (omega_k * radius ** k)

def check_monotonicity(radii_masses, k):
    ratios = [density_ratio(m, r, k) for (r, m) in radii_masses]
    pairs = zip(ratios, ratios[1:])
    monotone = all(b >= a - 1e-9 for (a, b) in pairs)
    return monotone, ratios

# For a stationary cone, each ratio is constant; for a general
# stationary varifold the sequence is nondecreasing as r shrinks.
```

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The first variation formula in coordinates

Writing the first variation in an orthonormal frame adapted to $S \in G(n,k)$ exposes its geometric content. With $p_S$ the orthogonal projection onto $S$ and $p_S^\perp$ onto its orthogonal complement,

$$\operatorname{div}_S X = \operatorname{tr}(p_S \circ DX) = \sum_{i,j} S_{ij}\, D_j X_i,$$

so that $\delta V(X) = \int \langle S, DX \rangle\, dV$ pairs the varifold's Grassmannian component against the deformation gradient. For a smooth submanifold with multiplicity one, integration by parts recovers the classical formula $\delta V(X) = -\int X \cdot H\, d\mathcal{H}^k$ with $H$ the mean curvature vector. The power of the varifold formulation is that this identity *defines* mean curvature for objects far too rough for classical differential geometry — countably rectifiable sets with $L^p$ curvature — and Allard's theorem shows this weak curvature already controls the geometry [1][5].

The density lower bound $\Theta^k \geq 1$ $\|V\|$-a.e. for integral varifolds prevents mass from leaking to infinitesimal scales, and together with upper semicontinuity forces the support to be closed and the singular set to be relatively closed — prerequisites for any regularity theory.

### 4.2 Allard's ε-regularity theorem

We now state the central regularity result in full [5]:

> **Theorem (Allard, 1972):** Let $V = v(M,\theta)$ be a rectifiable $k$-varifold in an open set $U \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+k}$ with generalized mean curvature $\mathbf{H} \in L^p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\|V\|)$, $p > k$. Assume $\theta \geq 1$ $\mu$-a.e., $0 \in \operatorname{spt}\mu$, $B_\rho(0) \subset U$, and that for some $k$-plane $T$ the tilt excess $E(0,\rho,T)$ and the scaled curvature $\rho^{1-k/p}\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^p}$ are smaller than a constant $\varepsilon = \varepsilon(n,k,p,\alpha)$. Then $\operatorname{spt}\mu \cap B_{\gamma\rho}(0)$ coincides with the graph of a $C^{1,\alpha}$ function $u : T \to T^\perp$, with $\alpha = 1 - k/p$.

Three remarks are in order. *First*, the hypothesis $p > k$ is sharp: mean curvature in $L^k$ does not imply regularity, as branched examples show. *Second*, the conclusion is optimal — curvature in $L^p$ yields precisely $C^{1,1-k/p}$ graphs, matching the classical Schauder theory. *Third*, the proof's harmonic approximation step is where the variational structure enters decisively: it is stationarity (or the $L^p$ curvature bound) that forces the blow-up limit of the Lipschitz approximation to be harmonic rather than merely $L^2$-close to a plane.

The theorem applies verbatim to stationary varifolds ($\mathbf{H} = 0$) and to varifolds arising as limits of smooth minimal surfaces with uniform area bounds — exactly the outputs of min–max constructions [3].

### 4.3 Singular structure: dimension bounds and the Simons cone

Where excess is not small, singularities occur. Their structure is governed by tangent cones. In codimension one, Federer's dimension-reduction argument shows the singular set of a stationary integral hypersurface has Hausdorff dimension at most $n-8$, because every tangent cone splits off a line and the only stable minimal cones in low dimensions are planes — a classification completed by Simons, whose eponymous cone

$$C_S = \{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^4 \times \mathbb{R}^4 : |x| = |y|\}$$

is stable and minimizing in $\mathbb{R}^8$, proving the bound $n-8$ sharp [1][3].

Schoen–Simon extended this to *stable* minimal hypersurfaces, proving curvature estimates and compactness for stable hypersurfaces with a uniform area bound — the regularity input that Pitts needed for min–max limits [6]. In higher codimension, branch points genuinely occur (complex varieties provide examples), and the regularity theory is substantially subtler; De Lellis' survey gives the definitive modern account [1]. Recent quantitative work of Naber–Valtorta provides Minkowski-type volume bounds on the strata of the singular set, upgrading Hausdorff dimension estimates to effective ones.

### 4.4 The Almgren–Pitts min–max construction

Min–max seeks *unstable* critical points of area via a mountain-pass procedure. Let $(M^{n+1}, g)$ be a closed Riemannian manifold and $\mathcal{Z}_n(M)$ the space of integral $n$-cycles with the flat topology. A **sweepout** is a continuous map $\Phi : (I, \partial I) \to (\mathcal{Z}_n(M), \mathcal{F})$ representing a nontrivial homology class, sweeping $M$ by hypersurfaces. The **width** is

$$W = \inf_{\Phi} \max_{t} \mathbf{M}(\Phi(t)),$$

the least possible maximal slice area. A minimizing sequence $\{\Phi_i\}$ is **pulled tight** by pushing each sweepout down along the gradient flow of area until every varifold in the critical set is stationary; Pitts' combinatorial **almost-minimizing** argument then shows the limit varifold is regular in small annuli, and Schoen–Simon curvature estimates promote it to a smooth embedded minimal hypersurface (for $3 \leq n+1 \leq 7$) [6][8].

The schematic logic can be expressed as a state machine:

```tla
---- MODULE MinMax ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES sweepout, width, criticalSet

Init == /\ sweepout \in Sweepouts
        /\ width = InfMaxArea(sweepout)
        /\ criticalSet = {}

PullTight == /\ criticalSet' = StationaryVarifolds(sweepout)
             /\ UNCHANGED <<sweepout, width>>

AlmostMinimizing == /\ \E V \in criticalSet : AlmostMinInAnnuli(V)
                    /\ criticalSet' = {V}
                    /\ UNCHANGED <<sweepout, width>>

Regularize == /\ SchoenSimonEstimates(criticalSet)
              /\ criticalSet' = {SmoothEmbeddedMinimalHypersurface}
              /\ UNCHANGED <<sweepout, width>>

Next == PullTight \/ AlmostMinimizing \/ Regularize
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<sweepout, width, criticalSet>>
====
```

The theorem of Almgren–Pitts–Schoen–Simon: *every closed Riemannian manifold of dimension between 3 and 7 contains a closed, smooth, embedded minimal hypersurface* [6][7].

### 4.5 The volume spectrum and Yau's conjecture

Marques–Neves refined min–max to a *$p$-parameter* family, producing for each $p \in \mathbb{N}$ a minimal hypersurface of area $\omega_p$, the **$p$-width**. The sequence $\{\omega_p\}$ obeys the **Weyl law**

$$\omega_p \sim a(n)\, \operatorname{Vol}(M)^{n/(n+1)}\, p^{1/(n+1)} \qquad (p \to \infty),$$

proved by Liokumovich–Marques–Neves, with the constant $a(n)$ computed by Chodosh–Mantoulidis [6]. From the Weyl law and a Lusternik–Schnirelmann-type argument, Irie–Marques–Neves proved that for *generic* (bumpy) metrics the union of minimal hypersurfaces is dense, and Marques–Neves–Song proved equidistribution [7].

Yau's 1982 conjecture — that every closed 3-manifold contains infinitely many minimal surfaces — was settled in full by Song, who localized the Marques–Neves machinery to the *core* of the manifold, removing the genericity hypothesis [7]. Zhou's resolution of the **multiplicity one conjecture** guarantees the min–max hypersurfaces occur with multiplicity one for generic metrics, and the index estimates of Marques–Neves bound the Morse index by the number of parameters — completing a Morse-theoretic picture of the area functional envisioned since Almgren [6][8].

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 The excess-decay estimate

The quantitative core of Allard's proof is the improvement of flatness:

> **Lemma (Excess decay):** Under the hypotheses of Allard's theorem, there exist $\theta \in (0,1)$ and $C$ such that $E(0, \theta\rho, T') \leq \theta^{2\alpha} E(0,\rho,T)$ for a possibly rotated plane $T'$, with $|T' - T| \leq C\sqrt{E(0,\rho,T)}$.

Iterating gives $E(0, \theta^j \rho) \leq \theta^{2\alpha j} E(0,\rho)$, whence the tangent planes converge at a Hölder rate and the support is a $C^{1,\alpha}$ graph. The proof's architecture is summarized below [5]:

| Step | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Tilt vs height | Excess small | $L^2$ height bound via test vector fields |
| Lipschitz approximation | Height bound | Graph over $T$ outside a small bad set |
| Harmonic approximation | Stationarity / $L^p$ curvature | $L^2$-closeness to a harmonic function |
| Excess improvement | Harmonic decay estimates | Decay at the smaller scale |
| Iteration | Decay at all scales | $C^{1,\alpha}$ graph |

### 5.2 Width estimates and the Weyl law

Gromov and Guth proved sublinear upper bounds on the $p$-widths by explicit sweepout constructions (bend-and-cancel families), while the Weyl law gives the sharp asymptotic growth. Numerically, for the unit 3-sphere the first width is $4\pi$ (the equatorial 2-sphere), and $\omega_p$ grows like $p^{1/3}$ — a prediction confirmed by the Marques–Neves–Song equidistribution theorem, which implies the corresponding minimal surfaces become uniformly distributed in $M$ [6][7].

A compact computational model of a sweepout width is:

```rust
struct Sweepout {
    slices: Vec<Surface>,
}

impl Sweepout {
    fn max_area(&self) -> f64 {
        self.slices.iter().map(|s| s.area()).fold(0.0, f64::max)
    }
    fn width(candidates: &[Sweepout]) -> f64 {
        candidates.iter().map(|s| s.max_area()).fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min)
    }
}
// The min-max hypersurface realizes the infimum of maximal slice areas.
```

### 5.3 Regularity of min–max limits: proof outline

Pitts' regularity argument proceeds in three stages. *First*, the pull-tight procedure of Almgren produces a stationary varifold $V$ in the critical set with $\|V\|(M) = W$. *Second*, the combinatorial almost-minimizing property implies $V$ is almost minimizing in small annuli, hence (by a replacement argument) smooth and stable in each annulus punctured at finitely many points. *Third*, the Schoen–Simon curvature estimates for stable hypersurfaces rule out the punctures in dimensions $n+1 \leq 7$, because a stable minimal hypersurface with an isolated singularity would contradict the Bernstein-type rigidity [6][8]. The Simons cone shows this dimension restriction is sharp.

##6 Limitations

1. **Dimensional barrier.** The Simons cone in $\mathbb{R}^8$ is a stable, area-minimizing hypercone with an isolated singularity; consequently no regularity theory for stationary varifolds can hold in full generality above dimension 7. Min–max in higher dimensions produces varifolds with a singular set of dimension at most $n-7$ (Schoen–Simon), not smooth hypersurfaces [1][3].
2. **Integrability threshold.** Allard's theorem requires mean curvature in $L^p$ with $p > k$; the borderline $p = k$ is false in general, and varifolds with merely $L^1$ curvature can be purely singular.
3. **Orientation loss in varifolds.** Forgetting orientation means varifolds cannot encode the Plateau boundary condition; min–max varifold limits may also exhibit higher multiplicity, and removing it required the deep work of Zhou on multiplicity one [6].
4. **Palais–Smale failure for currents.** Oppositely oriented sheets cancel in the flat topology, so currents cannot directly host a min–max critical point theory — the original motivation for varifolds [3].
5. **Higher codimension.** Branch points are unavoidable (holomorphic varieties), and no Allard-type theorem is known for general elliptic integrands; the regularity theory there remains a major open frontier [1].
6. **Ambient singularities.** Extending ε-regularity to Alexandrov spaces and other singular ambients is recent and partial; curvature bounds in the synthetic sense do not yet recover the full excess-decay machinery [4].
7. **Uniqueness of tangent cones.** Whether tangent cones at a singularity are unique remains open in general; Simon's Łojasiewicz-type argument settles it under integrability conditions, but the general case resists [1].

---

##7 Conclusion

Geometric measure theory transformed the study of minimal surfaces from a subject constrained by parametrization to one governed by measure, duality, and compactness. Varifolds supply the weak closure needed for critical-point theory: the first variation formula endows them with curvature, monotonicity yields tangent cones, and Allard's ε-regularity theorem promotes weak flatness to classical smoothness. Currents, retaining orientation and boundary, supply the complementary closure for minimization problems. The Almgren–Pitts min–max construction fuses these tools — varifold compactness, pull-tight, almost-minimizing replacements, Schoen–Simon estimates — into an existence machine whose modern refinements (the Marques–Neves volume spectrum, the Weyl law, multiplicity one) resolved Yau's conjecture and opened a Morse-theoretic study of the area functional [6][7][8]. The dimensional barrier at $n+1 = 8$, the structure of branch points in higher codimension, and the uniqueness of tangent cones remain the central open horizons — each a precise analytical question about how much regularity the area functional can enforce on its critical points.
