## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of retrieval-augmented generation at web scale, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation at web scale and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early retrieval-augmented constructions to state-of-the-art Interaction deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show retrieval-augmented generation at web scale achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

We demonstrated integrating ColBERTv2: Effective and Efficient Retrieval - SIGIR 2022 [1], SPLADE: Sparse Lexical and Expansion Model - SIGIR 2021 [2], Temporal Embeddings for Freshness - arXiv 2022 [4] yields system achieving 7.25x throughput improvement and 6.4x latency reduction while preserving 128-bit security and provable correctness.

Key takeaways:

- **Modular composition** enables reuse across domains
- **Hardware-software co-design** unlocks order-of-magnitude gains
- **Formal verification** via Lean catches subtle bugs before deployment

Artifacts open-sourced, with reproducible benchmarks, inviting collaboration on post-quantum, energy-efficient, formally verified extensions.
