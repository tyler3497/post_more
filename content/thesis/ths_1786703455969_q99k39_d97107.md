# Neuromorphic Spiking Neural Networks on Intel Loihi 2 and SpiNNaker2: STDP Learning Dynamics, Event-Driven AER Routing, Sparse Temporal Coding, and On-Chip Online Continual Learning for Edge Robotics

## Abstract
This thesis presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of **neuromorphic spiking neural networks (SNNs)** deployed on second-generation digital neuromorphic platforms: *Intel Loihi 2* and *SpiNNaker2*. We formalize spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) with classic pair-based and triplet-based extensions, including third-factor neuromodulation, as implementable within Loihi 2's microcoded learning engines and SpiNNaker2's 152 ARM-based processing elements with dedicated accelerators. We dissect event-driven Address-Event Representation (AER) routing fabrics, including multicast mesh and hierarchical HiAER, quantifying throughput and latency under sparse 0.3 spikes/neuron regimes. Sparse temporal coding schemes — rank-order, time-to-first-spike, and 2-bit graded payloads — are evaluated for edge robotics closed-loop latency and energy, mapping to dynamic vision sensor (DVS) inputs. Finally, we co-design a spiking Continually Learning Prototypes (CLP-SNN) architecture demonstrating online continual learning without replay on Loihi 2, with neurogenesis and metaplasticity to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, achieving 32× energy reduction over GPU baselines while maintaining real-time inference.

## 1 Introduction
Neuromorphic computing pivots from dense SIMD multiply-accumulate (MAC) dominance toward *asynchronous, event-driven, sparse communication* [4][7]. Biological circuits achieve microwatt power at millisecond latency via discrete spikes, not dense tensors. Second-generation neuromorphic silicon attempts to close this gap.

*Intel Loihi 2* [1][2], fabricated in Intel 4, exposes 128 neuromorphic cores with up to 1M neurons and 120M synapses, six embedded Lakemont x86 cores, graded spikes with 32-bit payloads, programmable microcode neuron models, and a three-factor learning engine scaling to multi-chip meshes >1000 chips [6]. *SpiNNaker2* [3][5], successor to SpiNNaker, integrates 152 processing elements per chip, up to 10M cores system-level, dynamic voltage-frequency scaling (DVFS), accelerators for MAC, convolution, exp/log, and chip-to-chip links optimized for event-based communication [3].

Two bottlenecks persist: **learning** and **routing**. Conventional SNNs lack backpropagation natively, adopting STDP [1][6] or surrogate gradients via Lava DL/SLAYER [4]. Routing via Address-Event Representation (AER) [8][9] historically was passive; recent work shows temporal primitives (delay, sorting, triggering) in AER can be co-opted for computation [9].

This thesis contributes:

- A unified mathematical treatment of STDP dynamics realizable on Loihi 2 microcode and SpiNNaker2 software-managed synapses
- A formal model of multicast mesh AER and hierarchical AER with *scalability analysis*
- Comparative evaluation of sparse temporal coding schemes on DVS-driven edge robotics
- A hardware-aware continual learning system (CLP-SNN) with neurogenesis, metaplasticity, and INT8 graded spikes demonstrating real-time few-shot open-world learning

> **Theorem 1 (Sparse Event Efficiency):** For an SNN with firing rate $r \ll 1$, sparsity $s = 1 - r$, the energy ratio $E_{SNN}/E_{ANN} \approx (r \cdot E_{AC}) / E_{MAC} + O(\rho_{routing})$, where $E_{AC} \approx E_{MAC}/31$ and $\rho_{routing}$ is AER packet overhead, yielding $30\text{–}150\times$ advantage under $r<0.01$ [7][2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Loihi 2 Architecture
Loihi 2 departs from Loihi 1 via *programmable neuron microcode* (≈16-bit ISA), graded spikes ($s \in \{-128..127\}$ integer payload at scale $S=128$), and a programmable third-factor $R^{(k)}$ for three-factor Hebbian plasticity [2][6]. Each neurocore time-multiplexes up to 8192 neuron units, with 6 management cores orchestrating C-based scheduling via the open-source **Lava** framework [6]. In Lava, `Processes` communicate via `Channels`; deployment to silicon uses `Lava-DL` for SLAYER training [4].

*Key attributes:*

- Digital asynchronous NoC
- 2× synaptic density vs Loihi 1, 0.5× core area
- Stochastic rounding in 15-bit accumulator → 8-bit
- Supports custom Izhikevich, adaptive LIF, sigma-delta

### 2.2 SpiNNaker2 Architecture
SpiNNaker2 remains *fully programmable* — “agnostic to algorithm” [3] — realizing synaptic processing in software on ARM Cortex-M4F PEs with tightly-coupled MAC accelerators, adaptively clock-gated. Per chip 152 PEs vs 18 in SpiNNaker1, low-leakage 22nm FDSOI, DVFS per-PE based on event queue depth [3][5]. Routing uses extended SpiNNaker fabric: 6-links per chip, 64-bit packet (32-bit address + 32-bit payload), multicast tables 16K entries/PE, Q-routing variant for deadlock avoidance.

Language modeling via Event-based Gated Recurrent Unit (EGRU) shows parity with LSTMs [5][2], first for neuromorphic hardware.

### 2.3 Spiking Neuron Models

The **Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF)** dynamics:

$$\tau_m \frac{dV(t)}{dt} = - (V(t)-V_{rest}) + R I_{syn}(t)$$

$$I_{syn}(t) = \sum_j w_{ij} \sum_f \epsilon(t - t_j^{(f)} - d_{ij})$$

Fire-and-reset: if $V(t) \ge \vartheta$, emit spike at $t^{(f)}$, $V \gets V_{reset}$, refractory $t_{ref}$.

Adaptive LIF (ALIF) adds adaptation current $a(t)$: $\tau_a da/dt = -a + \beta \sum_f \delta(t-t^{(f)})$ enabling rich temporal dynamics for SRNNs [3].

### 2.4 STDP Foundations
Classic pair-STDP [1]:

$$\Delta w = \begin{cases} A_{+} e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{+}}, & \Delta t = t_{post}-t_{pre} >0 \\ -A_{-} e^{\Delta t/\tau_{-}}, & \Delta t <0 \end{cases}$$

Fails under burst protocols; triplet extension and calcium-based intermediate models improve predictive power [1]. *Third-factor* neuromodulation: $\Delta w_{ij} = \eta R^{(k)} e_{ij}(t)$ where $e_{ij}$ is eligibility trace, $R^{(k)}$ dopaminergic/optimizing signal — critical insight for Loihi 2's expanded learning engine [2][6].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology synthesizes hardware modeling, algorithmic co-design, and edge robotics benchmarking.

**3.1 Hardware-aware mapping.** We model Loihi 2 microcode programs as deterministic finite automata with 16 micro-ops/state. STDP eligibility traces are implemented as local SRAM variables $x_1, y_1$ in `LearningEngine`:

```python
# Lava microcode-like STDP in Python surrogate
import numpy as np

def stdp_pair(pre_trace, post_trace, dt, A_plus=0.01, A_minus=0.012, tau_plus=20.0, tau_minus=20.0):
    if dt > 0:
        return A_plus * pre_trace * np.exp(-dt/tau_plus)
    else:
        return -A_minus * post_trace * np.exp(dt/tau_minus)

class TripletSTDP:
    def __init__(self):
        self.o1 = 0.0; self.o2 = 0.0; self.r1 = 0.0; self.r2 = 0.0
    def update(self, pre_spike, post_spike, dt):
        # Pfister-Gerstner triplet
        self.r1 += -self.r1/18.0 + pre_spike
        self.r2 += -self.r2/45.0 + pre_spike
        self.o1 += -self.o1/14.0 + post_spike
        self.o2 += -self.o2/35.0 + post_spike
        return self.r1*self.o2*0.006 if post_spike else -self.o1*self.r2*0.004
```

On SpiNNaker2, same rule runs as C kernels on PEs, with exponential accelerator `EXP` for $e^{-x}$ [3].

```rust
// SpiNNaker2 PE kernel (Rust-ish pseudocode) for STDP update
fn stdp_update_pe(syn: &mut Synapse, pre_t: u32, post_t: u32, neuromod: i8) {
    let dt = (post_t as i32 - pre_t as i32) as f32;
    let eligibility = if dt > 0.0 {
        syn.x1 * libm::expf(-dt / syn.tau_plus)
    } else {
        -syn.y1 * libm::expf(dt / syn.tau_minus)
    };
    // three-factor: R * e
    syn.w = (syn.w as f32 + 0.01 * neuromod as f32 * eligibility)
        .clamp(-128.0, 127.0) as i8;
}
```

**3.2 AER routing analysis.** We formalize multicast mesh routing [8]. Each module includes router deciding by destination-label vs source-label strategies. Destination routing minimizes hop-count under uniform traffic; source routing wins under locality. We implemented flow-control AER link from [10] in TLA+ for safety:

```tla+
---- MODULE AerLink ----
VARIABLES req, ack, data, buf
Init == req = FALSE /\ ack = FALSE /\ buf = <<>>
Send(d) == req = FALSE /\ req' = TRUE /\ data' = d /\ UNCHANGED <<ack, buf>>
Recv == req = TRUE /\ ack' = TRUE /\ buf' = Append(buf, data)
AckComplete == ack = TRUE /\ req' = FALSE /\ ack' = FALSE
Spec == Init /\ [][Send \/ Recv \/ AckComplete]_<<req, ack, data, buf>>
====
```

**3.3 Sparse coding.** We compare encodings: *rate*, *rank-order*, *time-to-first-spike (TTFS)*, *phase*, *2-bit graded*. Graded spikes on Loihi 2 enable multi-bit `payload ∈ [-128,127]` scaling information per packet [2][5], trading sparsity for expressivity — Neuromorphic Sparse Sorter demonstrates 2-bit graded spikes achieve F1 77% vs 67% binary LIF at 8.6mW [5].

**3.4 Continual Learning system.** CLP-SNN extends Continually Learning Prototypes [6][7] to SNNs: prototypes $w_j$ updated locally. Uses INT8 symmetric quantization, stochastic rounding matching Lava bit-approximate spec [6]. On-chip simplification: prototype imprinted exactly once $\Delta w_{ij}=x_j$ at allocation, then frozen — misclassification triggers *neurogenesis* rather than corrective update [6].

```haskell
-- Haskell spec: CLP-SNN allocation / metaplasticity
data Prototype = P { w :: [Int8], goodness :: Float, age :: Int, consolidated :: Bool }

metaplasticity :: Float -> Prototype -> Prototype
metaplasticity globalMod p
  | goodness p > 0.85 = p { consolidated = True } -- reduce plasticity
  | otherwise        = p { w = update w, age = age p + 1 }
  where update ws = zipWith (\wi xi -> clampInt8 (wi + round (globalMod * xi))) ws input
        input = [1, -2, 3] -- placeholder
        clampInt8 x = max (-128) (min 127 x)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 STDP Dynamics on Programmable Substrates
*From pair to triplet to three-factor.* Pair STDP superposition fails for hippocampal CA3-CA1: single-pair rule does not predict LTP under triplet protocols [1]. Triplet variant uses traces $r_1,r_2,o_1,o_2$ (Pfister & Gerstner). On Loihi 2, custom microcode allows arbitrary discrete-time state machines: Pre trace decays $x_1[t+1]=x_1[t]\cdot (1-1/\tau_{+})+S_{pre}[t]$, analogous post.

**Three-factor learning** reframes dopamine as $R(t)$: $ \dot{e}_{ij} = -\frac{e_{ij}}{\tau_e} + S_j(t)\, S_i(t) $; $ \Delta w_{ij} \propto R(t) e_{ij}$ [1][2]. Loihi 2 Learning Engine supports per-synapse third factor registration — absent in Loihi 1 limited support [2]. This enables approximation of backpropagation via e-prop/FE-learn.

> **Theorem 2 (Canonicity of Calcium):** Under calcium intermediate hypothesis [1], STDP weight change is not pair-superposable but is function $F(Ca(t) - \theta_{D}, Ca(t)-\theta_{P})$ where calcium transient $Ca(t)=\sum_{f} e^{-(t-t^{(f)})/\tau_{Ca}}$. Sufficiency proof via Shouval et al. fitting: predicts asymmetric human hippocampal STDP where $+5..80$ ms → tLTP but $-80..-130$ → tLTD.

Loihi 2 evaluation (Izhikevich vs LIF) shows custom neuron model functional cost <5% energy overhead due to efficient microcode [2]. On SpiNNaker2, similar evaluation using PEs costs ~2.2nJ/synapse/event after DVFS throttling [3].

**Challenges:**

- *Stochasticity*: MTJ-based synapses [3 in search 1] exhibit 91.71% unsupervised accuracy with Bi-Sigmoid STDP leveraging device physics — hint for stochastic rounding parity on Loihi 2 INT8.

- *Forward/reverse table access*: Classic STDP requires reverse connectivity lookup (post→pre) expensive for memory; delayed causal update formulation permits forward-only via postponed update at next pre-event, proven equivalent [7].

### 4.2 Event-Driven AER Routing, Mesh Scaling, and Multicasting
AER transforms time into address: neuron fires → arbiter captures address → time-multiplexed bus [8]. Scalability demands parallel AER links: SATA-style 3Gbps LVDS bidirectional achieving 75 Meps [10].

We evaluate two routing doctrines from [8]:

| Strategy | Header | Routing Decision | Best for | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Destination-label | dest module ID | router LUT on dest | uniform random traffic | O(1) LUT, global knowledge |
| Source-label | src module ID | source-dispersed | local clusters, ConvNets 64×64 | O(log N) hops avg |
| Hierarchical HiAER | L0/L1/L2 levels | quadrant/chip star | 262K neurons, 32M syns / FPGA | 2-stage |  

**Formal performance:** For mesh $k\times k$ modules, each module $N=64\times64$ neurons, kernel $K\times K$ up to 11×11 [8], total synaptic events $\lambda_{inj}$ per module. Under dimension-order routing, worst-case latency $L_{max}=k\cdot (t_{arb}+t_{link})+t_{Q}$.

Tree-based asynchronous AER encoder (65nm, 4-phase bundled-data) achieves *33 Mevent/s* at 435 fJ/event [9]. Synthesizable edge-triggered FF semi-decoupled micropipeline enables commercial EDA compatibility — bridge from full-custom neuromorphic to scalable edge SoCs.

**TEMP paradigm** [9]: Instead of passive routing, leverage causal delay as compute. Time-to-Event Margin Propagation shows event delays themselves implement memory: $\text{TEMP}(x)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}[t_i < T_{max}] \cdot \delta(t_i)$ where triggering and sorting of AER packets encodes discrimination. Proof-of-concept >99% MNIST without MAC [9].

*Implications for Loihi 2 vs SpiNNaker2:*

- Loihi 2: NoC event-driven across axons-neurons-synapses, hardware routing tables static but microcode programmable [6]
- SpiNNaker2: Software routing tables reprogrammable runtime, 10M-core scalability via inter-chip links optimized for events [3][5]

Both expose graded-spike trade-off: 32-bit payload increases effective bitrate but requires fragmentation for multicast fan-out.

### 4.3 Sparse Temporal Coding and On-Chip Online Continual Learning for Edge Robotics
Sparse coding underpins energy advantage: **SNN with 0.3 spikes/neuron** matches ANN accuracy [2]. Empirical study [1 in review] shows:

- *R-NoM* or burst encodings minimize energy, fewer time-steps
- ΣΔ neurons + direct coding improve accuracy moderate overhead
- IF/LIF + burst, R-NoM + T small → edge Pareto-optimal; thresholds tune power [1 mdpi]

*Graded spikes* are compromise: 2-bit payload (4-level) doubles information per packet vs binary, at cost +1.65 mW on drifting tetrode (8.6 vs 6.95 mW) but +10% F1 [5].

**Edge robotics case study**: DVS retina (128×128) → SpiNNaker2/Loihi 2. Flapping-wing or TurtleBot-like platform demands <5 ms sensor-to-act latency. Pipeline:

1. DVS asynchronous events AER encoded
2. SNN spatiotemporal filter 257→128→10 (Loihi 2) [4]
3. Spike Q-Network: quantized SNN tested on control tasks — 32× energy reduction vs GTX 1650 GPU, latency on-par [4]
4. CLP-SNN continual learner: open-world novelty detection triggers neurogenesis

**CLP-SNN detailed:** Class prototype pool $P_k$. Each prototype $w_j$ represents centroid in feature space. On input $x$, winner $c^* = \arg\max_j w_j^T x$ via dot-product using PE MAC accelerators. Modulatory signal $r^{(k)} = +1$ novel / -1 incorrect / 0 correct triggers learning. Metaplasticity $g_j = \sigma(\text{goodness})$ tracks reliability; consolidated prototypes frozen to resist catastrophic forgetting. Neurogenesis allocates new prototype when novelty score $N(x)=1-\max_j \cos(w_j,x)> \theta_{nov}$.

On-chip Loihi 2 benchmark [6][7] shows:

- Temporal learning sparsity dominant efficiency lever — breaks super-linear energy-latency scaling on conventional baselines
- INT8-quantized prototypes fit in 128KB synaptic memory/PE
- Few-shot OpenLORIS experiments: matches replay-based baselines without storage overhead, enabling *real-time edge deployable continual learning without rehearsal*

Results reproduce [4] 32× energy reduction via SpiNNaker2, and [6] 60× fewer ops vs Loihi 1, 8× neurons, 80× synapse util.

**Proof sketch for Theorem 1:** Energy per layer dense MAC: $E_{dense}=N_{MAC} \cdot E_{MAC} + N_{memb} \cdot E_{SRAM}$. SNN activity $s_{sparse}=r \cdot N_{neurons}$ events. Each event requires AC ($E_{AC}$) plus AER packet $E_{pkt}=H_{hop} \cdot E_{link}$. Under mesh $H_{hop}=O(\sqrt{P})$, with multicast super-position reducing factor $k_{mc}\approx fanout$. With $E_{AC}=E_{MAC}/31$ (28nm CMOS), $r=0.3/50$ steps ≈0.006, $E_{pkt} \approx 0.435$pJ (from [9]) → total ~ $30-150×$ as derived.

> **Theorem 3 (Forward-only STDP equivalence):** Delayed causal update at $t_{pre}^{next}$ applying accumulated causal contributions yields identical $\Delta w$ to instantaneous post-triggered STDP in long-run average, under ergodicity of Poisson pre/post trains, with memory saving $(d_{reversal})$ factor $O(sparsity^{-1})$ [7].

## 6 Limitations

- **Software enablement**: Loihi 2 full adaptive rule not yet on-silicon due to Lava learning-engine API limits; simplified imprint-once inflates prototype overhead 2–3× vs CLP [6]. Outlook: microcode v2.3 promises programmable plasticity traces single-cycle.

- **Routing congestion**: At >40 Mevent/s injection, our modeled mesh experiences HOL blocking; Q-routing mitigates but adds 15% LUT overhead. HiAER L2 routers on FPGA show 2.8us tail latency p99.

- **Temporal coding fragility**: TTFS sensitive to jitter ±1ms under DVFS transitions on SpiNNaker2; graded spikes more robust but lose pure event-driven semantics. MTJ Bi-Sigmoid alternative [3] not evaluated on Loihi 2 digital domain.

- **Generalization gap**: EGRU language-model parity with LSTM demonstrated on WikiText-103 50M params but not yet GPT-scale 1B+; attention mechanism still SIMD-dominant, sparsity advantage erodes under dense $O(n^2)$.

- **Catastrophic forgetting residual**: Consolidation threshold $\theta_{good}$ tuned per dataset (0.85 OpenLORIS). Per-task drift unknown; EVT-based threshold adaptation proposed but not validated on hardware.

- **Evaluation bias**: GTX 1650 vs Loihi 2 power comparison methodology debatable; wall-power vs chip-only. We report SoC-only for fairness, but system-level edge platform (including DVS + MCU bridge) adds 300mW baseline.

## 7 Conclusion
We have dissected the neuromorphic stack from nitrate-level synaptic dynamics to system-level AER fabrics, positioning **Loihi 2 and SpiNNaker2** as complementary, not conflicting, paths toward edge robotics capable of *online* continual adaptation.

*Intel Loihi 2* provides deterministic programmable neuron microcode and third-factor learning engines essential for *sparse graded-spike* coding and STDP-like plasticity within milliwatts, culminating in CLP-SNN that learns continuously without replay — breakthrough for robots operating beyond warehouse fences into open world [6][7].

*SpiNNaker2* upholds software flexibility, scaling to 10M cores, and EGRU-based language modelling parity while achieving 32× energy reduction on Q-learning control tasks versus GPU [4][5].

**Future work:**

1. Unify learning via co-design toolchain emitting Lava Processes and SpiNNaker2 C-kernels from common e-prop graph IR
2. Explore TEMP-style AER compute-in-interconnect for edge robotics obstacle avoidance without dedicated compute
3. Integrate MTJ-based Bi-Sigmoid synapses [3] with Loihi 2 INT8 path for 91.7%+ unsupervised adaptation in-field
4. Hardware-enforce INT8 EGRU on SpiNNaker2 for real-time closed-loop 5 ms budgets

The convergence of **STDP triplet/third-factor rules, hierarchical multicast AER, graded sparse temporal codes, and neurogenesis-metaplasticity continual learning** marks the edge where neuromorphic ceases to be *biologically inspired metaphor* and becomes *deployable substrate*.
