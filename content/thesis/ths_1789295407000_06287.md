# Computational Protein Design with Diffusion Models: RFdiffusion Backbone Generation, ProteinMPNN Sequence Design, and All-Atom Energy Validation

## Abstract

Generative diffusion models have transformed *de novo* protein design from an energy-landscape optimization problem into a learned denoising process over the SE(3) manifold of backbone frames. This thesis develops the complete mathematical and engineering stack of the modern diffusion-based protein design pipeline: (i) the forward and reverse stochastic processes that corrupt and regenerate protein backbone frames $F_i = (R_i, \mathbf{t}_i) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$, with IGSO3 handling rotational diffusion; (ii) RFdiffusion [1], which fine-tunes the RoseTTAFold structure prediction network as the denoising backbone with self-conditioning; (iii) ProteinMPNN [2], the order-agnostic graph neural network that designs sequences onto generated backbones with 52.4% native-sequence recovery; and (iv) the all-atom extensions (RFdiffusionAA, LigandMPNN) and the orthogonal validation layer (AlphaFold2 self-consistency, Rosetta ref2015 energy funnels) that convert *in silico* candidates into experimentally validated binders, enzymes, and symmetric assemblies. We derive the variational objective, analyze equivariance, quantify conditioning mechanisms, and discuss fundamental limitations: PDB-manifold bias, the designability–novelty trade-off, and the absence of thermodynamic guarantees.

## 1 Intro

Protein design is the inverse problem of protein folding: rather than mapping sequence to structure, the designer specifies a desired geometry, topology, or function and searches for a sequence that realizes it. For decades, the dominant paradigm was physics-based: Rosetta's Monte Carlo fragment assembly guided by the ref2015 energy function [6] produced landmark *de novo* folds, enzymes, and nanomaterials — but suffered from an imperfect energy approximation and local-optimizer search easily trapped on rugged landscapes.

The deep-learning era reframed the problem. Structure prediction networks (AlphaFold2 [5], RoseTTAFold) demonstrated that sequence–structure relationships could be learned end-to-end with atomic accuracy, providing a *differentiable* oracle for the folding map. Early inversion methods such as **hallucination** [9] performed gradient ascent on sequence space to maximize network confidence, but their sequences were frequently poorly expressed and insoluble — the adversarial-example pathology of activation maximization [11].

Diffusion models resolve this tension by learning the *distribution* of protein structures directly. Instead of optimizing a single structure against an oracle, a diffusion model learns $p(\mathbf{x}_0)$, the density of physically realizable backbones, and samples from it — conditionally, when design specifications are imposed. RFdiffusion [1] operationalizes this by fine-tuning RoseTTAFold on a structure-denoising objective, converting it from predictor into generator. The empirical results are unprecedented: hundreds of experimentally characterized symmetric assemblies, metal-binding proteins, and binders, with a designed influenza-haemagglutinin binder confirmed by cryo-EM to be nearly identical to the design model.

This thesis presents the full pipeline: the SE(3) geometry and score-based generative background; RFdiffusion's forward/reverse processes, ProteinMPNN's decoding, and validation machinery; a deep dive into self-conditioning, all-atom extensions, conditioning, and self-consistency theory; empirical results; and the limitations defining the field's frontier.

> **Theorem:** (Informal designability guarantee). Under the RFdiffusion reverse process with self-conditioning, samples $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ concentrate on the learned manifold $\mathcal{M}$ of designable backbones; for any backbone $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{M}$, ProteinMPNN's expected native-sequence recovery lower-bounds the probability that an AlphaFold2 self-consistency screen passes, establishing the pipeline's end-to-end *in silico* validity.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The protein design pipeline: backbone, sequence, validation

Computational protein design classically decomposes into three stages, preserved in the diffusion era with new machinery per stage:

1. **Backbone generation.** Produce residue frames $F_i = (R_i, \mathbf{t}_i)$ satisfying geometric desiderata (topology, symmetry, interfaces, motifs).
2. **Sequence design.** Fixed-backbone design: find an amino-acid sequence folding into the backbone, a combinatorial problem over $20^N$ sequences.
3. **Validation.** Assess realizability via energy evaluation, structure prediction of the designed sequence, and experimental characterization.

The diffusion pipeline instantiates these stages as **RFdiffusion** (backbone generation) → **ProteinMPNN** (sequence design) → **AlphaFold2 + Rosetta** (validation) [1, 2, 5, 6].

### 2.2 Diffusion models and score matching

Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) define a forward Markov chain that progressively corrupts data $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim q(\mathbf{x}_0)$:

$$q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I}), \qquad t = 1, \dots, T$$

with a variance schedule $\{\beta_t\}$ chosen so that $\mathbf{x}_T$ is approximately isotropic Gaussian.

$$p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid \mathbf{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t), \sigma_t^2 \mathbf{I})$$

Training minimizes a variational bound on the negative log-likelihood, which reduces to a denoising score-matching objective:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[ \left\lVert \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) \right\rVert^2 \right]$$

so the network learns the *score* $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} \log q(\mathbf{x}_t)$; sampling the reverse chain from $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ with conditioning yields $p(\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{c})$.

### 2.3 SE(3) geometry of protein backbones

Proteins live not in $\mathbb{R}^{3N}$ but on a manifold with rigid-body structure.

- **Translations** $\mathbf{t}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: standard Gaussian diffusion, $q(\mathbf{t}_t \mid \mathbf{t}_0) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{t}_0, \sigma_t^2 \mathbf{I})$.
- **Rotations** $R_i \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$: Brownian motion on the rotation group, with transition density given by the **isotropic Gaussian on SO(3)** (IGSO3) [7, 8]:

$$q(R_t \mid R_0) = \mathrm{IGSO3}(R_0 \exp(\mathbf{w}\,\sigma_t)), \qquad \mathbf{w} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(\mathfrak{so}(3))$$

The backbone distribution is invariant under global rigid transformations, so the denoising network must be **SE(3)-equivariant** or use invariant features.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 RFdiffusion: fine-tuning a structure predictor into a generator

The central methodological insight of RFdiffusion [1] is *transfer learning across tasks*: RoseTTAFold, trained to map sequence to structure, already encodes a powerful prior over protein geometry.

- **Input change.** In RoseTTAFold, the primary input is the amino-acid sequence (1D track) plus templates/MSA. In RFdiffusion, the primary input is the *diffused residue frames* $(\mathbf{x}_t)$ — noisy coordinates and orientations — with the sequence track replaced by a mask or placeholder.
- **Output.** The network predicts the clean structure $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ (final 3D coordinates) from $\mathbf{x}_t$, rather than predicting structure from sequence.
- **Self-conditioning.** At each timestep $t$, the model receives not only $\mathbf{x}_t$ but also its own previous prediction $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^{t+1}$ as a template input (analogous to recycling in AlphaFold). The next iterate is a noisy interpolation toward $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^t$, markedly improving sample quality.

**Training objective.** The loss combines frame-aligned point error on the predicted $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ with distogram and orientation auxiliary losses inherited from RoseTTAFold:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{RFdiff}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0}\left[ \mathrm{FAPE}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0(\mathbf{x}_t, t), \mathbf{x}_0) + \lambda_{\text{dist}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} + \lambda_{\text{ori}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{ori}} \right]$$

Notably, the authors found **mean-squared error** crucial [1]: because MSE is *not* invariant to the global reference frame, it promotes continuity between successive timesteps, stabilizing the reverse trajectory.

```python
# Pseudocode: RFdiffusion reverse sampling with self-conditioning
def rfdiffusion_sample(model, n_res, T=200, sigma_schedule):
    # Initialize from noise: random frames
    x_t = sample_random_frames(n_res)          # R ~ Uniform(SO3), t ~ N(0, I)
    x0_prev = zeros_like(x_t)                  # self-conditioning template
    for t in reversed(range(1, T + 1)):
        x0_hat = model(x_t, t, template=x0_prev)   # predict clean structure
        x0_prev = x0_hat.detach()                  # self-condition for next step
        # noisy interpolation toward predicted x0
        x_t = interp(x_t, x0_hat, alpha(t)) + sigma_schedule[t] * randn_like(x_t)
    return x0_hat
```

### 3.2 Conditioning mechanisms: from noise to specification

RFdiffusion handles diverse tasks via sampling-time conditioning [1]:

| Design task | Conditioning mechanism | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional monomer | None; pure noise input | Novel topologies, high designability |
| Topology-constrained | Secondary-structure / block-contact specification | Specified fold classes realized |
| Binder design | Target structure fixed; binder initialized as noise | Cryo-EM-validated HA binder |
| Symmetric oligomers | Symmetry operations enforced on frames | C6 rings, icosahedral assemblies |
| Enzyme active-site scaffolding | Motif frames fixed; rest diffused (inpainting) | Catalytic geometries scaffolded |
| Motif scaffolding (symmetric) | Symmetric motif copies fixed | Metal-binding sites designed |

Motif scaffolding is **inpainting on the SE(3) manifold**: at each reverse step, motif frames are replaced with noised ground-truth values while the scaffold is freely generated.

### 3.3 ProteinMPNN: order-agnostic sequence design

ProteinMPNN [2] solves fixed-backbone sequence design with an MPNN over the residue graph:

- **Graph construction.** Nodes are residues; edges connect the $k=48$ nearest neighbors by Cα distance. Node features encode backbone dihedrals and local geometry; edge features encode relative orientations, distances (via radial basis functions), and sequence separation.
- **Order-agnostic autoregressive decoder.** The decoder predicts amino acids one position at a time, but in a **random decoding order** sampled per training example:

$$p(\mathbf{s} \mid \mathbf{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^{N} p(s_{\pi_i} \mid \mathbf{x}, s_{\pi_1}, \dots, s_{\pi_{i-1}}), \qquad \pi \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(S_N)$$

Random-permutation training makes the model robust to arbitrary decoding orders and enables **tied decoding** for symmetric oligomers.

**Performance.** ProteinMPNN achieves **52.4% native-sequence recovery** (vs. 32.9% for Rosetta), with particular gains on surface residues. Experimentally, ProteinMPNN-redesigned sequences rescued designs that failed with Rosetta sequences: in the RFdiffusion binder campaigns, switching to ProteinMPNN was decisive for the high success rates [1, 2].

```haskell
-- Conceptual: order-agnostic decoding as a permutation-equivariant fold
decodeOrderAgnostic :: Backbone -> [Residue] -> IO Sequence
decodeOrderAgnostic bb residues = do
  pi <- randomPermutation residues          -- sample decoding order
  foldM step emptySeq pi
  where
    step partial r =
      let ctx = encodeContext bb partial    -- MPNN encoder over fixed backbone + decoded prefix
      in sampleFrom (decoderDist ctx r) >>= \aa -> return (assign partial r aa)
```

### 3.4 The validation layer: AlphaFold2 self-consistency and Rosetta energies

A designed backbone–sequence pair $(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{s})$ is validated *in silico* before experimental testing, via two orthogonal screens:

1. **AlphaFold2 self-consistency (scTM / scRMSD).** Predict the structure of the designed sequence with AlphaFold2 in single-sequence mode and compare to the design backbone: $\mathrm{scTM} = \mathrm{TM\text{-}score}(\mathrm{AF2}(\mathbf{s}), \mathbf{b})$, passing if $\mathrm{scTM} > 0.8$ (monomers). High scTM means the sequence *encodes* the design per an independent oracle — the operational definition of **designability** (with pLDDT > 80, pAE < 5 Å).

2. **Rosetta energy evaluation.** Score with the ref2015 all-atom energy function [6] and check for a **folding funnel**: under *ab initio* relaxation from perturbed conformations, energies should decrease monotonically with RMSD to the design. A deep, narrow funnel indicates a large energy gap — the thermodynamic signature of foldability.

```rust
// Pseudocode: in-silico validation funnel
fn validate_design(backbone: &Backbone, seq: &Sequence) -> ValidationReport {
    let af2 = alphafold_predict(seq, msa_mode::SingleSequence);
    let sctm = tm_score(&af2.coords, &backbone.coords);
    let plddt = af2.mean_plddt();
    // Rosetta relax ensemble -> energy vs RMSD funnel
    let decoys: Vec<Decoy> = (0..50).map(|_| rosetta_relax(seq, &backbone)).collect();
    let funnel = funnel_depth(&decoys); // E gap between native-like and non-native
    ValidationReport { sctm, plddt, funnel,
        pass: sctm > 0.8 && plddt > 80.0 && funnel.is_deep() }
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Self-conditioning and the geometry of the reverse trajectory

Self-conditioning — feeding $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^{t+1}$ back as a template — changes the effective sampling dynamics.

> **Theorem:** (Self-conditioning as posterior-mean iteration). With $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^t = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{x}_t, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^{t+1}]$, the self-conditioned chain is a Rao-Blackwellized posterior-mean estimator, reducing per-step variance by $I(\mathbf{x}_0; \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0^{t+1} \mid \mathbf{x}_t) \geq 0$.

The MSE-loss observation [1] complements this: a frame-invariant loss alone permits the network to "teleport" the structure between timesteps — rotating the entire prediction — because such moves are invisible to the loss.

### 4.2 Why order-agnostic decoding matters for design

Classical autoregressive protein models decode N→C terminally, imposing an arbitrary causal structure: position $i$ cannot condition on position $j > i$, even when $j$ is spatially adjacent and energetically coupled (a disulfide pair, a salt bridge).

### 4.3 All-atom extensions: RFdiffusionAA and LigandMPNN

Backbone-only diffusion cannot design *functions* depending on side chains or non-protein atoms: small-molecule pockets, catalytic sites, protein–ligand interfaces.

### 4.4 The theory of self-consistency metrics

Why should $\mathrm{scTM} > 0.8$ predict experimental success?

1. **Independence of errors.** RFdiffusion and AlphaFold2 share architectural ancestry but were trained on different objectives (denoising vs. MSA-based prediction). Agreement between differently-trained oracles is stronger evidence than either alone.
2. **Single-sequence mode.** AF2 is run without MSAs for designed sequences, forcing reliance on learned physical priors rather than coevolutionary memorization. Passing means the sequence is *intrinsically* foldable.
3. **Empirical calibration.** Across RFdiffusion campaigns, *in silico* success correlated strongly with *in vitro* success (expression, monodispersity, binding) [1].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental validation results

Experimentally [1], at unprecedented scale:

- **Symmetric oligomers.** Hundreds of designed cyclic, dihedral, and higher-order assemblies were expressed; a large fraction formed the intended oligomeric state (SEC, negative-stain EM).
- **Protein binders.** Binders against multiple targets (influenza haemagglutinin, *B. anthracis* protective antigen) were validated by yeast display and biolayer interferometry; the cryo-EM structure of a designed HA binder was **nearly identical to the design model** [1].
- **Motif scaffolding.** Enzyme active sites and metal-binding motifs were scaffolded into *de novo* backbones with motif RMSD < 1 Å.

Success rates of ~20% from just 96 expressed proteins per task [3] are an order-of-magnitude improvement over prior campaigns requiring thousands of designs per success.

### 5.3 Formal argument: why the variational objective yields designable samples

We sketch why minimizing the denoising objective produces samples on the *designable* manifold rather than merely the *PDB-like* manifold.

> **Theorem:** (Designability via denoising). Let $q(\mathbf{x}_0)$ be the distribution of experimentally realized protein backbones (the PDB manifold $\mathcal{M}$), and let $p_\theta$ be trained to optimality on the denoising objective. Then samples $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \sim p_\theta$ satisfy $\mathrm{dist}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0, \mathcal{M}) \to 0$ in the large-data, large-capacity limit, and — because every $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{M}$ is by construction realized by some sequence — there exists a sequence distribution (which ProteinMPNN approximates) placing mass on folders of $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$.

*Proof sketch.* (1) The denoising objective bounds $\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0)$; at optimality $p_\theta = q$ and samples concentrate on $\mathcal{M}$. (2) Self-conditioning (§4.1) projects iterates toward high-density regions, suppressing off-manifold drift. (3) For $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \in \mathcal{M}$, designability follows from $\mathcal{M}$'s definition as realized structures; ProteinMPNN's 52.4% recovery [2] shows $p(\mathbf{s} \mid \mathbf{x})$ is learnable. The theory–practice gap is finite-network approximation error, measured empirically by scTM. ∎

The argument's weak point — which §6 addresses — is step (1): $q$ is the distribution of *crystallizable, deposited* proteins, not of all physically realizable ones.

```tla+
---- MODULE ProteinDesignPipeline ----

Init == backbone \in BackboneSpace /\ sequence = "none" /\ validated = FALSE
Generate(b) == backbone' = b /\ UNCHANGED <<sequence, validated>>

    /\ validated' = (scTM(backbone, sequence) > 0.8 /\ funnelDeep(backbone, sequence))
    /\ UNCHANGED <<backbone, sequence>>
Next == \E b \in BackboneSpace : Generate(b)
     \/ \E s \in SeqSpace : DesignSeq(s)
     \/ Validate
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<backbone, sequence, validated>>
THEOREM ValidatedImpliesDesignable == Spec => [](validated => designable(backbone, sequence))
====
```

## 6 Limitations

1. **PDB bias.** RFdiffusion learns $q(\mathbf{x}_0)$ from deposited structures — soluble, crystallizable proteins. Disordered regions, membrane proteins, and transient conformations are underrepresented; novel folds remain rare and designs inherit PDB statistics. A *data* limitation bounding the reachable design space.

2. **Proxy leakage.** The pipeline optimizes scTM (designability), Rosetta energy (thermodynamics), display binding (function) — each proxy leaks. AF2 self-consistency favors AF2's comfort zone; ref2015 omits explicit water and polarization; *in vitro* binding need not imply *in vivo* efficacy or developability. The ~20% hit rate [3] still means 4 of 5 designs fail.

3. **No thermodynamic guarantees.** Nothing in the diffusion objective bounds $\Delta G$ or the misfolding energy gap. The Rosetta funnel is a post-hoc screen, not a design constraint.

4. **Conditioning brittleness.** Exact symmetry or motif constraints need careful noise schedules; over-constraining collapses diversity, under-constraining violates constraints. Flat or highly charged interfaces remain hard targets.

5. **Compute gating.** Training needs hundreds of GPU-days; the all-atom, multistate frontier stays compute-gated despite open-sourced inference code [1].

6. **Biosecurity dual-use.** The same pipeline designs binders against arbitrary targets; open-source models create a governance challenge with no technical fix.

## 7 Conclusion

The diffusion-based protein design pipeline — RFdiffusion for SE(3) backbone generation, ProteinMPNN for order-agnostic sequence design, AlphaFold2 self-consistency and Rosetta energy funnels for validation, and all-atom extensions for functional design — represents the most successful paradigm shift in computational protein design since the introduction of fragment assembly. Its power derives from a clean factorization: learn the distribution of realizable structures with a denoising objective on the correct manifold, then solve the inverse sequence problem with a graph network trained on the true conditional, then filter with independent oracles.

The quantitative leap — from single-digit experimental success rates to ~20% per 96-well plate, from months of expert Rosetta modeling to minutes of sampling — has already produced cryo-EM-validated binders, novel symmetric nanomaterials, and small-molecule binders with designed pockets. The remaining challenges are equally clear: escaping PDB bias, closing the expressibility gap, embedding thermodynamic guarantees into the generative objective itself, and governing dual-use capabilities.

The most promising direction is unifying generation and validation: diffusion models whose reverse process is directly guided by energy-based or AF2-based critics (as RFdiffusionAA begins to do with ligand conditioning [4]), and sequence-space diffusion (e.g., EvoDiff-style models [3]) that co-design sequence and structure in a single stochastic process. When the sampler itself optimizes designability, the pipeline's current three-stage factorization may collapse into one — and the distinction between *generating* a protein and *proving* it will fold may finally disappear.
