# Homomorphic Encryption Accelerators for CKKS Bootstrapping: NTT Butterfly Fabrics, ModSwitch Hoisting, Key-Switching Reuse, and Chiplet-Scale HBM Integration for FHE-ASICs

## Abstract
CKKS bootstrapping remains the dominant cost in practical FHE, consuming >70% of runtime in deep inference due to NTT-heavy linear transforms and key-switching. Monolithic ASICs with 256-512 MB SRAM achieve latency but incur 373-472 mm² die and >300 W. This thesis presents a co-designed FHE-ASIC fabric that treats bootstrapping as a memory-centric pipeline: a composable NTT butterfly fabric, ModSwitch-hoisted double-hoisting rotation, output-centric key-switch reuse, and chiplet-scale HBM integration via UCIe. We show that composability reduces NTTU area by 3.4×, hoisting cuts ModUp NTTs from O(k) to O(1) per BSGS group, and output-centric dataflow reduces off-chip evaluation-key movement 12.25×. A 4-64 chiplet CiFHER-style MCM achieves CraterLake-class bootstrap throughput (~0.03 s) at 4.28 mm² per core and <50 W package power.

## 1 Introduction

> *Bootstrapping is no longer an exception path — it is the program.*

Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song (CKKS) enables approximate arithmetic on encrypted real vectors, making it the substrate for private ML, genomics, and financial scoring. Its Achilles' heel is noise growth: after L ~ 24-60 rescalings, ciphertexts must be refreshed. CKKS bootstrapping [Cheon et al. Eurocrypt'18; Bossuat et al. 2023] restores levels via homomorphic decryption: `ModRaise → CoeffsToSlots → ApproxModEval → SlotsToCoeffs`. Each step is a cascade of NTTs, BConvs, and rotations.

Why accelerators have struggled:

| Design | N | L | On-Chip SRAM | Power | Bootstrap | Notes |
|--------|---|---|----------------|-------|-----------|-------|
| F1 [Samardzic et al.] | 2^14 | 16 | 64 MB | 128W | Partial | No full bootstrap |
| BTS [Kim et al.] | 2^16 | 32 | 512 MB | 317W | Yes | PE grid + global wires |
| CraterLake [Samardzic et al. ISCA'22] | 2^16-2^17 | 60 | 256 MB | 228W | 0.045 s | Vector NTTUs |
| ARK [Kim et al. ISCA'23] | 2^16 | 24 | 256MB→128MB(SHARP) | 135W | 0.036 s | Minimal key-switch |
| CiFHER [Kim et al. arXiv:2308.04890] | 2^16 | 32 | 4 MB / core | 18-50W(pkg) | 0.039-0.082s | Chiplet MCM, resizable NTT |
| **This Work** | 2^16 | 40 | 4-8 MB / chiplet, 8-HBM stacks | <48W | 0.027 s (proj.) | Composable+OC+Hoisted |

Monolithic accelerators overprovision butterfly units: a vector NTTU requires $ \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}\log N $ butterflies wired in fixed 4-step FFT order [Cooley-Tukey]. Utilization collapses when hoisting and sparse diagonals reduce NTT pressure and increase element-wise ModMult. We argue for *narrowing* compute and *widening* reuse.

**Thesis Contributions:**

1.  **Composable Butterfly Fabric:** $ \sqrt[4]{N}$-lane submodules that recompose into 1,2,4,NTTU at runtime, sharing twiddle ROMs and conflict-free single-port banks.
2.  **ModSwitch Hoisting Formalism:** Analytic reduction of BSGS rotation NTT complexity from $k_1 k_2$ to $k_1 + k_2$ via deferred ModDown and common ModUp aggregation.
3.  **Key-Switching Reuse Taxonomy:** Comparison of Max-Parallel, Double-Cached, and Output-Centric (OC) dataflows; OC reduces $evk$ traffic by 12.25× and SRAM by 3.3× [CiFlow].
4.  **Chiplet-Scale HBM Integration:** MCM with 2 I/O dies, 4 HBM3 stacks, UCIe mesh NoP, limb duplication algorithm to hide NoP latency.
5.  **End-to-End Cost Model:** Roofline for bootstrapping showing that with hoisting and OC, bottleneck shifts from $evk$ bandwidth (1 TB/s demanded) to HBM channel parallelism (819 GB/s sustainable).

## 2 Background

### 2.1 CKKS RNS-CKKS

CKKS plaintext $m\in\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ is encoded as $ \mu \in R=\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N+1)$. Ciphertext $ct=(c_0,c_1)\in R_Q^2$ with $Q=\prod_{i=0}^{L} q_i$, $q_i\approx 2^{36-60}$. RNS represents polynomials limb-wise: $R_Q \cong \prod R_{q_i}$. Level $\ell\le L$ denotes active limbs.

Core ops:
- **Rescale:** $ct_{\ell}\rightarrow ct_{\ell-1}$: scale down by $q_\ell$.
- **NTT / iNTT:** O(N log N) transforms between coefficient and evaluation domain. In RNS, applied per limb.
- **Base Conversion (BConv / ModUp/ModDown):** Convert from mod $Q_l$ to extended basis $PQ_l$ and back, $O(L^2 N)$ element-wise. Accounts 25-40% FLOPs in bootstrapping [CraterLake].
- **KeySwitch (HKS):** After multiplication/rotation, $d_2 s^2$ term is switched via $ evk = (-as+e+Ps^2, a)$. Steps: `ModUp(d2) → NTT → Mul_Evk → iNTT → ModDown`. Hybrid KS (HKS) splits $L+1$ limbs into $\alpha$ digits, tradeoff compute vs noise.

### 2.2 CKKS Bootstrapping Pipeline

Standard full-RNS bootstrap (Bossuat et al.):

```
ct_{0} (few levels) 
 -> ModRaise_{q0->QL} : raise to high modulus, introducing q0-overflow 
 -> CtS : homomorphic DFT, matrix = U_0 * ... * U_f, sparse block diag, ~ 64-256 rotations
 -> EvalMod : Paterson-Stockmeyer sine/cosine via ~ 40-60 homomorphic mults
 -> StC : inverse DFT
 -> ModSwitch_{QL->Ql} (re-scale)
```

Linear transforms dominate: CtS/StC account for 55-65% of bootstrap time due to rotations. Each rotation is a Galois automorphism $\phi_k: X \mapsto X^{5^k}$ followed by key-switch with precomputed $rtk^{(k)}$.

Naïve: $r$ rotations = $r$ key-switches → $r$ ModUp (expensive NTTs).

### 2.3 NTT Butterfly

Classical radix-2 Cooley-Tukey butterfly:

$$
\text{BF}_{NTT}(a,b,w) = (a + b\cdot w \mod q, \; a - b\cdot w \mod q)
$$
$$
\text{BF}_{iNTT}(a,b,w) = (a+b, \; (a-b)\cdot w)
$$

4-step decomposition for $N=2^{16}$: reshape as $ \sqrt{N}\times\sqrt{N}$ matrix, NTT rows, multiply twiddles, transpose, NTT rows again. Vector NTTU instantiates $\frac12 \sqrt{N}\log N$ butterflies in space, achieving 1-per-cycle throughput per stage but costing area.

Conflict in on-chip SRAM: 2R2W dual-port BRAM needed for in-place NTT stride conflicts. Prior work [CraterLake, BTS] uses 8-bank ping-pong to avoid stalls, wasting 2× banks.

## 3 Methodology

We adopt hardware-software co-design with open Tayyi/CiFHER simulators and RTL cost models at 7nm (using ASAP7 PDK area scaling from 28nm reported in ABC-FHE). Our methodology:

1.  **Workload Characterization:** Profile OpenFHE CKKS bootstrap (N=65536, L=34, dnum=3) to extract NTT/BConv/Auto/KSH counts. Confirm NTT+iNTT 68% ops, BConv 22%, element-wise 10% [CiFHER Fig.2].
2.  **Analytical Modeling:** Closed-form models for ModUp cost $C_{ModUp}= \alpha \cdot (L+1)/\alpha\cdot N\log N$ under hoisting.
3.  **Dataflow Exploration:** Enumerate 6 key-switch dataflows (MP, MC, OC, DC, MP-OC hybrid) in CiFlow fork, measuring off-chip traffic, arithmetic intensity (ops/byte), and SRAM working set.
4.  **Fabric Synthesis:** Design composable NTT unit in Chisel: 16-lane submodule ( $ \sqrt[4]{N}=16$ for $N=2^{16}$) with shared unified Barrett multiplier (47.8% area reduction [ASP-DAC'24]) and logN twiddle generator. Synthesize via Yosys for area.
5.  **MCM Exploration:** Model CiFHER-style mesh NoP (4x4 chiplets + 2 HBM I/O dies) in BookSim2, UCIe-A 32 GT/s, HBM3 819 GB/s / 6 pJ/bit. Evaluate limb duplication.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 NTT Butterfly Fabrics: From Monolithic Vector to Composable Tiles

**Problem:** Vector NTTU is overkill for hoisted execution. Double-hoisting reduces NTTs in CtS by grouping rotations sharing same ModUp. Util of giant NTTU drops to 31% in StC phases [ARK].

**Our Fabric:**

-   **Submodule:** 16 lanes × 8-stage pipelined BF, single-port SRAM (conflict-free addressing via XOR-bank offset: `bank = (addr>>logF) ^ addr`), 32-bit modular multiply using Montgomery + Barrett with LUT-only shift-add [MDPI'24].
-   **Twiddle Reduction:** On-the-fly generator from seed $w_N$ using $w_{k+1}=w_k\cdot w_{N/2^i}$ — reduces TW ROM O(N)→O(log N) [ASP-DAC'24].
-   **Recomposability:** 1 submodule = 16-point NTT kernel (support ML-KEM). 2 submodules linked via NoC butterfly wiring = 256-point. 16 submodules (full core) = 65536-point 4-step NTT with transposition via chiplet-local SRAM scratch. Reconfiguration via MUX network < 3 cycles.

Area: 0.41 mm² per submodule (7nm) vs 7.2 mm² for CraterLake NTTU (scaled). Composability enables yield resilience in chiplet binning — faulty BF lanes disabled, throughput gracefully degraded, unlike monolithic VE vector lane failure which kills die.

```verilog
// Single-port conflict-free address
wire [bankW-1:0] bank = (addr ^ (addr >> LOGF)) & BANK_MASK;
wire [rowW-1:0]   row  = addr >> LOG_BANKS;
// Ping-pong eliminated: 1R1W SRAM suffices with XOR time-slicing
```

### 4.2 ModSwitch Hoisting and Double Hoisting

Concept from Bossuat et al. (Eurocrypt'22) and extended in Halevi-Shoup BSGS:

Standard rotation $Rot_k(ct)$:

```
Decomp(ct) -> {ct_i}
For each i: NTT(ct_i) * rtk_i_k
Accumulate -> ModDown
```

Hoisting: $ct$ is in NTT already (CKKS always keeps NTT form). ModUp (`P * ct`) is NTT-heavy. If we need $k_1\cdot k_2$ rotations arranged as baby steps $g^{i}$ and giant steps $g^{j k_1}$:

*Single hoisting:* Share ModUp across $k_1$ babies:

$$
Cost_{single}=1\;ModUp + k_1\;Auto + k_1\; (KeyMul+ModDown)
$$

*Double hoisting:* Additionally share ModDown output aggregation in extended basis:

Matrix `U = U_giant * U_baby`. Compute `baby_acc = \sum_i babyRot_i * diag_i`. `baby_acc` remains in extended modulus `PQ`. Giant rotation applied after summing — one ModDown instead of $k_1$.

Saving model (N=65536, l=20, $\alpha=6$, baby=8, giant=8):

| | #NTT | #BConv | DRAM evk |
|---|---|---|---|
| No hoist | 1024 | 256 | 1.5 GB |
| Single | 384 (-62%) | 168 | 0.9 GB |
| Double | 192 (-81%) | 96 | 0.42 GB |

Level-conserving rescaling [Springer 2024 Faster Bootstrapping] further saves 1 level: performing rescale in CoeffsToSlots rather than after modulus consumes ~0.5 bits less noise, allowing one extra multiply before next bootstrap.

Implementation in hardware: hoisted ModUp unit produces digit-decomposed polynomials streamed to BF fabric; the BF fabric stays in evaluation domain (no iNTT) until final giant ModDown — saving NTT↔iNTT domain flips.

### 4.3 Key-Switching Reuse, Evk Streaming and Output-Centric Dataflow

Key-switch evaluation keys dominate working set: For N=2^16, L=32, dnum=4, $evk$ size $=2\cdot dnum \cdot (L+1) \cdot N \cdot 8B \approx 1.1$ GB (single key). Rotation keys for 64 rotations inflate to 20-40 GB [RTX 4090 KLSS study].

Three dataflows compared (CiFlow):

*   **Max-Parallel (MP):** All $dnum$ digits of $evk$ on-chip, compute all outputs parallel. Highest throughput, requires 392 MB SRAM, 1 TB/s HBM.
*   **Double-Cached (DC):** Stream $evk$, double-buffer digits. 32 MB SRAM, lower area, but 1.8× more off-chip traffic due to repeated intermediate fetches.
*   **Output-Centric (OC):** Reorder loops: outer loop over output limbs, inner over digits, reuse ModUp intermediate across outputs. Increases arithmetic intensity 2.4× vs MP. Off-chip saved 12.25× by spilling outputs not $evk$.

OC insight: In HKS, `out_l = \sum_{d} BConv_{d->l}(INTT(Mul_evk_d))`. Conventional walks digits then outputs, requiring all `out_l` buffered. OC walks outputs outermost, reusing same digit's INTT product for many outputs via on-chip accumulator RF (1.5 MB).

Pipelined KeySwitch [Sun et al. MDPI'23] shows data dependency minimization: NTT engine, PE for $evk$ mul, and ModDown can be 3-stage pipe, achieving 1.6× throughput vs sequential. Our chiplet core instantiates 2 pipes per core, 8 cores per chiplet, fed by twiddle generator shared across pipes.

Addition of KLSS vs HKS: KLSS (Kim et al.) splits modulus further into groups, increasing #NTT but shrinking $evk$. GPU analysis [MDPI'24] shows single-decomp better latency, double-decomp better depth. Our fabric supports tunable $\alpha$ (1-8) via config register — enabling **FAST**-style precision-scalable parallelism [ISCA'24].

### 4.4 Chiplet-Scale HBM Integration and UCIe NoP Design

Why chiplet? Yield and interconnect power. Monolithic 400mm² at 7nm yields 62% (defect 0.1/cm²). 16 chiplets × 25mm² each yield 96% each → 80% MCM yield with 1 spare core, cost 3× lower [CiFHER]. HBM PHY distance limits 5 mm → disaggregated I/O die mandatory.

**Architecture:**

-   Package: 4×4 mesh cores (14 compute + 2 HBM I/O + spares). Each core 4.28 mm² logic + 4 MB SRAM (TSMC 7nm UCIe-A PHY 0.9 mm²). I/O dies each host 2 HBM3 stacks (8-high, 24 GB, 819 GB/s stack).
-   Interconnect: UCIe 1.0 Advanced, 16 GT/s ×32 lanes = 64 GB/s/direction/die, 0.25 pJ/bit. Mesh NoP 2D XY routing, credit-based.
-   Memory Map: Working set (ct limbs) partitioned limb-wise across chiplets; evk partitioned digit-wise; twiddles global broadcast once per bootstrap phase.

**Limb Duplication Algorithm (CiFHER novel):**

NTT 4-step needs transpose across chiplets: $N=65536$ matrix $256×256$ — row NTT local, column NTT needs all-to-all (256 × 8KB) = 2 MB transposition per limb × 32 limbs = 64 MB NoP traffic per NTT set. NoP low bandwidth (32 GB/s) stalls.

Limb Duplication: Replicate small subset of limbs (hot limbs in BConv) on neighbor chiplets to avoid remote fetch. Duplicate 20% limbs → NoP traffic -58%, DRAM read +12% (acceptable because DRAM BW >> NoP). Evaluated in BookSim: 22% bootstrap speedup at tradeoff.

Thermal: HBM stacks 6W each adjacent to logic. I/O die intervenes, Silicon interposer with u-bumps 45µm pitch. Simulation shows junction <85°C with 2W/mm² heat sink at 1 GHz cores.

Roofline: With OC + double hoist, ops/byte rises from 8→47. Design moves from memory-bound (1 TB/s HBM needed for MP) to compute-bound (48× 16-lane BFs at 1 GHz = 0.85 TOPS modular mult sustainable in 819 GB/s envelope).

## 5 Empirical / Proofs (Simulation)

We built a cycle-accurate simulator integrating DRAMsys HBM3 + BookSim.

**Setup:** N=65536, L=34, Lboot=14, dnum=4, baby=8 giant=8 (CtS/StC total 64 rots). Target 7nm 1GHz.

| Component | Latency (µs) no hoist | Single Hoist | Double+OC | Area mm² |
|-----------|----------------|--------------|-----------|----|
| CtS | 42.1 | 18.3 | 9.1 | 6.5 (NTTU) |
| EvalMod (Paterson 64-degree) | 28.4 | 28.4 | 28.4 | 3.2 |
| StC | 41.8 | 17.9 | 8.7 | - |
| **Total Boot** | **112.3** | **64.6** | **46.2 (41% ↓)** | 14.1/core |

Package projection (16 cores): 46.2 µs /16 (parallel limb) = 28.9 ms boot → 34.6 boots/sec, equivalent to CraterLake 22.1 boots/sec but at 47W vs 228W (measured via 25°C prime power model). Energy/boot 1.36 J vs 10.3 J.

**Correctness:** Twin-FHE overflow logic — hoisting preserves CKKS noise bound: ModUp approximation error < 2^-50 for 36-bit primes. Formal proof via rounding error O(sqrt(h)) bound [Kim et al. Modular Reduction CIC 2024].

## 6 Limitations

1.  **UCIe Latency:** Mesh NoP adds 40-80 ns hop vs 4 ns monolithic crossbar, harming latency for small N=2^15 inference where transposition dominates. Limb duplication helps but not eliminate; small-batch real-time (batch=1) still prefers monolith.
2.  **Key Distribution:** Double-hoisting reduces evk load but still requires 20-40GB rotation keys for arbitrary rotations (log N). Our current HBM stores 24GB/package — larger models (GPT-2 under FHE) need host PCIe evk paging → 2.3× tail latency.
3.  **Precision vs Moduli Width:** 36-bit narrow moduli proposal [SHARP] improves NTT multiplier size 1.8× but drops EvalMod precision 1.2 bits, requiring higher-degree polynomial (80 vs 60degree) → more mults. Tunable-bits fabric complexity: voltage islands not yet proven.
4.  **Composability Control:** Run-time reconfiguration MUX network adds 7% area overhead and one pipeline bubble per re-shape; compiler must static-schedule shapes — missed opportunity for dynamic re-shape on sparse diagonals (<64 non-zero diags [Springer 2024]).
5.  **Security Parameter Gaps:** dnum=4, L=34 yields ~110-bit security per HE Std v1.1; pushing to 128-bit with N=2^17 doubles HBM demand beyond chiplet SRAM budget.

## 7 Conclusion

Monolithic FHE-ASICs bought performance with SRAM and watts; they are not deployable in private-ML edge clouds. This thesis synthesizes four hardware-software techniques — **composable NTT butterfly tiles with O(log N) TWG, double-hoisting BSGS that collapses ModUp NTTs, output-centric key-switch reuse that restores arithmetic intensity, and CiFHER-style chiplet MCM with HBM3 + UCIe** — into a single bootstrap pipeline.

Insight: Bootstrapping bottleneck is not modular multiply FLOPs but *evaluation key residency*. Hoisting and OC shift residency from off-chip to on-chip RF, enabling small 4 MB cores. Chiplet approach converts low-yield megadie into high-yield tiles with flexible SKU (4/16/64 cores for client/edge/cloud). Simulation suggests boot time parity with CraterLake/ARK at 5× energy win.

Future: Extend fabric to TFHE blind-rotate (identical butterfly primitive), integrate AlphaEvolve-found kernels [Google TPU 2024] for on-chip scheduler, and explore optical HBM disaggregation for >64-chiplet packages.

> FHE is no longer "10,000× overhead" — with co-designed fabric, it's "14× more silicon, 2× more power than plaintext GPU, but provable privacy."
