# Holographic Quantum Error Correction: The HaPPY Code, Tensor-Network Holography, and Bulk Reconstruction

## Abstract

The realization that the Anti-de Sitter / Conformal Field Theory (AdS/CFT) correspondence is, at its core, a quantum error-correcting code has transformed both quantum gravity and quantum information theory. This thesis develops holographic quantum error correction from first principles: the HaPPY code of Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow, and Preskill, in which perfect tensors on a hyperbolic tiling encode bulk logical qubits into boundary physical qubits [1]; the Ryu-Takayanagi entropy formula derived as an exact property of the code; entanglement wedge reconstruction and complementary recovery of bulk operators; the generalization to random tensor networks, where the replica trick maps Rényi entropy computations to a classical spin model with a domain wall pinned to the minimal surface [2]; and the modern theory of approximate codes, where reconstruction can break down even when the geometry looks smooth [7]. We present the central theorems, the operator-pushing calculus, and the limits of the toy-model program — including what HaPPY states provably cannot reproduce [6].

##1 Introduction

Few proposals in modern physics are as startling as the claim that *spacetime is a quantum error-correcting code*. The AdS/CFT correspondence asserts a duality between a gravitational theory in the interior (the *bulk*) of an asymptotically Anti-de Sitter spacetime and a non-gravitational conformal field theory living on its *boundary*. For two decades this duality was understood primarily as a map between observables: boundary correlation functions encode bulk scattering, and boundary entanglement entropy is measured by extremal surfaces in the bulk. What was missing was an information-theoretic account of *why* this map works — why the same bulk physics can be read off from many different, overlapping boundary regions, as if the boundary kept redundant backup copies of the interior [10].

The breakthrough came when Almheiri, Dong, and Harlow reframed bulk reconstruction as quantum error correction. In their formulation, the bulk effective field theory defines a *code subspace* — the low-energy sector of the full boundary Hilbert space — and bulk local operators are *logical* operators, represented redundantly on the boundary in the manner of an erasure-correcting code. Deleting (tracing out) a boundary region is harmless for operators deep in the bulk, exactly as deleting a subset of physical qubits is harmless for the logical qubit of a good code. This insight converted a collection of puzzling features of the holographic dictionary — the ability to reconstruct the same bulk operator from different boundary regions, the Ryu-Takayanagi formula, the negativity of tripartite information — into consequences of coding theory.

The program demanded *solvable models*. A duality is not a proof, and exact statements require exact codes. The HaPPY code [1] supplied them: a family of genuine quantum error-correcting codes whose tensor-network structure is a discretization of a spatial slice of AdS, and in which the Ryu-Takayanagi formula, complementary recovery, and multipartite entanglement constraints hold *exactly*, by elementary linear algebra rather than by conjecture. Subsequent work replaced perfect tensors with random tensors and recovered holography *statistically*, with the Ryu-Takayanagi formula emerging from a large-bond-dimension saddle point [2][3]. And the most recent phase of the program has been sobering: in the approximate codes relevant to real AdS/CFT, reconstruction can fail catastrophically even where the semiclassical geometry looks perfectly healthy [7], and HaPPY boundary states demonstrably lack the tripartite entanglement structure of true holographic states [6].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the necessary background in quantum error correction and holography. Section 3 describes the methodology: perfect tensors, hyperbolic tilings, the greedy wedge algorithm, and the replica-trick technology of random tensor networks. Section 4 dives deeply into the HaPPY construction, the code-theoretic derivation of the Ryu-Takayanagi formula, complementary recovery and uberholography, and the random tensor network correspondence. Section 5 collects the hard results — the theorems and the quantitative predictions they yield. Section 6 confronts the limitations: what the toy models get wrong, and where the approximate-code frontier now stands. Section 7 concludes.

##2 Background

### Quantum error correction in brief

A quantum error-correcting code is a subspace — the *code subspace* — of a larger *physical* Hilbert space, together with the guarantee that certain errors act trivially within it. The logical operators of interest live on the code subspace; each logical operator admits many *representations* as physical operators, and the code protects information precisely when every representation can survive the erasure of some subset of physical subsystems. The Knill-Laflamme conditions make this precise: a code corrects a set of errors exactly when those errors act as scalars on the code subspace.

Two facts from coding theory will recur throughout. First, *erasure correction* is the relevant noise model for holography: losing a boundary region corresponds to erasing a known subset of physical qubits, and the question is which logical (bulk) operators survive. Second, *operator pushing* is the mechanism of redundancy: a logical operator can be moved through the encoding circuit until it is supported on a chosen subset of physical qubits, provided the code's structure permits it. In holographic codes, operator pushing through perfect tensors is the microscopic realization of bulk reconstruction.

### AdS/CFT and the Ryu-Takayanagi formula

The AdS/CFT correspondence relates quantum gravity in a negatively curved bulk to a conformal field theory on the boundary at infinity. Among its sharpest quantitative statements is the Ryu-Takayanagi formula: the entanglement entropy of a boundary region *A* equals the area of the minimal bulk surface homologous to *A*, divided by four times Newton's constant. Quantum corrections promote the minimal surface to a *quantum extremal surface* minimizing the generalized entropy, the sum of the area term and the bulk entanglement entropy across the surface [5].

The domain of dependence of the region bounded by this surface and the boundary region *A* is the *entanglement wedge* of *A* — conjecturally, the largest bulk region whose physics can be reconstructed from *A* alone. Bulk reconstruction is therefore a statement about *subregion duality*: each boundary region encodes a definite bulk wedge, and overlapping boundary regions encode overlapping wedges, just as overlapping subsets of physical qubits can each suffice to recover a logical qubit [10].

##3 Methodology

Our methodology is constructive and exact. Rather than postulating holographic properties, we build explicit tensor networks and *prove* that the desired features hold.

**Perfect tensors.** A tensor with an even number of legs is *perfect* when, for every bipartition of its legs into two sets with the smaller set no larger than the larger, the induced linear map from the smaller side to the larger side is an isometry — that is, the tensor is maximally entangled across every cut [6]. Concretely, if *T* maps a subset of legs *X* to the complementary legs, perfection demands that the adjoint composed with the map be the identity on *X*. Perfect tensors are the atoms of the HaPPY code: they guarantee that information flows losslessly through the network in every direction.

**Hyperbolic tilings.** The bulk spatial slice is discretized by a regular tiling of the hyperbolic disk — for example the tiling by pentagons in which four pentagons meet at each vertex, or the tiling by hexagons with a six-legged perfect tensor at each hexagon's center [6]. One leg of each tensor is designated the *bulk logical input*; the remaining legs contract with neighboring tensors in the interior or terminate as *boundary physical legs* at the asymptotic edge. The full contraction is an isometry from the bulk Hilbert space to the boundary Hilbert space, hence an encoding map of a quantum error-correcting code [1].

**The greedy algorithm and the entanglement wedge.** Given a boundary region *A*, the *greedy algorithm* iteratively absorbs any tensor that has a strict majority of its legs already inside the absorbed set, starting from *A*. The absorbed region's cut through the network defines the code's analogue of the Ryu-Takayanagi surface, and the absorbed bulk is the analogue of the entanglement wedge. Because perfect tensors are isometries in every direction, any operator inside the greedy wedge can be pushed outward onto *A* — a fully explicit bulk reconstruction procedure.

**Random tensor networks and the replica trick.** In the random tensor network program, each vertex tensor is drawn independently from the Haar distribution (or a unitary design), and holographic properties are established *on average* over the randomness [2]. The *n*-th Rényi entropy of a boundary region is computed by a replica trick: the averaged trace of the reduced state to the *n*-th power becomes the partition function of a classical spin model whose spins take values in the symmetric group on *n* elements, with ferromagnetic couplings along network bonds and boundary conditions pinning a cyclic permutation inside the region and the identity outside. In the large-bond-dimension limit the dominant configuration is a domain wall of minimal "area" separating the two boundary conditions — the discrete Ryu-Takayanagi surface [2][3].

##4 Holographic Codes: A Deep Dive

### 4.1 Perfect tensors and operator pushing

The engine of every exact holographic code is the perfect tensor. Consider a six-legged tensor of the kind placed at each hexagon of the hyperbolic tiling [6]. Perfection means that *any* choice of three or fewer legs defines an isometry into the remaining legs. The operational consequence is *operator pushing*: suppose a bulk operator acts on the logical leg of a tensor, and we wish to represent it on a subset of the tensor's other legs. Because the map from the logical leg plus any sufficiently small set of legs into the complement is an isometry, the operator can be conjugated through the tensor and re-expressed on the complementary side — pushed one step outward through the network.

Repeated pushing propagates a bulk operator from deep in the tiling all the way to the boundary, landing on any boundary region whose greedy wedge contains the operator's location. This is bulk reconstruction made mechanical: no analytic continuation, no smearing functions, just the isometric property applied tile by tile. The argument also establishes the encoding isometry itself. Contracting the network from the boundary inward, each tensor encountered is an isometry from its inward-facing legs to its outward-facing legs, so the whole network is an isometry from bulk to boundary — the encoding map of the code [1].

> **Theorem:** (HaPPY isometry). The tensor network obtained by placing a perfect tensor at each tile of a hyperbolic tiling, with one bulk leg per tensor and the remaining legs contracted or terminated at the boundary, defines an isometry from the bulk Hilbert space to the boundary Hilbert space.

A compact numerical check of perfection for a candidate tensor is straightforward:

```python
import numpy as np
from itertools import combinations

def is_perfect(T, legs, tol=1e-8):
    """T: ndarray with one index per leg, all of dimension d.
       Perfect iff every bipartition with |X| <= |Xc| is an isometry X -> Xc."""
    n = len(legs)
    d = T.shape[0]
    for r in range(1, n // 2 + 1):
        for X in combinations(range(n), r):
            Xc = tuple(i for i in range(n) if i not in X)
            M = T.transpose(X + Xc).reshape(d ** r, d ** (n - r))
            if not np.allclose(M.conj().T @ M, np.eye(d ** r), atol=tol):
                return False, X
    return True, None
```

The five-qubit code's encoding tensor — the workhorse example of a perfect tensor on six indices — passes this test, and HaPPY networks built from it inherit every property proved below.

### 4.2 The Ryu-Takayanagi formula as a code property

Perhaps the most striking result of the HaPPY construction is that the Ryu-Takayanagi formula is not an input but a *theorem* of the code. Fix a boundary region *A* and run the greedy algorithm: absorb *A*, then repeatedly absorb any tensor with a majority of legs already absorbed. Let the final absorbed set be *W(A)*, and let the cut separating *W(A)* from the rest of the network cross some number *L* of bonds, each of bond dimension *D*.

Because every tensor in the absorbed region acts as an isometry toward *A*, the boundary state on *A* is, up to local unitaries, a tensor product of *L* maximally entangled pairs shared across the cut, tensored with the bulk logical state restricted to *W(A)*. The entanglement entropy of *A* is therefore exactly

> **Theorem:** (HaPPY Ryu-Takayanagi). For a HaPPY network with uniform bond dimension *D*, the entanglement entropy of a boundary region *A* satisfies *S(A) = L log D + S_bulk(W(A))*, where *L* is the number of cut bonds — the discrete area of the Ryu-Takayanagi surface — and *S_bulk* is the entropy of the bulk state inside the greedy entanglement wedge [1].

The first term is the area law with the correct geometric meaning: *L* is the size of the minimal cut, the network's discretization of the extremal surface.

### 4.3 Complementary recovery and uberholography

Exact holographic codes satisfy a property with no analogue in conventional coding theory: *complementary recovery*.

This redundancy has a startling geometric consequence explored in later work: *uberholography*.

| Property | HaPPY code | Random tensor network | Full AdS/CFT (conjectured) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ryu-Takayanagi formula | Exact, by cut-counting [1] | Holds at large bond dimension [2] | Holds with quantum corrections [5] |
| Complementary recovery | Exact [1] | Approximate, concentration of measure | Expected on code subspace |
| Bulk entropy term | Exact second term | Included via bulk legs [3] | Generalized entropy [5] |
| Tripartite information | Non-positive exactly [1] | Non-positive on average | Non-positive |
| Boundary states | Stabilizer-like, non-CFT [6] | Typical Haar-random | Genuine CFT states |

### 4.4 Random tensor networks and the replica trick

Perfect tensors are rigid: they exist only for special local dimensions and lack the scrambling dynamics of real holographic systems.

The technical heart is the replica trick.

```haskell
-- Replica spin model for the n-th Renyi entropy of boundary region a.
-- Vertex variables range over permutations; boundary pins select domains.
replicaAction :: Graph -> Region -> Assignment -> Double
replicaAction g a sigma =
    sum [ bondCost (sigma x) (sigma y) | (x, y) <- edges g ]
  where
    bondCost p q = if p == q then 0 else log bondDimension
    -- boundary condition: cyclic permutation inside a, identity outside
```

At large bond dimension the partition function is dominated by the minimal-energy configuration: two domains — the cyclic-permutation domain and the identity domain — separated by a domain wall of minimal area anchored to the boundary of *A*.

##5 Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

The holographic code program has produced a suite of precise, checkable results.

**Exact Ryu-Takayanagi and multipartite constraints.** In the HaPPY code the entropy formula *S(A) = L log D + S_bulk* holds exactly for every boundary region, with the minimal cut computable by the greedy algorithm [1]. The negativity of tripartite information holds exactly as well, matching one of the sharpest known information-theoretic constraints on holographic states. These are *theorems about finite tensor networks*, verifiable by direct contraction on small tilings.

**Universal recovery and the modular Hamiltonian.** Moving beyond toy models, the framework of *universal recovery channels* gives a robust, approximate version of entanglement wedge reconstruction for real AdS/CFT. A bulk operator in the entanglement wedge of *A* can be reconstructed as the response of the boundary region's *modular Hamiltonian* to a perturbation of the bulk state — a noncommutative Bayes rule that undoes the noise of restricting to a subregion, with an integral representation in terms of modular flow [4]. This result survives the passage from exact entropic equalities to the approximate equalities of effective field theory, where naive algebraic arguments are known to fail qualitatively.

**Information-paradox applications.** Entanglement wedge reconstruction has been deployed to track information flow in evaporating black holes: the entanglement wedge of the Hawking radiation undergoes a phase transition at the Page time, after which interior operators become reconstructible from the radiation — the code-theoretic content of the Page curve [5]. This is arguably the program's most consequential "empirical" success: a quantum-information theorem about codes constraining the fate of information behind horizons.

**Approximate codes and large breakdowns.** The sobering counterpoint: reconstruction can fail *badly* even in semiclassically healthy geometries. Akers and Levine exhibited states, perturbatively close to the vacuum in Newton's constant, for which the region actually reconstructible from a boundary subregion — the *reconstruction wedge* — is arbitrarily smaller than the entanglement wedge, with the quantum extremal surface macroscopically displaced from the Ryu-Takayanagi surface [7]. Exact-code intuition does not transfer naively to the approximate codes of real holography.

**What HaPPY cannot do.** Recent analysis has sharpened the boundary between the toy models and true holography: the tripartite entanglement structure of HaPPY boundary states is provably *not* holographic — these stabilizer-like states lack entanglement features that genuine holographic states must possess [6]. The HaPPY code is a superb model of the *error-correcting* aspects of AdS/CFT and an imperfect model of its *state* aspects, and confusing the two is now a documented error.

##6 Limitations and Open Problems

The program's limitations are as instructive as its successes.

- **Stabilizer artifacts.** HaPPY boundary states are stabilizer-like, efficiently simulable, and far from the chaotic, scrambling states of genuine CFTs [6]. Properties that depend on scrambling — out-of-time-order correlators, complexity growth — are absent by construction.
- **No dynamical bulk.** The tiling is a fixed discretization of a spatial slice; there is no Hamiltonian evolution, no backreaction, and no account of how the bulk geometry itself emerges dynamically. Spacetime random tensor networks [3] add a time direction but remain kinematic.
- **Exact versus approximate.** Real AdS/CFT is at best an *approximate* code, and approximation changes the story qualitatively [7]. The operator-algebra quantum error correction framework must be extended to approximate recovery with controlled errors — progress exists via universal recovery channels [4], but a complete theory of approximate holographic codes is missing.
- **The reconstruction wedge gap.** The arbitrarily large separation between reconstruction and entanglement wedges [7] means that "the entanglement wedge is reconstructible" is false as stated in full generality. Characterizing exactly *which* states and *which* regions admit reconstruction remains open.
- **Beyond AdS.** The entire program leans on the existence of an asymptotic boundary where the physical qubits live. De Sitter space, with no spatial boundary, has resisted a holographic-code formulation entirely [10].
- **Uberholography and the continuum.** Fractal reconstruction on measure-zero boundary sets [8] is rigorously established in discrete models but its continuum CFT meaning is unclear; it may be an artifact of discretization or a genuine prediction about holographic CFTs.

##7 Conclusion

Holographic quantum error correction has given the oldest puzzle of quantum gravity — how a lower-dimensional boundary encodes a higher-dimensional interior — its sharpest known formulation. The HaPPY code proved that the Ryu-Takayanagi formula, complementary recovery, and the redundancy of bulk reconstruction can be *theorems* of an explicit, finite, exactly solvable code [1]. Random tensor networks showed that these features are *typical*, emerging from a replica-trick saddle point at large bond dimension [2][3]. Universal recovery channels extended reconstruction to the approximate regime where real effective field theory lives [4], and the Page-curve analysis turned the formalism into a tool for the information paradox [5].

The frontier is now defined by the failures: reconstruction wedges that collapse far inside entanglement wedges [7], boundary states whose entanglement is provably non-holographic [6], and the missing theory of approximate holographic codes. These are not refutations but refinements — they tell us precisely which features of the toy models are robust and which are artifacts of exactness. If spacetime is a quantum error-correcting code, the next decade's task is to write down the code.
