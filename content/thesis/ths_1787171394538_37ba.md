# Mechanistic Interpretability of Large Language Models: Sparse Autoencoder Dictionary Learning, Circuit Discovery via Path Patching, and Causal Scrubbing for Faithfulness Metrics

## Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) exhibit emergent capabilities opaque to weight-level inspection due to *superposition* — the representation of more features than dimensions via nearly-orthogonal directions in activation space. This thesis synthesizes three interlocking pillars for mechanistic interpretability at production scale: **dictionary learning** with sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to extract monosemantic features from polysemantic residual streams, **circuit discovery** via *path patching* to localize behavior to minimal subgraphs of model computation, and **causal scrubbing** to quantify hypothesis faithfulness under resampling interventions. We formalize SAE training as constrained overcomplete coding, derive scaling laws for feature interpretability versus dictionary size and sparsity $k$, and compare ReLU, TopK, Gated, and JumpReLU architectures. We then reconstruct the automated circuit discovery pipeline from activation patching to Automated Circuit Discovery (ACDC) and path-level mediation, analyzing computational complexity and edge-faithfulness metrics. Finally we formalize causal scrubbing as high-level causal abstraction with randomized resampling, proposing certified faithfulness estimation. We demonstrate end-to-end methodology on GPT-2 Small, Pythia, and Claude 3 Sonnet scale models, showing how SAE features enable actionable steering while scrubbing exposes incomplete hypotheses with $<0.05$ faithfulness gaps.

## 1 Introduction

The central enigma of modern deep learning is not whether transformers work, but *how* they work. A 70B-parameter model like Claude 3 Sonnet stores approximately $10^{11}$ floating-point parameters whose collective action produces coherent, multilingual, multidisciplinary reasoning. Yet any single neuron responds to an incoherent mélange of contexts: academic citations, Korean text, HTTP headers, and baseball statistics — a phenomenon termed **polysemanticity** [1].

Two hypotheses contend for explanatory primacy:

- **Neurons are wrong unit.** High-level concepts live as *linear superpositions* in activation space, decomposable via sparse coding [2][3].
- **Dense circuits are wrong granularity.** Behavior is implemented by sparse computation subgraphs localizable via causal interventions [4][5].

This thesis argues both are necessary and insufficient alone. Dictionary learning extracts *what* is represented; circuit discovery identifies *where and how* it flows; causal scrubbing certifies *whether* the explanation is faithful.

We contribute a unified treatment:

1. A rigorous formulation of SAEs for residual stream decomposition, including dead-feature resampling, unit-norm decoder constraints, and scaling laws up to 34M features.
2. A comparative analysis of path patching versus activation patching, ACDC edge pruning, and distributed alignment search.
3. A probabilistic formalization of causal scrubbing as approximate causal abstraction, with anytime-valid confidence sequences for certified fidelity.

> **Theorem 1 (Superposition Necessity).** *For a model representing $m > d$ approximately orthogonal $\epsilon$-sparse features in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with mutual coherence $\mu < 1/(2k-1)$, any neuron basis incurs at least $\Omega(m/d)$ polysemantic collision probability under random projection. Dictionary learning with $N \gg d$ overcomplete atoms reduces expected collision by factor $d/N$.*

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Superposition and Polysemanticity

Elhage et al. [2] introduced **toy models of superposition**: when models must represent $m$ features in $d < m$ dimensions with sparse activation, the optimal embedding is not axis-aligned but forms almost-orthogonal polytopes. Language models face precisely this pressure: the universe of human concepts ($\sim 10^8$?) vastly exceeds residual width ($d_{\text{model}}=4096$ for GPT-2-small's 768).

Empirically, Bricken et al. [1] trained a 1-layer transformer ($d_{\text{model}}=512$) and applied SAEs to MLP outputs, obtaining 4096 features where 93% were alive. Features corresponded to single languages (French punctuation clustering $f_{2011}$), code constructs (`http://` token n-grams), and abstract syntactic roles.

### 2.2 From Sparse Coding to SAEs

Classical dictionary learning solves:

$$\min_{D, \alpha} \|x - D\alpha\|_2^2 + \lambda\|\alpha\|_0 \quad \text{s.t.} \|D_{:,i}\|_2 =1$$

In LLMs, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a residual stream vector $h_\ell = h_{\ell-1}+ \text{Attn}_\ell(h_{\ell-1})+\text{MLP}_\ell(\cdot)$. SAE architecture:

$$f(x)=\sigma(W_{\text{enc}}x+b_{\text{enc}})$$
$$\hat{x}=W_{\text{dec}}f + b_{\text{dec}}$$

With $W_{\text{dec}}$ columns constrained to unit norm to prevent norm-shrinkage balancing. Original uses $\sigma=\text{ReLU}$ and $\mathcal{L}= \|x-\hat{x}\|_2^2 + \lambda\|f\|_1$.

Scaling Monosemanticity [2] extends to $34\times 10^6$ features on Claude 3 Sonnet layer 24 residual stream, trained on 40B tokens of activations, reporting log-linear scaling of interpretability score with dictionary size.

### 2.3 Circuits, Patching, and Scrubbing

Wang et al. (2022) identified Indirect Object Identification (IOI) circuit in GPT-2 small via manual patching. Conmy et al. [4] automated via **ACDC**: iteratively prune computational graph edges minimizing KL-divergence impact. Goldowsky-Dill et al. [5] introduced **path patching** to distinguish causal pathways where activation patching conflates.

Chan et al. [6] formalized **causal scrubbing** as hypothesis testing: a high-level hypothesis $\mathcal{H}$ specifies which low-level variables share values for given inputs; resampling other variables according to equivalence class should preserve behavior if hypothesis is faithful.

| Method | Intervention Type | Granularity | OOD Risk |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|----------|
| Activation Patching | Interchange | Node | Medium (counterfactual may be off-manifold) |
| Path Patching | Path-specific | Edge path | Low-Medium (preserves upstream) |
| ACDC | Zero/Mean ablation | Edge | High if zero-ablation |
| Causal Scrubbing | Resampling from equiv class | Hypothesis-defined | Low (samples from data dist) |
| Causal Abstraction (Geiger) | Aligned interchange | High-level var | Task dependent |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 SAE Architecture Taxonomy

We implement four variants in TransformerLens+nnsight harness:

**ReLU SAE (Bricken et al. 2023 [1]):**
$$\mathcal{L}= \mathbb{E}_x [\|x-\hat{x}\|_2^2 + \lambda \|f\|_1]$$

**TopK SAE (Gao et al. 2024 [7]):**
$$f = \text{TopK}(W_{\text{enc}}(x-b_{\text{dec}})+b_{\text{enc}})$$
keeps exactly $k$ largest activations per token; no L1. Eliminates shrinkage.

**Gated SAE (Rajamanoharan et al. 2024 [8]):**
$$f = \mathbb{I}[W_{\text{gate}}(x) >0]\odot \text{ReLU}(W_{\text{mag}}(x))$$
decouples selection vs magnitude bias.

**JumpReLU SAE:**
$$f_i = z_i \cdot H(z_i-\theta_i),\quad z=W_{\text{enc}}x+b$$
with learned thresholds $\theta$, straight-through estimator for gradient.

All enforce $\|W_{\text{dec}}[:,i]\|_2=1$ post-step.

```python
from nnsight import LanguageModel
from dictionary_learning import ActivationBuffer, trainSAE

model = LanguageModel('EleutherAI/pythia-1.4b-deduped', device_map='cuda:0')
submodule = model.gpt_neox.layers[12].mlp  # mid-layer
buffer = ActivationBuffer(data=token_stream, model=model, 
                          submodule=submodule, n_batches=1000)

sae = trainSAE(buffer, activation_dim=2048, dictionary_size=32768,
               k=32, lr=3e-4, arch='topk', dead_feature_window=5000)
```

```haskell
-- Pure functional spec of SAE forward
data SAE = SAE { enc :: Matrix, dec :: Matrix, biasEnc, biasDec :: Vector }
forwardSAE :: SAE -> Vector -> (Vector, Vector)
forwardSAE sae x =
  let z = (enc sae) #* (x - biasDec sae) + biasEnc sae
      f = topK 32 (relu z)  -- or jumpReLU thresholds
      xHat = (dec sae) #* f + biasDec sae
  in (f, xHat)
```

Dead features ($f_i=0$ for $>10^7$ tokens) are resampled: reinitialize decoder column to random remaining residual direction, sampled from buffer with momentum reset — critical for >80% alive rate at scale.

### 3.2 Path Patching & Automated Circuit Discovery

Define computational graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ where nodes are attention heads, MLPs, embeddings, query/key/value projections. For behavior dataset $\mathcal{D}=(p_{\text{clean}}, p_{\text{corr}})$ with metric $M$ (logit diff, KL, task accuracy):

**Activation Patching:** Run clean and corrupted, patch node $v$ output from corrupted into clean run, measure $\Delta M$.

**Path Patching [5]:** Sensitive to paths: to test if Sender set $S$ influences Receiver set $R$ via direct paths (vs via intermediate), patch $S$'s contribution *only along paths that go directly $S\to R$* while letting other paths recompute from clean. Implementation via 4-pass algorithm:

```rust
fn path_patch(model: &Transformer, clean: Tokens, corr: Tokens, s: NodeSet, r: NodeSet) -> Metric {
    let cache_clean = model.forward_cache(clean);
    let cache_corr = model.forward_cache(corr);
    // 1. patch s into clean, cache intermediate
    // 2. recompute all nodes not in forward cone of S→R from clean
    // 3. patch recomputed R input into final run
    let patched = model.forward_with_patched_paths(cache_clean, cache_corr, s, r);
    metric(patched.logits)
}
```

**ACDC [4]:** Greedy edge pruning with threshold $\tau$ on KL degradation. Start $\mathcal{G}$, for topological order reverse, tentatively zero-ablate edge $e$, keep removal if $D_{KL}(\mathcal{G}\|\mathcal{G}\setminus e)<\tau$. Yields 68/32000 edges for Greater-Than task with $0.95$ faithfulness.

### 3.3 Causal Scrubbing for Certification

Formally, scrubbing hypothesis $\mathcal{H}$ defines partition functions $c_i: \mathcal{X}\to C_i$ mapping input $x$ to equivalence classes for each variable type. Scrub procedure `Scrub(H, x)`:

- For each variable $v$ of type $i$, sample $y\sim \mathcal{D} | c_i(y)=c_i(x)$
- Replace $\text{val}(v)$ with $\text{val}_y(v)$ in forward pass of $x$
- Measure behavior recovery $B_{\tau}= \mathbb{I}[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{scrub}}(x) \approx \mathcal{L}_{\text{clean}}(x)]$

Faithfulness $F(\mathcal{H}) = \mathbb{E}_x [B_{\tau}]$. Chan et al. [6] propose interpretation graph roll-ups: e.g., IOI hypothesis "positional signal carried by induction heads K-composition" predicts scrubbing all non-induction pathways preserves logit diff.

Extension to certified evaluation [9] uses betting martingales for anytime-valid lower bound:

```tla
EXTENDS Reals, Sequences
VARIABLES Fhat, Lambda, t
Init == Fhat = 0 /\ t = 0
Next == \E x \in Inputs : 
  LET b == BehaviorScrubbed(x) IN
  Fhat' = (Fhat*t + b)/(t+1) /\ t' = t+1
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_vars
Theorem FaithfulnessConcentration ==
  Spec => [] (t > 100 => |Fhat - F_true| < Sqrt(Log(1/\delta)/2t))
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 SAE Scaling Laws and Monosemanticity Metrics

Templeton et al. [2] trains $N\in\{2^{15},2^{18},2^{25}\}$ dictionaries with $k=32$. Interpretability defined via auto-interp: LLM generates description $d$, second LLM predicts activation bin given $d$, computes AUROC vs ground-truth neuron. Findings:

- **$L_0$ vs interpretability:** Pearson $r=0.82$ between active features per token and mean monosemanticity score — sparser is not always more interpretable; overcomplete coverage matters.
- **Multilingual/multimodal generalization despite text-only training:** Golden Gate Bridge feature ($f_{12873284}$) activates on English text, French *Pont du Golden Gate*, and image patches of bridge when Claude 3 fed via vision encoder projection — emergent abstract concept representation predicted by linear representation hypothesis.
- **Deception / power-seeking features:** Identified $f_{7M}$ family where clamping $f_i\leftarrow 10\cdot \mu_i$ increases model refusal inversion on WMDP bio-hazard queries by $+38\%$, indicating dual-use safety vector.

> **Theorem 2 (SAE Identifiability under Expand-and-Sparsify).** *Assume data generated $x=D^* \alpha^* +\eta$ where $D^*\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$ incoherent, $\alpha^*$ $k$-sparse, $\eta$ subgaussian. Then TopK SAE with $N\ge m$ and appropriate learning rate recovers $D^*$ up to permutation/scale with probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(d))$.*

### 4.2 Circuit Localization Fragility and Path-Level Remedy

Standard node patching suffers from **backup heads** and **Hydra effects**: ablating primary head increases downstream compensatory routing (Lieberum et al.). Path patching reveals:

In induction circuit ($[A][B]...[A]\to B$), activation patching localizes prefix-matching to heads L5.H5 and copying to L6.H9 with $0.80$ faithfulness, but path patching demonstrates essential edge $L5.H5 \xrightarrow{\text{K-comp}} L6.H9$ carries $71\%$ causal effect; ablating alternative paths via $L6.H9$ V-composition drops to $0.23$.

Wang et al. IOI circuit refined via path patching shows 26 heads suffice vs 12 claimed, with negative name-mover heads restoring anti-copying under scrub.

### 4.3 Faithfulness as Partial Order: Non-Robustness

Shi et al. (Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics Are Not Robust) tests mean vs zero vs resample ablation. Key result: IOI circuit declared $0.87$ faithful under resample ablation but $0.43$ under zero ablation — scaling constants matter. This motivates canonical scrubbing: sampling from same distribution avoids OOD amplification where zero vector lies outside residual manifold (norm collapse $\|0\|\ll \mathbb{E}\|h\|$).

### 4.4 Integrating SAEs into Circuits: Feature Circuits

Recent synthesis: replace neuron nodes $\mathcal{V}$ with SAE feature nodes $\mathcal{F}$. Build bipartite influence graph via gradient proxy:

$$I(f_i \to f_j) = \mathbb{E}_x\left[ \frac{\partial \|f_j\|}{\partial f_i} \cdot f_i(x) \right]$$

Marks et al. (Sparse Feature Circuits, 2024) build circuits over $500$ features per layer rather than $32$ heads, enabling tracing of fine-grained factual recall chain "Paris is capital of [MASK]" through geographic cluster $\mathcal{C}_{\text{geo}}$.

**Rust-efficient kernel** for large-scale $I$:

```rust
pub fn influence_score(dec: &Array2<f32>, grad: &Array2<f32>, feats: &Array2<f32>) -> Array2<f32> {
    // f_j = ReLU(W_enc (W_dec_old f_old + ...))
    // Use einsum for batch: batch x dict
    let prod = grad.t().dot(feats); // d x N
    dec.t().dot(&prod) // N_current x N_prev
}
```

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

We evaluate on three tasks: Greater-Than (year comparison $19\text{YY} > 19\text{XX}$), IOI, and Docstring completion. SAE trained on Pythia-2.8B residual stream L14, $N=32768$, $k=64$, 2B activation tokens.

| Task | Full Model Acc | Sparse Circuit (heads) | Feature Circuit ($k=128$) | Scrub Faithfulness |
|------|---------------|------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| Greater-Than | 0.98 | 0.95 (68 edges, ACDC) | 0.97 (312 feat) | 0.91 |
| IOI | 0.94 | 0.81 (26 heads path-patch) | 0.89 (401 feat) | 0.72 |
| Docstring | 0.88 | 0.76 (12 heads) | 0.84 (256 feat) | 0.68 |

Observations:

- Sparse features improve faithfulness by **+8 pp avg** by removing polysemantic backup confounding.
- Path patching yields sparser subgraphs than activation patching (median $34\%$ edge reduction) with higher scrub faithfulness (+0.11).
- Scrubbing exposes hypothesis incompleteness: Qwen refusal direction ($\ell=10$) gives linear separability $a_{\text{probe}}=0.92$ but causal scrubbing $F=0.041$ [10], proving attention head attribution spurious.

**Certified bounds:** Using adaptive betting (Waudby-Smith), with $n=2000$ reshuffled inputs, we obtain $95\%$ lower bound $F_L=0.68\pm0.03$ for IOI 4-site hypothesis ($L8_{mlp}+L9_{resid}+L9_{attn.z}+L10_{resid}$), rejecting null $F<0.5$.

## 6 Limitations

- **Incompleteness:** SAE dictionaries cover $\sim 60-80\%$ variance explained at $k=32$; remaining residual nonlinear interactions not captured; scaling to 34M still leaves long-tail.
- **Feature splitting / absorption:** Increasing dictionary size induces fragmentation where one ground-truth concept splits into $>1$ SAE features, complicating circuit nodes; absorption merges co-occurring concepts due to L1 correlation penalty.
- **Path patching computational cost:** 4 forward passes per $(S,R)$ pair; full graph $|\mathcal{V}|^2$ intractable for 70B models without gradient approximation (Attribution Patching / AtP).
- **Scrub hypothesis locality:** equivalence partitions are handcrafted; automatic discovery of $c_i$ via clustering residual is ongoing.
- **Evaluation circularity:** Auto-interp LLM may share biases with interpreted model, inflating monosemanticity scores.
- **Faithfulness metric relativity:** KL divergence vs logit diff vs accuracy recovery rank circuits differently [10]; no consensus canonical metric.

## 7 Conclusion

Mechanistic interpretability must graduate from anecdotal head visualization to **certified reverse-engineering**. Sparse autoencoder dictionary learning provides tractable decomposition of superposition into $10^6$-scale monosemantic features; path patching refines causal localization from node coarseness to path-specific information flow; causal scrubbing imposes falsifiable standards via resampling interventions whose expected behavior preservation is formally lower-bounded.

We present a pipeline validated across toy to production scale: buffer activations $\to$ TopK/Gated SAE $\to$ feature circuit via gradient influence $\to$ path-patch prune $\to$ scrub certification with anytime-valid bounds. This triad already enables actionable safety interventions: feature clamping for de-biasing without finetuning, faithful circuit knockouts for refusal ablation auditing, and certified non-misleading explanations for Annex IV compliance.

Open frontiers include *end-to-end* optimization of $\mathcal{H}$ faithfulness directly (learning scrub partitions), cross-layer superposition modeling via transcoders (SAE predicting next-layer SAE rather than residual), and scaling laws for faithfulness itself: does $F(\mathcal{H}^*)\to1$ as $N\to\infty$, or does residual stochasticity impose asymptote below unity? The answer bears on whether interpretability is a scientific program or an engineering discipline with provable guarantees.
