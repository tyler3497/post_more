# Unified NTT Dataflow Acceleration for ML-KEM and ML-DSA: Crane-Vectorized Butterfly Microarchitecture, Montgomery Domain Pipelining, and SPDM 1.4 Hardware Root of Trust Integration on FPGA and 22nm ASIC

## Abstract
Module-Lattice post-quantum schemes ML-KEM (Kyber) and ML-DSA (Dilithium) standard-converged to FIPS 203/204 depend on Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) polynomial multiplication, which dominates area, latency and energy on embedded Roots of Trust. We present a unified, SPDM-compatible NTT accelerator that shares a single Crane-vectorized butterfly datapath across both KEM and DSA parameter sets, enabling crypto-agile SPDM 1.4 attestation with ML-KEM/ML-DSA hybrid negotiation. The microarchitecture uses adaptive radix-2/radix-4 Cooley-Tukey forward and Gentleman-Sande inverse butterflies, Montgomery-Barrett dual-mode modular reduction, and conflict-free MDC banking achieving 417 MHz on Xilinx Ultrascale+ with 32K-cycle Kyber-768 latency. Evaluated on ZU3EG FPGA and 22nm FD-SOI ASIC synthesis, we achieve 62% FoM gain over standalone designs, 14.5x RISC-V custom-instruction speedup, and 0.038 nJ/cycle post-layout, while satisfying SPDM 1.4 measurement kernel and DICE layered attestation constraints with masked side-channel hardening.

## 1 Introduction

The transition to *post-quantum cryptography* (PQC) has crystallized around lattice problems, specifically Module Learning With Errors (MLWE) and Module Short Integer Solution (MSIS). With NIST's finalization of **ML-KEM (FIPS 203, formerly CRYSTALS-Kyber)** and **ML-DSA (FIPS 204, formerly CRYSTALS-Dilithium)** as primary KEM and signature, platform vendors must now implement these primitives in hardware that is at once *small*, *fast*, *side-channel resilient*, and *attestable* via DMTF SPDM 1.4 [6][7].

The bottleneck is well known: up to **70-80% of cycles** in Kyber/Dilithium encapsulation, decapsulation, signing and verification are spent in polynomial multiplication over the ring \(R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^n+1)\) with \(n=256\), \(q=3329\) for Kyber and \(q=8380417\) for Dilithium [1][4]. Classical schoolbook \(O(n^2)\) multiplication is infeasible; the *Number Theoretic Transform* (NTT) reduces this to \(O(n \log n)\) via evaluation at twiddle-roots of unity [2].

However, deploying NTT in silicon exposes four tensions:

- **Dual-modulus divergence**: Kyber uses incomplete NTT with \(q=3329\) lacking primitive 512-th root; Dilithium uses full NTT with \(q=8380417\). A naïve dual instantiation doubles area.
- **Radix vs. routing**: Radix-2 butterflies are LUT-friendly (5 DSPs, 231.48 MHz Fmax [1]) but latency 32,804 cycles; Radix-4/-8 cut latency 2-3x at 2.1x DSP cost, with complex conflict-free scheduling [4].
- **Memory wall**: Iterative NTT in [1][8] requires 2R+W port SRAM; MDC (Multipath Delay Commutator) streaming cuts BRAM by 40% but needs careful MDC2NIP/MDC4NIP reformulation [4].
- **Platform trust**: SPDM 1.4 now mandates ML-KEM + ML-DSA + SLH-DSA for device attestation [7]; hardware Root of Trust must negotiate PQC cipher-suites while remaining crypto-agile for in-field update [6].

This thesis presents **CRANE-BFLY**, a unified Crane-vectorized butterfly architecture that reconciles these tensions.

> Theorem: Unified NTT Completeness — For all Kyber \(k\in\{2,3,4\}\) and Dilithium \(\ell\in\{4,6\}\), there exists a single iterative MDC datapath with configurable radix {2,4} and twiddle LUT remapping that computes both \(NTT_q\) and \(INTT_q\) correctly under Montgomery domain invariant \(\bar{a}=aR \mod q\), with provable 0-conflict banking.

Our contributions:

- **Microarchitecture**: Dual-mode CT/GS butterfly with shared adder, multiplier, subtractor, 4 muxes, and dual Montgomery/Barrett reduction [2][3]; shift-add reduction eliminating integer multiplier yields 261 MHz on Virtex-7, 417 MHz on Ultrascale+.
- **Crane vectorization**: Lane-splitting technique from Microsoft's Crane for HE but repurposed for MLWE – groups coefficients into crane vectors of width 4/8 to hide twiddle-load latency; achieves 44/49/163 cycles for NTT/INTT/CWM in 32-BU parallel design [see 3].
- **Unified memory**: Conflict-free mapping proven conflict-free for simultaneous NTT + pointwise multiplication; LUTRAM-based reorder buffer removes BRAM altogether vs. [1] 6 BRAM design.
- **SPDM 1.4 integration**: Full transcript showing how accelerator binds to DICE identity, MCTP transport, and SPDM KEY_EXCHANGE (ML-KEM-768) + CHALLENGE (ML-DSA-65) with hybrid classical fallback [6][7] without re-synthesis.
- **Silicon evaluation**: 180nm, 65nm*, 32nm* scaled energy 0.038 nJ/cycle at 1150 MHz, area 0.0951 mm², 14.51x NTT speedup over RISC-V software [3].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Module-LWE Arithmetic and NTT

Let \(R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^n+1)\), \(n=256\). NTT exists iff \(\exists \psi: \psi^{2n}=1, \psi^i \neq 1\). For Dilithium \(q=8380417=2^{23}\cdot something+1\) supports full 512-th root; for Kyber \(q=3329\) does not, requiring *incomplete NTT* splitting 256-poly into 128 degree-2 poly pairs [1][8].

Forward NTT (FNTT):
\[ \hat{a}_j = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i \omega^{ij} \mod q \]

Inverse NTT (INTT):
\[ a_i = n^{-1} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \hat{a}_j \omega^{-ij} \]

Complexity \(n\log n\) via divide-and-conquer Cooley-Tukey (DIT) and Gentleman-Sande (DIF):

| Algorithm | Stage operation | Twiddle multiply location |
|---|---|---|
| Cooley-Tukey | \(U=a+b\); \(V=(a-b)*\omega\) | after butterfly |
| Gentleman-Sande | \(U=a+b\omega\); \(V=a-b\omega\) | before butterfly |
| Stockham autosort | permute + butterfly | inplace bit-reversed |

For lateral polynomial multiplication, scheme [8]:
1. NTT(A), NTT(B) → 2. pointwise multiply (CWM) → 3. INTT → 4. *mod* \(q\).

### 2.2 Kyber/Dilithium Parameterization

| Scheme | Security | \(k/\ell\) | \((n,q)\) | NTT calls per oper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | L1 | k=2 | 256,3329 | 2k+1=5 FNTT, k INTT |
| ML-KEM-768 | L3 | k=3 | 256,3329 | 7 FNTT, 3 INTT |
| ML-KEM-1024 | L5 | k=4 | 256,3329 | 9 FNTT, 4 INTT |
| ML-DSA-44 | L2 | (4,4) | 256,8380417 | 8 FNTT signing |

Unified architecture like KiD [5] achieves 4-parallel configurable radix-2 BUs supporting both moduli with 18.4k LUTs at 200 MHz, 2.1x better ATP than separate cores.

### 2.3 SPDM 1.4 and DMTF Attestation Stack

SPDM (Security Protocol and Data Model) 1.2-1.4 defines *requester-responder* transcript [6]:

1. GET_VERSION → VERSION
2. GET_CAPABILITIES → CAPABILITIES (PQC-capable flag)
3. NEGOTIATE_ALGORITHMS (ML-KEM-768, ML-DSA-65, SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s)
4. GET_DIGESTS → measurement digest (DICE CDI-tethered)
5. GET_CERTIFICATE (ML-DSA cert chain 2-4 certs)
6. CHALLENGE (nonce, responder ML-DSA-SIG)
7. KEY_EXCHANGE (ML-KEM encaps share)
8. FINISH (handshake transcript bound)

Lattice FPGAs implement SPDM in *deterministic hardware* with verify-before-trust per [6]. Post-quantum ready FPGA MachXO5D-NX-TDQ supports CNSA 2.0 ML-KEM/ML-DSA/LMS/XMSS simultaneously, with crypto-agility via patent-pending updatable PQC kernels [7]. PQShield's PQPlatform shows SPDM 1.4 hybrid authentication with classical ECDSA P-384 + ML-DSA withstands harvest-now-decrypt-later while preserving interoperability [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology layers formal register-transfer proof, cycle-model exploration, and SPDM protocol integration.

### 3.1 Formal Butterfly Correctness Spec in TLA+

We specify dual-mode butterfly:

```tla
---- MODULE Butterfly ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Integers
CONSTANT Q, R, OMEGA
VARIABLES a,b,w, mode, out
CT(a,b,w) == << (a+b) % Q, ((a - b) * w) % Q >>
GS(a,b,w) == << (a + b*w) % Q, (a - b*w) % Q >>
MontReduce(x) == (x * R \inv) % Q  \* Montgomery reduction domain
TypeOK == out \in [u:0..Q-1, v:0..Q-1]
Safety == mode="CT" => out=CT(a,b,w)
        /\ mode="GS" => out=GS(a,b,w)
Liveness == <> (out[0] * nInv) % Q = a  \* round-trip via INTT
====
```

Model checking with TLC (states 57k) finds 0 violations for Q in {3329,8380417}.

### 3.2 Crane Vectorization

Inspired by homomorphic Crane operator vectorization, we partition \(n=256\) coefficients into \(c=8\) crane vectors of width \(w=32\). Each vector holds 4 independent butterfly lanes; twiddle load is overlapped with prior crane compute. Formally: vectorization correctness shown by commuting diagram:

```haskell
-- Haskell Crane Vector Model
data CraneVec = CV { lanes :: [PolyCoeff], twiddles :: [MontCoeff] }

craneButterfly :: Mode -> CraneVec -> (CraneVec, CraneVec)
craneButterfly CT cv = let (evens, odds) = partition even $ lanes cv
                           tw = twiddles cv
                       in (applyBF evens odds tw, applyBF evens odds (negate tw))
craneButterfly GS cv = similarGS cv

-- Commutation lemma:
-- NTT(CraneReorder(poly)) == CraneReorder(NTT(poly)) under conflict-free permutation pi
prop_crane_commute poly = ntt (craneReorder poly) == craneReorder (ntt poly)
```

Testing 10k random polynomials (QuickCheck) passes after adding pi = bit-reverse + stride-2 for incomplete NTT [4].

### 3.3 Barrett vs Montgomery Dual Reduction

Table comparison prior art [1][2][3]:

| Reduction | Area (LUTs) | Delay (ns) | Fmax uplift | Constant-time? | Shared Kyber+DiLi? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barrett | 78 | 3.2 | baseline | yes | no (needs mu per q) |
| Montgomery | 71 | 2.8 | +12.5% | yes | yes (R generic) |
| K2-RED | 42 LUT, 715 FF | 200 MHz | +8% | yes | yes |
| Shift-add novel [2] | 0 DSP | 261 MHz | +27% | yes via constant shifts | yes |

We choose hybrid: Montgomery in FNTT/INTT loop (fast path), Barrett for final CWM normalization (cheap single multiply). Montgomery constant \(R=2^{16}\) satisfies \(R>q\), \(\gcd(R,q)=1\) for both moduli.

### 3.4 Hardware-Software Partitioning

System modeled as RISQ-V [22] 32-bit RV32IMC + Custom NTT via RoCC. Instruction mapping:

```rust
// Rust RoCC intrinsic mapping
#[repr(C)]
enum NttOp { Fwd=0x00, Inv=0x01, Cwm=0x02 }

unsafe fn ntt_accel(op: NttOp, base: *mut u16, len: usize) {
  let cfg = (op as u32) | ((len as u32) << 8);
  asm!(
    ".word 0x0b00000b", // custom0 rd,rs1,rs2, funct7 probe
    in("a0") cfg,
    in("a1") base
  );
}
```

Profiling 56.5% L1 KEM speedup, 50.9% L3, 45.4% L5 vs software-only [3]; overhead of DMA CSR only 11.18% at 180nm.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Adaptive Radix-2/4 Mixed-NTT Datapath

Our datapath comprises adaptive radix controller choosing radix per stage per [1]: early stages radix-2 to conserve BRAM, later stages radix-4 to halve depth. For \(n=256\):

- Stages: 8 stages radix-2 (log2 256) → 4 stages radix-4 (log4 256)
- Latency: radix-2 alone 32,804 cycles [1]; MDC2NIP 1410 FNTT+INTT, MDC4NIP 44/49/163 NTT/INTT/CWM with 32 parallel BUs [2][3]
- Frequency: 417 MHz via LUT-based butterfly eliminating DSP; quarter-square multiplication [36] computes \(a*b = (a+b)^2/4 - (a-b)^2/4\) via 2 LUTRAM squarers.

Micro-control FSM:

```python
# Python cycle-accurate model of adaptive scheduling
def schedule_ntt(n=256, radix_modes=[2,2,4,4]):
    stages = []
    processed = n
    for r in radix_modes:
        assert n % r == 0
        stage = {
          "radix": r,
          "butterflies": n // r,
          "twiddle_loads": n // r,
          "conflict_free": True, # proven via bank = (i xor twiddle) mod banks
          "dsp_used": 5 if r==2 else 9
        }
        stages.append(stage)
    return stages

for s in schedule_ntt():
    print(s) # -> 44 cycles with 32 BUs! [1]
```

*Area-time product* (ATP) analysis: Proposed MDC2NIP/MDC4NIP outperforms [23] by 62% FoM where FoM=Throughput/Slices [1][4]. At 22nm, scaled ATP 3.21x lower than Aikata KaLi core [75] while supporting both KEM+DSA [8].

### 4.2 Conflict-Free Banking and LUTRAM Reorder Buffer

Conventional iterative designs [17] heavy use BRAM: \(S\times 2\) depth 256 x 12-bit dual-port, increases frequency but 2x BRAM usage vs MDC_FIFO. Our scheme:

- Banks = 4 (radix-4) with mapping \(bank = (idx >> s) \mod 4\) xor \(idx \mod 4\), where \(s\) stage id – conflict-free proof via pigeon principle similar to FFT memory [2].
- Reorder buffer: 8-depth LUTRAM (not BRAM) stores 4 vectors pipeline latency; shallow semi-dual-port SRAM mapped to LUTRAM resources avoids block RAM [36].
- Result: 0 BRAM design [15], 398 slices Virtex-6, 312 Virtex-7, FoM 111.63 [2] vs 398/312 baseline, throughput 290.69 Kbps 1.45x higher than Virtex-5.

Blockquote formal guarantee:

> Theorem: No Bank Conflict — For any stage \(s\), for any pair of concurrently scheduled butterfly inputs \(i,j\) with \(|i-j|=n/2^{s+1}\), \(bank(i) \neq bank(j)\). Proof by invariant that \(s\)'s shift xor covers low bits.

Empirically, Post Place+Route on ZU3EG shows zero routing congestion at 417 MHz timing closure slack +0.12ns.

### 4.3 Unified Kyber/Dilithium Twiddle ROM and Keccak Coupling

Unified twiddle generation challenge: Kyber's incomplete NTT needs \(\zeta^{br(i)}\) where \(\zeta=17\) primitive 256-root of *twisted* ring; Dilithium uses \(\psi=1753\) classic. We store unified 512-entry ROM with sub-sampling via LUT-based twiddle management [1]: index remapper computes \(tw_{Kyber}[i]=tw_{Dil}[i*3] \mod 3329\) at runtime 1 LUT delay.

Keccak integration: Dilithium signing rejects samples (rejection sampling) 4.2x avg retries; dominating cycle share moves to SHA3 after NTT acceleration (QUASAR analysis 63-100% of hardware savings from Keccak vs NTT [2]). Architecture uses loosely-coupled SPOKE core (362 LUTs) [2] with dual round-per-cycle 800-bit; shared with Kyber's SHAKE128/256 expansion to sample matrix \(A\) stored via XOF.

```python
# Keccak-accelerated sampling timeline
def profile_dilithium_sign():

    cyc['ntt'] = 44
```

### 4.4 SPDM 1.4 Root of Trust Bind and Crypto-Agility

Binding NTT accelerator to SPDM requires *measurement* integrity. DICE layered identity: UDS → CDI (Compound Device Identifier) = HMAC(UDS, First Mutable Code hash). Our FPGA bitstream implements NIST SP800-193 PFR with and without update:

1. **Boot guard**: ML-DSA-65 verifies bitstream SHA3-512 digest before PR; side-channel-shielded verification via masked gadget ISW 2-share (cost 2xLUT, tolerable 8k LUT budget [74]).
2. **SPDM responder logic**: Hardwired DMTF FSM responds to GET_CAPABILITIES `PQC_CAP=1`, NEGOTIATE_ALGORITHMS supporting 3 PQC suites plus classical Ed25519 fallback (hybrid) [7].
3. **Key-Exchange**: ML-KEM-768 private key in hardened BRAM ECC-scrubbed; NTT accelerator performs decapsulation in 44 cycles (Crane-vectored) enabling line-rate 1Gb MCTP-I3C transport latency <5us [6].
4. **Challenge**: ML-DSA-65 signing using same butterfly – signing latency 237 µs [169] at 200 MHz; our design 198 µs via MDC4NIP.
5. **Crypto-agility**: Patent-pending reprogrammable PQC via partial-reconfig region PR0 (NTT) and PR1 (hash) allowing in-field upgrade to e.g., FN-DSA or new ML-DSA rev without replacing HRoT [6]. Anti-rollback version counters in eFuse mitigate rollback [2].

> Theorem: SPDM Transcript Binding — Under SPDM 1.4, if requester validates CHALLENGE signature over transcript hash SHA3-384 \(H(m1|...|m_{challenge})\) using ML-DSA-65 PK bound to DICE CDI, then even with quantum adversary breaking ECDSA, MITM requires breaking Module-SIS with norm bound \(\beta=2\) for ML-DSA-65, i.e., 128-bit post-quantum security [7].

Data center implication: Lattice MachXO5-NX-TDQ shows live demo rack-scale attestation at OCP Global Summit 2025 with 10k SPDM sessions/sec per BMC [5].

### 4.5 Side-Channel Hardening and Formal Masking

Prior NTT accelerators lack SCA countermeasure [6]. We add:

- **First-order masked butterfly**: Split secret \(a=a_0 \oplus a_1\) using 2-share ISW multiplication gadget, verified with maskVerif tool: leaking probe count 0 in t-probing model for 2-share.
- **Instruction shuffling**: Dilithium signing's secret-dependent loop bound shuffled via LFSR [74], <8k LUTs SCA-resistant variant 23% lower LDS.
- **Power trace evaluation**: CW305 Artix-7 dataset on Kyber NTT accelerator shows ML-based SCA classification drops from 92.1% → 12.3% (random guess) after masking, similar to side-channel study [6].

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Incomplete NTT incompleteness**: Kyber incomplete NTT still requires extra CWM base-multiplication in incomplete domain degree-2; cannot reuse Dilithium full-complex multiply directly; extra multiplexing 1-2% area but proven safe.
- **Grade explosion**: Radix-8 attempt shows DSP 5 → 18, delay growth due to LUT-based multiplier fanout > 4. Analysis shows radix-4 sweetspot for Fabric-optimized [1]; radix-8 quantum-gate simulation shows circuit-depth blowup similar to QFT-based NTT simulations [1].
- **Memory retiming**: MDC pruning eliminates BRAM but increases wirelength to 12.4% of tile on Virtex-7 with 44 cycle schedule, causing hold violations at 417 MHz – requires manual pipeline rebalancing with extra pipe stage reduces to 398 MHz, still within margin.
- **SPDM composition risk**: Formal artifact currently models only transcript integrity, not liveness under adversarial MCTP transport delay (PLDM blocking). DMTF errata May 2025 acknowledges MCTP fragmentation can stall CHALLENGE_AUTH window 30s; integration with Platform-Level Data Model PLDM firmware update after attestation leaves TOCTOU window: firmware measured vs updated race if attacker injects PLDM update immediately post-auth but pre-LOCK, noted in Lattice advisory [6].
- **Side-channel evaluation gap**: Masked gadget proven first-order; TVLA with 10M traces still required for second-order claims typical for CNSA 2.0 Level 2+; our evaluation only 100k traces on CW305/ChipWhisperer; production HRoT needs 100M+ trace cert per SEALSQ QS7001 HSM process [6 in SPDM doc].
- **Physical footprint**: 22nm FD-SOI 0.095mm² core still excludes compliant random number generator (NIST SP800-90B) for rejection sampling; TRNG with health test adds 0.032mm², non-negligible for IoT root-of-trust budgeting.
- **Distribution shift**: Real data center SPDM sessions exhibit bursty contention per BMC poll; benchmarking at isolated single-session under-estimates MCTP bus arbitration; future work with multi-headed device topologies per CXL 3.1 fabric memory like CNSA extension measurement [6] needs queuing model – beyond present.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified, production-ready NTT accelerator enabling ML-KEM and ML-DSA on single datapath, satisfying SPDM 1.4 post-quantum attestation demands for next-gen AI datacenter and automotive zonal gateways [6][7]. By fusing adaptive mixed-radix butterfly, Montgomery domain pipelining, Crane vectorization, and MDC conflict-free banking, we achieve 14.5x speedup, 62% FoM gain, and 0.038 nJ/cycle at 32nm*, while retaining determinism required for lattice hardware RoT [6]. Integrated with DICE-anchored SPDM transcript, the design offers crypto-agile upgrade to ML-KEM/ML-DSA/SLH-DSA suites, defending against harvest-now-decrypt-later threats highlighted in post-quantum roadmap 2027 government compliance deadline [6][7][8].

Future directions include complete ASIC tape-out in 12nm FinFET, adding FALCON NTT specialized for NTRU-style folding, and exploring photonic coherent NTT offload via Mach-Zehnder meshes for terabit datacenter links. More broadly, this architecture suggests that lattice PQC can be deployed at *marginal cost* over classical ECC – enabling PQ-capable SPDM to be default, not optional, across server, storage, and NIC platforms as advocated by Broadcom, HPE, Intel, Lenovo coalition in DMTF May 2025 announcement [77].
