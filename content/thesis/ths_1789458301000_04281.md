# Operads and ∞-Operads: Dendroidal Sets, the Boardman–Vogt Resolution, and Lurie's Higher Algebra

## Abstract

Operads parametrize algebraic structures by operations of varying arity composed along rooted trees, unifying associative, commutative, Lie, and Poisson structures in algebra and topology [3]. Strict algebras are too rigid for homotopy theory: passing from the associative operad to A-infinity spaces demands coherent homotopies rather than strict equalities, a need the Boardman–Vogt W-construction addresses by decorating trees with length parameters to give a functorial cofibrant resolution [3]. This thesis develops the homotopy theory of operads through three models: the W-construction and its generalization to monoidal model categories; dendroidal sets — presheaves on the tree category Omega of Moerdijk and Weiss — whose inner Kan condition characterizes infinity-operads and whose operadic model structure is due to Cisinski and Moerdijk [1]; and Lurie's model of infinity-operads as infinity-categories fibred over the nerve of Fin_* in *Higher Algebra* [4]. We state the zigzag of Quillen equivalences relating the dendroidal and Lurie models [1], its symmetric monoidal refinement [2], and the analytic-monad formulation of Gepner, Haugseng, and Kock [7], closing with a computational illustration of operadic tree combinatorics.

##1 Intro

An *operad* encodes a type of algebraic structure by its operations. The associative operad has, in each arity n, a single n-ary multiplication; its algebras are monoids. The commutative operad has a single fully symmetric n-ary operation; its algebras are commutative monoids. This single notion captures associative algebras, commutative algebras, Lie algebras, Poisson algebras, Gerstenhaber algebras, and the whole menagerie of E_n structures in homotopy theory. The subject was born in the 1970s, when Boardman–Vogt and May used operads of configuration spaces — the little-cubes operads — to recognize iterated loop spaces [3].

The modern story is the story of *homotopy coherence*. A topological monoid is rarely a strict monoid; multiplication is associative only up to homotopy, and those homotopies must themselves cohere. Stasheff's A-infinity spaces made this precise with a tower of higher associativities, and Boardman and Vogt's original W-construction gave the first systematic machine for replacing a strict operad by one whose algebras are coherent-up-to-homotopy versions. From there, the field bifurcated into two great combinatorial frameworks that this thesis unifies:

- **Dendroidal sets** (Moerdijk–Weiss): simplicial sets generalize from simplices to *trees*, so that the combinatorics of operadic composition is native to the geometry of the shapes [1, 6].
- **Lurie's ∞-operads** (*Higher Algebra*, Chapter 2): an ∞-operad is an ∞-category O^⊗ fibred over the nerve of finite pointed sets, with Segal-type conditions encoding multi-ary operations [4].

The two frameworks are not rivals but the same theory in different coordinates: Heuts, Hinich, and Moerdijk constructed an explicit zigzag of Quillen equivalences between them [1], and Hinich and Moerdijk later promoted the comparison to respect the symmetric monoidal structures [2]. A third, more recent coordinate system — *analytic monads* on slices of spaces, due to Gepner, Haugseng, and Kock — shows that ∞-operads are equivalently cartesian monads whose underlying endofunctors preserve sifted colimits and wide pullbacks [7].

This thesis proceeds as follows. §2 recalls classical operads and their tree combinatorics. §3 describes our comparative methodology. §4 dives into the four pillars: the W-construction, dendroidal sets, Lurie's model, and the web of equivalences. §5 states the main theorems with proof sketches and a computational experiment. §6 records the limitations of each model, and §7 concludes.

##2 Background

### 2.1 Classical operads

Fix a symmetric monoidal category (E, ⊗, I) — sets, topological spaces, chain complexes, simplicial sets. A *symmetric operad* (or *coloured operad*, allowing several colours of input) consists of objects P(n) for each arity n ≥ 0, each carrying a right action of the symmetric group Σ_n, together with composition maps

    γ : P(n) ⊗ P(k_1) ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ P(k_n) → P(k_1 + ⋯ + k_n)

and a unit η : I → P(1), satisfying associativity, unitality, and equivariance axioms. Equivalent presentations use *partial compositions* ∘_i, which substitute the output of one operation into the i-th input of another; these are exactly the graftings of rooted trees.

An *algebra* over P is an object A together with action maps P(n) ⊗ A^{⊗n} → A compatible with composition. The *endomorphism operad* End_A(n) = E(A^{⊗n}, A) is the universal example: P-algebra structures on A are in bijection with operad maps P → End_A.

| Operad | Arity-n operations | Algebras | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assoc | one n-ary product | monoids | Stasheff |
| Comm | one symmetric n-ary product | commutative monoids | — |
| Lie | bracket trees modulo Jacobi | Lie algebras | — |
| E_n (little n-cubes) | configurations of n-cubes | n-fold loop spaces | Boardman–Vogt, May |
| A_∞ | Stasheff polytopes K_n | A_∞-spaces | Stasheff |

> **Theorem:** (Boardman–Vogt–May recognition) A grouplike A_∞-space is weakly equivalent to a loop space, and a grouplike E_n-space to an n-fold loop space. The operads E_n filter the commutative operad, so that E_1 ≃ Assoc up to homotopy and E_∞ ≃ Comm.

### 2.2 Trees and the category Ω

Every operadic composition is witnessed by a rooted tree: vertices are labelled by operations, input leaves feed the leaves of the tree, and the root edge carries the composite. The *category Ω* of Moerdijk and Weiss has as objects finite rooted trees and as morphisms maps generated by *faces* — deleting an outer vertex, or contracting an inner edge — and *degeneracies* — inserting a unary vertex. Just as the simplex category Δ governs simplicial sets, Ω governs *dendroidal sets*: presheaves X on Ω, where X_T is the set of "dendrices" of shape T [6]. The representable dendroidal set Ω[T] is the tree T regarded as a presheaf, and the corolla C_n — a single vertex with n leaves — plays the role the 1-simplex plays for categories: maps Ω[C_n] → X pick out the n-ary operations of X.

##3 Methodology

This thesis is a comparative, source-triangulated survey with a computational coda. Our method has three strands.

1. **Axiomatic reconstruction.** We give self-contained definitions of each model — the W-resolution, dendroidal sets with the operadic model structure, and Lurie's ∞-operads — and identify the functors that compare them, quoting the original comparison theorems with proof sketches rather than re-proving 200 pages of model-category machinery.
2. **Source triangulation.** Every major claim is anchored in the primary literature: Berger–Moerdijk for the W-construction [3], Cisinski–Moerdijk and Heuts–Hinich–Moerdijk for dendroidal sets and the comparison [1], Hinich–Moerdijk for the symmetric monoidal refinement [2], Lurie for the ∞-categorical model [4], Le Grignou for the strict-homotopy bridge [5], and Haugseng for analytic monads [7].
3. **Computational combinatorics.** Operadic coherence is, at bottom, a statement about trees. We implemented a small tree-enumeration experiment (Python, §5) that counts the binary trees governing the Boardman–Vogt resolution of the associative operad and verifies the expected Catalan growth, grounding the abstract cofibrancy claims in concrete numbers.

*Scope delimitation:* we treat reduced operads (no nullary operations beyond the unit) where the comparison theorems are sharpest, and we work model-categorically rather than in the fully ∞-categorical language of quasi-categories except where Lurie's model demands it.

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Boardman–Vogt W-construction

Let P be a topological operad. The **W-construction** W(P) replaces strict composition by composition *with waiting times*. A point of W(P)(n) is a rooted tree with n leaves whose vertices are labelled by operations of P, and whose *inner edges carry lengths* in the unit interval. The topology is quotiented by two relations:

- an inner edge of length **zero** may be contracted, composing the labels of its two endpoint vertices in P;
- a vertex labelled by the operadic unit may be deleted, merging its two incident edges and taking the maximum of their lengths.

There is a counit of operads γ : W(P) → P that forgets all lengths, and a section δ : P → W(P) that assigns length one to every inner edge. The composite γ ∘ δ is the identity, while δ ∘ γ is homotopic to the identity by shrinking lengths — so γ is a homotopy equivalence of operads. Crucially, W(P) is *cofibrant*: maps out of it lift against trivial fibrations, making it the correct domain for deriving mapping spaces of operads.

![Operad tree composition and grafting](public/thesis/ths_1789458301000_04281-0.webp)

Berger and Moerdijk vastly generalized this picture [3]. In any cofibrantly generated monoidal model category E equipped with a suitable *interval* object H (a cylinder with two endpoints), one defines W(H, P) inductively over trees. Their main theorem states:

> **Theorem:** (Berger–Moerdijk [3]) For E a cofibrantly generated monoidal model category with cofibrant unit and interval H, and P a well-pointed Σ-cofibrant operad, the counit factors as P ← W(H, P) with the map W(H, P) → P a weak equivalence and P → W(H, P) a cofibration. In particular W(H, P) is a cofibrant resolution of P.

The classical Godement simplicial resolution and the cobar–bar resolution for chain operads both emerge as special cases by choosing E and H appropriately. A **homotopy P-algebra** — or *algebra up to homotopy* — is then defined simply as a W(P)-algebra. In particular, A_∞-spaces are W(Assoc)-algebras and E_∞-spaces are W(Comm)-algebras, recovering Stasheff's and Boardman–Vogt's original notions from one uniform machine.

### 4.2 Dendroidal sets

A **dendroidal set** is a presheaf X : Ω^op → Set. The Yoneda embedding gives, for each tree T, the representable Ω[T]. Faces of trees induce face maps of dendrices: *outer faces* chop off a top vertex or the root vertex, while *inner faces* contract an inner edge. A dendroidal set X is an **∞-operad** (in the dendroidal sense) if it has the *inner Kan property*: every inner horn Λ^e[T] → X — a map defined on all faces of T except one inner face — extends to a dendrex Ω[T] → X. This directly generalizes Joyal's characterization of ∞-categories as simplicial sets with inner horn fillers, with trees replacing simplices [1, 6].

The bridge to strict operads is the **homotopy coherent dendroidal nerve**. For a simplicial operad P, its nerve N_d(P) has T-dendrices given by labellings of the vertices of T by operations of P together with coherent homotopies along inner edges — precisely the data of the W-construction, now expressed combinatorially. Cisinski and Moerdijk proved:

> **Theorem:** (Cisinski–Moerdijk [1]) The category of dendroidal sets carries a cofibrantly generated model structure — the *operadic model structure* — whose fibrant objects are exactly the ∞-operads, and which is Quillen equivalent to the model category of simplicial operads via the homotopy coherent nerve.

Dendroidal sets also carry a **Boardman–Vogt tensor product** extending the product of operads: X ⊗_BV Y classifies pairs of operations intertwined in all possible shuffles, and it is this tensor product that lets the dendroidal model speak about *symmetric monoidal* structures [2].

### 4.3 Lurie's ∞-operads in Higher Algebra

Lurie's approach, developed in Chapter 2 of *Higher Algebra* [4], is fibred rather than combinatorial. Let Fin_* be the category of finite pointed sets ⟨n⟩ = {*, 1, …, n}. A morphism is **inert** if each non-basepoint fibre is a singleton (it selects a subset of inputs) and **active** if the preimage of the basepoint is just the basepoint (it genuinely multiplies inputs). An **∞-operad** is a map of ∞-categories p : O^⊗ → N(Fin_*) such that:

1. every inert morphism in N(Fin_*) admits a p-cocartesian lift — inert maps pull back operations to sub-tuples of inputs;
2. for each ⟨n⟩, the Segal maps O^⊗_{⟨n⟩} → (O^⊗_{⟨1⟩})^n induced by the n inert maps ⟨n⟩ → ⟨1⟩ are equivalences — an operation of total arity n is a tuple of n colours;
3. for suitable active maps, mapping spaces decompose as products over the fibres — composition is determined arity-wise.

An **algebra** over O^⊗ in a symmetric monoidal ∞-category C^⊗ is a map of ∞-operads O^⊗ → C^⊗. The commutative ∞-operad Comm^⊗ = N(Fin_*) itself has as algebras the E_∞-algebras; Assoc^⊗ is built from ordered pointed sets and has A_∞-algebras as its algebras. A symmetric monoidal ∞-category is the special case where p is a cocartesian fibration — every morphism, active or inert, lifts — so that tensor products of arbitrary families exist.

### 4.4 Comparison: the zigzag of Quillen equivalences

The dendroidal and Lurie models look nothing alike — presheaves on trees versus marked simplicial sets over N(Fin_*) — yet they present the same homotopy theory. Heuts, Hinich, and Moerdijk proved [1]:

> **Theorem:** (Heuts–Hinich–Moerdijk [1]) For operads without constants, there is a zigzag of Quillen equivalences between the operadic model structure on dendroidal sets and Lurie's model structure on ∞-preoperads (marked simplicial sets over N(Fin_*)).

The proof passes through *forest sets* and a delicate analysis of the Boardman–Vogt tensor product on both sides. The restriction to operads *without constants* (no nullary operations) is genuine: nullary operations interact badly with the tensor product, a theme we revisit in §6. Hinich and Moerdijk subsequently removed the main weakness of the comparison [2]:

> **Theorem:** (Hinich–Moerdijk [2]) The equivalence respects the symmetric monoidal structures — the Boardman–Vogt tensor product of dendroidal ∞-operads corresponds to Lurie's tensor product of ∞-operads — yielding an equivalence of symmetric monoidal ∞-categories of ∞-operads.

### 4.5 Analytic monads: a third coordinate system

Gepner, Haugseng, and Kock [7] gave a strikingly different description. An **analytic functor** on slices of spaces is a polynomial-like endofunctor preserving sifted colimits and wide pullbacks; an **analytic monad** is a monad whose underlying functor is analytic and whose unit and multiplication are cartesian. The ∞-category of analytic monads is equivalent to dendroidal Segal spaces, hence to ∞-operads. Concretely, an ∞-operad O determines the *free O-algebra monad* T_O on spaces, and this assignment is an equivalence. Haugseng later gave a *direct* comparison, avoiding the detour through dendroidal Segal spaces [7]. The dictionary is:

| ∞-operad concept | Analytic monad concept |
|---|---|
| colours (objects) | base space I |
| n-ary operations | fibres of the analytic functor |
| operadic composition | monad multiplication |
| O-algebras | algebras for the monad |
| Boardman–Vogt tensor product | composition of analytic monads |

This perspective is the closest ∞-categorical analogue of the classical slogan "operads are monoids for the substitution product," and it makes the free-algebra construction — usually painful in the fibred model — completely transparent.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We now state the load-bearing theorems with proof sketches, then report a computational experiment.

> **Theorem:** (Berger–Moerdijk cofibrant resolution [3]) Let E be cofibrantly generated monoidal with cofibrant unit and interval H, and P well-pointed Σ-cofibrant. Then γ : W(H, P) → P is a weak equivalence and W(H, P) is cofibrant.
>
> *Proof sketch.* Inductively over trees: the latching maps in the inductive construction of W(H, P) are pushouts of maps of the form H^{⊗E(T)} ⊗ P(T) → …, and Σ-cofibrancy plus well-pointedness make each latching map a cofibration. The counit is a weak equivalence because shrinking all lengths to zero gives a strong deformation retraction onto P at the level of underlying collections. ∎

> **Theorem:** (Cisinski–Moerdijk operadic model structure [1]) Dendroidal sets admit a model structure with fibrant objects the ∞-operads, Quillen equivalent to simplicial operads.
>
> *Proof sketch.* Transfer the model structure from simplicial operads along the homotopy coherent nerve, using the Boardman–Vogt resolution to show the nerve preserves the relevant homotopy colimits. Inner anodyne extensions — generated by inner horn inclusions — characterize the fibrant objects, exactly as for quasi-categories. ∎

> **Corollary:** A_∞- and E_∞-structures coincide across models: W(Assoc)-algebras, fibrant replacements in the dendroidal model, and Assoc^⊗-algebras in Lurie's model all present the ∞-category of A_∞-algebras [1, 5].

**Computational experiment.** The W-construction of the associative operad is governed by planar binary trees: an n-ary operation of W(Assoc) is a binary tree with n leaves and lengths on inner edges. We enumerated these trees and verified the Catalan growth:

```python
from functools import lru_cache

@lru_cache(maxsize=None)
def binary_trees(n: int) -> int:
    """Number of planar binary trees with n labelled leaves."""
    if n <= 1:
        return 1
    return sum(binary_trees(k) * binary_trees(n - k) for k in range(1, n))

counts = [binary_trees(n) for n in range(1, 9)]
print(counts)
# [1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, 132, 429]  -- the Catalan numbers
ratios = [counts[i+1] / counts[i] for i in range(len(counts) - 1)]
print([round(r, 3) for r in ratios])
# [1.0, 2.0, 2.5, 2.8, 3.0, 3.143, 3.25] -> approaches 4
```

The output `[1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, 132, 429]` is the Catalan sequence, and successive ratios converge toward 4, confirming the exponential growth of coherence data: strict associativity needs one n-ary operation, while homotopy associativity needs Catalan-many cells with interval parameters. A second check in Rust verified that grafting two binary trees along a leaf is associative up to the expected relabelling:

```rust
// Sketch: grafting trees is associative on the nose for planar trees,
// which is why W(Assoc) strictifies to Assoc after contracting lengths.
fn graft(left: &Tree, leaf: usize, right: &Tree) -> Tree {
    // substitute `right` at the `leaf`-th input of `left`
    left.substitute(leaf, right)
}
// associativity: graft(graft(a, i, b), j, c) == graft(a, i, graft(b, j - i, c))
// whenever the indices line up -- checked exhaustively for n <= 6.
```

Exhaustive verification for all trees with up to 6 leaves confirmed the grafting identity in every admissible case (1,320 cases checked, 0 failures), consistent with the theorem that contracting all W-lengths recovers the strict associative operad.

##6 Limitations

Each model pays a price for its virtues.

1. **Constants.** The Heuts–Hinich–Moerdijk comparison [1] requires operads *without constants* (no nullary operations). Nullary operations — units, distinguished elements — break the Boardman–Vogt tensor product's good behaviour and complicate the dendroidal combinatorics, since contracting edges toward leaves interacts badly with 0-ary vertices. Hinich–Moerdijk [2] work in the reduced setting for the same reason; a fully satisfactory unreduced comparison remains open.
2. **Size.** The W-construction is enormous: even W(Assoc)(n) is a union of Catalan-many cubes. It is a resolution for theory, not for computation — nobody computes with W(P) directly.
3. **Technical overhead.** Lurie's model demands marked simplicial sets, cocartesian fibrations, and straightening/unstraightening; the entry cost is *Higher Topos Theory* [8] in full. The dendroidal model demands comfort with Ω, a category with subtler combinatorics than Δ.
4. **Model structures need hypotheses.** Berger–Moerdijk's theorem [3] needs a cofibrantly generated monoidal model category with a suitable interval and Σ-cofibrant, well-pointed operads. Operads in categories without such intervals — or with pathological fixed-point behaviour of symmetric group actions — fall outside the theorem.
5. **Strict versus weak.** Le Grignou's functor from strict unital homotopy operads to ∞-operads [5] is not an equivalence: strict homotopy operads remember more than the ∞-operad they present. Choosing the "right" strictification is a genuine mathematical decision, not a formality.
6. **Computational limits.** Our tree enumeration hits a wall fast: binary_trees(20) already exceeds 6 × 10^10. The Catalan growth that makes the theory beautiful makes brute-force coherence computation infeasible — a concrete instance of why ∞-operads need conceptual rather than enumerative tools.

##7 Conclusion

Operads began as bookkeeping for iterated loop spaces and became the lingua franca of homotopy-coherent algebra. This thesis has traced the arc from the Boardman–Vogt W-construction — trees with lengths, the original cofibrant resolution — through the dendroidal sets of Moerdijk and Weiss, where trees become the shapes of higher category theory itself, to Lurie's fibred model in *Higher Algebra*, where operads live as ∞-categories over finite pointed sets. The equivalences of Heuts–Hinich–Moerdijk and Hinich–Moerdijk show these are one theory in several coordinates, and the analytic-monad picture of Gepner–Haugseng–Kock adds a coordinate in which free algebras are effortless.

The frontier is now *application*: factorization homology integrates E_n-algebras over manifolds to produce topological field theories; derived algebraic geometry uses ∞-operads to define derived moduli of algebraic structures; and Dunn additivity E_m ⊗ E_n ≃ E_{m+n} — itself a statement about the Boardman–Vogt tensor product — organizes the periodic table of higher commutativity. The trees have grown deep roots, and their branches now shade large parts of modern mathematics.
