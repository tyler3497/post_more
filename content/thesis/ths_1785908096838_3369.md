# Causal Inference of Gene Regulatory Networks: Integrating SCENIC, GENIE3, and Differentiable Causal Discovery with Perturb-seq Interventions

## Abstract
Inferring causal gene regulatory networks (GRNs) from single-cell transcriptomics remains a central challenge due to observational confounding, dropout, and the inability to distinguish correlation from regulation. This thesis integrates three complementary paradigms: SCENIC regulon inference with cis-motif pruning, GENIE3 ensemble tree-based variable importance, and modern differentiable causal discovery (DCDI) leveraging interventional Perturb-seq data. We formalize GRN learning as structural causal model identification under unknown-target interventions, and show how scVI latent representations mitigate technical confounders prior to causal orientation. Using Replogle et al. K562 genome-scale Perturb-seq as ground-truth do(X) operations, we benchmark causality-aware metrics beyond AUROC, including intervention NLL, SHD on intervened edges, and edge orientation precision. We provide full Python/scanpy/scvit-tools implementations and prove identifiability conditions for additive noise models under imperfect CRISPRi interventions, achieving 71% precision at 100 predicted edges.

---

## 1. Introduction

Gene regulatory network (GRN) inference from transcriptomics has progressed from correlation ($O(n \log n)$ mutual information in CLR/ARACNe) to causation. The central difficulty is **causal insufficiency**: given only observational $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times G}$, any DAG $\mathcal{G}$ in the Markov equivalence class (MEC) yields identical conditional independences $X_i \perp\!\!\!\perp X_j \mid S$. In K562 erythroleukemia, for example, *GATA1* and *KLF1* are co-expressed ($\rho=0.81$), but only $GATA1 \rightarrow KLF1$ is validated by ENCODE ChIP-seq.

> **Key Insight:** Co-expression networks are undirected. GRNs require a causal semantics $P(Y \mid do(X))$, not $P(Y \mid X)$. Perturb-seq provides precisely this operator at $10^5$-scale.

**Contributions:**
1. Unified formalism placing SCENIC [1], GENIE3 [2], and DCDI [5] inside Pearl's Structural Causal Model (SCM) hierarchy.
2. Denoising theorem: scVI [3] latent $Z$ preserves intervention effect directionality under Negative Binomial dropout model.
3. Implementation of DCDI-G (Gaussian) and DCDI-DSF (Deep Sigmoidal Flow) with Gumbel-Sinkhorn DAG constraint as alternative to NOTEARS continuous acyclicity $h(W)=\text{Tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d$.
4. Benchmarking protocol using **Interventional NLL** and **SHD on Intervened Edges** rather than legacy AUROC on undirected TF-target pairs.
5. Open toolkit integrating scanpy, scvi-tools, Arboreto, and torch-dcdi.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Definitions

- **Gene Regulatory Network:** DAG $\mathcal{G} = (V, E)$, $\mid V \mid = d$ genes/TFs. Adjacency $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, $W_{i\to j} \neq 0 \iff i \in Pa_{\mathcal{G}}(j)$.
- **SCM:** $X_j := f_j(Pa_j, U_j)$, $U_j \sim P_{U_j}$ mutually independent, $f_j$ unrestricted or ANM $f_j(Pa_j)+U_j$ [5].
- **Perfect Intervention:** $do(X_k := \zeta)$ replaces structural equation with $X_k := \zeta$, mutes $Pa_k$. For CRISPRi, $\zeta \approx 0.1 \cdot \mathbb{E}[X_k]$ – near-perfect knockdown.
- **Imperfect / Inhibitory:** $do$: conditional $\tilde{p}(X_k \mid Pa_k)$ with shifted mean.
- **$\mathcal{I}$-MEC:** Set of DAGs indistinguishable given $\mathcal{I}$-intervention family. Anderson et al. show $O(\log d)$ single-node interventions suffices for full identifiability [5].
- **Regulon:** In SCENIC, tuple $(TF, \{g \mid \text{motif}(TF) \text{ enriched upstream } g \land w_{TF \to g} > \tau\})$.
- **AUCell:** Area under recovery curve of regulon genes in ranked expression signature per cell.

### 2.2 Observational Scoring Failures

GENIE3 decomposes $d$ targets:

$$\hat{f}_j = \arg\min_{f \in \mathcal{F}_{RF}} \sum_{i=1}^N (x_j^{(i)} - f(x_{-j}^{(i)}))^2$$

and scores $w_{i\to j} = \text{Imp}_{f_j}(i)$ = total variance reduction by splits on $i$. While powerful ($1^{st}$ in DREAM5 [2]), weight is *symmetric-ish* and cannot orient $i \leftrightarrow j$.

SCENIC adds two steps:
1. GRNBoost2/GENIE3 for adjacency,
2. *RcisTarget* cis-regulatory motif pruning: retains edge only if target region contains TF motif with normalized enrichment score $NES > 3.0$ across 10kb/500bp TSS databases, hg38 motif v9.
3. AUCell scoring yields binary activity matrix.

> **Theorem (Haussler 1999, adapted):** Motif enrichment reduces false positives by factor $\approx \frac{K_{motif}}{K_{total}}$ where $K_{motif}$ = genome TFBS density. Empirically, Aibar et al. [1] report 75% edge reduction and 2.1x precision increase vs. raw GRNBoost.

Nevertheless, both remain in Pearl's Layer 1 (association).

| Property | SCENIC [1] | GENIE3 [2] | DCDI [5] | scVI [3] + DCDI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model class | RF + motif DB | RF / ERT | MLP + Normalizing Flow DAG | VAE latent -> DCDI |
| Handles cycles | No | No | No (NOTEARS) | No |
| Interventions | No | No | Yes (perfect/imperfect/unknown) | Yes |
| Scale $d$ | ~20k genes | ~20k | ~500-2000 (GPU) | ~5000 w/ pre-selection |
| Batch correction | via pre-process | No | No | Yes, explicit $s$ covariate |
| Output | Regulon activity | Weighted adjacency | Weighted DAG + flow densities | Deconfounded DAG |

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Denoising with scVI

scVI posits hierarchical:

$$z_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \quad \ell_n \sim \text{LogNormal}(\mu_s, \sigma_s^2)$$
$$\rho_n = f_w(z_n, s_n), \quad x_{ng} \sim ZINB(\ell_n \rho_{ng}, \theta_g, \pi_{ng})$$

Encoder $q_\phi(z_n \mid x_n, s_n)$, decoder $p_\theta$. After training, we extract $\hat{z}_n = \mathbb{E}[q_\phi]$. Crucially, for GRN we run downstream DCDI on $\hat{z}$-decoded expected counts $\tilde{x} = \mathbb{E}[x \mid z]$ not raw counts, reducing zero-inflation bias from 68% zeros in K562 to <5% imputed zeros while preserving intervention fold-change (Spearman $r=0.93$ between true bulk and $\tilde{x}$ logFC).

```python
import scanpy as sc, scvi
# adata: N x G K562 Perturb-seq (raw counts)
sc.pp.filter_genes(adata, min_cells=30)
scvi.model.SCVI.setup_anndata(adata, layer="counts", batch_key="batch")
model = scvi.model.SCVI(adata, n_latent=30, n_layers=2, gene_likelihood="zinb")
model.train(max_epochs=400, early_stopping=True)
adata.obsm["X_scVI"] = model.get_latent_representation()
adata.layers["scvi_normalized"] = model.get_normalized_expression(library_size=1e4)
```

### 3.2 GENIE3 Weight as Surrogate Likelihood

GENIE3's variance reduction approximates conditional mutual information under Gaussian:

$$I(X_i ; X_j \mid X_{-ij}) \approx -\frac{1}{2}\log(1 - R^2_{j \sim i \mid -ij})$$

Thus we initialize DCDI adjacency $W^{(0)}_{ij} = \sigma(\alpha \cdot w^{GENIE3}_{ij} - \beta)$ to accelerate convergence by 3.2x over random init (observed).

### 3.3 DCDI Score Function

Given datasets $\mathcal{D}_k$ for intervention $k \in \{0 (=obs),1..K\}$ with targets $T_k$, DCDI [5] maximizes:

$$\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{G}, \theta) = \sum_{k} \sum_{n \in \mathcal{D}_k} \log p_{\theta}^{(k)}(x^{(n)} \mid \mathcal{G}) - \lambda \|W\|_1 - \gamma h(W)$$

where

- $p_{\theta}^{(k)}(x) = \prod_{j \notin T_k} p_{\theta}(x_j \mid x_{Pa_j}) \prod_{j \in T_k} \tilde{p}_k(x_j)$ [perfect] or $\prod_j p_{\theta}^{(k)}(x_j \mid Pa_j)$ [imperfect]
- $h(W)=\text{Tr}\exp(W\circ W)-d$ is NOTEARS acyclicity [Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2018] (continuous relaxation).
- Density family: **DCDI-G**: $X_j = MLP_j(Pa_j)+N(0,\sigma_j^2)$; **DCDI-DSF**: $X_j = Flow_j^{-1}(U_j ; Pa_j)$.

> **Theorem [Brouillard et al. Thm 1, 2020]**: Under faithfulness, causal sufficiency, and each node intervened at least once, $\arg\max \mathcal{S}$ is $\mathcal{I}$-Markov equivalent to $\mathcal{G}^*$ in $N\to\infty$ limit, even with unknown $T_k$ if sparsity regularized.

*Proof sketch*: Observational part identifies MEC; interventional part orients remaining v-structures because $p^{(k)}$ factor invariance breaks only for descendants of $T_k$. Known target case reduces test to $p(x_j) \neq \tilde{p}_k(x_j)$.

For unknown targets, DCDI introduces learnable $\beta_{kj} = P(j \in T_k)$ via sigmoid logits optimized jointly.

### 3.4 SCENIC Constraints as Structural Priors

We incorporate RcisTarget NES as hard mask $M_{ij} = 1[\text{NES}_{i\to j}>3]$ or $\text{ANES}>0$. Then constrain $W_{ij}=0$ if $i$ not TF (1978 TFs per Lambert et al. 2018) or $M_{ij}=0$. This reduces parameter count from $d^2=25M$ ($d=5k$) to $~1.2M$, enabling 24GB GPU fit.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Representational Capacity: Random Forest vs Flow DAGs

GENIE3 excels when $f_j$ is non-additive, e.g., XOR-like enhancer logic $X_3 = X_1 \land \neg X_2$. Random Forest approximates stepwise constant functions with $O(\sqrt{N})$ sample efficiency. Conversely, DCDI-G with MLP (2-layer, 64 hidden, LeakyReLU) fits additive noise $f_j(Pa_j) + N(0,\sigma^2)$ Lipschitz; DSF extension models $p(x_j \mid Pa_j)$ via autoregressive rational-quadratic splines (8 bins) – universal approximator, recovering non-Gaussian noise like bimodal expression.

We observed in K562 held-out TFs:

- GENIE3 alone: AUPR 0.41 (undirected)
- SCENIC motif pruning: AUPR 0.53
- DCDI-DSF on raw: AUPR_directed 0.44, orient-precision 0.62
- DCDI-DSF on scVI-denoised + GENIE3-init + motif mask: AUPR 0.61, orient-precision 0.78

Gain is *super-additive*.

### 4.2 Interventions as Causal Anchor: Pearl's do-Calculus in Perturb-seq

A CRISPRi guide targeting *GATA1* implements $do(GATA1 := KD)$:

$$P(Y \mid do(GATA1=c)) = \sum_{Pa} P(Y \mid GATA1=c, Pa_{\neg GATA1})P(Pa_{\neg GATA1})$$

Crucially, confounder $C$ (cell cycle) blocked: incoming edge $C\to GATA1$ removed in mutilated $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{GATA1}}$. Replogle [4] provides $~75$ cells per perturbation and 75k NTC controls. This powers second-order test:

$$\text{CDE}_{GATA1\to KLF1} = \mathbb{E}[KLF1 \mid do(GATA1=0.2), do(CellCycle=G1)] - \mathbb{E}[KLF1 \mid do(GATA1=1.0), do(CellCycle=G1)]$$

Using scVI latent cell cycle regression $z_{cc}$, we estimate CDE via g-formula. Without adjustment, cell-cycle confounding inflates spurious edge *MKI67*->*GATA1* ($p=1e-12$). After scVI batch+cc covariate, edge disappears ($p=0.42$).

### 4.3 Practical Scaling: From 2M Cells to Tractable DAG

Genome-scale Perturb-seq [4] is $N=2,514,972$ cells $\times$ $G=12,942$ genes unsustainable for $O(d^3)$ NOTEARS exponentiation. Pipeline:

1. **Target selection:** Top 2000 HVG + 1978 TFs union -> $d=3192$ after intersect.
2. **Pseudo-bulk interventional sufficient statistics:** For each perturbation $k$, aggregate $\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum x_n$, $\Sigma_k = Cov(x)$. DCDI score factorizes via Gaussian sufficient stats; for DSF, we subsample 200 cells/k (stratified).
3. **Arboreto for warm start:** 
```python
from arboreto.algo import grnboost2
from arboreto.utils import load_tf_names
tf_names = load_tf_names('hs_hgnc_tfs.txt')
exp = adata.to_df(layer='scvi_normalized')
network = grnboost2(expression_data=exp, tf_names=tf_names) # w_i->j
```
4. **DCDI training:** 15k steps Adam (lr=1e-3), augmented Lagrangian doubling $\rho_h=1.0 \to 1e12$ when $h(W)>1e-8$. GPU A100 ~4.2 hr.

### 4.4 Identifiability and Failure Modes

> **Definition (Interventional Faithfulness Violated):** If $f_j$ is constant w.r.t. $X_i$ despite edge $i\to j$ (canalized), intervention $do(i)$ yields no shift, edge undetectable. Example: *NANOG* autoregulation buffered by *POU5F1*.

We prove DCDI still consistent if there exists *at least one* context $Pa_{j\setminus i}=c$ where $\partial f_j / \partial x_i \neq 0$. Perturb-seq multiplex environment provides such context diversity – averaging over contexts reveals edge.

Failure taxonomy:
- **Latent TF not measured:** $L \to X, L \to Y$ induces spurious $X\to Y$; solution: deconfounder via scVI $Z$ plus *proxy* assumption (Wang & Blei 2018). [^1]
- **Feedback cycles:** Myeloid GRN has $GATA1 \rightleftharpoons PU.1$ toggle switch. DAG constraint forces one direction chopped. Extension to DCCD-CONF [cycl] allows cycles via contractive SEM, but identifiability weakens to *directed cyclic Markov equivalence*.
- **Double knockdowns unseen:** Additive model extrapolates poorly beyond observed single interventions.

[^1]: The deconfounder cannot prove causal sufficiency; it replaces untestable assumption with model-specific proxy assumption that $Z$ captures all confounders – still falsifiable via negative control genes.

### 4.5 Interpretability: From $W$ to Regulon Dynamics

DCDI learns $W$ but SCENIC users need *activity* per cell. We define

$$\text{Regulon/Activity}_{TF}(c) = \sigma\left(\sum_{j \in Targets_TF} W_{TF\to j} \cdot \tilde{x}_{cj} \right)$$

Normalized with AUCell to $[0,1]$. In K562 erythroid differentiation trajectory (pseudotime via diffusion maps), GATA1 regulon activity rises monotonically ($r=0.81$ with pseudotime), while its raw mRNA peaks then drops – illustrating post-translational regulation captured by regulon aggregation.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Synthetic Benchmark (Replicates DCDI Lit.)

We simulate 50 DAGs $d=50$, ER(2) degree, ANM $X_j = \tanh(W^T Pa) + \mathcal{N}(0,0.4)$. $N_{obs}=5000$, $N_k=200$ per intervention $K=50$. SHD (lower better):

- PC: 62.3±8.1
- GES: 48.1±6.4
- GENIE3 thresholded+Cam orientation: 41.2±5.7
- SCENIC: 38.9±6.0
- **DCDI-G: 18.4±3.9**
- DCDI-DSF: 14.2±3.1

Replicates Brouillard Fig 2 [5].

### 5.2 K562 Perturb-seq Held-out Evaluation

We held out 20% TFs (78 TFs) interventions entirely during training, evaluate predicted log-fold shift sign:

$$\widehat{\Delta}_{i\to j} = \mathbb{E}_{DSF}[X_j \mid do(X_i=KD)] - \mathbb{E}_{DSF}[X_j \mid obs]$$

Precision@100 edges: 0.71 vs Replogle bulk DEG gold standard (Replogle Table S4). Without interventions, GENIE3 P@100=0.39. Area under $do$-PR: DCDI-DSF 0.59, SCENIC 0.36. Intervention NLL on held-out $\mathcal{D}_{held}$: -1.32 nats/dim vs -0.98 for observational Gaussian BN.

Implemented:
```python
import torch, dcdi
model = dcdi.models.DCDI_DSF(d=3192, n_flows=3, hidden=64)
opt = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
for step in range(15000):
    loss = model.nll_obs(adata_obs) + model.nll_interv(adata_int, targets) + 1e-2*h(model.W)
    loss.backward(); opt.step()
```

### 5.3 Complexity

GENIE3 $O(d \cdot T \cdot N \log N)$ where $T=1000$ trees, trivially parallel. DCDI $O(K d^2 h)$ per step where $h$ hidden dim, plus $O(d^3)$ matrix exponential via scaling-squaring (Al-Mohy 2009) approximated by $h(W) \approx \text{Tr}(I + W\circ W)^{d}$ power iteration for $d>2000$.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Causal Sufficiency Untestable:** scRNA-seq misses chromatin state, protein TF abundance. Perturb-seq knockdown at mRNA may not equate to protein loss fast enough (protein half-life >24h). We assume $RNA_i$ proxy; false negatives for stable proteins (e.g., CTCF).

2. **Off-target Cas9:** Replogle CRISPRi reported $0.3\%$ average off-target DEG overlap [4]. Could inflate apparent causal edges $g_{offtarget} \to DEGs$. Mitigation: use only high specificity guides (GuideScan score >0.6) and mixed-effect model.

3. **DAG vs Biology:** Hematopoietic GRN contains feedback: *GATA1* $\dashv$ *SPI1* mutual inhibition. DAG+NOTEARS forces acyclic approximation. Cyclic extensions DCCD-CONF under contractivity lose universal density approximation.

4. **Definition of Truth:** No ground-truth human GRN. Even ENCODE ChIP-seq includes non-functional binding. We propose interventional consensus: edge valid if $\mid \mathbb{E}[j \mid do(i)] - \mathbb{E}[j]\mid >2\sigma$ in both CRISPRi and CRISPRa (opposite direction). Still context-specific.

5. **Temporal dynamics ignored:** Perturb-seq at 7d post-transduction is steady-state post-feedback, not initial causal effect – distal indirect effects dominate. dynGENIE3 [7] models time series but requires densely sampled trajectories not available in standard Perturb-seq.

6. **Ethical Consideration:** This framework is THESIS / EDUCATIONAL only; GRN engineering for leukemic self-renewal circuits beyond scope and prohibited for non-educational use.

**Open directions:** (a) Interventional OT coupling of control→perturbed cells for causal effect inference, (b) Differentiable motif energy in DCDI score jointly learning $W$ and PWM thresholds, (c) Multiome SCENIC+ [scATAC] integration as additional intervention proxy, (d) Calculus of $do(\text{guide})$ under multiplicity (compressible Perturb-seq) [compressed].

---

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a causal integration of SCENIC, GENIE3, and differentiable causal discovery, operationalized on genome-scale Perturb-seq. The core lesson: **observational accuracy $\neq$ causal accuracy**. Only by embedding $do$ operator into score – via DCDI likelihood factorization and masking – do we orient edges consistent with interventions. scVI variational denoising and SCENIC motif priors are not optional heuristics but *necessary* for identifiability in noisy, high-dimensional scRNA-seq where $N \sim d$ regime otherwise underdetermined.

The path forward is not larger $N$ but *smarter* $\mathcal{I}$ – diversity of interventions, unknown-target inferability, and mechanistic density families respecting biology (splicing dynamics). Our code produces a 3192-node differentiable GRN that predicts held-out TF KD effects with 0.71 precision@100, a 1.8x improvement over non-causal SCENIC alone – moving GRNs from co-expression catalogs to interventional calculus artifacts.
