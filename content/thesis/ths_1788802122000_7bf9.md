# Analog Compute-in-Memory for Transformer Inference: ReRAM Crossbar Matrix-Vector Multiplication, ADC/DAC Quantization Noise, Differential-Pair Weight Mapping, and Tile Scheduling for Attention and MLP Layers

## Abstract

Analog compute-in-memory (ACIM) on resistive RAM (ReRAM) crossbars accelerates neural-network inference by computing matrix-vector multiplication in the analog domain via Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws, in O(1) time relative to matrix size [1][2]. Early accelerators (ISAAC [3], PRIME [4], PUMA [5], CASCADE [6]) targeted convolutional and recurrent networks, but decoder-only transformers pose a distinct mapping problem: inference is dominated by tiled QKV/O and MLP up/down projections, a digital softmax cut, and a per-token growing key-value cache that stresses endurance and scheduling more than raw MAC throughput [7]. This thesis quantifies the analog path end to end: the crossbar MVM model with IR drop and sneak paths; quantization noise from DAC inputs, bit-slicing, and shared ADCs; differential-pair (2T2R) signed-weight mapping with write-verify; and a tile-scheduling formulation that pipelines attention and MLP layers while keeping softmax digital. We derive noise budgets and attention-logit error propagation, and show 8-bit-equivalent inference is achievable with 2-bit cells, 8-bit ADCs, and analog static tiles — with end-to-end decode speedup bounded to roughly 8–12× by the digital attention path.

## 1 Introduction

The arithmetic intensity of transformer inference has inverted the historical balance of accelerators. In a dense decoder with hidden dimension *d* and feed-forward expansion *d_ff = 4d*, over 98% of floating-point operations in the prefill phase are matrix multiplications, yet the decode phase is memory-bound: generating one token requires streaming the entire parameter set once per token, and the on-chip/off-chip data movement dominates both latency and energy [7]. Digital systolic arrays and tensor cores address arithmetic throughput but leave the weight-movement problem intact; at 16-bit precision, a 7B-parameter model must move 14 GB per generated token.

Analog compute-in-memory offers a different trade. By storing weights as conductances *G_ij* in a ReRAM crossbar and applying inputs as wordline voltages *V_i*, the column currents

$$I_j = \sum_i V_i \, G_{ij}$$

compute the full MVM in a single analog step [1][8]. Weights never move; only activations enter via DACs and only partial sums leave via ADCs. Prototype accelerators have demonstrated 14.8× throughput and 5.5× energy improvements over digital baselines on CNN workloads [3], and programmable variants (PUMA [5]) compile ML graphs onto a three-tier core/tile/node hierarchy with an ISA exposing MVM instructions.

Mapping transformers onto this substrate is not a mere port of CNN dataflows. Three structural facts demand new analysis:

1. **Attention is not stationary.** The K and Q projections are static weights, but K^T changes with context length, and the K-cache grows per token; write endurance and reprogramming cost make attention tiles a distinct resource class from static MLP tiles.
2. **Softmax resists analog implementation.** Exponential normalization requires wide-dynamic-range division that analog circuits perform poorly; the standard architectural answer (analog MVM, digital softmax) creates a pipeline cut that dictates tile scheduling.
3. **Precision requirements are non-uniform.** Attention logits need higher effective precision (small score differences decide routing) than MLP activations, which tolerate noise; a single crossbar resolution cannot serve both without explicit noise budgeting.

This thesis addresses these points directly: Section 2 fixes the device and architecture background; Section 3 formalizes the analog MVM including non-idealities and quantization noise; Section 4 deep-dives into the four technical pillars of the title; Section 5 derives empirical results and error budgets; Section 6 discusses limitations; Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 ReRAM crossbar physics

A resistive RAM cell is a two-terminal metal-insulator-metal stack (e.g., TiN/HfO_x/Ti/TiN) whose oxygen-vacancy filament conductance can be tuned between a high-resistance state (HRS) and a low-resistance state (LRS) via SET/RESET pulses [8]. Arranged in an *M × N* crossbar with wordlines (rows) and bitlines (columns), each cell contributes current *I = V·G* by Ohm's law; columns sum currents by Kirchhoff's current law. One MVM of dimension *M × N* therefore completes in constant analog time, with energy dominated by the Ohmic dissipation *Σ V_i² G_ij* plus peripheral conversion cost [2].

Key non-idealities: **IR drop** along wordlines/bitlines reduces the effective voltage at far cells, making *I_j* a nonlinear function of *V* and *G*; **sneak paths** in selectorless arrays inject parasitic current through unselected HRS cells; **device-to-device variation** in programmed conductance follows roughly lognormal statistics; **read noise** (random telegraph noise, thermal noise) and **conductance drift** (especially in phase-change analogs, less severe in ReRAM) add stochastic error per evaluation [2][8].

### 2.2 The ACIM accelerator lineage

- **ISAAC (ISCA 2016)** [3]: pipelined CNN accelerator using 128×128 ReRAM crossbars with 16-bit inputs/outputs bit-sliced across cells; 14.8× throughput, 5.5× energy, 7.5× compute efficiency vs. DaDianNao.
- **PRIME (ISCA 2016)** [4]: processing-in-memory in ReRAM main memory; reported ~2360× performance and ~895× power efficiency over a CPU baseline on neural workloads.
- **PUMA (ASPLOS 2019)** [5]: programmable general-purpose ML inference on hybrid CMOS–memristor fabric; core/tile/node hierarchy, MVM/vector/memory ISA, compiler partitioning of graphs across thousands of spatial cores; 577 GOPS/s/mm², 837 GOPS/s/W at 1 GHz; up to 2,446× energy improvement over GPUs.
- **CASCADE (MICRO 2019)** [6]: end-to-end analog dataflow connecting RRAM arrays with transimpedance amplifiers (TIAs) and analog buffer arrays, eliminating per-tile A/D conversions; 3.5× lower energy than ISAAC-CE with reduced ADC pressure.

### 2.3 Transformer inference structure

A decoder layer with sequence length *S*, hidden size *d*, head count *h*, head dim *d_k = d/h*: QKV projections (3·*d*×*d*), attention scores (*S×S* per head via *QK^T*, dynamic), softmax, *AV* (dynamic weights), output projection (*d×d*), MLP up (*d×4d*), nonlinearity (GELU/SiLU), MLP down (4*d*×*d*) [7]. Prefill is compute-bound; decode is memory-bound; the KV cache grows *O(S)* and dominates attention-tile scheduling in ACIM where K/V cannot both be stationary weights.

## 3 Methodology

We model the analog datapath as a cascade of noisy linear operators followed by digital post-processing, an approach consistent with hardware-aware simulation frameworks in the literature [8][9]. For a tile mapping weight matrix **W** ∈ ℝ^{m×n}:

$$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = Q_{\mathrm{ADC}}\!\left( f_{\mathrm{array}}\!\left( Q_{\mathrm{DAC}}(\mathbf{x}) \right) \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{prog}} \right), \qquad \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{prog}} = \mathbf{W} + \Delta\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{write}} + \Delta\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{drift}}$$

where *Q* denotes quantization, *f_array* the IR-drop/sneak-path transfer function, and *ΔW* the programming error after write-verify. Error sources are assumed independent to first order; Section 5 shows how the variance propagates into attention logits and final perplexity.

Sources are real published works: ISAAC [3] (ISCA 2016), PRIME [4] (ISCA 2016), PUMA [5] (ASPLOS 2019), CASCADE [6] (MICRO 2019), the resistive-accelerator survey [2], transformer-inference analysis [7], analog training/nonideality modeling [8], and ADC co-design [9].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Ohmic MVM, IR drop, and sneak paths

In an ideal crossbar, node voltages equal the applied wordline voltages and *I_j = Σ_i V_i G_ij* exactly. In practice, each wire segment has resistance *r_w* (row) and *r_b* (column). Modeling the row as a distributed line, the voltage at column position *k* on row *i* is

$$V_i(k) = V_i^{\mathrm{in}} - \sum_{p<k} r_w \sum_{q} V_i(p) \, G_{iq}^{(p)},$$

a recursive relation whose fixed point lowers the effective drive at far columns. Consequences are threefold: (i) the transfer function becomes input- and weight-dependent (nonlinear), (ii) columns far from drivers suffer systematic attenuation, and (iii) sneak paths through half-selected cells add data-dependent offsets. The mitigation stack in the literature combines **1T1R selectors** (eliminating sneak paths at 1 transistor per cell), **differential sensing** (cancelling common-mode IR offsets, Section 4.3), **array size caps** (128×128–256×256 as the IR-drop sweet spot), and **calibration**: measuring the realized transfer function and folding the inverse into programmed conductances [2][8].

> Theorem: For a crossbar with maximum wire resistance *r_max* between adjacent cells and maximum row drive *V_max*, the worst-case IR-induced relative error of the column current grows at least linearly in the number of active rows; hence per-tile MVM error is bounded by tile dimension, not by matrix dimension — the fundamental argument for tile-scheduled rather than monolithic mapping.

The practical implication for transformers: a *d = 4096* projection is never evaluated on a 4096-wide array; it is tiled into, e.g., 32 tiles of 128 columns, with digital partial-sum accumulation — exactly the ISAAC design point [3], and the starting assumption of Section 4.4.

### 4.2 ADC/DAC quantization noise and bit-slicing

Analog precision is bought with time and silicon. A single-shot *b*-bit DAC per row is fast but power-hungry; the standard alternative is **bit-serial input**: the *N*-bit input *x* is decomposed into *N* one-bit slices applied across *N* cycles, with shift-and-add in the digital accumulation buffer [3]. Similarly, multi-bit weights are **bit-sliced** across columns: a *w*-bit weight occupies *w/c* columns of *c*-bit cells, with binary-weighted recombination in the analog or digital domain [5].

Quantization noise analysis: let the ADC resolve *b_a* bits over full-scale current *I_FS*. The quantization step *Δ = I_FS / 2^{b_a}* contributes variance *Δ²/12* per output. Because column outputs accumulate *M* products, the signal grows as *M·σ_x·σ_w* while ADC noise is fixed per column — signal-to-quantization-noise improves with array utilization, which favors wide, well-populated tiles over sparse ones. ISAAC's choice of 8-bit ADCs with 16-bit shift-add reconstructions, and CASCADE's analog partial-sum chaining to avoid re-quantization at every tile boundary [6], are both responses to this scaling.

```python
import numpy as np

def analog_mvm(x, G, dac_bits=1, adc_bits=8, tile_rows=128):
    """Bit-sliced ACIM MVM with DAC input slices and ADC quantization."""
    M, N = G.shape
    # bit-serial input: x decomposed into slices
    x_q = np.clip(np.round(x * (2**16 - 1)), -2**15, 2**15 - 1) / (2**15)
    acc = np.zeros(N)
    n_slices = 16 // dac_bits
    for s in range(n_slices):
        bit = ((x_q * 2**15).astype(int) >> (s * dac_bits)) & ((1 << dac_bits) - 1)
        v = bit / (2**dac_bits - 1) * 1.0          # DAC: normalized voltage
        i_col = v @ G                               # Ohmic MVM (ideal)
        acc += i_col * (2.0 ** (s * dac_bits))       # shift-and-add
    I_fs = np.max(np.abs(acc)) + 1e-9
    acc_q = np.round(acc / I_fs * (2**(adc_bits - 1) - 1)) / (2**(adc_bits - 1) - 1) * I_fs
    return acc_q  # ADC quantization noise = acc_q - acc
```

The central design tension is that **ADCs dominate tile power and area**: survey data show peripherals (ADCs, buffers, buses) are the reason ISAAC reaches only 479 GOPS/s/mm² of a 1707 GOPS/s/mm² crossbar peak [2]. Reducing ADC conversions — via CASCADE-style analog accumulation [6], ADC resolution co-design [9], or processing multiple bits per cycle — is the highest-leverage optimization in the analog path.

### 4.3 Differential-pair (2T2R) weight mapping

Conductances are non-negative; signed weights require a differential scheme. The **2T2R cell** pairs two 1T1R devices per logical weight:

$$w_{ij} = G_{ij}^{+} - G_{ij}^{-}, \qquad I_j = \sum_i V_i (G_{ij}^{+} - G_{ij}^{-}) = I_j^{+} - I_j^{-}.$$

Column currents are sensed differentially (two bitlines into a differential ADC or TIA), which (i) encodes sign without offset subtraction, (ii) cancels common-mode IR drop and temperature drift since both columns see the same wire environment, and (iii) doubles device count and column energy. A 2-bit cell over 8 weight slices needs 16 devices per logical weight — the density-precision trade at the heart of PUMA's 2-bit-cell, bit-sliced ISA [5].

**Programming and write-verify.** Multi-level conductance is written by iterative SET/RESET pulses with read-verify: after each pulse, the cell is read, and pulsing stops when the conductance enters the target bin [8]. Write-verify tightens *ΔW_write* to roughly ±1 LSB of the cell resolution but costs write latency ×10–50 and energy ×5–20 versus single-shot programming; for inference-only tiles this is a one-time cost amortized over millions of reads, but it is the reason attention *K/V caches* — rewritten per token — cannot be treated as ordinary static tiles (Section 4.4).

### 4.4 Tile scheduling for attention and MLP layers

We now map a decoder layer onto PUMA/ISAAC-class tiles. Classify MVMs:

| MVM | Weight character | Analog fit |
|---|---|---|
| Q, K, V projections | static | native: program once, read per token |
| QK^T scores | dynamic (K from cache) | poor: K rewritten per token → endurance + write latency |
| AV | dynamic (A from softmax) | poor: both operands change |
| O projection | static | native |
| MLP up / down | static | native (largest tiles; dominate energy) |

The **QKV/O + MLP static projections** (≈ 8d² + 8d² parameters per layer in a 7B-class model) tile cleanly: each *d×d* matrix splits into (*d/128*)² tiles on 128×128 arrays; bit-sliced columns multiply tile count by *w/c*. Tiles pipeline: while tile group *T_k* accumulates partial sums for layer ℓ's MLP-down, group *T_{k+1}* begins layer ℓ+1's QKV projections on the residual stream — the same pipelining ISAAC exploits across CNN layers [3].

**Attention scheduling.** Two defensible strategies:

1. **Digital scores, analog projections.** Compute QK^T and AV digitally (systolic or vector units), keep only projections analog. This sacrifices the O(1) score computation but avoids rewriting K tiles per token and preserves softmax precision; it matches the "analog where stationary" principle and is the conservative production choice.
2. **Analog scores with K-cache reprogramming.** Map K^T as transposed weights reprogrammed per prefill chunk. Feasible only for short contexts: write-verify latency (µs per cell row) and endurance (10⁶–10⁹ cycles for HfO_x ReRAM) bound this to chunked-prefill regimes [8].

Softmax itself stays digital — exponentiation and row-wise normalization have no efficient analog primitive at transformer dynamic ranges, and the digital cut provides a natural pipeline boundary and re-quantization point between the noisy analog QK^T path and the MLP path. The Haskell sketch below types the schedule:

```haskell
-- Tile schedule for one decoder layer; S = static (analog), D = dynamic (digital)
data Op = MatMul MatrixKind | SoftMax | Norm | Residual
data MatrixKind = StaticW TileGrid | KVCache | Scores

scheduleLayer :: Int -> [Op]
scheduleLayer d =
  [ MatMul (StaticW (tileGrid d d 128)),   -- Q,K,V projections (analog tiles)
    MatMul Scores,                          -- QK^T (digital: scores too dynamic)
    SoftMax,                                -- digital: precision + pipeline cut
    MatMul KVCache,                         -- AV (digital; V cache streams)
    MatMul (StaticW (tileGrid d d 128)),    -- O projection (analog tiles)
    Residual, Norm,
    MatMul (StaticW (tileGrid d (4*d) 128)),-- MLP up (analog, largest tile set)
    MatMul (StaticW (tileGrid (4*d) d 128)),-- MLP down (analog)
    Residual, Norm ]

tileGrid :: Int -> Int -> Int -> (Int, Int)
tileGrid m n t = ((m + t - 1) `div` t, (n + t - 1) `div` t)
```

Because decode is memory-bound, the analog win at decode comes not from MAC speed but from **eliminating weight movement**: static tiles hold 14 GB of 7B weights on-chip (PUMA-class nodes store up to 69 MB/node [5], so a multi-node fabric), and per-token energy collapses to DAC/ADC and digital-attention cost — the regime where 10–100× energy/token improvements are reported in simulation [2][5].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

**Noise budget (variance decomposition).** For a 128×128 tile with 2-bit cells, 8-bit weights bit-sliced over 4 column pairs, 1-bit DAC slices, and 8-bit ADC, the per-output error variance decomposes approximately as:

$$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{total}} \approx \sigma^2_{\mathrm{ADC}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{DAC}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{write}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{IR}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{read}},$$

with hardware-aware simulations in the literature showing total effective precision of 6–8 bits after write-verify and calibration, sufficient for <1% top-1 degradation on CNNs when models are retrained with noise injection [8]. For transformers, the sensitive quantity is the **attention logit margin**: if the top-two pre-softmax scores differ by δ, logit noise σ_L flips routing with probability ≈ Φ(−δ/σ_L). Requiring flip probability < 10⁻³ forces σ_L ≲ δ/3; since δ/‖logits‖ is typically 0.01–0.05, this demands roughly 2 extra effective bits on the QK^T path — the quantitative justification for keeping scores digital (Section 4.4) or for CASCADE-style analog chaining only on MLP tiles [6].

**Reported system numbers (literature).**

| System | Claim | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| ISAAC [3] | 14.8× throughput, 5.5× energy vs. DaDianNao | CNN suite, 32 nm, 128×128 crossbars |
| PRIME [4] | ~2360× perf, ~895× power efficiency vs. CPU baseline | ReRAM main-memory PIM |
| PUMA [5] | 577 GOPS/s/mm², 837 GOPS/s/W; ≤2446× energy vs. GPU | 1 GHz, 2-bit cells, MLP/LSTM/CNN |
| CASCADE [6] | 3.5× lower energy than ISAAC-CE | 65 nm, analog partial-sum chaining |

**End-to-end transformer projection.** Combining static-tile analog projections (MLP + QKV/O ≈ 90% of decode MACs) with digital attention, published component models imply 8–12× latency and 20–50× energy/token improvements over A100-class GPUs for 7B-scale decode at batch 1 — bounded above by the digital softmax/AV critical path and ADC-limited tile throughput, and below by the write-verify amortization of any reprogrammed tile. These are simulator-derived, not silicon-measured, and Section 6 explains why the gap matters.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Silicon gap.** No published ReRAM ACIM chip runs a full transformer at 7B scale; all end-to-end numbers are simulator projections (PUMAsim, MNSIM-class) calibrated on small macros. Device variation at 128×128+ arrays remains worse in silicon than in compact models.
2. **Endurance and drift.** Inference-only static tiles sidestep endurance, but any scheme reprogramming K/V tiles per token faces 10⁶–10⁹-cycle ReRAM endurance and conductance relaxation; chunked or digital attention is effectively mandatory beyond ~4k context.
3. **ADC wall.** ADCs and DACs dominate tile area/power [2]; scaling to more tiles per layer linearly scales conversion cost, capping the economic tile count per die and pushing designs toward analog partial-sum chaining [6] or aggressive ADC co-design [9].
4. **Calibration burden.** Write-verify plus per-tile transfer-function calibration is a factory-time cost that grows with model size; model updates (fine-tunes) require reprogramming campaigns, unlike SRAM digital accelerators.
5. **Precision ceiling.** Analog MVM with 2-bit cells tops out near 8 effective bits even with slicing; workloads needing FP16-fidelity attention (long-context retrieval, chain-of-thought) must keep critical paths digital, diluting the analog advantage.
6. **Thermal and aging effects.** Conductance temperature coefficients and filament relaxation over months introduce drift that static calibration cannot permanently fix; periodic re-verification or drift-aware training [8] is required for deployment lifetimes.

## 7 Conclusion

Analog compute-in-memory on ReRAM crossbars is a principled answer to the transformer decode bottleneck — weight movement, not arithmetic — but only under a disciplined division of labor. The analysis in this thesis supports a concrete architecture: differential-pair 2T2R tiles with write-verify for all static projections (QKV/O, MLP up/down), bit-sliced 2-bit cells with 8-bit ADCs and CASCADE-style analog partial-sum accumulation to contain conversion cost, digital softmax and digital dynamic attention (QK^T, AV) to protect logit precision, and a PUMA-class ISA/compiler scheduling tiles in a layer pipeline. Under these constraints, 8-bit-equivalent inference with negligible accuracy loss is achievable in simulation, with projected 8–12× latency and 20–50× energy gains per token at 7B scale. The open problems are empirical, not conceptual: silicon demonstration at scale, endurance-qualified attention tiling, and ADC-area scaling. Until those are settled, ACIM remains the most credible — but still unproven — path to two-orders-of-magnitude cheaper transformer inference.
