# Variational Quantum Algorithms: VQE, QAOA, Barren Plateaus, and the Trainability Frontier of NISQ Optimization

## Abstract

Variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) — parameterized quantum circuits optimized by a classical loop — are the dominant algorithmic paradigm of the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era. This thesis develops a unified account of their two canonical instantiations, the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) and the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA), and confronts them with the phenomenon that most threatens their scalability: **barren plateaus**, regions of the training landscape where gradients vanish exponentially with system size. We trace the variational hybrid architecture from its 2014 origins through the 2018 discovery of gradient collapse in random deep circuits, the 2021 refinement that cost-function *locality* decides trainability even at shallow depth, and the sobering proof that hardware noise alone induces plateaus independent of initialization. We formalize the expressibility–trainability tradeoff, derive the gradient-variance scaling laws, and survey the mitigation frontier — local cost functions, structured ansätze, smart initialization, and quantum natural gradients. The thesis closes with empirical case studies and the open question defining NISQ optimization: whether any variational protocol stays trainable where classical simulation fails.

## 1. Introduction

The decade since VQE's 2014 proposal [1] has been shaped by one pragmatic observation, articulated most influentially by Preskill [4]: the first useful quantum computers would be *noisy*, *intermediate-scale*, and incapable of fault tolerance — the NISQ regime. Algorithms demanding deep coherent circuits, such as phase estimation for quantum chemistry, were therefore set aside in favor of **hybrid quantum–classical** schemes: shallow parameterized quantum circuits evaluated on the device, with parameters optimized by a classical computer in a feedback loop [2].

Two such algorithms became the field's workhorses. **VQE** variationally upper-bounds the ground-state energy of a Hamiltonian by minimizing the expectation value of a trial state prepared by a parameterized circuit [1]. **QAOA** applies alternating layers of a problem (cost) Hamiltonian and a mixing Hamiltonian to solve combinatorial optimization problems such as MaxCut, with approximation quality improving as the circuit depth parameter *p* grows [3]. Both promise short circuits, tolerance to some coherent errors via variational absorption, and applicability to real devices.

That promise collided with a geometric obstruction found by McClean *et al.* in 2018 [5]: for a wide class of reasonable parameterized circuits, the probability that the gradient along any direction is nonzero to fixed precision is **exponentially small in the number of qubits** — a *barren plateau*, vast, flat, unnavigable. Later theory generalized the result: cost-function *locality* matters as much as circuit depth [6]; hardware noise alone induces plateaus no initialization can escape [7]; and the *expressibility–trainability* tension looks fundamental, not incidental.

This thesis has three aims: **(i)** a self-contained, mathematically precise treatment of VQE and QAOA as optimization procedures; **(ii)** barren-plateau theory from first principles — concentration, 2-designs, cost locality, noise-induced plateaus; **(iii)** the mitigation frontier, honestly assessed. One question runs throughout: *can variational algorithms be trained beyond classical simulability?* Our answer: not yet, and not without structural concessions.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The variational principle and the hybrid loop

The variational principle — $\\langle \\psi | H | \\psi \\rangle \\geq E_0$ for any normalized $|\\psi\\rangle$, with equality iff $|\\psi\\rangle$ is a ground state — becomes an algorithm in VQE [1]: a parameterized circuit $U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})$ prepares $|\\psi(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})\\rangle$; a quantum processor estimates $E(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})$; a classical optimizer (Nelder–Mead, COBYLA, SPSA, gradient-based) proposes new parameters; iterate until convergence or the shot budget is exhausted.

The quantum computer's job in this loop is **expectation-value estimation**. After a fermion-to-qubit mapping (Jordan–Wigner, Bravyi–Kitaev, parity), molecular Hamiltonians decompose into Pauli strings,

$$H = \\sum_{j=1}^{M} h_j P_j, \\qquad P_j \\in \\{I, X, Y, Z\\}^{\\otimes n},$$

with $M = \\mathcal{O}(n^4)$ for second-quantized chemistry. Each $\\langle P_j \\rangle$ is estimated by repeated preparation and measurement (shots $\\sim \\epsilon^{-2}$ per term); grouping commuting terms for joint measurement mitigates the overhead, but the measurement problem remains VQE's most persistent practical tax [2].

> **Theorem (Variational absorption of coherent error).** If the physical ansatz is $\\tilde{U}(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) = \\mathcal{E} \\circ U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})$ for a *parameter-independent* coherent error $\\mathcal{E}$, the classical optimizer automatically compensates for $\\mathcal{E}$ to the extent the ansatz manifold permits [2].

This made VQE seem NISQ-native — no error correction, only *learnable* noise. Section 4.4 shows this optimism was half right.

### 2.2 Parameterized quantum circuits and the parameter-shift rule

A general VQA optimizes

$$C(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) = \\langle 0 | U^\\dagger(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) H U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) | 0 \\rangle,$$

where $U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) = \\prod_{l=1}^{L} U_l(\\theta_l) W_l$ interleaves parameterized rotations with fixed entangling structure. Analytic gradients come bias-free via the **parameter-shift rule**: if $U_l(\\theta_l) = e^{-i\\theta_l G}$ with $G$ having eigenvalues $\\pm r$, then

$$\\frac{\\partial C}{\\partial \\theta_l} = r\\,\\big[\\,C(\\boldsymbol{\\theta} + \\tfrac{\\pi}{4r}\\hat{e}_l) - C(\\boldsymbol{\\theta} - \\tfrac{\\pi}{4r}\\hat{e}_l)\\,\\big].$$

For Pauli generators ($r = 1/2$) each gradient component costs exactly two circuit evaluations — which made gradient-based VQA training *look* cheap. The barren-plateau literature is the story of why this cheapness is illusory: the gradient exists, but its *magnitude* collapses.

### 2.3 The NISQ constraint surface

Preskill's NISQ characterization [4] fixes the operating envelope: tens to hundreds of physical qubits, $10^{-2}$–$10^{-3}$ gate error rates, coherence permitting tens to hundreds of gate layers, no error correction. Variational algorithms are essentially the *only* near-term advantage candidates — but must satisfy three constraints:

1. **Shallow depth**: circuits must fit inside the coherence window, limiting expressibility.
2. **Modest shot budgets**: each expectation value costs shots; optimization over hundreds of parameters multiplies that cost.
3. **Trainability**: the landscape must have gradients resolvable above shot noise — the subject of this thesis's central chapters.

### 2.4 Ansatz taxonomy

| Ansatz | Structure | Strengths | Trainability concerns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hardware-efficient | Alternating single-qubit rotations + fixed entangling layer | Matches device connectivity; short depth | Deep versions form approximate 2-designs → plateaus [5] |
| UCCSD (unitary coupled cluster) | $e^{T - T^\\dagger}$ with single/double excitations | Chemically motivated; compact for molecules | Deep, gate-heavy; noise-induced plateaus [7] |
| QAOA / alternating operator | $\\prod_k e^{-i\\beta_k B} e^{-i\\gamma_k C}$ | Problem-structured; few parameters ($2p$) | Reachability deficits at fixed $p$; noise plateaus [7] |
| ADAPT-VQE | Iteratively grown operator pool | Compact, problem-adapted | Greedy growth cost; gradient screening overhead |
| QCNN (quantum convolutional) | Hierarchical local unitaries + pooling | Provably plateau-free for local costs | Limited expressibility by design |

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Formal setup

We study VQAs as non-convex stochastic optimization over the unitary group, analyzing *gradient statistics* of $C(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})$ under parameter distributions (typically uniform on $[0, 2\\pi)$). The key quantity is

$$\\mathrm{Var}_{\\boldsymbol{\\theta}}\\left[\\frac{\\partial C}{\\partial \\theta_k}\\right],$$

the variance of a partial derivative. By Chebyshev's inequality, exponentially small variance means $|\\partial_k C| < \\epsilon$ with overwhelming probability for any fixed $\\epsilon$ — resolving the gradient then needs exponentially many shots: **the optimizer is blind**.

### 3.2 Measuring trainability numerically

We adopt the standard numerical methodology of [5, 6]:

1. **Ansatz instantiation**: layered circuits of depth $L$ with entangling blocks approximating unitary 2-designs.
2. **Gradient sampling**: draw $S$ parameter vectors, estimate each $\\partial_k C$ by the parameter-shift rule, and compute the empirical variance.
3. **Scaling fit**: repeat for $n = 2, 4, \\dots, 20$; fit $\\log \\mathrm{Var}$ vs. $n$ to distinguish exponential from polynomial decay.
4. **Cost ablation**: compare a global cost ($1 - |\\langle 0|^{\\otimes n} U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})|0\\rangle|^2$) against a local cost ($1 - \\frac{1}{n}\\sum_j \\langle Z_j \\rangle$) at matched depth.

The Python reference implementation below (§5) carries out steps 1–3 for a hardware-efficient ansatz, reproducing the exponential decay signature of [5].

### 3.3 Analytical instruments

Three tools recur throughout the proofs:

- **Unitary 2-designs**: ensembles $\\{p_i, U_i\\}$ reproducing the first two moments of the Haar measure; random circuits of sufficient depth form approximate 2-designs, enabling exact moment calculations of $\\mathrm{Var}[\\partial_k C]$.
- **Weingarten calculus**: integration of polynomial functions over the unitary group, used to evaluate $\\mathbb{E}[\\langle\\psi|H|\\psi\\rangle]$ and its derivatives under Haar-random $U$.
- **Concentration of measure (Levy's lemma)**: Lipschitz functions on high-dimensional spheres concentrate around their mean — in a $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space *almost every* direction looks the same, so derivatives along any fixed direction are almost always tiny.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 VQE: from photonic chips to the measurement wall

Peruzzo *et al.* [1] demonstrated VQE on a photonic chip, computing the HeH$^+$ ground-state energy curve — the first end-to-end proof that a quantum device plus a classical optimizer could do *ab initio* chemistry. McClean *et al.* [2] supplied the theoretical scaffolding: gradient-estimation complexity, error resilience, and measurement-problem scaling.

The mature VQE pipeline — Hamiltonian construction in a basis set, ansatz selection (UCCSD for fidelity, hardware-efficient for device fit), commuting Pauli-term grouping for measurement, gradient-based or gradient-free optimization, and error mitigation layered atop — carries a quantified cost at every stage. Measurement alone demands $\\mathcal{O}(M/\\epsilon^2)$ shots, and *chemical accuracy* ($\\sim$1.6 mHa) strains NISQ repetition rates: the **measurement wall**. Yet the deepest difficulty is not shots per evaluation — it is that the *landscape itself* may carry no usable signal, a discovery that reframes every stage above.

### 4.2 QAOA: alternating unitaries and the adiabatic connection

Farhi, Goldstone, and Gutmann [3] introduced QAOA for combinatorial optimization: with a diagonal cost Hamiltonian $H_C|z\\rangle = C(z)|z\\rangle$ and mixer $H_B = \\sum_j X_j$, the depth-$p$ state is

$$|\\boldsymbol{\\gamma}, \\boldsymbol{\\beta}\\rangle = \\prod_{k=1}^{p} e^{-i\\beta_k H_B}\\, e^{-i\\gamma_k H_C}\\, |+\\rangle^{\\otimes n},$$

and the classical loop maximizes $\\langle H_C \\rangle$ over $2p$ angles. This is a **Trotterized adiabatic path**: as $p \\to \\infty$ it recovers the exact optimum. For $p = 1$ MaxCut on 3-regular graphs, Farhi *et al.* proved approximation ratio $\\geq$ **0.6924** — a genuine quantum guarantee, below the classical Goemans–Williamson 0.8786 bound.

Zhou *et al.*'s systematic study [8] is the definitive empirical complement. Key findings:

- **Heuristics beat random initialization exponentially**: INTERP-style strategies (interpolating optimal angles from depth $p$ to $p+1$) find quasi-optimal parameters in $\\mathcal{O}(\\mathrm{poly}(p))$ time versus $2^{\\mathcal{O}(p)}$ random restarts.
- **Depth helps**: approximation ratios on random graphs improve steadily to $p = 50$ in simulation, with stretched-exponential gains.
- **Non-adiabatic mechanisms matter**: QAOA succeeds on instances where adiabatic annealing fails from vanishing spectral gaps — variational freedom exploits diabatic transitions productively.
- **Reachability deficits**: at fixed $p$, the ansatz manifold may contain no good solution at all — an *expressibility* limit distinct from trainability.

QAOA thus exemplifies the central tension: its $2p$-parameter landscape is low-dimensional enough to optimize, yet its structured form is a special case of the noise-induced plateau theorems [7] — structure grants no immunity.

### 4.3 Barren plateaus: the geometry of gradient collapse

The McClean *et al.* [5] result, stated precisely:

> **Theorem (Barren plateaus, McClean *et al.* 2018).** Consider a cost $C(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) = \\langle 0|U^\\dagger(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) H U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})|0\\rangle$ where $U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})$ is a layered ansatz whose blocks form independent local 2-designs (in particular, sufficiently deep random circuits), and $H$ is a global observable. Then for any parameter direction $\\theta_k$,
>
> $$\\mathrm{Var}_{\\boldsymbol{\\theta}}\\left[\\frac{\\partial C}{\\partial \\theta_k}\\right] \\in \\mathcal{O}\\left(\\frac{1}{2^n}\\right) \\quad \\text{(more precisely } \\mathcal{O}(b^{-n}) \\text{ for } b > 1\\text{)},$$
>
> so the probability that $|\\partial_k C| \\geq \\epsilon$ is exponentially suppressed in $n$ for any fixed $\\epsilon > 0$ [5].

The intuition is concentration of measure: a deep random circuit explores Hilbert space nearly uniformly, the cost concentrates around its mean, and a nearly-constant function has nearly-zero gradient nearly everywhere. Resolving a gradient of magnitude $\\sim 2^{-n/2}$ against shot noise $\\sim 1/\\sqrt{N_{\\mathrm{shots}}}$ needs $N_{\\mathrm{shots}} \\sim 2^n$ — **exponential measurement overhead per optimization step**.

Cerezo *et al.* [6] then proved the unexpected: the circuit need not be deep at all.

> **Theorem (Cost-function-dependent plateaus, Cerezo *et al.* 2021).** Under the same local 2-design block assumption, if $C$ is defined via a **global** observable (e.g., fidelity to $|0\\rangle^{\\otimes n}$), gradients vanish exponentially *even for shallow circuits* of $\\mathcal{O}(1)$ depth. If instead $C$ uses a **local** observable (acting nontrivially on $\\mathcal{O}(1)$ qubits), then for circuits of depth $\\mathcal{O}(\\log n)$ the gradient variance decays at worst **polynomially**, $\\mathrm{Var}[\\partial_k C] = \\Omega(1/\\mathrm{poly}(n))$ [6].

This is the most actionable theorem in the VQA literature: *the measurement operator is a trainability dial*. Global costs — fidelities, full-system observables — are untrainable at scale; local costs — sums of few-body terms, precisely the form of molecular Hamiltonians and MaxCut objectives — keep polynomial gradients at logarithmic depth, verified numerically up to **100 qubits**. For VQE and QAOA, whose natural costs are already local, a reprieve; for QML with global fidelities, an obituary.

The plateau mechanisms, taxonomized:

| Mechanism | Trigger | Scaling | Escapable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random-initialization BP [5] | Deep unstructured circuits ≈ 2-designs | $\\mathrm{Var} \\sim b^{-n}$ | Yes — structured init, shallow/local design |
| Cost-dependent BP [6] | Global observables, any depth | $\\mathrm{Var} \\sim b^{-n}$ | Yes — switch to local costs |
| Noise-induced BP [7] | Local Pauli noise + depth $\\Omega(n)$ | Gradient $\\sim q^{L}$ decaying | **No** — fundamental under the noise model |
| Entanglement-induced BP | Excessive entanglement → measure concentration | Related to above | Partially — restrict entangling structure |

### 4.4 The mitigation frontier and noise-induced plateaus

Wang *et al.* [7] delivered the sobering counterpart:

> **Theorem (Noise-induced barren plateaus, Wang *et al.* 2021).** Under local Pauli noise of strength $q < 1$ per layer, for a generic layered ansatz of depth $L$ (including QAOA and UCC as special cases), the magnitude of the cost-function gradient decays as $\\mathcal{O}(q^{L})$; if $L$ grows linearly with $n$, gradients vanish exponentially in $n$ **regardless of initialization, cost locality, or ansatz structure** [7].

NIBPs are conceptually distinct from noise-free plateaus: not about *where you start* but *what hardware does to the state* — depolarizing channels shrink every Bloch vector toward the maximally mixed state, flattening all landscape features indiscriminately, confirmed numerically for QAOA under realistic noise. The implication is stark: **adding depth to gain expressibility simultaneously destroys trainability**, and no ansatz evades the tradeoff — only less noise does.

The mitigation strategies that survive scrutiny:

1. **Local cost functions.** The Cerezo prescription [6]: few-body observables, depth $\\mathcal{O}(\\log n)$ — the cheapest, most robust intervention.
2. **Identity / layerwise initialization.** Start blocks near the identity or train layer-by-layer so the circuit never begins as a 2-design.
3. **Problem-inspired structure.** QAOA's alternating form, UCC excitations, symmetry-respecting ansätze restrict the manifold — at the price of reachability deficits.
4. **Quantum natural gradient.** Rescaling updates by the Fubini–Study metric accelerates navigation of narrow valleys, though it cannot resurrect a truly flat landscape.
5. **Noise-aware co-design.** Since NIBPs are fundamental under local noise [7], the durable answers are better hardware, mitigation that lowers $q$, and ansätze whose depth stays below the noise horizon.

The emerging picture is a **Pareto frontier** — expressibility (depth, entanglement, parameter count) versus trainability (gradient variance, shot efficiency). Every successful VQA sits at a negotiated point on this curve, never at both extremes.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 Reproducing the exponential decay signature

The script below implements the §3.2 methodology: a hardware-efficient ansatz of depth $L = n$, global cost $C = 1 - |\\langle 0|^{\\otimes n} U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta})|0\\rangle|^2$, and Monte Carlo estimation of $\\mathrm{Var}[\\partial_1 C]$ via the parameter-shift rule — reproducing the McClean *et al.* [5] exponential-decay signature up to the $n \\approx 10$ statevector limit.

```python
import numpy as np

def layered_ansatz(theta, n, L):
    """Hardware-efficient ansatz: RY rotations + linear CNOT chain, L layers.
    Returns the 2^n-dim statevector. theta has shape (L, n)."""
    psi = np.zeros(2**n, dtype=complex); psi[0] = 1.0
    for l in range(L):
        # single-qubit RY rotations
        for j in range(n):
            c, s = np.cos(theta[l, j]/2), np.sin(theta[l, j]/2)
            rot = np.array([[c, -s], [s, c]])
            psi = apply_single(psi, rot, j, n)
        # entangling layer: linear CNOT chain
        for j in range(n - 1):
            psi = apply_cnot(psi, j, j + 1, n)
    return psi

def apply_single(psi, gate, target, n):
    psi = psi.reshape([2]*n)
    psi = np.tensordot(gate, psi, axes=([1], [n - 1 - target]))
    # move the new axis back to the target position
    return np.moveaxis(psi, 0, n - 1 - target).reshape(-1)

def apply_cnot(psi, control, target, n):
    psi = psi.reshape([2]*n)
    idx_c, idx_t = n - 1 - control, n - 1 - target
    out = psi.copy()
    # flip target where control == 1
    sel = [slice(None)]*n; sel[idx_c] = 1
    tgt = [slice(None)]*n
    block = out[tuple(sel)]
    block = np.flip(block, axis=idx_t if idx_t < idx_c else idx_t - 0)
    out[tuple(sel)] = block
    return out.reshape(-1)

def global_cost(theta, n, L):
    psi = layered_ansatz(theta, n, L)
    return 1.0 - abs(psi[0])**2          # 1 - fidelity with |0>^n

def grad_var(n, L, S=200, k=(0, 0)):
    """Empirical Var[dC/d theta_k] over S random initializations (parameter shift)."""
    grads = np.zeros(S)
    for s in range(S):
        th = np.random.uniform(0, 2*np.pi, size=(L, n))
        tp, tm = th.copy(), th.copy()
        tp[k] += np.pi/2; tm[k] -= np.pi/2
        grads[s] = 0.5*(global_cost(tp, n, L) - global_cost(tm, n, L))
    return grads.var()

for n in [2, 4, 6, 8]:
    print(f"n={n:2d}  Var[dC/dtheta] ~ {grad_var(n, L=n):.2e}")
# Expected output (illustrative): variance falls ~4x per added qubit pair,
# i.e. exponential decay, matching the O(2^-n) prediction of [5].
```

The variance collapses roughly an order of magnitude every few qubits — the numerical fingerprint of a barren plateau. Replacing the global fidelity with a local cost $C_{\\mathrm{local}} = 1 - \\frac{1}{n}\\sum_j \\langle Z_j \\rangle$ at $L = \\mathcal{O}(\\log n)$ depth flattens the decay to polynomial, reproducing the Cerezo *et al.* [6] dichotomy in miniature.

### 5.2 Case study: VQE at the measurement wall

The Peruzzo demonstration [1] used a handful of photonic qubits for HeH$^+$; a 20-qubit molecule multiplies Pauli terms into the thousands, each needing $\\sim 10^4$–$10^6$ shots for millihartree precision — term grouping and classical shadows compress constants, not asymptotics. Crucially, chemistry Hamiltonians are sums of *local* terms, so VQE's natural cost sits on the trainable side of the Cerezo dichotomy [6] at shallow depth. The emerged practical recipe — UCC-inspired or ADAPT-grown ansätze, local Pauli-grouped costs, identity initialization, shot-frugal optimizers — approximates a constrained optimization: *maximize chemical expressibility subject to logarithmic depth and local costs*.

### 5.3 Case study: QAOA depth, heuristics, and noise

Zhou *et al.* [8] supply QAOA's quantitative backbone: INTERP-initialized depth-$p$ QAOA improves steadily to $p = 50$ in noiseless simulation and beats quantum annealing on small-gap instances via non-adiabatic paths. Wang *et al.* [7] overlay the noise reality: under local Pauli noise, the depth that buys approximation quality exponentially suppresses the training gradient. The practitioner compromises among **depth**, **noise**, and **trainability** — and the current-hardware optimum sits at small $p$, where advantage over classical heuristics remains unproven.

### 5.4 Proof sketch: why 2-designs flatten gradients

Sketch of the McClean *et al.* [5] argument: write $U(\\boldsymbol{\\theta}) = U_L V(\\theta_k) U_R$ with $V(\\theta_k) = e^{-i\\theta_k G}$; then $\\partial_k C = i\\,\\langle 0| U_R^\\dagger [G, U_L^\\dagger H U_L] U_R |0\\rangle$. If $U_L, U_R$ are independent approximate 2-designs, $\\mathbb{E}[\\partial_k C] = 0$ by symmetry, and Weingarten integration reduces the variance to traces of powers of $H$ divided by $d^2 - 1$ with $d = 2^n$ — each Haar-averaged moment contributes $\\sim 2^{-n}$. The exponential is not a circuit artifact; it is the *dimension of Hilbert space* asserting itself: escape requires breaking the 2-design assumption structurally.

## 6. Limitations

An honest assessment must record where the theory — and this thesis — stops.

1. **Average-case vs. worst-case.** Plateau theorems concern *random* circuits and initializations; structured ansätze (QAOA, UCC, ADAPT-VQE) are not 2-designs, and rigorous trainability guarantees for them remain sparse — the "structured enough to train" vs. "expressive enough to matter" frontier is largely empirical.
2. **Noise models vs. reality.** The NIBP theorem [7] assumes local Pauli noise; real devices add correlated, non-Markovian, and leakage errors whose trainability impact is less understood.
3. **Classical simulability tension.** Provably trainable regimes (shallow, local, low-entanglement) overlap uncomfortably with regimes where tensor-network methods compete — a *provable* advantage for a *trainable* VQA remains the central open problem.
4. **Optimization hardness.** Beyond gradient decay lie NP-hardness of the training problem itself, local minima, and shot-noise-limited gradient precision.
5. **Scope.** Gate-based VQAs only; adiabatic, analog, and measurement-based schemes obey related but distinct laws, and hardware results are cited through the literature.

## 7. Conclusion

Variational quantum algorithms began as a pragmatic compromise with noisy hardware [1, 3, 4] and matured into a theory of *trainability itself*: McClean *et al.* [5] showed random deep circuits are untrainable; Cerezo *et al.* [6] showed cost-function locality controls plateau onset and handed practitioners a design rule; Wang *et al.* [7] showed hardware noise imposes plateaus no design rule evades. With the QAOA performance studies [8] and hybrid foundations [2], these reframe NISQ optimization as navigating **expressibility** vs. **trainability** vs. **noise resilience**.

The program ahead: *theoretically*, characterize trainability for structured ansätze beyond the 2-design regime, unify the plateau theories, and either prove a trainable VQA with super-classical performance or delineate why none exists; *practically*, co-design ansätze, costs, initializations, and mitigation as one trainability-maximizing package — the local-cost, shallow-depth, identity-initialized, noise-aware VQA is the best current answer: an engineering compromise, not a theorem.

Whether variational algorithms deliver the first practical quantum advantage remains open. What is settled are the terms of debate: any scalable-VQA claim must survive the barren-plateau audit — *show the gradients*. That demand, forged over a decade of hard-won theory, is the field's most durable contribution.
