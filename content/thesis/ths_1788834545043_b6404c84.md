# Speculative Decoding for LLM Inference: EAGLE-3 Draft Heads, Medusa Multi-Head Decoding, Tree Verification, Token Acceptance-Rate Theory, and Production Serving Integration

## Abstract

Speculative decoding has emerged as the dominant lossless paradigm for accelerating autoregressive large language model (LLM) inference, decoupling token *generation* (cheap draft) from token *validation* (expensive target-model pass) [1][2]. This thesis unifies the four technical pillars of the modern speculative-decoding stack: EAGLE-3's training-time test and direct token prediction [5], Medusa's multi-head decoding with tree attention [6], tree-structured verification with an information-theoretic account of token acceptance rates [7], and the KV-cache memory-subsystem interplay that ultimately bounds production throughput [8]. We derive the expected-accepted-tokens formula $E[N]=(1-\alpha^\gamma)/(1-\alpha)$, characterize the speculation-length tradeoff, and analyze how continuous batching in vLLM and RadixAttention-structured execution in SGLang reshape acceptance dynamics under load [8][9]. Empirically, EAGLE-3 reports up to 6.5\u00d7 wall-clock speedup on LLaMA-Instruct 3.1 8B and 1.38\u00d7 throughput gains at batch size 64 in SGLang [5]. We close with a candid appraisal of the limits of speculative sampling: verification saturation, entropy-driven rejection, and the memory wall.

---

## 1 Introduction

Autoregressive decoding is the central bottleneck of large language model serving. Generating a sequence of $T$ tokens requires $T$ sequential forward passes of a model with tens of billions of parameters, and each pass is *memory-bandwidth bound*: the arithmetic intensity of single-token decoding is so low that GPU compute units idle while weights stream from HBM [8]. The result is a latency profile dominated not by FLOPs but by bytes moved. Speculative decoding restructures the problem: a cheap *draft* mechanism proposes $\gamma$ candidate tokens and the target model validates them in a **single** forward pass, since verification is parallelizable across positions [1][2].

This restructuring can be made **lossless**: the output distribution is provably identical to the target model's [1][2]. Since 2023 the field has advanced along four axes, each attacking a different term in the speedup equation:

1. **Draft quality** \u2014 how well the cheap model predicts the target distribution (EAGLE, EAGLE-2, EAGLE-3) [3][4][5].
2. **Speculation structure** \u2014 whether drafts are linear sequences or trees of candidates (SpecInfer, Medusa) [6][7].
3. **Acceptance theory** \u2014 how acceptance probability \u03b1 and speculation depth \u03b3 interact to determine expected throughput [1][2][7].
4. **Systems integration** \u2014 how KV-cache management, continuous batching, and scheduling in engines such as vLLM and SGLang change the constants of the analysis [8][9].

We treat the full stack: foundations (Section 2), unified methodology (Section 3), the four axes (Section 4), empirical results and proofs (Section 5), limitations (Section 6), and outlook (Section 7).

> **Theorem (Lossless Guarantee, informal):** Under the rejection-sampling acceptance rule of Leviathan et al. [1] and Chen et al. [2], speculative decoding produces token sequences distributed *identically* to autoregressive sampling from the target model $q(\cdot\mid x_{<t})$, for both greedy and stochastic decoding.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Autoregressive decoding and the memory wall

A decoder-only transformer generates tokens one at a time: at step $t$ it computes $x_t \sim q(\cdot \mid x_{<t})$ using the KV-cache for incremental attention. Each step is memory-bandwidth bound \u2014 a LLaMA-3.1-70B decode moves ~140 GB of weights per token, flooring latency at ~42 ms on an H100 regardless of arithmetic throughput [8].

### 2.2 Speculative sampling (Leviathan; Chen)

Speculative sampling introduces a draft distribution $p(\cdot \mid x_{<t})$, cheaper to evaluate than $q$. The draft generates $\gamma$ guesses $\tilde{x}_{t+1}, \dots, \tilde{x}_{t+\gamma}$; the target evaluates $q$ at all positions **in parallel**. Tokens are accepted via:

- Accept $\tilde{x}_{t+i}$ with probability $\min\left(1, \frac{q(\tilde{x}_{t+i} \mid x_{<t+i})}{p(\tilde{x}_{t+i} \mid x_{<t+i})}\right)$.
- On first rejection at position $i$, sample a replacement from the residual distribution $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0, q - p))$ and discard the rest.

> **Theorem:** The expected acceptance probability of a draft token equals $\sum_{x \in \mathcal{V}} \min(p(x), q(x)) = 1 - D_{TV}(p, q)$ [1][2] \u2014 *draft fidelity is the only thing that matters*.

### 2.3 The speedup equation

Let $c$ be the cost ratio of one draft forward pass to one target forward pass ($c \ll 1$), $\gamma$ the speculation length, and $\alpha$ the per-token acceptance probability. The number of tokens accepted per iteration is a truncated geometric random variable:

$$
E[N] = \sum_{i=1}^{\gamma} \alpha^{i-1} = \frac{1-\alpha^\gamma}{1-\alpha}.
$$

Expected speedup is approximately $E[N] / (1 + \gamma c)$: the marginal value of $\gamma$ decays in $\alpha^\gamma$, and when $c \to 0$ (Medusa's heads [6]) the optimal $\gamma$ is limited only by verification cost and memory.

### 2.4 The draft-model taxonomy

Draft mechanisms fall into three families:

| Family | Example | Draft cost $c$ | Typical $\alpha$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent small model | TinyLlama-1.1B drafting for 70B | $\sim$0.05 | 0.4\u20130.6 |
| Feature-reuse heads | EAGLE / EAGLE-2 | $\sim$0.01 | 0.7\u20130.85 |
| Parameter-free heads | Medusa decoding heads | $\sim$0.002 | 0.6\u20130.8 |
| Retrieval / n-gram | REST, Lookahead | $\approx$ 0 | 0.3\u20130.5 |

The field has moved from separate draft models toward draft heads reusing the target's own computation, culminating in EAGLE-3's abandonment of feature prediction [3][4][5][6].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is analytical and synthetic: we formalize the speculative-decoding pipeline as a composition of four modules, analyze each module's design space with the published results as ground truth, and derive the joint optimization that a production serving stack must solve. We do not introduce a new algorithm; instead we provide the first unified treatment connecting EAGLE-3's draft-head innovations [5], Medusa's tree decoding [6], tree-verification theory [7], and the KV-cache/serving layer [8][9].

The pipeline is:

1. **Drafting.** A lightweight mechanism (EAGLE-3 head, Medusa heads, or retrieval) produces a *token tree* \u2014 a set of candidate sequences sharing prefixes \u2014 of total size $B$ and maximum depth $\gamma$.
2. **Tree verification.** The target model scores every node of the tree in one forward pass using *tree attention*, a causal mask generalized to the tree partial order [7].
3. **Acceptance.** A sequential rejection-sampling walk down the highest-probability path of the tree yields the accepted prefix; a bonus token is sampled from the target distribution at the rejection point [1][2].
4. **Cache maintenance.** Accepted tokens are committed to the KV-cache; rejected branches are discarded, and the draft mechanism is re-seeded from the new state.

We analyze correctness, throughput (the tree-generalized speedup equation), memory, and serving integration, grounding claims in the published record [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9].

```python
# Canonical speculative-decoding loop (greedy case, linear draft)
def speculative_decode(target, draft, prefix, gamma):
    # 1. DRAFT: cheap model proposes gamma tokens
    guesses = [draft.sample(prefix + guesses) for _ in range(gamma)]
    # 2. VERIFY: one target forward pass scores all positions
    logits = target(prefix + guesses)  # parallel over positions
    # 3. ACCEPT: sequential walk with residual resampling
    accepted = []
    for i, g in enumerate(guesses):
        if target_argmax(logits[i]) == g:
            accepted.append(g)
        else:
            accepted.append(target_argmax(logits[i]))  # bonus token
            break
    else:
        accepted.append(target_argmax(logits[gamma]))  # all accepted: bonus
    return prefix + accepted
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 EAGLE-3: Training-Time Test and Direct Token Prediction

EAGLE [3] introduced the key insight that the target model's own top-layer *features* are far more informative for drafting than the logits of a small separate model. The draft head autoregresses at the **feature level**: given the target's second-to-top-layer feature $f_t$ and the embedding of the next token $e_{t+1}$, a single decoder layer predicts $\hat{f}_{t+1}$, which is passed through the target's LM head to obtain draft logits. Because the draft head is a single layer operating on already-computed features, its cost $c$ is roughly 1% of a target pass, yet its acceptance rates substantially exceed those of independent draft models [3].

EAGLE-2 [4] added *dynamic tree drafting*: the head expands a draft tree on the fly, ranking nodes by a feature-uncertainty confidence score and pruning low-value branches, raising expected accepted tokens per verification pass at modest cost.

**EAGLE-3 [5] makes two decisive breaks**, motivated by EAGLE's diminishing returns from data scaling, traced to the *feature-prediction* objective:

1. **Abandoning feature prediction for direct token prediction.** EAGLE-3's draft model predicts tokens directly rather than regressing intermediate features. This removes the information bottleneck that capped EAGLE's scaling curve and lets the draft model benefit fully from data scale \u2014 the "scaling law" the authors demonstrate on MT-bench with LLaMA-3.1-8B as target [5].
2. **Multi-layer feature fusion via training-time test.** Rather than conditioning only on top-layer features, EAGLE-3 fuses features from *multiple* layers of the target model. The "training-time test" technique aligns the training objective with the test-time acceptance criterion, so the draft head is optimized for exactly the quantity \u2014 accepted-token yield \u2014 that determines speedup.

The reported results are striking: up to **6.5\u00d7 speedup** on chat and reasoning models, roughly **1.4\u00d7 over EAGLE-2**, and a **1.38\u00d7 throughput improvement at batch size 64** when integrated into the SGLang serving framework [5]. EAGLE-3 completes the arc from draft model to draft head: a thin trainable shim riding the target's representations.

### 4.2 Medusa: Multi-Head Decoding with Tree Attention

Medusa [6] takes the draft-head idea to its logical extreme. Instead of one autoregressive draft head, Medusa attaches $K$ lightweight heads to the target model's final hidden state, each predicting the token at a *fixed future offset*: head $k$ predicts $x_{t+k}$ directly, in parallel, with no autoregression between heads. The draft cost is therefore nearly constant in $\gamma$ (a few linear layers), making $c \approx 0.002$ \u2014 the cheapest draft in the taxonomy.

The catch is *independence*: because heads do not condition on each other's outputs, their joint predictions can be incoherent. Medusa compensates with a **tree-structured candidate set**: for each head, take the top-$s_k$ tokens, and form the Cartesian tree of combinations. A specialized **tree attention** mask \u2014 where each candidate token attends only to its ancestors in the tree plus the shared prefix \u2014 allows the target model to verify the entire tree in a single forward pass [6][7].

Medusa offers two operating modes:

- **Medusa-1:** the base model is frozen; only the heads are trained. This is a drop-in acceleration for existing checkpoints.
- **Medusa-2:** the base model is fine-tuned jointly with the heads, trading some training cost for higher acceptance rates.

With typical tree sizes (e.g., total candidates $B \approx 64$), Medusa achieves 2.2\u20132.8\u00d7 wall-clock speedups on Vicuna-class models [6]. Its chief virtue is simplicity: no separate draft model to deploy, no feature plumbing \u2014 just extra heads and a tree mask. Its chief weakness is the independence assumption, which caps acceptance rates below those of autoregressive draft heads like EAGLE-3's on high-entropy text.

### 4.3 Tree Verification and Token Acceptance-Rate Theory

SpecInfer [7] generalized speculative decoding from linear drafts to **token trees** and provided the systems architecture \u2014 tree attention, speculative sampling over trees, and continuous-batching-aware scheduling \u2014 that all later tree methods inherit. The theoretical question is: given a draft tree $\mathcal{T}$ and a per-edge acceptance probability, what is the expected accepted yield, and how should we *shape* the tree?

For a linear draft, the answer is the truncated geometric sum derived in Section 2.3. For a tree, let each node $v$ have draft probability $p_v$ (the product of head probabilities along its path) and let the target-model acceptance of the node's token, conditioned on its ancestors being accepted, be $\alpha_v$. Then the expected number of accepted tokens is the sum over nodes of the probability that the entire root-to-node path is accepted:

$$
E[N] = \sum_{v \in \mathcal{T}} \prod_{u \in \mathrm{path}(v)} \alpha_u.
$$

This formula yields three design principles:

1. **Breadth beats depth at low $\alpha$.** When acceptance is poor, deep paths contribute exponentially little; the tree budget is better spent on alternative first tokens. When $\alpha$ is high, deep linear chains dominate.
2. **Confidence-ordered expansion is optimal.** EAGLE-2's dynamic drafting [4] is a greedy approximation to expanding the tree in order of $\prod \alpha_u$, which is optimal for a fixed node budget by a standard exchange argument.
3. **Verification is not free.** Tree attention costs $O(B^2)$ attention work over $B$ tree nodes plus the prefix. The optimal tree size balances $E[N]$ against the verification FLOPs, which is why production deployments typically cap $B$ at 32\u201364 [6][7].

> **Theorem (Tree acceptance decomposition):** For any draft tree verified with tree attention and accepted via sequential rejection sampling along the realized path, the expected accepted length equals the sum of path-acceptance probabilities, and the procedure remains lossless with respect to the target distribution [2][7].

An important subtlety: acceptance probabilities are *not* stationary. They depend on the entropy of the target distribution at each position \u2014 low-temperature or highly constrained generation (code, JSON) has sharply peaked $q$ and therefore high $\alpha$, while open-ended creative text has lower $\alpha$ [1][2]. Adaptive schemes that shrink $\gamma$ when recent acceptance drops are a natural consequence of the theory.

### 4.4 KV-Cache Interplay and Memory Scheduling

All of the above is moot if the memory subsystem cannot serve the verification pass efficiently. Speculative decoding interacts with the KV-cache in three ways:

1. **Cache amplification** \u2014 verification materializes KV entries for all $B$ tree nodes; rejected branches waste write bandwidth.
2. **Prefix sharing** \u2014 vLLM's PagedAttention [8] shares prefix pages by reference across tree nodes, provisionally allocating only speculative suffix pages.
3. **Batching tension** \u2014 in continuous batching [8], one request's large tree inflates batch attention FLOPs quadratically, potentially *reducing* overall throughput \u2014 the central scheduling tradeoff.

SGLang's RadixAttention [9] adds another dimension: its radix-tree cache shares prompt prefixes across *requests*, which interacts with speculation because common prefixes (system prompts, few-shot examples) are exactly the low-entropy regions where draft acceptance is highest. EAGLE-3's reported 1.38\u00d7 throughput gain at batch 64 in SGLang [5] reflects this synergy \u2014 structured caching plus high-$\alpha$ drafting compound rather than merely add.

```rust
// Sketch: tree-attention mask construction for verification
// Each candidate node attends to its ancestors + shared prefix.
fn tree_attention_mask(tree: &TokenTree, prefix_len: usize) -> Vec<Vec<bool>> {
    let n = prefix_len + tree.nodes.len();
    let mut mask = vec![vec![false; n]; n];
    for (i, node) in tree.nodes.iter().enumerate() {
        let row = prefix_len + i;
        // attend to full shared prefix
        for j in 0..prefix_len { mask[row][j] = true; }
        // attend to ancestors only (not siblings / descendants)
        let mut a = node.parent;
        while let Some(p) = a {
            mask[row][prefix_len + p] = true;
            a = tree.nodes[p].parent;
        }
        mask[row][row] = true;
    }
    mask
}
```

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The lossless proof (sketch)

We sketch the Chen et al. [2] argument for stochastic decoding. Fix a position with draft distribution $p$ and target $q$. The algorithm accepts draft token $x$ with probability $\min(1, q(x)/p(x))$ and otherwise resamples from $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0, q-p))$. The output distribution is:

$$
P_{\mathrm{out}}(x) = p(x)\min\left(1,\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}\right) + \left(1 - \sum_{x'} \min(p(x'), q(x'))\right) \frac{\max(0, q(x)-p(x))}{\sum_{x'} \max(0, q(x')-p(x'))}.
$$

Since $\sum \max(0, q-p) = 1 - \sum \min(p,q)$, this simplifies to $P_{\mathrm{out}}(x) = \min(p(x),q(x)) + \max(0, q(x)-p(x)) = q(x)$, position-wise yielding exact equality with target autoregressive sampling. \u25a1

### 5.2 Reported speedups

| Method | Target model | Reported speedup | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speculative sampling [2] | LaMDA / T5-XXL | 2\u20133\u00d7 | Independent draft model |
| EAGLE [3] | Vicuna-13B | \u223c3\u00d7 | Feature-level autoregression |
| EAGLE-2 [4] | LLaMA-3-8B | \u223c4\u00d7 | Dynamic tree drafting |
| **EAGLE-3 [5]** | **LLaMA-3.1-8B** | **up to 6.5\u00d7** | **Training-time test, token prediction** |
| Medusa [6] | Vicuna-7B/13B | 2.2\u20132.8\u00d7 | Multi-head, tree attention |
| SpecInfer [7] | LLaMA-7B/13B | 1.5\u20132.5\u00d7 | Tree verification + batching |

Two patterns stand out. First, every generation of the EAGLE line roughly multiplies the previous speedup by 1.3\u20131.4\u00d7 \u2014 the gains come from draft fidelity, exactly as the $1 - D_{TV}(p,q)$ identity predicts [3][4][5]. Second, the EAGLE-3 result *in a serving framework* (1.38\u00d7 throughput at batch 64 in SGLang [5]) is arguably the more important number: it shows the method survives contact with batching, scheduling, and real memory pressure.

### 5.3 The acceptance-rate curve

Plugging representative values into $E[N] = (1-\alpha^\gamma)/(1-\alpha)$:

| $\alpha$ | $\gamma=3$ | $\gamma=5$ | $\gamma=7$ | $\gamma=10$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 1.75 | 1.94 | 1.99 | 2.00 |
| 0.7 | 2.19 | 2.76 | 3.06 | 3.24 |
| 0.85 | 2.57 | 3.71 | 4.58 | 5.34 |
| 0.95 | 2.85 | 4.52 | 6.03 | 7.69 |

The lesson is quantitative and unforgiving: at $\alpha = 0.5$, speculation beyond $\gamma = 3$ is nearly worthless; at $\alpha = 0.95$, depth pays handsomely. Draft-head research is, numerically, the project of pushing deployments from the top rows of this table to the bottom rows [3][4][5][6].

### 5.4 Production serving integration

vLLM integrates speculation with PagedAttention so draft tokens occupy provisional pages discarded atomically on rejection [8]; SGLang's RadixAttention raises effective $\alpha$ on shared-prefix agentic workloads [9]. The serving consensus: enable speculation for latency-sensitive small-batch serving, shrink $\gamma$ and $B$ as batches grow, and co-design near-zero-$c$ draft heads with the scheduler [5][8][9].

## 6 Limitations

**Verification saturation.** Speculative decoding converts a memory-bandwidth problem into a compute problem: the verification pass does $\gamma\times$ (or $B\times$) the attention work. On long contexts, where attention itself becomes the bottleneck, speculation can *slow down* decoding. There is a context-length crossover beyond which $\gamma = 1$ (no speculation) is optimal, and production systems must detect it [7][8].

**Entropy-driven rejection.** Acceptance probability is bounded by the sharpness of the target distribution. On high-entropy creative writing at high temperature, even EAGLE-3's $\alpha$ degrades, and the method's advantage shrinks toward unity [1][2][5]. Speculative decoding is a bet on predictability; it loses that bet exactly where models are most "creative."

**The memory wall persists.** Tree verification materializes KV entries for rejected branches. At large batch sizes with PagedAttention, the provisional-page churn can fragment the block table and increase scheduling overhead [8]. The 1.38\u00d7 SGLang throughput figure [5], while positive, is far from the 6.5\u00d7 latency figure \u2014 batching dilutes speculation's wins.

**Training and staleness.** Draft heads must be trained per target model (Medusa-1 excepted [6]), and they go stale when the target is fine-tuned. EAGLE-3's data-scaling result [5] mitigates but does not eliminate this maintenance tax.

**Evaluation fragility.** Speedup ratios are notoriously hardware-, batch-, and dataset-dependent. A 6.5\u00d7 figure on MT-bench with greedy decoding does not transfer to temperature-1.0 open-ended generation on different accelerators [1][5]. The field would benefit from a standardized serving benchmark with fixed SLOs.

## 7 Conclusion

Speculative decoding has matured from a clever sampling trick [1][2] into a full-stack discipline spanning draft-head architecture (EAGLE-3 [5]), parallel multi-head prediction (Medusa [6]), tree-structured verification theory [7], and memory-aware serving systems (vLLM [8], SGLang [9]). The unifying equation \u2014 acceptance probability equals one minus the total-variation distance between draft and target \u2014 has organized a remarkably productive research program: every major advance has been, in essence, a better way to make $p$ approximate $q$ cheaply.

EAGLE-3's training-time test represents the current frontier of that program, abandoning feature regression for direct token prediction with multi-layer fusion and demonstrating that draft heads, like the models they serve, obey scaling laws [5]. Yet the deepest lesson of the production results is humbler: in real serving stacks, speculation's latency wins are partially taxed by batching, scheduling, and the KV-cache. The next frontier is therefore not a better draft head in isolation but **co-design** \u2014 schedulers that adapt speculation depth to load, caches that share speculative state across requests, and serving benchmarks that measure what users actually feel. The memory wall has not fallen; speculative decoding has simply taught us to climb it several tokens at a time.
