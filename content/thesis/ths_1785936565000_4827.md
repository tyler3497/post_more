# Dependent Optics via Polynomial Functors: A Yoneda-Lemma Foundation for Profunctor-Based Bidirectional Programming

## Abstract
We unify dependent optics, polynomial functors, and profunctor optics into a single fibrational framework for bidirectional programming. Starting from indexed categories encoding admissible forward and backward maps, we reconstruct dependent lenses and prisms as morphisms in Poly = Σ_{A:Type} A→Type, where comonoids coincide with categories and bicomodules with parametric lenses. The Yoneda lemma yields the profunctor representation theorem: Optic(S,T,A,B) ≅ ∫_{P∈Tamb} Set(P(A,B), P(S,T)), generalizing to dependent Tambara representations classifying contravariant presheaves on optics. We show how polynomial composition captures sequential bidirectional transformation and how dependent types enforce put-get laws by construction, improving on classical Foster–Greenwald lens laws. Applications to database view-update, string transducers, and dynamical systems are sketched with complexity $O(n \log n)$ composition bounds.

---

## 1. Introduction

Bidirectional programming addresses the *view-update problem*: given a source $S$ and a view $A = get(S)$, how does an update $B$ to the view propagate back to $T = put(S, B)$? Classical *asymmetric lenses* (Foster et al. 2007) axiomatized this as:

- `get : S → A`
- `put : S × B → T` 
- with laws `PutGet`, `GetPut`, `PutPut`.

In functional programming this concrete representation is insufficiently compositional. Two insights transformed the field:

1.  **Optics as coends.** Riley (2018) and Milewski (2017) define, for an action $\bullet : \mathbf{M} \times \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{C}$ of a monoidal $\mathbf{M}$,
    $$\mathbf{Optic}_{\bullet}\binom{S}{T}\binom{A}{B} = \int^{M \in \mathbf{M}} \mathbf{C}(S, M \bullet A) \times \mathbf{C}(M \bullet B, T)$$
    For $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{C}$ with $\times$, we recover lenses; for $+$, prisms; for other monoidal actions, traversals, grates, kaleidoscopes.

2.  **Profunctor encoding.** Pickering, Gibbons, Wu (2017) showed every such optic is representable as
    $$l : \forall P. \text{Tambara } P \Rightarrow P A B \to P S T$$
    enabling lens composition as ordinary function composition in Haskell.

The limitation remained: *dependency*. Real-world schemas have $B$ depending on $a:A$; positions determine admissible directions. *Polynomial functors*, championed by Spivak, Niu (2025) as $p(y)=\sum_{a:A} y^{B(a)}$, are precisely dependent lenses in disguise. A morphism $p \to q$ is a forward map on positions $f: A \to C$ plus backward map $f^\sharp : \forall a. D(fa) \to B(a)$.

*Dependent optics* (Vertechi 2022, Capucci et al., Clark) generalize both by replacing the monoidal action with *indexed categories* $\mathcal{L} : \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{Cat}$, $\mathcal{R}: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{Cat}$. The resulting optic permits $a$ and $b$ to live in fibers varying over state. This thesis proves that:

- Dependent optics subsumes polynomial lenses;
- Yoneda-driven Tambara theory extends verbatim;
- Bidirectional laws become fibrational coherence conditions.

> **Contributions:** Unified syntax, proof of profunctor representation for dependent case, complexity analysis, and identification of open composition problems.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Classical Lenses and Bidirectional Laws

Foster et al. introduced *combinators for bidirectional tree transformations* to solve view-update where $get$ forgets information. A well-behaved lens satisfies:

- **GetPut:** $put(s, get(s)) = s$
- **PutGet:** $get(put(s, v)) = v$
- **PutPut:** $put(put(s, v), v') = put(s, v')$

These are equivalent to coalgebra laws for the costate comonad. Generalizations include *prisms* for sum types, *traversals* for containers.

Weaknesses: lens concatenation $l_1 ; l_2$ requires manual proof of law preservation, and handling of dependent schema $e.g.$ $S = \Sigma_{t:Table} Row(t)$ is impossible.

### 2.2 Profunctor Optics and Tambara Modules

Let $Prof(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}) = [\mathbf{C}^{op}\times\mathbf{D}, \mathbf{Set}]$. A profunctor $P$ has *strength*:

> **Definition 2.1 (Tambara):** A Tambara module for actions $\bullet_{\mathbf{C}}, \bullet_{\mathbf{D}}$ is $\alpha_{M,A,B} : P(A,B) \to P(M\bullet A, M\bullet B)$ natural satisfying coherence w.r.t $\otimes$ and $I$.

Write $\mathbf{Tamb}$ for category thereof. The free Tambara on $y_{(A,B)}$ is:

$$\Psi y_{(A,B)}(S,T) = \int^{M} \mathbf{C}(S, M\bullet A)\times \mathbf{D}(M\bullet B,T) = \mathbf{Optic}(S,T,A,B)$$

The profunctor representation theorem states:

> **Theorem:** $\mathbf{Optic}\binom{S}{T}\binom{A}{B} \cong \int_{P\in\mathbf{Tamb}} \mathbf{Set}(P(A,B), P(S,T))$

*Proof sketch:* Yoneda $P(A,B) \cong Nat(y_{(A,B)}, P)$ then free-forgetful adjunction $\Phi \dashv U \dashv \Theta$ between profunctors and Tambara modules, then Yoneda again evaluating at $(S,T)$. Details in Boisseau-Gibbons (2018) and Román (2020).

This *is* the Yoneda lemma applied twice plus density.

### 2.3 Polynomial Functors

A polynomial functor $p : \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Set}$ is $p(X)=\sum_{a:A} X^{B(a)}$ for bundle $B \to A$. We denote $p$ as pair $(A,B)$. Category $\mathbf{Poly}$:

- Objects: polynomials
- Morphisms: dependent lenses $(f, f^\sharp)$ as above
- Tensor $\otimes$ = Dirichlet product for parallel interaction
- Composition $\triangleleft$ = substitution for sequential interface nesting

Key facts (Niu-Spivak 2025):

- $y = (1,1)$ is unit for $\triangleleft$
- Comonoids in $(\mathbf{Poly},\triangleleft, y)$ are *small categories*
- Comonoid homomorphisms are *cofunctors*
- Bicomodules $c \nrightarrow d$ are parametric dynamical systems, lenses, Mealy machines

Thus $\mathbf{Poly}$ already *is* a category of dependent optics.

| Optic Family | Action $\mathbf{M}$ | Profunctor Constraint | Poly View |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lens | $(\mathbf{C},\times)$ | Strong | $p = S y^T$, $q = A y^B$ |
| Prism | $(\mathbf{C},+)$ | Choice | $p = S + T y$ |
| Traversal | Finset traversable | Traversing | $p = \sum_n S_n y^n$ power series |
| Dependent Lens | Indexed $\mathcal{L}$ | Dep-Tambara | Polynomial morphism in slice |
| Closed optic | Internal hom | Closed | $p = [A,T]$ Cartesian closure |

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

We adopt Vertechi's indexed formalism. Fix base category $\mathcal{C}$ (typically $\mathbf{Set}$ or $\mathbf{Type}$). Let $\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{R}: \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{Cat}$ be *indexed categories*: for each $I\in\mathcal{C}$, categories $\mathcal{L}^I, \mathcal{R}^I$, with reindexing $f^*: \mathcal{L}^J \to \mathcal{L}^I$.

> **Definition 3.1 (Dependent Optic):** For boundaries $\binom{X}{X'}$ where $X\in\mathcal{C}$, $X'\in\mathcal{L}^X$, and $\binom{Y}{Y'}$ similarly, define
$$
\mathbf{DepOptic}\binom{X}{X'}\binom{Y}{Y'} = \int^{M\in\mathcal{C}_{/X\times Y}} \mathcal{L}^{X}(...) \times \mathcal{R}^{Y}(...)
$$
Concretely for $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{R}=\mathbf{Fam}$ (families) over $\mathbf{Set}$:
$$\mathbf{DOptics}\binom{S}{T}\binom{A}{B} = \sum_{f:S\to A} \prod_{s:S} B(f(s)) \to T(s)$$
witnessing position-dependence $B(a)$ varies with $a$.

*Method Steps:*

1.  Encode interface as polynomial $p=(S,T)$ where $S$ positions, $T(s)$ directions.
2.  Morphism in $\mathbf{Poly}$ yields dependent lens exactly as optic with $\mathcal{L}$ = slices.
3.  Lift Tambara to dependent Tambara: family $\alpha_I : P_I(A,B) \to P_{M\otimes I}(M\bullet A, M\bullet B)$ indexed.
4.  Prove Yoneda reduction carries over by fibred Yoneda (Street).

Complexity:

- Composing $p \xrightarrow{(f,f^\sharp)} q \xrightarrow{(g,g^\sharp)} r$ costs $O(n \log n)$ if maps stored in balanced tree, since $f^\sharp$ must be pulled back: $(g\circ f)^\sharp(s) = f^\sharp(s) \circ g^\sharp(f(s))$.
- Profunctor composition is $O(1)$ function composition but reification back to concrete costs $O(|M|)$ coend reduction.

```haskell
-- Profunctor optic in Haskell (van Laarhoven style simplified)
type Optic p a b s t = p a b -> p s t

class Profunctor p where
  dimap :: (s -> a) -> (b -> t) -> p a b -> p s t

class Profunctor p => Strong p where
  first' :: p a b -> p (a,c) (b,c)

-- Lens as strong profunctor optic
type Lens a b s t = forall p. Strong p => Optic p a b s t

lens :: (s -> a) -> (s -> b -> t) -> Lens a b s t
lens get set = \pab -> dimap (\s -> (get s, s)) (\(b,s) -> set s b) (first' pab)

-- Dependent version (Agda sketch)
record Poly : Set1 where
  field
    Pos : Set
    Dir : Pos -> Set

record Lens (p q : Poly) : Set where
  field
    onPos : Pos p -> Pos q
    onDir : (a : Pos p) -> Dir q (onPos a) -> Dir p a
```

```python
# Python representation of Poly morphism as dependent lens
from typing import Callable, Dict, List
class Poly:
    def __init__(self, positions: List[str], directions: Dict[str, List[str]]):
        self.pos = positions
        self.dir = directions  # pos -> list[dir]

def compose(f_onPos, f_onDir, g_onPos, g_onDir):
    """O(n log n) composition of dependent lenses"""
    def h_pos(a): return g_onPos(f_onPos(a))
    def h_dir(a, d_r):
        # pullback: D_r -> D_q -> D_p
        return f_onDir(a, g_onDir(f_onPos(a), d_r))
    return h_pos, h_dir
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Yoneda, Co-Yoneda and Representation

> **Theorem:** *Dependent optics are classified by dependent Tambara modules.*

Formally, let $\mathbf{Tamb}_{dep}$ be category of fibred Tambara profunctors $P: \Sigma_{I} (\mathcal{L}^I)^{op}\times \mathcal{R}^I \to \mathbf{Set}$ with strength $\int$-compatible. Then
$$Nat(y_{(A,B)}, P) \cong P(A,B)$$
by *fibred Yoneda*, and $\Psi_{dep} \dashv U$ free-forgetful yields
$$\int_{P} [P(A,B), P(S,T)] \cong \mathbf{DepOptic}(S,T,A,B)$$

*Intuition:* $y_{(A,B)}$ is path shape; $\Psi$ extends path by arbitrary context $M$ linked via indexed action $M\bullet -$. The coend $\int^{M}$ hides context as existential; profunctor quantification hides it as universal via Yoneda. This duality is instance of *Isbell duality* between coend and end representations.

In type-theoretic language, this is *Church encoding* vs *coChurch*: concrete lens = $\Sigma$-type, profunctor lens = $\Pi$-type.

### 4.2 Polynomials as Dependent Lenses: The $Poly = Lens$ Correspondence

Spivak-Niu Theorem 3.26: $\mathbf{Poly}(p,q) \cong \mathbf{DepLens}(p,q)$. Proof:

- $p = \sum_{a:A} y^{B(a)}$
- $q = \sum_{c:C} y^{D(c)}$
- Morphism $p\to q$ is natural transformation $\sum_a (-)^{B(a)} \Rightarrow \sum_c (-)^{D(c)}$
- By Yoneda, maps $A \to C$ and $D(f(a)) \to B(a)$.

Composition in $\mathbf{Poly}$ as substitution: $(p \triangleleft q)(X)= p(q(X))$ corresponds to plugging interfaces. Dependent lens composition is *nested bidirectional update*: forward $s\mapsto f(s)$, backward $t(f(s)) \mapsto T(s)$ factoring through residual $M$.

> **Remark:** A category is same as comonoid $c$ with $c \to y$ counit extracting identities and $c \to c\triangleleft c$ comultiplication encoding codomain and composition. Thus comodule $p: c \nrightarrow d$ is lens whose positions evolve according to $c,d$ dynamics – bridging dynamical systems and optics.

### 4.3 Bidirectional Laws Made Fibrational

Classical laws are *extrinsic*: after constructing $get/ put$, verify $PutGet$ etc. Dependent types make them *intrinsic*:

```rust
// Rust-like with dependent refinement (pseudo)
struct DepLens<S, A, F: Fn(S)->A, B: Fn(A)->Type, T> {
    get: F,
    put: forall s:S, B<F(s)> -> T(s),
    law_put_get: forall s b. get(put(s,b)) == relevant_coercion(b)
}
```

In indexed setting, we require $f$ section-retraction: the forward map $f: S\to A$ and family $f^\sharp_s : B(f(s)) \to T(s)$ such that $f \dashv f^\sharp$ over $\mathcal{C}$. For closed dependent optics (Vertechi Sec 4), backward map is of type $\prod_{a:S} \mathcal{C}(T_a, B_{f(a)})$, automatically satisfying PutPut because composition of pullbacks is strictly associative in $\mathcal{C}_{/I}$.

Failure mode: mixing optics with $\times$ vs $+$ actions violates distributive law – Boisseau-Gibbons lattice shows lenses and prisms do not freely compose to affine traversals without coproduct of monads construction.

### 4.4 Extensions: Functor Lenses and Closed Optics

*Functor lenses* (Abou-Saleh et al.) allow $put: S \to F S$ where $F$ functor; dependent version allows $F_a : \mathcal{L}^{S} \to \mathcal{L}^{A}$. Example: $get : List(S) \to S^*$ with update mapping over elements.

Closed dependent optics correspond to $p = [A,T]$ internal hom in $\mathbf{Poly}$: object $\underline{\mathbf{Poly}}(p,q) = \sum_{f} \prod_{a} ...$ exponential closed structure. This yields *grate* optics for $\to$-types.

Table of extensions:

| Extension | Base | Fiber | Law | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Simple lens | Set | Set | PutGet | Record field |
| Dependent lens | Set | Fam | PutGet fiberwise | Table column $Row(t)$ |
| Prism | Set | Set^op | Review law | `Maybe a` |
| Functor lens | Cat | [I,Cat] | Coalgebra | `Traverse` |
| Closed | CCC | CCC | Curry | `a -> b` |

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

### 5.1 Proof of Dependent Profunctor Representation

> **Theorem 5.1:** For indexed $\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R}$ with finite limits, $$\mathbf{DepOptic}(S,T,A,B) \cong \int_{P\in\mathbf{Tamb}_{dep}} [P(A,B), P(S,T)]$$

*Proof outline:* (Román style)

1.  $\mathbf{DepOptic} = \Psi_{dep} y_{(A,B)}(S,T)$ by definition of free Tambara.
2.  $Nat(\Psi y_{(A,B)}, P) \cong Nat(y_{(A,B)}, U P)$ by adjunction.
3.  $Nat(y_{(A,B)}, U P) \cong P(A,B)$ by fibred Yoneda (Borceux 4.3.1 generalized).
4.  End over $P$ of $[P(A,B), P(S,T)] = Nat(Hom(-, (A,B)), Hom(-,(S,T)) \circ U)$ evaluating at $Nat$.
5.  Co-Yoneda reduces end to $\Psi y_{(A,B)}(S,T)$.

Strictness requires Beck-Chevalley for pullback-stable coends, satisfied when $\mathcal{C}$ locally cartesian closed. $\square$

### 5.2 Benchmark as Thought Experiment

Consider synchronizing JSON schema $S = \{users: Map Id (Profile\times Pref)\}$ to view $A = List(Id)$. Classical lens put is $O(n)$ with reallocation; dependent lens $B(a)=Pref(a)$ restricts update to already-existing user, avoiding orphan insertion, thus $O(\log n)$ with balanced tree index.

Polymorphic profunctor composition: `lens1 . lens2 . prism` in Haskell unifies to single loop fusion because intermediate $M$ existential never materialized – GHC RULES optimize $dimap$-fusion to $O(n)$ instead of $O(n \log n)$ materialized $M$ tuple.

### 5.3 Lawfulness Preservation

We show Tambara structure preserves GetPut via diagram chase. For strong profunctor $P$, strength commutes with unit $I$, i.e. $\alpha_I = id$. Since optic embedding uses $\alpha_{M}$ plus $dimap get/set$, GetPut condition $get\circ put = id$ becomes $\alpha_{I}\circ dimap = id$ which holds by monoidal functor axioms $m_{I}\bullet A \cong A$. PutPut similarly follows from associativity $\alpha_{M\otimes N}= \alpha_M\circ \alpha_N$.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1.  **Composition of heterogeneous optics lattice.** Pickering et al. identified sublattice where lens+prism = affine traversal but required ad-hoc distributive law. For dependent optics, lattice of indexed categories under cartesian product lacks coproducts unless assuming extensivity and Van Kampen colimits (Vertechi Thm 3.8). Open: characterize $\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R}$ sufficient for arbitrary mixed dep-optic to have canonical normal form. Distributive laws between comonads in $\mathbf{Poly}$ are rare (Ahman-Uustalu?).

2.  **Coherence of higher dependences.** $Poly$ is 1-categorical; $\infty$-dependent optics needing homotopy pullbacks for schema evolution (evolution of $A$ type itself dependent) require $\infty$-$\mathbf{Poly}$ (finite-limit $\infty$-category). Niu's thesis says comonoids become $\infty$-categories; law verification becomes coherence infinite tower.

3.  **Performance of reified Tambara.** Profunctor encoding erases $M$ causing *code bloat* in Scala/C# compilers that lack rank-2 polymorphism specialization. Manifest existential $M$ represented as $\exists M. (S\to M\times A)\times(M\times B\to T)$ causes boxing. Mitigation via *defunctionalized optics* (optics as data, not functions) but loses neat function composition.

4.  **Bidirectional laws with effects.** Real puts are effectful: IO, Maybe (failure). Extending dependent optics to Kleisli $F$-actions retains Tambara structure only if effect functor $F$ is *applicative* and commutes with $M$-action via distributive law $M\bullet F \Rightarrow F\bullet M$. Classification of such laws incomplete.

5.  **Verification burden.** While dependent types enforce laws intrinsically, they push complexity to type-checker: checking $f^\sharp$ well-typed requires equality decision in $\mathcal{C}$, undecidable when $\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{Set}$ with $\beta\eta$. Lean4 implementation of $\mathbf{Poly}$ hits $universe$ bump: $Poly : Type_1$ not $Type$.

> **Open Conjecture:** *Every dependent optic arises as bicomodule between copresheaf comonoids.* Capucci et al. suggest via Yoneda $y: \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{C}^{op}, Set]$ embedding; proof requires showing every indexed category is equivalent to $\mathcal{C}_{/p}$ for some polynomial $p$ – essentially Giraud's theorem for locally cartesian closed categories.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We traced a categorical arc from Foster's concrete $get/put$ lenses through profunctor optics' Yoneda encoding $\int_{P} [P(A,B),P(S,T)]$ to Spivak-Niu polynomial functors where $p(y)=\sum_{a} y^{B(a)}$ morphisms *are* dependent lenses, and finally to Vertechi dependent optics via indexed Tambara modules. Yoneda lemma is the unifying engine: once to identify profunctor with natural transformation space, again to collapse coend with representable, third time fibrated to fiber over position.

Bidirectional programming benefits pragmatically: schemas with $\Sigma$-types enforce invariants at type level, profunctor composition enables library-level fusion, and $\mathbf{Poly}$'s $\triangleleft$ tensor models sequential system interaction compositionally. The remaining gap – lattice of mixed dependent optics lacking canonical coproducts and effectful law classification – invites deeper 2-categorical and homotopical analysis.

> *An optic is not a data accessor: it is a protocol for maintaining consistency across representation change, and its best syntax is a polynomial with a proof that Yoneda knew it all along.*
