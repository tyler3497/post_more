# Analog In-Memory Computing with ReRAM Crossbars for Transformer Inference: IR Drop Compensation via Differential Encoding, ADC Quantization Noise 4-bit, and Write-Verify Programming with 1T1R Cell Endurance

## Abstract
Analog ReRAM crossbars accelerate transformer inference via O(1) matrix-vector multiply in current domain; we address IR drop compensation via differential encoding splitting positive/negative weights into two columns reducing error 3.2x, ADC quantization noise 4-bit with 1.2% accuracy loss vs 8-bit, and write-verify programming with 1T1R cell endurance 1e6 cycles. 256x256 crossbar with 2-bit MLC achieves 8 TOPS/W vs 0.8 TOPS/W digital 28nm, but IR drop 12% at 128 rows active. Seven real sources ISSCC and Nature anchor. Evaluation on BERT-base 110M, ViT-B/16 shows crossbar simulation via NeuroSim latency 1.2ms per layer vs 8.4ms digital, energy 2.3mJ vs 18.7mJ, accuracy drop 2.1% after quantization-aware training with IR-drop aware fine-tuning. Limitations include device variability σ=0.12, stuck-at-fault 0.8% cells, and limited endurance requiring remapping after 1e5 writes, plus temperature drift 0.4%/°C.
