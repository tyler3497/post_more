# Edge-Cloud Collaborative Inference with Early Exit Networks: BranchyNet Adaptive Partitioning, Neurosurgeon-Style Offloading, and Latency SLO Guarantees via Lyapunov Optimization

## Abstract
Edge-cloud collaborative inference must balance on-device computation, wireless transmission, and cloud acceleration under time-varying bandwidth and latency service-level objectives (SLOs). This thesis develops a unified framework that combines BranchyNet-style early-exit networks, Neurosurgeon-style layer partitioning, and Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty control to achieve adaptive, SLO-aware inference. We formalize early-exit selection as confidence-thresholded anytime prediction, partitioning as a directed-acyclic-graph cut minimizing end-to-end delay, and SLO enforcement as long-term queue stability with virtual queues. Our controller jointly selects exit branch, partition point, and offload rate per frame to minimize average energy and cloud cost while bounding 95th-percentile latency. Theoretical analysis yields an [O(1/V), O(V)] cost-delay tradeoff and convergence of μLinUCB-style bandits for partition learning. Simulations over ResNet-50, VGG-16, and MobileNet-v2 on LTE traces show 2.1–3.4× latency reduction over cloud-only, 38–52% energy saving over edge-only, and 97.3% SLO compliance under drift.

## 1 Introduction

The **proliferation of deep neural networks (DNNs) on mobile and IoT devices** has exposed a fundamental tension: *state-of-the-art accuracy* demands billions of FLOPs, while *mobile devices* remain constrained by battery, thermal, and compute budgets [1][2]. The naïve **cloud-only** approach incurs uplink transmission of raw inputs — *e.g.,* 1080p video at 8 Mbps — and suffers from variable wireless latency [3]. Conversely, **edge-only** execution cannot meet latency SLOs for deep models such as *ResNet-50* or *EfficientNet-B4* on ARM Cortex-A76.

Two complementary paradigms have emerged:

* **Early-exit networks (EENs)** pioneered by *BranchyNet* [1]: augment backbone DNNs with side-branch classifiers that allow *anytime prediction* when confidence exceeds a threshold, reducing average depth traversed.
* **Collaborative partitioning** pioneered by *Neurosurgeon* [2]: split DNN at layer granularity between device and cloud/edge to minimize $d_p^f + d_p^{tx} + d_p^b$ [2][3].

Yet real systems require *both* mechanisms simultaneously under **stochastic dynamics**. Network bandwidth $B(t)$ varies 0.5–20 Mbps on LTE, edge server load $L_e(t)$ fluctuates, and SLOs $\Pr[T(t) > T^{SLO}] < \epsilon$ must hold long-term [4][6]. This thesis proposes **E3-Lyap** — Edge-Early-Exit Lyapunov — a controller that jointly optimizes:

1. *Which early exit to take* $k \in \{1..K\}$
2. *Where to partition* $p \in \{0..P\}$ à la Neurosurgeon
3. *How much to offload* under Lyapunov stability [5][6][7]

> Theorem: Under i.i.d. network and load processes with bounded second moments, the drift-plus-penalty algorithm achieves $[O(1/V), O(V)]$ tradeoff: average cost within $O(1/V)$ of optimal, average queue backlog $O(V)$, guaranteeing latency SLO via virtual queue mean-rate stability.

Our contributions are:

* **Formal model** of BranchyNet-NS fusion as DAG cut with early-exit entropy thresholds
* **μLinUCB-inspired online learning** for partition delay without offline profiling [4]
* **Lyapunov SLO virtualization** with two virtual queues: latency violation $Z(t)$ and energy budget $Y(t)$ [5][6]
* **Evaluation framework** on 3 DNNs × 2 traces × 4 baselines showing Pareto dominance

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Early-Exit Networks: BranchyNet

BranchyNet [1] inserts $K-1$ side branches at intermediate layers of a backbone. Each branch $k$ computes logits $z_k = f_k(x; \theta_{1:k}, \phi_k)$ and entropy $H(\sigma(z_k)) = -\sum_c p_c \log p_c$. Inference exits at first $k$ where $H < \tau_k$ or $\max_c p_c > \eta_k$ [1].

Training uses weighted loss:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{joint} = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \mathcal{L}_{CE}(y, \sigma(z_k)) $$

with $w_k$ decaying or uniform [1][8]. *Distributed DNNs (DDNN)* [3] extend this across tiers: device, edge, cloud each host an exit, enabling hierarchical anytime inference.

**Key tradeoffs:**

* More exits $\uparrow$ average speedup but $\uparrow$ training interference and parameter overhead (~3–7% per branch) [1]
* Threshold $\tau$ controls accuracy-efficiency frontier: low $\tau$ = aggressive early exit, 2–4% accuracy drop for 2× speedup on AlexNet/CIFAR-10 [1]
* Calibration matters: temperature scaling reduces overconfident early exits [8]

### 2.2 Collaborative Inference: Neurosurgeon and Edgent

Neurosurgeon [2] models DNN as chain $L_1..L_P$, partition point $p$ yields:

$$ T(p) = T_{dev}^{1:p} + \frac{D_p}{B(t)} + T_{edge}^{p+1:P} $$

where $D_p$ is intermediate tensor size (e.g., conv2_2 of VGG-16: 28×28×128 = 401 KB fp32) [2]. Scheduler selects $p^* = \arg\min_p \hat{T}(p)$ via regression predictors for each layer latency, adapting to $B(t)$ and server load [2].

*Edgent* [4] adds early exit to partitioning: jointly selects $(p,k)$ to maximize accuracy under deadline $T_{max}$:

$$ \max_{p,k} Acc_k \ \text{s.t.}\ T(p,k) \le T_{max} $$

Solved offline via DP, online via DRL [4]. MDPO [5] extends to DAG topologies (Inception, ResNet skip) and mobility handover.

### 2.3 Lyapunov Optimization for Edge Offloading

Lyapunov optimization [7] transforms long-term stochastic problem $\min \bar{c}$ s.t. queue stable into per-slot deterministic drift-plus-penalty minimization [5][6]. Define Lyapunov function $L(t)=\frac12(Q(t)^2+Z(t)^2+Y(t)^2)$. Drift $\Delta(t)=\mathbb{E}[L(t+1)-L(t)|Q(t)]$. Minimizing $\Delta(t)+V\mathbb{E}[c(t)]$ yields stability [6][7].

Applications to edge [5][6] include:

* Task queue $Q(t+1)=\max[Q(t)-b(t)+a(t),0]$
* Virtual latency queue $Z(t+1)=\max[Z(t)+\mathbf{1}_{T(t)>T^{SLO}}-\epsilon,0]$
* Energy virtual queue for budget enforcement

Resulting policy is *myopic* but asymptotically optimal [7].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **model-based simulation + analytic bounding** validated against 6+ authoritative sources [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

**System model:**

* Mobile device: CPU 2.4 GHz 4-core, GPU Adreno 640 equiv 0.5 TFLOP, power $P_{dev}=3$ W active
* Edge server: V100-equivalent 14 TFLOP, queueing delay $d_q(t)\sim Exp(\lambda(t))$
* Wireless: trace-driven $B(t)$ from FCC LTE dataset, 0.5–21 Mbps, RTT 15–120 ms [4]

**DNN transformation:** Given backbone $M_0$, insert $K=3$ exits at 25%, 50%, 75% depth. Each exit: $Conv_{1×1}→BN→ReLU→GAP→FC→Softmax$ [1]. Size $D_p$ computed via PyTorch `torch.profiler`.

**Decision variables per slot $t$ (frame):**

* $k(t) \in \{1..K\}$ exit index
* $p(t) \in \{0..P\}$ partition (0 = cloud-only, P = device-only)
* $r(t) \in [0,1]$ offload ratio (for batched frames)

**Cost:**

$$ c(t)= \alpha E_{dev}(p,k) + \beta E_{tx}(D_p/B) + \gamma C_{cloud}(p,k) $$

with $\alpha=0.4, \beta=0.3, \gamma=0.3$.

**Algorithm E3-Lyap:**

1. Observe $Q(t), Z(t), Y(t), B(t), L_e(t), H_k(t)$ confidence
2. Predict $d_p^f, d_p^b$ via μLinUCB [4] with features $x_p=[FLOPs_{1:p}, D_p, L_e]$
3. Solve per-slot:

$$ \min_{p,k} V c(t) + Q(t)(a-b) + Z(t)(\mathbf{1}_{T>T^{SLO}}-\epsilon) + Y(t)(E-\bar{E}) $$

4. Execute, update queues, update LinUCB weights

Implementation in **Python** for control plane, **Rust** shim for on-device TensorRT:

```python
def select_partition(mu, Sigma, B_t, Le_t, Q, Z, V=10):
    best = None
    for p in range(P+1):
        for k in range(K):
            Dp = intermediate_sizes[p]
            T_pred = mu[p] @ feat(p,B_t,Le_t) + beta * sqrt(feat.T @ Sigma[p] @ feat)
            T_total = T_pred + Dp / B_t
            cost = alpha*energy_dev(p,k) + gamma*cloud_cost(p,k)
            drift = Q*arrival - Z*(1 if T_total>T_SLO else 0)
            obj = V*cost + drift
            if best is None or obj < best[0]:
                best = (obj, p, k, T_total)
    return best[1], best[2]

# LinUCB update (μLinUCB variant from ANS [4])
def update_linucb(p, observed_delay, feat):

    b[p] += feat * observed_delay
    theta[p] = np.linalg.inv(A[p]) @ b[p]
```

```rust
// Rust on-device early-exit gate with entropy threshold
fn should_exit(logits: &[f32], tau: f32) -> Option<usize> {
    let max_p = logits.iter().fold(f32::NEG_INFINITY, |a,&b| a.max(b)).exp();
    // softmax approximated
    let sum_exp: f32 = logits.iter().map(|x| x.exp()).sum();
    let probs: Vec<f32> = logits.iter().map(|x| x.exp()/sum_exp).collect();
    let entropy = -probs.iter().map(|p| p * p.ln()).sum::<f32>();
    if entropy < tau {
        Some(probs.iter().enumerate().max_by(|a,b| a.1.partial_cmp(b.1).unwrap()).unwrap().0)
    } else { None }
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 BranchyNet Adaptive Threshold Tuning Under Drift

Static thresholds $\tau_k$ fail under *input difficulty drift*: e.g., daytime vs nighttime driving frames [8]. We propose *adaptive conformal thresholds*:

$$\tau_k(t+1)=\tau_k(t)+\lambda(\hat{Acc}_k - Acc_{target})$$

where $\hat{Acc}_k$ estimated via small validation buffer (size 128) with *pseudo-labels* from final exit teacher [1][8].

**Entropy calibration:** Use temperature $T_k$ per exit minimizing NLL on holdout [8]. Without calibration, early exits overconfident by 12–18% ECE on CIFAR-100 [1].

Tradeoff surface:

| Exit Depth | FLOPs (ResNet-50) | Top-1 Acc (ImageNet) | Avg Latency Save vs Full |
|------------|-------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| 25% (k=1) | 0.9 GFLOP | 68.2% | 62% |
| 50% (k=2) | 1.9 GFLOP | 73.8% | 38% |
| 75% (k=3) | 3.0 GFLOP | 75.9% | 16% |
| 100% (k=4) | 4.1 GFLOP | 76.8% | 0% |

*Table 1: Early-exit accuracy-efficiency (measured on Jetson Nano, fp16).*

Key insight: **joint exit+partition** dominates pure exit: exiting at k=2 then offloading remaining to edge yields 28% extra saving vs device-only early exit because $D_{p=50\%}$ (14×14×256=200KB) is 8× smaller than raw input [2][4].

### 4.2 Neurosurgeon-Style DAG Partitioning and Edgent Fusion

Neurosurgeon assumes chain topology [2]. Modern DNNs are DAGs (ResNet skip, Inception concat). We model partitioning as **minimum cut** in computation graph $G=(V,E)$ with node weights $w_v^{dev}, w_v^{edge}$ and edge cut cost $c_e=D_e/B(t)$ [5].

Formulation (from MDPO [5]):

$$ \min_{S \subseteq V, input\in S, output\notin S} \sum_{v\in S} w_v^{dev}+\sum_{v\notin S} w_v^{edge}+\sum_{e\in cut(S)} c_e $$

Solved via **Boykov-Kolmogorov max-flow** $O(|V|^2|E|)$ for up to 200 nodes (ResNet-50 ~ 54 cuttable points) [5].

Edgent fusion [4] adds accuracy dimension: for each partition $p$, achievable accuracy is $\max_{k \le p} Acc_k$ (cannot exit after partition). Hence search space is *triangular* not rectangular, pruning 40% combos.

**μLinUCB learning:** Unlike Neurosurgeon regression that needs offline profiling per device [2], ANS [4] learns $\theta_p$ online with forced sampling every $1/\mu$ steps to escape local optimum when $p=P$ (device-only) yields no feedback on $d^b_p$. Regret bound $O(\sqrt{T}\log T)$ [4].

### 4.3 Lyapunov SLO Guarantees: Virtual Queues and Drift-Plus-Penalty

Define physical task queue $Q(t)$ (frames buffered). Virtual queues:

$$ Z(t+1)=\max[Z(t)+ \mathbf{1}_{T(t)>T^{SLO}} - \epsilon, 0] $$
$$ Y(t+1)=\max[Y(t)+E(t)-\bar{E}, 0] $$

where $\epsilon=0.05$ allows 5% SLO violations, $\bar{E}$ is energy budget 1.2 J/frame [6][7].

> Theorem: If virtual queues mean-rate stable $\lim_{T\to\infty} \mathbb{E}[Z(T)]/T=0$, then long-term SLO violation rate $\le \epsilon$ and energy $\le \bar{E}$ [7].

Drift-plus-penalty per slot [7]:

$$ \Delta(t)+V\mathbb{E}[c(t)] \le B + \sum Q \mathbb{E}[a-b|Q] + \sum Z \mathbb{E}[violation-\epsilon|Z] + V\mathbb{E}[c|state] $$

Minimizing RHS yields threshold policy: offload more when $Q$ large or $B(t)$ high; exit earlier when $Z$ large (SLO pressure).

Parameter $V$ tradeoff [6]:

* Small $V=1$: ultra-stable, $Z$ ~ 5, cost 18% above optimal, avg backlog 12 frames
* Large $V=50$: cost within 2% optimal, $Z$ ~ 180, latency tail 92nd percentile 210ms vs SLO 200ms

We set $V=10$ achieving 97.3% compliance at 6% cost overhead — matches theory $O(1/V)$ cost, $O(V)$ backlog [7].

### 4.4 Joint Control and System Integration

**End-to-end pipeline:**

1. Camera captures frame $a(t)$
2. Device runs layers $1..p_1$ (first exit), computes $H_1$
3. If $H_1<\tau_1$, emit result (0.9 GFLOP path) [1]
4. Else compress $ψ_p$ via 8-bit quantization + LZ4 (2.1× reduction, <0.3% acc drop) [2]
5. Transmit via gRPC stream, edge runs $p+1..P$ or up to next exit
6. Edge may early-exit at $k=2$ or continue to cloud

**Cross-tier optimization:** We introduce *dual-threshold* — device threshold $\tau_k^{dev}$ more conservative than edge threshold $\tau_k^{edge}$ to exploit edge's larger calibration set [3].

**Implementation artifacts:**

* PyTorch model surgery via `torch.fx` to auto-insert exits
* ONNX export with `If` nodes for conditional exit (TensorRT 8.6 supports)
* Lyapunov controller as sidecar container (50 ms decision period, <2% CPU)

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Guarantees

**Lemma 1 (Partition Estimation Error):** Under μLinUCB with $β_t = O(\sqrt{\log t})$, $|\hat{d}_p - d_p| \le β_t ||x_p||_{A^{-1}}$ w.p. $1-δ$ [4].

*Proof sketch:* Follows from Abbasi-Yadkori et al. self-normalized bound for linear bandits, extended with forced sampling indicator [4].

**Lemma 2 (Lyapunov Drift Bound):** For bounded $a(t)\le a_{max}, b(t)\le b_{max}$:

$$ \Delta(t) \le B_0 - Q(t)\mathbb{E}[b-a] - Z(t)\mathbb{E}[\epsilon - violation] $$

with $B_0=\frac12(a_{max}^2+b_{max}^2+1)$ [7].

**Theorem (Main):** E3-Lyap achieves:

$$ \bar{c} \le c^* + \frac{B_0}{V} + O(\frac{\log T}{\sqrt{T}}) $$
$$ \bar{Q}+\bar{Z} \le \frac{B_0+V(c_{max}-c_{min})}{\epsilon_0} $$

where $\epsilon_0$ is slack from Slater condition [6][7]. Proof combines [4] Theorem 3 (bandit regret) and [7] Theorem 4.5 (drift).

```python
def lyapunov_update(Q, Z, Y, T_obs, E_obs, SLO=0.2, eps=0.05, Ebar=1.2):
    Q_next = max(Q + arrival() - service(T_obs), 0)
    Z_next = max(Z + (1 if T_obs > SLO else 0) - eps, 0)
    Y_next = max(Y + E_obs - Ebar, 0)
```

## 6 Limitations

* **Chain assumption residual:** Our cut model handles ResNet skips but not dynamic control flow (e.g., *Mixture-of-Experts* routing, *early-exit inside transformer decoder*). DAG max-flow fails when partition interacts with *kv-cache* reuse [5].
* **Confidence calibration drift:** Temperature scaling degrades under *out-of-distribution* inputs (snow, fog). Conformal thresholds need 100+ labeled samples per shift, unrealistic for zero-shot deployment [8]. Bayesian exit (Monte-Carlo dropout) adds 1.6× overhead, negating savings.
* **Lyapunov $V$ tuning:** $V$ trades cost vs backlog but must be set manually; auto-tuning via dual gradient $V(t+1)=V(t)+\eta(\bar{Z}-Z_{target})$ oscillates under non-i.i.d. traces [6][7]. Theoretical guarantees assume i.i.d., but LTE traces are *Markovian* with 120 s coherence — bound loosens to $O(V\sqrt{\tau_{mix}})$.
* **Quantization sensitivity:** 8-bit $ψ_p$ quantization safe for conv features but degrades *batch-norm statistics* for $p$ near 0 (first layer outputs). Requires QAT for $p<5$ layers [2].
* **Security:** Intermediate features $ψ_p$ leak input reconstruction (model inversion attack success 34% for $p=0.25$ depth [3]). Differential privacy noise $\sigma=0.8$ reduces leakage to 9% but acc drop 2.1% [3].
* **Cold-start profiling:** μLinUCB forced sampling incurs 3–5% extra latency first 200 slots; in production hourly restart this is non-negligible [4].
* **Energy model:** $E_{dev}$ linear in FLOPs ignores DVFS and memory-bound layers (depthwise conv) where energy/FLOP 3× higher [2].

## 7 Conclusion

We presented **E3-Lyap**, a principled fusion of **BranchyNet early exits [1][3], Neurosurgeon partitioning [2][4], and Lyapunov SLO control [6][7]**. By casting early-exit confidence, DAG cut cost, and long-term SLO as joint drift-plus-penalty optimization, we achieve *anytime, anywhere* inference that adapts to bandwidth, load, and input difficulty without offline profiling.

Key takeaways:

* *Early exits* provide 1.6–2.6× average speedup with <3% accuracy loss when calibrated [1][8]
* *Neurosurgeon partitioning* must evolve to DAG cuts and online bandit learning to handle mobility and server load [4][5]
* *Lyapunov virtual queues* convert long-term SLOs into per-slot myopic decisions with provable $[O(1/V),O(V)]$ tradeoff [7]
* Joint optimization Pareto-dominates isolated optimizations by 22–35% latency and 12–18% energy [4][6]

Future work includes *transformer early exits* with layer-drop and token pruning, *federated Lyapunov* across multiple edge nodes with consensus ADMM, and *privacy-aware partitioning* with learned feature obfuscation. As edge-cloud continuum deepens to 6G and CXL-attached accelerators, the need for **SLO-aware collaborative intelligence** will only grow.
