# Data Assimilation in Numerical Weather Prediction: Ensemble Kalman Filters, Four-Dimensional Variational Analysis, and Adjoint Methods

## Abstract

Data assimilation is the machinery by which the noisy, incomplete observations of the Earth system are fused with the governing dynamics of numerical weather prediction (NWP) models to estimate the atmospheric state. This thesis develops the two dominant operational paradigms from first principles: **sequential ensemble filtering** with the *ensemble Kalman filter* (EnKF), and **variational smoothing** with *four-dimensional variational assimilation* (4D-Var) built upon the *adjoint method*. We derive the Kalman update, expose the linearization assumptions that render it fragile in high-dimensional systems, and show how ensembles restore flow-dependent covariance information at affordable cost [1] [3]. We construct the variational cost functional, derive its gradient through the adjoint of the tangent linear model, and detail the incremental formulation of Courtier et al. that made 4D-Var operationally tractable [4] [5]. Hybrid ensemble–variational schemes, weak-constraint extensions admitting model error [2], and preconditioning [6] are examined. Empirical evidence from convective-scale radar assimilation [7] and the Lorenz-96 idealization reveals the characteristic failure modes — ensemble collapse, filter divergence, and adjoint linearization breakdown — and the remedies: covariance inflation and localization, outer-loop re-linearization, and bias-corrected weak-constraint 4D-Var.

## 1 Introduction

Numerical weather prediction rests on an uncomfortable truth: the *initial conditions* of the forecast matter at least as much as the model itself. Lorenz's discovery of sensitive dependence on initial conditions implies that even a perfect model produces useless forecasts within days if initialized poorly, while contemporary experience shows that improvements in the analysis have historically contributed as much forecast-skill gain as increases in model resolution. Yet the observing system is profoundly inadequate as a direct state measurement: satellites observe radiances rather than model variables, in-situ networks are sparse and irregular, and the model state dimension — of order $10^8$ for a global system — dwarfs the number of effectively independent observations.

Data assimilation is the discipline that reconciles these facts. Given a prior forecast (the *background*) with quantified uncertainty, and a set of observations with quantified error statistics, it seeks the state that is most consistent with both, in the precise sense of Bayes' theorem. Two lineages of solution have matured into operational practice:

1. **Sequential Bayesian filtering**, in which the analysis at each observation time is updated recursively from the forecast, following the Kalman program [1]. The ensemble Kalman filter replaces the explicit, intractable propagation of the error-covariance matrix with a Monte Carlo representation: an *ensemble* of model integrations whose sample statistics approximate the forecast-error covariance [3].
2. **Variational smoothing**, in which the best trajectory over a time window is found by minimizing a cost function penalizing deviations from the background and from all observations within the window. The gradient of this cost function with respect to the initial condition is computed with startling efficiency — one backward integration regardless of state dimension — by the *adjoint method* [4].

> The two approaches are often framed as rivals, but they are more properly understood as duals: both minimize, under Gaussian assumptions, the same Bayesian posterior; the filter computes it recursively while the variational method computes it as a smoother over a window.

This thesis derives both frameworks, compares their computational anatomies, and evaluates their empirical behavior on realistic and idealized testbeds.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Bayesian formulation

Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ denote the model state vector, with $n \sim 10^7$–$10^8$ in modern NWP. The background (prior forecast) $\mathbf{x}^b$ is assumed distributed as $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^b, \mathbf{B})$, and observations $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^p$ are related to the state through the observation operator $\mathcal{H}$ via $\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}) + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^o$, with $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^o \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{R})$. Under these Gaussian assumptions, Bayes' theorem yields a posterior that is also Gaussian, and its maximum — the analysis $\mathbf{x}^a$ — minimizes the three-dimensional variational (3D-Var) cost function

$$
J(\mathbf{x}) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^b)^T\mathbf{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^b) + \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{y}-\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}))^T\mathbf{R}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})).
$$

This is the foundation articulated by Lorenc [4, classical reference] and operationalized at every major NWP centre. The difficulty of the entire field is that $\mathbf{B}$ — the background-error covariance matrix — is *unknowable in closed form*: it is $n \times n$, impossible to store, let alone to evolve exactly through the nonlinear dynamics.

### 2.2 The Kalman filter and its curse of dimensionality

For linear dynamics $\mathbf{x}_k = \mathbf{M}_k\mathbf{x}_{k-1}$ and linear observations, the Kalman filter gives the exact recursive Bayesian solution. The forecast step propagates the covariance, $\mathbf{P}^f_k = \mathbf{M}_k \mathbf{P}^a_{k-1}\mathbf{M}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k$, and the analysis step applies the Kalman gain $\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}^f_k\mathbf{H}_k^T(\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{P}^f_k\mathbf{H}_k^T + \mathbf{R}_k)^{-1}$ to produce $\mathbf{x}^a_k = \mathbf{x}^f_k + \mathbf{K}_k(\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{x}^f_k)$. The elegance is exact; the cost is catastrophic. Propagating $\mathbf{P}$ requires, in principle, $n$ integrations of the model per cycle — roughly a hundred million model runs per assimilation window — which is computationally inconceivable.

Two escapes exist, and they define the field:

- **Fix the covariance**: optimal interpolation and 3D-Var assume a static, climatological $\mathbf{B}$. Cheap, but blind to the *flow of the day* — it cannot know that today's forecast is especially uncertain along a developing baroclinic zone and especially certain elsewhere.
- **Sample the covariance**: the EnKF [3] propagates an ensemble of $N \ll n$ states (typically 20–100) through the nonlinear model and forms $\mathbf{P}^f \approx \frac{1}{N-1}\mathbf{X}'{\mathbf{X}'}^T$ from ensemble perturbations. The covariance is rank-deficient but *flow-dependent*, and the cost is $N$ model integrations per cycle.

### 2.3 Variational smoothing and the adjoint miracle

The variational school takes a different route. Instead of propagating covariances, 4D-Var [4] [5] seeks the initial state $\mathbf{x}_0$ minimizing

$$
J(\mathbf{x}_0) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}_0-\mathbf{x}^b)^T\mathbf{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_0-\mathbf{x}^b) + \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\mathbf{y}_i - \mathcal{H}_i(\mathbf{x}_i))^T\mathbf{R}_i^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_i - \mathcal{H}_i(\mathbf{x}_i)),
$$

where $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathcal{M}_{0\to i}(\mathbf{x}_0)$ is the nonlinear model trajectory. The gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_0} J$ would naively require perturbing each of the $n$ state components — $n$ forward integrations. The *adjoint method* computes it with **one forward and one backward integration**, regardless of $n$. The adjoint model is the transpose of the tangent linear model, integrated backward in time, forced by observation departures; the resulting adjoint variable at $t_0$, combined with the background term, is exactly the gradient [5]. This is arguably the single most consequential algorithmic insight in the history of NWP: it made 4D-Var, and with it the direct assimilation of satellite radiances, computationally feasible at ECMWF from 1997 onward.

## 3 Methodology

We now give constructive, implementable statements of both algorithms.

### 3.1 The stochastic ensemble Kalman filter

Each ensemble member $k = 1,\dots,N$ is integrated forward with the full nonlinear model, $\mathbf{x}^{f,(k)} = \mathcal{M}(\mathbf{x}^{a,(k)})$, perturbed observations are formed as $\mathbf{y}^{(k)} = \mathbf{y} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(k)}$, $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(k)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{R})$, and each member is updated independently:

$$
\mathbf{x}^{a,(k)} = \mathbf{x}^{f,(k)} + \mathbf{K}\big(\mathbf{y}^{(k)} - \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}^{f,(k)})\big),
$$

with $\mathbf{K}$ computed from ensemble-estimated covariances. In practice, deterministic square-root variants (ETKF, LETKF) avoid perturbed observations and are preferred for their reduced sampling noise [3].

A minimal reference implementation of the perturbed-observation EnKF analysis step is:

```python
import numpy as np

def enkf_analysis(Xf, y, H, R, rho=1.0, loc=None):
    """Stochastic EnKF analysis.
    Xf : (n, N) forecast ensemble; y : (p,) obs; H : (p, n); R : (p, p).
    rho: multiplicative inflation; loc: (n, n) localization matrix or None.
    """
    n, N = Xf.shape
    xf_bar = Xf.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
    Xp = np.sqrt(rho) * (Xf - xf_bar)          # inflated perturbations
    Yp = H @ Xp                                # obs-space perturbations
    PfHt = (Xp @ Yp.T) / (N - 1)
    if loc is not None:                        # Schur-product localization
        PfHt = loc * PfHt
    S = (Yp @ Yp.T) / (N - 1) + R
    K = PfHt @ np.linalg.inv(S)               # Kalman gain
    Xa = np.empty_like(Xf)
    for k in range(N):
        yk = y + np.random.multivariate_normal(np.zeros_like(y),
                                               np.diag(np.diag(R)))
        Xa[:, k] = Xf[:, k] + K @ (yk - H @ Xf[:, k])
    return Xa
```

Two practical ingredients appear above and are *non-negotiable* in real systems: **inflation** ($\rho > 1$), countering the systematic underestimation of spread caused by sampling error and unrepresented model error; and **localization**, the Schur (element-wise) product of the sample covariance with a compact-support correlation function, which damps spurious long-range correlations arising from finite ensemble size [3].

### 3.2 Incremental 4D-Var

The operational form of 4D-Var is the *incremental* formulation of Courtier et al. [4] [5]. Rather than minimizing in the full nonlinear state, one linearizes around a reference trajectory and minimizes a quadratic cost in the increment $\delta\mathbf{x}_0$:

$$
J(\delta\mathbf{x}_0) = \tfrac{1}{2}\delta\mathbf{x}_0^T\mathbf{B}^{-1}\delta\mathbf{x}_0 + \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\mathbf{H}_i\mathbf{M}_{0\to i}\delta\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{d}_i)^T\mathbf{R}_i^{-1}(\mathbf{H}_i\mathbf{M}_{0\to i}\delta\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{d}_i),
$$

where $\mathbf{d}_i = \mathbf{y}_i - \mathcal{H}_i(\mathbf{x}_i)$ are the innovation departures and $\mathbf{M}_{0\to i}$, $\mathbf{H}_i$ are tangent linear operators. The gradient is

$$
\nabla J = \mathbf{B}^{-1}\delta\mathbf{x}_0 + \sum_{i=0}^{N}\mathbf{M}_{0\to i}^T\mathbf{H}_i^T\mathbf{R}_i^{-1}(\mathbf{H}_i\mathbf{M}_{0\to i}\delta\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{d}_i),
$$

and the adjoint variables are computed recursively backward in time, $\delta\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i = \mathbf{M}_{i+1}^T\delta\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i+1} + \mathbf{H}_i^T\mathbf{R}_i^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i$ with $\delta\hat{\mathbf{x}}_N = \mathbf{H}_N^T\mathbf{R}_N^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_N$ [2]. The decisive engineering advantage: the inner-loop minimization can run the tangent linear and adjoint models at **reduced resolution**, dramatically cutting cost, while periodic *outer loops* re-linearize around the updated trajectory [5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Why the EnKF works — and what it actually approximates

A subtle and widely misunderstood result clarifies the EnKF's theoretical status [1]: the EnKF does *not* sample the joint posterior over the trajectory (the object particle filters target), but rather a specific *marginal* — the filtering distribution at the current time. Linear analysis shows that the EnKF, viewed as a sequential sampler of the joint posterior, collapses exactly as particle filters do. Its practical success in NWP is therefore not paradoxical: for forecasting, the marginal is the operationally relevant object, and the EnKF approximates it well when the effective dimension of the unstable subspace is modest relative to ensemble size. This reframes "collapse" from a death sentence into a diagnostic: monitor the marginal, not the joint.

### 4.2 The anatomy of the adjoint computation

The adjoint derivation is best understood through the chain rule applied to the discrete model. If the forward model advances $\mathbf{x}_{i+1} = \mathcal{M}_i(\mathbf{x}_i)$ with tangent linear $\mathbf{M}_i$, then sensitivity of $J$ to $\mathbf{x}_0$ propagates as $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i}J = \mathbf{M}_i^T\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{i+1}}J + \mathbf{H}_i^T\mathbf{R}_i^{-1}(\mathcal{H}_i(\mathbf{x}_i)-\mathbf{y}_i)$. Integrated backward from the end of the window, this single sweep accumulates the contributions of *all* observations at *all* times into one gradient vector. The cost ratio is decisive: one adjoint integration costs roughly 1.5–2× a forward integration [5], so the gradient costs ~3 model integrations *total*, versus $n+1$ by finite differences. This constant-factor scaling in $n$ is what permits 4D-Var to assimilate millions of satellite radiances.

### 4.3 Weak-constraint 4D-Var: admitting the model is wrong

Strong-constraint 4D-Var assumes a perfect model: the trajectory is fully determined by $\mathbf{x}_0$. Operational models are imperfect, and forcing the analysis to satisfy a flawed model injects systematic error. *Weak-constraint* 4D-Var [2] augments the control vector with a model-error (bias) term $\boldsymbol{\beta}$, adding $\tfrac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\beta}^g)^T\mathbf{Q}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\beta}^g)$ to the cost and allowing the trajectory to deviate from pure model dynamics. ECMWF's Technical Memorandum 477 derives the corresponding adjoint forcing and shows how the bias control variable absorbs systematic tendencies, measurably improving stratospheric and tropical analyses [2]. The price is a larger, less well-conditioned minimization problem — which motivates preconditioning research such as randomized-SVD approximations to the control-variable transform that preserve time-parallelism [6].

### 4.4 Hybrid ensemble–variational assimilation: the operational synthesis

The contemporary frontier is the *hybrid*: use the EnKF's flow-dependent ensemble covariance to define the $\mathbf{B}$ matrix inside a variational minimization. Formally, $\mathbf{B} = \beta_c\mathbf{B}_c + \beta_e\mathbf{P}^e$, a weighted sum of a static climatological covariance and the localized ensemble sample covariance. The variational machinery contributes the adjoint-based handling of nonlinear observation operators and long windows; the ensemble contributes flow dependence without requiring a tangent linear and adjoint model for the covariance itself (in 4DEnVar, even the window propagation is replaced by ensemble trajectories) [4]. ECMWF's operational evolution — from 4D-Var to ensemble of data assimilations (EDA) to hybrid EDA–4D-Var — reflects the empirical finding that hybrids beat either parent on headline forecast scores.

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

### 5.1 Convective-scale radar assimilation with the EnKF

The canonical demonstration of EnKF capability at small scales assimilated simulated Doppler radar observations (radial velocity and reflectivity) into a cloud-resolving model of a splitting supercell [7]. Because covariances between *observed* radial velocity and *unobserved* temperature, moisture, and condensate are estimated from the ensemble, the filter retrieved accurate analyses of unobserved variables after roughly six radar volume scans (~30 minutes), purely through multivariate ensemble correlations — a feat essentially impossible for static-covariance methods at convective scales. Multi-case real-data experiments confirmed robustness across supercellular, linear, and multicellular regimes, with prior-fit RMS of 3–6 m s$^{-1}$ in radial velocity [7].

### 5.2 Lorenz-96 benchmarks and preconditioning

In the 40-variable Lorenz-96 model — the standard chaotic testbed for DA algorithms — the state formulation of incremental *weak-constraint* 4D-Var was solved with conjugate gradients, and randomized-SVD preconditioners for the control-variable transform were shown to accelerate the minimization in the first few iterations while preserving time-parallel structure [6]. The lesson generalizes: as operational windows lengthen and model error terms grow the control space, *preconditioning is not optional* — it determines whether the minimization converges within the operational time budget at all.

### 5.3 Comparative characteristics

| Property | Ensemble Kalman Filter | Incremental 4D-Var |
|---|---|---|
| Core object | Filtering marginal (sequential) | Window smoother (batch) |
| Covariance model | Flow-dependent, rank-$N$ sample | Static $\mathbf{B}$ (+ ensemble in hybrids) |
| Nonlinear observations | Handled via ensemble $\mathcal{H}$ | Tangent linear + adjoint of $\mathcal{H}$ |
| Cost per cycle | $N$ nonlinear integrations ($N\!\sim\!50$) | ~3–30 model integrations (inner × outer loops) |
| Needs adjoint model? | No | Yes (major development cost) |
| Typical failure mode | Ensemble collapse / filter divergence | Adjoint linearization breakdown; slow minimization |
| Standard remedies | Inflation, localization [3] | Outer loops, preconditioning [5] [6] |
| Operational home | Convective scale, ensembles | Global deterministic (ECMWF heritage) |

> Theorem: *Under linear dynamics, linear observation operators, and Gaussian errors, the Kalman filter analysis is the exact Bayesian posterior mean, and 4D-Var with a window of one observation time and exact minimization attains the identical analysis.* The practical divergence of the two methods therefore measures, precisely, the combined effect of nonlinearity, rank deficiency, and inexact minimization — the three great sources of suboptimality in operational data assimilation.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires cataloguing what remains unresolved:

- **Gaussianity is a fiction.** Real observation errors (notably for satellite radiances and radar reflectivity) and real background errors are non-Gaussian; both frameworks linearize and Gaussianize, and bias-correction schemes are Band-Aids on a structural wound.
- **Model error is the elephant in the room.** Weak-constraint 4D-Var [2] parameterizes it but cannot identify it uniquely from the same observations that estimate the state; the bias term risks absorbing signal that belongs to the initial condition.
- **Localization destroys balance.** Covariance localization in the EnKF, while statistically necessary [3], can disrupt geostrophic and hydrostatic balances, injecting gravity-wave noise that must be filtered by digital initialization or incremental analysis updates.
- **The adjoint is a maintenance burden.** Every change to the nonlinear forecast model — new physics, new numerics — demands a corresponding change to the tangent linear and adjoint codes, a cost that has driven centres toward adjoint-free ensemble-variational formulations.
- **Scalability walls persist.** Neither method has been demonstrated at full fidelity on the $10^9$-variable states implied by kilometre-scale global ensembles; the community is betting on hybrids, reduced precision, and machine-learned surrogates.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Data assimilation is where the mathematics of inverse problems meets the unforgiving reality of the atmosphere. The ensemble Kalman filter taught the field that *flow-dependent uncertainty can be sampled rather than derived*, buying adaptivity at the price of sampling noise that must be tamed by inflation and localization [1] [3]. Four-dimensional variational assimilation taught that *the gradient of a window-long misfit can be computed for the price of one backward integration*, buying global optimality at the price of an adjoint model and a fragile linearization [4] [5]. The empirical record — from supercell radar retrievals [7] to ECMWF's weak-constraint stratosphere [2] to preconditioned Lorenz-96 minimizations [6] — shows each method failing exactly where its founding assumptions break, and succeeding spectacularly where they hold. The operational future is neither pure filter nor pure smoother but the hybrid: ensemble covariances inside variational machinery, model error admitted as a control variable, and minimization accelerated by modern preconditioning. The atmosphere, indifferent to our algorithms, continues to reward whichever formulation most honestly quantifies what it does not know.
