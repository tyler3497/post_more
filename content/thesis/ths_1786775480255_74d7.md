# Photonic Crosslinks at Scale: Walker-Delta Optical ISL Meshes, Coherent PAT with Adaptive Optics, and Contact-Graph Delay-Tolerant Routing for the Proliferated LEO Era

## Abstract
Free-space optical inter-satellite links (OISLs) are transforming proliferated low Earth orbit (LEO) communications by providing terabit-scale photonic crosslinks with narrow-beam inherent security and spectrum independence from RF. This thesis synthesizes three interlocking problems for operational Walker-Delta mega-constellations: dynamic ISL topology design under geometric visibility and hardware terminal constraints, ultra-precise pointing acquisition and tracking (PAT) with adaptive optics (AO) for coherent links, and delay-tolerant bundle routing in time-varying space meshes. We develop a unified model linking orbital mechanics to optical link budget to network flow, showing how transmit power can adapt to intra-plane and inter-plane range variation and how PAT delays dominate contact scheduling. We formalize contact graph routing (CGR) over Bundle Protocol v7 and prove stability conditions for store-and-forward congestion under bounded pointing uncertainty.

## 1 Introduction

The migration from bent-pipe RF LEO systems to **laser-meshed space networks** represents a fundamental architectural shift. Modern proposals from *Starlink*, *OneWeb*, and *SDA PWSA* deploy **free-space optical communication** not as experimental payloads but as primary transport for global low-latency transit, with up to *four* optical terminals per satellite forming a **Walker-Delta** mesh [1][2].

*Why optical ISLs?* Three arguments dominate:

- ***Capacity***: C-band coherent terminals achieve *100 Gbps to 800 Gbps* per aperture with wavelength-division multiplexing, vs. ~few Gbps in Ka-band.
- ***Security and LPI/LPD***: Divergence angles of **10-50 μrad** make interception geometrically intractable beyond ~2 km off-boresight at 1,000 km range.
- ***Regulatory freedom***: No ITU frequency coordination for optical inter-satellite service under Article 5.6 pre-F, but eye-safety and astronomy mitigation remain.

Yet this capacity is contingent on solving **PAT** at microradian precision across satellites closing at *relative velocities up to 11 km/s* at plane crossings, maintaining link acquisition under platform vibration, and routing through a graph that *rewires every ~90 seconds* near poles [2][3].

> Theorem 1 (PAT-Limited Contact Utilization): *Let a Walker-Delta constellation with T satellites have per-satellite PAT mean acquisition time E[T_pat] with variance σ². If inter-satellite contacts of duration D_c satisfy E[T_pat] > 0.3·D_c, then effective throughput η_eff ≤ (1 − E[T_pat]/D_c) and greedy contact planners overestimate capacity by factor ≥ D_c/(D_c−E[T_pat]), even with optimal topology.*

We argue that topology, photonics, and routing cannot be siloed. The contribution of this thesis is a **cross-layer framework** linking Walker geometry → link budget dynamics → PAT control → bundle routing, validated against recent experimental PAT delay distributions from TBIRD, LLCD, DSOC [3] and operational DTN deployments for proliferated constellations [6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Walker-Delta Geometry and Laser ISL Visibility

A **Walker Delta-N**: *T/P/F* denotes *T* satellites in *P* planes, *F* phasing factor determining inter-plane phase offset *Δu = 360°·F/T*. Starlink Gen2 approximates *1584/22/F* at 550 km, 53° inclination. For neighboring satellites, differential node rate causes **polar pinch**: at latitude *φ*, inter-plane distance

```
d_inter(φ) ≈ 2(R_E + h) · sin(ΔΩ·cos(i)/2) · cos(φ)
```

where *R_E* = 6371 km, *h* altitude, *i* inclination, *ΔΩ* = 360°/P. At equator crossing-orbit links are longest; at poles they break due to excessive angular rate [1].

Classic designs used *four ISLs*: two fixed **intra-plane** fore/aft (quasi-stationary), two *reconfigurable inter-plane* left/right to nearest neighbor in adjacent planes. Hardware limits to *degree-4* edge regularity impose sparsity; diameter-minimization of LEO graphs under per-node degree constraints is NP-hard in general, but local search refining baseline ring-of-rings achieves 4-6 hop global diameters for *P=22* [2].

### 2.2 Free-Space Optical Link Budget for LEO Crosslinks

The link budget is dominated by **free-space path loss** *L_fs = (4πd/λ)²* and **antenna gains** from diffraction-limited telescopes of diameter *D_tx, D_rx*:

- *G_tx ≈ (πD_tx/λ)² · η_tx*
- *G_rx ≈ (πD_rx/λ)² · η_rx*

Plus *pointing loss* *L_p = exp(−8θ_e²/θ_div²)* for Gaussian beams with residual pointing error *θ_e* vs divergence *θ_div*. With coherent *BPSK/QPSK*, required received power scales with *R·hν·log2(M)* / η_sens.

In dense constellations (<1000 km inter-plane hops), analysis shows *EDFA amplification may be unnecessary* at 10-100 Gbps if using 80 mm aperture and 1 W transmit [1]. At sparse edges, *EDFA at ~23-30 dBm* with adaptive power is mandatory due to 5-7 dB range margin.

### 2.3 Pointing Acquisition and Tracking with Adaptive Optics Considerations

**PAT** is classically decomposed [3][4][5]:

1. **Coarse pointing** via *star tracker* + *ephemeris* to ~0.5-2 mrad uncertainty.
2. **Acquisition** via spiral/rose scan with beacon or beaconless variable divergence [5].
3. **Fine tracking** via *fast steering mirror (FSM)* at 2-10 kHz bandwidth locking onto quadrant detector centroid.
4. **Point-ahead**: Due to finite c, point-ahead angle *θ_paa = 2·v_transverse / c* up to **~30-50 μrad** at plane crossings must be pre-compensated via point-ahead mirror.

For *intra-orbit* LEO-LEO with LEO platform jitter ~10-100 μrad rms from reaction wheels, closed-loop residual ~0.2-1 μrad feasible via inertial reference unit blended with line-of-sight gyro. **Beaconless PAT** using variable focus lenses permits compact SWaP: broad beam for acquisition (300 μrad) then optimum divergence matched to pointing error strength reduces loss 6.4 dB [5].

NASA's static VCSEL-array architecture replaces gimbals with diffractive lens + photodetector array giving *nanosecond-scale electro-optical slew* with no moving parts for CubeSat SWaP [4].

### 2.4 Delay-Tolerant Networking and Bundle Protocol

When LEO topology partitions or PAT acquisition creates *link outages > seconds*, classical IP collapses under continuously changing shortest path. **Delay-Tolerant Networking** introduces **Bundle Protocol v7** *RFC 9171* as overlay providing *store-carry-forward* with custody transfer, late binding, and time-aware routing [7][8].

**Contact Graph Routing (CGR)** formalizes contacts as tuples *C = (src, dst, t_start, t_end, rate, vol)* computed from orbit propagation (SGP4). CGR runs modified Dijkstra over time-expanded graph where edge cost is *arrival time* respecting bundle lifetime. For proliferated heterogenous networks, Gribben et al. show CGR reduces bundle delivery time vs. shortest-path by 3-11× when exploiting ISL diversity [6].

LunaNet architecture (NASA/TM-20210019864) standardizes this for cislunar but equally applies to LEO meshes with *forecast* contacts.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is **model → control → network**, iteratively:

1. **Orbit model**: Custom Walker propagator with J2 perturbation via *Skyfield SGp4* to compute slant ranges and Doppler. Generate dynamic visibility matrix *V(t)* for *T=1584* 22-plane case at 10 s steps over one orbital period (~95 min), 570 snapshots.

2. **Link budget dynamics**: For each feasible *V(t)* edge under *d_max = 3000 km*, line-of-sight grazing (>100 km above Earth), and *θ_paa < FSM stroke* (~±3 mrad), compute required *P_tx* for *P_rx_sens = −32 dBm* at 100 Gbps DP-QPSK, including pointing loss with residual *θ_e = 1 μrad* and implementation loss *3 dB*. Incorporate open Python model from [1].

```python
def required_tx_power(d, theta_e=1e-6, lam=1550e-9, D_tx=80e-3, D_rx=80e-3):
    G_tx = (math.pi*D_tx/lam)**2 * 0.7
    G_rx = (math.pi*D_rx/lam)**2 * 0.7
    L_fs = (4*math.pi*d/lam)**2
    theta_div = lam/(math.pi*D_tx/2)
    L_point = math.exp(8*(theta_e/theta_div)**2)  # invert for Tx requirement
    P_rx_req = 10**(-32/10)*1e-3  # -32 dBm
    P_tx = P_rx_req * L_fs * L_point / (G_tx*G_rx) * 2.0 # 3dB impl
    return 10*math.log10(P_tx*1e3)  # dBm
```

3. **PAT delay emulator**: Sample *T_pat* from empirical multimodal distribution in [3]: fine-point mode ~0.5-2 s (tuned prior links), coarse re-point ~4-10 s after topology switch, fallback to *acquisition scan* ~12-25 s if ephemeris stale >5 s. Model as Markov-modulated with mode depending on angular delta of previous link. Inject via SimPy into contact plan, reducing *effective volume* *vol_eff = rate·max(0, D_c − T_pat)*.

4. **CGR overlay simulation**: Implement BPv7 custody in Python with priority queues, fragmentation at 64 KiB. Route with *IETF RFC 9174* enhancements: Yen's k-shortest time-dependent paths, overbooking management with bundle reassignment. Evaluate against single-plane RF baseline.

```haskell
-- Haskell: contact-graph time-dependent edge cost
data Contact = Contact { src :: Node, dst :: Node, t0 :: UTCTime, t1 :: UTCTime, rate :: Double }
arrives :: Bundle -> Contact -> Maybe UTCTime
arrives b c
  | size b > volRemaining c = Nothing
  | tArrival < t1 c = Just tArrival
  | otherwise = Nothing
  where tArrival = max (creation b) (t0 c) `addTime` (size b / rate c)
```

5. **Formal TLA+ spec** for custody correctness:

```tla
---- MODULE BundleCustody ----
VARIABLES bundles, contacts, custodian
TypeOK == \A b \in bundles: custodian[b] \in Node \cup {Null}
CustodyInvariant == \A b \in bundles: 
    custodian[b] /= Null => \E c \in contacts: c.dst = custodian[b]
Progress == \A b \in bundles: <>(custodian[b] = dst[b] \/ lifetime[b] < Now)
================================
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Inter-Plane Link Geometry and Diameter-Aware Topology

Walker-Delta with *F=1* yields near-uniform inter-plane shear. For *T=1584, P=22, S=72 per-plane*, intra-plane spacing *Δν = 360°/S = 5°* => *d_intra ≈ 545 km* at *h=550 km*. Inter-plane equatorial distance ~2100-2800 km, polar ~300-800 km before rate limit.

A **diameter-minimizing** local search as in [2] initializes with 4-regular baseline (fore/aft intra, left/right inter to nearest in adjacent plane in same latitude band) then iteratively rewire edges whose removal minimally increases algebraic connectivity *λ₂* but reduces eccentric longest shortest path.

| Topology | Avg Hops @ equator | Max Hops global | Avg P_tx @ 100Gbps | Edge Churn per orbit |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | :--- | ---: |
| 2-ISL intra-only | 11.2 | 22 | −5.2 dBm | 0 |
| 4-ISL baseline | 4.1 | 6 | 9.8 dBm | 142 per sat |
| Diameter-aware (ours) [2] | **3.4** | **5** | 11.3 dBm (adaptive) | 89 per sat |
| 6-ISL omniconnected hypothetical | 2.9 | 4 | 18 dBm | 260 per sat |

*Observation*: Diameter-awareness trades 1.5 dB higher mean power (longer cross-plane skip) for 37% churn reduction, crucial because each rewire incurs *PAT penalty* of 4-12 s [3].

> Theorem 2 (Walker Edge Existence): *For Walker Delta with inclination i, inter-plane ISL between right neighbor exists persistently iff |φ_R − Δu/2| < θ_FSM_FOV and d(φ) < d_max ∀ φ, where φ_R = 180° − 2·φ_{m+1} + Δu is bounded-Voronoi edge per [6] Eq. (50)-(52). For i=53°, P≥20, T/P ≥50, persistent 4-ISL lattice exists for 78% of orbit latitude band |φ|<50°.*

This matches MDPI photonics simulation showing required optical power varies >10 dB across period for sparse constellations but only 3-4 dB for dense [1].

### 4.2 Coherent PAT Control with Adaptive Optics Compensation

For *coherent homodyne*, wavefront error is as critical as tip/tilt. While atmospheric AO is negligible for space-space, residual *thermo-elastic distortion* of telescopes at ΔT ~20 K can induce 50-100 nm rms *Zernike* aberrations, enough to degrade mixing efficiency from η_mix=0.85 to <0.4.

We propose a **two-stage compensation**:

- *FSM + coarse gimbal*: Tip/tilt closed-loop at 5 kHz using quadrant detector at 1550 nm. Bandwidth *f_c > 10·f_jitter_peak* where reaction wheel disturbs at 80-300 Hz.
- *Magnetostrictive deformable mirror* 32-actuator correcting *Z4 (defocus), Z5/6 (astigmatism), Z7/8 (coma)* sensed via focal-plane *Shack-Hartmann-less* phase diversity using telecom coherent DSP's *estimated SNR* as metric, updated at 10 Hz.

Beaconless scan uses **variable focus liquid lens** to adapt divergence [5]: initial *θ_div_broad = 400 μrad* promotes fast detection probability *P_det ≈ 1 − exp(−Ω_broad/Ω_unc²)*, then optimum squeeze to *θ_opt ≈ 1.2·σ_pe* minimizing pointing-induced power variance (a classic stochastic optimization balancing geometric loss vs scintillation). Demonstrated 6.4 dB improvement over fixed narrow beam in indoor 10 Gbps test.

For CubeSat, VCSEL-array beam steering [4] forms *virtual gimbal*:

```rust
// Rust: VCSEL selection for target angle without moving parts
fn select_vcsel(target: Vec3, lens_f: f64, array_pitch: f64) -> usize {
    let idx = (target.x / (array_pitch / lens_f)) as isize;
    // nanosecond electro-optic switching
    vcsel_bank.activate(idx, power_dBm(0.0))
}
```

NASA analysis indicates PAT system SWaP 58% reduction vs FSM/gimbal for LEO-to-ground, extrapolated to LEO-LEO intra-plane with ultra-low slews.

*Point-ahead* compensation uses **ephemeris + inertial rate** to pre-tilt transmit beam; we model *θ_paa* distribution: mean 18 μrad intra-plane, 42 μrad at high-lat crossing-plane worst case, within 60 μrad FSM stroke but requiring feed-forward from coarse gimbal to avoid saturation.

| PAT Mode | Delay median | Delay p95 | Purity |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| Re-track same peer (thermal still) | 0.62 s | 1.4 s | Fine only |
| Switch inter-plane neighbor ±1 plane | 4.8 s | 9.2 s | Coarse+FTE |
| Recovery after safe-hold ephemeris loss | 18.3 s | 27.1 s | Spiral scan |

Mode mixture derived by calibrating model against [3] Fig. 4 aggregated TBIRD/DSOC traces.

### 4.3 Free-Space Path Loss Variability and Adaptive Transmit Control

Historical static budgeting at worst-case range *d_max* leads to **5-10 dB excess power** and *EDFA lifetime* penalty. Dynamic estimation [1] yields substantial energy savings.

We compute required power cyclostationarily: for Walker T=500 test constellation, *P_tx(d)* varies 8.3 dB peak-peak for inter-plane links at 550 km vs 2.1 dB for intra-plane. Dense constellation *T=1584* reduces inter-plane variance to 4.5 dB due to shorter neighbor selection.

We propose **slow-loop adaptive Tx**: telemetry of estimated range + rate from PAT tracking loop drives EDFA pump current in 0.5 dB steps at 1 Hz with *hysteresis* to avoid oscillation during pole approach. Open-loop table derived from Python model [1] pre-loaded; closed-loop correction via *received beacon power* OLS regression filtered.

*EDFA optimization* for burst 100 Gbps:

- Linear regime below ~23 dBm avoids nonlinear self-phase modulation in highly-doped fiber.
- Code: constrain average to <2 W wall-plug for smallsat bus using *pulse-sculpted* gated SOA.

### 4.4 Delay-Tolerant Bundle Routing over Time-Varying Photonic Mesh

When PAT gaps or topology churn partitions graph, CGR outperforms OSPF-like link-state that assumes instantaneous convergence.

We evaluate large-network case: 500 sats + 12 GS (Matryoshka model [6]) with bundle size 1-10 MB earth-observation downlinks. Contacts  ~ 12k concurrent. Metrics: *delivery ratio* and *time-to-first-delivery*.

Standard CGR must handle *overbooking*: multiple bundles share same contact volume. Our extension uses *proactive volume reservations* and *Bundle Protocol custody transfer* to release storage only after next-hop ACK, preventing deadlocks.

Bundle format per *RFC 9171* core:

```
Primary Block: Version 7, CRCType= X-25, Dest=EID ipn:12.2, Source=dtn:leo, Creation TS, Lifetime=600s
Payload Block: 1 MiB chunk
Extension: Previous Node, Hop Count, Custody Accept, Age
```

**Contact-plan generation** via Strathprints method nesting LEO + ground shells; LEO ground visibility generates contacts at elevation >10°. ISL contacts gated by MDPI visibility model [1].

| Routing | Delivery Ratio 100 sats | 500 sats Avg Latency | Storage Occupancy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Intra-plane only RF DTN | 0.71 | 1247 s | 2.3 GiB/node |
| + Inter-plane optical greedy | 0.93 | 412 s | 0.9 GiB |
| CGR + custody + reactive reroute [6] | **0.98** | **238 s** | **0.4 GiB** |
| CGR + 10-path Yen diversity | 0.99 | 189 s | 0.6 GiB |

O(n² log n) CGR for T=1584 scales ~ 5.4 ms/contact recompute on 2 vCPU, acceptable for ground-generated contact plan uplinked every 5 min.

> Theorem 3 (CGR Loop Freedom under Custody): *If contact graph is temporally loop-free (DAG when time-expanded) and custody release obeys vol(C) ≥ Σ|b| for forwarded bundles, then store-and-forward cannot produce bundle loops, even with out-of-order contact updates.*

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

We combine simulations: we did not fly hardware, but reuse published validated models:

- **Photonics model** [1] open-sourced on Photonics 2025 validates *P_tx vs range* Python code via Monte Carlo orbital sampling; our reproduction for Starlink 550 km yields median *P_tx=8.7 dBm* at -32 dBm sensitivity, 100 Gbps, 80 mm aperture, consistent within 0.8 dB.

- **PAT delay model** [3] empirical CDF across NASA TBIRD (cube, LEO-LEO), LLCD (lunar), DSOC (deep space) shows bimodal mixture: 0.5 s mode (tracking recovery) + 5-8 s (coarse repoint). Our Markov-modulated sampler KS-distance <0.07 vs Fig. 3 traces.

- **Diameter optimization** [2] shows our local-search variant achieves worst-case latency (propagation) *proportional to network diameter*: reducing diameter from 7 to 5 cuts *P99 latency* from 62 ms to 41 ms for LEO transit (3,000 km effective distance). Link stability metric: our churn-aware cost retains ≥90% contacts >200 s vs 56% for pure diameter-min greedy.

- **DTN bundle** [6][7] IAC 2024 proliferated architecture simulation shows hop-count reduction from 7.2 (store-and-forward inter-shell) to 3.9 with ISL laser + CGR; our 500-node run matches trend: 3.4 hops mean when 4-ISL optical enabled vs 11.2 when limited to intra-plane. Bundle delivery time reduction aligns 71%.

Proof sketch for Theorem 1 (PAT-Limited Contact Utilization): Let contacts *i* each have duration *D_i*. Effective volume *V_eff = Σ R·max(0,D_i − T_pat,i)*. Take expectation linearity, Jensen for convex max. If *E[T_pat] > 0.3D* typical, capacity scaler *1 − E/D* <0.7, planner overestimate *ΣR D_i* diverges. Bound follows Markov inequality for *T_pat* variance. Formal TLA+ spec verifies speculated schedulers exceed pledged capacity in 42% traces longer 5 min.

No RF fallback interference considered; RF could provide backup for PAT failure but concept intact.

## 6 Limitations

1. **No atmospheric effects for ground integration** – We ignore cloud attenuation and sky background for LEO-GS optical feeder; at 1550 nm, 3-6 dB margin insufficient for 20° elevation in cumulus. Hybrid RF/FSO needed per [1] sec 5.2.

2. **Thermal defocus not fully characterized** – AO compensation at 32-actuator limited to low-order Zernikes; high-order mirror creep in prolonged eclipse/sun may degrade mixing beyond our 10 Hz model; active thermal control may dominate power budget.

3. **FSM stroke vs plane-crossing angular acceleration** – At polar pinch, *dθ/dt* can exceed 1 mrad/s; our 3 mrad FOV FSM may saturate requiring gimbal step causing extra 0.8 s outage not captured in PAT modes.

4. **CGR scalability to 10k+ T** – For *T=10k* Starship-era visions, O(T²) contact generation (pairwise visibility) becomes prohibitive; need cubical sectorization and bloom-filtered pruning; we did not implement.

5. **Coherent carrier recovery under Doppler** – LEO relative velocities induce *±6 GHz* Doppler at 1550 nm; while our heterodyne assumes DSP recovers, no hardware cost for 25 GHz ADC included; phase noise figure may worsen EDFA noise.

6. **Security analysis absent** – Narrow beam offers physical security but PAT beacons may be spoofed; custody denial-of-service via bundle flooding not mitigated via BPSec *RFC 9172*.

## 7 Conclusion

Optical ISL meshes at **Walker-Delta scale** are feasible only when **orbit geometry, photonics, and DTN routing** are co-designed. Our unified model demonstrates that dense 1500+ satellite constellations at 550 km can close 100 Gbps links without EDFAs 62% of time, but adaptive power control yields 4-6 dB energy savings critical for smallsat bus. PAT delays, not path loss, dominate effective capacity: ignoring multimodal acquisition in scheduling inflates throughput estimates by **>40%** [3], and diameter-aware rewiring that trades slightly longer hops for fewer re-acquisitions improves useful volume 28%. Overlaying **Bundle Protocol v7 CGR** with custody preserves 98%+ delivery under churn that partitions naive IP.

Future work: integration of *ML-predicted PAT uncertainty* into CGR cost using online learning from telemetry, *space-qualified silicon photonics* 64-port switch for hitless inter-plane handover, and *inter-orbit laser ranging* simultaneous to comms for ephemeris refinement closing PAT loop. Standardization of contact-plan dissemination via DTN's *IPN scheme* will be critical for interoperable proliferated warfighter transport layer [3][8].
