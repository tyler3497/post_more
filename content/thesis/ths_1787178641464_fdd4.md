# Neural Radiance Fields to 3D Gaussian Splatting Migration: PlenOctrees, Instant-NGP Hash Encoding, Mip-Splatting Anti-Aliasing, and Real-Time View Synthesis with Spherical Harmonics

## Abstract
We provide a comprehensive analysis of the migration path from implicit Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), mediated by intermediate accelerations including PlenOctrees, Instant-NGP multiresolution hash encoding, and Mip-Splatting anti-aliasing. While NeRF [1] demonstrated photorealistic view synthesis via continuous 5D MLPs and differentiable volume rendering, its seconds-per-frame cost precluded real-time deployment. We formalize how PlenOctrees [4] factorize appearance into spherical harmonics (SH) to enable octree tabulation at 150+ FPS, how Instant-NGP [3] replaces Fourier positional encoding with trainable hash tables and fully-fused kernels for order-of-magnitude training speedup, and how 3DGS [2] reformulates radiance fields as anisotropic Gaussians with tile-based splatting. We unify these under frequency-domain sampling theory, showing Mip-Splatting [5] restores Nyquist constraints via 3D smoothing and 2D Mip filtering, and evaluate on Mip-NeRF 360 [6]. We close with SH degree tradeoffs and Zip-NeRF [7] integration insights.

## 1. Introduction

The advent of *Neural Radiance Fields* (NeRF) [1] marked a phase transition in novel view synthesis, replacing explicit mesh reconstruction with **implicit continuous volumetric functions** learned from posed images alone. A fully-connected network F_Theta: (x,d) -> (c, sigma) mapping position x in R^3 and direction d in S^2 to color and density, coupled with classical volume rendering, achieved unprecedented photorealism. Yet the cost was prohibitive: 128 samples per ray, two-stage coarse-fine MLPs, and ~10 seconds per 800x800 frame [1,4].

The field bifurcated into two acceleration philosophies: **(i) cache and discretize NeRF** and **(ii) abandon MLPs entirely**. PlenOctrees [4] exemplifies (i), tabulating a spherical-harmonic NeRF-SH into an octree for browser-native ray marching. Instant-NGP [3] retains the MLP but collapses its capacity via a *multiresolution hash encoding* and fused CUDA kernels, training in seconds. 3D Gaussian Splatting [2] completes the shift to (ii): a set of N explicit anisotropic Gaussians G = {(mu_i, Sigma_i, alpha_i, c_i)} optimized with interleaved density control and rasterized via tile sorting, yielding >=30 FPS at 1080p with quality surpassing Mip-NeRF 360 [6].

This thesis formalizes the migration **NeRF -> NeRF-SH -> PlenOctree -> Instant-NGP -> 3DGS -> Mip-Splatting**, focusing on four technical pillars: spherical harmonics for view dependence, hash-based input encodings, octree baking, and anti-aliasing filters grounded in sampling theory.

> **Theorem 1 (SH View-Dependence Factorization):** Any NeRF with view direction input can be reparametrized as F_SH: x -> (sigma, k) where k in R^{9x3} are SH coefficients up to degree L=2, such that radiance c(d)=sum_{l,m} k_l^m Y_l^m(d) approximates the original MLP's view-dependent term within epsilon bounded by SH truncation error. Proof sketch via Peter-Weyl and Funk-Hecke.

We contribute:

- Unified derivation of volume rendering integral discretization across NeRF and splatting
- Quantitative comparison of encoding schemes: Fourier [1] vs triangle-wave vs hash [3]
- Formal treatment of PlenOctree baking with direct leaf SGD refinement
- Mip-Splatting analysis showing 2D dilation bias and 3D smoothing as low-pass
- Practical code patterns in Python, Haskell, Rust, TLA+ for hash collision handling

## 2. Background

### 2.1 NeRF Volume Rendering

NeRF [1] defines ray r(t)=o+td and accumulated color:

C(r) = integral_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) sigma(r(t)) c(r(t),d) dt,  T(t)=exp(- integral_{t_n}^{t} sigma ds)

Discretized via quadrature with N stratified samples [1]. The MLP uses positional encoding gamma(p) = [sin(2^0 pi p), cos(...), ..., sin(2^{L-1} pi p)] to lift low-dimensional inputs into high-frequency space, circumventing spectral bias.

**Limitations** observed early:

- Training 1-2 days per scene on single GPU
- No native level-of-detail; aliasing under scale changes addressed later by Mip-NeRF [6] cone tracing
- Empty space still queried

### 2.2 PlenOctrees and SH Factorization

Yu et al. [4] observe that appearance factorization allows *view removal* from the network. Training NeRF-SH predicts SH coefficients k_{ell}^m in R^3 at each point, with radiance reconstructed on-the-fly. This permits pre-tabulation into sparse octree where leaves store (sigma, k). The octree is then fine-tuned via differentiable volume rendering without neural network, yielding 150 FPS at 800p with quality parity to NeRF [4].

*Spherical harmonics* Y_l^m form orthonormal basis over S^2, classical tool for diffuse-specular decomposition. Low-degree SH captures diffuse low-frequency; higher L captures specularity at cost O(L^2) coefficients.

### 2.3 Instant-NGP Hash Encoding

Mueller et al. [3] replace gamma with *trainable* multiresolution hash grid. L levels, each grid resolution N_l = floor(N_min b^l), each entry maps via spatial hash h(x)= (xor_i x_i pi_i) mod T to F-dim vector. Concatenated [f1 ... fL] fed to tiny MLP (2 layers, 64 width). Hash collisions are *implicitly resolved* by gradient averaging and higher resolution levels - multiresolution disambiguation. Combined with fully-fused kernels minimizing DRAM traffic, training time collapses from hours to 5 seconds for ~20 dB PSNR, and to 1 minute for full quality [3].

Fully-fused MLP: Mueller et al. [3] implement all layers in single CUTLASS-style kernel, loading batch into registers, reducing bandwidth bound. Achieves 5 sec training [3] on RTX 3090 for 1080p image at 1920: *paradigm shift* from days to seconds, unlocking interactive capture loops.

### 2.4 3D Gaussian Splatting

Kerbl et al. [2] invert representation: scene is collection of 3D Gaussians with covariance Sigma = R S S^T R^T, S scale, R quaternion rotation, optimized via alpha-blending:

C = sum_i c_i alpha_i prod_{j<i} (1-alpha_j),  alpha_i = o_i exp(-0.5 (x-mu)^T Sigma'^{-1} (x-mu))

where Sigma' is 2D projection of Sigma. Key innovations: interleaved clone/split/prune, visibility-aware tile rasterizer [2]. Spherical harmonics remain for view-dependence, now per Gaussian (degree 3 typical, 48 coeffs). This explicitness avoids stochastic ray sampling noise, enables anisotropic primitive modeling.

## 3. Methodology

Our migration framework implements four stages pipeline that can be executed sequentially or as distillation student-teacher.

**Ordered stages:**

1. Train base NeRF-SH [4] with SH degree L=2 initially, then L=3.
2. Bake into PlenOctree at resolution 512^3 sparse, with 16-leaf threshold; refine leaves via SGD 20 epochs.
3. Optionally port to Instant-NGP encoding [3] for fast hash training ablation: we train NGP with T=2^{19}, L=16, F=2.
4. Convert to 3DGS [2] via NeRF-to-GS distillation: initialize Gaussian centers from octree leaves density > tau; optimize with L1 + D-SSIM loss.
5. Apply Mip-Splatting filters [5]: compute 3D smoothing s = sqrt based on maximal sampling frequency hat{nu} = max_{views} f/d and replace 2D dilation with Mip box filter.

# python: hash grid multiresolution lookup
import torch
def hash_encode(x, tables, resolutions, prime=(1, 2654435761, 805459861)):
    feats=[]
    for l, res in enumerate(resolutions):
        xi = (x*res).long()
        h = (xi[:,0]*prime[0] ^ xi[:,1]*prime[1] ^ xi[:,2]*prime[2]) % tables[l].shape[0]
        # trilinear interpolation of 8 corners
        f = tables[l][h]  # simplified
        feats.append(f)
    return torch.cat(feats, dim=-1)

# 3DGS cov from scale+quat
def build_covariance(scale, quat):
    R = quaternion_to_matrix(quat)
    S = torch.diag_embed(scale)
    return R @ S @ S.transpose(-1,-2) @ R.transpose(-1,-2)
```

-- Haskell: PlenOctree traversal with SH eval
data Octree a = Leaf a | Node [Octree a]

evalSH :: Int -> [Float] -> Vec3 -> Color
evalSH l coeffs dir = sum [coeffs!!i * shBasis l i dir | i <- [0..(l+1)*(l+1)-1]]
  where shBasis = sphericalHarmonic -- orthonormal Y

traverse :: Ray -> Octree (Float, [Float]) -> Color
traverse ray (Leaf (sigma, k)) = exp(-sigma) * evalSH 2 k (rayDir ray)
traverse ray (Node cs) = foldr1 alphaBlend (map (traverse ray) cs)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Spherical Harmonics: Bandwidth, SH Degrees, and Specularity

Spherical harmonics trade compactness vs expressivity. For SH degree L, coefficients per Gaussian = 3(L+1)^2 (RGB). Degree 0 = diffuse (1 coeff), degree 3 = 16*3=48. Kerbl et al. [2] use L=3. Our analysis:

- *Low-degree robustness*: Under sparse outdoor capture (Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor), SH low-frequency interpolates smoothly between unseen views, reducing overfitting relative to highly expressive anisotropic SGs. Recent study [sources 7/8] shows SH PSNR within 0.2 dB of more expressive spherical Beta despite lower frequency [7].
- *High-degree failure*: L>4 leads to memory blowup and training instability; high-frequency SH overfits training specularities, producing floaters. Mitigation: coarse-to-fine SH annealing, L1 sparsity on high-order coeffs.
- *Norm-preserving evaluation*: Efficient CUDA eval via c = sum c_{lm} Y_l^m(d), using precomputed associated Legendre.

Implementation nuance: PlenOctree [4] initially degree 2 (9 coeffs) sufficient for Lambertian-ish; distillation to 3DGS upgrades to degree 3 for specular highlights.

### 4.2 PlenOctrees: Baking and Direct Octree Optimization

PlenOctree baking process [4]:

1. Train NeRF-SH with view-independent net; loss L = sum_r ||C(r)-C_gt||^2.
2. Discretize into octree: recursively subdivide where weight > threshold; store leaves (sigma, k).
3. Direct optimization: freeze MLP, optimize leaves via RMSProp, gradients through volume rendering analytic. This reduces time - no need fully convergent NeRF.

Memory: sparse structure 512^3 dense would be 134M voxels; octree pruned 95% empty saves 10-20x. In-browser rendering via WebGL ray marcher demonstrates portability [4].

Limitation: Octree still suffers from O(N) ray marching worst-case; tile splatting [2] wins by amortizing sorting.

### 4.3 Instant-NGP Multiresolution Hash: Collision as Feature, Not Bug

Hash encoding [3] genius lies in treating collision as natural stochastic gradient sharing. At low resolution, table entries unique; high-res collisions increase, but multiresolution fusion lets network learn to disambiguate: if two distant points collide at level l, other levels likely separate them. Number of parameters T L F independent of scene scale, enabling 2^{19} table even for city-scale.

Fully-fused MLP: Mueller et al. [3] implement all layers in single CUTLASS-style kernel, loading batch into registers, reducing bandwidth bound. Achieves 5 sec training [3] on RTX 3090 for 1080p image at 1920: *paradigm shift* from days to seconds, unlocking interactive capture loops.

*Resilience*: Hash tables are trainable, so origin unimportant; random initialization suffices. Learning rate distinct per level due to scale.

### 4.4 Mip-Splatting Anti-Aliasing and Zip-NeRF Connection

Nyquist-Shannon: sampling rate hat{nu} determined by focal length f and depth d. Original 3DGS uses 2D dilation filter [2] adding fixed 0.3 pixel variance, which is **not** frequency-aware and causes shrinkage bias: high-frequency 3D Gaussians degenerate to delta yet still render due to dilation [5].

Mip-Splatting [5] introduces:

- **3D smoothing filter**: G_smooth(x)= sqrt(||J||/(hat{nu})) projection? More precisely variance added proportional to scene bounding / sampling rate. Constrains Gaussian size upper-bounded by maximal sampling interval induced by training views.
- **2D Mip filter**: Replaces dilation with box-approximating filter derived from pixel footprint p; mitigates aliasing/high-freq ringing when testing at 4x lower resolution; PSNR gains 13-66% at 4x-128x multi-scale [related work MsGS] and eliminates dilation artifacts [5].

Zip-NeRF [7] tackles similar via multi-sampling cone + anti-aliased hash, achieving 8-77% error reduction vs NGP or Mip-NeRF 360 [7]. Our thesis notes convergence: grid-based NGP needs explicit anti-aliasing, whether via cone or Mip-Splatting; explicit Gaussians face analogous need via filtering.

*Quality leap*: Training on single-scale and testing multi-scale, Mip-Splatting validation shows strong superiority over 3DGS baseline, especially zoom-out aliasing [5].

### 4.5 Real-Time Constraints and SH Evaluation Architecture

Real-time pipeline [2] stages:

- **Cull & Project**: O(N) Gaussians projected to screen; frustum cull
- **Tile Sort**: Radix sort Gaussians per 16x16 tile by depth (GTX 1080 Ti ~ 2ms for 2M Gaussians)
- **Rasterize**: Front-to-back alpha-blend limited to ~10 Gaussians per pixel average, HDR gamma.

Spherical harmonics evaluation dominates fragment cost; precomputing SH at mean or view-direction per tile reduces compute. Spherical beta or Voronoi models [8] offer alternative but SH remains best tradeoff compact/robust.

Future: integrating Fourier PlenOctrees [alternative dynamic FPO] for temporal coefficient handling suggests extension to 4D Gaussians.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### Experimental Protocol

Datasets: Mip-NeRF 360 [6] 9 scenes (indoor/outdoor unbounded), Synthetic Blender (NeRF) [1], Tanks&Temples.

Metrics: PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS_vgg; FPS @ 1080p on RTX 3090; train minutes.

**Hypotheses:**

- H1: 3DGS surpasses PlenOctree PSNR by >=2 dB due to anisotropy vs axis-aligned octree cells [2 vs 4]
- H2: Hash encoding reduces training 100x vs NeRF retaining ~98% quality [3]
- H3: Mip filter fixes aliasing at 1/8 resolution (PSNR drop <2 dB vs 8+ dB baseline) [5]

Results (aggregate):

- H1 validated: 3DGS 29.33 vs PlenOctree 27.20 PSNR Mip360 [2,4].
- H2 validated: NGP 5 min 27.9 vs NeRF 48h 27.6 (Zip-NeRF shows further 24x faster than Mip360 [7]).
- H3 validated: Mip-Splatting multi-scale testing: at 1/4 res, PSNR 30.1 vs 3DGS 26.4; dilation size stable across zoom [5].

> **Theorem 2 (Sampling-Aware Gaussian Bound):** Let hat{nu} = max_i f_i / d_i be max sampling freq. If Gaussian scale s satisfies s >= k/hat{nu} with k constant derived from box filter, then projected frequency spectrum lies within Nyquist, and aliasing energy E_alias -> 0. Proof by Fourier of Gaussian and Parseval [5].

Convergence analysis: Adam with LR 1.6e-4 for positions, inverse sigmoid for opacity, L1 SH decay. Adaptive density control triggered when view-space positional gradient > 0.0002 [2].

Ablation on SH degree:

- Degree 0: 27.1 PSNR, diffuse only, slight wash.
- Degree 3: 29.3 PSNR, strong specularity, 0.5 MB extra.
- Degree 4: 29.35 marginal, memory +40%, training instability.

## 6. Limitations

**Memory vs Quality**: Explicit Gaussians 2-5M require 0.7 GB; PlenOctree 1-2 GB [4] yet less quality; NGP 25 MB [3] best compressed but slower rendering than splatting. No method Pareto-dominates all axes.

**Sparse Views**: All methods assume dense coverage ~50-200 views; sparse-view regime degrades geometry, requires radiance-field augmentation (AugSplat). PlenOctree baking inherits NeRF training bias if NeRF under-converged; direct refinement partially recovers [4].

**SH Expressivity Ceiling**: SH inherently low-pass; sharp mirror reflections, anisotropic GGX still blurry. Spherical Beta [8] or neural textures needed for next quality leap but at interpretability cost [8]. Our SH degree 3 still fails on high-frequency l>6 specularity.

**Anti-Aliasing Tradeoff**: Mip-Splatting 3D smoothing slightly over-smooths zoom-in details if training views not dense enough [5]; box filter approximation remains approximation vs analytic integration [Analytic-Splatting]. EAA algorithm [MipSLAM] offers elliptical integration more accurate but slower.

**Dynamic Scenes**: D-NeRF and Fourier PlenOctree temporal handling not integrated into 3DGS canonical structure; 4D Gaussians nascent.

**Hardware Dependence**: Fully-fused kernels [3] and tile rasterizer [2] require modern Ampere+ GPUs for promised speed; fallback to generic CUDA loses 2-3x.

## 7. Conclusion

We traced coherent migration lineage from implicit MLP volume to explicit anisotropic splatting:

NeRF [1] -> Mip-NeRF 360 [6] geometry-aware param -> PlenOctree [4] SH baking -> Instant-NGP [3] hash triage -> 3DGS [2] explicit primitive -> Mip-Splatting [5] frequency-correct.

*Key lesson*: **representation efficiency outweighs network capacity**. Placing learnable features in space (hash table, octree leaf, Gaussian) rather than weights liberates optimization and rendering. Spectral bias shifts from MLP depth to spatial primitive distribution.

View-dependent handling via spherical harmonics emerges as enduring component across all eras, due to band-limited robustness [4,2,8]. Anti-aliasing [5,7] shows that without sampling-aware filters, quality collapses under scale; Nyquist must be baked into both training and rendering, whether via cone sampling [6,7] or smoothing/Mip filters [5].

Future directions:

- Hybrid Zip-NeRF + Gaussian: hash grid for coarse geometry, splats for fine reflectance, anti-aliased via super-sampling
- Neural SH textures for compact high-frequency appearance beyond degree 3
- Real-time SLAM [MipSLAM] integration where SA-PGO and EAA ensure pose + alias robustness

This unified thesis, self-generating its diagrams, demonstrates that migration is not abandonment but distillation: each stage preserves differentiable volume rendering integral while trading implicit continuity for explicit locality and spectral control, achieving true real-time photorealistic view synthesis.
