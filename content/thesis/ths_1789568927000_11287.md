# Spectral Sequences in Algebraic Topology: Exact Couples, Fibration Spectral Sequences, and the Adams Resolution of the Stable Homotopy of Spheres

## Abstract

Spectral sequences are the central computational apparatus of algebraic topology, converting geometric filtrations into algebraic machines whose successive pages approximate homology, cohomology, and stable homotopy groups through a tower of differentials. This article develops the theory from Massey's exact couples to the four great spectral sequences of twentieth-century topology: the Serre spectral sequence for fibrations, which unlocks the cohomology of loop spaces and yields Serre's theorem on the finiteness of the homotopy groups of spheres; the Leray spectral sequence, which organizes sheaf cohomology over a continuous map; the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence, which computes arbitrary generalized cohomology theories from ordinary cohomology via the skeletal filtration; and the Adams spectral sequence, which expresses the stable homotopy groups of spheres as derived functors of Hom over the Steenrod algebra and underpins the nilpotence and periodicity theorems of chromatic homotopy theory. We emphasize convergence criteria, multiplicative structure, transgression, extension problems, and the limits of computability.

## 1 Intro

In algebraic topology, exact sequences rarely suffice. The long exact sequence of a fibration relates three objects at a time; when the geometry presents a tower of interrelated spaces — the skeleta of a CW base, the skeletal filtration of a spectrum — one needs a device that organizes an entire *tower* of long exact sequences simultaneously. The spectral sequence is that device: pages $E_r$ of bigraded groups, each with a differential $d_r$ of increasing bidegree, such that each page is the homology of the last, stabilizing under favorable circumstances to an associated graded of the object under computation [1][2].

The classical examples arise from fibrations. Given a Serre fibration $F \to E \to B$, the Serre spectral sequence begins with $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B; H^q(F))$ and converges to $H^{p+q}(E)$, turning knowledge of the base and the fiber into knowledge of the total space — or, run in reverse, knowledge of the total space and base into the homology of loop spaces [3][4]. Jean-Pierre Serre's 1951 dissertation deployed this machine to prove that the homotopy groups $\pi_i(S^n)$ are finitely generated for $i > n$, a result that reshaped homotopy theory and inaugurated the subject's modern computational era [5].

Beyond fibrations, the paradigm recurs. Leray's spectral sequence organizes sheaf cohomology over a continuous map; Atiyah and Hirzebruch showed that *every* generalized cohomology theory of a CW complex is controlled by a spectral sequence starting from ordinary cohomology with coefficients in the theory's coefficient groups; and Adams constructed a spectral sequence whose second page is $\mathrm{Ext}$ over the Steenrod algebra and whose abutment is the 2-primary stable homotopy groups of spheres [6].

This article develops these machines in a unified language: exact couples as the algebraic chassis, the Serre sequence with its multiplicative structure and transgression, the Leray and Atiyah–Hirzebruch sequences as instances of the same filtration philosophy, and the Adams spectral sequence through the cobar construction to the Hopf invariant one theorem. We close with limitations — extension problems, non-convergence, and the boundary between what spectral sequences promise and what they deliver.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Filtered complexes and the first spectral sequence

A *filtered complex* is a chain complex $C$ equipped with an increasing filtration $\cdots \subseteq F_{p-1}C \subseteq F_pC \subseteq \cdots$ compatible with the differential. The associated graded complex $\mathrm{gr}(C)$ has trivial differential only in degenerate cases; the spectral sequence interpolates between the homology of the associated graded and the associated graded of the homology. Concretely, one defines groups

$$E^r_{p,q} = \frac{Z^r_{p,q}}{B^r_{p,q} + Z^{r-1}_{p-1,q+1}}$$

where $Z^r_{p,q}$ denotes $r$-almost-cycles in filtration degree $p$ and $B^r_{p,q}$ denotes $r$-almost-boundaries. The differential $d^r: E^r_{p,q} \to E^r_{p-r,q+r-1}$ records the failure of cycles to persist deeper into the filtration. This *filtration spectral sequence* converges to $\mathrm{gr}(H(C))$ whenever the filtration is bounded below and exhaustive [1].

### 2.2 Exact couples

Massey's exact couple packages the same data without explicit cycle groups. An *exact couple* is a diagram of bigraded modules $D \xrightarrow{i} D \xrightarrow{j} E \xrightarrow{k} D$ exact at each vertex, with $i$ of bidegree $(-1,1)$, $j$ of bidegree $(0,0)$, and $k$ of bidegree $(1,0)$ in the homological convention [1][6]. The composite $d = j \circ k: E \to E$ satisfies $d^2 = 0$ because $k \circ j = 0$ by exactness, and one forms the *derived couple* with $D' = \mathrm{im}(i)$ and $E' = H(E, d)$. Iterating yields pages $E^r$ and differentials $d^r = j^{(r)} \circ k^{(r)}$ [1].

> **Theorem:** (Massey) Every exact couple of bigraded modules determines a spectral sequence, and the spectral sequence of a filtered complex arises from the exact couple built from the long exact sequences of the pairs $(F_pC, F_{p-1}C)$. The abutment is the associated graded of the colimit homology when the filtration is bounded below and exhaustive.

### 2.3 Convergence, edge homomorphisms, and transgression

A spectral sequence *converges* to a graded group $H$ if each $E^r_{p,q}$ stabilizes to $E^\infty_{p,q}$ and there is a filtration $F_pH$ with $E^\infty_{p,q} \cong F_pH_{p+q}/F_{p-1}H_{p+q}$. One writes $E^r_{p,q} \Rightarrow H_{p+q}$. Along the axes one finds *edge homomorphisms*: for a first-quadrant cohomological spectral sequence, $E_2^{p,0} \to E_\infty^{p,0} \hookrightarrow H^p$ and $H^q \twoheadrightarrow E_\infty^{0,q} \hookleftarrow E_2^{0,q}$, which in the Serre case become restriction to the fiber and pullback along the basepoint inclusion.

*Transgression* is the differential $d_r: E_r^{0,r-1} \to E_r^{r,0}$ in the cohomological Serre spectral sequence, defined on classes that survive to page $r$ in the leftmost column; its image lies in the bottom row. Transgressive classes mediate between the cohomology of the fiber and that of the base and generate the Gysin sequence for sphere bundles [3].

## 3 Methodology

Our method is uniform across all four spectral sequences: **choose a filtration, build the exact couple, derive the pages, and identify the $E_2$ term and abutment geometrically.**

| Spectral sequence | Filtration | $E_2$ term | Abutment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serre (homology) | Skeleta of the base $B$ | $H_p(B; H_q(F))$ | $H_{p+q}(E)$ |
| Serre (cohomology) | Skeleta of the base $B$ | $H^p(B; H^q(F))$ | $H^{p+q}(E)$ |
| Leray | Pushforward of injective resolution | $H^p(Y; R^qf_*\mathcal{F})$ | $H^{p+q}(X; \mathcal{F})$ |
| Atiyah–Hirzebruch | Skeleta of a CW complex $X$ | $H^p(X; E^q(\mathrm{pt}))$ | $E^{p+q}(X)$ |
| Adams | Adams resolution of the sphere spectrum | $\mathrm{Ext}^{s,t}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{F}_2, \mathbb{F}_2)$ | $\pi_{t-s}^S \otimes \mathbb{Z}_{(2)}$ |

*Table: the five machines, their filtrations, second pages, and abutments.*

For the Serre spectral sequence we filter the total space by pulling back the skeletal filtration of the base: $X_p = p^{-1}(B^p)$. The long exact sequences of the pairs $(X_p, X_{p-1})$ assemble into an exact couple whose derived pages have $E^1_{p,q} = H_{p+q}(X_p, X_{p-1})$ and, after identifying relative homology with the homology of the base tensored against fiber homology via excision and the Künneth theorem, $E^2_{p,q} = H_p(B; H_q(F))$ when $\pi_1(B)$ acts trivially on $H_*(F)$; in general one needs local coefficients [4][5].

The multiplicative structure is essential: the cup product on $H^*(E)$ induces a product on each page satisfying the Leibniz rule $d_r(xy) = d_r(x)y + (-1)^{|x|}xd_r(y)$, so that $E_{r+1} = H(E_r, d_r)$ as algebras [4]. Differentials are therefore derivations, and a single nonzero differential on a generator propagates multiplicatively.

For the Leray spectral sequence, given $f: X \to Y$ and a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$, one takes an injective resolution of $\mathcal{F}$, pushes forward, and filters by the resolution degree; the resulting Grothendieck spectral sequence reads $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Y; R^qf_*\mathcal{F}) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X; \mathcal{F})$.

The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence applies the same skeletal filtration to any cohomology theory $E^*$ represented by a spectrum: the cofiber sequences $X^{p-1} \to X^p \to X^p/X^{p-1}$ yield a long exact sequence in $E^*$, and the associated exact couple has $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X; E^q(\mathrm{pt})) \Rightarrow E^{p+q}(X)$.

The Adams spectral sequence is built differently: rather than filtering a space, one resolves the *sphere spectrum* by a tower of spectra $X = X_0 \leftarrow X_1 \leftarrow X_2 \leftarrow \cdots$ where each map induces a surjection on mod $p$ cohomology and each cofiber is a wedge of Eilenberg–MacLane spectra. Applying $\pi_*$ yields an exact couple with $E_2^{s,t} = \mathrm{Ext}^{s,t}_{\mathcal{A}_p}(\mathbb{F}_p, \mathbb{F}_p) \Rightarrow \pi_{t-s}^S \otimes \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, where $\mathcal{A}_p$ is the mod $p$ Steenrod algebra [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Exact couples in practice: the derived couple and bidegrees

The power of the exact couple formalism is that convergence and naturality become diagram chases. In the homological Serre exact couple one sets $D^1_{p,q} = H_{p+q}(X_p)$ and $E^1_{p,q} = H_{p+q}(X_p, X_{p-1})$, with $i$ induced by inclusion, $j$ the connecting quotient map, and $k$ the boundary of the pair. The bidegrees $(-1,1)$, $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$ for $(i,j,k)$ force the $r$-th differential $d^r$ to have bidegree $(-r, r-1)$: each derivation consumes one more unit of filtration depth [1][5].

A concrete implementation of the derived-couple iteration clarifies the bookkeeping. The following Python sketch computes successive pages of a finite first-quadrant spectral sequence given as bigraded vector spaces with matrices for the differentials:

```python
import numpy as np

def page_turn(E, d):
    """E: dict (p,q) -> dim; d: dict ((tgt),(src)) -> matrix of d_r.
    Returns E_next as dimension dicts."""
    E_next = {}
    for key, n in E.items():
        rank_out = sum(np.linalg.matrix_rank(M)
                       for (tgt, src), M in d.items() if src == key)
        rank_in = sum(np.linalg.matrix_rank(M)
                      for (tgt, src), M in d.items() if tgt == key)
        E_next[key] = (n - rank_out) - rank_in  # dim ker - dim im
    return E_next
```

An exact couple is a triangle of maps exact at each vertex; in Haskell one may encode the derived-couple step as a pure function on a record:

```haskell
data ExactCouple a = EC
  { bigD   :: [(Int,Int,a)]          -- D-module generators with bidegree
  , bigE   :: [(Int,Int,a)]          -- E-module generators with bidegree
  , iMap   :: a -> a                -- bidegree (-1, 1)
  , jMap   :: a -> a                -- bidegree (0, 0)
  , kMap   :: a -> a                -- bidegree (1, 0)
  }

differential :: ExactCouple a -> (a -> a)
differential ec = jMap ec . kMap ec   -- d = jk, and d^2 = 0 by exactness

derived :: ExactCouple [Int] -> ExactCouple [Int]
derived ec = EC
  { bigD = imageOf (iMap ec)
  , bigE = homologyOf (differential ec)
  , iMap = restrict (iMap ec)
  , jMap = inducedJ ec
  , kMap = inducedK ec
  }
```

And the filtration bookkeeping itself can be specified as a state machine, here in TLA+ for a bounded filtration whose associated spectral sequence must converge:


### 4.2 The Serre spectral sequence: fibrations, transgression, and the Gysin sequence

Let $F \to E \xrightarrow{p} B$ be a Serre fibration with $B$ simply connected (so local coefficients are unnecessary). The cohomological Serre spectral sequence has $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B; H^q(F)) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(E)$, first quadrant, with differentials $d_r$ of bidegree $(r, 1-r)$ [4][5]. Two structural facts drive every computation:

1. **The bottom row is the cohomology of the base.** $E_2^{p,0} = H^p(B)$; the edge homomorphism $H^p(B) \to H^p(E)$ is pullback along $p$.
2. **The left column is the cohomology of the fiber.** $E_2^{0,q} = H^q(F)$, and differentials leave it via transgression $\tau = d_r: E_r^{0,r-1} \to E_r^{r,0}$.

When the fiber is a sphere $S^{n-1}$, only rows $q = 0$ and $q = n-1$ are nonzero, and the spectral sequence collapses to a long exact sequence — the **Gysin sequence**:

$$\cdots \to H^{k-n}(B) \xrightarrow{\smile e} H^k(B) \xrightarrow{p^*} H^k(E) \to H^{k-n+1}(B) \xrightarrow{\smile e} \cdots$$

where $e \in H^n(B)$ is the Euler class, the transgression of the fiber's orientation class [5]. This single construction unifies the cohomology of projective bundles, the Thom isomorphism, and the classical Gysin sequence of sphere bundles.

### 4.3 The Leray spectral sequence and the Atiyah–Hirzebruch machine

Leray's construction predates Serre's and operates in sheaf cohomology. For a continuous map $f: X \to Y$ and a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$, the higher direct images $R^qf_*\mathcal{F}$ are the sheaves on $Y$ associated to $U \mapsto H^q(f^{-1}(U); \mathcal{F})$. The Leray spectral sequence

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Y; R^qf_*\mathcal{F}) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X; \mathcal{F})$$

expresses the cohomology of the total space through the base's cohomology with coefficients in the fiberwise cohomology sheaves [2]. When $f$ is a fibration with constant fiber cohomology, this recovers the Serre spectral sequence with local coefficients.

The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence generalizes the same idea to arbitrary cohomology theories. Let $E^*$ be represented by a spectrum $\mathbb{E}$, and let $X$ be a CW complex with skeleta $X^p$. The cofiber sequences $X^{p-1} \to X^p \to X^p/X^{p-1} \simeq \bigvee S^p$ give long exact sequences in $E^*$-cohomology; the exact couple has $E_1^{p,q} = C^p(X; E^q(\mathrm{pt}))$, the cellular cochains with coefficients in the coefficient groups, and hence

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X; E^q(\mathrm{pt})) \Rightarrow E^{p+q}(X).$$

For complex $K$-theory, $E^q(\mathrm{pt}) = \mathbb{Z}$ for even $q$ and $0$ for odd $q$ (Bott periodicity), so the differentials $d_{2r+1}: H^p(X; \mathbb{Z}) \to H^{p+2r+1}(X; \mathbb{Z})$ are stable cohomology operations — the first nontrivial one is related to $Sq^3_{\mathbb{Z}}$. For complex cobordism $MU^*$, the coefficient ring is the Lazard ring and the AHSS computes $MU^*(X)$ from ordinary cohomology [2][6].

Collapse criteria of this kind are the working topologist's bread and butter: they reduce the computation of $K^*(\mathbb{C}P^n)$ or $MU^*$ of familiar spaces to ordinary cohomology.

### 4.4 The Adams spectral sequence and stable homotopy

The Adams spectral sequence attacks the stable homotopy groups of spheres $\pi_*^S = \mathrm{colim}_n \, \pi_{*+n}(S^n)$, among the deepest unsolved computational problems in mathematics. Fix a prime $p$ (we describe $p = 2$). Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the mod 2 Steenrod algebra. An *Adams resolution* of a spectrum $X$ is a tower $X = X_0 \leftarrow X_1 \leftarrow X_2 \leftarrow \cdots$

with cofibers $K_s$ wedges of mod 2 Eilenberg–MacLane spectra and each map $X_s \to K_s$ surjective on mod 2 cohomology. Applying $\pi_{t}$ gives an exact couple whose $E_1$ page is a free resolution of $H^*(X; \mathbb{F}_2)$ over $\mathcal{A}$. Taking homology in $s$ yields [6]:

$$E_2^{s,t} = \mathrm{Ext}^{s,t}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{F}_2, H^*(X; \mathbb{F}_2)) \Rightarrow \pi_{t-s}(X)^{\wedge}_2,$$

converging to the 2-completed stable homotopy of $X$. For $X = S^0$ one obtains $\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{F}_2, \mathbb{F}_2) \Rightarrow \pi_*^S \otimes \mathbb{Z}_{(2)}$, the **classical Adams spectral sequence**.

The $E_2$ page is computed by the *cobar construction* or by minimal resolutions; its bigraded structure is displayed in an *Adams chart* with $t-s$ horizontal and $s$ vertical. The element $h_0 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,1}$ detects multiplication by 2; $h_1 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,2}$ detects the Hopf map $\eta$; $h_2 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,4}$ detects $\nu$; $h_3 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,8}$ detects $\sigma$. Adams's differential $d_2(h_4) = h_0h_3^2$ is the key step in the **Hopf invariant one theorem**: the only Hopf invariant one maps are $\eta, \nu, \sigma$ in dimensions $1, 3, 7$ [6].

The table below records the first stable stems as read off the Adams $E_2$ page through the range where differentials are fully understood:

| $k$ | $\pi_k^S$ | Detected by | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $1$ | Degree |
| 1 | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $h_1$ | Hopf map $\eta$ |
| 2 | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $h_1^2$ | $\eta^2$ |
| 3 | $\mathbb{Z}/24$ | $h_2$, $h_0^3h_2$ | Hopf map $\nu$; $d_3$ kills $h_0$-tower |
| 6 | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $h_2^2$ | $\nu^2$ |
| 7 | $\mathbb{Z}/240$ | $h_3$ | Hopf map $\sigma$ |
| 8 | $\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$ | $h_1h_3$, $h_0h_3$ | $\eta\sigma$, hidden extension |
| 9 | $\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3}$ | $h_1^2h_3$, $h_0^2h_3$, $h_1d_0$ | Multiple $h_0$-multiples |

*Table: low-dimensional stable homotopy groups of spheres via the mod 2 Adams spectral sequence [6].*

The Adams spectral sequence is the foundation of *chromatic homotopy theory*: the nilpotence and periodicity theorems of Devinatz–Hopkins–Smith, which organize $\pi_*^S$ by the height of formal group laws, are proved by analyzing the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence, the $MU$-based variant with $E_2 = \mathrm{Ext}_{MU_*MU}(MU_*, MU_*)$ [6].

## 5 Empirical and Proof-Theoretic Content

We sketch three complete computations that demonstrate the machinery, each verifiable against the cited references.

**Computation 1: $H^*(\mathbb{C}P^n; \mathbb{Z})$.** Apply the Serre spectral sequence to the Hopf fibration $S^1 \to S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb{C}P^n$. The $E_2$ page has nonzero rows only for $q \in \{0,1\}$. Since $H^*(S^{2n+1})$ vanishes in positive degrees below $2n+1$, the transgression $d_2: E_2^{0,1} = \mathbb{Z} \to E_2^{2,0} = H^2(\mathbb{C}P^n)$ is an isomorphism onto the generator $x$, and multiplicativity forces $E_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{n+1})$ with $|x| = 2$, collapsing for degree reasons [4][5].

**Computation 2: collapse of the AHSS for $K^*(\mathbb{C}P^n)$.** The $E_2$ page is $H^*(\mathbb{C}P^n; \mathbb{Z})$ concentrated in even bidegrees tensored with $\mathbb{Z}[\beta^{\pm 1}]$, $|\beta| = 2$. All differentials $d_{2r+1}$ go from even to odd total degree and hence vanish; the sequence collapses at $E_2$ and there are no extension problems over $\mathbb{Z}$ in even degrees, giving $K^0(\mathbb{C}P^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{n+1})$ with $x = 1 - [\gamma^1]$, the reduced Hopf bundle [2].

**Computation 3: the first differential in the Adams spectral sequence.** In $\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{F}_2, \mathbb{F}_2)$ one has $h_0 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,1}$, $h_1 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,2}$, $h_2 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,4}$, $h_3 \in \mathrm{Ext}^{1,8}$. Adams proved $d_2(h_4) = h_0h_3^2 \ne 0$ using secondary cohomology operations, showing no Hopf invariant one element exists in dimension 15 — the first case of the Hopf invariant one theorem [6].

The skeletal filtration of $B$ is bounded below and exhaustive, so the exact couple built from $(X_p, X_{p-1})$ satisfies the Mittag-Leffler condition; the standard exact-couple convergence theorem identifies $E^\infty$ with the associated graded of the skeletal filtration on $H_*(E)$ [1][5].

## 6 Limitations

Spectral sequences are not oracles; their limitations are structural.

- **The extension problem.** $E_\infty$ is only the *associated graded* of the abutment; reassembling $H_n$ from its filtration quotients requires solving extensions the spectral sequence does not determine. Hidden extensions, invisible on $E_2$, are endemic in the Adams spectral sequence.
- **Differentials are hard.** The machine promises that *some* differential computes the answer but does not name it. Determining $d_r$ requires external input: comparison maps, known low-degree groups, Steenrod operations, or Toda brackets.
- **Convergence failures.** Without boundedness hypotheses, $E^\infty$ may not exist or may not equal the associated graded; $\lim^1$ terms obstruct strong convergence (Boardman's conditional convergence).
- **Local coefficients and chromatic limits.** Non-simply-connected bases require twisted coefficients throughout. The Adams–Novikov spectral sequence detects periodic phenomena beautifully but is blind to certain torsion; the telescope conjecture was disproved in general, showing the chromatic picture has intrinsic limits.
- **Uncomputability in the large.** No practical algorithm computes $\pi_n(S^2)$ for all $n$; the groups grow wildly, and spectral sequences organize but do not tame this growth.

## 7 Conclusion

From Massey's exact couples to the chromatic reorganization of stable homotopy theory, spectral sequences have been the engine room of algebraic topology for three generations. The Serre spectral sequence made the homology of fibrations computable and proved the finiteness of higher homotopy groups of spheres; the Leray sequence wove sheaf cohomology into the same fabric; the Atiyah–Hirzebruch sequence showed that every generalized cohomology theory is, to first approximation, ordinary cohomology with exotic coefficients; and the Adams spectral sequence translated the geometric problem of stable homotopy into the algebraic problem of homological algebra over the Steenrod algebra, opening the road to nilpotence, periodicity, and chromatic homotopy theory.

The unity of the subject is the unity of the method: filter, form the exact couple, turn the pages, and read off the associated graded. Where the method stalls — extension problems, unknown differentials, convergence failures — it stalls informatively, marking the precise boundary of current knowledge.
