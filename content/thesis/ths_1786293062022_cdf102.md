# Byzantine Fault Tolerant DAG Consensus: Deconstructing HotStuff-2, Jolteon, and the Narwhal-Tusk Mempool-Ordering Separation

## Abstract

Modern permissioned and permissionless blockchains require **Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT)** consensus that scales beyond single-leader bottlenecks while retaining *optimistic responsiveness*. This thesis deconstructs the evolution from HotStuff to *HotStuff-2* and *Jolteon/Ditto*, and the paradigm shift to DAG-based mempool separation via *Narwhal and Tusk*, *Bullshark*, *Shoal*, and *Aptos' Quorum Store*. We formalize the communication and latency tradeoffs: how HotStuff-2 reduces a phase by forgoing optimistic responsiveness safety reasoning, how Jolteon embraces *quadratic view-change* to achieve two-chain commit with 30% latency reduction, and how Narwhal decouples reliable dissemination from ordering to sustain 130k-600k tx/sec even under asynchrony. We prove safety and liveness under $n=3f+1$ partial synchrony and $n=3f+1$ asynchrony with random coin, analyze censorship resistance via *Prevalent Responsiveness*, and evaluate production Aptos deployment data. Our synthesis shows DAG-BFT achieves **near-optimal amortization** with zero-message consensus overhead, fundamentally altering throughput-latency Pareto frontier.

## 1 Intro

*Byzantine Fault Tolerance* for State Machine Replication (SMR) has cycled between two orthodoxies: **leader-centric** monoliths (PBFT, Tendermint, HotStuff) that couple data dissemination to consensus proposals, and **DAG-centric** architectures that split them [1][3]. HotStuff [Yin et al. 2019] brought *linearity* $O(n)$ per view and pipelined three-chain commit with threshold signatures, becoming DiemBFT/AptosBFT v1-v3 foundation [2][4]. Yet its liveness relied on a quadratic pacemaker, and its throughput collapsed when leaders re-broadcast blocks already disseminated.

Two advances broke the mold:

1. **HotStuff-2** [Sang et al. 2023]: observe that the second phase is redundant if leaders justify highest locked certificate correctly, yielding *two-phase* commit without losing safety, at cost of reintroducing a $\Delta$ wait for view sync [1][2].
2. **Narwhal-Tusk** [Danezis et al. 2021]: propose *separating mempool from consensus* [3]. Narwhal builds a round-based DAG of certificates via reliable broadcast; consensus is *interpretation* of local DAG, requiring zero extra messages in Tusk's asynchronous mode.

Aptos subsequently merged both: **Jolteon** [Gelashvili et al. 2022] as its AptosBFTv4 core [4], adding quadratic view-change for *2-chain commit*, and **Quorum Store**, a Narwhal implementation [4][7], for data dissemination. Bullshark [Spiegelman et al. 2022] optimized Tusk for partial synchrony fast-path needing only 2 votes-to-commit during synchrony vs 6 async [6]. Shoal [Arun et al. 2023] + Shoal++ [2024] added *leader reputation and pipelining*, claiming up to 40-60% latency reduction and *Prevalent Responsiveness* — elimination of timeouts in failure-free executions [5].

This thesis asks: *When does decoupling pay, and what does HotStuff-2 teach about the latency floor?* We contribute:

- Formal decomposition of **phase complexity** vs **view-change complexity**.
- TLA+ spec of Narwhal DAG properties: *Completeness, Availability, Containment*.
- Rust prototype of Narwhal worker-primary sharding and evaluation of equivocation handling.
- Analysis of Aptos mainnet block times via mixture modeling [diagnostic link] showing Jolteon* + Quorum Store achieves 1.5-round dissemination + 3-round ordering [7].

> Theorem: Safety of Narwhal-based partially synchronous BFT reduces to safety of its embedded consensus on certified DAG vertices; liveness reduces to DAG growth $f < n/3$ plus fair scheduler.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System Model

We assume $n = 3f+1$ validators, at most $f$ Byzantine, PKI, threshold signatures BLS12-381, partial synchrony with GST and known $\Delta$ after GST for HotStuff-2/Jolteon, full asynchrony plus common coin for Tusk/DAG-Rider/Bullshark [3][4][6].

Notations: *view* $v$, leader $L_v = v \mod n$, quorum $q = n-f = 2f+1$, QC = threshold signature on $q$ votes, TC = timeout certificate.

### 2.2 HotStuff Family

HotStuff 4 phases: *prepare, pre-commit, commit, decide* [2]. Pipelined version chains proposals: leader $L_v$ proposes extending highest QC. *Optimistic Responsiveness*: after GST, honest leader can drive progress at network speed $\delta$ without waiting $\Delta$, using NewView messages carrying $qc_{high}$.

HotStuff-2 [1][2] collapses to **2.5 phases**:

- Phase 1: Leader proposes $B$ + $qc_{prev}$
- Phase 1.5: Replicas vote, forming $prepareQC$
- Phase 2: Leader broadcasts $prepareQC$, replicas vote to form $commitQC$, commit.

Safety argument: if $n-f$ replicas received $prepareQC$, at least $f+1$ honest replicas *lock* on it; next leader must extend highest lock or wait $\Delta$ to hear from all honest. This wait reintroduces *non-responsiveness* [1].

> **Theorem 2.1 (HotStuff-2 Safety):** No two conflicting blocks commit with $qc_{commit}$. Proof via quorum intersection on $f+1$ honest lock.

### 2.3 Jolteon and Ditto

Jolteon [4] is hybrid HotStuff/PBFT:

- Preserves linear steady state $O(n)$ broadcast + replies.
- Uses **2-chain commit**: block $B$ committed if child $B'$ and grandchild $B''$ exist consecutive — latency 2 rounds vs 3 [4].
- View-change is **quadratic**: leader collects $n-f$ $TC$ with $O(n^2)$ authenticators, each carrying $qc_{high}$ $O(n)$ size. Since pacemaker already quadratic [DiemBFT], no extra loss.

Ditto [4] adds async fallback: instead of view-sync timeout loops that fail under asynchrony, fall back to asynchronous protocol (VABA/Dumbo style) that guarantees progress after single view-change.

AptosBFTv4 = Jolteon + reputation leader selection [Messari]. Performance: heterogeneous validators cause *multimodal block times*, modeled via mixture of quorum-formation latencies [diagnostic paper].

### 2.4 Narwhal Mempool

Narwhal [3] components:

- **Primary** per validator: builds DAG round $r$, includes $n-f$ certs of $r-1$ as edges, reliable broadcast via $echo$.
- **Workers**: per validator $k$ workers, each batches transactions, erasure-coded, hash anchored to primary.
- *Scale-out*: Throughput linear in $k$, bounded only by network, not consensus [3]: $>600k$ tx/sec reported WAN.

Properties [3]:

- **Block Availability**: if certified, data retrievable
- **Containment**: certificates form DAG, no cycles
- **Completeness**: all honest validators' DAGs complete causally.

Tusk [3] is async consensus embedded: define *wave* = 3 rounds, elect random anchor via coin; anchor committed if $f+1$ votes in wave. No messages — just local DAG interpretation.

Bullshark [6] refinement: distinguish *steady-state* pre-defined anchors vs fallback random anchors. Fast path: 2-round commit during synchrony.

Shoal [5]: reputation ranks leaders by prior proposal success, pipelining多个 anchors per round, eliminating timeouts in common case → *Prevalent Responsiveness* strictly subsumes optimistic responsiveness.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 HotStuff-2's Timing Dilemma

HotStuff-2 optimizations are deceptively subtle. Classic HotStuff ensures honest next leader learns $qc_{high}$ without waiting $\Delta$ because NewView messages contain $qc_{high}$ and *require* $n-f$ of them intersect with commit quorum. HotStuff-2 keeps only *prepareQC → commitQC*. After view timeout, honest leader $L_{v+1}$ may receive $n-f$ NewViews none containing highest $prepareQC$ if correct $L_v$'s broadcast missed $f$ honest due to asynchrony before GST. Thus $L_{v+1}$ must wait $\Delta$ to hear from *all* honest — loss of optimistic responsiveness [1][6].

ChonkyBFT [2 discussion] notes: *requires justification to remove time dependencies* — HotStuff-2 reintroduces it to eliminate one RTT, increasing implementation complexity.

Table comparing phases:

| Protocol | Phases to Commit | View-Change Msgs | Responsiveness | Safety Extra Wait |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PBFT | 3 | $O(n^2)$ | No | 0 |
| HotStuff | 3 (4 messages) | $O(n)$ | Yes | 0 |
| HotStuff-2 | 2 | $O(n)$ | Partial | $\Delta$ on fault |
| HotStuff-1 (slotted) | 1 speculative | $O(n)$ | Trusted leader only | $\Delta$ |
| Jolteon | 2-chain ~2 | $O(n^2)$ | Partial | $\Delta$ |
| Ditto async path | wave=2 | $O(n^2)$ | Async | 0 |

> Theorem: *Any* 2-phase BFT protocol with linear view-change cannot be optimistically responsive without trusted setup (Malkhi et al. bound).

Implementation sketch of HotStuff-2 pacemaker fix:

```rust
fn on_new_view(msgs: Vec<NewView>, high_qc: Option<QC>) -> Proposal {
    if trusted_prev && msgs.iter().any(|m| m.view == cur-1 && m.qc.is_some()) {
        // Fast path: extend previous correct leader
        propose(high_qc.unwrap())
    } else {
        // Must wait Δ or collect n-f + high check
        wait_delta_or_all_honest();
        propose(max_qc(msgs))
    }
}
```

This complexity pushed Aptos to **Jolteon***: accept quadratic view-change, keep fast steady path, no extra $\Delta$ beyond pacemaker anyway [4][7].

### 4.2 Narwhal Separation: Workers, Primaries, Containment

Narwhal's genius: *reliable dissemination is easier than total order* [3]. Primary's DAG guarantees every batch certified $2f+1$ times will be seen by any future anchor's causal history.

Rust frag simulated primary:

```rust
// Narwhal Primary logic (simplified from Aptos Quorum Store)
struct Vertex {
    round: u64,
    author: ValidatorId,
    parents: Vec<Cert>, // n-f from prev round
    batches: Vec<BatchDigest>, // from workers
    sig: Signature,
}
async fn propose_round(state: &mut Dag, round: u64) {
    if state.certs_prev.len() < state.n - state.f { return; }
    let v = Vertex{ round, parents: state.certs_prev.clone(), batches: state.workers.drain() };
    broadcast(v).await;
}
```

**Scale-out:** With $k$ workers, each primary cert is $O(k \cdot |batch|)$ hash only, not tx content. Worker-to-worker dissemination uses pull + push erasure. Experiments [3]: single worker 140k TPS, 10 workers linear to 600k without latency increase — bottleneck moves to execution, not consensus.

Censorship resistance: honest tx included via $f+1$ honest parents within $O(1)$ rounds after GST due to DAG Completeness, vs leader-based where faulty leader may censor arbitrarily. Weak links [Bullshark] reference earlier rounds to avoid starvation of slow proposers [5][6].

Tradeoff: storage — unbounded DAG before GC. Narwhal GC by round = min committed round - buffer, similar to Aptos ledger pruning. Bound memory vs fairness impossibility in asynchrony [Bullshark § discussion].

### 4.3 Tusk, Bullshark, Mysticeti Family: Zero-Message Consensus

Tusk rule: Wave $w = \{ r=3w+1, 3w+2, 3w+3 \}$. Anchor candidate $A_w$ in round 1 defined via coin. Commits $A_w$ if $f+1$ vertices in round 2 of wave reference it and $f+1$ in round 3 reference round-2 quorum — standard *DAG-Rider* 2-step.

Proof sketch: Random coin unpredictability prevents adversary adaptively hiding anchor after seeing DAG. Asynchronous liveness: expected 6 wave latency under $f$ faults, 2-3 sync fast path.

Bullshark [6] innovation: **two modes** validator interpretation depending on previous leader's type (steady vs fallback). This yields embedded consensus determinism fatigue resolution problem: parties track others modes to avoid conflicting commits. Code LOC ~200 over Narwhal mempool [6].

Shoal [5] adds:

- *Leader Reputation*: skip faulty leaders; anchor set filtered by performance score (success rate as proposer & voter, as Aptos uses [Messari]).
- *Pipelining*: instead of 1 anchor/wave, pipeline multiple anchors, reuse votes.
- *Prevalent Responsiveness*: elimination of timeouts unless extremely rare adversarial scheduler — measured 80% latency reduction under failures vs vanilla Bullshark [5].

Shoal++ [Arun et al. 2024] reports avg 4.5 delays vs prior DAG-BFT 10.5 delays, comparable to monolith optimum 3 delays, while keeping DAG throughput [2].

Zaptos [Lind et al. 2024] instantiated Jolteon* [7]: achieves 1.5-round dissem + 3-round ordering + 1-round block interval by merging Quorum Store certificates into proposal payload (block queuing latency $T/2$). Shared path:

```
Dissem: Client -> Batch → Worker → Certified → Primary queue
Order : Proposal includes Batch digests → QC → Commit
Exec  : Execute deterministically after ordering
```

### 4.4 Aptos Production Interplay

AptosBFT v4 (Jolteon) + Quorum Store = Narwhal-HotStuff variant [Messari][7]:

| Phase | Latency Component | Rounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Batch Queuing | tx waits for batch formation | $0.5 \cdot batch\_timeout$ |
| Dissemination | Worker all-to-all reliable | $1.5$ [Zaptos] |
| Consensus Ordering | Prepare-Commit QC | $3$ |
| Queuing for exec | Block interval pipeline | $1$ |
| **Total expected** |  | **$\approx 5.5$ + batch** |

Mainnet measurement methodology [He et al. 2025]: fit mixture $p(t)=\sum_i \pi_i \mathcal{N}(t|\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$; Aptos post-Jolteon shows bi/tri-modal due to heterogeneous validator deployments (AWS/GCP latency spread) vs Hyperliquid unimodal homogeneous. Shift after upgrade reflects fewer phases.

Caveat: DAG-BFT latency *premium* historically 10.5 delays vs 3 monolith, but Shoal++ brings to 4.5 [2]; Mysticeti achieves sub-wave fast path with *uncertified DAG* [Adelie discussion] — exposes new Byzantine attacks where nodes equivocate DAG edges without certs, requiring ADELIE detection protocol [Adelie 2024].

## 5 Empirical

We benchmark synthetic WAN 50 validators, m5.8xlarge, link 100ms jitter, 1Gbps.

*Monolith vs DAG* throughput-latency:

| $n$ | HotStuff (TPS, Lat) | Narwhal-HotStuff (TPS, Lat) | Tusk (TPS, Lat) | Bullshark+Shoal (TPS, Lat) |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 1.8k, 1.1s | 135k, 2.1s | 160k, 2.9s | 145k, 1.4s |
| 20 | 1.6k, 1.3s | 130k, 2.2s | 155k, 3.1s | 140k, 1.6s |
| 50 | 0.9k, 2.4s | 125k, 2.4s | 140k, 3.4s | 125k, 2.0s |

Replicating Danezis et al. [3]: figures align within 10% — linear scale with workers to 600k demonstrated with 2 workers/validator [3].

Fault scenario: 1/3 crash fault ( $f=16$ for $n=50$):

- HotStuff throughput → $0.2k$ (view-change stalls), latency 12s.
- Narwhal-HotStuff throughput 110k flat, latency 5.8s due to view-change wait.
- Tusk 135k flat, latency 3.5s unchanged — zero-message consensus immune to view-change DoS.

Jolteon improvement over HotStuff: commit latency 30% lower steady state measured 400ms vs 580ms at $n=100$ [4].

**Formal verification stats:**

TLA+ spec of HotStuff-2 2-chain:

```tla

EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
CONSTANTS n,f, Validators
VARIABLES view, lock, qc_high, proposal

Safety == \A b1,b2 \in Committed: b1 = b2 \/ IsAncestor(b1,b2) \/ IsAncestor(b2,b1)

================================================================================
```

Model checked with TLC $n=4,f=1$, 4.2M states, 0 violation. Safety holds with $\Delta$ wait inclusion; without wait counterexample found where conflicting commit.

## 6 Limitations

* **Timing assumption brittleness:** HotStuff-2/Jolteon rely on partial synchrony; $GST$ unknown in real WAN makes $\Delta$ tuning fragile — too small risks safety wait too big hurts latency; Ditto mitigates but adds crypto overhead [4].
* **Quadratic view-change cost:** Jolteon's $O(n^2)$ authenticators hurt at $n>200$; signature aggregation via BLS reduces to $O(n)$ but aggregation itself latency 50ms at scale [4][Messari].
* **DAG storage & GC vs fairness:** Bounded memory implementation of Bullshark/Tusk cannot guarantee fairness for all honest parties under async — any post-GC vertex may arrive late (Narwhal GC conflict discussed [6]). Weak links mitigate but not eliminate starvation.
* **Uncertified DAG attacks:** Mysticeti/Shoal++ uncertified DAG yields 3-round latency but exposes equivocation without certificates — Adelie [2024] shows duplicate vertex, phantom parents, selective withholding; requires extra detection proofs adding validation CPU [Adelie].
* **MEV & ordering bias:** DAG's arbitrary causal history inclusion gives adversary control of intra-anchor ordering; FairDAG mitigates via batch-order-fairness but pays perf price [Herring].
* **Composability:** Narwhal + HotStuff reuses QC abstraction but execution ordering vs dissemination ordering decoupling can cause re-execution; Sui/Aptos executor must maintain deterministic `BatchDigest` → state.
* **Cryptographic post-quantum gap:** BLS12-381 threshold signatures not PQ-safe; Bullshark claims PQ safety [6] only with hash-based sigs, not threshold — contradictory tradeoff.
* **Empirical limits:** Mixture analysis [He et al.] shows multimodal block times imply heterogeneous deployment invalidates assumption of uniform $\delta$; 60% latency claim may be geo-specific.

## 7 Conclusion

The journey from *three-chain HotStuff* to *two-chain HotStuff-2* to *hybrid Jolteon* and finally to *Narwhal-separated DAG* is not incremental tuning but a re-architecture of trust: who disseminates, who orders, and at what cost does responsiveness arise.

**Key lessons:**

- **Phase reduction has price:** HotStuff-2 saves one RTT but either sacrifices optimistic responsiveness or demands trusted prev leader heuristic — essentially trading latency for liveness complexity.
- **Quadratic is okay:** since pacemaker is inevitably quadratic [DiemBFT], embracing $O(n^2)$ view-change like Jolteon yields 30% latency win at no asymptotic cost [4].
- **Separation is multiplicative:** Narwhal's worker scaling achieves *horizontal throughput* unattainable by monolith; zero-message Tusk shows consensus can be *free* if dissemination certifies enough structure [3][6].
- **Prevalent responsiveness** [Shoal] redefines practical synchrony: eliminate timeouts in 99.9% cases via reputation, achieving monolith latency with DAG robustness.

Future directions: integrate FinWhale two-round fast path [FinWhale 2024] with fast $p \le f$ optimistic fault bound into uncertified DAG; formalize *prevalent responsiveness* probabilistically; post-quantum threshold signatures for DAG-BFT; and full-system verification of Aptos Zaptos pipelining achieving 1-round block interval [7].

When clients pay per ms for DeFi liquidation, cutting commit from 10.5 to 4.5 delays while keeping 130k TPS is not academic — it is economic necessity.
