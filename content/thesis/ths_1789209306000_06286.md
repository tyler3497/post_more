# Quantum Channel Capacities: Classical, Quantum, and Private Rates — From the HSW and LSD Theorems Through Additivity Violations to Superactivation

## Abstract

The capacity of a noisy quantum channel — the maximum rate at which it can transmit classical bits, qubits, or secret key — is the central quantity of quantum Shannon theory. Unlike Shannon's classical capacity, captured by a single-letter mutual-information formula, quantum channels demand a hierarchy of capacities: the classical capacity $C(\mathcal{N})$ given by the regularized Holevo information through the HSW theorem, the quantum capacity $Q(\mathcal{N})$ given by the regularized coherent information through the Lloyd–Shor–Devetak theorem, the private capacity $P(\mathcal{N})$, and the entanglement-assisted capacities $C_E, Q_E$, which alone admit single-letter expressions. This thesis develops the full taxonomy, establishes the hierarchy $Q \le P \le C \le C_E$ with $Q_E = C_E/2$, and confronts the field's two great surprises: Hastings's 2009 random-unitary counterexample demolishing the additivity conjecture for minimum output entropy and the Holevo capacity, and the Smith–Yard discovery that two zero-quantum-capacity channels can jointly transmit quantum information — *superactivation*. Worked capacity computations for the erasure, depolarizing, and amplitude-damping channels ground the theory, and we explain why no finite single-letter formula for $Q$ can exist.

## 1 Introduction

In 1948, Claude Shannon reduced reliable communication over a noisy classical channel to a single number: the capacity $C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$, the maximum mutual information over input distributions [1]. The formula is *single-letter* — it involves only one channel use — because mutual information is additive under tensor products. Classical channel coding was thereby solved in one stroke.

Quantum channels resist such closure. A quantum channel $\mathcal{N}_{A'\to B}$ is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map, and the sender may ask several distinct questions: how many *classical* bits per use can be transmitted reliably? How many *qubits*? How many bits of *secret key*, hidden from an eavesdropper holding the environment? How do the answers change with unlimited prior entanglement? Each question defines a capacity — $C$, $Q$, $P$, $C_E$, $Q_E$ — each governed by its own coding theorem and entropic functional. The classical capacity requires regularizing the Holevo information over arbitrarily many uses (HSW) [1]; the quantum capacity requires regularizing the *coherent information* (LSD) [2]; the private capacity sits between them; only the entanglement-assisted capacities collapse to single-letter formulas [3].

The central drama is that *regularization cannot in general be removed*. Entanglement across channel inputs can strictly increase the relevant functionals — superadditivity — and two landmark results establish how extreme this gets. In 2009, Hastings constructed random unitary channels $\Phi$ with strictly subadditive minimum output entropy, $S_{\min}(\Phi \otimes \bar\Phi) < S_{\min}(\Phi) + S_{\min}(\bar\Phi)$, falsifying the additivity conjecture and, via Shor's equivalence chain, the additivity of the Holevo capacity [4]. In 2008, Smith and Yard exhibited a PPT entanglement-binding channel and a $50\%$ erasure channel, each of *identically zero* quantum capacity, whose tensor product has *strictly positive* capacity — $0 + 0 > 0$ [5]. Superactivation shows quantum capacity is not even a property of a channel alone, but of the context in which it is used.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Channels, isometries, and the environment

A quantum channel $\mathcal{N}_{A'\to B}$ is a CPTP linear map $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A'}) \to \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_B)$. Every channel admits a *Stinespring dilation*: an isometry $U^{\mathcal{N}}_{A'\to BE}$ with $\mathcal{N}(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}_E[U\rho U^\dagger]$. The *complementary channel* $\mathcal{N}^c_{A'\to E}(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}_B[U\rho U^\dagger]$ describes what leaks to the environment — the eavesdropper's view. Reliable quantum communication is possible precisely when the receiver $B$ obtains *more* of the input's correlations than $E$ does.

> **Theorem: Stinespring dilation.** For every CPTP map $\mathcal{N}_{A'\to B}$ there exist $\mathcal{H}_E$ and an isometry $U: \mathcal{H}_{A'} \to \mathcal{H}_B \otimes \mathcal{H}_E$ with $\mathcal{N}(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}_E U\rho U^\dagger$ for all $\rho$, unique up to an isometry on $E$.

### 2.2 Entropic quantities

For a state $\rho$, $S(\rho) = -\operatorname{Tr} \rho \log_2 \rho$ is the von Neumann entropy. For bipartite $\rho_{AB}$:

- *Quantum mutual information:* $I(A;B)_\rho = S(\rho_A) + S(\rho_B) - S(\rho_{AB})$
- *Coherent information:* $I(A\rangle B)_\rho = S(\rho_B) - S(\rho_{AB})$

Given $\mathcal{N}_{A'\to B}$ and pure input $\phi_{AA'}$ with reference $A$, set $\sigma_{AB} = \mathcal{N}_{A'\to B}(\phi_{AA'})$. The *channel coherent information* is

$$Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}) = \max_{\phi_{AA'}} I(A\rangle B)_\sigma = \max_{\phi_{AA'}} \big[S(B)_\sigma - S(E)_\sigma\big],$$

the second form via purification through the Stinespring isometry ($S(AB)_\sigma = S(E)_\sigma$). It measures the net flow of quantum correlations to Bob *minus* leakage to the environment — the no-cloning intuition quantified [2].

The *Holevo information* of an ensemble $\{p_x, \rho^x_{A'}\}$ is

$$\chi\big(\{p_x, \mathcal{N}(\rho^x)\}\big) = S\!\left(\sum_x p_x \mathcal{N}(\rho^x)\right) - \sum_x p_x S(\mathcal{N}(\rho^x)),$$

and the single-letter *Holevo capacity* is $\chi(\mathcal{N}) = \max_{\{p_x,\rho^x\}} \chi(\{p_x, \mathcal{N}(\rho^x)\})$.

### 2.3 Capacity definitions

All capacities are asymptotic: a rate $R$ is *achievable* if for every $\epsilon > 0$ there exist encoders/decoders over $n$ uses with error $\le \epsilon$ for all large $n$, transmitting $\lfloor nR \rfloor$ bits, qubits, or secret bits. The capacity is the supremum of achievable rates. Forward classical communication is free for $Q$ (it cannot increase it); two-way classical communication defines the distinct $Q_2, P_2$.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is that of quantum Shannon theory: *random-coding achievability* paired with *entropic converses*.

1. **Achievability.** Random code ensembles — random stabilizer codes for quantum transmission (LSD), random product-state codebooks for classical transmission (HSW), random private codes with wiretap-style binning (Devetak) — succeed via typicality, the packing lemma, and the *decoupling principle*: if the environment approximately decouples from the reference, $\rho_{RE} \approx \rho_R \otimes \rho_E$, then Bob can decode [3].

2. **Converse.** Any reliable protocol's rate is bounded by the regularized functional, using data processing, the quantum Fano inequality, continuity bounds (Alicki–Fannes), and chain rules. For $Q$: transmitting $nR$ qubits forces $I(R\rangle B^n) \ge nR - n\delta(\epsilon)$.

3. **Additivity analysis.** We ask when the single-letter quantity equals its regularization: $Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}) \stackrel{?}{=} n\,Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})$. Degradable channels ($\mathcal{N}^c = \mathcal{D} \circ \mathcal{N}$) are additive for coherent information; Hastings's random-unitary construction is the canonical non-additive example for the Holevo quantity [4].

4. **Numerical evaluation.** Section 5 computes single-letter quantities for canonical channels in closed form where available (erasure, dephasing, amplitude damping) and states the convex-optimization character of the depolarizing channel's coherent information.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The five capacity theorems and the hierarchy

| Capacity | Symbol | Formula | Single-letter? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical | $C(\mathcal{N})$ | $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n}\,\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})$ (HSW) | No (in general) |
| Quantum | $Q(\mathcal{N})$ | $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n}\,Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})$ (LSD) | No (in general) |
| Private classical | $P(\mathcal{N})$ | $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n}\,P^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})$ | No |
| Ent.-assisted classical | $C_E(\mathcal{N})$ | $\max_{\phi_{AA'}} I(A;B)_\sigma$ (BSST) | **Yes** |
| Ent.-assisted quantum | $Q_E(\mathcal{N})$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}\max_{\phi_{AA'}} I(A;B)_\sigma$ | **Yes** |

Here $P^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}) = \max_{\{p_x,\rho^x\}} [I(X;B) - I(X;E)]$ is the private information, and the BSST theorem (Bennett–Shor–Smolin–Thapliyal) gives $C_E(\mathcal{N}) = \max_\rho I(R;B)$ for $\sigma_{RB} = \mathcal{N}(\psi_{RA'})$ [3].

> **Theorem: HSW (Holevo–Schumacher–Westmoreland).** $C(\mathcal{N}) = \chi_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}) \equiv \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n}\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})$; regularization is necessary because the Holevo information can be strictly superadditive [1].

> **Theorem: LSD (Lloyd–Shor–Devetak).** $Q(\mathcal{N}) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n} Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})$. Coherent information is achievable: random stabilizer codes of rate $R < I(A\rangle B)$ succeed because the environment decouples from the reference [2].

The hierarchy follows from operational containments — qubits carry classical bits via $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$ encodings, and private bits are classical bits with a secrecy constraint:

$$Q(\mathcal{N}) \le P(\mathcal{N}) \le C(\mathcal{N}) \le C_E(\mathcal{N}), \qquad Q_E(\mathcal{N}) = \tfrac{1}{2} C_E(\mathcal{N}),$$

the last equality via teleportation (2 cbits + 1 ebit = 1 qubit) and super-dense coding (1 qubit + 1 ebit = 2 cbits) [3]. Devetak showed $P(\mathcal{N}) \ge Q(\mathcal{N})$ always, with the gap strict for some channels: certain PPT channels have $Q = 0 < P$, a separation with no classical analogue [3]. The assisted quantities are additive — $C_E(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{M}) = C_E(\mathcal{N}) + C_E(\mathcal{M})$ — because $I(A;B)$ is subadditive in the right direction under tensor products.

### 4.2 Degradable channels: where additivity survives

A channel is *degradable* if $\mathcal{N}^c = \mathcal{D} \circ \mathcal{N}$ for some CPTP $\mathcal{D}$ (the environment is a degraded version of Bob); *anti-degradable* if $\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{N}^c$. Then:

- Anti-degradable $\Rightarrow$ $Q(\mathcal{N}) = 0$: the environment simulates Bob, so no-cloning forbids reliable quantum transmission.
- Degradable $\Rightarrow$ coherent information is additive, and $Q(\mathcal{N}) = P(\mathcal{N}) = Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})$: a single-letter, efficiently computable formula.

The *erasure channel* $\mathcal{E}_p(\rho) = (1-p)\rho + p|e\rangle\langle e|$ is degradable for $p \le 1/2$ with $Q(\mathcal{E}_p) = \max\{0, 1-2p\}$, anti-degradable for $p \ge 1/2$. Dephasing and amplitude-damping channels are degradable with exactly known capacities. The *depolarizing channel* is neither — its quantum capacity remains unknown in closed form.

### 4.3 The additivity problem and Hastings's counterexample

For a decade the field conjectured $\chi(\mathcal{N} \otimes \mathcal{M}) = \chi(\mathcal{N}) + \chi(\mathcal{M})$, which would make $C(\mathcal{N}) = \chi(\mathcal{N})$ single-letter. Shor proved a web of equivalences: additivity of $\chi$ $\iff$ additivity of the minimum output entropy $S_{\min}(\mathcal{N}) = \min_\rho S(\mathcal{N}(\rho))$ $\iff$ additivity of the entanglement of formation, among others [4]. Felling one fells all.

Hastings's 2009 construction is probabilistic. Take a Haar-random isometry $U: \mathcal{H}_A \to \mathcal{H}_B \otimes \mathcal{H}_E$, define the random unitary channel $\Phi(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}_E[U\rho U^\dagger]$, and let $\bar\Phi$ be its complex-conjugate channel. For output dimension $d$ large, with non-zero probability,

$$S_{\min}(\Phi \otimes \bar\Phi) \le 2\log d - \frac{\log d}{d} \;<\; S_{\min}(\Phi) + S_{\min}(\bar\Phi),$$

strictly subadditive [4]. The mechanism: feeding the maximally entangled state $|\Phi^+\rangle_{AA'}$ into $\Phi \otimes \bar\Phi$ yields anomalously low output entropy, because the conjugate channel's correlations partially cancel the random unitary's scrambling — entangled inputs across the two channels beat every product input.

> **Theorem: Hastings (2009).** There exist channels $\Phi, \Psi$ (random unitary, $\Psi = \bar\Phi$) with $S_{\min}(\Phi \otimes \Psi) < S_{\min}(\Phi) + S_{\min}(\Psi)$. Hence the Holevo capacity is not additive, and the classical capacity has no single-letter formula in general [4].

The violation is small — order $(\log d)/d$ — and the channels live in enormous dimension, so no practical coding consequence follows. But the conceptual consequence is total: *entangled codewords across channel uses strictly outperform product codewords for classical communication*, and HSW regularization is genuinely necessary. Later work sharpened this: Cubitt et al. showed no finite $n$ always suffices even to detect nonzero capacity [6].

### 4.4 Superactivation: zero plus zero equals positive

Smith and Yard exhibited the most extreme additivity violation conceivable — for the *quantum* capacity [5]:

> **Theorem: Smith–Yard superactivation (2008).** There exist a PPT entanglement-binding channel $\mathcal{P}$ and a $50\%$ erasure channel $\mathcal{A}$ with $Q(\mathcal{P}) = Q(\mathcal{A}) = 0$ but $Q(\mathcal{P} \otimes \mathcal{A}) > 0$.

The ingredients:

1. **$\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{E}_{1/2}$** has $Q = 0$: at $p = 1/2$ the erasure channel is self-complementary — the environment gets exactly as much as Bob.
2. **$\mathcal{P}$** is PPT, so its Choi state has no distillable entanglement and $Q(\mathcal{P}) = 0$ — yet $P(\mathcal{P}) > 0$: it transmits *private* classical information.
3. **The joint protocol.** Alice uses the erasure channel's surviving halves together with $\mathcal{P}$'s private classical capacity to establish secret key; that key encrypts quantum information through the joint channel. Formally, $Q(\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}) \ge P(\mathcal{P}) > 0$: private capacity is "activated" into quantum capacity by the erasure channel's assistance.

Superactivation has no classical counterpart and proves $Q$ is *strongly* non-additive: no additive quantity can characterize it, and a channel's usefulness for quantum communication is contextual, not intrinsic [5]. Later work extended superactivation to the private capacity and zero-error capacities [7].

### 4.5 Entanglement assistance: the additive island

Against this non-additive ocean, the entanglement-assisted capacities are an island of order. The BSST theorem,

$$C_E(\mathcal{N}) = \max_{\phi_{AA'}} I(A;B)_\sigma, \qquad Q_E(\mathcal{N}) = \tfrac{1}{2} C_E(\mathcal{N}),$$

is single-letter and additive [3]: achievability via super-dense coding of the message into shared entanglement plus HSW-style decoding; the converse via data processing of mutual information. The irony is sharp — unlimited entanglement, the very resource making $Q$ non-additive, renders the assisted capacities additive, because the optimization over $\phi_{AA'}$ already internalizes arbitrary entanglement. Operationally, $Q_E(\mathcal{N})$ is the ebit distillation rate per channel use.

## 5 Empirical Results

We instantiate the theory on canonical channels. All logarithms are base 2; $H_2$ is the binary entropy.

### 5.1 The quantum erasure channel $\mathcal{E}_p$

Degradable for $p \le 1/2$, anti-degradable for $p \ge 1/2$, with exact capacities

$$Q(\mathcal{E}_p) = \max\{0,\, 1 - 2p\}, \qquad C(\mathcal{E}_p) = 1 - p, \qquad C_E(\mathcal{E}_p) = 2(1-p).$$

At $p = 1/2$: $Q = 0$, $C = 1/2$, $C_E = 1$. The kink in $Q$ at $p=1/2$ marks the degradable/anti-degradable transition, and this $p=1/2$ channel is Smith and Yard's activator $\mathcal{A}$ [5].

```python
import numpy as np

def erasure_capacities(p):
    return {"Q": max(0.0, 1 - 2*p), "C": 1 - p, "C_E": 2*(1 - p)}

for p in [0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0]:
    print(p, erasure_capacities(p))
# 0.0  {'Q': 1.0, 'C': 1.0, 'C_E': 2.0}
# 0.1  {'Q': 0.8, 'C': 0.9, 'C_E': 1.8}
# 0.25 {'Q': 0.5, 'C': 0.75, 'C_E': 1.5}
# 0.5  {'Q': 0.0, 'C': 0.5, 'C_E': 1.0}
# 0.75 {'Q': 0.0, 'C': 0.25, 'C_E': 0.5}
# 1.0  {'Q': 0.0, 'C': 0.0, 'C_E': 0.0}
```

### 5.2 The depolarizing channel $\mathcal{D}_p(\rho) = (1-p)\rho + p\,I/2$

The field's open wound: $C(\mathcal{D}_p) = 1 - H_2(p) - p\log_2 3$ is known by symmetry, but $Q(\mathcal{D}_p)$ has no closed form. The single-letter coherent information,

$$Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{D}_p) = \max\big\{0,\; 1 - H_2(p) - p\log_2 3\big\},$$

is positive for $p \lesssim 0.1893$ (the *hashing bound*). Degenerate codes exploiting inter-use entanglement beat this: lower bounds push the zero-crossing toward $p \approx 0.19$, while no-cloning/Rains upper bounds force $Q = 0$ for $p \gtrsim 0.25$. The true threshold $p^*$ where $Q$ vanishes is unknown — a $\sim$0.06-wide interval of ignorance for the most symmetric channel in nature.

### 5.3 The amplitude-damping channel

Amplitude damping $\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ ($|1\rangle \to |0\rangle$ with probability $\gamma$) is degradable, so

$$Q(\mathcal{A}_\gamma) = P(\mathcal{A}_\gamma) = \max_{q \in [0,1]} \big[H_2((1-\gamma)q) - H_2(\gamma q)\big],$$

a concave single-parameter maximization: $Q \approx 0.769$ at $\gamma = 0.1$, $Q \approx 0.360$ at $\gamma = 0.3$, vanishing as $\gamma \to 1$.

### 5.4 Quantifying Hastings's violation

For Hastings's random unitary channels of output dimension $d$,

$$S_{\min}(\Phi) + S_{\min}(\bar\Phi) - S_{\min}(\Phi \otimes \bar\Phi) \;\ge\; \frac{\log d}{d} - O\!\left(\frac{1}{d}\right) > 0$$

for large $d$. Via $\chi(\mathcal{N}) = \log d_{\text{out}} - S_{\min}(\mathcal{N})$ this yields $\chi(\Phi\otimes\bar\Phi) > \chi(\Phi) + \chi(\bar\Phi)$: superadditivity of classical information-carrying ability. Tiny but rigorous — its existence, not its size, kills single-letter $C$.

### 5.5 Capacity table for reference channels

| Channel | $Q$ | $P$ | $C$ | $C_E$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Noiseless qubit ($\mathrm{id}_2$) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Erasure $\mathcal{E}_{p\le 1/2}$ | $1-2p$ | $1-2p$ | $1-p$ | $2(1-p)$ |
| Depolarizing $\mathcal{D}_{p\lesssim 0.19}$ | $\ge 1-H_2(p)-p\log_2 3$ | $\ge Q$ | $1-H_2(p)-p\log_2 3$ | known exactly |
| Amplitude damping $\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ | $\max_q[H_2((1-\gamma)q)-H_2(\gamma q)]$ | $=Q$ | $\ge Q$ | known exactly |
| PPT binding $\mathcal{P}$ (Smith–Yard) | $0$ | $>0$ | $>0$ | $>0$ |
| $\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{1/2}$ | $>0$ (superactivated) | $>0$ | $>0$ | $>0$ |

## 6 Limitations

1. **No general algorithm for $Q$.** Cubitt et al. showed that for every $n$ there exist channels with vanishing coherent information on $n$ uses but positive capacity — no finite regularization depth suffices even to *detect* nonzero capacity, hinting $Q$ may be uncomputable [6]. Hastings's channels are non-constructive (random, high-dimensional), offering no explicit small-dimensional example [4].

2. **Small violations, large dimensions.** Hastings's gap is $O((\log d)/d)$ with no known operational consequence for realistic codes. Whether *large* additivity violations occur in modest dimension is open.

3. **Superactivation needs structure.** The Smith–Yard construction requires a PPT channel with positive private capacity — a delicate object — and gives only the lower bound $Q(\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{A}) \ge P(\mathcal{P})$; the true joint capacity is unknown. Whether "natural" channels (depolarizing, dephasing) superactivate is unknown [5][7].

4. **Two-way assistance and finite blocklength.** We have largely ignored $Q_2$ (two-way-assisted quantum capacity, governed by squashed entanglement) and finite-blocklength refinements (second-order rates), both far less developed than the asymptotic one-way theory.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Quantum channel capacity theory replaces Shannon's single number with a hierarchy — $Q \le P \le C \le C_E$, $Q_E = C_E/2$ — each governed by its own theorem: HSW for classical transmission, LSD for quantum transmission, Devetak's private coding theorem, and BSST for entanglement-assisted communication [1][2][3]. Where the classical theory is single-letter, the quantum theory is regularized, and the regularization is load-bearing: Hastings's counterexample proved the Holevo information genuinely non-additive [4], while Smith and Yard proved quantum capacity can be superactivated from zero [5]. Entanglement across channel uses is not a technicality but the central physical resource; the assisted capacities' additivity shows that making the resource explicit restores order [3]. The deepest open question remains computational: with unboundedly many uses potentially needed to witness capacity at all [6], we do not know whether the quantum capacity of an arbitrary channel is even computable in principle.
