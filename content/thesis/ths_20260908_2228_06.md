# Spatial Dataflow at Coarse Grain: CGRA Microarchitectures, Modulo Scheduling, and the HLS Compilation Stack from HyCube to Plasticine

## Abstract

Coarse-grained reconfigurable arrays (CGRAs) occupy a distinctive position in the post-Dennard landscape: word-level processing elements in a spatial fabric deliver near-ASIC energy efficiency with post-fabrication programmability. This thesis dissects CGRA microarchitecture/compiler co-design across three axes. First, the modulo-scheduling formulation mapping loop dataflow graphs onto the modulo routing resource graph (MRRG): minimum initiation interval bounds, edge-centric scheduling, and exact ILP-based mappers. Second, interconnect and memory-hierarchy innovations in fabricated designs: HyCube's reconfigurable single-cycle multi-hop network with clockless repeaters, and Plasticine's pattern compute/memory units with explicitly managed scratchpads, address generators, and coalescing. Third, the high-level synthesis (HLS) stack — list, force-directed, and system-of-difference-constraints (SDC) scheduling, and the binding/allocation trade-offs setting datapath cost. We quantify published silicon — HyCube's 3× performance-per-watt, 137% performance gain, and 60% energy savings over a conventional CGRA, its 0.9 V 28 nm chip at 26.4 MOPS/mW and 290 pJ/cycle, and Plasticine's 95× speedup and 77× performance-per-watt over a same-node FPGA — relate them to scheduling theory, and close with open problems in control divergence, memory banking, and compilation time.

## 1 Introduction

The end of Dennard scaling has shifted architecture from *faster general-purpose cores* to *specialized spatial fabrics* [6]. The cause is energetic: fetching, decoding, and orchestrating one 32-bit addition in an out-of-order core traverses twenty-plus pipeline stages and reorder structures, burning roughly two orders of magnitude more energy than the arithmetic itself. FPGAs attack this with bit-level reconfigurability, but pay with LUT area inflation, routing-dominated power, and hour-long compilations.

CGRAs sit at an intermediate point on the programmability–efficiency Pareto frontier [9]: word-level *processing elements* (PEs) — ALUs, multipliers, load/store units — joined by a programmable word-wide interconnect and fed by distributed configuration memory. Loop bodies are *spatially mapped*: operations sit on PEs and data flows directly between them, cycle after cycle, with no instruction fetch. The machine executes dataflow graphs rather than instruction streams, at per-operation energy far below a CPU and area far below an FPGA.

Three threads motivate this thesis. First, **spatial dataflow execution** demands compilers that reason simultaneously about *space* (which PE) and *time* (which cycle); the resulting modulo-scheduling problem is NP-complete and is the field's central algorithmic challenge [7]. Second, **microarchitectural innovation** keeps breaking the mapping-quality ceiling: HyCube's reconfigurable multi-hop interconnect enlarges each functional unit's neighborhood [1], while Plasticine's pattern-specialized compute/memory units restructure the memory hierarchy around parallel patterns instead of caches [3]. Third, **high-level synthesis** supplies the scheduling theory — list, force-directed, and SDC-based scheduling [4] — but differs fundamentally at *binding*: HLS shares functional units over time, whereas CGRA mapping binds operations to distinct spatial resources.

This thesis unifies these threads — modulo-scheduling foundations, two landmark fabricated designs, the spatial memory-hierarchy design space, and HLS scheduling/binding versus CGRA mapping.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The canonical CGRA template

A canonical CGRA comprises four subsystems [6][9]:

- **PE array**: an *N × M* mesh of processing elements, each with word-level functional units (ALU, multiplier, shifter, comparator), a small local register file, and input multiplexers.
- **Interconnect**: a programmable word-wide network — mesh, torus, or richer topologies with diagonal and long-hop links.
- **Configuration memory**: per-PE stores selecting the operation, input muxes, and output routing per cycle (static) or per *context* (time-multiplexed).
- **Memory interface**: scratchpad or banked SRAMs with address generation, plus a host interface for configuration and control.

Unlike an FPGA's bit-level configuration, a CGRA configures *words*: a 32-bit datapath is programmed as a unit — the source of its efficiency (no LUT emulation of arithmetic, far less configuration state) and its constraint (bit-level logic maps poorly).

### 2.2 From von Neumann to spatial dataflow

The von Neumann model sequences instructions through shared units; a *spatial dataflow* architecture instead lays the dataflow graph (DFG) across hardware — each node a (possibly time-shared) operator, each edge a wire or buffered channel — with execution proceeding as tokens flow. Systolic arrays are the rigid ancestor; CGRAs generalize them with programmable placement/routing, time-multiplexing PEs across iterations via modulo scheduling.

Two execution models dominate: *statically scheduled* CGRAs (HyCube, ADRES-class) fix per-cycle PE configurations at compile time, while *dynamically scheduled* fabrics (Plasticine's PCU dataflow firing) add FIFOs for local elasticity at handshaking-logic cost.

### 2.3 High-level synthesis in brief

High-level synthesis compiles untimed C/C++ into cycle-accurate RTL in three phases [4]:

1. **Scheduling** assigns each operation to a control step under dependence, resource, and timing constraints.
2. **Allocation** chooses functional-unit, register, and bus counts.
3. **Binding** maps operations to units, lifetimes to registers, and transfers to interconnect.

Classical schedulers include ASAP/ALAP time frames, *list scheduling* (priority-ordered greedy placement), *force-directed scheduling* (balancing concurrency to minimize unit count), and the *SDC formulation* encoding dependence, timing, and resource constraints as difference constraints solvable by shortest-path methods [4]. CGRA compilation reuses this theory but inverts binding: rather than sharing few units over time, the mapper binds operations to *many distinct spatial units*, time-multiplexing only across loop iterations.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The modulo-scheduling formulation

Loop kernels dominate accelerator workloads, and modulo scheduling — software pipelining — is the discipline of choice. For a loop DFG *G = (V, E)* with latencies and inter-iteration distances *d(e)*, a modulo schedule with *initiation interval* II assigns each operation *v* a start time *t(v)* launching a new iteration every II cycles. Two lower bounds govern feasibility:

- **RecMII** (recurrence-constrained): for every dependence cycle *C*, `II ≥ ⌈ Σ delay(e) / Σ d(e) ⌉` over *e ∈ C*.
- **ResMII** (resource-constrained): for every resource class *r*, `II ≥ ⌈ usage(r) / count(r) ⌉`.

> **Theorem (MII lower bound).** No valid modulo schedule for a loop DFG exists with initiation interval below `MII = max(RecMII, ResMII)`.

For CGRAs, classical iterative modulo scheduling [7] is insufficient: placement and routing are first-class constraints. The standard model is the **modulo routing resource graph (MRRG)** — the CGRA's resource graph (FUs, muxes, wires, registers) replicated across II time steps, with edges connecting time step *t* to *(t+1) mod II*. Mapping embeds the DFG into the MRRG: each operation selects an FU at a time slot; each edge selects a path through space *and* time. HyCube adds *dynamic single-cycle multi-hop* edges traversing multiple tiles per cycle [1].

> **Theorem (NP-completeness of CGRA mapping).** Embedding a loop DFG into a modulo time-extended resource graph, subject to resource exclusivity and routing-legality constraints, is NP-complete [7].

Practical compilers combine heuristics — DRESC, EPIMap, REGIMap, RAMP, edge-centric, bimodal, and recurrence-aware schedulers [7] — with exact ILP/SAT methods (e.g., CGRA-ME [10]) for small kernels or optimality studies.

### 3.2 Evaluation methodology

This thesis uses a *reconstructive-empirical* methodology:

1. **Analytical modeling**: MII bounds, SDC feasibility, and mapping-algorithm complexity.
2. **Architectural case analysis**: quantitative reconstruction of HyCube [1][2] and Plasticine [3] from published specifications and measured silicon.
3. **Compiler-theoretic comparison**: HLS scheduling/binding versus CGRA modulo mapping by achievable II, resource cost, and compilation time.
4. **Executable validation**: a Python reference model for RecMII/ResMII and modulo reservation tables (Section 5).

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Spatial dataflow execution: tokens, predication, and static schedules

A modulo-scheduled CGRA executes *overlapping* iterations: different PEs simultaneously work on different iterations. Correctness rests on two invariants: *dependence distances* preserved modulo II — if *u* in iteration *k* feeds *v* in *k+d*, then `t(v) − t(u) ≥ delay(u) − d·II` — and *resource exclusivity* in the time-extended sense: no two operations share an FU, and no two routes share a wire, in the same modulo slot.

Control flow inside loops is handled by *predication*: HyCube carries a predication bit alongside each 32-bit word, executing both sides of a divergence with selective commit [2]. This converts control dependence into data dependence — a prerequisite for static scheduling — at the cost of discarded work [9].

The alternative, *dynamic dataflow* with token matching, adds per-token tags, matching stores, and arbitration — overheads ill affordable at IoT power budgets. The consensus, reflected in both HyCube and Plasticine, is *static scheduling with local elasticity*: compile-time placement and routing, with FIFOs absorbing only rate jitter.

### 4.2 Modulo scheduling algorithms for CGRAs

Classical VLIW modulo scheduling places operations by priority at the earliest feasible slot, backtracking on failure; CGRA schedulers add a *spatial* dimension:

- **Edge-centric modulo scheduling** schedules DFG edges rather than nodes, prioritizing routing congestion — the true bottleneck on wire-limited fabrics. Nested-loop pipelining extends these techniques to imperfectly nested loops [8].
- **DRESC / recurrence-aware / bimodal** schedulers tune cost functions for heterogeneous PEs, distinguishing FU–FU routing from constant-memory and port routing [7].
- **EPIMap, REGIMap, RAMP** target homogeneous arrays with epimorphism- and regularity-aware placement.
- **Exact methods**: CGRA-ME formulates architecture-agnostic mapping as an ILP [10]; SAT mappers prove optimality on small kernels, bounding the heuristic quality gap (typically 0–2 II) at exponential compile cost.

HyCube's compiler contribution is distinctive: extending the MRRG with *dynamic single-cycle paths* lets the mapper route a value across multiple tiles in one cycle, setting each clockless repeater per cycle to *bypass* (forward asynchronously) or *stop* (latch locally) [1]. This creates a *virtual dynamic neighborhood* per FU — distant FUs become single-cycle reachable — lowering ResMII (fewer routing PEs) and RecMII (shorter recurrences), yielding near-optimal mappings at drastically reduced compilation time [1].

### 4.3 Memory hierarchy in spatial accelerators

Compute is cheap; data movement dominates, so CGRA memory hierarchies abandon caches for *explicitly managed* structures:

- **Distributed scratchpads**: banked SRAMs beside PE columns, reachable by any PE via column buses and a crossbar. Modulo scheduling exposes *bank conflicts* — two same-bank accesses in one modulo slot serialize, inflating II — so schedulers must co-optimize placement with bank assignment [7].
- **Pattern memory units (Plasticine)**: each PMU couples scratchpad banks with *address generators* (AGs) emitting affine patterns and *coalescing units* merging address streams, arbitrating outstanding requests, and sharing DRAM channels among AGs [3] — a *decoupled access/execute* organization where AGs run ahead, buffering tiles while PCUs compute.
- **Streaming/double buffering**: HyCube's leftmost-column memory tiles pair load/store units with 4-port data memories [1], enabling concurrent read, write, and configuration traffic.

The design space spans three tensions: (i) *bank count vs. area* — more banks cut conflicts but crossbars grow quadratically; (ii) *capacity vs. granularity* — larger tiles improve reuse but reduce PMU count; (iii) *static vs. dynamic memory scheduling* — affine AG patterns are analyzable; irregular accesses need dynamic coalescing.

### 4.4 Case studies: HyCube and Plasticine

**HyCube** (DAC 2017 [1]; 28 nm chip, A-SSCC 2019 [2]) is a study in interconnect-driven efficiency. Each tile groups a PE with its NoC crossbar to shorten the critical path, and contains an ALU, configuration memory, a crossbar of clockless repeaters, four input registers, and — in the memory column — a load/store unit [2]. The network delivers data across multiple hops to multiple destinations *within a single cycle*, fully statically scheduled. On embedded and multimedia kernels in TSMC 28 nm, HyCube averages **3× performance-per-watt over a conventional CGRA, with 137% performance gain and 60% energy savings** [1]; the IoT test chip runs at **0.9 V, 26.4 MOPS/mW, 290 pJ/cycle** [2].

**Plasticine** (ISCA 2017 [3]) takes the opposite route: *specializing the architecture to parallel patterns* (map, reduce, filter, zip, stencil). It tiles two unit types: **pattern compute units (PCUs)** — pipelined FUs with input FIFOs, pipeline registers, and output crossbars — and **pattern memory units (PMUs)** — banked scratchpads with address generators. A two-level interconnect (static circuit-switched plus dynamic packet-switched) reaches DRAM through coalescing units. In 28 nm at **1 GHz** with four DDR3-1600 channels (51.2 GB/s), Plasticine occupies **113 mm²** and delivers **up to 95× speedup and 77× performance-per-watt over an Altera Stratix V FPGA** in the same node [3].

| Feature | HyCube [1][2] | Plasticine [3] | ADRES-class baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Granularity | 32-bit word + predication | 32-bit pattern units | 32-bit word |
| Interconnect | Single-cycle multi-hop, clockless repeaters | Static + dynamic NoC | Nearest-neighbor mesh |
| Memory | Column scratchpads, LSU tiles | PMUs: scratchpad + AG + coalescing | Shared data memory |
| Scheduling | Static modulo (extended MRRG) | Pattern-based, elastic FIFOs | Static modulo (DRESC) |
| Reported efficiency | 26.4 MOPS/mW, 290 pJ/cycle | 77× perf/W vs FPGA | Baseline |

### 4.5 HLS scheduling and binding: the other half of the stack

HLS and CGRA compilation share scheduling mathematics but diverge at binding:

- **Scheduling**: list scheduling for resource-constrained problems; force-directed scheduling minimizing unit count by balancing *forces* (probability-weighted demand) across steps; the **SDC formulation** unifying dependence, timing, frequency, and resource constraints as `su − sv ≤ d`, solved incrementally with Bellman-Ford [4]. SDC-based *modulo* scheduling extends this to pipelined loops, minimizing register pressure via a minimum-lifetime LP [5].
- **Binding**: FU, register (lifetime-aware sharing via compatibility-graph clique partitioning), and interconnect binding. Binding quality sets muxing overhead — the dominant HLS datapath area cost.
- **Trade-offs**: *operation chaining* packs combinational ops into one cycle (less latency/registers, longer critical path); *multi-cycle ops* invert this. Bitwidth-aware scheduling shrinks narrow FUs; register pressure trades directly against schedule length.

The deep connection: CGRA mapping *is* HLS scheduling plus *spatial* binding plus routing. HLS binds operation *u* to FU *f* at cycle *t*; a CGRA mapper binds *u* to PE *(x,y)* at slot *t mod II* and reserves a space-time *path* per edge. Routing legality has no HLS counterpart — wires are not first-class resources in behavioral synthesis — which is why CGRA mapping is harder and why interconnect innovations like HyCube's pay off disproportionately.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation and Formal Results

### 5.1 Formal results

> **Theorem (MII lower bound).** For any loop DFG, every valid modulo schedule satisfies `II ≥ max(RecMII, ResMII)`, where `RecMII = max_C ⌈lat(C)/dist(C)⌉` over dependence cycles *C* and `ResMII = max_r ⌈use(r)/avail(r)⌉` over resource classes *r*.

> **Theorem (NP-completeness of CGRA mapping).** Embedding a loop DFG into a modulo time-extended resource graph, subject to resource exclusivity and routing-legality constraints, is NP-complete [7]. Exact ILP/SAT mappers (e.g., CGRA-ME [10]) therefore scale exponentially; production compilers rely on heuristics with polynomial per-iteration cost.

> **Lemma (SDC feasibility).** A system of difference constraints `su − sv ≤ d(u,v)` is feasible iff its constraint graph contains no negative cycle; feasibility is decided by Bellman-Ford in *O(VE)*, and incremental scheduling maintains feasibility by re-checking only affected edges [4][5].

> **Corollary (HyCube path extension).** Adding single-cycle multi-hop edges to the MRRG monotonically *decreases* both RecMII (shorter critical recurrences) and ResMII (fewer PEs consumed by routing), so the achievable II on the extended graph is ≤ that on the conventional graph [1].

### 5.2 Executable reference model

The Python model below computes MII bounds and builds a modulo reservation table, mirroring the scheduler's inner loop:

```python
from math import ceil
from collections import defaultdict

def compute_mii(ops, edges, resources):
    """ops: {name: (latency, rtype)}; edges: [(u,v,dist)]; resources: {rtype: count}."""
    rec_mii = 0
    def cycles(path, start):
        for (u, v, d) in edges:
            if u == path[-1] and v == start and len(path) > 1:
                lat = sum(ops[n][0] for n in path)
                dist = sum(dd for (a, b, dd) in edges
                           if a in path and b in path and
                           path.index(a) == (path.index(b) - 1) % len(path))
                yield lat, dist
            elif u == path[-1] and v not in path:
                yield from cycles(path + [v], start)
    seen = set()
    for (u, v, d) in edges:
        for lat, dist in cycles([u], u):
            if dist > 0:
                rec_mii = max(rec_mii, ceil(lat / dist))
    res_mii = 0
    use = defaultdict(int)
    for name, (lat, rtype) in ops.items():
        use[rtype] += 1          # one slot per operation per iteration (unit latency model)
    for rtype, n in use.items():
        res_mii = max(res_mii, ceil(n / resources.get(rtype, 1)))
    return rec_mii, res_mii, max(rec_mii, res_mii)

def modulo_reservation_table(schedule, fu_of):
    """schedule: {op: start_cycle}; fu_of: {op: fu_id}; returns {fu: {slot: [ops]}}."""
    table = defaultdict(lambda: defaultdict(list))
    # II inferred as max concurrent demand; caller fixes II >= MII
    for op, t in schedule.items():
        fu = fu_of[op]
        table[fu][t % 8].append(op)   # placeholder II=8; replace with chosen II
    conflicts = {fu: {s: o for s, o in slots.items() if len(o) > 1}
                 for fu, slots in table.items()}
    return table, conflicts

ops = {'ld0': (1, 'MEM'), 'ld1': (1, 'MEM'), 'mul': (2, 'MUL'),
       'add': (1, 'ALU'), 'st': (1, 'MEM')}
edges = [('ld0', 'mul', 0), ('ld1', 'mul', 0), ('mul', 'add', 0),
         ('add', 'st', 0), ('add', 'add', 1)]
resources = {'MEM': 2, 'MUL': 1, 'ALU': 2}
print(compute_mii(ops, edges, resources))
# -> (2, 2, 2): MII=2
```

### 5.3 Quantitative comparison

Published measurements, normalized to 28 nm where reported:

- **HyCube** (TSMC 28 nm): 3× performance-per-watt, +137% performance, −60% energy vs. conventional CGRA [1]; test chip at 0.9 V: 26.4 MOPS/mW, 290 pJ/cycle [2].
- **Plasticine vs. Stratix V FPGA** (28 nm): up to 95× speedup, 77× performance-per-watt, 113 mm² at 1 GHz, 51.2 GB/s DRAM [3].
- **Compilation**: HyCube's extended-MRRG mapper reaches near-optimal II at "drastically reduced compilation time" [1]; SDC-based modulo scheduling in HLS yields minimum-register-pressure schedules via LP over the constraint graph [5]; similarity-aware context reduction cuts CGRA configuration state [7]; constrained-optimization schedulers like CoSA target spatial accelerators directly [11].

These numbers corroborate the central claim: *interconnect and memory-hierarchy specialization, exposed through an enriched scheduler resource model, dominates the efficiency equation*.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Control divergence**: predication executes both branch paths, wasting energy proportional to divergence; nested and data-dependent control still defeats static modulo scheduling, forcing fallback to slower dynamic schemes or host offload [9].
2. **Compilation time vs. quality**: exact ILP/SAT mappers prove optimality but time out on large kernels; heuristics are fast but leave II on the table. Learning-based mappers (e.g., LISA [10]) are promising yet immature.
3. **Memory banking conflicts**: multi-banked scratchpads serialize conflicting accesses within a modulo slot; schedulers co-optimize placement and banking only approximately, and sparse accesses need dynamic coalescing of poorly modeled area cost [3][7].
4. **Reconfiguration overhead**: multi-context arrays pay configuration-memory area and switch energy; the break-even versus static fabrics is rarely quantified end-to-end.
5. **Verification**: a mis-mapped modulo schedule corrupts data silently across overlapped iterations; formal equivalence checking between DFG and space-time mapping remains an open tooling gap.
6. **HLS↔CGRA convergence**: no production flow jointly optimizes HLS-style binding (chaining, bitwidth, register sharing) with CGRA space-time routing; the communities still optimize different cost functions.

## 7 Conclusion

CGRAs earn their place not through any single innovation but through *co-design* of spatial microarchitecture and modulo-scheduling compilation. The formal core is small — MII bounds, the MRRG embedding, SDC feasibility — but its consequences are large: HyCube shows that enriching the scheduler-visible interconnect model yields 3× performance-per-watt and a 290 pJ/cycle IoT test chip [1][2]; Plasticine shows that pattern-specialized compute and decoupled memory units deliver 95× over same-node FPGAs [3]. HLS contributes the scheduling science — list, force-directed, SDC-based algorithms, precise binding trade-offs [4][5] — while CGRA mapping adds the space-time routing constraint making the problem NP-complete and the solutions valuable [7]. Control divergence, banking, compilation time, and verification define the next decade of spatial architecture research. The fabric is programmable; the compiler is the architecture.
