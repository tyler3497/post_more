# The Discrete Turn in Audio: Residual Vector Quantization, Neural Codecs, and Language Modeling over Acoustic Tokens

## Abstract

Neural audio codecs have undergone a paradigm shift: from hand-crafted psychoacoustic transforms to learned encoder–quantizer–decoder pipelines that compress raw waveforms into discrete tokens at rates as low as 1.5 kbps while preserving near-transparent quality [1][3]. At the heart of this revolution lies **residual vector quantization (RVQ)**, a hierarchical scheme that refines reconstructions through successive codebook lookups, alongside codebook-free alternatives such as *finite scalar quantization*. This thesis surveys the architecture, training, and evaluation of modern neural codecs — **EnCodec** [1], **SoundStream**, and the **Descript Audio Codec (DAC)** [3] — and the second-order transformation they enabled: treating audio as a *language* over discrete tokens. Frameworks such as **AudioLM** [6], **VALL-E**, and **MusicGen** recast generation as autoregressive modeling over acoustic and semantic tokens, inheriting both the compression efficiency and the inductive biases of their tokenizers. We formalize RVQ's distortion dynamics, compare quantizer designs, analyze adversarial and multi-scale spectral training objectives, and review empirical benchmarks spanning MUSHRA, ViSQOL, and WARP-Q, concluding with open problems in bitrate elasticity, semantic–acoustic disentanglement, and streaming under strict latency budgets.

---

## 1. Introduction

Audio was among the last signal modalities to fall to end-to-end learning. For decades, compression meant *hand-crafted* transforms: the modified discrete cosine transform of MP3 and AAC, the hybrid CELT/SILK architecture of Opus, each painstakingly tuned against psychoacoustic masking models. These codecs are engineering triumphs, yet they share a structural ceiling — the transform, the bit allocation, and the entropy coder are designed, not learned, and adapting them to a new domain means returning to first principles.

Neural audio codecs invert this relationship. An **encoder** network maps the raw waveform to a latent sequence; a **quantizer** discretizes it into tokens; a **decoder** reconstructs the waveform; and adversarial plus spectral losses shape the entire pipeline toward perceptual fidelity [1][3]. The discrete bottleneck is the crux: it is simultaneously a compression interface and a *tokenization* interface. Once audio is a sequence of discrete tokens, the entire machinery of modern language modeling — transformers, autoregressive sampling, prompting, in-context conditioning — becomes applicable to sound.

Three systems anchor this thesis. **EnCodec** (Meta, 2022) demonstrated that a single convolutional–LSTM autoencoder with residual vector quantization could beat hand-crafted codecs and SoundStream on MUSHRA at matched bitrates across speech and music [1][7]. **SoundStream** (Google) pioneered the end-to-end RVQ codec with streaming support, establishing the architectural template the field still follows. **DAC** (Descript, 2023) pushed universality: a single model compressing 44.1 kHz audio of *any* domain — speech, music, environment — into tokens at roughly 8 kbps, a ~90× compression ratio [3][4].

The second act is generative. **AudioLM** showed that combining *semantic* tokens (from a masked audio model) with *acoustic* tokens (from SoundStream) yields speech and piano continuations that are simultaneously high-fidelity and long-term coherent [6]. **VALL-E** applied language modeling to EnCodec tokens for zero-shot text-to-speech; **MusicGen** and **MusicLM** extended the paradigm to music; **VampNet** built masked generative modeling on DAC tokens [8]. The codec is no longer just a compressor — it is the *alphabet* of audio language models.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From VQ-VAE to Residual Quantization

Vector quantization entered deep learning through the **VQ-VAE**: an encoder output **z** in R^D is replaced by its nearest codeword in a learned codebook C = {e_1, …, e_K}, and gradients flow through the non-differentiable lookup via the straight-through estimator. A *commitment loss* keeps encoder outputs near codewords. Single-stage VQ, however, faces a brutal trade-off: fine reconstruction demands an enormous codebook, but large codebooks suffer **codebook collapse**, where only a fraction of entries are ever used.

**Residual vector quantization** resolves this by *factorization*: *M* small codebooks applied in series, each quantizing the residual of the previous stage. With *K* entries per codebook, the representable set has effective cardinality *K*^M while training only *M·K* vectors — exponential capacity at linear cost. SoundStream introduced this to audio codecs, and every major neural codec since — EnCodec, DAC — has adopted it [1][3][8].

### 2.2 Adversarial Vocoding and Spectral Losses

Reconstruction losses alone (L1/L2 on waveforms) produce muffled, phase-blurred audio. The field borrowed from neural vocoders: **multi-scale discriminators** on waveforms and complex spectrograms force the decoder's output distribution toward real audio. EnCodec introduced the **multi-scale STFT (MS-STFT) discriminator**, judging complex spectrograms at several window sizes to penalize phase errors [1][2]. DAC added a **multi-scale mel-spectrogram loss** and periodic *snake* activations targeting harmonic structure [3].

### 2.3 Streaming and Causality

Telephony and live applications impose hard latency budgets. A *causal* codec may only use past context: causal convolutions, unidirectional LSTMs, and frame-synchronous quantization. EnCodec ships a causal 24 kHz mono model alongside a non-causal 48 kHz stereo model, letting deployment trade latency for quality [2]. DAC achieves constant GPU memory on arbitrarily long files by iterating over overlapping chunks with careful padding derived from the encoder/decoder receptive fields [8].

### 2.4 Evaluation: Subjective and Objective Metrics

Codec quality is ultimately perceptual. The **MUSHRA** (MUltiple Stimuli with Hidden Reference and Anchor) test presents listeners with the reference, the coded samples, and low-quality anchors, yielding scores on a 0–100 scale — the gold standard, but expensive. Objective proxies include **ViSQOL** (Virtual Speech Quality Objective Listener), a full-reference metric comparing spectro-temporal similarity, and **WARP-Q**, designed for coded-audio quality prediction. Objective metrics correlate imperfectly with perception, so credible papers report both [1][3].

## 3. Methodology

Despite architectural variation, modern neural codecs share a training methodology refined across the SoundStream → EnCodec → DAC lineage [1][3][8].

**Architecture.** The encoder is a stack of strided 1D convolutional blocks (downsampling the waveform to a frame rate of 75–86 Hz), optionally followed by recurrent layers (LSTMs in EnCodec) and a final projection. The RVQ bottleneck discretizes each frame into *M* code indices. The decoder mirrors the encoder with transposed convolutions, upsampling tokens back to waveform rate.

**Quantizer training.** Codebooks are typically initialized with k-means over encoder outputs and updated with exponential moving averages. The commitment loss anchors encoder outputs to codewords. A critical technique is **quantizer dropout**: during training, a random prefix of the *M* codebooks is used, forcing the model to produce reasonable reconstructions at *every* bitrate prefix. This yields a single model supporting variable bitrates — though DAC's authors identified a side effect: dropout *hurts* full-bandwidth quality, since the network hedges across prefixes rather than specializing [4].

**Losses.** The generator minimizes a weighted sum of: (1) **time-domain L1**; (2) **multi-scale mel-spectrogram L1**; (3) **adversarial hinge loss** against waveform and MS-STFT discriminators; (4) **feature-matching loss**; (5) **commitment loss**. EnCodec adds a gradient **balancer** to stabilize the joint optimization [2].

**Bitrate control.** Bitrate is set by the number of active RVQ stages. Since RVQ is hierarchical, decoding the first *m* of *M* codebooks yields a valid lower-bitrate reconstruction — an *embedded* bitstream reminiscent of scalable image coders. The effective bitrate follows directly from the token geometry:

```python
def effective_bitrate(frame_rate_hz: float, n_codebooks: int, bits_per_code: int) -> float:
    """Bits per second of an RVQ token stream."""
    return frame_rate_hz * n_codebooks * bits_per_code

# EnCodec @ 24 kHz mono: 75 Hz frames, 32 codebooks x 10 bits
print(effective_bitrate(75, 32, 10) / 1000)      # 24.0 kbps
# DAC @ 44.1 kHz: 44100/512 = 86.13 Hz, 9 codebooks x 10 bits
print(effective_bitrate(44100 / 512, 9, 10) / 1000)  # ~7.75 kbps
```

**Evaluation protocol.** Papers report MUSHRA for subjective quality, ViSQOL/STOI/PESQ for objective quality, and bitrate/token-rate for efficiency, typically against Opus/EVS anchors and prior neural codecs at matched bitrates [1][3][7].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Residual Vector Quantization: Formalism

Let the encoder produce **z** in R^D per frame. RVQ with *M* codebooks C_1, …, C_M, each of size *K*, defines the recursion: r_0 = **z**; q_m = argmin over e in C_m of ||r_{m-1} − e||; r_m = r_{m-1} − q_m. The transmitted token tuple is (i_1, …, i_M) with i_m the index of q_m, costing *M*·log2(*K*) bits per frame; the decoder reconstructs z-hat = sum of q_m. The residual norm ||r_m|| measures the distortion remaining after stage *m*.

> **Theorem (RVQ distortion decomposition).** Let **z** be an encoder embedding, r_0 = **z**, and r_m = r_{m-1} − Q_m(r_{m-1}) where Q_m denotes nearest-codeword lookup in codebook *m*. Then the quantized embedding z-hat = sum_{m=1..M} Q_m(r_{m-1}) satisfies **z** − z-hat = r_M exactly, and if each stage's worst-case quantization error obeys ||r_{m-1} − Q_m(r_{m-1})|| ≤ ε_m over the residual distribution, then ||**z** − z-hat|| ≤ sum_{m=1..M} ε_m.

*Proof sketch.* Telescoping: **z** − sum q_m = r_0 − (r_0 − r_1) − … − (r_{M-1} − r_M) = r_M. The bound follows from the triangle inequality applied iteratively. ∎

The theorem exposes RVQ's design logic: *early codebooks capture coarse structure with large ε_1; late codebooks refine with small ε_m*. Empirically, the first codebook carries phonetic and linguistic content while later ones encode speaker timbre and acoustic detail — a hierarchy that language models exploit by predicting coarse tokens before fine ones [6][8].

### 4.2 Finite Scalar Quantization and Codebook-Free Alternatives

RVQ's weakness is codebook *management*: dead entries, EMA tuning, and collapse at scale. **Finite scalar quantization (FSQ)** eliminates the codebook entirely. Each latent dimension is independently squashed through a bounded nonlinearity (e.g., tanh) and rounded to one of *L* discrete levels: q = round(floor(L/2) · tanh(z)). With *d* dimensions and *L* levels per dimension, the implicit codebook has size *L*^d — every combination is reachable by construction, so *collapse is impossible* and no auxiliary losses are needed. The straight-through estimator carries gradients through the rounding. FSQ trades RVQ's learned, anisotropic partitions for a fixed, axis-aligned grid; the encoder learns to *use* the grid efficiently, and downstream language models benefit from the perfectly utilized token space. The conceptual contrast is sharp:

| Property | Single-stage VQ | RVQ | FSQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Codebook | learned, size *K* | *M* learned codebooks, size *K* each | none (implicit *L*^d grid) |
| Effective capacity | *K* | *K*^M | *L*^d |
| Collapse risk | high | moderate | none |
| Bitrate scalability | no | yes (prefix decoding) | limited |
| Training complexity | EMA + commitment | EMA × *M* + dropout | straight-through only |

FSQ is increasingly favored for *tokenizer-first* designs where the codec exists to serve a language model, while RVQ dominates where fine-grained bitrate scalability matters [8].

### 4.3 EnCodec: Multi-Bandwidth Streaming Architecture

EnCodec's encoder stacks **4 convolutional blocks** that downsample 24 kHz audio to **75 Hz** frames, followed by **2 LSTM layers** and a final convolution producing 128-dimensional embeddings [7]. The RVQ bottleneck uses **32 codebooks of 1024 entries** (10 bits each); the decoder mirrors the encoder with transposed convolutions. A **multi-scale STFT discriminator** on complex spectrograms plus time-domain discriminators provides adversarial supervision, and a gradient balancer stabilizes the joint objective [1][2].

Two operating points ship in the official repository [2]: a **causal 24 kHz mono** model (for streaming) and a **non-causal 48 kHz stereo** model trained on music. Target bandwidths are 1.5, 3, 6, 12, 24 kbps (24 kHz) and 3, 6, 12, 24 kbps (48 kHz) — all served by *one* RVQ stack via quantizer dropout. At 24 kbps, all 32 codebooks are active (75 × 32 × 10 = 24,000 bps); at 1.5 kbps, only 2 are.

A distinctive EnCodec contribution is the **pretrained entropy language model**: a small transformer trained on the RVQ token stream that losslessly compresses the representation by up to **40%** with zero quality change, pushing effective rates well below the nominal bitrate [2]. This was an early hint of the codec-as-language-model symbiosis.

The discrete codes double as representations: **EnCodecMAE** trains masked autoencoders to predict EnCodec tokens, showing the quantized latent space retains most perceptually relevant information for speech *and* music, and that EnCodec achieves higher MUSHRA than hand-crafted codecs and SoundStream at the same bitrate [7].

### 4.4 DAC: High-Fidelity Universal Compression with Improved RVQGAN

DAC asks whether *one* model can compress *all* audio — speech, music, environmental sound — at high fidelity. The answer is a 44.1 kHz model producing tokens at **~8 kbps**, roughly **90× compression** of 16-bit PCM [3][4][5]. The token geometry is precisely documented: encoder strides (2, 4, 8, 8) give a hop of 512 samples, i.e., 44100/512 ≈ **86.13 Hz** token rate; **9 codebooks** of **10-bit** tokens with **8-dimensional** latent embeddings yield 86.13 × 9 × 10 ≈ **7752 bps** [8].

Architectural improvements over the RVQGAN baseline include: **periodic inductive biases** via *snake* activations that model harmonic signals more naturally than ReLUs; a **multi-scale STFT discriminator** operating on complex spectrograms; a **multi-scale mel-spectrogram reconstruction loss**; and improved vector quantization training with better codebook utilization [3][4].

DAC ships models at 16, 24, and 44.1 kHz via `pip install descript-audio-codec` [5], with MIT-licensed weights. A JAX reimplementation verified bit-exact token equivalence with the PyTorch original and demonstrated constant-memory chunked encoding of arbitrarily long audio [8].

The type structure of such a pipeline is elegantly expressed functionally:

```haskell
data Codebook = Codebook
  { cbId    :: Int
  , entries :: Vector (Vector Float)  -- K codewords of dimension D
  }

data QuantizedFrame = QuantizedFrame
  { residual :: Vector Float
  , codes    :: [Int]                 -- one index per RVQ stage
  }

rvqEncode :: [Codebook] -> Vector Float -> QuantizedFrame
rvqEncode cbs z = foldl step (QuantizedFrame z []) cbs
  where
    step (QuantizedFrame r cs) cb =
      let i = nearestIndex cb r
      in QuantizedFrame (r - (entries cb ! i)) (cs ++ [i])

-- Decoding is pure summation: the homomorphism from token
-- tuples back to embedding space.
rvqDecode :: [Codebook] -> [Int] -> Vector Float
rvqDecode cbs idxs = foldl (+) zero [entries cb ! i | (cb, i) <- zip cbs idxs]
```

### 4.5 From Tokens to Language Models: AudioLM, VALL-E, and MusicGen

Once audio is discrete, generation becomes **language modeling**. The pioneering framework is **AudioLM** [6]: it observes that acoustic tokens (from SoundStream) reconstruct well but capture little long-term structure, while *semantic* tokens — discretized activations of a **w2v-BERT** masked model — capture phonetics and prosody but reconstruct poorly. AudioLM therefore trains a *hierarchical* transformer: a coarse stage models semantic tokens for long-term coherence, and fine stages model acoustic tokens conditioned on the semantics. Given a **3-second prompt** of an unseen speaker, it generates syntactically and semantically plausible speech continuations preserving speaker identity, prosody, and recording conditions — with *no transcripts or annotations*. Trained on piano recordings, the same architecture generates coherent continuations in melody, harmony, tone, and rhythm [6]. The authors also trained a detector for AudioLM-generated speech, addressing misuse risk at the source.

**VALL-E** applies the same philosophy to text-to-speech: EnCodec tokens as the target language, an autoregressive stage for the first RVQ codebook and a non-autoregressive stage for the rest, enabling **zero-shot** voice cloning from a short enrollment clip [8]. **MusicLM** builds on a SoundStream variant for text-conditioned music; **MusicGen** uses an EnCodec-derived tokenizer with delay-pattern codebook interleaving; **VampNet** applies masked-token modeling to DAC tokens for music inpainting [8].

The token-rate arithmetic matters enormously here. DAC's 86 Hz × 9 streams ≈ **775 tokens/second**; EnCodec at 24 kbps produces 75 × 32 = **2400 tokens/second**. Flattening strategies — *coarse-to-fine*, *delay patterns* (staggered codebook streams), or *parallel* prediction of fine codebooks — trade sequence length against modeling fidelity and define the current engineering frontier of audio LMs.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Analysis

### 5.1 Codec Comparison

| Codec | Sampling rate | Bitrates | Frame rate | Codebooks | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EnCodec [1][2] | 24 kHz mono (causal) | 1.5 / 3 / 6 / 12 / 24 kbps | 75 Hz | 32 × 1024 | speech + general audio |
| EnCodec [1][2] | 48 kHz stereo | 3 / 6 / 12 / 24 kbps | 75 Hz | 32 × 1024 | music |
| DAC [3][4] | 44.1 kHz (also 16/24 kHz) | ~8 kbps (7752 bps) | 86.13 Hz | 9 × 1024 (10-bit), 8-dim latents | universal |
| SoundStream | 24 kHz | variable via RVQ depth | 75 Hz | RVQ hierarchy | speech, streaming |

### 5.2 Reported Quality

- **EnCodec** achieves higher MUSHRA scores than hand-crafted codecs and SoundStream at matched bitrates, for both speech and music [7].
- **DAC** significantly outperforms competing audio compression algorithms in both subjective listening tests and objective metrics, at lower bitrate than prior codecs for equivalent quality [4].
- EnCodec's auxiliary transformer **entropy model** reduces bitrate by up to **40%** losslessly [2].
- **AudioLM** continuations preserve speaker identity and prosody from 3-second prompts and generate coherent piano music without symbolic supervision [6].

### 5.3 Formal Bitrate–Distortion Reading

Applying the distortion decomposition of §4.1, decoding only the first *m* < *M* codebooks yields bitrate *m*·log2(*K*)·*F* (with frame rate *F*) and error bounded by the partial sum of ε_m plus ||r_m|| — i.e., *graceful degradation*, which is precisely what quantizer dropout trains for. The cost is capacity dilution at full bandwidth, the effect DAC's authors flagged [4].

A useful reference calculation: EnCodec at 6 kbps uses 8 of 32 codebooks (75 × 8 × 10 = 6000 bps), while DAC uses all 9 codebooks at 7752 bps — yet DAC's universal model is reported competitive with or better than EnCodec at comparable rates on general audio [4], indicating that architectural improvements (periodic activations, multi-scale mel loss) buy more than raw bits.

## 6. Limitations

**Quantizer dropout's hidden tax.** The technique enabling multi-bitrate models measurably degrades full-bandwidth quality, since the network hedges across bitrate prefixes instead of specializing [4]. Truly elastic codecs without this penalty remain open.

**The semantic gap.** Acoustic codecs optimize waveform reconstruction, not linguistic content. Tokens that reconstruct beautifully can still under-serve language models on content-heavy tasks, motivating semantic distillation into the first RVQ stages (as in SpeechTokenizer and AudioLM's hybrid scheme) [6].

**Streaming latency.** Causal models pay a quality penalty versus non-causal ones at the same bitrate; chunked long-form encoding (as in DAC-JAX) trades latency against throughput via window sizing [8]. Sub-50 ms interactive budgets remain challenging at high fidelity.

**Codebook pathology.** RVQ training requires EMA updates, careful initialization, and collapse monitoring across *M* codebooks — operational complexity that FSQ sidesteps, at the cost of fixed grid geometry (§4.2).

**Evaluation.** ViSQOL and WARP-Q correlate imperfectly with human judgment, particularly for generative artifacts; MUSHRA is authoritative but slow and expensive. The field lacks a cheap, reliable perceptual metric for *generative* audio quality.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Neural audio codecs have collapsed the distance between compression and generation. RVQ provided the mathematical workhorse — exponential representational capacity at linear parameter cost with embedded bitrate scalability — while EnCodec industrialized it into streaming multi-bandwidth models and DAC universalized it to all audio at 8 kbps. The deeper legacy, however, is conceptual: the discrete token turned audio into a *language*, and AudioLM, VALL-E, and MusicGen are its first literature.

The frontier lies in resolving the tensions this thesis has mapped: elasticity without the quantizer-dropout tax, semantic richness without sacrificing acoustic fidelity, codebook-free quantization at RVQ's scalability, and perceptual metrics worthy of generative audio. The discrete turn in audio is not a compression story. It is an *alphabet* story, and we are still learning to read.
