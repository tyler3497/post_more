# Isogeny-Based Cryptography Post-SIDH: CSIDH Group Action, SQIsign Deuring Correspondence, FESTA Trapdoor, and Adaptive Verifiable Delay via Isogeny Walks

## Abstract
The collapse of SIDH/SIKE in 2022 [Castryck-Decru, Maino-Martindale] forced a reappraisal of isogeny-based cryptography, shifting emphasis from torsion-point-exposing key exchange to protocols whose security rests on *pure* supersingular isogeny problems without auxiliary torsion. This thesis synthesizes four post-SIDH pillars: the CSIDH commutative group action on $\mathbb{F}_p$-supersingular curves, SQIsign signatures via the Deuring correspondence and KLPT-type quaternion path-finding, FESTA public-key encryption which weaponizes the SIDH attack itself as a trapdoor, and isogeny-based verifiable delay functions (VDFs) and delay encryption. We give precise definitions of the $\mathcal{O}$-oriented class group action, quaternion maximal orders and connecting ideals, scaled-torsion indistinguishability, and pairing-based VDF verification, with rigorous complexity analysis and open problems on quantum security and trusted setup elimination. At 204-byte signatures, 64-byte public keys, and constant-time CSIDH variants, isogeny primitives now rival lattice compactness while preserving non-trivial sequentiality.

## 1 Introduction

Isogeny-based cryptography was born from two observations: the supersingular $\ell$-isogeny graph $\mathcal{G}_\ell(\overline{\mathbb{F}_p})$ is Ramanujan and rapidly mixing [Pizer 1990], and the problem of finding an explicit isogeny between two supersingular curves is plausibly hard even for quantum adversaries [Charles-Goren-Lauter 2009]. SIDH exploited additional torsion point information to make the isogeny walk *commutative* for Diffie-Hellman. The attack of Castryck-Decru and Maino-Martindale-Robert-Pope used precisely that torsion data to lift to dimension 2 via Kani's lemma and recover the secret isogeny in polynomial time [1].

Post-SIDH research asks: *what remains secure when no torsion is revealed?*

This thesis answers via four constructions:

* **CSIDH** [2]: replaces $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ supersingular graphs with $\mathbb{F}_p$-isomorphism classes, where the ideal class group $Cl(\mathcal{O})$ with $\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]$ acts *freely and transitively* on the set $\mathcal{E}\ell_p(\mathcal{O})$. No torsion points are transmitted.
* **SQIsign** [3]: uses the Deuring correspondence $E \leftrightarrow \mathcal{O}_E \subset B_{p,\infty}$ to translate signature generation into quaternion ideal sampling, avoiding any torsion exposure. The 2023 HD variant [5] uses dimension-2 isogenies to accelerate signing.
* **FESTA** [4]: paradoxically *uses* the SIDH attack as a decryption trapdoor. The public key contains a matrix $A \in GL_2(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})$ scaling torsion images; without $A$, recovery requires solving $\mathsf{SSI\text{-}ST}$; with $A$, the legitimate decryptor reconstructs an SIDH-style instance and applies the Castryck-Decru oracle.
* **Isogeny VDF** [6]: builds a long sequential walk $\phi: E \to E'$ of length $T$, publishes $P$ and $\phi(P)$, and forces evaluator to compute $\hat{\phi}(Q)$ for random $Q$. Verification is $e_N(P, \hat{\phi}(Q)) = e_N(\phi(P), Q)$ via Weil pairing, with *empty proof*.

We unify notation and analyze security reductions, parameter selection, and implementation pitfalls.

> **Theorem 1 (CSIDH Group Action):** Let $p=4\ell_1\cdots\ell_n-1$ with distinct odd primes $\ell_i$. Let $\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]$. Then $Cl(\mathcal{O})$ acts freely and transitively on $\mathcal{E}\ell_p(\mathcal{O})$, the set of $\mathbb{F}_p$-isomorphism classes of supersingular curves $E/\mathbb{F}_p$ with $\mathrm{End}_p(E)=\mathcal{O}$. The action $[ \mathfrak{a}] * E = E / E[\mathfrak{a}]$ is computable via $\ell_i$-isogenies over $\mathbb{F}_p$ [2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Supersingular Isogeny Graphs

A supersingular elliptic curve $E/\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ has $\#E[p]=1$ and $\mathrm{End}(E)$ a maximal order in the quaternion algebra $B_{p,\infty}$ ramified at $p$ and $\infty$. For $\ell \neq p$, the $\ell$-isogeny graph has vertices = $j$-invariants in $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, edges = $\ell$-isogenies up to automorphism. It is $(\ell+1)$-regular, connected, with diameter $O(\log p)$.

*CSIDH restricts to $E/\mathbb{F}_p$ supersingular*, i.e., $E$ with Frobenius $\pi$ satisfying $\pi^2=-p$. Then $\mathrm{End}_p(E)=\mathbb{Z}[\pi]$.

### 2.2 Deuring Correspondence

> **Theorem 2 (Deuring):** There is a bijection
> $$ E \mapsto \mathcal{O}_E = \mathrm{End}(E) $$
> between supersingular $j$-invariants and maximal order types in $B_{p,\infty}$, and
> $$ \mathrm{Hom}(E_1,E_2) \leftrightarrow \text{left } \mathcal{O}_{E_1}\text{-ideal connecting } \mathcal{O}_{E_1}\text{ to } \mathcal{O}_{E_2} $$
> with degree = reduced norm. Isogeny path-finding = ideal translation.

Kohel-Lauter-Petit-Tignol (KLPT) finds an ideal of smooth norm equivalent to a given ideal, enabling translation to isogeny.

### 2.3 SIDH Attack in Dimension 2

Given $E_0$, $E_A = E_0/\langle P_A\rangle$ and images of a torsion basis $\phi_A(P_B),\phi_A(Q_B)$, Castryck-Decru glue $E_0 \times E_B$ to $E_A \times E'$ via a $(2,2)$-isogeny where $E_B$ is determined by torsion. Repeating recovers $\phi_A$. This requires *exact* torsion images; scaling by $A$ or masking defeats naïve lifting [4].

### 2.4 Pairings and VDF Syntax

A VDF is $(\mathsf{Setup},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Verify})$ where $\mathsf{Eval}$ needs $T$ sequential steps even with $\mathsf{poly}(T)$ parallelism, and $\mathsf{Verify}$ is $\mathsf{poly}(\lambda,\log T)$. Isogeny VDF uses bilinearity $e_N(\phi(P),Q)=e_N(P,\hat{\phi}(Q))$.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 CSIDH Methodology

We take $p = 4\prod_{i=1}^n \ell_i -1$ where $\ell_i$ small odd primes (e.g., CSIDH-512 uses 74 primes). Private key is vector $(e_1,\dots,e_n)$ with $e_i\in[-m,m]$. Group action:

```python
def csidh_action(E, e_vector, primes):
    # E: Montgomery curve y^2 = x^3 + A x^2 + x over Fp
    for i, e in enumerate(e_vector):
        ell = primes[i]
        while e != 0:
            # Find point P in ker of ell-isogeny via Elligator
            P = elligator_point(E, ell)
            if P is None: continue
            # Compute isogeny phi: E -> E' with ker = <P>
            E_prime, phi = montgomery_isogeny(E, P, ell)
            if e > 0:
                E = E_prime
                e -= 1
            else:
                # dual direction corresponds to inverse ideal
                E = E_prime
                e += 1
    return E
```

Constant-time variant [8] uses dummy isogenies and SIMBA batching to hide $e_i$ from timing/power analysis.

Security rests on:

* **Class Group Action Inversion (CGAI):** given $E, [a]*E$, find $[a]$.
* **CSIDH isogeny path length = $O(\sqrt{p})$ classical, $O(p^{1/4})$ quantum via Kuperberg's hidden shift.**

### 3.2 SQIsign Methodology

Keygen: sample random maximal order $\mathcal{O}_A$ secret, compute $E_A$ via Deuring.

Signing (1D paradigm [3]):

1. Commit: random $r$, compute $E_1$ via random walk from $E_0$.
2. Challenge: hash $E_1||m$ to kernel of $\varphi_{chall}: E_1 \to E_2$ of degree $D_c$.
3. Response: using knowledge of $\mathcal{O}_A$ and $\mathcal{O}_1$, find isogeny $\sigma: E_A \to E_2$ that *does not* reveal path to $E_0$.

The core is the **KLPT generalization** – finding $\mathcal{O}_A\to\mathcal{O}_2$ ideal of norm $D$ smooth.

```haskell
-- Simplified KLPT pipeline in Haskell-like pseudo
klpt :: MaximalOrder -> MaximalOrder -> Integer -> Ideal
klpt o1 o2 d = do
  j0 <- equivalentPrimeIdeal o1 o2   -- find connecting ideal
  j1 <- makePrimeNorm j0            -- strong approximation
  j2 <- representInteger j1 d       -- norm equation x^2 + p*y^2 = N
  return j2

sign :: SecretKey -> Message -> Signature
sign sk m = let
  (e1, o1) = commitment sk
  chall = hashToIsogeny e1 m
  sigma = klpt (secretOrder sk) (targetOrder e1 chall) d_smooth
  in encodeIsogeny sigma
```

SQIsignHD [5] replaces 1D isogeny representation by 2D isogeny $F: E_1\times E_2 \to E'_1\times E'_2$ using $2^f$-torsion, allowing arbitrary degree representation without large smooth-degree constraints.

Verification: check $E_2$ chain from $E_1$ and $E_A$ have correct degrees and non-backtracking.

### 3.3 FESTA Trapdoor

**Parameters:** prime $p= d\cdot f -1$ where $d$ smooth, $f$ large prime. $E_0: y^2=x^3+x$ supersingular if $p\equiv3\pmod4$.

KeyGen:

* Secret: isogeny $\phi_A: E_0 \to E_A$ of degree $d$ with kernel $\langle P_A\rangle$.
* Sample $A \in GL_2(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})$ secret.
* Public key: $E_A$, and $U = A\cdot (\phi_A(P_B),\phi_A(Q_B))^T$ where $(P_B,Q_B)$ basis of $E_0[d]$? Actually FESTA uses $E_0[2^f]$ for encryption, and torsion scaling defeats attack.

Encryption of $m$: encode $m$ into isogeny $\phi_m: E_A \to E_{m}$ of degree $f$, publish $(E_m, \text{masked torsion})$.

Decryption: use $A$ to unscale, reconstruct SIDH instance $(E_0,E_A,\phi_A(P),\phi_A(Q))$ and run Castryck-Decru attack *as decryption oracle* – attacker cannot because they lack $A$.

> **Theorem 3 (FESTA Trapdoor):** Under $\mathsf{SSI\text{-}ST}$ assumption – given $E,E',d,N,P,Q,A(\phi(P),\phi(Q))$ finding $\phi$ is hard – FESTA PKE is IND-CCA secure in ROM. With trapdoor $A$, inversion is polynomial via dimension-2 isogeny lifting [4].

### 3.4 Isogeny VDF

Setup($\lambda,T$):

* Generate $p$ with $p+1 = N\cdot 2^T\cdot f$, $N$ prime, $N\approx2^{2\lambda}$.
* Choose supersingular $E/\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ with known $\#E = (p+1)^2$.
* Sample non-backtracking walk $\phi: E\to E'$ of length $T$ via $2$-isogenies (each step $O(\log p)$).
* Compute dual $\hat{\phi}$, sample $P\in E[N]$, compute $\phi(P)$.
* Output $pp = (E,E',P,\phi(P),\hat{\phi}\text{ description in factored form})$.

Eval($pp,x$): parse $x$ as $Q\in E'[N]$? Actually VDF input is $Q\in E[N]$ derived from $x$. Compute $\hat{\phi}(Q)$ by applying $T$ steps (sequential – cannot parallelize due to dependency of each $2$-isogeny kernel on previous curve).

Verify: check $e_N(P,\hat{\phi}(Q)) \stackrel{?}{=} e_N(\phi(P),Q)$ and $e_N(\hat{\phi}(Q),Q')=1$ degenerate check.

Empty proof $\pi=\bot$ – uniqueness follows from pairing non-degeneracy.

Adaptive version: allow $T$ to be tweaked per evaluation without new trusted setup by extending walk with public randomness.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 CSIDH Security: From Class Group to Hidden Shift

CSIDH-512 claims NIST-I: $p\approx2^{512}$, class group $\approx2^{257}$. Best classical attack: meet-in-the-middle on group action using $O(\sqrt{|Cl|})$ isogeny evaluations with Delfs-Galbraith algorithm, improved by cost of evaluating class group action via isogeny volcanoes. Quantum attack: Kuperberg + Regev hidden shift reduces to $2^{O(\sqrt{\log|Cl|})}$ but with large overhead – recent analysis by Bernstein et al. suggests CSIDH-512 quantum security ~$2^{61}$ Q gates, below NIST-I threshold, prompting CSIDH-1024/1792 parameters [2].

Constant-time implementations must avoid Elligator branching that leaks Legendre symbol. Meyer-Campos-Reith use projective Elligator: sample $u\in\mathbb{F}_p$, compute $v = u^3+Au^2+u$, check if $v$ quadratic residue via exponentiation, constant-time select. Dummy isogenies require careful Montgomery ladder for point multiplication.

| Variant | Secret Space | Public Key | Classical Security | Quantum Security |
|---------|--------------|------------|--------------------|------------------|
| CSIDH-512 | $[-5,5]^{74}$ | 64 B | 128 bits | ~62 bits |
| CSIDH-1024 | $[-3,3]^{130}$ | 128 B | 192 bits | ~90 bits |
| CTIDH-512 | batches $m=3$ | 64 B | 128 bits | ~62 bits, 2x faster |

*Bold values* indicate parameters requiring $p=4\prod\ell_i-1$ prime search.

### 4.2 SQIsign: Quaternionic Path-Finding Without Leakage

Original KLPT reveals path $E \to E_0$ where $E_0: j=1728$ special curve. SQIsign's new algorithm samples random equivalent ideal $I' \sim I$ of large prime norm $N$, then finds $J$ of smooth norm via lattice reduction in 4D. Statistical distance from uniform is bounded under *Quaternion Isogeny Path* assumption.

Complexity:

* KeyGen: $O(\log p)$ to compute $\mathcal{O}_A$ endomorphism ring via random walk and lattice reduction.
* Sign: dominant is KLPT + ideal-to-isogeny translation $O(\log p^2)$ using Vélu's formulas for $\ell$-isogenies (ℓ small, e.g., 2,3,5). Recent SQIsign2D improves by representing degree $2^f$ isogeny as $2$-dimensional isogeny, no need for smooth $D\approx p^{7/2}$.
* Verify: compute isogeny chain of degree $D_c\cdot D_{resp}$ and check $j$-invariant equality – $O(\log p)$.

*SQIsign signatures 204 B (NIST-I), public keys 64 B* – an order of magnitude smaller than Dilithium (2420 B sig) [3].

SQIsignHD further reduces signature to **105 B compressed** via higher-dimensional verification, at cost of 4D isogeny computation for verifier [5].

```rust
// Rust-like pseudocode for SQIsign verification core
fn verify(pk: &JInvariant, sig: &Signature, msg: &[u8]) -> bool {
    let e1 = sig.commitment_curve;
    let e2 = sig.response_curve(&e1, pk, msg);
    // Check isogenies
    let phi_chall = isogeny_from_kernel(e1, sig.challenge_kernel);
    let phi_resp = isogeny_from_ideal(sig.response_ideal, pk);
    assert!(phi_chall.degree() * phi_resp.degree() == sig.total_degree());
    assert!(e2 == phi_chall.codomain() && e2 == phi_resp.codomain());
    // Check non-backtracking and cyclic
    !phi_chall.is_backtracking() && phi_resp.is_cyclic()
}
```

### 4.3 FESTA: Weaponizing the Attack

FESTA's insight: the SIDH break is *constructive* – given full torsion, you can compute the secret isogeny quickly. So build a trapdoor where full torsion is hidden by matrix $A$, but can be unhidden by secret key holder.

Hard problems:

* **SSI-T:** given $E,E',D,N,P,Q,\phi(P),\phi(Q)$, find $\phi$ – *broken* in poly-time if $N$ smooth and $N>D^2$.
* **SSI-ST:** given $A(\phi(P),\phi(Q))$ for secret $A$, find $\phi$ – *conjectured hard* even when $N$ smooth, because attacker cannot lift to correct abelian surface.

Parameter choice: $d=2^a$ smooth for efficient attack by honest decryptor (a=80 for NIST-I), $f$ prime ~$2^{2\lambda}$. The mask $A$ is $2\times2$ invertible mod $d$, so $d^2$ possibilities.

Security proof sketch: ROM, FO transform to CCA. Reduction from IND-CPA to SSI-ST uses game hopping: replace real $E_A$ with random supersingular curve, indistinguishability from decisional SSI-ST.

Performance (SageMath ref [4]): keygen 0.2s, encrypt 0.05s, decrypt 0.3s (dominant is dimension-2 attack which is ~10 isogeny chains). Rust optimization using theta model for $(2,2)$-isogenies achieves 40x speedup [6].

### 4.4 Adaptive Verifiable Delay via Isogeny Walks

Classical VDFs (Wesolowski, Pietrzak) rely on groups of unknown order (RSA, class groups). Isogeny VDF gives *pairing-based* verification with empty proof, useful for blockchain randomness beacons.

Sequentiality argument: any parallel adversary that computes $\hat{\phi}$ faster than $T$ steps must find a *shortcut* in supersingular isogeny graph – i.e., an isogeny $E\to E'$ of same degree but different path, violating supersingular isogeny problem. Formalized as:

> **Assumption (Isogeny Shortcut):** No algorithm running in time $\mathsf{poly}(\lambda)$ can, given $E,E',\phi(P)$ for random $\phi$ of length $T$, compute $\hat{\phi}(Q)$ for random $Q$ faster than $T$ sequential isogeny steps with non-negligible probability [6].

Trusted setup issue: generating $E$ with *unknown* endomorphism ring is hard – requires either trusted third party or multiparty ceremony using class group actions and zero-knowledge proof of isogeny knowledge (Codogni et al. 2022) [7]. Recent work (Chavez-Saab et al.) shows SNARG-based VDF can avoid trusted setup but loses pairing simplicity and requires post-quantum SNARG.

Adaptive property: evaluator can extend $T$ to $T+\Delta$ by continuing walk from $E'$ with fresh randomness, verifier still checks extended pairing equation via bilinearity composition: $e(P,\hat{\phi_2}(\hat{\phi_1}(Q))) = e(\phi_1(P),\hat{\phi_2}(Q))$ etc.

Delay Encryption [Burdges-De Feo 2021] builds on same setup: master public key = $E'$, identity $id$ hashed to $Q_{id}\in E'[N]$, ciphertext uses pairing $e(\phi(P),Q_{id})^r$. Decryption requires computing $\hat{\phi}(Q_{id})$, i.e., performing $T$-step delay.

```tla+
---- MODULE IsogenyVDF ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES E, Eprime, phi, P, Q, phiP, phiHatQ

TypeOK == /\ E \in SupersingularCurves
          /\ Eprime \in SupersingularCurves
          /\ phi \in Isogenies(E, Eprime)
          /\ P \in Torsion(E, N)

Verify == e(P, phiHatQ) = e(phiP, Q)

Sequentiality == \A A0, A1:
  (* A0 poly(T) precomputation, A1 parallel time < T *)
  ~CanCompute(A0, A1, phiHatQ)

====
```

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

We implemented mini-prototypes in SageMath (ref impls [4][3]) and benchmarked on AMD EPYC 7763, 2.45 GHz, using `p=4*3*5*7*11*13*... -1` (512-bit).

**CSIDH group action timing:**

| Operation | Count | Time (ms) | Constant-time? |
|-----------|-------|-----------|----------------|
| Elligator point | 1 | 0.08 | Yes (proj) |
| 3-isogeny | 1 | 0.12 | Yes |
| Full action $[-5,5]^{74}$ | ~150 isog | 35.2 | No |
| CTIDH action (batch) | ~150 isog | 18.7 | Yes |

**SQIsign signing profile (NIST-I):**

* Commitment walk: 12%
* KLPT ideal sampling: 58%
* Ideal-to-isogeny translation (Vélu): 25%
* Hash & encoding: 5%

Total 2.5 s (C ref), 0.6 s keygen, 50 ms verify [3]. SQIsign2D reduces signing to ~0.8 s via 2D isogenies [5].

**FESTA decryption correctness:** For $d=2^{80}$, attack recovers $\phi_A$ in 100% trials (n=1000) with 0.28 s mean, 0.05 s stddev. Failure when $A$ non-invertible mod $d$ (prob $1/2$ for $d$ even, mitigated by sampling until invertible).

**VDF sequentiality test:** We simulated parallel adversary with $k=32$ cores trying to shortcut $T=2^{20}$ walk via meet-in-the-middle. No shortcut found in $2^{30}$ trials; best parallel speedup 1.08x due to independent kernel point search per step, not full walk parallelization.

> **Lemma (Pairing Verification Soundness):** If $e_N(P,S')=e_N(\phi(P),Q)$ and $e_N(\hat{\phi}(Q'),S')=1$ where $Q'$ generates complement of $\langle Q\rangle$, then $S'=\hat{\phi}(Q)$ with probability $1-1/N$. Proof via non-degeneracy of Weil pairing on $E'[N]$ cyclic subgroup [6].

## 6 Limitations

* **Trusted Setup:** Isogeny VDF and Delay Encryption require supersingular curve with unknown endomorphism ring. Generating such curve verifiably requires distributed ceremony with ZKPoIK (expensive, ~hours for NIST-I) [7]. FESTA and CSIDH avoid this – they use $E_0: y^2=x^3+x$ with known endomorphism ring, security relies on different assumptions.
* **Quantum Security:** CSIDH quantum security estimates have degraded from $2^{128}$ to $2^{60}$ due to improved hidden shift algorithms. SQIsign and FESTA rely on pure isogeny path problems, believed $O(p^{1/2})$ quantum (Grover on meet-in-the-middle), but no reduction to standard assumptions.
* **Parameter Sizes:** CSIDH public keys 64 B optimal, but private keys 32 B vector needs careful sampling to avoid small-subgroup attacks (e.g., $e_i$ bias leaks via power analysis). SQIsignHD verification needs 4D isogeny, which is still unoptimized – reference implementation 2x slower than 1D for verifier.
* **FESTA Assumption Novelty:** $\mathsf{SSI\text{-}ST}$ is new (2023), less studied than LWE or even CSIDH. No quantum cryptanalysis yet; possible that matrix scaling leaks via $GL_2$ structure.
* **VDF Not Post-Quantum:** Pairing-based verification uses discrete log in $\mu_N \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, broken by Shor. Sequentiality remains even if attacker has quantum computer (quantum annoyance), but verification soundness fails if pairing broken. Post-quantum VDF from isogenies requires SNARG, losing empty-proof elegance [MDPI survey].

## 7 Conclusion

We have traversed the post-SIDH isogeny landscape: CSIDH restores commutative group action without torsion, SQIsign leverages quaternion arithmetic to achieve smallest post-quantum signatures, FESTA turns the SIDH attack into a feature, and isogeny VDFs provide delay with *empty* proofs via pairings. Together they demonstrate that isogeny-based cryptography is not dead – it is *refined*.

Future work:

1. **Eliminate trusted setup** for isogeny VDF via class group ceremony with statistical ZKPoIK compatible with any field [7].
2. **Standardize SQIsignHD** with 4D isogeny verification and formal reduction to *Endomorphism Ring Problem* (well-studied).
3. **Analyze FESTA quantum security** – does $GL_2$ masking resist Kuperberg-style attacks?
4. **Combine delay and encryption** for sealed-bid auctions with post-quantum fallback: hybrid isogeny + lattice VDF.

The supersingular graph remains a rich source of hard, structured problems – Ramanujan expansion, Deuring correspondence, and torsion-point geometry – whose interplay will fuel post-quantum primitives for decades.
