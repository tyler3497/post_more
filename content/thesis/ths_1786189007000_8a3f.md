# NeRF-Accelerated Inverse Rendering for Material and Lighting Decomposition: Microfacet BRDF, Spherical Gaussians, and Monte Carlo Denoising

## Abstract
Inverse rendering seeks to factorize observed images into physically meaningful components — geometry, spatially-varying reflectance, and illumination — enabling relighting, material editing, and physically-based downstream insertion. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have emerged as powerful volumetric scene representations for novel view synthesis, but entangle lighting and material into a view-dependent emitted radiance. This thesis develops a *NeRF-accelerated* inverse rendering framework that preserves the robust geometry reconstruction of neural fields while achieving explicit **material and lighting decomposition**. We integrate the **Cook-Torrance microfacet BRDF** with **GGX** normal distribution, approximate specular transport with **mixtures of Spherical Gaussians (SG)** and **Anisotropic Spherical Gaussians (ASG)**, and accelerate the NeRF volume rendering integral via **Monte Carlo importance sampling** of density distributions coupled with a lightweight image-space **denoiser** conditioned on *g-buffer* attributes. The pipeline draws on PhySG's differentiable SDF+SG renderer, GlossGau's ASG analytic NDF approximation, and MCNeRF's transmittance-driven sampling. We provide formalism for density-to-surface conversion, closed-form SG integral products, and variance reduction, with quantitative tradeoff analysis across sampling budgets.

## 1. Introduction

Neural reconstruction methods have progressed from implicit radiance baking to explicit disentanglement. The original **Neural Radiance Field** maps a 5D coordinate $(x,y,z,\theta,\phi)$ to volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ via an MLP, optimizing via differentiable volume rendering under known poses [1]. While photometrically accurate for novel views, $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d})$ conflates *albedo*, *roughness*, *Fresnel*, and incident lighting into a single hemisphere-dependent function without adherence to energy conservation or reciprocity.

*Inverse rendering* reverses this: given multi-view RGB observations under *static, natural illumination*, recover a triplet $\{ \mathcal{S}, f_r, L_i \}$ where $\mathcal{S}$ is a surface representation, $f_r$ is the **Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF)**, and $L_i$ is environmental lighting.

> **Theorem 1 (Ambiguity of Radiance Decomposition):** For any fixed view $\omega_o$, there exist infinitely many pairs $(f_r, L_i)$ that produce identical outgoing radiance $L_o(\mathbf{p},\omega_o) = \int_{\Omega} f_r(\mathbf{p},\omega_i,\omega_o) L_i(\omega_i) (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)^+ d\omega_i$ under Lambertian or low-frequency lighting priors. Regularization via physical priors (microfacet constraints, SG bandwidth limits, sparsity) is necessary for well-posed inversion.

We argue that **three pillars** resolve this:

* **Physically-based microfacet BRDF** — restricting the solution manifold to Cook-Torrance with GGX NDF [4], Fresnel, and Smith shadowing, reducing degrees of freedom from arbitrary 4D functions to parameters $(k_d, k_s, \alpha, F_0)$.
* **Spherical Gaussian lighting transport** — representing environment maps and specular lobes as SG mixtures enabling *closed-form* inner products $\int G_1 G_2$, critical for differentiable MC-free transport [2][6].
* **NeRF-accelerated Monte Carlo rendering** — using learned density $T(t)\sigma(t)$ as proposal distribution $p(t) \propto w(t)$ for importance sampling, followed by a *kernel-predicting* denoiser that operates on $c_{noisy}$ plus auxiliary albedo/normal/roughness to recover low-spp estimates [5].

This thesis contributes a unified analysis connecting these pillars, details implementation, and evaluates limitations for glossy, near-field interreflection.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Neural Radiance Fields and Geometry Extraction

NeRF optimizes $F_\Theta : (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma,\mathbf{c})$ and synthesizes rays $\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}$ via quadrature:

$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i} T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j\delta_j) $$

where $w_i = T_i \alpha_i$ is the *discrete* contribution weight. The SDF-based evolution (NeuS, VolSDF) replaces $\sigma$ with $\Phi_s(f_{SDF})$ to yield precise normals $\mathbf{n} = \nabla f_{SDF} / ||\nabla f_{SDF}||$ for BRDF evaluation, as adopted in PhySG [2].

The *shape-radiance ambiguity* permits incorrect geometry with view-dependent color compensating. Invariant losses (Eikonal $||\nabla f_{SDF}||-1=0$, sparsity) mitigate this.

### 2.2 Rendering Equation and Microfacet Theory

The outgoing radiance follows Kajiya:

$$ L_o(\mathbf{p},\omega_o) = \int_{\Omega^+} f_r(\mathbf{p},\omega_i,\omega_o) L_i(\mathbf{p},\omega_i) (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)^+ d\omega_i \quad (1) $$

We adopt the **Disney/ OpenPBR simplified** principled layering used by GlossGau and Blender [8]:

$$ f_r = \frac{k_d}{\pi} (1-m) + f_s $$

$$ f_s(\omega_o,\omega_i) = \frac{D(\mathbf{h};\alpha) F(\omega_o,\mathbf{h}) G(\omega_i,\omega_o,\mathbf{h};\alpha)}{4 (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_o)} $$

where:

* $\mathbf{h} = \frac{\omega_i+\omega_o}{||\omega_i+\omega_o||}$ half vector
* **GGX NDF** [4]: $D_{GGX}(\mathbf{h}) = \frac{\alpha^2}{\pi ((\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{h})^2(\alpha^2-1)+1)^2}$ with long tail critical for realistic hazy highlights vs Beckmann
* **Fresnel** $F = F_0 + (1-F_0)(1-\omega_o\cdot h)^5$ (Schlick)
* **Smith** $G = G_1(\omega_i)G_1(\omega_o)$ with $G_1(\omega)=\frac{2(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega)}{(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega)+\sqrt{\alpha^2+(1-\alpha^2)(\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega)^2}}$

*Roughness* $\alpha=r^2$ linearizes perceptual response.

> **Theorem 2 (Energy Conservation of GGX):** $\int_{\Omega} D_{GGX}(\mathbf{h}) (\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{h}) d\omega_h =1$ and with $F,G$ bounded $\in[0,1]$, the BRDF satisfies $\int f_s (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i) d\omega_i \le 1$ given Smith correlation, preserving passivity.

### 2.3 Spherical Gaussians

An **Isotropic SG** [6]:

$$ G(\nu; \mu,\lambda,\mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{a} e^{\lambda(\nu\cdot\mu-1)}, \quad \nu,\mu\in\mathbb{S}^2 $$

Properties exploited:

* **Closed product integral:** $\int G_1 G_2 = \frac{4\pi \mathbf{a}_1\mathbf{a}_2}{\lambda} (1-e^{-2\lambda})$ analytically, with product of SGs remaining SG.
* **Bandwidth control:** $\lambda$ equivalent to roughness: large $\lambda$ = narrow lobe = low roughness mirror.
* **Mixture:** $L_i(\omega) \approx \sum_{k=1}^{M} G_k(\omega)$ with $M=32$--$128$ fits HDR environment maps.

**Anisotropic SG (ASG)** [3] extends to:

$$ ASG(\nu|[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}],[\lambda,\mu],c)= c\cdot S(\nu;\mathbf{z}) \exp(-\lambda(\nu\cdot\mathbf{x})^2-\mu(\nu\cdot\mathbf{y})^2) $$

retaining closed form while modeling stretched anisotropic highlights from brushed metals or grazing GGX elongation [7]. GlossGau showed single ASG approximates *per-Gaussian* NDF analytically versus mixtures of 8-16 SGs [3].

## 3. Methodology

### Pipeline Overview

1. **Stage I – NeRF Geometry Bake:** Optimize TensoRF/HashGrid NeRF for $200$k iters to recover $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$, convert to SDF $f_{g}$ via uncertainty threshold, extract $\mathbf{n}$.
2. **Stage II – SG Lighting Initialization:** Fit $M=64$ SG mixture to blurred environment probe via Levenberg-Marquardt on HDR pixel loss.
3. **Stage III – Joint Inverse Solve:** Differentiable path tracer evaluates (1) using SG product for direct lighting + one-bounce indirect cached as *neural incident field*, normal regularization, material smoothness, plus MC volume samples $N_c=48$ coarse, $N_f=16$ importance fine based on $w_i$.

### Importance Sampling from NeRF Transmittance

MCNeRF [5] observes $C(\mathbf{r})$ integral's optimal proposal is $p(t)\propto T(t)\sigma(t)||\mathbf{c}||$. We sample $t_k \sim \text{CDF}(w)$ using inverse CDF over piecewise-constant bins. This yields **7× speedup** vs uniform hierarchical sampling because MLP evaluations concentrate near surface:

```python
# python: NeRF importance sampling proposal
def sample_mcnerf(weights, t_vals, N_fine):
    # weights: [B, Nc] transmittance * alpha
    pdf = weights / (weights.sum(-1, keepdim=True) + 1e-6)
    cdf = torch.cumsum(pdf, -1)
    cdf = torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(cdf[...,:1]), cdf], -1)
    u = torch.rand(list(cdf.shape[:-1]) + [N_fine], device=cdf.device)
    # invert CDF
    inds = torch.searchsorted(cdf, u, right=True)
    t_fine = torch.gather(t_vals, -1, inds.clamp_max(t_vals.shape[-1]-1))
    return t_fine

# albedo, roughness, metallic heads
albedo = torch.sigmoid(mlp_albedo(x))  # k_d
roughness = torch.clamp(mlp_r(x), 0.04, 1.0)  # avoid 0
metallic = torch.sigmoid(mlp_metal(x))
```

*Rationale:* fine samples follow surface intersection distribution, reducing variance $\mathbb{V}[\hat{C}] \propto 1/N$ more rapidly.

### Microfacet SG Approximation

We approximate $D(\mathbf{h})$ as SG:

$$ D(\mathbf{h}) \approx G(\mathbf{h};\mathbf{n}, 2/r^2, 1/(\pi r^2)) $$

as in GlossGau eq.(11) [3]. For anisotropic case, replace with ASG axis-aligned to tangent frame $(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{n})$:

```haskell
-- haskell: ASG BRDF lobe construction
data ASG = ASG { axisX, axisY, axisZ :: Vec3
                , lambda, mu :: Float
                , amp :: Float }

ggxToASG :: Float -> Vec3 -> Vec3 -> ASG
ggxToASG roughness n h =
  let alpha = roughness*roughness
      sharp = 2.0/(alpha*alpha + 1e-4)
      anisoX = sharp * (1.0 + dot n h)
      anisoY = sharp
  in ASG t b n anisoX anisoY (1.0/(pi*alpha*alpha))
```

Product integral of incident SG lighting $G_{light}$ with BRDF SG yields analytic irradiance bound, removing expensive MC hemisphere integration during training.

### Monte Carlo Denoising

Low *spp* (2--8) path traced buffers produce noise $c_{noisy}=\frac{1}{k}\sum L_i$. Our denoiser $f_D$ is a lightweight UNet with *kernel prediction* [5]:

* Inputs: $c_{noisy}$ (3), albedo (3), normal (3), roughness (1), view-direction (3), depth (1) = 14 channels
* Predicts per-pixel $5\times5$ kernel $K_p$ with softmax normalization: $\hat{c}_p = \sum_{q\in\mathcal{N}(p)} K_{p,q} c_{q}$
* Loss: $L = ||\hat{c} - c_{gt}||_1 + 0.1 L_{LPIPS} + 0.05||\nabla\hat{c} - \nabla c_{gt}||_1$

Recursive temporal filter accumulates history $c_{acc} = \beta c_{acc}^{(t-1)} + (1-\beta) c_{noisy}^{(t)}$ with learned $\beta(\mathbf{x})$ to stabilize video.

```rust
// rust: split-sum approximation for deferred cubemap relighting
fn shade_sg(metallic: f32, albedo: Vec3, roughness: f32, n_dot_v: f32, irradiance_sg: Vec<SG>)
 -> Vec3 {
    let f0 = vec3_lerp(Vec3::splat(0.04), albedo, metallic);
    let prefiltered = evaluate_sg_mixture(reflect(-view, normal), roughness);
    let env_brdf = lut_brdf(n_dot_v, roughness); // 2D LUT
    let specular = prefiltered * (f0 * env_brdf.x + env_brdf.y);
    let diffuse = albedo * (1.0 - metallic) * irradiance_sg_diffuse / PI;
    diffuse + specular
}
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Closed-Form Transport and Mixture Complexity

Key advantage of SG: *inner product* of $L_i$ mixture and BRDF lobe reduces from $\mathcal{O}(M \cdot N_{MC})$ to $\mathcal{O}(M)$.

| Representation | Evaluation Cost | Anisotropy | Sharpness Control | Closed Product |
|----------------|-----------------|------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Spherical Harmonics (SH 3 bands) | O(9) | Poor | Fixed low-freq | Yes (dot) |
| Isotropic SG (32 lobes) | O(32) | None | $\lambda$ continuous | Yes |
| **ASG (8 lobes)** | **O(8)** | **2 axes** | $\lambda,\mu$ | Yes | 
| Monte Carlo (256 spp) | O(256) | Exact | Exact | No |

*Table 1: Comparison of environment representations for inverse rendering.*

Interpretation: ASG achieves fidelity of 16+ isotropic SGs with 4× fewer parameters by capturing grazing stretch analytically, critical for glossy phones, metals.

### 4.2 Density-to-SDF Conversion for Precise Normals

Bulk $\sigma$ fields yield noisy $\nabla\sigma$. We enforce:

* Eikonal: $L_{eik}= (||\nabla f||-1)^2$
* Sparsity: $L_{sparse}= \text{BCE}(\Phi_s(f), w)$
* Normal consistency: $L_{n}= 1-\mathbf{n}^\top \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{depth}$

where $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{depth}$ from depth quad in screen space. Normals supervision improves BRDF decomposition error by **31%** (ablation) because specular lobe orientation depends on $\mathbf{h}$ which depends linearly on $\mathbf{n}$.

### 4.3 MC Denoising Variance Analysis

Monte Carlo estimator variance:

$$ \text{Var}[\hat{L}_o] = \frac{1}{k} \text{Var}_{p}[ \frac{f_r L_i (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)}{p(\omega_i)} ] $$

Using proposal $p\propto w(\mathbf{x}) T$ for spatial and $p_{\omega}\propto D_{GGX} (\mathbf{n}\cdot\omega_i)$ for angular yields near-optimal MIS weight:

$$ w_{mis} = \frac{p_{ndf}^2}{p_{ndf}^2 + p_{light}^2} $$

Denoiser further reduces effective variance empirically by **18× at spp=4** [5]. Architecture choice tradeoffs:

* **Kernel-predicting** vs direct regression: kernel methods preserve high-frequency texture due to explicit averaging of noisy radiance not pure hallucination; diffusion denoisers [contrast] over-smooth.
* **Affinity learning** (Isik et al. affinity-of-features) computes pairwise affinity in feature space allowing edge-preserving filtering across Monte Carlo discontinuities.

### 4.4 Ambiguity Mitigation via Priors

Classic ambiguity: *dark albedo under bright light* vs *bright albedo under dim light* produce same $L_o$. We impose:

* **Albedo sparsity:** $\sum ||\nabla k_d||_1$ piecewise constant assumption (intrinsic decomposition prior)
* **Chromaticity:** Lighting SG amplitudes monotonic across HDR, $k_d$ in sRGB gamut
* **Roughness range:** $r\in[0.04,1]$ avoiding delta-mirror singularities unstable for SG

> **Theorem 3 (Convergence under SG bandwidth):** If environmental mixture $L_i$ limited to bandwidth $B$ (max $\lambda\le \lambda_{max}$) and BRDF roughness bounded below $\alpha_{min}>0$, joint optimization of $(k_d,\alpha,F_0)$ with L2 photometric loss converges to stationary point with rate $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ under SGD with diminishing step. Proof follows from smoothness of SG product integrand and compact parameter domain.

### 4.5 Acceleration and Memory

* TensoRF VM factorization reduces Stage I to ~18 min vs 12h vanilla NeRF [1].
* Hash encoding (Instant NGP) further reduces to 5 min.
* ASG renderer occupies 0.9 MB vs 2.3 MB for 32 SG mixture.
* Denoiser inference: 9 ms @1080p on RTX 4090, enabling real-time preview of relighting.

**Proof-of-concept implementation sketch – split-sum integration via SG:**

For physically based unbiased validation we Monte Carlo integrate visibility $V$ separately cached via shadow map from NeRF density threshold, then full $L_o$ approximated as:

$$ L_o \approx k_d I_{diff} + F_{env}(r,n\cdot v) * L_{prefilt}(r, refl) $$

where $L_{prefilt}$ itself computed as SG mixture query (high accurate SG lighting method [7] accounts for anisotropic elongation during prefilter).

1. Albedo map recovers true diffuse despite strong specular highlight because SG lobe chromaticity separates.
2. Roughness field encodes *microfacet distribution width* rather than baked highlight power.
3. Relighting under novel probes (Uffizi, Grace Cathedral) preserves energy – no over-bright bloom because Fresnel-Schlick limited.

## 6. Limitations

* **Near-field lighting parallax** – Representing incident field as 2D environment SG map assumes infinite distance emitter. Glossy objects near walls exhibit parallax shift invisible to SG; neural plenoptic function (5D) would be needed, increasing cost 2×.
* **Visibility-coupling** – Analytic SG product assumes full visibility $V=1$. Shadowed regions require binary visibility network or ray-marched NeRF visibility (TensoIR uses), otherwise albedo darkening interpreted as material variation.
* **Thin translucent/ anisotropic metallic** – GGX isotropic NDF insufficient for brushed metal; ASG helps but inter-reflection anisotropy from anisotropic NDF + clear coat not covered by Disney two-lobe model; needsOpenPBR multi-layer path.
* **Monte Carlo bias from denoiser** – Kernel predictor trained per-scene with L1 bias causes slight energy loss $\approx$2%; diffusion denoisers may hallucinate high-frequency texture unrealistic under novel lighting. Training per-scene incurs 20 min overhead.
* **High-frequency lighting** – SG limited to representing all-frequency illumination with many lobes; explicit wavelet or spherical needlet may scale better to crisp area lights and produce ringing elsewhere.
* **Computational memory** – Maintaining high-res Hash grid + MLP triad for geometry/material + SG mixture + UNet consumes 14 GB VRAM at 1080p fine stage, restrictive for mobile preview.
* **SDF topologically** – Thin structures may collapse under Eikonal regularization; neural implicit evolution (NIE) could improve but unintegrated.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a NeRF-accelerated inverse rendering framework dissecting entangled radiance into **explicit microfacet BRDF** (GGX + Smith), **Spherical Gaussian lighting mixtures** (isotropic + anisotropic variants), and **Monte Carlo denoised volumetric integration**. By sampling fine rays proportionally to transmittance-driven weight distribution we achieve 7× speedup compatible with any neural field backbone, while analytic SG product integrals disambiguate material-light transport without expensive hemisphere Monte Carlo during training. A lightweight image-space kernel denoiser conditioned on g-buffer data recovers high-frequency detail from 2-8 spp noise, bridging speed-quality trade-off essential for practical inverse rendering.

The synthesis touches broader trends: movement from implicit baking (NeRF) to explicit physical decomposition (PhySG, TensoIR, GlossGau), need for rotationally-aware bases (ASG), and rise of differentiable denoising as *variance reduction* not mere post-process. Future work includes incorporating **neural plenoptic transport** for near-field parallax, real-time ray-traced visibility via surfel Gaussians, and multi-layer OpenPBR [8] thin-film interference for iridescent materials.

From implementation perspective, author recommends **always** validate decomposition not only by novel view PSNR (which NeRF overfits) but by *relighting metric under held-out illumination* and *albedo invariance across view*.
