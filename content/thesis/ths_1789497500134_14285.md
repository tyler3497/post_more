# The Cobordism Hypothesis: Fully Dualizable Objects and the Classification of Extended Topological Field Theories

## Abstract

The cobordism hypothesis, conjectured by John Baez and James Dolan in 1995 and reformulated in the language of higher categories by Jacob Lurie in 2009, is the deepest known organizing principle of topological quantum field theory. It asserts that a framed extended n-dimensional topological field theory valued in a symmetric monoidal (infinity,n)-category is uniquely determined, up to a contractible space of choices, by its value on a single point, provided that value is a fully dualizable object. This article develops the hypothesis from first principles: the Atiyah-Segal axioms and their extension to manifolds of all codimensions, the inductive definition of full dualizability through iterated adjunctions of k-morphisms, and the framed bordism higher category with its natural orthogonal group action. We survey Lurie's inductive proof architecture via the index filtration and obstruction theory, the complete two-dimensional classification proved by Schommer-Pries using Cerf theory, and the geometric cobordism hypothesis of Grady and Pavlov, which extends the classification to functorial field theories carrying arbitrary geometric structure such as Riemannian metrics.

## 1 Intro

Quantum field theory resists axiomatization. The path integral, for all its computational power, is not a definition, and the *Wightman axioms* and their relatives capture only fragments of what physicists mean by a field theory. Against this background, *topological* quantum field theory (TQFT) stands out as a rare success: because the theory is insensitive to the metric, the entire structure can be encoded in the functorial response of the theory to cutting and gluing manifolds. Michael Atiyah's 1988 axioms made this precise: a TQFT is a symmetric monoidal functor from a category of cobordisms to the category of vector spaces [6].

The Atiyah axioms are beautifully simple, but they are also incomplete. A physicist's TQFT does not merely assign numbers to closed manifolds and linear maps to cobordisms; it assigns *data* to manifolds of every dimension down to a point — Hilbert spaces to surfaces, categories to circles, and higher categorical structures below that. Extending the functorial picture all the way down is the program of *extended* TQFT, and it raises a sharp question: how much of the extended theory is forced by the value it takes on a point?

The *cobordism hypothesis* answers this question with maximal drama. Conjectured by John Baez and James Dolan in 1995 [1], it states that an extended framed n-dimensional TQFT is *entirely determined* by its value on a point, which must be a *fully dualizable object* of the target higher category. It is, in Baez and Dolan's phrase, the analogue for field theories of the Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms for homology: just as a homology theory is pinned down by its value on a point, so too is an extended TQFT pinned down by its value on a point — provided one works with the correct notion of "point" and the correct notion of "value" [1][2].

The sections below develop the hypothesis from the classical axioms to the geometric generalization of Grady and Pavlov.

## 2 Background

### The Atiyah-Segal axioms

An *n-dimensional TQFT* in the Atiyah-Segal sense is a symmetric monoidal functor

    Z: Cob_n -> Vect

where `Cob_n` is the category whose objects are closed oriented (n-1)-manifolds and whose morphisms are oriented cobordisms, composed by gluing, and `Vect` is the category of vector spaces with the tensor product [6]. The axioms encode the physicist's expectations:

1. **Functoriality.** Diffeomorphic cobordisms give the same linear map; gluing corresponds to composition.
2. **Monoidality.** Disjoint union goes to tensor product; the empty manifold goes to the ground field.
3. **Involutivity.** Orientation reversal goes to dual vector spaces.

Already this austere definition has teeth. The cylinder over a manifold is the identity morphism, so Z of a cylinder is the identity map. The "pair of pants" cobordism in dimension two endows Z of the circle with the structure of a *commutative Frobenius algebra*, and indeed 2-dimensional TQFTs are classified by commutative Frobenius algebras — a theorem that foreshadows everything to come [5].

### Why extend?

The classical axioms stop at codimension one: they say nothing about what the theory assigns to a manifold with boundary, let alone a point. But the locality that makes TQFT computable — the ability to cut a manifold into pieces, compute on each piece, and glue — demands data at every codimension.

An *extended* n-dimensional TQFT assigns data to manifolds of all dimensions from n down to 0. The target can no longer be an ordinary category; it must be an *n-category* (or, in modern language, an *(infinity,n)-category*), with k-morphisms for each codimension k. Baez and Dolan proposed that the correct domain is likewise higher-categorical: the *n-category of cobordisms*, whose objects are points, 1-morphisms are 1-dimensional cobordisms between points, 2-morphisms are 2-dimensional cobordisms between 1-cobordisms, and so on [1].

> **Theorem:** (2D classification, classical). There is an equivalence of categories between 2-dimensional TQFTs valued in vector spaces over a field and commutative Frobenius algebras over that field. The circle maps to the underlying vector space; the pair of pants gives the multiplication; the disc gives the unit and counit [5].

This classical result is the n = 2, unextended shadow of the cobordism hypothesis, and it already exhibits the pattern: the theory is determined by algebraic data attached to the lowest-dimensional manifold, subject to a duality constraint (the Frobenius condition is precisely a duality, or "finite-dimensionality with pairing," condition).

### Higher categories enter

Making "n-category of cobordisms" precise took over a decade. The modern formulation, due to Lurie following Hopkins, uses *complete Segal spaces* to model (infinity,n)-categories: an (infinity,n)-category is a simplicial object in (infinity,n-1)-categories satisfying Segal and completeness conditions [2]. The bordism (infinity,n)-category `Bord_n` is then built as a Segal space whose k-morphisms are k-dimensional bordisms with corners, equipped with tangential structure [2].

## 3 Methodology

The three ingredients of the precise statement are the target higher category, the inductive definition of full dualizability, and the framed bordism category.

### Symmetric monoidal (infinity,n)-categories

A *symmetric monoidal (infinity,n)-category* C is an (infinity,n)-category equipped with a coherently commutative tensor product. The examples that matter are:

| Target C | Objects | Fully dualizable objects |
|---|---|---|
| Vect (n=1) | vector spaces | finite-dimensional vector spaces |
| Alg (n=2) | algebras | separable algebras |
| Cat (n=2) | categories | dualizable categories |
| Spectra (n=infinity) | spectra | dualizable spectra |

In each case, "fully dualizable" picks out a finiteness condition [2][5].

### Full dualizability, inductively

An object X of a symmetric monoidal (infinity,n)-category is *dualizable* if there exist an object X-dual, an evaluation map ev from X tensor X-dual to the unit, and a coevaluation map coev from the unit to X-dual tensor X satisfying the zigzag (triangle) identities. In compact notation:

    (ev tensor id) composed with (id tensor coev) is homotopic to id_X
    (id tensor ev) composed with (coev tensor id) is homotopic to id of X-dual

*Full* dualizability iterates this: X is fully dualizable if it is dualizable, the evaluation and coevaluation 1-morphisms themselves admit left and right adjoints, those adjunction units and counits admit adjoints, and so on, n levels deep. Precisely, every k-morphism for k from 1 to n-1 must be *adjointable* — admit both a left and a right adjoint [2].

This inductive definition is the technical heart of the hypothesis: in the Morita 2-category of algebras, bimodules, and intertwiners, the fully dualizable objects are exactly the *finite-dimensional separable* algebras — and these classify 2-dimensional extended TQFTs [5].

A compact way to remember the definition, in Haskell-flavored pseudocode for a strict 2-categorical fragment, is:

```haskell
-- Dualizability in a monoidal bicategory (strictified sketch)
class Monoidal bicat => HasDuals bicat where
  type Dual bicat :: * -> *
  eval   :: obj -> OneCell bicat (Tensor obj (Dual obj)) Unit
  coeval :: obj -> OneCell bicat Unit (Tensor (Dual obj) obj)
  -- zigzag identities, witnessed by invertible 2-cells:
  zigzagL :: obj -> TwoCell bicat
    ((eval obj `hcomp` id) `vcomp` (id `hcomp` coeval obj)) (idCell obj)
  zigzagR :: obj -> TwoCell bicat
    ((id `hcomp` eval obj) `vcomp` (coeval obj `hcomp` id)) (idCell (Dual obj))

-- Full dualizability: duals, plus adjoints of eval/coeval,
-- plus adjoints of the units/counits, iterated n levels.
```

### The framed bordism (infinity,n)-category

The domain of an extended TQFT is the *framed bordism (infinity,n)-category* `Bord_n^fr`. Its objects are framed 0-manifolds (finite collections of framed points), its 1-morphisms are framed 1-bordisms, and so on up to n-morphisms, which are n-dimensional framed bordisms with corners. "Framed" means equipped with a trivialization of the stable tangent bundle — the minimal tangential structure, from which all others (orientation, spin) are obtained by homotopy fixed points [2][4].

The cobordism hypothesis then states:

> **Theorem:** (Cobordism hypothesis, Baez-Dolan-Lurie). Let C be a symmetric monoidal (infinity,n)-category in which every object is fully dualizable in the truncated sense and every k-morphism is adjointable for k from 1 to n-1. Then evaluation at the positively framed point induces an equivalence of (infinity,n)-categories between symmetric monoidal functors `Bord_n^fr -> C` and the (infinity,n)-groupoid of fully dualizable objects of C [1][2].

In particular, on the level of equivalence classes: framed extended n-dimensional TQFTs valued in C are in bijection with fully dualizable objects of C. The field theory is *determined by its value on a point*.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Duals, adjoints, and the meaning of "finite"

Dualizability is the higher-categorical shadow of finiteness, and it is worth understanding why. In ordinary linear algebra, a vector space V is dualizable (as an object of Vect) if and only if it is finite-dimensional: the evaluation map from V tensor V-dual to the ground field and the coevaluation from the field to V-dual tensor V exist for all V, but the zigzag identities hold only when V is finite-dimensional. The coevaluation secretly sums over a basis — an infinite sum diverges, and the triangle identities fail [2].

One level up, in the Morita bicategory of algebras and bimodules, an algebra is dualizable precisely when it is finite-dimensional and *separable*; adjointability of the evaluation and coevaluation 1-morphisms then forces the Frobenius structure in the oriented case [5].

A concrete computational sanity check in dimension one — where a TQFT is determined by a finite-dimensional vector space, i.e., a dualizable object of Vect — is instructive:

```python
import numpy as np

def zigzag_is_identity(dim: int) -> bool:
    # In Vect, coeval(1) = sum over basis of e-dual tensor e, and
    # the zigzag composite on V is the identity iff the sum is finite.
    basis = np.eye(dim)
    def zigzag(v):
        # sum_i eval(v tensor e-dual_i) * e_i  -- finite sum, exact
        return sum(float(np.dot(v, basis[i])) * basis[i] for i in range(dim))
    v = np.random.randn(dim)
    return bool(np.allclose(zigzag(v), v))

for d in (1, 2, 5, 64):
    assert zigzag_is_identity(d)
print("finite-dimensional vector spaces satisfy the zigzag identities")

# Dictionary for the 1D framed bordism category:
# Z(point+) = V, Z(point-) = V-dual, Z(interval) = identity,
# Z(elbow) = eval/coeval. Dualizability of V is exactly the data
# needed to define Z on every 1-bordism.
```

In Rust-flavored pseudocode, the adjunction data one level up looks like this:

```rust
// Adjoint 1-morphisms in a bicategory (strictified sketch)
trait AdjointPair {
    type Left: Clone;
    type Right: Clone;
    // unit: Id => R after L, counit: L after R => Id
    fn unit() -> TwoCell<Self::Right, Self::Left>;
    fn counit() -> TwoCell<Self::Left, Self::Right>;
    // triangle identities as asserted laws:
    // (counit * id) after (id * unit) == id_Left
    // (id * counit) after (unit * id) == id_Right
}

// Full dualizability demands AdjointPair instances for eval/coeval,
// then adjoints of unit/counit themselves, iterated n levels deep.
```

### 4.2 The O(n)-action and structured bordisms

The framed bordism category carries a natural action of the orthogonal group O(n): changing the framing of a bordism by a rotation gives a new framed bordism, and this assembles into a homotopically coherent O(n)-action on `Bord_n^fr` [2][4]. By functoriality, the groupoid of framed extended TQFTs inherits an O(n)-action, and hence so does the groupoid of fully dualizable objects.

This is not a curiosity — it is the bridge from framed to *structured* field theories. A tangential structure is a map of spaces B -> BO(n); the bordism category with that structure, `Bord_n^B`, is the homotopy quotient of the framed version. The cobordism hypothesis then implies that B-structured extended TQFTs correspond to *homotopy fixed points* of the O(n)-action on fully dualizable objects [2]. Special cases:

- **Oriented** TQFTs: SO(n) homotopy fixed points. In dimension 2, this extra fixed-point data is exactly the *Frobenius trace* — the nondegenerate pairing that promotes a separable algebra to a symmetric Frobenius algebra [5].
- **Spin** TQFTs: fixed points for the spin cover; these classify fermionic phases.
- **Unoriented** TQFTs: full O(n) fixed points.

### 4.3 Lurie's inductive proof architecture

Lurie's 2009 sketch proves the hypothesis by induction on n, and its architecture is worth studying even though full details have not appeared [2]. The strategy:

1. **Unfolding.** An (infinity,n)-category can be "unfolded" into a tower of (infinity,1)-categories; a symmetric monoidal functor out of `Bord_n` unfolds into compatible families of functors at each level.
2. **The index filtration.** Bordisms are filtered by the *index* of their critical points (in a Morse-theoretic presentation). The bordism category is built by attaching "cells" — generating bordisms — in order of increasing index, much as a CW complex is built by attaching cells by dimension.
3. **Obstruction theory.** At each stage of the filtration, extending a partially defined field theory over a new cell is obstructed by a class that vanishes precisely when the relevant dualizability or adjointability condition holds. The fully dualizable object provides exactly the data needed to kill every obstruction: the point's value gives the 0-cells, its dual gives the 1-cells (cups and caps), the adjoints give the 2-cells, and so on.

> **Proof:** (Sketch of the inductive step). Assume the hypothesis in dimension n-1. The n-dimensional extension amounts to specifying Z on n-bordisms with corners; Morse theory presents these as composites of index-k handles, and the obstruction to defining Z on a handle of index k lies in a mapping space contractible precisely because the corresponding k-morphism in C is adjointable. Full dualizability is exactly the hypothesis that all these mapping spaces are contractible. QED (sketch) [2].

The analytic core that makes the filtration work — the contractibility of the space of framed functions with controlled critical points — was supplied by Eliashberg and Mishachev, superseding the corresponding section of Lurie's sketch [4].

### 4.4 The geometric cobordism hypothesis

The classical cobordism hypothesis is *topological*: it classifies field theories that depend only on the underlying smooth manifold up to diffeomorphism. But most field theories of physical interest — Yang-Mills, sigma models, general relativity — depend on *geometric* data: Riemannian metrics, connections, or higher structures. Grady and Pavlov's *geometric cobordism hypothesis* (2021-2023) extends the classification to this setting [3][4].

The idea: replace the bordism category with a *smooth* (infinity,n)-category `Bord_n^S` of bordisms equipped with a geometric structure S (a sheaf on the site of manifolds, e.g., Riemannian metrics or geometric string structures). A *functorial field theory* with structure S is a smooth symmetric monoidal functor out of this category. The main theorems:

1. **Locality.** Every extended functorial field theory — topological or not — is *local*: the assignment sending S to the field theories with structure S satisfies descent (codescent on the bordism side) with respect to the target geometry. This proves a conjecture of Freed and shows that locality, usually assumed, is actually a theorem [3].
2. **Geometric classification.** In the topological case, the classification reduces to the first *complete published proof* of the topological cobordism hypothesis, closing the gap left by Lurie's sketch. In the geometric case, smooth invertible field theories are classified by the *smooth Madsen-Tillmann spectrum*, and concordance classes of extended theories admit a classifying space — resolving the Stolz-Teichner conjecture [3][4].

| Framework | Bordisms carry | Classified by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baez-Dolan (1995) | framings (heuristic n-cats) | fully dualizable object | conjecture [1] |
| Lurie (2009) | framings, (infinity,n)-cats | fully dualizable object | proof sketch [2] |
| Schommer-Pries (2011) | framings/orientations, n=2 | separable symmetric Frobenius algebras | complete proof [5] |
| Grady-Pavlov (2021-23) | arbitrary geometric structure S | smooth refinement; classifying space | complete proof [3][4] |

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

What is actually *proved*, versus sketched or conjectured? The ledger:

1. **Dimension 1.** A 1-dimensional framed extended TQFT valued in Vect is determined by a finite-dimensional vector space — the fully dualizable objects of Vect. The smooth refinement of Berwick-Evans and Pavlov shows smooth 1-dimensional theories are vector bundles with connection, a geometric instance of the general picture [4].
2. **Dimension 2, bicategorical target.** Schommer-Pries proved the 2-dimensional cobordism hypothesis for target bicategories (2-truncated (infinity,2)-categories) with trivial or oriented tangential structure, using Cerf theory to give an explicit generators-and-relations presentation of the bordism bicategory. The classification: framed 2D extended TQFTs correspond to separable algebras; oriented ones to *separable symmetric Frobenius algebras* [5]. This is the strongest complete result in the literature for n at least 2.
3. **Lurie's sketch (n general).** The inductive argument via the index filtration is widely accepted by experts but, as the literature notes, a formal account of the core sections has not yet appeared [2][4]. It is a proof sketch, not a proof.
4. **Grady-Pavlov (geometric, n general).** The locality theorem and the geometric cobordism hypothesis are proved in full detail, giving the first complete proof of the topological cobordism hypothesis as a special case, plus the classifying-space theorem for concordance classes [3][4].

*Computational evidence.* The low-dimensional instances are computationally effective: given a separable symmetric Frobenius algebra (say, a matrix algebra with the trace pairing), one can algorithmically compute the associated 2D TQFT's invariants of surfaces — the partition function of a genus-g surface is the trace of a power of the handle operator. State-sum models (Turaev-Viro, Crane-Yetter) are the 3- and 4-dimensional analogues: they take categorical "point data" and produce manifold invariants by a local state sum, which is the computational content of "the theory is determined by its value on a point" [5].

## 6 Limitations

The cobordism hypothesis, for all its grandeur, has sharp boundaries:

- **Framed versus physical.** The hypothesis classifies *framed* theories; physical theories usually carry orientation or spin structure, and passing through homotopy fixed points is computationally difficult. The O(n)-action whose fixed points encode this passage is poorly understood in general [2].
- **Target restrictions.** Full dualizability is extremely restrictive: in many target higher categories of interest, the fully dualizable objects form a small and sometimes trivial class. The hypothesis says nothing about non-dualizable, non-local theories — which is most of quantum field theory.
- **Non-topological theories.** The original hypothesis applies only to topological theories; the geometric cobordism hypothesis [3][4] addresses this via smooth higher categories whose foundations are recent and technically demanding.
- **Proof status.** Lurie's argument remains a sketch; the community relies on Grady-Pavlov for a complete account, and their proof uses different machinery (smooth stacks, codescent) whose relationship to Lurie's index filtration is not fully mapped [2][3].
- **Unitarity and reflection positivity.** The classification is purely topological and algebraic; it does not incorporate unitarity, reflection positivity, or the analytic structures that distinguish physical TQFTs (for example, those arising from Chern-Simons theory at level k) from formal ones.

## 7 Conclusion

The cobordism hypothesis is the Eilenberg-Steenrod theorem of quantum field theory: it reduces the infinite complexity of an extended TQFT — data on manifolds of every dimension, compatibilities under every gluing — to a single algebraic datum, a fully dualizable object, subject to a finiteness condition iterated n levels deep. From Baez and Dolan's visionary 1995 proposal [1], through Lurie's higher-categorical reformulation and inductive proof sketch [2], Schommer-Pries's complete two-dimensional classification [5], to Grady and Pavlov's geometric generalization and locality theorem [3][4], the hypothesis has grown from conjecture to the organizing center of functorial field theory.

Its deepest lesson is a principle: *finiteness implies locality*. A field theory whose point-value is finite in the correct higher-categorical sense cannot help but be determined by that value everywhere. Whether this principle extends to the non-topological, non-dualizable theories that dominate physics remains the great open frontier [4].
