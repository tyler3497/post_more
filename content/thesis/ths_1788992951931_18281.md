## Abstract Reasoning about fine-grained shared-memory concurrency demands separating what a thread *owns* from what it merely *shares* under rampant interference. Separation logic, introduced by Reynolds and extended to concurrency by O'Hearn, supplies this ownership discipline. **Iris** is a higher-order concurrent separation logic framework, mechanized in Coq, that distills decades of concurrency-logic research into an orthogonal core: step-indexed resource algebras ("cameras") for user-defined *ghost state*, and *invariants* tying ghost state to physical state. ## 1. Introduction Concurrent programs are everywhere and notoriously hard to get right. Even the simplest operations on a shared counter — `x := x + 1` — compile to a load, an increment, and a store, and two threads executing this sequence can interfere: one thread's load is stale by the time it stores, and increments vanish. Scale this pattern to a lock-free stack or a scalable memory allocator, and the space of interleavings explodes combinatorially while the *observable* behavior should remain simple: the stack should still behave like a stack, with each operation appearing to take effect at a single atomic instant. This property, **linearizability**, is the gold standard for concurrent object correctness, yet proving it for fine-grained implementations was for decades a heroic, non-compositional endeavor. The central intellectual move that changed the picture is *separation*. Classical Hoare logic reasons about whole program states: a triple $\{P\}\,c\,\{Q\}$ says that if the entire state satisfies $P$, executing $c$ yields a state satisfying $Q$. Concurrency breaks this picture, because "the entire state" is perpetually being mutated by other threads. Separation logic replaces the global-state view with an *ownership* view: a program fragment owns precisely the resources it touches, and the **frame rule** > **Theorem:** If $\{P\}\,c\,\{Q\}$ then $\{P * R\}\,c\,\{Q * R\}$ for any frame $R$ disjoint from $c$'s footprint. says that reasoning about $c$ is valid in any larger context that does not touch $c$'s resources. ## 2. Background

### 2.1 From Hoare logic to separation

Floyd–Hoare logic assigns meaning to programs via triples $\{P\}\,c\,\{Q\}$. Its power is compositional reasoning about sequential control flow, but it is state-global: specifications describe whole machine states. Reynolds's **separation logic** (2002) [1] made the heap a first-class, *partially* described entity. The key innovations were:

- The **points-to assertion** $\ell \mapsto v$: the heap consists of *exactly one* cell $\ell$ holding value $v$.
- The **separating conjunction** $P * Q$: the heap splits into disjoint sub-heaps satisfying $P$ and $Q$.
- The **frame rule**, enabling local reasoning: specifications mention only the footprint of a command.

*Example.* Consider in-place list reversal. The specification

$$\{\mathsf{list}(l, xs)\}\ \mathtt{reverse}(l)\ \{\mathsf{list}(l, \mathrm{reverse}(xs))\}$$

mentions only the list segment; any other heap cells are framed away untouched. This modularity — specifications that scale with the code rather than with the program — was the revolution.

### 2.2 Concurrent separation logic and its discontents

O'Hearn's CSL [2] extended the frame rule to parallel composition:

$$\frac{\{P_1\}\,c_1\,\{Q_1\} \qquad \{P_2\}\,c_2\,\{Q_2\}}{\{P_1 * P_2\}\ c_1 \parallel c_2\ \{Q_1 * Q_2\}}$$

Ownership transfers between threads at fork/join, while *shared* resources are protected by invariants. CSL's **disjoint concurrency rule** handled fork-join parallelism and lock-based programs with grace. But three developments exposed its limits:

1. **Fine-grained algorithms.** Treiber's lock-free stack, the Michael–Scott queue, and hazard-pointer reclamation synchronize with CAS loops, not locks. Their atomicity points are *logical*: a push linearizes at its successful CAS, but a concurrent pop may "help" complete it, and helping means the linearization point lies *outside* the operation's own code.
2. **Logics proliferate.** Rely–guarantee (Vafeiadis), deny–guarantee (Dodds et al.), TaDA (da Rocha Pinto et al.), FCSL (Nanevski et al.), iCAP (Svendsen–Birkedal), CaReSL — each added bespoke primitives: tokens, regions, subjective state, atomicity contexts. Each new idiom demanded a new logic with a new soundness proof.
3. **Higher-order state.** Verifying programs with higher-order functions and stored code requires *impredicative* invariants — invariants that can mention other invariants or Hoare triples themselves — which classical step-indexing handled only awkwardly.

> **Observation:** The history of concurrency logics is a history of ever-more-expressive primitives, each requiring a new metatheory. Iris inverts the trend: fix a minimal, orthogonal core and *derive* the primitives.

The POPL 2015 paper "Iris: Monoids and Invariants as an Orthogonal Basis for Concurrent Reasoning" [3] announced this program: partial commutative monoids express protocols on shared state; invariants enforce them. The JFP 2018 article "Iris from the ground up" [4] is the definitive, self-contained account of Iris 3.1, and our primary reference.

## 3. Methodology

Our methodology is twofold: *conceptual* and *mechanized*.

**Conceptual.** We reconstruct Iris's core from first principles — step-indexed models of resources, the base logic, the derived mechanisms — following the "from the ground up" narrative, and we illustrate the payoff with a linearizability proof for the Treiber stack. Where the original papers give full Coq developments, we give the *mathematical* content in prose, with the crucial definitions stated precisely and the crucial lemmas as theorem blocks. The goal is that a reader who understands the model could, with the Iris Coq library, reproduce the case study.

**Mechanized.** Iris is not a paper logic; it is a Coq framework. Every definition we describe has a Coq counterpart: cameras are Coq typeclasses (`Camera`), the base logic's entailment is a Coq Prop, and proof rules are Coq lemmas proved sound against the model. The **adequacy theorem** connects the logic to operational reality: if $\vdash \{\mathsf{True}\}\,e\,\{v.\, \phi(v)\}$ is provable, then every execution of $e$ is safe and every terminating execution returns $v$ with $\phi(v)$. We rely on this theorem as the methodological bridge between derived proof rules and machine-checked guarantees.

**Scope of verification.** Our case study targets a classic fine-grained algorithm under sequential consistency. We treat weak-memory extensions (the GPS/iGPS logics built on Iris) as related work, and we are explicit in §6 about what Iris does *not* buy: termination, liveness, and real-time bounds require separate machinery.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cameras: a generic algebra of resources

The single most important definition in Iris is the **camera** — a *step-indexed, ordered, unital resource algebra*. Conceptually it generalizes the partial commutative monoid (PCM) that underlies ordinary separation logic's heap model, in three ways:

1. **Step-indexing** — to support impredicative, higher-order ghost state (ghost state that stores propositions about ghost state).
2. **Validity and core** — a validity predicate $\checkmark$ distinguishing consistent from inconsistent resource compositions, and a *core* operation $|x|$ extracting the duplicable ("persistent") part of a resource.
3. **Composition** — cameras compose by product, sum, and function space, so complex protocols are assembled from simple algebraic pieces rather than hand-rolled.

Formally, a camera is a tuple $(M, \mathcal{V}, |{-}|, \cdot, \varepsilon, {\preceq}, {\equiv_n})$ where $\cdot$ is a partial composition, $\mathcal{V}$ a step-indexed validity predicate, and ${\equiv_n}$ an *ordered family of equivalences* (OFE): $x \equiv_n y$ means "$x$ and $y$ agree up to $n$ steps of computation." Step-indexing solves the classic circularity of higher-order store — worlds that contain assertions about worlds — by stratifying agreement: to unfold a definition at step $n$, you need only agreement at step $n{-}1$. The later modality $\triangleright P$ internalizes this: $\triangleright P$ holds of resources valid for one *fewer* step, and Löb induction,

> **Theorem (Löb):** $(\triangleright P \Rightarrow P) \Rightarrow P$,

licenses guarded recursive definitions — recursive predicates, recursive invariants, recursive ghost-state protocols — that would be viciously circular without step-indexing.

| Camera | Carrier | Composition | Intuition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `excl` (exclusive) | $\{\mathsf{Excl}(x), \bot\}$ | $\mathsf{Excl}(x)\cdot\mathsf{Excl}(y)$ invalid | Single-owner token |
| `frac` (fractional) | $q \in (0,1] \cap \mathbb{Q}$ | $q_1 + q_2 \le 1$ | Splittable read permission |
| `auth` (authoritative) | $\bullet x$ (authoritative), $\circ x$ (fragment) | Fragments must agree with authority | Shared knowledge with a source of truth |
| `agree` | $\mathsf{Agree}(x)$ | Valid iff all agree | Immutable shared facts |
| `gmap` | Finite maps $K \rightharpoonup M$ | Pointwise composition | Per-location ghost state |
| `option` / `sum` / `prod` | Lifts of $M$ | Lifted pointwise | Combinators |

The **authoritative camera** `auth` deserves emphasis: $\bullet x$ is the authoritative element recording the "true" value, while fragments $\circ y$ are consistent only if $y \preceq x$. This single construction yields fractional permissions, monotonically evolving state, and agreement — mechanisms that were primitive in earlier logics. ### 4.2 The Iris base logic and its modalities

Remarkably, the entire edifice rests on a *small* base logic: higher-order separation logic over an arbitrary camera, with three modalities doing the heavy lifting:

- **$\triangleright P$ (later):** step-indexed guard, enabling Löb induction and guarded recursion.
- **$\Box P$ (persistence):** $P$ holds without owning exclusive resources — the "always" modality; $\Box P \vdash P * \Box P$.
- **${\Rightarrow\!\!\!\equiv}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2} P$ (fancy update):** from resources in namespaces $\mathcal{E}_1$ one may reach a state satisfying $P$ while opening invariants in $\mathcal{E}_1$ (mask discipline prevents re-entrant invariant opening).

From these, everything classical is *derived*: Hoare triples via weakest preconditions ($\mathsf{wp}\ e\ \{\Phi\}$), invariants $P^{\mathcal{N}}$ (duplicable knowledge that $P$ holds of shared state, opened only around atomic steps), and the full CSL rule set. The ESOP 2017 paper "The Essence of Higher-Order Concurrent Separation Logic" [5] showed that the base logic plus these definitions suffices — the fancy machinery is not assumed but *proved*.

### 4.3 Ghost state patterns: protocols as algebra

With cameras and the base logic in place, concurrency reasoning becomes *protocol design*. Three patterns recur:

1. **State-transition systems (STS).** Model an object's abstract state as a camera whose elements are protocol states with a transition relation; the invariant ties the physical representation to the current abstract state. A lock's protocol is `unlocked ⇄ locked`; a stack's is richer.
2. **Fractional permissions.** Read-sharing of a heap cell: $\ell \xmapsto{q} v$ with $q \in (0,1]$; full $q=1$ permits writing, any $q>0$ permits reading. Fractions compose by addition, so permissions split across readers and rejoin for the writer.
3. **Prophecy variables.** For linearizability with *future-dependent* linearization points — where the instant an operation "takes effect" depends on actions of *other* threads that have not happened yet — Iris uses **prophecy variables** [8]: ghost variables whose value is *prophesied* before it is determined, with the proof later resolving the prophecy consistently. "The future is ours" (POPL 2020) gave them a sound separation-logic account, unlocking proofs of algorithms (e.g., the Harris–Michael linked list, RDCSS) that defeated all prior logics.

> **Theorem (Adequacy, informal):** If $\vdash \{\mathsf{True}\}\,e\,\{v.\,\phi(v)\}$ in Iris, then $e$ never gets stuck and every value it returns satisfies $\phi$.

Adequacy is proved once, against the operational semantics, and inherited by every logic instantiated on the framework — the methodological payoff of the framework design.

### 4.4 Case study: linearizability of the Treiber stack

We now sketch the signature Iris-style proof: the **Treiber stack**, a lock-free LIFO where `push` allocates a node and CAS-swings the head pointer, and `pop` reads the head and CAS-swings to its successor, retrying on failure.

*Step 1 — abstract state.* Define the logical contents as a list $xs$ tracked by an authoritative camera: the invariant owns $\bullet xs$ (authoritative) and hands each thread fragments.

*Step 2 — invariant.* The shared invariant ties physical to logical:

$$I \;\triangleq\; \exists xs.\; \mathsf{head} \mapsto \mathsf{nodes}(xs) * \bullet xs$$

where $\mathsf{nodes}(xs)$ is the linked list in the heap representing $xs$.

*Step 3 — atomic triples and linearization.* Each operation is specified by a *logically atomic* triple $\langle xs.\, P \rangle\, e\, \langle v.\, Q \rangle$: the operation appears to take effect at a single instant even though its implementation interleaves. For `push(v)`:

$$\langle xs.\, I \rangle\ \mathtt{push}(v)\ \langle \_.\, I[xs := v{::}xs] \rangle$$

The proof opens the invariant around the successful CAS — the *linearization point* — updates the authoritative list ghost state, and closes the invariant. Failed CAS iterations simply retry without touching logical state.

*Step 4 — helping and prophecies.* In algorithms where one thread's linearization point lies inside another thread's execution (elimination, helping), prophecy variables resolve the future-dependence: the proof prophesies *which* thread will perform the decisive step and *what* value it will install, then discharges the prophecy when the step occurs. The soundness argument — that no proof can "cheat" by prophesying inconsistently — rests on the step-indexed model: prophecies are resolved against the actual execution trace in the adequacy proof.

The punchline is methodological: the *same* two mechanisms (ghost-state cameras, invariants) handle locks, lock-free structures, helping, and prophecy. What changed between CSL and Iris is not the set of examples but the *cost of adding a new mechanism*: in Iris it is a derived lemma, not a new logic.

## 5. Empirical Results / Proofs

Iris's claims are substantiated by a large, public, machine-checked corpus:

- **RustBelt** (POPL 2018): the first formal safety proof for Rust's type system, mechanized in Iris/Coq, covering `Cell`, `RefCell`, `Mutex`, channels, and the `rayon`/`crossbeam` idioms — roughly 30k lines of Coq establishing that safe Rust cannot cause data races even with `unsafe` internals.
- **VST and CompCert integration**: an Iris instance for CompCert C reproduces the Verified Software Toolchain's program logic, showing Iris subsumes mature sequential verification.
- **ReLoC** (LICS 2018): a relational logic for fine-grained concurrency built *on top of* Iris, used to prove contextual refinements — e.g., that a fine-grained counter refines an atomic one.
- **Prophecy case studies**: the POPL 2020 prophecy paper verified the RDCSS and Harris–Michael set algorithms whose proofs were previously out of reach.
- **Recognition**: the 2023 **Alonzo Church Award** cited the four foundational Iris papers (POPL 2015; ICFP 2016; ESOP 2017; JFP 2018) for "outstanding contributions to logic and computation."

| Project | Target | Scale (approx.) | Iris mechanism exercised |
|---|---|---|---|
| RustBelt | Rust type soundness | ~30k lines Coq | Lifetimes as ghost state, invariants |
| VST-on-Iris | CompCert C logic | full VST rule set re-derived | Sequential adequacy, step-indexing |
| Leaf | Temporary sharing | ICFP 2023 | Guarded protocols, borrowing |

| ReLoC | Contextual refinement | LICS 2018 paper + Coq | Relational reasoning atop Iris |

The *shape* of the evidence matters as much as its volume: each project instantiates the same framework rather than extending the trusted base. Soundness is proved once per instantiation against the operational semantics; the framework's model is the shared, audited core.

## 6. Limitations

Honesty about limits is part of the method. Iris's limitations are real and active research topics:

1. **Proof effort.** Iris proofs are manual and expensive. RustBelt-scale verification demands expert labor; the logic gives you the *expressive power* to state the proof, not the automation to find it. Decision procedures for fragments (e.g., the Diaframe proof-search tool) are nascent.
2. **Semantic-model complexity.** The full model — step-indexed cameras, OFEs, the later modality, fancy updates with mask discipline — is genuinely hard to learn. A user who only applies derived rules need not master it, but anyone *extending* the framework must.
3. **Safety, not liveness or termination.** Iris proves *partial* correctness: no stuck states, no data races, functional correctness of terminating executions. Termination, lock-freedom (as a liveness property), fairness, and real-time bounds are out of scope; logics like LiLi or extensions with ordinals address these separately.
4. **Memory-model boundaries.** The core Iris development assumes sequential consistency. Weak-memory reasoning (C11-style) has been built atop Iris (GPS, iGPS, Cosmo), but each weak-memory instantiation re-does significant semantic work, and the integration with the fancy-update discipline is subtle.
5. **Trusted base.** Adequacy bottoms out in Coq's kernel and the operational semantics of the object language. Iris shrinks the *logic-specific* trusted base, but the Coq kernel, the language semantics, and the compiler remain trusted — verification is relative, not absolute.
6. **Ghost-state design is an art.** Choosing the right camera and protocol for a new algorithm is the creative step; the logic cannot tell you that an authoritative list, rather than a multiset, is the right abstraction for your stack. Badly chosen ghost state yields proofs that fight the logic.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Separation logic taught us to reason about programs through *ownership*; concurrent separation logic taught us to govern sharing through *invariants*; Iris teaches us that these two ideas, made fully generic through step-indexed resource algebras, are *enough*. The framework's enduring contribution is not any single verified artifact but the inversion of the research methodology: instead of inventing a new logic for each new concurrency idiom, we derive the idiom's reasoning principles inside one machine-checked core. The empirical record — RustBelt, VST, ReLoC, prophecy-based linearizability proofs, and the Alonzo Church Award — suggests the core is the right one.

The frontier is now *usability*: proof automation that matches the framework's expressiveness, liveness and weak-memory integration that preserves the framework's modularity, and pedagogical tools that make the camera/invariant discipline as routine as the frame rule once became. Parkinson asked for the right core logic of concurrency; Iris is the strongest candidate we have — and the work of making it the *everyday* logic has only begun.
