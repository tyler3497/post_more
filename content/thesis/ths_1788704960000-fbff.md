# Visual-Inertial SLAM with Factor Graphs: Preintegration on Manifolds, Loop Closure via Bag-of-Words Place Recognition, and Incremental Smoothing with iSAM2

## Abstract

Visual-inertial simultaneous localization and mapping fuses metric inertial sensing with bearing-only vision inside a single maximum-a-posteriori estimator expressed as a factor graph [1][2]. This thesis develops the three pillars of a modern visual-inertial SLAM system: (i) **on-manifold IMU preintegration on SO(3)** that compresses hundreds of high-rate inertial measurements between keyframes into one relative-motion factor whose bias dependence is linearized analytically, eliminating reintegration during optimization [1]; (ii) **appearance-based loop closure** built on a hierarchical bag-of-binary-words vocabulary with inverted and direct indices, followed by geometric verification and pose-graph correction that bounds long-term drift [4][2]; and (iii) **incremental smoothing with iSAM2**, where the Bayes tree turns nonlinear re-optimization into localized clique edits with fluid relinearization and incremental variable reordering [3]. The theory is grounded in the measurement models of MEMS inertial sensors, the geometry of the rotation manifold, and the algebra of sparse nonlinear least squares, and is evaluated through the EuRoC MAV benchmark protocol [7] and the historical lineage from VINS-Mono [5] to ORB-SLAM3 [2], which report centimeter-level trajectory accuracy in real time.

## 1 Introduction

Simultaneous localization and mapping with a camera and an inertial measurement unit is the minimum sensor suite for metric six-degree-of-freedom state estimation: the camera supplies rich bearing information while the IMU supplies metric scale, gravity direction, and high-bandwidth motion between frames [5]. Neither sensor suffices alone: a monocular camera cannot observe scale, and an IMU alone drifts cubically in position within seconds. *Tightly coupled* visual-inertial estimation fuses raw pixel observations and raw inertial readings in one joint nonlinear optimization, and has displaced loosely coupled and filter-based designs wherever accuracy matters [8].

The central difficulty is **rate asymmetry**. A MEMS IMU delivers measurements at 100–1000 Hz while keyframes are selected far more sparsely; inserting a state node per IMU sample would make real-time optimization impossible, so the IMU is marginalized analytically between keyframes — *preintegration* [1]. A second difficulty is **drift**: only loop closure — recognizing a previously visited place — can bound it, and appearance-based place recognition with bags of binary words achieves this at millisecond latency [4]. A third is **incremental inference**: re-solving the full nonlinear least-squares problem at every keyframe is infeasible as the trajectory grows, which motivates the Bayes-tree machinery of iSAM2 [3].

This thesis unifies these three threads. We derive the on-manifold preintegration theory of Forster et al. [1] with first-order bias Jacobians, construct the visual-inertial factor graph with reprojection and inertial factors, develop the DBoW2 vocabulary-tree pipeline for loop detection and geometric verification [4], and present the iSAM2 algorithm that maintains the MAP estimate incrementally [3], closing with empirical evidence from the EuRoC datasets [7] and the failure modes that remain.

> **Thesis claim:** On-manifold IMU preintegration with analytic bias correction [1], loop closure via hierarchical binary-word place recognition with geometric verification [4][2], and incremental optimization over a Bayes tree [3] together achieve bounded-drift, centimeter-accurate, real-time visual-inertial SLAM — substantiated by ORB-SLAM3's 3.6 cm average EuRoC stereo-inertial accuracy [2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Rotations, manifolds, and the exponential map

Rotations live on the special orthogonal group **SO(3)**, a three-dimensional Lie group that is not a vector space. Optimization over rotations must therefore be performed *on the manifold*, using the exponential map $\mathrm{Exp}: \mathfrak{so}(3) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ and its inverse, the logarithmic map $\mathrm{Log}$ [1]. Perturbations are applied multiplicatively, $\mathbf{R} \leftarrow \mathbf{R}\,\mathrm{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol{\phi})$, so Gauss-Newton updates $\delta\boldsymbol{\phi} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ always land on the manifold, and covariance propagation through the exponential map uses the **right Jacobian** $\mathbf{J}_r(\boldsymbol{\phi})$ [1]. Euclidean linearization of rotations (e.g., Euler angles) introduces singularities and inconsistent Jacobians that destroy estimator consistency.

### 2.2 The inertial measurement model

A strapdown MEMS IMU measures angular velocity and specific force in the body frame:

$$\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t) = \boldsymbol{\omega}(t) + \mathbf{b}^g(t) + \boldsymbol{\eta}_g(t),$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf{a}}(t) = \mathbf{R}_{WB}^\top(t)\big(\mathbf{a}^W(t) - \mathbf{g}^W\big) + \mathbf{b}^a(t) + \boldsymbol{\eta}_a(t),$$

where $\mathbf{b}^g, \mathbf{b}^a$ are slowly varying biases modeled as random walks and $\boldsymbol{\eta}_g, \boldsymbol{\eta}_a$ are white Gaussian noise [1]. The accelerometer measures *specific force* — acceleration minus gravity — which is why gravity direction is observable from inertial data and why gravity must be handled explicitly in preintegration.

### 2.3 Factor graphs and MAP estimation

A factor graph is a bipartite graph $G = (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{F}, \mathcal{E})$ with variable nodes $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}$ and factor nodes $f_k \in \mathcal{F}$ encoding measurement likelihoods. Under Gaussian noise, MAP estimation reduces to nonlinear least squares [3]:

$$\mathcal{X}^\star = \arg\min_{\mathcal{X}} \sum_k \big\|\mathbf{r}_k(\mathcal{X}_k)\big\|^2_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k},$$

solved by iteratively linearizing around the current estimate and solving the normal equations. For SLAM the resulting information matrix is sparse, and its square-root factorization dominates cost — the structure iSAM2 exploits incrementally [3].

The keyframe state in visual-inertial estimation concatenates pose, velocity, and biases [1][5]:

$$\mathbf{x}_i = \big[\mathbf{R}_i,\; \mathbf{p}_i,\; \mathbf{v}_i,\; \mathbf{b}^g_i,\; \mathbf{b}^a_i\big] \in \mathrm{SO}(3) \times \mathbb{R}^{12}.$$

### 2.4 Observability

Visual-inertial odometry has **four unobservable degrees of freedom**: global position (3) and yaw about gravity (1). Scale and gravity direction are observable only if the motion excites the accelerometer — which is why ORB-SLAM3 performs MAP inertial-only optimization over a short window to initialize scale, gravity, biases, and velocities jointly [6]. Linearizing about yaw inconsistently is a classic source of filter inconsistency.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology assembles a complete visual-inertial SLAM pipeline: (1) on-manifold IMU preintegration producing one factor per keyframe interval with analytic bias Jacobians; (2) a factor graph of reprojection, preintegration, and prior factors solved as a MAP problem; (3) a DBoW2 place-recognition front end proposing loop candidates, verified geometrically and corrected through pose-graph optimization; and (4) iSAM2 incremental smoothing maintaining the solution in real time via the Bayes tree.

### 3.1 Preintegration: from hundreds of measurements to one factor

Between keyframes $i$ and $j$, the IMU produces $m$ measurements (often $m > 100$). Instead of adding $m$ states, we integrate the kinematics once, defining quantities *independent of the initial state and of gravity* [1]:

$$\Delta\mathbf{R}_{ij} \triangleq \prod_{k=i}^{j-1} \mathrm{Exp}\big((\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_k - \mathbf{b}^g_k - \boldsymbol{\eta}^g_k)\Delta t\big),$$
$$\Delta\mathbf{v}_{ij} \triangleq \sum_{k=i}^{j-1} \Delta\mathbf{R}_{ik}\,(\tilde{\mathbf{a}}_k - \mathbf{b}^a_k - \boldsymbol{\eta}^a_k)\,\Delta t,$$
$$\Delta\mathbf{p}_{ij} \triangleq \sum_{k=i}^{j-1} \Big[\Delta\mathbf{v}_{ik}\Delta t + \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\mathbf{R}_{ik}\,(\tilde{\mathbf{a}}_k - \mathbf{b}^a_k - \boldsymbol{\eta}^a_k)\,\Delta t^2\Big].$$

These are not physical increments but mathematical constructs: because gravity and the initial velocity/position have been factored out, the pseudo-measurements depend only on the IMU readings and the biases [1]. The residual compares predicted and measured preintegrated quantities, with bias dependence linearized about $\bar{\mathbf{b}}^g_i, \bar{\mathbf{b}}^a_i$ via Jacobians $\partial\Delta\bar{(\cdot)}_{ij}/\partial\mathbf{b}$ accumulated *during* the single preintegration pass [1]:

$$\mathbf{r}_{\Delta\mathbf{R}_{ij}} = \mathrm{Log}\!\Big(\big(\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{ij}(\bar{\mathbf{b}}^g_i)\,\mathrm{Exp}(\tfrac{\partial\Delta\bar{\mathbf{R}}_{ij}}{\partial\mathbf{b}^g}\delta\mathbf{b}^g)\big)^\top \mathbf{R}_i^\top \mathbf{R}_j\Big),$$
$$\mathbf{r}_{\Delta\mathbf{v}_{ij}} = \mathbf{R}_i^\top(\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{g}\Delta t_{ij}) - \big(\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{ij} + \tfrac{\partial\Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}}{\partial\mathbf{b}^g}\delta\mathbf{b}^g + \tfrac{\partial\Delta\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}}{\partial\mathbf{b}^a}\delta\mathbf{b}^a\big),$$
$$\mathbf{r}_{\Delta\mathbf{p}_{ij}} = \mathbf{R}_i^\top(\mathbf{p}_j - \mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{v}_i\Delta t_{ij} - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{g}\Delta t_{ij}^2) - \big(\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{ij} + \tfrac{\partial\Delta\bar{\mathbf{p}}_{ij}}{\partial\mathbf{b}^g}\delta\mathbf{b}^g + \tfrac{\partial\Delta\bar{\mathbf{p}}_{ij}}{\partial\mathbf{b}^a}\delta\mathbf{b}^a\big),$$

When the optimizer updates the bias estimate, a first-order correction — a Jacobian-vector product costing microseconds — replaces a full reintegration costing milliseconds. This is the decisive engineering contribution of [1]: the IMU factor becomes a first-class citizen of real-time bundle adjustment, and its 15-dimensional covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{ij}$ (9 motion + 6 bias random-walk states) is propagated alongside the mean with right-Jacobian noise transport [1].

### 3.2 The visual-inertial factor graph

The graph contains three factor types. **Reprojection factors** tie a 3D landmark $\mathbf{l}_m$ observed in keyframe $i$ to the measured keypoint $\mathbf{z}_{im}$:

$$\mathbf{r}_{im} = \boldsymbol{\pi}\big(\mathbf{R}_i^\top(\mathbf{l}_m - \mathbf{p}_i)\big) - \mathbf{z}_{im},$$

with $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ the pinhole (or fisheye) projection, wrapped in a Huber robust cost to down-weight mismatches [2][5]. **Preintegration factors** connect consecutive keyframe states as derived above [1], a **prior factor** anchors the first pose and initial biases, and landmarks may be eliminated analytically (*structureless vision factors*), avoiding optimization over thousands of 3D points [1].

| Factor | Variables | Residual dim. | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reprojection | pose $i$, landmark $m$ | 2 | visual geometry, Huber-robust |
| IMU preintegration | $\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j$ | 15 (9 motion + 6 bias) | metric motion, gravity, scale |
| Prior | $\mathbf{x}_0$ | 15 | gauge fixing, initial bias |
| Loop closure | poses $i, j$ (distant) | 6 (SE(3)) or 7 (Sim(3)) | drift correction |

### 3.3 Loop closure via bag-of-words place recognition

Odometry drifts without bound; only recognizing a previously mapped place can correct it. DBoW2 builds a **vocabulary tree** by hierarchical k-means clustering of binary descriptors (BRIEF/ORB): with branching factor $k$ and depth $L$, the leaves define $k^L$ *visual words* [4]. Each keyframe becomes a TF-IDF-weighted bag-of-words vector, queried through two indices: the **inverse index** maps each word to the keyframes containing it for sub-linear retrieval, and the **direct index** stores per-keyframe features at tree nodes, yielding putative correspondences for the geometric check without brute-force descriptor matching [4].

A candidate survives only after **geometric verification** — RANSAC-based PnP or fundamental-matrix estimation on the direct-index correspondences — plus a temporal-consistency test over neighboring keyframes, which drives the false-positive rate to effectively zero [4]. ORB-SLAM3 extends this with a higher-recall scheme feeding a multi-map *Atlas*: when tracking is lost, a new map starts and is merged seamlessly upon re-observation, so no information is discarded [2].

Accepted loops inject a relative SE(3) — or Sim(3), to correct monocular scale drift — constraint between temporally distant keyframes, followed by pose-graph optimization and, in the full system, global bundle adjustment [2][5].

### 3.4 Incremental smoothing with iSAM2

Batch re-optimization at every keyframe is infeasible as the graph grows. iSAM2 maintains the MAP estimate incrementally using the **Bayes tree**, a directed clique tree from the eliminated factor graph [3]. Each clique $C_k = F_k : S_k$ stores the conditional $p(F_k \mid S_k)$ over frontal variables given its separator. The key insight: *adding new factors only affects the cliques on the path from the involved variables to the root*; those cliques are detached, re-eliminated with the new factors, and re-attached, while the rest of the tree is untouched [3].

**Fluid relinearization** relinearizes only variables whose estimate moved more than a threshold $\beta$, eliminating periodic batch steps; **incremental variable reordering** (constrained COLAMD on the affected subtree) keeps elimination sparse as loops close [3]. After a loop closure, the affected subtree is exactly the set of variables whose linearization point or connectivity changed — which is why the Bayes tree is the natural back end for visual-inertial SLAM [1][3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The bias Jacobian: why preintegration survives optimization

The subtle point of [1] is that the bias appears *inside* the product of exponentials, so the preintegrated rotation is a nonlinear function of $\mathbf{b}^g$. During the single forward pass, the algorithm also propagates its derivative with respect to the bias via the SO(3) right Jacobian, with analogous recursions for the velocity and position Jacobians. Because bias drift between keyframes is small (bias random walk is typically $10^{-4}$–$10^{-3}$ units/$\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$), the first-order correction $\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{ij}(\bar{\mathbf{b}}^g)\,\mathrm{Exp}(\mathbf{J}^g_{\Delta\mathbf{R}}\,\delta\mathbf{b}^g)$ stays accurate across the hundreds of linearization iterations of a typical solve [1]. This converts an $O(m)$ reintegration per iteration into an $O(1)$ correction — the difference between milliseconds and seconds per optimization step on long keyframe intervals.

### 4.2 From bearing measurements to the reprojection factor

A calibrated camera measures the *bearing* to a landmark, not its depth. For keypoint $\mathbf{z}_{im}$ with covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{im}$ (scaled by pyramid octave, as in ORB-SLAM3 [2]), the information-weighted residual enters the MAP objective under a Huber kernel $\rho$:

$$\min \sum_{i,m} \rho\!\left(\big\|\mathbf{r}_{im}\big\|^2_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{im}}\right) + \sum_{(i,j)} \big\|\mathbf{r}_{\text{IMU},ij}\big\|^2_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{ij}}.$$

Two decisions dominate practical accuracy. **Covariance scaling by scale level**: features at coarser pyramid octaves carry larger uncertainty, and weighting them accordingly is worth several millimeters of ATE on EuRoC [2]. **Robustification**: a single mismatched loop closure or outlier landmark with a quadratic cost can destroy a converged estimate; the Huber kernel bounds outlier influence while remaining smooth at the origin [5].

```python
import numpy as np

def imu_residual(x_i, x_j, preint, g, dt):
    """Preintegrated IMU residual, bias-corrected analytically [1]."""
    R_i, p_i, v_i, bg_i, ba_i = x_i
    R_j, p_j, v_j, _, _ = x_j
    dbg, dba = bg_i - preint.bg_bar, ba_i - preint.ba_bar
    dR = preint.dR @ so3_exp(preint.J_dR_dbg @ dbg)
    dv = preint.dv + preint.J_dv_dbg @ dbg + preint.J_dv_dba @ dba
    dp = preint.dp + preint.J_dp_dbg @ dbg + preint.J_dp_dba @ dba
    r_dR = so3_log(dR.T @ R_i.T @ R_j)
    r_dv = R_i.T @ (v_j - v_i - g * dt) - dv
    r_dp = R_i.T @ (p_j - p_i - v_i * dt - 0.5 * g * dt**2) - dp
    return np.concatenate([r_dR, r_dv, r_dp])
```

### 4.3 The vocabulary tree as a metric on appearance

DBoW2's vocabulary tree is a *discretization of descriptor space* [4]. Training runs hierarchical k-means on millions of descriptors; at query time, each descriptor descends the tree to a leaf word in $O(L)$ time. The TF-IDF-weighted BoW vector turns place recognition into sparse vector retrieval, and the complete pipeline — including FAST+BRIEF extraction — runs in ~22 ms per frame on 26,300-image sequences, an order of magnitude faster than contemporary FAB-MAP [4].

The **direct index** is the underappreciated half of the design: by storing which features of a keyframe fall under each tree node, correspondences between a query frame and a candidate are proposed at the node level and refined geometrically, rather than by exhaustive descriptor comparison [4]. ORB-SLAM3 builds on this foundation with improved recall, using it not only for loop closure but for *map merging* across sessions — which is what lets the Atlas reuse all previous information instead of only the last seconds of odometry [2].

### 4.4 The Bayes tree under loop closure

Consider a trajectory with a loop-closure factor connecting keyframe $1$ and keyframe $N$, typically in distant Bayes-tree branches. Adding the factor marks every clique on the paths from both variables to the root as affected; only this (often small) subtree is re-eliminated [3]. Fluid relinearization then checks each affected variable's delta against $\beta$ and relinearizes selectively. The practical consequence: a loop closure that would force a full batch refactorization instead costs a localized update, keeping the estimator real-time even on trajectories with thousands of keyframes [1][3].

> **Theorem (Locality of incremental updates):** Incorporating new factors requires re-elimination of exactly the cliques containing variables connected to the new factors or their separators — the minimal subtree spanning the involved variables and the root. All other cliques, and the conditional densities they encode, are provably unaffected [3].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Benchmark protocol: the EuRoC MAV datasets

The EuRoC datasets [7] are the de facto standard for visual-inertial evaluation: 11 sequences with synchronized stereo imagery (20 Hz), an ADIS16448 IMU (200 Hz), and millimeter-accurate ground truth from a laser tracker (machine hall) or motion capture (Vicon room), spanning the full easy-to-difficult spectrum:

| Sequence | Environment | Character |
|---|---|---|
| MH_01_easy – MH_02_easy | Machine hall | good texture, slow flight |
| MH_03_medium – MH_05_difficult | Machine hall | fast motion, poor illumination |
| V1_01_easy – V1_03_difficult | Vicon room | aggressive maneuvers, motion blur |
| V2_01_easy – V2_03_difficult | Vicon room | rapid yaw, low texture |

Accuracy is reported as absolute trajectory error (ATE) RMSE after SE(3) alignment; because yaw and position are unobservable, alignment must not mask estimator inconsistency — a subtlety analyzed in the VIO evaluation literature [8].

### 5.2 Measured accuracy

Forster et al. validate preintegration with a 50-run Monte Carlo consistency analysis and real flights: on a 430 m indoor trajectory their full pipeline (SVO front end, preintegration, structureless factors, iSAM2) drifts **0.3 m over 360 m** versus 0.7 m for OKVIS and MSCKF, with markedly reduced yaw drift — the direct payoff of consistent on-manifold linearization [1]. Average iSAM2 update time is 10 ms on a laptop CPU, confirming real-time incremental smoothing [1].

VINS-Mono [5] demonstrates the sliding-window tightly-coupled alternative: robust initialization, preintegrated IMU factors with bias correction, loop detection, and 4-DOF pose-graph optimization achieve trajectory errors competitive with OKVIS across EuRoC while adding failure recovery and online calibration. The independent benchmark of Delmerico and Scaramuzza [8] places VINS-Mono, OKVIS, and ROVIO at the accuracy frontier on EuRoC, with filter-based MSCKF trading accuracy for the lowest CPU load — confirmation that optimization-based tightly-coupled formulations dominate when compute permits.

ORB-SLAM3 [2] is the current state of the art in this lineage: its MAP visual-inertial estimator — including MAP-based IMU initialization [6] — with stereo cameras attains an **average accuracy of 3.6 cm on the EuRoC drone sequences** and 9 mm under fast hand-held motion in TUM-VI room sequences, a 2–5× improvement over previous approaches [2]. Its multi-map Atlas with high-recall place recognition converts these odometry-grade numbers into SLAM-grade bounded drift: revisits anywhere in the map history trigger merges rather than unbounded error growth [2].

### 5.3 Proof sketch: consistency of the preintegrated estimator

The MAP estimator on the preintegrated IMU factor is consistent (its covariance matches its error statistics) provided linearization points stay near the true state. (i) The preintegrated measurements are exact nonlinear functions of the IMU data given the bias linearization point; the only approximation is first-order bias correction, second-order in the (small) bias drift [1]. (ii) Covariance propagates through the true nonlinear kinematics with the right Jacobian — first-order uncertainty transport exact on the manifold, unlike Euler-angle filters that linearize the parameterization itself [1]. (iii) The 50-run Monte Carlo evaluation shows NEES inside the 95% $\chi^2$ confidence interval [1]. ∎

The failure mode is the precondition: after long open-loop motion, linearization points drift far from truth, the bias correction degrades, and covariance becomes optimistic — precisely the regime where loop closure must intervene before inconsistency compounds [3][4].

## 6 Limitations

**Linearization fragility.** Every component — preintegration bias correction, reprojection, iSAM2 fluid relinearization — is a first-order method. Large bias excursions (e.g., thermal drift on low-cost IMUs) or violent motions that invalidate the linearization point break the approximations silently.

**Perceptual aliasing and dynamic scenes.** Bag-of-words place recognition assumes a static, distinctive world. Repetitive structures (corridors, parking garages) generate false loop candidates; geometric verification rejects most, but misses true loops in exactly the texture-poor environments where drift is worst [4]. Dynamic objects corrupt both the BoW statistics and the static-world assumption of bundle adjustment.

**Initialization and degeneracy.** Scale, gravity, and biases are unobservable without motion excitation [6]. Hovering or constant-velocity flight leaves the estimator under-constrained; degenerate motions (pure rotation, which starves triangulation of parallax) remain a fundamental observability limit, not an implementation bug.

**Computational scaling of loop closure.** While iSAM2 localizes updates, a loop closure still re-eliminates a subtree whose size grows with loop length, and global bundle adjustment after map merges is a batch operation [2][3]. Long-term autonomy therefore still requires map sparsification or submapping beyond the core theory.

**Sensor realities.** Rolling-shutter cameras violate the global-shutter projection model; IMU saturation during aggressive motion clips the very measurements preintegration trusts; time misalignment beyond a few milliseconds biases scale. Online temporal calibration [5] mitigates but does not eliminate these effects.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Visual-inertial SLAM is a solved problem only in the sense that its mathematical foundations are now classical: on-manifold preintegration [1] made inertial factors compatible with keyframe-rate optimization; bag-of-binary-words place recognition [4] made loop closure fast and reliable; the Bayes tree [3] made the resulting nonlinear optimization incremental. Systems from VINS-Mono [5] to ORB-SLAM3 [2] have converted this theory into centimeter-accurate, real-time, open-source reality on the EuRoC benchmark [7]. The open frontier is robustness under assumption violation — dynamics, degeneracy, aliasing, sensor imperfection — rather than another decimal of accuracy on well-lit trajectories.
