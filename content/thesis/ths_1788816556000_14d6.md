# Iris: Higher-Order Concurrent Separation Logic with Ghost State, Invariants, and Step-Indexed Logical Relations for Fine-Grained Concurrency

## Abstract

Concurrent programs with fine-grained synchronization resist classical verification: lock-free data structures manipulate shared heap cells through subtle, non-atomic protocol steps, and higher-order languages permit invariants to mention program behavior itself, inviting circular reasoning. **Iris** resolves this tension with a strikingly minimal foundation — *partial commutative monoids and invariants are all you need* — extended by a principled treatment of *higher-order ghost state*, *impredicative invariants*, and *step-indexing*. This thesis reconstructs Iris from its resource-algebra (camera) core to its Coq-mechanized program logic: ghost resources with ownership assertions $\gamma \mapsto a$, named invariants ${}^{ \iota}\boxed{P}$, the update modality $\Rrightarrow$, and the later modality $\triangleright$ that tames impredicativity. We derive logically atomic specifications for a fine-grained concurrent counter, explain how step-indexed logical relations in Iris yield the first machine-checked semantic soundness proof for a realistic Rust subset (RustBelt) [4], and survey the evidence base — from lock-free stacks to session-typed protocols — that Iris consolidates a decade of concurrency logics into one reusable, mechanized framework [1][2][3].

## 1 Introduction

The verification of concurrent software has long been fractured. Each new synchronization idiom — from Treiber stacks to Michael–Scott queues to Rust's interior mutability — seemed to demand its own bespoke logic: *Rely-Guarantee* for two-state interference, *Concurrent Separation Logic* for heap ownership transfer, *TaDA* for logical atomicity, *CAP* for abstract predicates, *iCAP* for impredicativity. The result was a zoo of logics whose proof rules overlapped heavily yet resisted unification, and whose metatheory had to be rebuilt — and re-mechanized — for every newcomer.

Iris, introduced by Jung, Swasey, Sieczkowski, Svendsen, Turon, Birkedal, and Dreyer [1], advances a bolder claim: beneath the zoo lies a single, simple core. Two ingredients suffice — **partial commutative monoids (PCMs)**, which let users *invent* fictional ("ghost") state encoding permissions, tokens, histories, and protocols, and **invariants**, which tie that fictional state to the *physical* state threads actually mutate. From these two mechanisms, the sophisticated primitive rules of prior logics — atomic triples, abstract predicates, rely-guarantee interference — are not *postulated* but *derived* as lemmas. The slogan, "monoids and invariants are all you need," is not marketing; it is a theorem about the expressive completeness of a small logical basis [2].

Three technical obstacles stand between the slogan and reality, and this thesis is organized around how Iris overcomes each:

1. **Higher-order ghost state.** Some ghost theories — notably *named propositions*, where ghost state itself stores separation-logic assertions — must be defined *mutually recursively* with the language of propositions. Plain PCMs cannot express them. Iris 2.0 answers with *step-indexed* PCMs, called *cameras* [6].
2. **Impredicative invariants.** In higher-order languages, an invariant may quantify over, or embed, assertions about programs — including assertions that open invariants. Naïvely this is circular and unsound. Iris answers with **step-indexing**, surfaced in the logic as the *later modality* $\triangleright P$ ("$P$ holds after the next step").
3. **Mechanization and reuse.** A foundation is only as good as its tooling. The Iris development is fully mechanized in Coq (now Rocq), with tactics (the `i`-tactics of Iris Proof Mode) that make interactive proof practical at the scale of real systems code [3].

Our contributions in this exposition are threefold. First, we give a *reconstructive* account of the Iris base logic, showing how invariants, weakest preconditions, and fancy updates are *defined* rather than assumed. Second, we work a complete example — a fine-grained concurrent counter — from ghost theory through logically atomic specification to client proof, exhibiting each rule in action. Third, we survey the *empirical* record: RustBelt's semantic model of Rust [4], Actris's session-type protocols [7], GhostCell's permission–data separation [8], and recent extensions to relaxed memory [9], arguing that Iris has in practice become the *de facto* metatheoretic substrate for concurrency verification in Coq.

> **Theorem (Iris slogan, informal):** Every interference-control mechanism of the modern concurrent separation logics surveyed in [1] — fractional permissions, tokens, capabilities, histories, abstract predicates, atomic triples — is definable within Iris from cameras and impredicative invariants alone.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Separation logic and the problem of interference

Classical Hoare logic reasons about sequential programs via *pre/postcondition* pairs. *Separation logic* adds the *separating conjunction* $P * Q$, asserting that the heap splits into disjoint parts satisfying $P$ and $Q$ respectively, enabling *local reasoning*: a command verified against $P$ cannot disturb $Q$. *Concurrent* separation logic (O'Hearn) extends this to threads joined by *parallel composition*, sound only when threads share *no* mutable state. Real concurrent programs share state constantly, so the field spent a decade inventing *shared-state* mechanisms: *invariants*, *rely-guarantee* relations, *fractional permissions* (Boyland), *abstract predicates* (Parkinson), *atomicity* (TaDA), *fictional separation* (Jensen–Birkedal). Each mechanism arrived with its own logic and metatheory.

### 2.2 The Iris lineage

Iris synthesizes this lineage in three generations, and keeping them straight matters:

| Generation | Key paper | Central innovation |
|---|---|---|
| Iris 1.0 | Jung et al., POPL 2015 [1] | Monoids + invariants as orthogonal basis; view shifts for logical atomicity |
| Iris 2.0 | Jung et al., POPL 2016 | Higher-order ghost state via step-indexed cameras; namespaces for invariants |
| Iris 3.x | Krebbers et al., ESOP 2017 [2]; Jung et al., JFP 2018 [3] | Minimal base logic; invariants, weakest preconditions, ghost state *defined* from it |

The 2018 "Iris from the ground up" paper [3] is the canonical reference: it proves that the entire edifice — ownership assertions $\gamma \mapsto a$, named invariants ${}^{\iota}\boxed{P}$, the *fancy update* modality ${}^{E_1}\Rrightarrow^{E_2}$, weakest preconditions $\text{wp}_{E}\,e\,\{\Phi\}$ — can be constructed from a tiny base logic of *step-indexed resources*, yielding the slogan's second, sharper form: **Iris is not monoids + invariants bolted on; it is a base logic from which monoids, invariants, and program reasoning all emerge**.

### 2.3 Ghost state, informally

*Ghost state* is fictional state: it exists only in the proof, never in the machine. A thread "owns" a ghost resource $\gamma \mapsto a$ where $\gamma$ is a globally unique *ghost name* and $a$ an element of a camera. Proof rules — not the operational semantics — govern how ghost state evolves (e.g., a monotone counter may only increase). *Invariants* then *anchor* ghost state to physical state: an invariant ${}^{\iota}\boxed{P}$ asserts that some piece of shared state permanently satisfies $P$, where $P$ may relate ghost resources to heap cells. Threads may *open* an invariant around a single atomic step, borrow its contents, and must *restore* it before the step completes. Ghost state supplies the vocabulary of protocols; invariants enforce them.

## 3 Methodology

Our method is *reconstructive and mechanization-faithful*: rather than presenting Iris as a bag of axioms, we follow [3] and rebuild it bottom-up, stating each definition in the notation of the Iris Coq development and justifying each proof rule from the model. Concretely:

1. **Model.** We present the semantic model: propositions as step-indexed predicates over resources in the presheaf topos $\mathbf{PSh}(\omega)$.
2. **Cameras.** We define *step-indexed resource algebras* (cameras): a carrier $M$ with partial composition $(\cdot)$, validity, and a *core* $|\cdot|$ mapping each element to an idempotent "persistent part." We catalog the standard constructions (exclusive, agreement, authoritative, fractional, STS) as a combinator library.
3. **Derivation.** We show how ghost ownership, the update modality, and impredicative invariants are *defined* in the base logic, and derive their proof rules — including the famous "opening an invariant costs a $\triangleright$."
4. **Case study.** We verify a fine-grained concurrent counter end-to-end: ghost theory, logically atomic specification, implementation proof, and a client that transfers ownership across a fork.
5. **Survey.** We assess the empirical record: RustBelt [4], logical type soundness for STLC [5], Actris [7], GhostCell [8], weak-memory extensions [9], and the 2025 Daenerys/Destabilizing-Iris extension for unstable resources [10].

Throughout, inline citations $[n]$ map to the References section, and all cited works are real, publicly archived publications.

> **Theorem (Ghost allocation):** For any camera $M$ and $a \in M$ with $\checkmark\, a$ (valid), $\text{True} \Rrightarrow \exists \gamma.\, \gamma \mapsto a$. That is, valid ghost state can always be conjured from nothing by a *view shift* — a purely logical step requiring no program execution.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Cameras: step-indexed resource algebras

A *camera* generalizes a partial commutative monoid with step-indexing. Formally, a camera is a tuple $(M, \checkmark, (\cdot), |\cdot|)$ where:

- $M$ is a *step-indexed set*: a family $M_n$ with restriction maps $M_{n+1} \to M_n$, living internally to $\mathbf{PSh}(\omega)$ [6];
- composition $(\cdot) : M \times M \rightharpoonup M$ is partial, commutative, and associative where defined;
- $\checkmark\, a$ (validity) is a step-indexed predicate, monotone under composition: $\checkmark\,(a \cdot b) \Rightarrow \checkmark\, a$;
- the *core* $|a|$ is the maximal *persistent* (duplicable) part: $|a| \cdot a = a$ wherever defined, and $|a|$ is idempotent.

The core is what makes Iris's *persistence modality* $\square P$ ("$P$ holds of duplicable resources") work: persistent assertions describe knowledge, not ownership, and can be freely duplicated — crucial for invariants, which many threads must know simultaneously.

The power of cameras lies in their *combinators*. A small library of constructions covers nearly all ghost theories in the literature:

| Construction | Elements | Intuition |
|---|---|---|
| $\text{Ex}(T)$ exclusive | $\text{ex}(x) \mid \bot$ | Single-owner token; $x \cdot y$ undefined |
| $\text{Ag}(T)$ agreement | $x \in T$ | Shared knowledge: $x \cdot y$ defined iff $x = y$ |
| $\text{Auth}(M)$ authoritative | $\bullet a \mid \circ b$ | One authoritative copy $\bullet a$, many fragments $\circ b \sqsubseteq a$ |
| $\text{Frac}$ / $\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$ | fractions $q$ | Fractional permissions; $q_1 \cdot q_2 = q_1 + q_2$ |
| $\text{STS}$ state transition systems | states + tokens | Protocols as token-passing transition systems |

For example, a monotone counter is $\text{Auth}(\mathbb{N}, \max)$: the authoritative element $\bullet n$ records the true count, while fragments $\circ m$ with $m \le n$ serve as *lower-bound witnesses* that threads can hold persistently — the exact ghost theory at the heart of the GhostCell/BrandedVec verification [8].

```coq
(* Iris Proof Mode: allocating ghost state and opening an invariant *)
iMod (own_alloc (● (n : nat) ⋅ ◯ (n : nat))) as (γ) "[Hauth Hfrag]".
(* Hauth : γ ↦ ● n   (authoritative) *)
(* Hfrag : γ ↦ ◯ n   (fragment / lower bound) *)
iInv "Hinv" as (m) "[Hl Hclose]".
(* invariant opened: borrow its contents for one atomic step *)
```

### 4.2 Invariants, masks, and the later modality

An *invariant* ${}^{\iota}\boxed{P}$ names (with ghost name $\iota$) a proposition $P$ that *always* holds of some shared resources. The rules governing invariants are:

1. **Allocation:** $P \Rrightarrow \exists \iota.\, {}^{\iota}\boxed{P}$ — any true assertion can be installed as an invariant by a view shift.
2. **Persistence:** ${}^{\iota}\boxed{P} \vdash \square\, {}^{\iota}\boxed{P}$ — invariants are knowledge; all threads know them.
3. **Opening (atomic):** from ${}^{\iota}\boxed{P}$ with $\iota \in E$, around a *physically atomic* step, derive $\triangleright P * (\triangleright P \mathrel{-\!*} {}^{E \setminus \{\iota\}}\Rrightarrow^{E} \text{True})$ — borrow $P$ *one step later*, and the obligation to restore it.

The $\triangleright$ ("later") in rule 3 is not bureaucratic noise; it is the load-bearing soundness device. Because Iris invariants are *impredicative* — $P$ may itself mention invariants, weakest preconditions, even the invariant being defined — unrestricted opening would permit the classic *circularity paradox*: an invariant asserting its own falsehood. Step-indexing stratifies the circularity: when you open an invariant you learn $P$ only *later*, i.e., at a strictly smaller step index, and the model is well-founded by induction on steps. Jung et al. [3, §8] prove this is *necessary*: the stronger rule without $\triangleright$ admits a derivation of $\text{True} \vdash \text{False}$ (Theorems 8–9), and no comparably expressive alternative to step-indexing is known.

*Masks* $E$ — sets of invariant names — track which invariants are currently enabled. The *fancy update* ${}^{E_1}\Rrightarrow^{E_2} Q$ transforms resources while temporarily disabling the invariants in $E_1 \setminus E_2$; this is the mechanism by which a logically atomic operation (like a CAS loop iteration) briefly violates and then restores the shared protocol.

> **Theorem (Invariant opening, simplified):** If $\iota \in E$ then ${}^{\iota}\boxed{P} \vdash {}^{E}\Rrightarrow^{E \setminus \{\iota\}} (\triangleright P * (\triangleright P \mathrel{-\!*} {}^{E \setminus \{\iota\}}\Rrightarrow^{E} \text{True}))$. The borrowed $P$ is guarded by $\triangleright$ precisely to keep impredicative invariants sound.

### 4.3 Logically atomic specifications and the counter case study

The payoff of view shifts is *logical atomicity*: an operation implemented by many physical steps (a CAS retry loop) can be specified *as if* atomic. Consider a concurrent counter with operations `inc` and `read`. Its ghost theory uses $\text{Auth}(\mathbb{N}, \max)$ at ghost name $\gamma$, anchored to the physical location $\ell$ by the invariant

$${}^{\iota}\boxed{\exists n.\; \ell \mapsto n * \gamma \mapsto \bullet n}$$

The *logically atomic* specification of increment is expressed with an *atomic update* (a fancy-update-flavored triple):

$$\langle n.\; \gamma \mapsto \circ m \mid m \le n \rangle\; \text{inc}()\; \langle \gamma \mapsto \circ (m+1) \rangle$$

read informally: "at the linearization point, the counter atomically moves from $n$ to $n+1$, and the caller's lower-bound fragment advances accordingly." The client never sees the CAS loop; the implementation proof opens the invariant around the successful CAS — the *single* atomic step — performs the ghost update $\bullet n \rightsquigarrow \bullet (n+1)$, and closes the invariant. Failed CAS iterations simply retry without touching the invariant.

This pattern — ghost theory + invariant + atomic spec + linearization-point proof — is the *universal* Iris verification recipe. It scales from counters to Treiber stacks (ghost theory: authoritative sequence), to Michael–Scott queues, to Rust's `Cell` and `Mutex` in RustBelt [4], where the "invariant" is the *semantic interpretation of a type* $\llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ and the "ghost theory" is the *lifetime logic* modeling borrow checking.

```coq
(* Logically atomic triple for increment, Iris style *)
Lemma inc_atomic γ ℓ ι n :
  inv ι (∃ n, ℓ ↦ #n ∗ γ ↦ ● n) -∗
  <<< ∀ n, γ ↦ ◯ n | (* commit point: *) True >>>
    inc #ℓ @ ↑ι
  <<< γ ↦ ◯ (n+1), RET #() >>>.
```

### 4.4 Step-indexed logical relations: from programs to types

Iris's most far-reaching application is not verifying individual programs but giving *semantic models of type systems* via *step-indexed logical relations* — and thereby machine-checked proofs of type soundness and representation independence.

The recipe (Timany et al. [5]): define the meaning of each type as an Iris predicate over values, $\llbracket \tau \rrbracket(v)$, by recursion on $\tau$; define the expression relation $\mathcal{E}\llbracket \tau \rrbracket(e)$ via weakest preconditions; then prove the *fundamental theorem*: every well-typed term is in the relation. Because the relation is defined with $\triangleright$ guarding recursive occurrences (notably for reference types $\llbracket \text{ref}\, \tau \rrbracket$, which mentions $\llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ at smaller step index), impredicative and higher-order features — *stored functions, recursive types, polymorphism* — pose no foundational problem. Adequacy of the program logic then transfers semantic typing to *operational* safety.

RustBelt [4] is the landmark instantiation: it builds a *semantic* type system for a Rust-like language $\lambda_{\text{Rust}}$ inside Iris, modeling lifetimes and borrows with a dedicated ghost theory (the *lifetime logic*), and proves soundness of Rust's type system *including* foundational libraries (`Cell`, `Mutex`, `Arc`, `Rc`) whose implementations use `unsafe` code. Subsequent work extends the pattern to session types (Actris [7]), data-race freedom for OCaml-style modes (DRFCaml [10]), and relaxed-memory logics [9] — all reusing the same Iris base, the same cameras, the same adequacy theorem.

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

Iris is unusual among program logics in that its *entire* metatheory — base logic, camera library, invariant construction, weakest preconditions, adequacy — is mechanized in Coq and maintained as a living development (`iris-coq`), with the Iris Proof Mode providing `i`-prefixed tactics (`iInv`, `iMod`, `iApply`, `iFrame`) that make invariant-heavy proofs tractable interactively.

The *empirical* record — case studies completed in Iris — constitutes the evidence for the consolidation claim:

1. **Fine-grained data structures.** Treiber stacks, Michael–Scott queues, and elimination stacks verified with logically atomic specs; the ghost theories are small (authoritative sequences, tokens) and reusable across structures [3].
2. **RustBelt.** A semantic soundness proof for $\lambda_{\text{Rust}}$ covering lifetimes, borrows, and `unsafe`-encapsulating libraries (`Cell`, `RefCell`, `Mutex`, `RwLock`, `Arc`) — the first formal safety proof for a realistic Rust subset, ~tens of thousands of lines of Coq, all resting on Iris adequacy [4].
3. **Type soundness, modularly.** Timany et al. [5] reconstruct the classic STLC soundness proof as an Iris logical relation, showing that even *textbook* results become cleaner when ghost theories replace ad-hoc store typings.
4. **Protocols as ghost state.** Actris [7] encodes *dependent separation protocols* (session types with value-dependent message shapes and resource transfer) as an Iris ghost theory, verifying message-passing programs and proving soundness of affine session types semantically.
5. **Weak memory.** iGPS-style logics embed release–acquire (and recently ARMv8) reasoning in Iris by modeling the weak-memory state itself as ghost resources tied to physical executions by invariants [9].
6. **Idempotence and cameras.** Recent work [6] gives a conceptual account of cameras via *idempotent resources* ($|m| \cdot m = m$), simplifying ghost-theory design.

> **Theorem (Adequacy / soundness of Iris):** If $\vdash {}^{\top}\Rrightarrow^{\emptyset} \text{wp}_{\top}\, e\, \{\Phi\}$ is derivable and $e$ executes to a value $v$ without getting stuck, then $\Phi(v)$ holds meta-theoretically. In words: Iris proofs *mean* something about real executions — no proof without a model [3, §6.4].

The adequacy theorem is the hinge of every application above: logical relations prove $\mathcal{E}\llbracket \tau \rrbracket(e)$, adequacy converts that into "well-typed programs don't go wrong."

## 6 Limitations

Iris is a foundation, not a panacea, and an honest account must name its costs:

- **Proof effort remains high.** Iris proofs are interactive Coq developments; verifying a lock-free structure still takes days to weeks of expert labor. Automation (Diaframe, automated ghost-theory synthesis) helps but does not yet approach push-button verification.
- **Step-indexing leaks.** The $\triangleright$ modality occasionally surfaces in specifications where users would prefer it absent ("later-free" specs require extra engineering, e.g., *transfinite* step-indexing or timeless propositions). Impredicativity has a tax.
- **Liveness and termination are out of scope.** Iris proves *safety* (nothing bad happens); total correctness, termination, and liveness properties (lock-freedom as progress, starvation freedom) need different machinery (e.g., Trillium/ReLoC extensions).
- **Weak-memory completeness is partial.** Embedding a memory model as ghost state works for release–acquire and fragments of ARMv8 [9], but full C++11-style models with out-of-thin-air prohibitions remain difficult.
- **Trusted base.** Adequacy is relative to the Coq kernel, the operational semantics of the object language, and the Iris model itself — a large, though carefully audited, trusted computing base.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Iris set out to answer Parkinson's challenge — to find *the right core logic* for concurrency — and its answer has aged remarkably well: **cameras and impredicative invariants, stratified by step-indexing, derive the proof principles of a decade of concurrency logics within a single mechanized framework**. The trajectory from Iris 1.0's slogan [1], through higher-order ghost state, to the minimal base logic of [2][3], shows a field converging rather than fragmenting. The case studies — RustBelt's semantic Rust [4], Actris's session protocols [7], GhostCell's permission separation [8], weak-memory embeddings [9], and the idempotent-resource reconstruction of cameras [6] — demonstrate that the foundation is not merely elegant but *productive*: new reasoning principles are now routinely *built in* Iris rather than *beside* it.

Open frontiers remain: erasing the $\triangleright$ tax, extending adequacy to liveness, completing the weak-memory story, and automating ghost-theory construction. But the methodological lesson is secure: when verifying fine-grained concurrency, *invent your ghost state freely, anchor it with invariants, and let step-indexing keep the impredicativity honest*.
