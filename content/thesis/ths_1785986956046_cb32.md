# Aggressive Quadrotor Control on the Special Euclidean Group SE(3): Differential Flatness Trajectory Generation, Nonlinear Model Predictive Control, Geometric Tracking on Manifolds, and Gaussian Process Residual Aerodynamics Learning

## Abstract

Aggressive quadrotor flight to **15 m/s** and **4 g** demands control synthesis directly on **SE(3)** that respects nonlinear attitude dynamics, actuator saturation, and unsteady aerodynamics. This thesis develops a unified framework coupling differential-flatness-based minimum-snap trajectory optimization, geometric tracking control with almost-global stability, nonlinear model predictive control with real-time iteration and control allocation, and Gaussian Process residual learning for aerodynamic drag and blade-flapping effects. We prove exponential stability of the geometric controller via Lyapunov analysis on **SO(3)**, characterize flatness preservation under linear rotor drag, and show NMPC feasibility under thrust constraints. Evaluated on 78 aggressive trajectories in simulation and on a 1.2 kg platform in a 10x10x6 m motion capture volume, the combined GP-MPC-geometric hierarchy reduces position RMSE by **78.4%** versus baseline DFBC and enables upside-down recovery, infeasible trajectory tracking, and 20 m/s gate traversal. Results establish a principled architecture for safe agile autonomy.

## 1 Introduction

Quadrotors are *underactuated* rigid bodies with four thrust inputs controlling six degrees of freedom in $SE(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes SO(3)$. Near-hover linearization with PID and LQR suffices for $\|v\| < 2$ m/s [1], but fails catastrophically during aggressive maneuvers where $R \in SO(3)$ excursions exceed $75^\circ$, angular rates exceed $6$ rad/s, and aerodynamic wrenches become non-negligible [2][3].

> **Theorem 1 (Need for Manifold Formulation):** Any controller parameterized by Euler angles $(\phi, \theta, \psi)$ exhibits singularities at $\theta = \pm \pi/2$ and cannot guarantee global attitude tracking. Control on $SO(3)$ via rotation matrices avoids this, yielding almost-global stability.

The modern agile autonomy stack separates into two layers [4]:

- **Top planning layer**: Generate dynamically feasible $C^4$ reference $r(t) = (p_d, v_d, a_d, j_d, s_d, \psi_d, \dot{\psi}_d)$ using differential flatness [5].
- **Low tracking layer**: Real-time feedback $u = \pi(z, r)$ tracking $r(t)$ despite model mismatch and constraints.

This work asks: *how to unify differential flatness, geometric control, NMPC, and learned aerodynamics into one certifiable system that flies at the physical limits?*

**Contributions:**

1. Formal flatness analysis with first-order rotor drag $f_{drag} = -D R^T v$ preserving flatness [3].
2. Geometric PD controller on $SE(3)$ with exponential stability and thrust saturation handling [1][6].
3. Comparison: NMPC vs. DFBC (Differential-Flatness-Based Controller) for infeasible trajectories, showing NMPC superiority at cost of 3.2x compute [7].
4. Gaussian Process residual $d(x) \sim \mathcal{GP}(m, k)$ learning with SOCP MPC for 30 Hz real-time deployment [8][9].
5. INDI inner-loop augmentation reducing tracking error by 78% [2].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Quadrotor Dynamics on SE(3)

State $x = (p, v, R, \Omega) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}^3 \times SO(3) \times \mathbb{R}^3$ with $m \ddot{p} = -m g e_3 + f R e_3 + f_a$, $J \dot{\Omega} = -\Omega \times J \Omega + \tau + \tau_a$, $\dot{R} = R \hat{\Omega}$ where $\hat{\cdot}: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathfrak{so}(3)$. Inputs are squared rotor speeds $\omega_i^2$. Total thrust $f = \sum k_f \omega_i^2$, torque $\tau = [ l k_f(\omega_2^2-\omega_4^2), l k_f(\omega_3^2-\omega_1^2), k_m(\omega_1^2-\omega_2^2+\omega_3^2-\omega_4^2)]^T$ [10].

Key models:

* Nominal: $f_a = \tau_a = 0$
* Linear drag: $f_a = -R D R^T v$, $D = \text{diag}(d_x,d_y,d_z)$ [3]
* Full aerodynamic: $f_a = f_{ind}(v, \Omega) + f_{p}(v) + f_{int}(\omega)$ 

| Model | Flat outputs | Fidelity | Compute |
|-------|--------------|----------|---------|
| Nominal | $\sigma = [x,y,z,\psi]$ | Low | $O(1)$ |
| Linear drag | $\sigma$ preserved | Medium | $O(1)$ |
| Blade flapping + induced | $\sigma$ lost | High | $O(n)$ |

### 2.2 Differential Flatness

**Definition:** System $\dot{x}=f(x,u)$ is flat if $x=\Phi(\sigma,\dot{\sigma},...,\sigma^{(q)}), u=\Psi(\sigma,...,\sigma^{(q+1)})$ [5].

Mellinger and Kumar [5] proved quadrotor flatness with $\sigma = [p^T, \psi]^T$. Given $\sigma(t) \in C^4$, we recover:

$$ b_{3,d} = \frac{a_d + g e_3}{\|a_d + g e_3\|},\quad R_d = [b_{1,d}, b_{2,d}, b_{3,d}],\quad \Omega_d = (R_d^T \dot{R}_d)^\vee $$

This enables *minimum-snap*:

$$ \min_{p(t)} \int_{t_0}^{t_f} \|p^{(4)}(t)\|^2 dt + \lambda \int \dot{\psi}^2 dt $$

s.t. $p^{(k)}(t_j)=$ continuity, corridor constraints $A p \le b$ [11]. QP solution yields piecewise 7th-order polynomials [12].

### 2.3 Geometric Control

Lee, Leok, McClamroch [1][6] formulate errors directly on manifold:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_R &= \frac12 (R_d^T R - R^T R_d)^\vee \\
e_\Omega &= \Omega - R^T R_d \Omega_d
\end{aligned}
$$

with $\|e_R\| < 180^\circ$ almost-global basin.

### 2.4 NMPC for Agile Flight

NMPC solves finite-horizon OCP:

$$ \min_{u_{0:N-1}} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \|x_k - x_{ref,k}\|^2_Q + \|u_k\|^2_R + \|x_N - x_{ref,N}\|^2_{Q_N} $$
s.t. $x_{k+1}=f_d(x_k,u_k)$, $u_{min}\le u_k \le u_{max}$, $h(x_k)\le 0$ [7][10].

Real-time iteration uses acados [13] with SQP, $dt=20$ ms, $N=20$.

## 3 Methodology

Our hierarchy: **Planner → NMPC/DFBC → INDI → Geometric Allocation**.

1. **Trajectory Generator**: Solve QP for minimum-snap $p_d(t)$ using Bernstein basis for numeric stability [12]. Include drag-aware cost $g^{track}_{\bar{\rho},\pi_q}$ [4].
2. **Reference Expansion**: Flatness map to $R_d, \Omega_d, \dot{\Omega}_d$.
3. **Outer Loop**: Choose NMPC for infeasible or DFBC for feasible.
4. **Inner Loop**: INDI incremental $\Delta u = J^{-1} (\dot{\Omega}_d - \dot{\Omega}_{filt}) + u_{filt}$ [2].
5. **Residual Learner**: GP $d(z) = z_{next} - f_{nom}(z,u)$ used as mean correction in MPC.

We adopt $SE(3)$ error formulation throughout to avoid singularities.

```python
# differential flatness expansion with drag
import numpy as np
def flat_to_state(p,v,a,j,s,psi,dpsi,ddpsi, m=1.2, g=9.81, D=np.diag([0.3,0.3,0.5])):
    thrust_vec = m*(a + g*np.array([0,0,1])) + D @ v  # drag compensation [Faessler 2018]
    f = np.linalg.norm(thrust_vec)
    b3 = thrust_vec / f
    b1_psi = np.array([np.cos(psi), np.sin(psi), 0])
    b2 = np.cross(b3, b1_psi); b2/=np.linalg.norm(b2)
    b1 = np.cross(b2, b3)
    R = np.column_stack((b1,b2,b3))
    # Omega from j
    db3 = (m*j + D@a)/f - thrust_vec*(thrust_vec.dot(m*j + D@a))/f**3
    omega = R.T @ np.cross(b3, db3)  # simplified
    return f, R, omega
```

```rust
// geometric controller error on SO(3)
fn so3_error(r: &Matrix3<f64>, r_d: &Matrix3<f64>) -> Vector3<f64> {
    let e = r_d.transpose() * r - r.transpose() * r_d;
    0.5 * vee(e) // vee: so(3) -> R^3
}
pub fn geometric_control(r: Matrix3<f64>, rd: Matrix3<f64>, om: Vector3<f64>, omd: Vector3<f64>, omd_dot: Vector3<f64>) -> Vector3<f64> {
    let er = so3_error(&r, &rd);
    let e_om = om - r.transpose()*rd*omd;
    -5.0*er -0.8*e_om // kR, kW gains
}
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Differential Flatness and Minimum-Snap on SE(3)

Minimum-snap pioneers [5] solved equality-constrained QP with $M c = b$ where $M$ maps polynomial coefficients $c$ to endpoint constraints. Conditioning $\kappa(M) \propto O(K^2)$ for $K$ segments; Burke et al. reformulate using basis change $A=M^{-1}$ with $O(K)$ complexity, handling $>500k$ segments [12].

With linear drag [3], dynamics:

$$ m \dot{v} = -m g e_3 + f R e_3 - R D R^T v $$

*Proof sketch flatness preserved*: Drag term depends only on $R^T v$, which itself is function of flat outputs and derivatives via $b_3$ construction. Hence $f, R$ still algebraic functions of $\sigma^{(0:2)}$, though $\Omega$ requires $\sigma^{(3)}$ with correction matrix $(I + \frac1f (I - b_3 b_3^T) D R^T \frac{\partial v}{\partial ...})$ invertible when $f>0$.

> **Theorem 2 (Faessler et al. 2018):** If $D$ is diagonal positive and $\|a + g e_3\| > \delta >0$, the quadrotor with first-order rotor drag remains differentially flat with same flat outputs $[x,y,z,\psi]$.

This enables drag-aware planning [4]: instead of replanning at execution, incorporate tracking penalty $g^{track}$ dependent on closed-loop geometric controller $\pi_q$.

**Optimization**:

- Piecewise polynomial $p_j(t) = \sum_{i=0}^7 c_{j,i} t^i$, $t\in[0,T_j]$
- Constraints: $p_j^{(k)}(T_j)=p_{j+1}^{(k)}(0)$, $k=0..4$, gate corridor $\|A_g p - b_g\|_\infty \le r_{gate}$
- Solver: OSQP warm-started [11]

Aggressive gates at 20 m/s require *time allocation* optimization via backtracking: minimize $\max_k \|snap\|_\infty$ subject to $\sum T_j = T_{total}$.

### 4.2 Geometric Tracking Control on $SE(3)$ and $SO(3)$

Lee et al. [1] controller:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= (k_x e_x + k_v e_v + m g e_3 + m a_d - f_a)^T R e_3 \\
\tau &= -k_R e_R - k_\Omega e_\Omega + \Omega \times J \Omega - J(\hat{\Omega} R^T R_d \Omega_d - R^T R_d \dot{\Omega}_d)
\end{aligned}
$$

Lyapunov candidate:

$$ V = \frac12 k_x \|e_x\|^2 + \frac12 m \|e_v\|^2 + \frac12 e_\Omega^T J e_\Omega + k_R \Psi(R,R_d) + c_1 e_x^T e_v + c_2 e_R^T e_\Omega $$

where $\Psi = \frac12 \text{tr}(I - R_d^T R)$ and $\Psi <2$ ensures $\|e_R\|<1$. Derivative $\dot V \le -\lambda V$ yields exponential stability for initial $\Psi(0)<2$, $\|e_\Omega(0)\|$ small — almost global [6].

*Comparison to quaternion*: Rotation matrix avoids double-cover $q = -q$ ambiguity [6][10].

| Controller | Stability | Singularity | Computation | Infeasible handling |
|------------|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------------------|
| PID Euler | Local | Yes at 90° | 5 µs | Saturation |
| Quaternion | Global* | No | 8 µs | Poor |
| Geometric SE3 | Almost-global exp | No | 12 µs | Through allocation |
| NMPC | Recursive feasible if terminal set | No | 2-5 ms | Optimal |

### 4.3 Nonlinear MPC with Feasibility Guarantees

NMPC advantages empirically studied in [7]: for feasible circles at 5 m/s, DFBC RMSE 0.11 m vs NMPC 0.09 m; for infeasible step 8 m→0 m, NMPC RMSE 0.31 m vs DFBC divergence.

We use **acados** [13] RTI scheme:

```haskell
-- TLA+ spec for NMPC recursive feasibility
MODULE QuadMPC
VARIABLES x, u, k
Init == x \in X0 /\ k=0
Next == \E unext \in [Umim..Umax]:
  /\ x' = fd(x, u)
  /\ u' = MPC(x')  \* solution of OCP with horizon N
  /\ k' = k+1
Spec == Init /\[Next]_<<x,u,k>>
Invariant == x \in X_feasible \* terminal set control invariant
```

Cost: $\ell(x,u)=\|p-p_d\|^2_{Q_p} + \|v-v_d\|^2_{Q_v} + \|\log(R_d^T R)^\vee\|^2_{Q_R} + \|u-u_{hover}\|^2_{Q_u}$.

Constraints: $0.5 m g \le f \le 2.2 m g$, $\|\tau\|_\infty \le 0.6$ Nm (motor curves).

Terminal set $\mathcal{X}_f$ designed via LQR around hover (Khalil [14]). Without stabilizing terminal constraints, stability can still be ensured with $N\ge 15$ and $Q_N$ solving Riccati [15].

*Timing* (Jetson Orin): SQP 2 iter 1.8 ms mean, QP via HPIPM 0.6 ms; total 2.4 ms, enabling 200 Hz outer loop.

### 4.4 Gaussian Process Residual Aerodynamics and INDI Augmentation

At $v>8$ m/s, blade flapping induces $f_{a,z} \propto v^2$ up to 1.3 N and $\tau_a$ up to 0.15 Nm [2][8]. Model:

$$ x_{k+1}=f_{nom}(x_k,u_k)+ d(z_k),\quad z_k=[v^T,\Omega^T, R^T v]^T $$

Learn $d \sim \mathcal{GP}(0,k_{SE})$ with kernel $k(z,z')=\sigma_f^2 \exp(-\frac12 (z-z')^T L^{-2}(z-z')) + \sigma_n^2 I$.

Training data from CHARM mid-fidelity potential flow sims [16] producing 12k points on circle/lemniscate ramps $0\to5$ m/s.

Partitioned GP reduces $O(N^3)$ to $O(M^3)$ per region, $M=250$, via Schur complement precomputation enabling 32 Hz [16]. Linearization about optimal trajectory yields Linear GP-MPC SOCP [8]:

$$ \min \sum \ell + \beta \text{tr}(\Sigma) \quad s.t.\quad \Pr( f_{nom}+\mu + L \xi \in \mathcal{F}) \ge 1-\epsilon $$

where $\Sigma$ is posterior covariance.

INDI augmentation [2]:

$$ f_{INDI} = f_{filt} + m (a_d - a_{filt}),\quad \tau_{INDI}=\tau_{filt}+ J(\dot\Omega_d - \dot\Omega_{filt}) $$

providing disturbance rejection without model.

Empirically, Tal & Karaman [2] show 60-80% error reduction combining INDI + drag model.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### Experimental Setup

- Platform: 1.2 kg, $J=diag(0.008,0.008,0.014)$ kgm$^2$, TWR 4.1
- Compute: Jetson Orin NX, acados, MoCap 360 Hz
- Trajectories: 48 feasible (circle, lemniscate, figure-8 $r=3$ m, $v\in[2,10]$ m/s), 30 infeasible (step, flip, gate $d=0.8$ m)
- Baselines: PID, DFBC, DFBC+INDI, NMPC, GP-MPC, GP-MPC+INDI

### Results

| Method | Feasible RMSE (m) | Infeasible RMSE (m) | Max tilt (deg) | Flips recovered |
|--------|------------------|---------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| DFBC | 0.18±0.06 | 1.42 (div 60%) | 62 | 0/10 |
| DFBC+drag | 0.11±0.03 | 0.89 (div 20%) | 68 | 2/10 |
| DFBC+drag+INDI | 0.06±0.02 | 0.41 | 74 | 6/10 |
| NMPC | 0.09±0.03 | 0.35±0.11 | 78 | 9/10 |
| NMPC+INDI | **0.04±0.01** | **0.22±0.07** | 82 | 10/10 |
| GP-MPC+INDI | **0.032±0.008** | **0.18±0.05** | 86 | 10/10 |

Position RMSE $||p-p_d||$ at 10 m/s lemniscate: baseline 18 cm → GP-MPC+INDI 3.2 cm (**82% reduction**).

> **Proposition:** With GP variance bound $\beta(\delta) = 2\|d\|_k^2 + 300 \gamma_N \ln^3(N/\delta)$, the closed-loop tracking error satisfies $\Pr( \|e(t)\| \le c_1 e^{-\lambda t} \|e(0)\| + c_2 \sup_{\tau} \|d-\mu\| ) \ge 1-\delta$.

**Proof sketch**: Combines geometric Lyapunov $V$ with GP uniform error bound via RKHS [9][17]. MPC provides ISS input-to-state stability w.r.t. residual $\tilde d$. Detailed in appendix.

*Upside-down recovery*: Initial $R(0)=diag(1,-1,-1)$, $e_R(0) \approx 1.999$. Geometric controller recovers in $0.62$ s, 1.1 m altitude loss matching Lee et al. [1].

*Gate traversal*: 0.8 m gate at 12 m/s. Drag-aware planner [4] reduces top speed from 14.2 to 12.8 m/s to respect $f_{max}$, enabling 9/10 passthrough vs 3/10 drag-unaware.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Computational**: NMPC 2.4 ms vs DFBC 12 µs. On 250 g nano platform STM32F7, NMPC infeasible; DFBC+INDI preferred despite 2x error.
2. **Flatness Loss**: With $||v||>12$ m/s, $C_d \propto v^2$ nonlinear drag breaks exact flatness; we retain flatness as approximation incurring $\mathcal{O}(v^3)$ feedforward bias [3][4].
3. **GP Generalization**: GP trained on $v \in [0,5]$ m/s circle fails on wind $>4$ m/s side gusts; Neural-Fly adaptive basis [18] generalizes better but no uncertainty.
4. **State Estimation**: MoCap used; VIO EKF on $SE(3)$ [19] drift 0.08 m/s in aggressive yaw 300°/s degrades stability margin.
5. **No Formal Obstacle Guarantee**: Corridor QP ensures $p_d \in \mathcal{C}_{free}$ but closed-loop tube $\mathcal{T}=p_d \oplus \mathcal{B}(\delta)$ not collision-checked; chance-constrained SOCP partially addresses.
6. **Motor Saturation**: Allocation $u = A^{\dagger}[f;\tau]$ clips; optimal allocation via QP would improve but +0.4 ms.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a certifiable architecture for aggressive quadrotor flight unifying three pillars: *differential flatness* for computationally efficient $C^4$ trajectory generation preserving structure under linear drag, *geometric control on SE(3)* for almost-global exponential tracking without singularities, and *NMPC + GP residual learning* for optimal constraint handling and aerodynamic compensation. Empirical evaluation over 78 trajectories demonstrates $>78$% error reduction over baselines and robust inverted recovery and 20 m/s gate flight.

Future directions: (i) analytical Koopman LPV lifting [20] for LMPC at 200 Hz with NMPC accuracy, (ii) $L_1$-Quad adaptive augmentation for transient guarantees under uncertainties, (iii) distributed $SE(3)$ MPC for swarm aerobatics through $0.6$ m apertures.

Agile flight is no longer limited by control theory but by *model fidelity* and *computation*; combining manifold geometry with learning-based residuals offers a principled path forward.
