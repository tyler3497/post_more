# Asymmetric Numeral Systems: rANS, tANS, and the Modern Entropy-Coding Frontier from Duda's Construction to Zstandard and JPEG XL

## Abstract

Asymmetric Numeral Systems (ANS), introduced by Jarek Duda in 2009, constitute the first new family of entropy coders since the Huffman–arithmetic dichotomy was established in the 1970s: they attain the near-optimal compression rates of arithmetic coding while decoding at — and often beyond — Huffman speed, by replacing arithmetic coding's two-register interval with a *single natural number* as the coding state [1][2]. This thesis develops the full mathematical construction of the range variant (rANS) and the tabled variant (tANS, also known as Finite State Entropy), proves the amortized near-optimality of their renormalization procedures, characterizes symbol-spread optimization as the central redundancy-control problem of tANS, and traces the lineage from Duda's construction through Yann Collet's Finite State Entropy implementation [7] into production codecs: Zstandard's FSE-coded sequence streams and JPEG XL's MAANS entropy coder [9][10]. We provide complete reference implementations in Python and Rust, a quantitative rate–throughput comparison, worst-case redundancy bounds from recent theoretical work [5], and a frank accounting of ANS's limitations — its last-in-first-out discipline, quantization error, and error-propagation behavior — before concluding with open problems at the frontier of near-optimal

## 1. Introduction

For nearly four decades, lossless entropy coding was a two-horse race. **Huffman coding** (1952) is blazingly fast — one table lookup per symbol — but constrains symbol probabilities to dyadic rationals $2^{-k}$, incurring up to one full bit of redundancy per symbol on skewed distributions [1]. **Arithmetic coding** (Rissanen and Langdon, 1976; Pasco, 1976) escapes the dyadic constraint and approaches the Shannon entropy arbitrarily closely, but at the cost of a two-register interval state, multiplications and divisions per symbol, and comparatively intricate renormalization [3][4]. Practitioners were forced to choose between *speed* and *rate*, and the choice split the field: media codecs swallowed arithmetic coding's cost (JPEG's QM-coder, H.264's CABAC, JPEG 2000's MQ-coder), while bulk-data compressors (gzip, PNG, bzip2's front end) clung to Huffman.

In 2009, Jarek Duda's *"Asymmetric numeral systems"* broke the dichotomy [1]. The insight is disarmingly simple: a standard positional numeral system is an *optimal* encoder for a uniform symbol distribution — the state $x$ grows by exactly $\log_2 b$ bits per base-$b$ digit. Duda asked what happens when the "digits" have *unequal* probabilities, and constructed numeral systems that are asymmetric in exactly the sense the name suggests: the natural numbers are partitioned so that symbol $s$ "owns" a fraction $f_s/M$ of all integers, uniformly interleaved. Encoding a symbol then amounts to mapping the current state $x$ to the $x$-th integer owned by $s$; decoding reads the state modulo $M$ to recover the symbol and inverts the map. The result is a **stream code** — like arithmetic coding, it packs fractional bits across symbol boundaries — implemented with a **single state variable**, integer arithmetic, and (in the tabled variant) a single lookup per decoded symbol.

The practical consequences were dramatic. Duda's original tANS implementation decoded approximately **50% faster than Huffman coding** on a 256-symbol alphabet while matching arithmetic coding's compression rate [2]. Yann Collet's 2013 **Finite State Entropy (FSE)** reimplementation of tANS [7] became the entropy-coding core of **Zstandard** (Facebook/Meta, 2016), now one of the most widely deployed compressors on the planet, and ANS variants power **JPEG XL** (ISO/IEC 18181), Apple's **LZFSE**, and Google's **Draco** mesh codec. The theoretical community has since supplied worst-case redundancy bounds [5], optimal symbol-spread analyses [6], and a statistician's reformulation of ANS as a stack-structured push/pop interface with deep connections to latent-variable models and bits-back coding [4].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the classical background: Shannon entropy, the Huffman dyadic barrier, and arithmetic coding's interval machinery. Section 3 develops the rANS construction from first principles, including the coding function $C(s,x)$, its inverse, and the renormalization procedure that keeps the state bounded. Section 4 deep-dives into five topics: rANS renormalization arithmetic; the tANS finite-state automaton and FSE's decode tables; symbol-spread optimization; the binary ABS/uABS/rABS family; and ANS's deployment in Zstandard and JPEG XL. Section 5 gives redundancy proofs and empirical rate–throughput comparisons. Section 6 examines limitations, and Section 7 concludes with open problems.

## 2. Background: Entropy, Huffman, and Arithmetic Coding

### 2.1 The Shannon limit

For a discrete source $X$ over alphabet $\mathcal{A}$ with distribution $p$, the **Shannon entropy**

$$H(X) = -\sum_{s \in \mathcal{A}} p_s \log_2 p_s$$

is the fundamental lower bound: no uniquely decodable code can compress i.i.d.

### 2.2 Huffman coding and the dyadic barrier

Huffman coding assigns each symbol a codeword of integer length $\ell_s \approx \lceil -\log_2 q_s \rceil$, optimal among all *prefix codes*.

> **Theorem (Huffman redundancy bound):** For any distribution $p$, the Huffman code length satisfies $H(p) \le L_{\mathrm{Huffman}} < H(p) + 1$. The $+1$ is tight in the worst case.

### 2.3 Arithmetic coding: fractional bits at a price

Arithmetic coding escapes the dyadic barrier by encoding an *entire sequence* into a single sub-interval of $[0,1)$.

The price is structural.

This is the gap ANS fills: **a stream code with fractional-bit efficiency, but with a single state register and, in the tabled variant, no multiplications at all on the decode path.**

## 3. Methodology: The ANS Construction

### 3.1 The core idea: asymmetric numeral systems

A base-$b$ numeral system encodes a uniform digit stream with state update $x' = b \cdot x + d$: each digit costs exactly $\log_2 b$ bits, optimal for uniform digits.

Duda's construction [1][2] achieves this by **partitioning the natural numbers** among symbols.

$$C(s, x) = M \left\lfloor \frac{x}{f_s} \right\rfloor + \left( x \bmod f_s \right) + \mathrm{cdf}_s$$

where $\mathrm{cdf}_s = \sum_{t < s} f_t$.

Decoding inverts the map.

$$D(x') = \left( s,\; f_s \left\lfloor \frac{x'}{M} \right\rfloor + r - \mathrm{cdf}_s \right)$$

One verifies directly that $D(C(s,x)) = (s, x)$: **push** and **pop** are exact inverses [3].

### 3.2 Reference implementation: rANS in Python

The following implements the mathematical core (unbounded state), following the pedagogical formulation of Towns et al.

```python
from bisect import bisect_right
from math import log2

def build_cdf(freqs):
    cdf, acc = {}, 0
    for s, f in sorted(freqs.items()):
        cdf[s] = acc
        acc += f
    return cdf, acc  # acc == M, the precision

def push(x, s, freqs, cdf, M):
    """Encode symbol s onto state x. Returns new state."""
    f, c = freqs[s], cdf[s]
    return M * (x // f) + (x % f) + c

def pop(x, symbols, freqs, cdf, M):
    """Decode one symbol from state x. Returns (symbol, prev_state)."""
    r = x % M
    s = symbols[bisect_right([cdf[t] for t in symbols], r) - 1]
    prev = freqs[s] * (x // M) + r - cdf[s]
    return s, prev

# --- demo ---
freqs = {'a': 5, 'b': 3, 'c': 2}          # M = 10, q ~ (0.5, 0.3, 0.2)
cdf, M = build_cdf(freqs)
symbols = sorted(freqs)
x = 1                                     # initial state
msg = "abacaba"
for s in reversed(msg):                   # LIFO: encode back-to-front
    x = push(x, s, freqs, cdf, M)
out = []
while x > 1:
    s, x = pop(x, symbols, freqs, cdf, M)
    out.append(s)
assert ''.join(out) == msg
print("roundtrip OK, final state x =", x)
```

### 3.3 Renormalization: keeping the state bounded

The unbounded state above grows exponentially and is unusable in practice.

> **Theorem (rANS renormalization correctness):** Let $L = 2^R$, $b = 2^k$, and $x_{\max}(s) = \big\lfloor \tfrac{f_s}{M}\, bL \big\rfloor$. If $x < x_{\max}(s)$ then $C(s, x) < bL$, and repeated digit emission ($x \mapsto \lfloor x/b \rfloor$) strictly decreases $x$ until $x < x_{\max}(s)$. The decoder, reading digits back in reverse, restores $x$ exactly, so encoding remains invertible [1][3].

The amortized cost follows immediately: each emitted digit carries $\log_2 b$ bits, and the state grows by $\log_2(M/f_s)$ bits per symbol, so the total bitstream length after $n$ symbols is $\sum_i \log_2(M/f_{x_i}) + O(1) = n \cdot H(p, q) + O(1)$ — arithmetic-coding-class rate with per-symbol work dominated by a division (encoder) or a division plus table lookup (decoder).

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 rANS: the range variant and its arithmetic

rANS is the variant that most directly generalizes range coding: it handles large alphabets (256+ symbols) with a *per-symbol* $O(1)$ decode that binary-searches (or table-looks-up) the symbol from $r = x \bmod M$.

1. $r \leftarrow x \bmod M$ (one division, or one multiply with a reciprocal),
2. $s \leftarrow \mathrm{symbol}[r]$ (one table lookup into a $M$-entry table),
3. $x \leftarrow f_s \cdot \lfloor x/M \rfloor + r - \mathrm{cdf}_s$ (one multiply-add),
4. while $x < L$: $x \leftarrow (x \ll k) \mid \mathrm{read}_k()$ (renormalize from the bitstream).

Compared with arithmetic decoding's interval subdivision — two dependent multiplications plus carry/E3 handling — rANS performs roughly half the arithmetic, and crucially the operations form a short, predictable dependency chain that modern superscalar CPUs execute at 1–2 cycles per symbol in the tabled case.

A subtle but important point: because the decoder's final state $x_{\mathrm{final}}$ is the encoder's *initial* state and the emitted bitstream is read back-to-front, the encoder must know the total output length or store the final state in the stream header.

### 4.2 tANS: the finite-state entropy automaton

The **tabled** variant pushes the "single state" idea to its logical conclusion: restrict the state to a finite interval $[L, 2L)$ with $L = 2^R$, and *precompute everything*.

```rust
// tANS / FSE decode: one table lookup per symbol (cf. Zstd's FSE decoder)
struct DecEntry { symbol: u8, nb_bits: u8, new_state_base: u16 }

fn tans_decode(state: &mut u32, table: &[DecEntry], br: &mut BitReader, out: &mut Vec<u8>) {
    loop {
        let e = &table[*state as usize];   // single lookup
        out.push(e.symbol);
        let bits = br.read(e.nb_bits);     // 0..=R fresh bits
        *state = e.new_state_base as u32 + bits as u32;
        if br.empty() { break; }
    }
}
```

This is exactly the structure of Zstandard's FSE decoder: each state stores a symbol, an NB (number of bits) field, and a BL (baseline) field, with the next state computed as $\mathrm{BL} + \mathrm{bits}$ [8][11].

The price of tabulation is the **symbol spread problem**: we must assign each of the $L$ states to a symbol such that symbol $s$ owns exactly $L_s \approx q_s L$ states, and the assignment determines the automaton's redundancy.

| Coder | Rate (redundancy) | Decode ops/symbol | State | Direction | Table size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Huffman | up to +1 bit/symbol | 1 lookup | none (stateless) | FIFO | $O(\|\mathcal{A}\|)$ |
| Arithmetic / range | $\approx 2/n$ bits/symbol | ~2 mul/div + renorm | 2 registers | FIFO | $O(\|\mathcal{A}\|)$ |
| rANS | $\approx D(p\\|q) + O(1/n)$ | 1 div + lookup + mul-add | 1 integer | LIFO | $O(M + \|\mathcal{A}\|)$ |
| tANS / FSE | $\approx D(p\\|q) + \epsilon_{\mathrm{spread}}$ | **1 lookup** | 1 finite state | LIFO | $O(L + \|\mathcal{A}\|)$ |

### 4.3 Symbol spread: the central optimization problem

The tANS spread — which states belong to which symbol — is the variant's defining degree of freedom and its main source of redundancy beyond quantization.

Duda's 2021 analysis [6] shows that a **tuned spread** — sorting the approximate transition costs $p_s \log(1 + 1/i)$ against the stationary ansatz $\rho^0_x \approx \log(e)/x$ — measurably beats the pseudorandom spread, and that iterating the procedure (recomputing the true stationary distribution of the tuned automaton and re-sorting) converges toward a local optimum.

Recent theory has put tANS on rigorous footing: the *Efficiency of ANS Entropy Encoders* paper establishes tight asymptotic upper and lower bounds on tANS redundancy in terms of **empirical entropy**, without randomized source assumptions — worst-case guarantees, not just average-case [5].

### 4.4 The binary family: ABS, uABS, rABS

Before rANS and tANS there was **ABS** — Asymmetric *Binary* Systems — the minimal case of a two-symbol alphabet $\{0, 1\}$ with $P(1) = p$.

### 4.5 ANS in production: Zstandard and JPEG XL

**Zstandard.** Yann Collet's FSE [7] is tANS with an accuracy log AL (typically 4–9 bits, so $L = 16$–$512$ states) and two interleaved decoder states sharing one distribution table — the interleaving hides the bit-reader latency that would otherwise serialize the single-lookup loop [11]. In the Zstd block format, FSE entropy-codes the three **sequence streams** (literal lengths, match lengths, offsets) of every LZ77 block, while the Huffman weights of the literal coder are themselves FSE-compressed when the direct 4-bit representation is insufficient [8]. The format stores each FSE stream's initial state up front (AL bits, read first), then decodes back-to-front — the LIFO discipline made concrete in a bitstream. The result is a compressor that decompresses at multiple GB/s while matching or beating the ratios of traditional DEFLATE-family codecs, and FSE's tiny tables (a few KB) keep it cache-friendly at every level.

**JPEG XL.** The JPEG XL codestream (ISO/IEC 18181) specifies its entropy coding in Annex D, offering per-channel a choice of entropy coder: `0` = MABEGABRAC (binary arithmetic variant), `1` = MABrotli, `2` = **MAANS** — an rANS-based coder with meta-adaptive context modeling [9][10]. The reference encoder's strategy is telling: ANS for long streams where its near-optimal rate pays off, prefix (Huffman) codes for short streams where table overhead would dominate — the standard explicitly blesses this hybrid, LZ77-enabled design. JPEG XL's lossless mode, which consistently outperforms PNG/WebP-lossless/FLIF in independent benchmarks [10], leans heavily on MAANS-coded residuals from its self-correcting weighted predictor ensemble.

Beyond these flagships, ANS has quietly colonized infrastructure: Apple's **LZFSE** uses FSE/tANS as its entropy stage; Google's **Draco** 3D mesh codec entropy-codes attributes with rANS; and the genomics **CRAM** format (via htslib's `rans_static`) compresses sequencing quality scores with rANS — a striking case of information theory serving biology.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Proofs

### 5.1 The redundancy theorem, sketched

Consider a stationary ergodic source with true distribution $p$ encoded under a quantized model $f_s/M$. Each encoded symbol $s$ multiplies the state by $M/f_s$, appending $\log_2(M/f_s)$ bits; each renormalization emits exactly the bits the state can no longer hold. Summing over a block of $n$ symbols and dividing:

$$\bar{\ell}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \log_2 \frac{M}{f_{x_i}} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} \sum_s p_s \log_2 \frac{M}{f_s} = H(p) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \parallel q)$$

almost surely, where $q_s = f_s/M$. The $O(1/n)$ term absorbs the initial/final state overhead — the celebrated "two extra bits" style bound familiar from arithmetic coding. In words: **ANS's redundancy decomposes exactly into model mismatch $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)$ plus quantization error plus a vanishing per-block term.** There is no structural redundancy from the coding method itself — the interleaving construction is information-theoretically tight, which is why the method's rate matches arithmetic coding to within measurement noise [2][5].

For tANS, the argument refines: the automaton's Markov chain over states has a stationary distribution $\rho$, and the mean per-symbol cost is the $\rho$-expected transition cost. The worst-case analysis of [5] bounds this by the *empirical* entropy of the encoded sequence plus terms vanishing in $L$ — notably without assuming the source is random at all.

> **Theorem (tANS worst-case redundancy, informal [5]):** For any input sequence, the tANS encoder with $L = 2^R$ states produces a bitstream of length at most $n \cdot H_{\mathrm{emp}} + O(n/R) + O(R)$ bits, where $H_{\mathrm{emp}}$ is the empirical entropy of the sequence. As $R \to \infty$, the per-symbol redundancy vanishes uniformly over inputs.

### 5.2 Rate–throughput landscape

The following summarizes the published record (rates relative to Shannon entropy on standard corpora; throughputs on circa-2020 x86-64 cores):

| Coder | Rate vs. entropy | Decode throughput | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Huffman (canonical, tabled) | +0.01–1.0 bit/sym (distribution-dependent) | ~1–3 GB/s | Dyadic barrier dominates on skewed data |

## 6. Limitations

**The LIFO discipline is the fundamental constraint.** ANS decodes in reverse, so the encoder must buffer or reverse its input, and — more profoundly — the method composes awkwardly with **autoregressive models**, where each symbol's distribution depends on previously *decoded* symbols: under LIFO, "previously decoded" means "later in the original order," forcing the model to run backwards [4]. Arithmetic coding's FIFO queue is the natural fit there. Conversely, ANS's stack discipline is the natural fit for **latent-variable models** (bits-back coding), where decoding a latent sample *before* the data it explains is exactly what the mathematics wants [4]. The choice of entropy coder thus constrains the choice of probabilistic model — a coupling with no analogue in the Huffman era.

**Quantization and model cost.** Frequencies must be quantized to $f_s/M$ with $M$ a power of two, injecting quantization error on top of model mismatch; small alphabets and tiny blocks suffer disproportionately, since the table/state overhead ($O(L)$ entries, the stored initial state) amortizes poorly. This is why JPEG XL falls back to prefix codes for short streams [9].

**Error propagation.** ANS has a strong **avalanche property**: a single flipped bit desynchronizes the state irreversibly, and decoding cannot resynchronize — there are no codeword boundaries to catch [10]. Stream codes share this weakness with arithmetic coding, but ANS's dense state packing makes it total: the corrupted suffix is cryptographically indistinguishable from random. Applications needing error resilience must layer it on top (or, intriguingly, exploit the redundancy the other way — Duda's error-correction variant [1]).

**Encoder cost and asymmetry.** rANS encoding performs divisions and per-symbol threshold computations, making it several times slower than decoding; tANS encoding requires building spread tables. Workloads needing symmetric speed (e.g., in-memory database page compression on the write path) may prefer Huffman or LZ-only schemes.

**Patent and ecosystem friction.** Although Duda published in 2009, the subsequent thicket of ANS-related patent filings created years of licensing uncertainty that slowed adoption in standards bodies — a cautionary tale about the interaction of open research and intellectual-property strategy.

## 7. Conclusion

Asymmetric Numeral Systems resolved a tension that had structured the field of entropy coding for forty years: the apparent necessity of choosing between Huffman's speed and arithmetic coding's rate. The resolution turned out to be conceptual rather than incremental — replacing the *interval* with a *single integer state*, and replacing *subdivision* with *interleaving* — and its consequences are still unfolding. Theoretically, ANS completed the taxonomy of optimal stream codes and supplied the cleanest known interface (push/pop) between probabilistic models and bits, catalyzing the modern bits-back compression of latent-variable generative models [4]. Practically, it became the entropy-coding substrate of Zstandard and JPEG XL, two of the most consequential codec deployments of the last decade [8][9].

Open problems remain genuinely open. **Optimal symbol spread** for tANS has no known polynomial-time exact algorithm — the tuned-spread heuristics of [6] are effective but not provably optimal, and the problem's complexity class is unsettled. **Adaptive ANS**, which updates frequencies on the fly without rebuilding tables, lacks the elegant rescaling story of adaptive arithmetic coding. **Hardware ANS** — tabled automata map beautifully to FPGAs and ASICs, yet no major hardware codec has shipped a tANS block. And the **LIFO/FIFO divide** continues to shape the co-design of models and coders, with ANS on the stack side and arithmetic coding holding the queue. Duda's construction began as a curiosity about asymmetric digits; it ended as infrastructure. The next decade will determine how much further the stack can go.
