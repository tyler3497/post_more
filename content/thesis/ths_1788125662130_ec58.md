This thesis presents a comprehensive investigation into robot motion planning in high-dimensional c-space, addressing foundational challenges in robot motion planning in high-dimensional c-space through synthesis of recent advances in cryptographic protocols, systems architecture, and machine learning efficiency. We formalize the problem setting, survey evolution from early constructions to state-of-the-art deployments, and introduce novel analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Our contributions include taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal security reductions, and empirical evaluation methodologies that bridge theory and practice. We demonstrate that Robot Motion Planning in High-Dimensional C-Space achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open challenges in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future research. Experimental results across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for production-grade deployment in cloud and edge environments.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

*Limitations*:

- **Trusted Setup**: ALTRO: Augmented Lagrangian Trajectory Optimizer for Motion Planning requires SRS generation; although updatable via ceremony, it remains trust assumption. Future work explores transparent setups via FRI [7].

- **Parameter Tuning**: Noise budget, batch size, privacy budget require manual tuning; AutoML for robot motion planning in high-dimensional c-space is open.

1. **Post-quantum migration**: Replace KZG (pairing-based, quantum-vulnerable) with lattice-based commitments (e.g., Brakedown)
