# Diffusion Generative Models and Geometric Deep Learning in Protein Structure Prediction: From Evoformer to AlphaFold3

## Abstract

Protein structure prediction has undergone a methodological revolution over the past half-decade, transitioning from contact-based coevolutionary analysis to end-to-end differentiable architectures and, most recently, to diffusion-based generative models operating on raw atom coordinates. This thesis develops a unified theoretical account of the modern pipeline: the Evoformer and Pairformer attention architectures that distill evolutionary couplings into pairwise representations; the structure module and its invariant point attention (IPA) mechanism, which realize SE(3)-equivariant coordinate updates from learned attention weights; the diffusion generative paradigm that recasts structure prediction as conditional denoising on the manifold of atomic configurations, instantiated in RFdiffusion for de novo design and AlphaFold3's diffusion head for joint biomolecular prediction; and the single-sequence language-model alternative exemplified by ESMFold. We prove the equivariance properties of IPA, analyze the score-based training objectives of RFdiffusion and AlphaFold3, compare MSA-dependent and MSA-free regimes, and formalize the confidence metrics (pLDDT, PAE) that calibrate predictive uncertainty. We conclude with a critical assessment of open limitations: conformational dynamics, intrinsically disordered regions, and the gap between a single predicted structure and the thermodynamic ensemble it approximates.

## 1 Introduction

The three-dimensional fold of a protein encodes its function: catalytic mechanism, binding specificity, and allosteric regulation are geometric facts about atomic arrangements. For fifty years, from Anfinsen's demonstration that sequence determines structure [2], the community has pursued computational prediction of this mapping. Two waves of machine learning produced step changes. The first exploited *coevolutionary signals* in multiple sequence alignments (MSAs): correlated mutations at distant sequence positions betray spatial proximity, a principle operationalized by direct-coupling analysis and, later, deep residual networks predicting contact and distance distributions [3].

The second wave, initiated by AlphaFold2 (AF2) at CASP14, replaced the pipeline with an end-to-end differentiable architecture coupling an attention-based representation learner — the *Evoformer* — to a geometrically principled *structure module* with invariant point attention [2]. The third wave, now cresting, replaces deterministic coordinate regression with *diffusion generative modeling*: RFdiffusion (2023) fine-tuned the RoseTTAFold network on structure denoising to generate designable backbones from noise [3], and AlphaFold3 (AF3, 2024) discarded the structure module entirely in favor of a diffusion head that predicts the joint coordinates of proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions, and modified residues [1].

> **Thesis claim.** Modern protein structure prediction is best understood as *equivariant generative modeling on the configuration manifold* of atoms: attention mechanisms learn interaction graphs in the sequence and pair domains, SE(3)-equivariant operators translate these into coordinate updates that respect the symmetries of physical space, and diffusion objectives train the model as a denoiser over physically plausible conformations. Confidence metrics emerge as calibrated summaries of the posterior over structures.

This thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 reviews the background: the folding problem, coevolution, geometric deep learning, and diffusion models. Section 3 develops the methodology: Evoformer/Pairformer attention, IPA and the structure module, diffusion training objectives, and single-sequence models. Section 4 conducts a deep dive into equivariance theory, diffusion-on-frames formalisms, confidence calibration, and protein–ligand co-folding. Section 5 presents empirical results and proofs. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The protein folding problem

A protein is a sequence *s = (s₁, ..., s_L)* over an alphabet of 20 amino acids.

Key structural priors include:

- **Local rigidity.** Bond lengths and angles are approximately fixed; degrees of freedom reduce to backbone dihedrals (*φ*, *ψ*, *ω*). Each residue defines a rigid frame in SE(3), the special Euclidean group of rotations and translations.
- **Contact sparsity.** Only a small fraction of residue pairs are in contact, yet these determine the fold. MSA coevolution detects them statistically.
- **Frame semantics.** Under a global rigid motion *g ∈ SE(3)*, a predicted structure must transform identically: the network output must be *equivariant*, while scalar predictions (e.g., confidence) must be *invariant*. This is the central constraint of geometric deep learning.

### 2.2 From coevolution to attention

Direct-coupling analysis (DCA) modeled MSA columns with Potts models; precision matrices of the fitted coupling matrix revealed contacts.

### 2.3 Geometric deep learning

Geometric deep learning studies architectures that respect the symmetries of their domains [4][5].

1. **Scalarization methods (e.g., EGNN).** Node coordinates **xᵢ** are updated by radial sums of invariant messages: **xᵢ' = xᵢ + Σ_{j≠i}(xᵢ − xⱼ) φₓ(mᵢⱼ)** where *mᵢⱼ* depends only on invariant quantities (distances, features) [5]. Equivariance follows because relative vectors rotate with the frame.
2. **Steerable methods.** Features are typed by irreducible representations of SO(3) (spherical harmonics), and message passing uses Clebsch–Gordan tensor products; exemplars include Tensor Field Networks, SEGNN, NequIP/MACE, and the Equiformer, which interleaves equivariant self-attention with steerable non-linear updates [5][6].

AF2's structure module belongs to the first family in spirit: IPA computes attention weights from invariant features, then aggregates *points* expressed in per-residue local frames — a construction whose equivariance we prove in Section 5.

### 2.4 Diffusion generative models

Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) define a forward noising process *q*(**x**ₜ | **x**₀) adding Gaussian noise over *T* steps, and learn a reverse denoiser *p_θ*(**x**_{t−1} | **x**ₜ) parameterized as a score estimator [3].

```
L = E_{t, x₀, ε}[ || ε_θ(√ᾱₜ x₀ + √(1−ᾱₜ) ε, t) − ε ||² ]
```

i.e., the network learns to predict the noise added to a corrupted sample, which is equivalent to learning the score ∇ log *p*ₜ(**x**).

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Evoformer: attention over evolution

The Evoformer maintains two representations: the MSA stack *m_{si} ∈ ℝ^{cₘ}* (sequence *s*, position *i*) and the pair representation *z_{ij} ∈ ℝ^{c_z}* (positions *i*, *j*).

1. **Row-wise gated self-attention** with pair bias: attention logits for MSA row *s* are biased by a projection of *z_{ij}*, so pair information modulates evolutionary attention and vice versa.
2. **Column-wise gated self-attention** across the *s* dimension, mixing homolog information at each position.
3. **MSA→pair outer product mean**: the MSA stack is projected and its outer product averaged over sequences updates *z*.
4. **Triangle multiplicative update and triangle attention**: these enforce that *z_{ij}* is consistent over triples *(i, j, k)* — the discrete analogue of metric transitivity. Triangle attention comes in "starting node" and "ending node" variants, attending over the shared index.

After 48 blocks, *z* encodes a learned, geometry-consistent pairwise potential.

> **Theorem: Triangle consistency.** Let *d_{ij}* be distances implied by *z_{ij}* under an optimal decoder. Triangle multiplicative updates bias the representation toward satisfaction of the triangle inequality *d_{ij} ≤ d_{ik} + d_{kj}* for all triples, since the update *z_{ij} ← z_{ij} + Σ_k (a_{ik} ⊙ b_{kj})* aggregates paths through *k*, penalizing representations for which the direct estimate contradicts every indirect estimate.

### 3.2 The structure module and invariant point attention

The structure module maps *(sᵢ, z_{ij})* to backbone frames **Tᵢ = (Rᵢ, tᵢ) ∈ SE(3)**.

**Invariant Point Attention.** For residue *i* with frame **Tᵢ**, IPA computes per-head queries, keys, values as usual from *sᵢ*, but additionally generates *query points* **qᵢ^{hp} ∈ ℝ³** (in the local frame of *i*), *key points* **kⱼ^{hp}** (local frame of *j*), and *value points* **vⱼ^{hp}**. The attention weight is

```
aᵢⱼ^h = softmax_j( w_L ( (1/√c) qᵢ^{h⊤} kⱼ^h + bᵢⱼ^h  −  (γ^h w_C / 2) Σ_p || Tᵢ ∘ qᵢ^{hp} − Tⱼ ∘ kⱼ^{hp} ||² ) )
```

where *bᵢⱼ^h* is a scalar bias from *z_{ij}*.

```
oᵢ^{hp} = Tᵢ⁻¹ ∘ Σⱼ aᵢⱼ^h ( Tⱼ ∘ vⱼ^{hp} )
```

which, composed with the frame update, yields equivariant coordinate predictions.

**Frame updates.** After IPA, *sᵢ* is updated by attention output; a backbone-update network predicts a residual rigid motion *(ΔRᵢ, Δtᵢ)* applied as **Tᵢ ← Tᵢ ∘ (ΔRᵢ, Δtᵢ)**. Atom positions derive from ideal geometry placed in frames, then FAPE (frame-aligned point error) loss clamps predictions: FAPE computes atom errors in each residue's local frame and clamps at 10 Å, providing a rotation/translation-invariant, robust training signal. Auxiliary losses (distogram, masked-MSA, confidence heads) regularize.

### 3.3 AlphaFold3: the diffusion head

AF3 (2024) restructures the pipeline substantially [1]:

- **Input featurization.** A token-based scheme: each residue, each ligand atom, each nucleic acid base, and each ion becomes a token; MSAs, templates, and chemical graphs provide features.
- **Pairformer** replaces the Evoformer: it drops the MSA stack's column-wise machinery in favor of a lighter pair-biased transformer over the pair representation, while a separate, smaller MSA module supplies evolutionary features. This reduces compute while retaining accuracy.
- **Diffusion module replaces the structure module.** Instead of IPA-driven frame refinement, AF3 trains a diffusion model over raw atom coordinates conditioned on the Pairformer representations. The forward process adds noise to coordinates; the denoiser is a transformer operating on noisy atom positions with conditioning from pair/single representations. At inference, sampling starts from Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises — directly generating 3D coordinates for *all* atoms, including ligands and nucleic acids, with no residue-frame parameterization.

The diffusion objective handles variable chemistry uniformly: since coordinates are just points, a small molecule is represented no differently than a protein chain at the generative level, enabling a single model over all of biomolecular space [1].

Notably, AF3 predicts confidence heads (pLDDT per atom, PAE, predicted distance errors) *from the diffusion module's internal states*, and samples can be ranked or averaged.

### 3.4 RFdiffusion: diffusion for design

RFdiffusion (2023) takes the complementary direction: it *generates* proteins rather than predicting them [3].

1. **Self-conditioning.** Inspired by AF2's recycling, the model is conditioned on its own previous-step prediction *X̂₀*, dramatically improving sample quality [3].
2. **Frame-anchored MSE loss.** Training with mean-squared error in the *global* frame (not invariant) promotes continuity between diffusion timesteps, since successive *X̂₀* predictions stay aligned [3].
3. **Controllability.** Conditioning inputs specify topology, symmetry, motif scaffolds, or binder targets; the model generates backbones satisfying these constraints.

RFdiffusion achieved outstanding performance across unconditional monomer design, binder design, symmetric oligomer design, and enzyme active-site scaffolding, with experimental validation: hundreds of designs characterized, including a cryo-EM structure of an influenza haemagglutinin binder matching the design at near-atomic resolution [3].

### 3.5 Single-sequence models: ESMFold

MSA construction is the dominant computational cost of AF2/AF3 and fails for orphan proteins and designed sequences with no homologs.

Performance is slightly below AF2 on CASP-style benchmarks but inference is an order of magnitude faster, enabling the ESM Metagenomic Atlas of over 617 million predicted metagenomic structures [4].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The geometry of frames: SE(3) and protein backbones

A rigid body in 3D is an element of SE(3) = SO(3) ⋉ ℝ³.

Two parameterizations dominate:

| Aspect | Frame-based (AF2, RFdiffusion) | Coordinate-based (AF3) |
|---|---|---|
| State space | SE(3)^L (one frame per residue) | ℝ^{N×3} (all atoms) |
| Symmetry | Exact equivariance by construction | Approximate; learned via augmentation |
| Ligands / nucleic acids | Awkward (residue frames undefined) | Natural (atoms are atoms) |
| Diffusion target | Frames with SO(3) Brownian motion | Gaussian noise on coordinates |
| Physical priors | Bond geometry hard-coded | Learned from data |
| Hallucination risk | Low (ideal geometry enforced) | Higher (paper notes mitigation needed) [1] |

> **Theorem (Frame composition).** The map from torsion angles *(φᵢ, ψᵢ)* to global frames via **Tᵢ = Tᵢ₋₁ · M(φᵢ, ψᵢ)** with fixed bond-geometry matrices *M* is a diffeomorphism onto the manifold of ideal-geometry backbones, and is SE(3)-equivariant: left-multiplying **T₀** by *g ∈ SE(3)* left-multiplies every **Tᵢ** by *g*. Hence any frame-parameterized predictor with ideal geometry is automatically free of global-pose artifacts.

### 4.2 Equivariance of invariant point attention

We now prove that IPA, and therefore the AF2 structure module, is SE(3)-equivariant.

> **Theorem (IPA equivariance).** Let a global rigid motion *g = (R_g, t_g) ∈ SE(3)* act on frames as **Tᵢ ↦ g ∘ Tᵢ** and leave scalar features *(sᵢ, z_{ij})* invariant. Then the IPA attention weights *aᵢⱼ^h* are invariant, and the aggregated output points transform as **oᵢ^{hp} ↦ g ∘ oᵢ^{hp}**, i.e., IPA is SE(3)-equivariant.

*Proof sketch.* The scalar term *qᵢ^{h⊤} kⱼ^h* and bias *bᵢⱼ^h* depend only on invariant features. The spatial term involves *||Tᵢ ∘ qᵢ^{hp} − Tⱼ ∘ kⱼ^{hp}||²*; under *g*, each argument is left-multiplied by the rigid motion, and Euclidean distance is preserved, so the term — and hence *aᵢⱼ^h* — is invariant. The output *oᵢ^{hp} = Tᵢ⁻¹ ∘ Σⱼ aᵢⱼ^h (Tⱼ ∘ vⱼ^{hp})*: inserting *g*, we get *(gTᵢ)⁻¹ ∘ Σⱼ aᵢⱼ^h (gTⱼ ∘ vⱼ^{hp}) = Tᵢ⁻¹ g⁻¹ g ∘ (Σⱼ ...) = g ∘ oᵢ^{hp}*. The subsequent frame update predicts *(ΔRᵢ, Δtᵢ)* from invariant *sᵢ*, applied by right-composition **Tᵢ ← Tᵢ ∘ (ΔRᵢ, Δtᵢ)**, which commutes with left action of *g*. By induction over the 8 blocks, the whole structure module is equivariant. ∎

This argument is the workhorse behind AF2's generalization: the network cannot memorize absolute coordinates, so all capacity goes into learning *relative* geometry.

### 4.3 Diffusion on frames versus diffusion on coordinates

RFdiffusion diffuses the translational component in ℝ³ (standard Gaussian diffusion) and the rotational component on SO(3) via the IGSO(3) heat kernel, with a variance schedule tuned so that late-stage noise dominates global orientation while early-stage noise corrupts local geometry [3].

AF3's coordinate diffusion is conceptually simpler but subtler in practice: pure Gaussian diffusion on ℝ^{N×3} has no notion of chain connectivity, so the denoiser must *learn* covalent geometry from data.

```python
# Score-based denoising step, coordinate diffusion (AF3-style, schematic)
import torch

def denoise_step(x_t, t, conditioner, sigma_schedule):
    """One reverse-diffusion step on atom coordinates."""
    sigma_t = sigma_schedule[t]
    # Denoiser predicts the clean structure x0 from noisy x_t
    x0_hat = conditioner.denoiser(x_t, t)          # Pairformer-conditioned
    # Score of the perturbation kernel q(x_t | x0)
    score = (x0_hat - x_t) / (sigma_t ** 2)
    # Euler-Maruyama step of the reverse SDE
    x_prev = x_t + (sigma_t**2) * score * dt + sigma_t * torch.randn_like(x_t) * (dt ** 0.5)
    return x_prev, x0_hat
```

### 4.4 Confidence: pLDDT and PAE

A predicted structure without uncertainty quantification is scientifically unusable.

- **pLDDT** (predicted local distance difference test): per-residue (AF3: per-atom) score in the unit interval estimating the fraction of preserved local distances; >90 indicates very high confidence, 70–90 confident backbone, 50–70 low, <50 likely disordered. The network is trained to regress binned lDDT-Cα against ground truth.
- **PAE** (predicted aligned error): an *L×L* matrix where PAE_{ij} estimates the expected positional error of residue *i* when the prediction is aligned on residue *j*. Low PAE blocks reveal rigid domains; high off-diagonal PAE flags uncertain inter-domain orientations — often the biologically interesting signal.

> **Theorem (Calibration sketch).** If the confidence head is trained with cross-entropy against binned true errors on a representative validation distribution, and the structure head's error distribution is stationary across that distribution, then E[true lDDT | pLDDT = v] ≈ v: the head is calibrated in expectation. In practice, calibration degrades out-of-distribution (novel folds, low-MSA-depth targets), where pLDDT is systematically overconfident — a fact that motivates the conservative interpretation of low-confidence regions as disorder or flexibility rather than error [2].

### 4.5 Protein–ligand co-folding and induced fit

Classical docking separates receptor (fixed) from ligand (flexible), scoring poses with empirical force fields — a factorization that fails when binding remodels the pocket.

The deeper principle is that diffusion models learn an *energy landscape*: the score ∇ log *p*ₜ(**x**) approximates the gradient of a learned pseudo-energy, and denoising trajectories descend toward its minima.

### 5.2 Theoretical results

We collect the results proved or sketched in this thesis:

1. **Triangle consistency (Section 3.1).** Triangle multiplicative updates bias the pair representation toward metric-consistent geometries, providing a differentiable relaxation of transitive closure over residue contacts.
2. **IPA equivariance (Section 4.2).** Invariant attention weights plus frame-local point aggregation yield exact SE(3)-equivariance of the structure module; the network provably cannot exploit absolute coordinates.
3. **Frame diffeomorphism (Section 4.1).** Torsion-to-frame composition is a diffeomorphism onto ideal-geometry backbones and is SE(3)-equivariant, justifying the frame parameterization as a symmetry-respecting chart of the configuration manifold.
4. **Diffusion designability (Section 3.4).** Self-conditioning plus frame-anchored MSE loss yields continuity across denoising timesteps; empirically, this is the difference between RFdiffusion's ~20% experimental success and the near-zero rates of unconditioned coordinate diffusion [3].
5. **Calibration (Section 4.4).** Confidence heads trained against binned true errors are calibrated in-distribution; out-of-distribution overconfidence is the dominant failure mode of uncertainty quantification.

```rust
// Frame-aligned point error (FAPE), schematic: invariant, clamped, robust
fn fape(pred_frames: &[Frame], true_frames: &[Frame],
        pred_atoms: &[Vec3], true_atoms: &[Vec3], clamp: f32) -> f32 {
    let mut err = 0.0;
    let mut n = 0;
    for (i, (tf, pf)) in true_frames.iter().zip(pred_frames).enumerate() {
        // express both predictions in the TRUE local frame of residue i
        for j in 0..pred_atoms.len() {
            let p_local = pf.inverse().act(pred_atoms[j]);
            let t_local = tf.inverse().act(true_atoms[j]);
            err += (p_local - t_local).norm().min(clamp);
            n += 1;
        }
        let _ = i;
    }
    err / n as f32
}
```

## 6 Limitations

**Conformational dynamics.** AF2/AF3 predict a single structure — the mode of a learned conditional distribution — while proteins populate *ensembles*: apo/holo transitions, allosteric rearrangements, and folding intermediates. The network has no temperature, no kinetics, and no notion of free energy; pLDDT variation across residues is sometimes misread as dynamics, but it quantifies *epistemic* uncertainty, not thermal fluctuation. Diffusion models sample *a* distribution over structures, but the learned distribution is not the Boltzmann distribution, and sampling temperature is a heuristic, not a physical parameter.

**Intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs).** Roughly a third of the human proteome contains disordered segments. AF2 renders these as low-pLDDT "spaghetti" — arguably the correct *honest* output, since no single conformation exists. But the field still lacks a validated generative model of IDR *ensembles* matched to NMR/SAXS observables; diffusion models trained on PDB structures inherit the PDB's bias toward the crystallizable and the ordered.

**Hallucination and physicality.** AF3's coordinate diffusion can emit plausible-but-impossible chemistry — violated stereochemistry, unphysical ligand geometries — because covalent priors are learned rather than enforced [1]. The paper's mitigation (targeted data augmentation) is empirical, not guaranteed. Frame-based models avoid this by construction but cannot represent arbitrary chemistry.

**MSA dependence and data bias.** AF3 still benefits from MSAs; orphan proteins, designed sequences, and viral antigens under rapid evolution remain hard. The PDB itself is biased: overrepresented folds, crystallization artifacts, and the near-absence of membrane-protein conformational diversity bound what any supervised model can learn.

**Evaluation gaps.** DockQ, lDDT, and GDT measure geometric similarity to a reference, not functional correctness. A binder predicted at 1 Å RMSD may still fail experimentally due to expression, solubility, or off-target effects — the gap RFdiffusion's extensive wet-lab validation was designed to close [3].

## 7 Conclusion

Protein structure prediction has converged on a coherent mathematical picture: *attention learns the interaction graph, equivariant operators realize it in 3D, diffusion objectives train the generator, and confidence heads calibrate the posterior.* The Evoformer's triangle attention made pairwise geometry learnable; invariant point attention made coordinate prediction exactly equivariant; RFdiffusion showed that a structure predictor, fine-tuned as a denoiser on SE(3) frames, becomes a design engine with experimental hit rates two orders of magnitude above prior art; ESMFold showed that a large enough sequence model internalizes the evolutionary information MSAs provide; and AlphaFold3 unified the entire biomolecular menagerie — proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions — under a single diffusion head operating on raw coordinates [1][2][3][4].

The frontier is no longer *a* structure but the *ensemble*: dynamics, disorder, allostery, and the kinetics of binding. Diffusion models are the natural vehicle — they already define distributions, not points — but aligning the learned distribution with the Boltzmann ensemble, and validating against biophysical observables rather than static references, remains open. The next revolution will be thermodynamic.
