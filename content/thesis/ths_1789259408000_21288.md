# Score-Based Generative Models: Stochastic Differential Equations, Annealed Langevin Dynamics, and the Mathematics of Diffusion

## Abstract


Score-based generative models have displaced adversarial training as the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity synthesis across images, audio, and video. This article develops the complete mathematical arc: Hyvärinen's score matching and Vincent's denoising score matching, which reduce density estimation to regressing the gradient of the log-density; the noise-conditioning trick and annealed Langevin dynamics of Noise Conditional Score Networks; and the stochastic differential equation unification that casts progressive noising as a continuous-time diffusion, its exact time reversal as the generative process, and the probability flow ordinary differential equation as a deterministic twin sharing identical marginals. We derive the reverse-time SDE, recover Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models as a discretization of the variance-preserving SDE, analyze predictor–corrector samplers, exact likelihood computation through the instantaneous change of variables, classifier and classifier-free guidance, and consistency-model distillation toward single-step generation. Empirical landmarks from CIFAR-10 to ImageNet-64 are tabulated against the theory's predictions, and we close with the framework's open limitations: sampling cost, manifold mismatch, and the likelihood–quality gap.

## 1 Introduction


By 2019, deep generative modeling was split into two unsatisfying camps. Likelihood-based models — autoregressive networks, variational autoencoders, normalizing flows — offered tractable densities and stable training but produced blurry samples. Generative adversarial networks produced stunning samples but no likelihood, unstable minimax training, and mode collapse. Score-based modeling proposed a third axis: instead of learning the density $p(x)$ itself, learn its *score*, the gradient of the log-density $\nabla_x \log p(x)$, and generate samples by simulating Langevin dynamics driven by that score [1].

The score has one decisive advantage over the density: it is blind to the partition function. For an energy-based model $p(x) \propto \exp(-E(x))$, the score $\nabla_x \log p(x) = -\nabla_x E(x)$ never touches the intractable normalizer [1]. This single observation converts the hardest part of probabilistic modeling — normalization — into a non-problem.

Two obstacles blocked the path to competitive samples. First, the *manifold hypothesis*: real data concentrates near a low-dimensional manifold, where the score is ill-defined or unlearnable from finite data. Second, *mixing*: Langevin dynamics traverses low-density regions between modes at a glacial pace. Song and Ermon's Noise Conditional Score Network (NCSN) dissolved both with one mechanism — perturb the data with a geometric ladder of Gaussian noises, train a single network $s_\theta(x, \sigma)$ to estimate every noise level's score jointly, and sample with *annealed* Langevin dynamics that descends the ladder [3]. Ho, Jain, and Abbeel then showed the same idea as a variational Markov chain with a disarmingly simple objective: predict the injected noise [6]. Finally, Song et al. unified both lineages under stochastic differential equations: forward noising as a continuous-time diffusion, generation as its exact time reversal, and a deterministic probability flow ODE sharing the same marginals [7].

This article derives the entire framework from first principles, connects its discrete and continuous formulations, and surveys what a decade of theory predicts — and where it still falls short.

## 2 Background


### Score matching

Given i.i.d. samples from an unknown $p_{data}$, score matching fits a model score $s_\theta(x)$ by minimizing the Fisher divergence to the true score [1]:

$$J(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{p_{data}(x)}\big[\lVert s_\theta(x) - \nabla_x \log p_{data}(x)\rVert^2\big].$$

The true score is unknown, but integration by parts eliminates it. Under mild regularity:

$$J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p_{data}(x)}\big[\mathrm{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x)) + \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert s_\theta(x)\rVert^2\big] + \mathrm{const},$$

which depends only on data and the model [1]. The catch is the Jacobian trace: exact evaluation costs $O(d^2)$ per point in dimension $d$, which killed the method for high-dimensional data until denoising score matching arrived.

### Denoising score matching

Vincent showed that perturbing data with a known kernel $q_\sigma(\tilde{x} \mid x)$ and matching the *conditional* score yields an objective equivalent to matching the score of the perturbed marginal $q_\sigma(\tilde{x}) = \int q_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x)p_{data}(x)\,dx$ [2]:

> **Theorem (Denoising score matching).** For Gaussian perturbation $q_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\tilde{x}; x, \sigma^2 I)$, minimizing $\mathbb{E}[\lVert s_\theta(\tilde{x}) - \nabla_{\tilde{x}}\log q_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x)\rVert^2]$ is equivalent (up to a constant) to minimizing the Fisher divergence between $s_\theta$ and $\nabla \log q_\sigma(\tilde{x})$, with the closed-form target $-(\tilde{x}-x)/\sigma^2$.

> **Proof.** Expand the Fisher divergence to $q_\sigma(\tilde{x})$; the cross-term $\mathbb{E}_{q_\sigma}[\langle s_\theta(\tilde{x}), \nabla\log q_\sigma(\tilde{x})\rangle]$ factorizes through the joint $q_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x)p_{data}(x)$, where $\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\log q_\sigma(\tilde{x}\mid x) = -(\tilde{x}-x)/\sigma^2$ is known analytically. ∎

No Jacobian trace, no partition function — just regression against Gaussian noise. This is the workhorse beneath everything that follows [2].

### Langevin dynamics and its two failures

With a learned score, samples come from (overdamped) Langevin dynamics:

$$x_{i+1} = x_i + \tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\,\nabla_x\log p(x_i) + \sqrt{\epsilon}\,z_i, \qquad z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),$$

which converges to $p$ as $\epsilon \to 0$, $i \to \infty$ under standard conditions [1]. In practice it fails twice: on a low-dimensional data manifold the true score is undefined and the estimated score points nowhere useful, and even with a perfect score the chain mixes exponentially slowly across the near-empty voids between modes. Both failures are cured simultaneously by *annealing* over noise scales — the subject of the next section [3].

## 3 Methodology


### The noise-conditioning trick

NCSN trains one network on a geometric ladder $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2 > \cdots > \sigma_L$ (e.g., $\sigma_1 = 1$ down to $\sigma_L = 0.01$ with $L = 10$), minimizing the weighted sum [3]:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{L} \lambda(\sigma_i)\,\mathbb{E}_{p_{data}(x)}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\sigma_i^2 I)}\Big[\big\lVert s_\theta(\tilde{x},\sigma_i) + \tfrac{\tilde{x}-x}{\sigma_i^2}\big\rVert^2\Big], \quad \lambda(\sigma) = \sigma^2.$$

The weighting $\lambda(\sigma)=\sigma^2$ balances gradient magnitudes across scales, since $\lVert(\tilde{x}-x)/\sigma^2\rVert \propto 1/\sigma$ [3]. Large $\sigma$ smears the manifold into a well-behaved, full-support density where scores are learnable; small $\sigma$ recovers fine detail. The network is conditioned on $\sigma$ (or, equivalently, on a continuous time $t$), so one model serves all scales.

### Annealed Langevin dynamics

Sampling proceeds down the ladder. At level $i$, run $T$ Langevin steps with step size $\alpha_i = \epsilon \cdot \sigma_i^2 / \sigma_L^2$, initializing each level from the previous level's final state [3]:

1. Initialize $x$ from a broad Gaussian at the largest noise scale.
2. For $i = 1, \dots, L$: set $\alpha_i = \epsilon\,\sigma_i^2/\sigma_L^2$.
3. For $t = 1, \dots, T$: $x \leftarrow x + \tfrac{\alpha_i}{2}s_\theta(x,\sigma_i) + \sqrt{\alpha_i}\,z$, $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$.
4. Return $x$ as an approximate sample from $p_{data}$.

High-noise stages mix rapidly across modes; low-noise stages refine details.

A minimal implementation on a 2D mixture — where the perturbed score is analytic — shows the mechanism with no neural network at all:

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
centers = np.array([[np.cos(a), np.sin(a)]
                    for a in np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 8, endpoint=False)])
VAR = 0.02  # mixture component variance

def score(x, sigma):
    """Analytic score of the 8-Gaussian mixture convolved with N(0, sigma^2 I)."""
    v = sigma**2 + VAR
    d = ((x[:, None, :] - centers[None, :, :])**2).sum(-1)
    r = np.exp(-d / (2*v)); r /= r.sum(-1, keepdims=True)  # responsibilities
    return (r @ centers - x) / v

def annealed_langevin(n=2000, sigmas=np.geomspace(1.0, 0.01, 10),
                       steps=100, eps=2e-5):
    x = rng.normal(0, sigmas[0], size=(n, 2))
    for sigma in sigmas:
        alpha = eps * (sigma / sigmas[-1])**2
        for _ in range(steps):
            x = x + 0.5*alpha*score(x, sigma) \
                + np.sqrt(alpha)*rng.normal(size=x.shape)
    return x

samples = annealed_langevin()
print(samples.mean(0), samples.std(0))  # ~ [0,0], unit ring recovered
```

### The SDE unification

Song et al. observed that discrete noise ladders are Euler discretizations of a continuous diffusion [7].

$$dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw, \qquad t \in [0,T],$$

with drift $f$, scalar diffusion coefficient $g$, and Wiener process $w$. If $p_t(x)$ is the marginal at time $t$, Anderson's reverse-time theorem gives the exact generative SDE [5][7]:

> **Theorem (Reverse-time diffusion).** Under mild regularity, the time-reversed process satisfies
> $$dx = \big[f(x,t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla_x\log p_t(x)\big]\,dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w},$$
> where $\bar{w}$ is a reverse-time Wiener process. Replacing $\nabla_x\log p_t$ with a learned $s_\theta(x,t)$ yields a generative model whose only unknown is the score.

Even more consequentially, there exists a *deterministic* process — the probability flow ODE — with exactly the same marginals $p_t$ [7]:

$$dx = \Big[f(x,t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2\,\nabla_x\log p_t(x)\Big]\,dt.$$

Setting the diffusion term to zero while halving the score's influence preserves the Fokker–Planck evolution of the marginals.

Three SDE instances cover the literature [7]:

| SDE | Drift $f(x,t)$ | Diffusion $g(t)$ | Discrete ancestor |
|---|---|---|---|
| VP (variance preserving) | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | DDPM [6] |
| VE (variance exploding) | $0$ | $\sqrt{d\sigma^2(t)/dt}$ | NCSN [3] |
| sub-VP | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)(1-e^{-2\int_0^t\beta(s)ds})}$ | — |

Training generalizes denoising score matching to continuous time [7]:

$$\mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,T]}\Big[\lambda(t)\,\mathbb{E}_{x(0)}\mathbb{E}_{x(t)\mid x(0)}\lVert s_\theta(x(t),t) - \nabla_{x(t)}\log p_{0t}(x(t)\mid x(0))\rVert^2\Big],$$

where the Gaussian transition $p_{0t}$ makes the target analytic. With $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$, this objective is (up to constants) a weighted variational bound on the log-likelihood [7].

In Haskell-flavored pseudocode, the framework is three functions and one theorem:

```haskell
-- A diffusion is its drift and diffusion coefficient; the score completes it.
data Diffusion = Diffusion
  { driftF    :: Double -> Vec -> Vec   -- f(x, t)
  , diffuseG  :: Double -> Double       -- g(t)
  , scoreNet  :: Double -> Vec -> Vec   -- s_theta(x, t)
  }

reverseSDE :: Diffusion -> Double -> Vec -> Vec
reverseSDE d t x =
  driftF d t x ^-^ scale (diffuseG d t ^ 2) (scoreNet d t x)

probFlowODE :: Diffusion -> Double -> Vec -> Vec
probFlowODE d t x =
  driftF d t x ^-^ scale (0.5 * diffuseG d t ^ 2) (scoreNet d t x)
```

## 4 Deep Dive


### 4.1 DDPM as a discretized VP-SDE

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models define a forward Markov chain $q(x_t\mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$ [6]. The chain admits a closed form $q(x_t\mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0, (1-\bar\alpha_t)I)$ with $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s\le t}(1-\beta_s)$, and the reverse chain $p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)$ is learned.

$$L_{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon}\big[\lVert\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\,t)\rVert^2\big].$$

The SDE lens reveals why this works: by Tweedie's formula, $\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t) = -\sigma_t\,s_\theta(x_t,t)$ with $\sigma_t = \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}$ — noise prediction *is* scaled score matching [7]. As the number of steps $T\to\infty$ with $\beta_t$ shrinking appropriately, the DDPM chain converges to the VP-SDE, and its ancestral sampler becomes an Euler–Maruyama discretization of the reverse SDE [7].

### 4.2 The probability flow ODE and exact likelihoods

Because the probability flow ODE is deterministic and invertible, the change-of-variables formula applies along its trajectory.

$$\log p_0(x(0)) = \log p_T(x(T)) + \int_0^T \nabla\!\cdot\!\tilde f_\theta(x(t), t)\,dt,$$

where $x(T)$ is obtained by integrating the ODE forward from $x(0)$ and $p_T$ is the tractable prior [7]. The divergence is estimated without $O(d^2)$ cost via the Skilling–Hutchinson trace estimator, $\nabla\!\cdot\!\tilde f = \mathbb{E}_\epsilon[\epsilon^\top \nabla \tilde f\,\epsilon]$, requiring only vector–Jacobian products [7]. Song et al. reported 2.99 bits/dim on uniformly dequantized CIFAR-10 — competitive with the best autoregressive models — from a model trained purely with score matching [7].

The ODE view also unlocks fast sampling: adaptive solvers (e.g., Dormand–Prince) take large steps where the learned vector field is smooth, and DDIM [8] showed the deterministic limit can be reached from the discrete side too, via a non-Markovian forward process whose reverse step with $\eta = 0$ integrates the same flow.

### 4.3 Guidance: steering the reverse process

Conditional generation follows from Bayes' rule on scores: $\nabla_x\log p_t(x\mid y) = \nabla_x\log p_t(x) + \nabla_x\log p_t(y\mid x)$ [9]. *Classifier guidance* trains a noisy classifier $p_t(y\mid x)$ on perturbed data and injects its gradient, scaled by a guidance strength, into the reverse SDE — the technique behind the first diffusion models to beat GANs on ImageNet [9].

$$\tilde\epsilon_\theta(x_t, y) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\,\big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, y) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big), \qquad w > 1.$$

Pushing $w$ beyond 1 sharpens the implicit classifier $p(y\mid x) \propto p(x\mid y)/p(x)$ and trades diversity for fidelity — the single knob behind modern text-to-image quality [11].

### 4.4 Distillation: from a thousand steps to one

The framework's Achilles' heel is sampling cost — hundreds to thousands of network evaluations per image. Karras et al. attacked this systematically, separating the design space into training, preconditioning, and sampler choices, and reached FID 1.97 unconditional (1.79 class-conditional) on CIFAR-10 in just 35 evaluations [10]. A parallel line *distills* the ODE trajectory itself: consistency models learn a function $f_\theta(x_t, t)$ mapping any point on a probability-flow trajectory directly to its origin $x_0$, enforcing self-consistency $f_\theta(x_t,t) = f_\theta(x_{t'},t')$ along the same trajectory, which permits single-step generation [12]. Progressive distillation [8, 12] halves the step count iteratively by training a student to mimic two teacher steps in one.

## 5 Empirical and Theoretical Evidence


The theory makes quantitative predictions, and the record largely confirms them:

| Model | CIFAR-10 FID ↓ | CIFAR-10 IS ↑ | Likelihood (bits/dim) ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| NCSN [3] | — | 8.87 | — |
| DDPM [6] | 3.17 | 9.46 | — |
| NCSN++ / DDPM++ (SDE) [7] | 2.20 | 9.89 | 2.99 |
| EDM [10] | 1.97 (1.79 cond.) | — | — |

Several regularities stand out. **First**, the SDE unification was *predictive*, not merely descriptive: it identified the VE/VP distinction, prescribed the likelihood weighting $\lambda(t) = g(t)^2$, and its predictor–corrector samplers immediately improved on both parents [7]. **Second**, reweighting the objective away from the likelihood weighting systematically improves sample quality at the cost of bits/dim — the *likelihood–quality gap* — which is why DDPM's $L_{simple}$ (uniform-ish weighting) beats the true variational bound on FID [6][7][10]. **Third**, scale behaves as the manifold story predicts: high-noise score estimates transfer across datasets while low-noise detail must be relearned, matching the observation that large-$\sigma$ denoising is essentially generic [3][10].

Theoretically, the variational perspective closed the remaining gap: minimizing the denoising score-matching loss is equivalent to maximizing a lower bound on the likelihood of the plug-in reverse SDE, unifying the score-matching and variational derivations into one [6][7]. Convergence analyses now bound the full pipeline — score-estimation error plus discretization error of the reverse SDE — confirming that DDPM's sampler is an exponential-integrator discretization whose error vanishes as steps grow [7].

## 6 Limitations


The framework's successes are real, but five limitations define the research frontier.

- **Sampling cost.** Even with EDM's 35 evaluations [10] or consistency distillation [12], diffusion sampling is orders of magnitude slower than a GAN forward pass. Interactive and video applications remain compute-bound.
- **The likelihood–quality gap.** The weighting that maximizes likelihood ($\lambda = g^2$) is not the weighting that minimizes FID, and no current theory reconciles them [7][10]. A model can assign excellent likelihoods to blurry images.
- **Manifold mismatch.** The entire edifice estimates scores of *noise-perturbed* distributions $p_\sigma$, never $p_{data}$ itself. As $\sigma \to 0$ near a low-dimensional manifold, score estimation becomes statistically hard, and memorization of training points is empirically observed at small scales.
- **Guidance trade-offs.** Classifier-free guidance buys fidelity with diversity; large $w$ collapses modes and oversaturates [11]. Controllable generation still lacks a principled fidelity–diversity Pareto theory.
- **Discrete and structured data.** The SDE machinery is natively continuous. Text, graphs, and other discrete domains require awkward embeddings or separate discrete-diffusion formulations, where the theory is far less mature.

Evaluation itself is fragile: FID inherits Inception's biases, and likelihood is measured under dequantization schemes that are not comparable across papers. The field's metrics have not kept pace with its models.

---

## 7 Conclusion


Score-based generative modeling succeeded by refusing to model the density directly. Hyvärinen's score matching [1] removed the partition function; Vincent's denoising variant [2] removed the Jacobian trace; NCSN's noise conditioning and annealed Langevin dynamics [3] removed the manifold and mixing barriers; and the SDE unification [7] revealed DDPM [6], NCSN, DDIM [8], and the probability flow ODE as facets of one object — a diffusion, its time reversal, and the deterministic flow sharing its marginals.

The practical consequences — exact likelihoods, predictor–corrector samplers, guidance arithmetic, single-step distillation — all flow from that single mathematical core. What remains is to close the gaps the theory itself exposes: sampling cost, the likelihood–quality tension, and generation beyond continuous domains. The arc from a 2005 score-matching identity to the dominant generative paradigm of the 2020s suggests the next breakthrough will again be mathematical, not architectural.
