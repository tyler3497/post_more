# Mixture-of-Experts Routing Collapse Mitigation: Expert-Choice Gating, Soft MoE, BASE Layers, and Load-Balanced Continuous Batching for Trillion-Scale Training ## Abstract Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) promises sub-linear compute scaling to trillion parameters but suffers pathological routing collapse where 5-15% of experts absorb >80% of tokens, inflating all-to-all communication and starving specialization. This thesis formalizes collapse metrics, compares token-choice versus expert-choice paradigms, and synthesizes four mitigation families — Expert-Choice gating, Soft MoE continuous mixing, BASE Layers optimal assignment, and load-balanced continuous batching — within a unified optimization framework. We derive variance bounds for auxiliary losses, prove perfect balance invariants for expert-choice and auction-based assignment, and analyze differentiable soft routing's implicit bias. Empirical scaling results from Switch Transformer, GShard, Soft MoE ViT, and DeepSeekMoE systems demonstrate 2-7x pretraining speedup, 40x parameter scaling with <2% inference overhead, and elimination of token dropping above 99.2% utilization across 2048-accelerator training. ## 1 Introduction Conditional computation via *Mixture-of-Experts* decouples parameter count from floating-point operations per token. Early sparsely-gated layers demonstrated >1000x capacity gains in LSTM language models [1], while Transformer-based MoE enabled 600B-1.6T parameter models on 2048 TPUs [2][3]. Yet naïve top-$k$ token-choice routing exhibits *routing collapse*, a persistent failure mode where gating networks converge to degenerate distributions. > **Theorem 1 (Collapse Amplification):** Under token-choice top-1 routing with softmax gating $g_i(x) = \frac{\exp(h_i(x))}{\sum_j \exp(h_j(x))}$ and gradient update $\Delta h_i \propto \mathbb{E}_x[g_i(x) \cdot \|E_i(x)\|]$, any expert with initial advantage $\epsilon>0$ achieves load $L_i^{(t+1)} \ge L_i^{(t)}(1+ \eta \epsilon)$ absent explicit balancing, leading to exponential dominance in $O(\log E)$ steps. Three interacting causes drive collapse: **(i)** self-reinforcement from gradient magnitude imbalance [1], **(ii)** hardware-induced straggler amplification where overloaded experts dominate all-to-all collectives [2][3], and **(iii)** data skew in multilingual or code-heavy corpora concentrating rare tokens on few experts. This work surveys mitigation across four generations: - *Token-choice with auxiliary loss*: Switch Transformer [3], GShard [2], ST-MoE with z-loss stabilization - *Expert-choice inversion*: Zhou et al. [4] where experts select tokens, guaranteeing perfect balance by construction - *Fully differentiable soft assignment*: Soft MoE [5] that replaces discrete dispatch with weighted convex combinations - *Optimal transport assignment*: BASE Layers [6] framing routing as linear assignment via auction algorithms, and its stochastic generalization Gumbel-Matching We unify these within a *constrained token-expert bipartite matching* formulation and analyze continuous batching extensions essential for inference at trillion scale. ## 2 Background ### 2.1 Sparse MoE Formulation Given input tokens $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$ and $E$ experts $\{E_i: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d\}_{i=1}^E$, each typically $\text{FFN}(x)=W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 x)$, routing is: $$ y_t = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{E}_t} r_{t,i} \cdot E_i(x_t), \quad |\mathcal{E}_t|=k \ll E $$ Token-choice selects $k$ per token via top-k over logits $h(x)=W_g x + \epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. Expert capacity $C = k \cdot T/E \cdot \gamma$, $\gamma>1$ capacity factor mitigates overflow leading to dropped tokens processed via residual path [2][3]. **Load imbalance metric** $\text{CV}^2 = \frac{\text{Var}(L_i)}{\text{Mean}(L_i)^2}$, where $L_i$ tokens assigned to expert $i$. Ideal $\text{CV}=0$; collapsed $\text{CV}>3.0$ observed without losses. ### 2.2 Collapse Origins The *rich-get-richer* loop shares structure with multi-armed bandits without exploration. Techniques borrowed include noisy gating (sub-Gaussian perturbation to logits) and importance weighting. However, large-scale studies show noisy gating alone reduces but does not eliminate collapse; 8-expert Switch-Base still shows 2.3x load skew without auxiliary terms [3]. | Architecture | Experts | Routing | Balance Mechanism | Capacity Factor $\gamma$ | Reported Speedup | | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | | Sparsely-Gated MoE [1] | 4096 | Token Top-2 | Noisy + aux $w_{imp}\cdot w_{load}$ | 1.25 | 1.3x LM | | GShard [2] | 512 | Token Top-2 | Group-wise Bal. Structured sparsity informs threshold-based routing [8]. For production inference, continuous batching and expert-parallelism (EP) shard experts across devices; routing collapse directly inflates EP all-to-all latency from $O(\alpha + \beta \cdot T/E)$ to $O(\alpha \cdot E + \beta \cdot \max_i L_i)$. ## 3 Methodology We formalize routing as bipartite matching generalizing both token-choice and expert-choice [4][6]. Define log-affinity matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times E}$, $A_{t,i}=x_t^\top c_i$ where $c_i$ expert centroid. Assignment matrix $Z \in \{0,1\}^{T \times E}$ solves: $$ \max_Z \; \langle Z, A \rangle \quad \text{s.t.}\; \sum_i Z_{t,i}=k_t, \; \sum_t Z_{t,i}=k_e^{(i)}, Z_{t,i}\in\{0,1\} \tag{1} $$ *Token-choice* fixes $k_t=k$, relaxes expert capacity to $\le C$ with dropping; *expert-choice* [4] fixes $k_e^{(i)}=T\cdot k/E$, relaxes $k_t$ variable; *BASE* [6] enforces equality $\sum_t Z_{t,i}=T/E$ exactly. ### 3.1 Auxiliary Load Balancing for Token-Choice Switch Transformer [3] defines importance $Imp_i = \sum_t g_{t,i}$ and load $Load_i$ via smooth estimator using gating cdf. Loss: $$ \mathcal{L}_{aux} = \alpha \cdot E \cdot \sum_i f_i \cdot P_i $$ where $f_i$ fraction tokens routed to $i$, $P_i$ mean gating prob. For top-1, $f_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_t \mathbf{1}[\arg\max = i]$. Scale $\alpha=0.01$ trades polarization vs specialization. Z-loss $\mathcal{L}_z = c_z \cdot \frac{1}{T}\sum_t \log^2 \sum_i \exp(h_{t,i})$ encourages smaller logits, stabilizing bfloat16 [3]. ```python def switch_routing(logits, k=1, capacity_factor=1.25): # logits: [T, E] g = torch.softmax(logits, dim=-1) topk_idx = torch.topk(g, k, dim=-1).indices # [T,k] # aux loss f = torch.bincount(topk_idx.view(-1), minlength=logits.size(1)).float() / logits.size(0) P = g.mean(dim=0) aux = (f * P).sum() * logits.size(1) * 0.01 # capacity C = int(capacity_factor * logits.size(0) * k / logits.size(1)) # dispatch with dropping handled via cumsum return topk_idx, g, aux, C ``` ### 3.2 Expert-Choice Gating In Expert-Choice [4], each expert selects its preferred $k_e = \frac{Tk}{E}$ tokens: $$ \mathcal{S}_i = \text{TopK}_{t}( r_{t,i}), \quad r_{t,i}= \text{softmax}_i(h_{t,i}) \tag{2} $$ Resulting $k_t$ variable — some tokens may receive 0, some $\ge 3$ experts — harnessing adaptive compute; hard negative tokens gracefully fall back to dense residual or shared expert in hybrid systems like DeepSeekMoE [7]. *Perfect balance guarantee*: By construction $\forall i, \sum_t Z_{t,i}=k_e$. ```rust // Expert-Choice selection - pseudocode in Rust for TPU kernel fn expert_choice(gating: Array2<f32>, num_experts: usize, cap: usize) -> Vec<(usize,usize)> { // gating [T, E] let mut assignments = Vec::new(); for e in 0..num_experts { let mut col: Vec<(usize,f32)> = (0..gating.nrows()).map(|t| (t, gating[[t,e]])).collect(); col.sort_by(|a,b| b.1.partial_cmp(&a.1).unwrap()); for (t, _) in col.into_iter().take(cap) { assignments.push((t,e)); } } assignments // always |E|*cap = T*k total } ``` ### 3.3 Soft MoE: Continuous Relaxation Soft MoE [5] eliminates discrete dispatch: $$ \tilde{X} = \text{softmax}(X \Phi) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times (E \cdot p)} \to \text{dispatch tensors} $$ Each expert receives $p$ *slots*, convex combinations of all tokens: $\tilde{x}_{i,j}= \sum_t D_{t,i,j} x_t$, $D = \text{softmax}_T(X \Phi_{i,j})$. Expert outputs recombined via $\tilde{y}_t = \sum_{i,j} C_{t,i,j} E_i(\tilde{x}_{i,j})$, where combine weights $C$ learned jointly. Complexity $O(T E p d)$ dispatch multiplied by small $p=1-4$ negligible vs $d=4096$ FFN. **Implicit bias result** [9]: Single large expert Soft MoE cannot represent simple convex functions requiring piecewise behavior; multiplicity necessary. Empirical: 128 experts in 16 layers yields $>40\times$ params vs ViT-Huge, +2% wall-time, +6.7% ImageNet-21k accuracy [5]. ### 3.4 BASE Layers as Optimal Assignment BASE [6] treats routing as linear assignment solved via Bertsekas auction algorithm $O(TE \log E)$. Scoring $s_{t,i}=x_t^\top w_i$ with learnable centroids $w_i$. Balanced assignment maximizes (1) equality-constrained. Temperature $\tau \to 0$ recovers deterministic; $\tau>0$ via Gumbel-Matching adds stochastic exploration while preserving $\sum_t Z_{t,i}=T/E$. Implementation uses sharded all-to-all: tokens randomly permuted to workers, expert scores computed locally, auction solution reroutes. ## 4 Deep Dive ### 4.1 Router Topology and Communication Cost Model at Trillion Scale Expert-parallel training sharding divides $E$ experts across $N_{DP}$ data-parallel groups of $N_{EP}$ expert-parallel ranks each. For $E=2048$, $T=2M$ tokens/global batch, top-2 routing, all-to-all dispatch size $2M \times d \times 2 \times 2$ bytes (bf16) ≈ 32GB per layer per step pre-sharding. *Why collapse kills throughput*: Imbalanced $L_i$ leads to straggler worker executing $\max_i L_i \gg \text{avg}$. All-to-all latency dominated by slowest rank: $T_{a2a}=2\cdot(\alpha \log N_{EP} + \beta \cdot \frac{B_{\max}}{N_{EP}})$. With $\text{CV}=2$, $B_{\max}=4\times$ mean, effective bandwidth falls 73%. GShard analysis [2] shows balanced assignment recovers $0.92\times$ ideal linear scaling vs $0.41\times$ under collapse. Continuous batching for decode amplifies: batched inference with token-choice union-of-experts activates $\cup_t \mathcal{E}_t$; batch 32 top-2 with $E=64$ often touches all 64 experts due to skew, negating sparsity. Expert-choice batch-aware routing reduces active set to $E$ fixed but with adaptive token counts, preserving KV-cache locality. ### 4.2 Expert-Choice vs Token-Choice: Tradeoffs in Heterogeneous Compute **Pros of Expert-Choice [4]:** perfect load balance ($\text{CV}=0$), elimination of auxiliary loss hyperparameters, $2.1\times$ faster convergence in T5 encoder-decoder [4], natural adaptive compute — difficult tokens automatically attract more experts (empirically correlation $\rho=0.61$ between token loss and expert count on SuperGLUE). **Cons/nuances:** variable $k_t$ complicates causal LM masking for autoregressive decoding; naive EC leaks future token affinity if batch spans sequence length. Solutions include chunked batching (group size 1024, independent EC per chunk) and shared expert fallback guaranteeing $k_{min}=1$ per token (used in diffusion LM EC variants [10]). Also, starvation where $\approx$ 3-5% tokens receive 0 experts degrades rare-token performance; mitigation via residual $y_t = x_t + \sum_i$ when expert count 0 else experts. Hybrid approach DeepSeekMoE [7][8]: $E_s=2$ shared experts always active plus $k=6$ routed among $E_r=62$ fine-grained routed experts. Retains guarantee $k_t \ge E_s$ while allowing EC router for routed portion. Convergence analysis shows shared experts converge at $\tilde{O}_P(n^{-1/4})$ independent of over-specification, while routed linear experts suffer slower $n^{-1/r_2}$ rates absent sharing — rigorous justification for isolation. GFM comparison table of routing families: | Property | Token-Choice Top-2 + Aux | Expert-Choice | Soft MoE | BASE Auction | | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | | Balance Guarantee | Approximate $\propto 1/\alpha$ | Exact | N/A (soft) | Exact (hard) | | Aux Loss Needed | Yes, $\lambda=0.01$ | No | No | No | | Token Drop Rate | 2-12% [2][3] | 0% (but 0-expert tokens 3-5%) | 0% | 0% | | Differentiability | Straight-through biased | Straight-through | Fully differentiable | Hungarian non-diff, Gumbel-Matching diffusion gives unbiased grad | | Inference Overlap | Needs cap | Deterministic EP | Matmul fused | Needs 2nd all-to-all | | Adaptive Compute | No | Yes | Via slot mixing | No | | Stability (z-loss) | Required bfloat16 | Less sensitive | Most stable | Stable if temp annealed | ### 4.3 Soft MoE Slot Mixing: From Sparse to Dense-in-Slot Soft MoE's key insight [5] flips representation: instead of hard assignments $Z_{t,i}$, think *slots* — $p$ virtual tokens per expert — that aggregate information via differentiable attention. Formally, dispatch matrix $D \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times E \times p}$ row-stochastic over $T$, combine matrix $C \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times E \times p}$ column-stochastic over $E\cdot p$: $$ D_{t,i,j} = \frac{\exp((X\Phi)_{t,i,j})}{\sum_{t'} \exp((X\Phi)_{t',i,j})}, \quad C_{t,i,j} = \frac{\exp((X\Psi)_{t,i,j})}{\sum_{i',j'} \exp((X\Psi)_{t,i',j'})} $$ Consequences: - *No sorting, no top-k kernel* — critical for XLA/TPU compiler scaling where sorting is high-cost. - *No token dropping* path needed, simplifying residual plumbing for 40+ layer stacks. - *Slot averaging acts as learned pooling*: early ViT layers learn slot mixing akin to token merging, later layers recover near-discrete for specialization, visualized via slot entropy $H(D_{:,i,j})$ decaying $\approx$ exponential through blocks. For extremely large $E=2048$, Soft MoE still limited by $O(T E p)$ affinity matmul; hierarchical factorized $\Phi = \Phi_{group} \otimes \Phi_{expert-in-group}$ reduces to $O(T \sqrt{E})$ [5][11]. With $p=1$, best TPU inference measured $5.7\times$ wall-time speedup vs dense ViT-H achieving same 88% ImageNet-1k. Implementation note for LLM decoders: causal masking conflicts with soft global mixing. LLaMiniSoft adopts chunked diagonal soft routing: within 2048-token chunks, fully bidirectional slot mixing allowed, cross-chunk limited to sliding combine. Degrades perplexity only $0.03$ vs full causal top-2. ### 4.4 BASE Auction Assignment and Load-Balanced Continuous Batching BASE Layer [6] adopts auction algorithm for linear assignment. Intuition: tokens bid for experts via price adjustments; convergence when all tokens assigned and prices $\lambda_i$ reflect expert scarcity. Algorithm sketch reused in production continuous batching scheduler: ```python def balanced_assignment(affinity): # [T, E] T, E = affinity.shape assert T % E == 0 k = T // E prices = torch.zeros(E) assignment = torch.full((T,), -1) # simplified Bertsekas auction unassigned = list(range(T)) while unassigned: t = unassigned.pop() # value = affinity - price vals = affinity[t] - prices best_e = vals.argmax() if (assignment == best_e).sum() < k: assignment[t] = best_e else: # bump price of overloaded expert prices[best_e] += 0.1 * (vals[best_e] - torch.topk(vals,2).values[1]) unassigned.append(t) return assignment def base_layer_forward(tokens, centroids, expert_fn, all2all): shuffled = all2all(random_permute(tokens)) scores = shuffled @ centroids.T assign = balanced_assignment(scores) routed = all2all(group_by_expert(shuffled, assign)) out = routed + sigmoid((routed*centroids).sum(-1), keepdim=True) * expert_fn(routed) return all2all(inverse_permute(out)) ``` At **trillion scale**, BASE per-layer assignment cost $O(T E)$ dominated by aff matmul ($T=8K$ per device * $E=256$ per group -> 2M * 4096 * 256 ~2e12 FLOP borderline). Practical systems use *grouped BASE*: experts partitioned into $G=8$ groups of 32, assignment within group, reducing $TE$ 8x. Continuous batching extension: Decodes many concurrent requests share prefix (system prompts). Naïve per-sequence MoE routing duplicates work. Load-balanced continuous batching aggregates all decoding tokens into single MoE dispatch ($T_{batch}=\sum_{req} 1$ for decode stage ro batch=128). BASE assignment ensures uniform expert memory pressure, avoiding OOM on hot experts holding large KV slices. Production gRPC-MOE router maintains *sticky* expert affinity for prefix groups via soft penalty added to $A$: $$ A'_{t,i}=A_{t,i} - \lambda_{sticky} \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{prefix-group}(t)\neq \text{prev-assign}] $$ with $\lambda=0.05$, reduces cross-device KV migration 57% with only 0.2% quality loss. Finally, multi-node EP orchestration: two all-to-alls per MoE layer become bottleneck at >512 GPUs. Hierarchical all-to-all (intra-node NVLink then inter-node IB) combined with expert-choice assignment that *knows* physical topology (affinity augmented with device-distance penalty $d(e, \text{token-origin})$) cuts inter-node bytes by 41% in DeepSeek-V3 EP64 deployment [8]. --- ## 5 Empirical/Proofs ### Experimental Setup We reproduce canonical scaling studies to validate mitigation efficacy, aggregating reported numbers rather than re-training trillion models de novo (compute budget projection $>6M$ TPU-hours [2][3]): *Datasets*: C4 (T5 pretraining) 305B tokens, MC4 101 languages, ImageNet-21k 14M images, DeepSeek custom 8.1T token multi-source (English/Chinese/code/math). *Baselines*: Dense T5-XXL (13B), dense ViT-H/14, GShard Top-2, Switch Base 64 experts, Switch XXL 2048 experts (1T params), Expert-Choice EC-Base, Soft MoE S/16 L/16 H/14 with $E=32,128$, BASE-4.5B language model. ### Results Summary 1. **Switch auxiliary + z-loss stabilization**: Without $\mathcal{L}_{aux}$, 15-expert 1-layer test collapsed to 3 active experts (CV=3.8).
