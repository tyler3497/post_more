# Symplectic Field Theory: Contact Homology, Holomorphic Curves, and the Weinstein Conjecture

## Abstract

Symplectic Field Theory (SFT), sketched by Eliashberg–Givental–Hofer in 2000, is Floer theory for contact manifolds: it counts punctured holomorphic curves in symplectizations and symplectic cobordisms, asymptotic to closed Reeb orbits, and packages them into algebraic invariants of contact structures. We survey the foundations — contact geometry and Reeb dynamics, holomorphic buildings and SFT compactness, the master equation $H \circ H = 0$ — and the flagship applications: the Weinstein conjecture on periodic Reeb orbits (Hofer; Taubes via Seiberg–Witten), contact homology and its role in distinguishing contact structures, symplectic cobordism obstructions, and the relation to Gromov–Witten theory. We explain the analytical difficulties (transversality, polyfolds), the algebraic formalism (Weyl algebras, BV$_\infty$ structures), and modern developments: embedded contact homology, the chord conjecture, and arboreal skeleta. We close with the open frontier of fully rigorous foundations.

## 1 Introduction

A **contact manifold** $(M^{2n-1}, \xi)$ carries a maximally non-integrable hyperplane field $\xi = \ker \alpha$ with $\alpha \wedge (d\alpha)^{n-1} \ne 0$. The **Reeb vector field** $R_\alpha$, defined by $\alpha(R_\alpha) = 1$ and $d\alpha(R_\alpha, \cdot) = 0$, generates a canonical dynamics. The **Weinstein conjecture** (1978) asserts that $R_\alpha$ always has a periodic orbit. For decades this stood as contact geometry's central dynamical problem, attacked by Hofer's holomorphic-curve methods in dimension three [4].

Symplectic Field Theory, introduced by Eliashberg, Givental, and Hofer in a 113-page *GAFA* special volume paper (2000), generalizes this: instead of isolated holomorphic disks proving one orbit exists, count *all* punctured holomorphic curves asymptotic to Reeb orbits, in the symplectization $\mathbb{R} \times M$ and in symplectic cobordisms, and assemble the counts into a full algebraic package [1].

> **Thesis.** SFT is the statement that contact dynamics is a quantum field theory: Reeb orbits are the "particles," holomorphic curves are the "Feynman diagrams," symplectic cobordisms are the "interactions," and the resulting algebra — a differential graded Weyl algebra satisfying a master equation — is the partition function of the contact manifold. The Weinstein conjecture is the one-loop case.

## 2 Background: Contact Geometry and Reeb Dynamics

### 2.1 Contact structures

The standard contact structure on $\mathbb{R}^3$ is $\xi_{\mathrm{std}} = \ker(dz - y\,dx)$; on $S^3 \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ it is the complex tangencies. Contact structures divide into **overtwisted** (containing an overtwisted disk — flexible, classified by homotopy theory, Eliashberg 1989) and **tight** (rigid, e.g., the standard $S^3$). The Reeb flow of $\alpha_{\mathrm{std}}$ on $S^3$ is the Hopf fibration — all orbits periodic — but for a general contact form the dynamics can be wild.

### 2.2 Symplectization

The **symplectization** of $(M, \alpha)$ is $(\mathbb{R} \times M, d(e^t\alpha))$. Choosing an almost complex structure $J$ compatible with $d\alpha$ on $\xi$ and sending $\partial_t \mapsto R_\alpha$, one studies $J$-holomorphic maps $u: \dot\Sigma \to \mathbb{R} \times M$ from punctured Riemann surfaces. Finite **Hofer energy** forces the punctures to be asymptotic to cylinders over closed Reeb orbits [1][5]. These asymptotics are the bridge between holomorphic-curve counts and Reeb dynamics.

| Object | Symplectic analogue | SFT role |
|---|---|---|
| Closed Reeb orbit $\gamma$ | critical point | generator of the chain complex |
| Holomorphic cylinder | Floer trajectory | differential coefficient |
| Symplectic cobordism | cobordism of Hamiltonians | chain map / morphism |
| $\mathbb{R} \times M$ | — | the "free" theory |

## 3 Methodology: Holomorphic Buildings and Compactness

### 3.1 SFT compactness

Moduli spaces of punctured holomorphic curves are non-compact: curves can degenerate by **splitting** into multi-level *holomorphic buildings* — collections of curves in the symplectization ends and the cobordism middle, matched along Reeb orbits. Bourgeois, Eliashberg, Hofer, Wysocki, and Zehnder proved the **SFT compactness theorem** (2003): sequences of bounded-energy curves converge to buildings, generalizing Gromov compactness [5]. The combinatorics of buildings is the source of SFT's algebraic relations.

### 3.2 The algebraic formalism

SFT associates to $(M, \alpha)$ a graded **Weyl algebra** $\mathcal{W}$ generated by variables $p_\gamma, q_\gamma$ for each (good) Reeb orbit $\gamma$, with $[p_\gamma, q_{\gamma'}] = \kappa_\gamma \delta_{\gamma\gamma'}\hbar$. Counting rigid holomorphic curves defines a **Hamiltonian** $\mathbf{H} \in \hbar^{-1}\mathcal{W}$ satisfying the **master equation**

$$\mathbf{H} \star \mathbf{H} = 0,$$

so that $D = [\mathbf{H}, \cdot]$ is a differential. The homology $H_*(\mathcal{W}, D)$ is the **full SFT** invariant [1]. Specializations give the computable flavors:

- **Contact homology**: the commutative ($p$-variable) reduction — a DGA generated by Reeb orbits, differential counting index-1 cylinders.
- **Rational SFT**: genus-zero curves only.
- **Cylindrical contact homology**: cylinders in the symplectization — defined when no contractible orbits exist.

A symplectic cobordism $W: M_- \to M_+$ induces a DGA morphism (a "correspondence"), making SFT functorial — the topological-quantum-field-theory structure [1].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Weinstein conjecture via holomorphic curves

Hofer (1993) proved the Weinstein conjecture in dimension 3 when the contact structure is overtwisted or $\pi_2(M) \ne 0$: a Bishop family of holomorphic disks must degenerate, and the limit is a finite-energy plane asymptotic to a contractible Reeb orbit [4]. The remaining tight, atoroidal cases resisted holomorphic methods.

The full conjecture in dimension 3 was proved by **Taubes** (2007) via a spectacular detour: his isomorphism between embedded contact homology and Seiberg–Witten Floer homology implies that the ECH differential is non-trivial, forcing periodic Reeb orbits for *every* contact form on a closed 3-manifold [6]. The analytic foundations of ECH themselves rest on SFT compactness and gluing.

> **Theorem (Weinstein conjecture, dim 3).** Every Reeb vector field on a closed 3-manifold has a periodic orbit. (Hofer 1993 for overtwisted/$\pi_2 \ne 0$; Taubes 2007 in general.)

In higher dimensions the conjecture is **false**: examples without periodic orbits exist (K. Kuperberg-type constructions adapted to Reeb flows), so dimension 3 is sharp.

### 4.2 Distinguishing contact structures

Contact homology's first triumph: **Ustilovsky's exotic contact spheres** — infinitely many pairwise non-contactomorphic tight contact structures on $S^{4k+1}$ distinguished by linearized contact homology. The differential counts cylinders, and the resulting homology is a contact invariant (independent of $\alpha$ and $J$ up to isomorphism). Legendrian SFT similarly distinguishes Legendrian knots with identical classical invariants (Chekanov–Eliashberg DGA).

### 4.3 Cobordism obstructions

If $W$ is an exact symplectic cobordism $M_- \to M_+$, SFT gives a unital DGA map $CH(M_+) \to CH(M_-)$. Contrapositively, the absence of such a map obstructs cobordisms. This yields: **non-fillability** results (overtwisted manifolds are not exactly fillable — the DGA map to the empty manifold would be a contradiction), and **orderability** phenomena in contactomorphism groups.

### 4.4 Transversality and polyfolds

The elephant in the room: for multiply covered curves, generic $J$ does *not* achieve transversality, so the curve counts defining $\mathbf{H}$ are not obviously well-defined. The community's answer is **polyfold theory** (Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder): a generalized Fredholm framework with abstract multivalued perturbations. The full polyfold foundations of SFT remain a monumental work in progress; meanwhile, practitioners use **automatic transversality** (Wendl), **domain-dependent $J$**, or restrict to settings (ECH, linearized theories) where transversality is achievable.

```haskell
-- The SFT master equation, schematically
data SFTAlgebra = SFTAlgebra
  { generators :: [ReebOrbit]      -- p_gamma, q_gamma
  , hamiltonian :: WeylElement     -- H: counts rigid curves
  , differential :: WeylElement -> WeylElement  -- D = [H, .]
  }
-- Invariant: D . D == 0  iff  H * H == 0 (master equation)
```

### 4.5 Legendrian SFT and the chord conjecture

Restricting to curves with boundary on a Legendrian submanifold $\Lambda \subset M$ gives **Legendrian SFT**, generated by Reeb *chords* with endpoints on $\Lambda$. The Chekanov–Eliashberg DGA (the genus-zero, one-positive-end sector) distinguishes Legendrian knots that share all classical invariants — the first effective invariant of Legendrian isotopy beyond the Thurston–Bennequin and rotation numbers. Stretching the neck along $\Lambda$ proves instances of Arnold's **chord conjecture**: every closed Legendrian in a closed contact manifold carries a Reeb chord. The chord conjecture in full generality remains open, and its resolution would complete the Legendrian analogue of the Weinstein story.

## 5 Empirical and Computational Aspects

SFT is computable in favorable geometries. For **Boothby–Wang** contact manifolds (circle bundles over symplectic bases), Reeb orbits are fiber iterates and contact homology reduces to Gromov–Witten theory of the base. For **toric** contact manifolds, Morse–Bott techniques compute the DGA combinatorially. In celestial mechanics, the restricted three-body problem's contact geometry (Albers–Frauenfelder–van Koert–Paternain) uses holomorphic curves to find periodic orbits — SFT as a tool for space mission design.

A striking quantitative application is **contact non-squeezing**: Eliashberg–Kim–Polterovich used contact homology to show that certain contact embeddings are rigid, a contact analogue of Gromov's symplectic non-squeezing. The mechanism is pure SFT functoriality — a squeezing would induce a DGA map contradicting algebraic properties of the homology. Similarly, **orderability** of contactomorphism groups (the non-existence of positive contractible loops) is detected by SFT: a positive loop would give a null-homotopy of the identity DGA map, contradicting non-vanishing of contact homology. These results show that SFT's algebra encodes genuine geometric rigidity, not just formal bookkeeping.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Foundations incomplete.** Full SFT with general transversality awaits completed polyfold theory; many "theorems" are conditional on virtual perturbation technology.
2. **Computability.** Beyond toric/Morse–Bott cases, contact homology is essentially uncomputable; the DGA is infinite and the differential counts curves no one can enumerate.
3. **Higher dimensions.** The Weinstein conjecture fails in dimension $\ge 5$; SFT invariants exist but their dynamical content is murkier.
4. **Coefficients.** Torsion information in SFT over $\mathbb{Z}$ is poorly understood; most results use field coefficients.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Symplectic Field Theory reconceived contact topology as a field theory: the Reeb flow's periodic orbits are the states, holomorphic buildings are the interactions, and the master equation is the Ward identity. It proved the Weinstein conjecture in dimension 3 (via Taubes' ECH bridge), distinguished exotic contact structures, and obstructed symplectic cobordisms. The unfinished analysis — polyfolds, transversality for multiple covers — is not a blemish but the frontier: **SFT's algebraic vision outran its analysis, and closing that gap is the defining project of modern symplectic topology** [1][5][6].
