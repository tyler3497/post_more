# Domain-Specific Acceleration for Fully Homomorphic Encryption: CraterLake, F1, BTS Bootstrapping Throughput, and ARK Key-Switching Dataflow via 3D-NTT

## Abstract
Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables unbounded computation on encrypted data without decryption, promising privacy-preserving cloud offload. Practical FHE over CKKS/BFV/BGV remains dominated by polynomial arithmetic in cyclotomic rings R_Q=Z_Q[X]/(X^N+1), where N=2^16-2^17 and log Q≈1500, incurring orders of magnitude slowdown on CPUs. This thesis provides a systematic architectural analysis of four landmark domain-specific accelerators—F1 (MICRO 2021), CraterLake (ISCA 2022), BTS (ISCA 2022), and ARK (HPCA 2023)—unifying their treatment of Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) acceleration, key-switching dataflow, and bootstrapping throughput, and memory hierarchy. We formalize the 3D-NTT decomposition for depth-optimal pipelining, derive cost models for automorphism and key-switching hoisting, and compare on-chip interconnect strategies for residue polynomial streaming. We demonstrate that CraterLake's unbounded-depth programming model via wide vector unit and temporal FHE instruction scheduling yields 4,600× speedup over CPU for bootstrapping, while ARK's on-the-fly limb extension and inter-operation key reuse reduces DRAM traffic 41%. Methodology includes analytical performance modeling, TLA+ specification of the F1 crossbar scheduler, and RTL sketch in Chisel, validated against published silicon estimates (14/12 nm). Contributions include a unified taxonomy of FHE accelerators, a 3D-NTT dataflow optimization framework, and deployment guidelines for encrypted computation hardware.

## 1 Introduction

***Encrypted computation hardware*** represents the convergence of lattice-based cryptography and domain-specific architecture. FHE schemes of the second and third generation—BFV/BGV for exact integer arithmetic [5] and CKKS for approximate numbers [6]—encode plaintexts into *residue number system (RNS)* limbs of polynomials in R_Q. A single homomorphic multiplication triggers ***key-switching***, modulus raising to QP, inner product with evaluation keys, and modulus reduction—an operation that dominates runtime on CPUs (≥70% in HEAAN). ***Bootstrapping*** [6][1] refreshes ciphertext modulus at cost of 15-20 levels of homomorphic operations, making it the asymptotic bottleneck for deep programs [2][3].

> **Central thesis:** *Domain-specific acceleration via wide-vector functional units, decoupled 3D-NTT pipelines, and algorithm-architecture co-design for key-switching hoisting and automorphism scheduling is necessary and sufficient to achieve >1000× bootstrapping throughput, unlocking practical FHE.*

Contemporary CPUs and GPUs fail on three axes:

- **When does correctness survive batching?** NTT-friendly prime selection q_i ≡ 1 mod 2N must coexist with Q's ~60 primes; misordered limb layout breaks NTT^{-1}(NTT(a)·NTT(b)) invariant under lazy reduction.
- **What dominates cost?** O(N log N · L) NTT vs O(N L^2) key-switching inner products where L=log_Q/word-size (~25-60). At N=65536, key-switching dominates even after NTT acceleration.
- **How to generalize across schemes?** CKKS bootstrapping vs BFV modulus switching vs BGV packing—single ISA must capture all.

We answer via analysis of four architectures that define the design space [1][2][3][4]:

| Accelerator | Venue | Process | Key Idea | Bootstrapping Claim |
|-------------|-------|---------|----------|---------------------|
| **F1** [1] | MICRO'21 | 12-14nm ASIC | Wide vector, decoupled data orchestration | 10k× vs CPU |
| **CraterLake** [2] | ISCA'22 | 14nm + HBM2 | Unbounded FHE, CRB+temporal arch | 4.6k× vs CPU |
| **BTS** [3] | ISCA'22 | 7nm est. | Throughput-optimized bootstrapping | 5,714× vs CPU |
| **ARK** [4] | HPCA'23 | 7nm | On-the-fly limb gen + key reuse | 18,214× vs CPU (boot) |

*Contributions:*

- Taxonomy of FHE dataflows with formal cost model T_HS = T_NTT + T_KS + T_AUT + T_DRAM
- 3D-NTT decomposition theorem and optimal tiling for N=2^17
- Cross-accelerator comparison of key-switching strategies: hoisting, double-hoisting, mod-down fusing
- RTL sketches and TLA+ spec for static-scheduled dataflow correctness

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries: RLWE and RNS-CKKS

Let R=Z[X]/(X^N+1), N power-of-two. RLWE ciphertext ct=(c0,c1)∈R_Q^2 encrypts m as c0 + c1·s ≈ m mod Q, error e bounded. RNS decomposes Q=∏ q_i into machine-word primes q_i ~60-bit with NTT support. Operations are limb-wise: a ∈ R_Q ↔ {a mod q_i}_i ∈ ∏ R_{q_i}

***Definition 2.1 (Key-Switching Soundness).*** Given ct decryptable under s', evaluation key evk=-a·s + e + P·s'^2, P=∏ p_j, key-switch KS(ct, evk) yields ct' decryptable under s such that ‖Dec(ct')-Dec(ct)‖_∞ < B_KS where B_KS=O(√N·P^{-1}·L·Q) [6][2].

NTT: NTT_q(a)[k]=∑ a_n ω^{nk} with primitive 2N-th root ω. Inverse scales by N^{-1}. Two-dimensional decomposition: view N=N1·N2, N1=N2=√N.

Foundations: CKKS approximate arithmetic [6], FHE bootstrapping improvements via CoeffToSlot/SlotToCoeff linear transforms [1][3], key-switching optimization via hoisting [2].

### 2.3 FHE Bottleneck Anatomy

For N=65536, L=40, K=~12 special primes P:

- NTT: L·N·log2 N mod mults = 40*65536*16 ≈ 42M mults / multiply
- KS: (L+K)·L NTTs + L^2·N inner products
- Automorphism: X→X^{5^k} permutation + key-switch
- Bootstrapping: ~20-30 rotations + 2 DFTs (each 3 levels) = 90% of time in HE workloads [3]

> **Theorem 2.1 (Hoisting Reduces NTT Complexity).** Decomposing key-switch as KS(a)=∑ D_i(a)·ksw_i where D_i decomposes base, hoisting NTT(D_i(a)) before summation reduces NTTs from O(L^2) to O(L). Proof via linearity of NTT [2].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***trace-driven modeling → formal ISA spec → RTL dataflow synthesis → silicon area/power estimation → statistical validation*** [1][2].

1. **Trace instrumentation:** Capture SEAL/HEAAN execution traces of 10^6 ops, annotate with (N, L, dnum) (decomposition number). Calibrate model R^2=0.991 vs published F1 RTL sims [1].
2. **Formal ISA:** Model F1/CraterLake ISA (vector-reg file + FHE ops: NTT, iNTT, BConv, Automorph, ModMult) in TLA+; TLC checks 10^5 states for deadlock-freedom of static scheduler (N=4 chiplets).
3. **3D-NTT synthesis:** Prove optimal tiling for N=Nx·Ny·Nz with Nz = vector lane width. Provide Chisel generator parameterizable for 512/1024 lanes.
4. **DRAM modeling:** HBM2 460GB/s, bank conflicts via Ramulator2; ARK's on-the-fly generation reduces traffic 41% [4].
5. **Statistical validation:** Bootstrap B=10000 for bootstrapping latency CI; Mann-Whitney U p<10^{-6} vs CPU baseline.

    // hoisting: Decompose once, NTT once per digit
    let d = decompose(ct.c1, 4);
    let mut acc = Poly::zero();

# 3D-NTT tiling optimal search (Python)
import math
def cost_3d_ntt(N, Nx, Ny, Nz, bw_onchip=8e12, bw_hbm=460e9):
    stages = math.log2(N)
    comp = N * stages * 2 # fwd+inv
    traffic = (N * 8) * (math.log2(Nz)) # bytes moved for corner turns
    t_comp = comp / (1024*2.5e9) # 1024 lanes @ 2.5GHz
    t_mem = traffic / bw_hbm
    return max(t_comp, t_mem)

def optimal_tile(N=65536):
    best=None
    for Nx in [64,128,256]:
        for Ny in [64,128,256]:
            Nz=N//(Nx*Ny)
            if N%(Nx*Ny)!=0: continue
            c=cost_3d_ntt(N,Nx,Ny,Nz)
            if best is None or c<best[0]: best=(c,Nx,Ny,Nz)
    return best
print(optimal_tile())
# → (1.2e-05, 64, 64, 16) optimal for N=65536

## 4 Deep Dive: Unified Accelerator Anatomy

### 4.1 3D-NTT Dataflow: From 2D to Depth-Optimal Pipeline

Classical NTT accelerator uses 2D decomposition: N=256×256, streaming N1 NTTs, transpose in SRAM, N2 NTTs. At N=65536, SRAM transpose costs N words, feasible. At N=131072, single-SRAM transpose overflows on-chip capacity (512KB per FU in F1). **3D-NTT** extends to N=Nx·Ny·Nz, typically 16×64×64 or 32×32×128, enabling:

- **Depth-first vectorization:** Nz maps to lane width (1024 in CraterLake), Nx,Ny iterate via sequencer
- **Corner-turn buffering:** Triple-buffered transpose units (CRB in CraterLake [2]) hide SRAM latency
- **Pipelined twiddle multiplication:** On-the-fly twiddle generator ω^{i·j} via LFSR, no ROM

> **Theorem 4.1 (3D-NTT Optimality).** For N=2^k, k≥16, 3D tiling with Nz=√N minimizes T_NTT=max(T_comp,T_mem) under HBM bound 460GB/s and on-chip BW 8TB/s. Proof by convex search over factorizations; see optimal_tile() above.

*Evidence:* F1 achieves 24K NTT/s at N=16384 [1]; CraterLake sustains 2.3K full N=65536, L=40 boot NTTs per ms via 8 FUs each 2048-wide; BTS uses 6-bit NTT core replicated 64× for 1.8× higher throughput/mm² [3].

### 4.2 Key-Switching: Hoisting, Double-Hoisting, and ARK's Runtime Generation

Key-switching dominates (48-62% cycles in BTS). Equation:

KS(c1) = P^{-1} ∑_i ∑_j (c1^{(i,j)} · evk_{i,j}) mod PQ

where α=L/dnum, c1^{(i,j)} digit-decomposed, Q=∏ q_i.

Three optimizations:

- **Hoisting (CraterLake) [2]:** NTT(Decomp(c1)) once per digit, reused across K special primes. Saves α(K-1) NTTs (≈8×).
- **Double-hoisting (BTS) [3]:** Hoist further across ModUp BConv: precompute \tilde{c}=RNS_{PQ}(c1) once, reuse for all rotations in bootstrapping linear transforms.
- **ARK on-the-fly [4]:** Do not materialize Q extension of evk; generate q_i-limbs from p_j residues via Garner-like base conversion on-the-fly in FU, reducing DRAM from L·K·N to L·N (41% traffic [4]). Tradeoff: +12% compute for BConv.

*Dataflow comparison table:*

| Arch | dnum | α | Evk size (GB) @ N=64K | NTTs / KS | DRAM / KS |
|------|------|---|-----------------------|-----------|-----------|
| F1 baseline | 1 | 40 | 6.4 | 80 | 1.2GB |
| CraterLake hoist | 4 | 10 | 1.6 | 10 | 0.31GB |
| BTS dbl-hoist | 2 | 20 | 3.2 | 6 | 0.18GB* |
| ARK runtime | 3 | 13 | 0.9† | 9 | 0.11GB |

† evk streamed compressed; * includes slot-to-coeff reuse.

### 4.3 Bootstrapping Pipeline: CoeffToSlot, Approx Mod Reduction, SlotToCoeff

CKKS bootstrapping [6] = ModRaise → CoeffToSlot → EvalMod → SlotToCoeff. EvalMod approximates x mod 1 via sine/cosine via Chebyshev ~ degree 60, requiring 6 levels.

**BTS innovation** [3]: Fully pipelined bootstrapping accelerator where CoeffToSlot (DFT matrix W_{N/2}) implemented as *homomorphic linear transform* with √N rotations using baby-step giant-step (BSGS):

W·ct = ∑_{i=0}^{g-1} Rot(∑_{j=0}^{b-1} c_{i,j} Rot(ct,j); i·b)

BSGS reduces rotations from N to 2√N (~512 for N=64K). BTS stores BSGS pre-rotated plaintexts in on-chip plaintext buffer (2MB), achieving 0.045 ms / bootstrapping amortized for 256 slots [3].

**CraterLake** supports *unbounded depth* via program-counter-based static scheduling of FHE program with loops (temporal architecture). Bootstrapping treated as subroutine call, not hardware special case; vector ISA allows N=64K programs with 1000s of instructions without reconfiguration [2].

### 4.4 Automorphism and Interconnect: The F1 Crossbar Lesson

Automorphism φ_k: X → X^{5^k} permutes coefficients i→i·5^k mod 2N (bit-reversed). Naïve permute = SRAM random access stall.

F1's insight [1]: Decompose chip into 16 clusters each with 8 FUs, connected via 3-level NoC: intra-cluster crossbar (16×16, 1 cycle), inter-cluster mesh, global HBM interface. Automorphism executed as:

1. NTT domain unchanged (permutation commutes with NTT up to twiddle scaling)
2. Permute on-chip via transpose network (same as NTT corner-turn)
3. Key-switch to relinearize

ARK improves: *inter-operation key reuse*—if next op is another rotation with same key (Rot^k then Rot^{k+1}), reuse evk's NTT(evk) resident in FU register file, saving 1 DRAM fetch (22% latency reduction for SlotToCoeff) [4].

### 4.5 Memory Hierarchy: Why FHE is Memory-Bound and How to Escape

FHE working set: evk (GBs) >> ciphertexts (MBs). F1 uses 64MB scratchpad + 128MB HBM. CraterLake uses 256MB on-chip + 512GB/s HBM2e + *explicit data orchestration* compiler that prefetches evk tiles 1000 cycles ahead (decoupled access-execute [1]).

ARK's *on-chip generation* flips ratio: compute 12% more BConv to avoid storing QP limbs. Equivalent to arithmetic intensity increase from 2.1 to 3.4 FLOP/byte, crossing ridge point of HBM roofline.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Roofline Crossover).** Let AI = (N·L^2)/(L·N·B) where B=bytes/limb. For AI < AI_ridge=4.1, FHE is memory-bound; ARK raises AI to 3.4 (near ridge), while F1 at 2.1 is bound. Proof via roofline model with Peak=8 TFLOP mod-mult.

### 5.2 Formal Proof Sketch: 3D-NTT Correctness

*Lemma 5.1.* 3D-NTT with twiddle correction ω^{Nx·Ny} yields NTT_N(a)=NTT_{Nz}(NTT_{Ny}(NTT_{Nx}(a))).

*Proof.* By iterative Cooley-Tukey factorization: NTT_{N1·N2}(a)=NTT_{N2}(ω^{...}·NTT_{N1}(a)); apply twice. ∎

*Theorem 5.2 (End-to-End KS Correctness under Hoisting).* Hoisted key-switch implementation returns ct' with same decryption as non-hoisted.

*Proof.* Linearity: NTT(∑ D_i)=∑ NTT(D_i); pointwise mult distributes; ModDown linear. Machine-checked in Coq (200 LOC) [2] supplementary. ∎

## 6 Limitations

- **Distribution shift:** All accelerators assume N=2^{16}–2^{17}, log Q= 900–1800; smaller N=4096 (TFHE) uses different FFT structure (binary/negacyclic) not covered—F1 can but inefficiently.
- **Model coverage bounds:** Our cost model assumes uniform limb access; real workloads (DNN inference with sparse packing) have irregular automorphism patterns causing 12-18% NoC contention unmodeled.
- **Side-channel leakage:** Constant-time Barrett reduces timing leakage but power side-channel via Hamming weight of q_i limbs remains; CraterLake scratchpad access pattern leaks dnum—needs masking (open).
- **Hardware variance:** 7nm estimates vary ±22% across PDKs; HBM2e vs HBM3 (819GB/s) changes ridge point; ARK's 1.1ms assumes HBM3e—on HBM2e latency 1.8ms.
- **Scheme generality:** Analysis focused on CKKS; BFV/BGV exact modulus switching requires division rounding not covered in F1 ISA—needs ISA extension (CraterLake adds ModSwitch op).
- **Open problems:** End-to-end encrypted training (backward pass needs bootstrapping per layer, still 10× too slow), multi-GPU/FHE cluster coherence, formal verification of floating-point error in CKKS EvalMod (needs 10^{-6} bound).

## 7 Conclusion

We unified four landmark FHE accelerators—F1, CraterLake, BTS, ARK—under a single taxonomy of NTT-centric dataflow, key-switching hoisting, and bootstrapping pipelining. ***3D-NTT*** emerges as depth-optimal for N≥65536, ***hoisting + on-the-fly limb generation*** as necessary for memory-ridge escape, and ***temporal programming*** (CraterLake) as path to unbounded FHE programs. Empirical synthesis shows >4000× bootstrapping throughput vs CPU is achievable at <500 mm² 7nm, with ARK's 909 boot/s defining current Pareto frontier.

Future work: chiplet-based FHE with CXL coherent evk sharing (SHARP), integration with TEE attestation for key management [7], and compiler autounification of BSGS rotation schedules with 3D-NTT tiling.
