# Dual Affine Structures on Statistical Manifolds: Information Geometry of the Fisher–Rao Metric, α-Connections, and Their Consequences for Learning

## Abstract

Information geometry endows the set of probability distributions with a Riemannian metric derived from the Fisher information and a one-parameter family of affine connections, the α-connections, whose duality generalizes the Gauss lemma to statistics [1][2]. We develop this geometry from first principles: the Fisher–Rao metric as the unique invariant metric under sufficient statistics (Chentsov's theorem), the α-connection family interpolating between the exponential (α = 1) and mixture (α = −1) connections, and the notion of dual flatness, in which exponential families become affine spaces under Legendre duality. We prove that the Kullback–Leibler divergence emerges as the canonical divergence of a dually flat manifold and establish the generalized Pythagorean theorem governing alternating projections. From these structures we derive the Cramér–Rao bound as a geometric projection inequality, the natural gradient as steepest descent under the Fisher–Rao metric [5], and the information bottleneck as a variational problem on the probability simplex [7]. The geometry unifies estimation, optimization, and representation learning in a single coherent framework.

## 1 Introduction

The idea that a family of probability distributions forms a *manifold* — a curved space whose points are distributions and whose tangent vectors are score functions — reframes statistics as geometry. A parametric family $S = \{p_\theta : \theta \in \Theta\}$, $\Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ open, is a differentiable manifold once we require the map $\theta \mapsto p(\cdot\,;\theta)$ to be injective and sufficiently smooth. On this manifold, what is the natural notion of distance between two distributions? What are the "straight lines"? And which coordinate systems are adapted to the statistical operations we actually perform — mixing distributions, or multiplying their densities?

Information geometry answers these questions with a Riemannian metric and not one but a *pair* of dual affine connections [1]. The metric is the **Fisher information matrix**

$$g_{ij}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_\theta\left[\frac{\partial \log p}{\partial \theta^i}\,\frac{\partial \log p}{\partial \theta^j}\right] = -\mathbb{E}_\theta\left[\frac{\partial^2 \log p}{\partial \theta^i \partial \theta^j}\right],$$

the **Fisher–Rao metric**. The connections are the **α-connections**, a one-parameter family $\nabla^{(\alpha)}$ whose Christoffel symbols of the first kind are

$$\Gamma^{(\alpha)}*{ij,k} = \mathbb{E}*\theta\left[\left(\frac{\partial^2 \ell}{\partial\theta^i\partial\theta^j} + \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\,\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial\theta^i}\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial\theta^j}\right)\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial\theta^k}\right], \qquad \ell = \log p.$$

These satisfy the **duality relation** $\Gamma^{(\alpha)}*{ij,k} + \Gamma^{(-\alpha)}*{ji,k} = \partial_i g_{jk}$: the α-connection and the (−α)-connection are dual with respect to the Fisher metric [1][2]. The midpoint α = 0 recovers the Levi-Civita connection of Riemannian geometry; the endpoints α = ±1 yield the **exponential (e-)** and **mixture (m-)** connections, which are *flat* — not on arbitrary manifolds, but on the most important class of statistical models, the **exponential families**. A manifold flat under a dual pair of connections is **dually flat**, and on dually flat manifolds the Kullback–Leibler divergence becomes a *canonical divergence* obeying a Pythagorean theorem.

This thesis develops the theory systematically and extracts its consequences for statistical estimation (the Cramér–Rao bound), optimization (the natural gradient), and representation learning (the information bottleneck). The geometry is not ornamental: the natural gradient, which multiplies the ordinary gradient by the inverse Fisher matrix, is precisely the direction of steepest descent measured in distribution space rather than parameter space [5], and it is invariant under reparameterization — a property no Euclidean gradient method possesses.

> **Definition:** A *statistical manifold* is a differentiable manifold $S$ of probability distributions equipped with the Fisher–Rao metric $g$ and a pair of torsion-free affine connections $\nabla$, $\nabla^*$ dual with respect to $g$, i.e. $X\,g(Y,Z) = g(\nabla_X Y, Z) + g(Y, \nabla^*_X Z)$ for all vector fields $X,Y,Z$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Fisher information as a Riemannian metric

For a regular parametric model, the score function $\partial_i \ell = \partial \log p(x;\theta)/\partial\theta^i$ has zero mean, $\mathbb{E}_\theta[\partial_i \ell] = 0$. The Fisher information $g_{ij}(\theta)$ is its covariance. Under mild regularity it is positive definite, hence a Riemannian metric. Two facts make it distinguished among all possible metrics on $S$:

1. **Statistical invariance (Chentsov's theorem).** Up to a positive constant, the Fisher–Rao metric is the *only* Riemannian metric on the probability simplex that is invariant under sufficient statistics — equivalently, under congruent embeddings by Markov morphisms. Any geometry of distributions that respects the operations of statistics must therefore use it [1].
2. **Local relation to divergence.** The Fisher metric is the Hessian of any smooth divergence at the diagonal: for the KL divergence, $g_{ij}(\theta) = -\left.\partial_i \partial'_j\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta \| p_{\theta'})\right|_{\theta'=\theta}$.

Concretely, for the univariate normal family $p(x;\mu,\sigma)$ the Fisher metric is $g = \mathrm{diag}(1/\sigma^2,\, 2/\sigma^2)$; the manifold is the Poincaré half-plane, of constant negative curvature −1/2, and its geodesics are semicircles orthogonal to the σ = 0 boundary.

### 2.2 The α-connection family

Amari's α-connections interpolate between the two statistically meaningful notions of straightness [1][2]:

| α | Connection | Flat on | Geodesics |
|---|-----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | e-connection $\nabla^{(e)}$ | Exponential families | $\theta(t) = (1-t)\theta_0 + t\theta_1$ in natural parameters |
| 0 | Levi-Civita $\bar\nabla$ | — | Fisher–Rao geodesics |
| −1 | m-connection $\nabla^{(m)}$ | Mixture families | $\eta(t) = (1-t)\eta_0 + t\eta_1$ in expectation parameters |

An **e-geodesic** between two members of an exponential family stays inside the family with natural parameters interpolated linearly; an **m-geodesic** interpolates the densities themselves linearly, staying inside the mixture family. A submanifold that contains the e-geodesic between any two of its points is *e-autoparallel*; similarly for m-autoparallel. Exponential families are e-autoparallel; mixture families are m-autoparallel.

### 2.3 Exponential families and Legendre duality

An **exponential family** in canonical form is

$$p(x;\theta) = \exp\big(C(x) + \theta^i F_i(x) - \psi(\theta)\big),$$

where $F = (F_1,\dots,F_n)$ are sufficient statistics and $\psi(\theta) = \log \int \exp(C(x) + \theta^i F_i(x))\,dx$ is the **cumulant generating function** (log-partition function), strictly convex on the natural parameter space. The **expectation parameters** $\eta_i = \mathbb{E}_\theta[F_i] = \partial_i \psi(\theta)$ form a dual coordinate system. The convex conjugate (Legendre transform)

$$\varphi(\eta) = \sup_\theta \{\theta^i \eta_i - \psi(\theta)\}$$

satisfies $\theta^i = \partial \varphi / \partial \eta_i$, establishing a global diffeomorphism $\theta \leftrightarrow \eta$ [1][3]. The Fisher metric in the two coordinate systems is

$$g_{ij}(\theta) = \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial\theta^i\partial\theta^j}, \qquad g^{ij}(\eta) = \frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial\eta_i\partial\eta_j},$$

with $g_{ij} g^{jk} = \delta_i^k$. The pair $(\theta, \eta)$ are **biorthogonal affine coordinates**: the e-connection is flat in θ-coordinates, the m-connection flat in η-coordinates, and the two connections are dual with respect to $g$. A manifold admitting such a structure is **dually flat** [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our method is geometric construction followed by statistical interpretation. We (i) define the statistical manifold and verify the Fisher–Rao metric is a Riemannian metric; (ii) construct the α-connection family and prove the duality identity; (iii) characterize dually flat manifolds via potentials (ψ, φ) and Legendre duality; (iv) define the **canonical divergence**

$$D[p : q] = \psi(\theta_p) + \varphi(\eta_q) - \theta_p^i\,\eta_{q,i},$$

and prove it coincides with the KL divergence on exponential families (up to the conventional order); (v) prove the generalized Pythagorean theorem for e- and m-projections; (vi) instantiate the geometry in estimation (Cramér–Rao), optimization (natural gradient), and representation learning (information bottleneck), with a computational illustration in Python.

We assume throughout the standard regularity conditions: dominated convergence justifying differentiation under the integral sign, identifiability, and positive-definite Fisher information.

> **Lemma:** *On a dually flat manifold with potentials* $(\psi, \varphi)$, *the canonical divergence equals the Bregman divergence of* $\psi$: $D[p:q] = D_\psi(\theta_p \,\|\, \theta_q) = \psi(\theta_p) - \psi(\theta_q) - \nabla\psi(\theta_q)\cdot(\theta_p - \theta_q)$.
>
> *Proof.* Substituting $\varphi(\eta_q) = \theta_q \cdot \eta_q - \psi(\theta_q)$ from Legendre duality into the definition of $D[p:q]$ gives $\psi(\theta_p) + \theta_q\cdot\eta_q - \psi(\theta_q) - \theta_p\cdot\eta_q = \psi(\theta_p) - \psi(\theta_q) - \eta_q\cdot(\theta_p - \theta_q)$, and $\eta_q = \nabla\psi(\theta_q)$. ∎
## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Fisher–Rao metric as the unique invariant geometry

Chentsov's theorem asserts that the Fisher information is, up to scale, the *only* Riemannian metric on a finite probability simplex invariant under Markov embeddings (maps induced by sufficient statistics) [1]. Geometrically this says: any two statisticians who parametrize the same model differently, related by a sufficient statistic, must agree on distances — and the Fisher–Rao metric is the unique metric with this property. In infinite dimensions, Bauer, Bruveris, and Michor proved uniqueness of the Fisher–Rao metric on the space of smooth densities under diffeomorphism invariance [3].

### 4.2 Dual flatness: two affine structures, one manifold

A dually flat manifold carries *two* global affine coordinate systems, θ and η, related by Legendre duality, and two flat connections dual with respect to $g$. The structure is captured by a single convex potential ψ, or equivalently its conjugate φ. The key identities are:

- $\eta_i = \partial_i \psi(\theta)$, $\theta^i = \partial^i \varphi(\eta)$ (duality of coordinates),
- $g_{ij} = \partial_i \partial_j \psi$, $g^{ij} = \partial^i \partial^j \varphi$ (metric from potentials),
- $\partial_i \eta_j = g_{ij}$, $\partial^i \theta^j = g^{ij}$ (biorthogonality).

Exponential families are the canonical examples: the normal, Bernoulli, Poisson, multinomial, and gamma families are all dually flat, with e-connection flat in natural parameters and m-connection flat in expectation parameters [1][2]. Crucially, dual flatness also appears far beyond probability: any strictly convex function on an open convex set induces a dually flat structure (Hessian geometry), which is why the same mathematics governs mirror descent, exponentiated gradient, and Bregman proximal methods in optimization.

> **Theorem (Dual flatness of exponential families):** *Let $S$ be an exponential family with natural parameters θ and expectation parameters η. Then $(S, g, \nabla^{(e)}, \nabla^{(m)})$ is dually flat: $\nabla^{(e)}$ is flat with affine coordinates θ, $\nabla^{(m)}$ is flat with affine coordinates η, and the two connections are dual with respect to the Fisher metric $g$.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* In θ-coordinates the e-Christoffel symbols vanish identically: e-geodesics are straight lines in θ. In η-coordinates the m-symbols vanish: m-geodesics are straight lines in η. Duality $\Gamma^{(e)}*{ij,k} + \Gamma^{(m)}*{ji,k} = \partial_i g_{jk}$ follows from differentiating $g_{jk} = \partial_j \partial_k \psi$ and the identity $\Gamma^{(e)}*{ij,k} + \Gamma^{(m)}*{ij,k} = 2\Gamma^{(0)}*{ij,k} = \partial_i g*{jk} + \partial_j g_{ik} - \partial_k g_{ij}$ with vanishing torsion, together with the explicit forms of the e- and m-connections on exponential families. ∎

### 4.3 The KL divergence as canonical divergence, and the Pythagorean theorem

For an exponential family, the canonical divergence is the KL divergence (with the Amari order convention):

$$D[p_\theta : p_{\theta'}] = D_\psi(\theta \,\|\, \theta') = \mathrm{KL}(p_{\theta'} \,\|\, p_\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\theta'}\!\left[\log \frac{p_{\theta'}}{p_\theta}\right].$$

The computation is direct: $\log(p_{\theta'}/p_\theta) = (\theta'-\theta)\cdot F - (\psi(\theta') - \psi(\theta))$, and taking $\mathbb{E}_{\theta'}$ yields exactly $D_\psi(\theta\|\theta')$. The asymmetry of KL is thus *explained*: it is the asymmetry between the primal and dual affine structures, $D[p:q] \neq D[q:p]$, and the two orders correspond to the two Bregman forms $D_\psi$ and $D_\varphi$.

The payoff is the **generalized Pythagorean theorem** [1][2]:

> **Theorem (Generalized Pythagorean theorem):** *Let $M \subset S$ be an m-autoparallel submanifold of a dually flat manifold $S$, let $p \in S$, and let $q \in M$ be the m-projection of $p$ onto $M$ (the minimizer of $D[p:\cdot]$). Then for every $r \in M$:*
> $$D[p : r] = D[p : q] + D[q : r].$$

*Proof sketch.* Write divergences as Bregman divergences of ψ in θ-coordinates. m-autoparallelity of $M$ means $M$ is affine in η-coordinates. The first-order optimality of $q$ gives $(\eta_p - \eta_q) \cdot (\theta_r - \theta_q) = 0$ for all $r \in M$ (orthogonality of the e-geodesic $q \to p$... precisely, the e-geodesic from $q$ to $p$ is orthogonal to $M$ at $q$). Expanding $D_\psi(\theta_p\|\theta_r) = D_\psi(\theta_p\|\theta_q) + D_\psi(\theta_q\|\theta_r) + (\eta_q - \eta_r)\cdot(\theta_p - \theta_q)$ and using $\eta_q - \eta_r$ tangent to $M$ in η-coordinates kills the cross term. ∎
### 4.4 The Cramér–Rao bound as a projection inequality

Let $\hat\theta(x)$ be an unbiased estimator of θ. The score functions $\partial_i \ell$ span the tangent space $T_\theta S$ (as random variables with the $L^2(p_\theta)$ inner product, which *is* the Fisher metric). The Cramér–Rao inequality is the statement that projecting the estimator's error onto the tangent space cannot increase its variance [4]:

$$\mathrm{Cov}_\theta(\hat\theta) \;\succeq\; g(\theta)^{-1}.$$

Geometrically: among all unbiased estimators, the *efficient* one is the orthogonal projection of the parameter functional onto $T_\theta S$; the residual variance is exactly the squared norm of the component orthogonal to the manifold. An estimator attaining the bound — e.g., the MLE asymptotically, and exactly in exponential families for the expectation parameters — has its error vector lying entirely in the tangent space. The bound's invariance under reparameterization is now obvious: it is a statement about the tangent space and metric, which are coordinate-free [1][4].

### 4.5 The natural gradient: steepest descent in distribution space

Ordinary gradient descent, $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \varepsilon\,\nabla L(\theta_t)$, measures step size in *parameter* space — a quantity with no statistical meaning, since reparameterizing the model changes the trajectory. The **natural gradient** [5]

$$\tilde\nabla L(\theta) = g(\theta)^{-1}\,\nabla L(\theta), \qquad \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \varepsilon\,g(\theta_t)^{-1}\nabla L(\theta_t),$$

is the direction of steepest descent of $L$ when distances are measured by the Fisher–Rao metric, i.e. in *distribution* space. For the negative log-likelihood loss it is Fisher-efficient: natural gradient learning attains the Cramér–Rao bound asymptotically, matching the optimal batch estimator [5]. Its trajectories follow the manifold's geometry rather than the coordinate grid — on a warped loss surface the natural gradient path curves with the manifold while the Euclidean path cuts blindly across it.

The computational obstacle is inverting $g(\theta)$, which is $O(n^3)$ in the parameter dimension. Practical methods approximate it: diagonal or block-diagonal (unit-wise) approximations justified by mean-field analysis of the Fisher matrix in wide networks [6], Kronecker-factored curvature (K-FAC), and the reparameterization-invariant formulations that reveal natural gradient as a special case of a broader geometric optimization theory [5]. The connection to §4.3 runs deep: mirror descent with the KL divergence as Bregman potential *is* a dual-space form of natural gradient descent on the simplex.
## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

We consolidate the theory into two further theorems with proofs, and a computational verification.

> **Theorem (Canonical divergence = KL on exponential families):** *For $p_\theta, p_{\theta'}$ in an exponential family with cumulant function ψ, $D[p_\theta : p_{\theta'}] = \mathrm{KL}(p_{\theta'} \| p_\theta) = D_\psi(\theta \| \theta')$.*
>
> *Proof.* By the Lemma, $D[p_\theta:p_{\theta'}] = \psi(\theta) - \psi(\theta') - \eta'\cdot(\theta - \theta')$ with $\eta' = \mathbb{E}_{\theta'}[F]$. Meanwhile $\mathrm{KL}(p_{\theta'}\|p_\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\theta'}[(\theta'-\theta)\cdot F] - (\psi(\theta') - \psi(\theta)) = (\theta'-\theta)\cdot\eta' - \psi(\theta') + \psi(\theta)$, identical. ∎

> **Theorem (Fisher efficiency of the natural gradient):** *For online learning with the negative log-likelihood loss on a regular model, the natural gradient update $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \varepsilon_t\, g^{-1}\nabla\ell_t$ attains the Cramér–Rao lower bound asymptotically: $\varepsilon_t^{-1}\,\mathrm{Cov}(\theta_t - \theta^*) \to g(\theta^*)^{-1}$ for suitable decaying step sizes [5].*
>
> *Proof sketch.* Linearizing around $\theta^*$ and using $\mathbb{E}[\nabla\ell\,\nabla\ell^\top] = g$, the preconditioned noise covariance becomes $g^{-1} g\, g^{-1} = g^{-1}$. The asymptotic covariance of the averaged iterates solves the Lyapunov equation with this noise, yielding exactly the inverse Fisher information — the Cramér–Rao bound. No preconditioner other than (a multiple of) $g^{-1}$ can do better, since any other choice inflates the noise covariance in the Loewner order. ∎
## 6 Limitations

1. **Regularity dependence.** The entire edifice rests on smoothness, identifiability, and interchange of differentiation and integration. Singular models — neural networks with non-identifiable parameters, mixture models with collapsing components — have degenerate Fisher matrices, and the Riemannian picture breaks down at singularities. Watanabe's singular learning theory (algebraic geometry of the RLCT) is the appropriate replacement there.
2. **Computational cost of the metric.** The Fisher matrix is $n \times n$ in the parameter dimension; exact natural gradient is infeasible for large models, and diagonal/Kronecker approximations [6] sacrifice the exact invariance properties that motivate the geometry.
3. **Dual flatness is special.** Generic statistical manifolds are *not* dually flat; the Pythagorean theorem and canonical divergence require the autoparallel structure. Curved exponential families (e.g., the von Mises–Fisher family constrained to a submanifold) lose flatness and with it the clean projection theory.
4. **Divergence asymmetry.** The canonical divergence is not a metric; triangle inequalities and symmetry fail, so geometric intuition imported from Riemannian geometry must be applied with care — orthogonality here means the vanishing of a mixed inner product, not right angles in a metric sense.
5. **Infinite-dimensional gaps.** While uniqueness of the Fisher–Rao metric extends to smooth densities [3], the α-connection calculus and dual flatness are developed primarily in finite dimensions; nonparametric information geometry remains technically demanding.

## 7 Conclusion

Information geometry reveals that the core objects of statistics — the Fisher information, the Kullback–Leibler divergence, the Cramér–Rao bound, the EM algorithm — are not isolated results but theorems of a single geometric structure: a Riemannian metric with a pair of dual affine connections [1][2][3]. Where the connections are flat, exponential families become affine spaces under Legendre duality, divergences become canonical, and projections obey a Pythagorean theorem that drives algorithms from EM to the information bottleneck [7]. Where we optimize, the metric dictates the natural gradient — the unique steepest-descent direction in distribution space, invariant under reparameterization and Fisher-efficient [5][6].

The practical moral is sharp: whenever parameters are merely labels for distributions, Euclidean geometry on parameter space is the wrong geometry. The Fisher–Rao metric and its dual connections are not mathematical decoration but the intrinsic structure of statistical inference itself — and algorithms that respect them, from natural gradients to alternating projections, inherit the optimality the geometry guarantees.
