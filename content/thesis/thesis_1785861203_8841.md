# Convergent Semantics Under Weak Consistency: A Comprehensive Treatment of State-Based, Operation-Based, and δ-State Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types for Planetary-Scale Systems

## Abstract
Replicating mutable state across geographically-distributed replicas without synchronous coordination is a foundational challenge for cloud storage, collaborative editing, and edge computing. Conflict-free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs) provide a principled framework achieving *Strong Eventual Consistency* (SEC): any two replicas that have received the same set of updates converge deterministically, regardless of order, failures, or network partitions. This thesis presents a rigorous, self-contained exposition of CRDT theory as introduced by Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero, and Zawirski (2011) and its subsequent refinements, including op-based, state-based, and δ-state variants. We formalize the lattice-theoretic foundations of state-based CRDTs as monotonically-inflating join-semilattices, the commutativity and causal-delivery requirements of op-based CRDTs, and the hybrid δ-state decomposition that reduces dissemination overhead while preserving SEC even under at-most-once delivery. We systematically analyze canonical constructions — G-Counter, PN-Counter, G-Set, 2P-Set, LWW-Set, OR-Set, LWW-Register, MV-Register, and Replicated Growable Array (RGA) — proving convergence properties and highlighting semantic trade-offs such as add-wins vs. remove-wins. We further examine causal CRDTs using dot stores and version vectors to achieve bounded tombstone reclamation, and present Isabelle/HOL mechanized proofs of SEC by Gomes et al. (2017). Finally, we evaluate practical deployment in Riak, Redis-Enterprise CRDTs, Automerge, and Yjs, and extend the discussion to Byzantine-tolerant SEC via Kleppmann and Howard (2020). Our central thesis is that CRDT composition and delta-optimization closes the availability-consistency gap without sacrificing formal correctness.

> **Key Insight:** *A data type that can be modeled as a join-semilattice with inflations, or as a commutative monoid over causally-ordered operations, is automatically SEC; this single observation unlocks local-first software at global scale.*

## 1. Introduction

The CAP theorem (Gilbert and Lynch, 2002) forces partitioned systems to choose between consistency and availability. Linearizability requires quorum or leader coordination, incurring $O(n)$ latency at planetary scale. Eventual Consistency (EC) relaxes this, allowing any replica to accept updates locally, but naive EC is ad-hoc and error-prone, requiring application-specific merge logic prone to lost updates and divergence.

Shapiro et al. (2011) introduced *Strong Eventual Consistency* to remedy EC's deficiencies. A system satisfies SEC when:

1. **Eventual Delivery:** Every update delivered at some correct replica is eventually delivered at all correct replicas
2. **Convergence:** Replicas that have delivered the same set of updates are in an equivalent state
3. **Termination:** All method executions terminate

A **CRDT** is a data type satisfying SEC by construction. Two taxonomies dominate:

- **State-based (CvRDT):** Replicas exchange full state via join $\sqcup$ over a semilattice $(S,\sqsubseteq,\sqcup)$
- **Op-based (CmRDT):** Replicas broadcast operations that commute for concurrent ops and require causal delivery

This thesis unifies these models, proves their equivalence under emulation, and motivates $\delta$-state CRDTs (Almeida et al., 2018) as bandwidth-efficient hybrids.

We assume graduate familiarity with posets, distributed broadcast abstractions, and basic order theory.

## 2. Preliminaries / Formal Model

### 2.1 System Model

We consider $n$ replicas $p_i \in \mathcal{R}$, communicating over an asynchronous unreliable network providing *fair-lossy* delivery eventually retried. Clients invoke *update* $u$ and *query* $q$ locally. No global clock.

### 2.2 Join-Semilattices

> **Definition 2.1 (Join-Semilattice):** A poset $(S,\sqsubseteq)$ where every pair $x,y \in S$ has a Least Upper Bound $x \sqcup y$ such that $x \sqsubseteq x\sqcup y$, $y \sqsubseteq x\sqcup y$, and $\forall z: x \sqsubseteq z \land y \sqsubseteq z \implies x\sqcup y \sqsubseteq z$. Consequently $\sqcup$ is *commutative* $x\sqcup y = y\sqcup x$, *associative* $(x\sqcup y)\sqcup z = x\sqcup (y\sqcup z)$, and *idempotent* $x\sqcup x = x$.

For state-based CRDTs we require:

- **Inflation:** $\forall u \in U, s \in S: s \sqsubseteq u(s)$
- **Merge is LUB:** $merge(s_1,s_2) = s_1 \sqcup s_2$
- **Compare is Order:** $s_1 \sqsubseteq s_2$

![State-based semilattice merge diagram](/thesis/thesis_1785861203_8841-0.webp)

### 2.3 Op-Based Requirements

> **Definition 2.2 (Op-Based CRDT):** Operations $o$ from set $O$ satisfy:
> - **Causality:** If $o_1 \prec_{hb} o_2$, every replica delivers $o_1$ before $o_2$
> - **Commutativity of concurrent ops:** If $o_1 \parallel o_2$ (concurrent), $o_1 \circ o_2 = o_2 \circ o_1$
> - **Exactly-once:** In classical formulation, no duplication (relaxed in $\delta$-state)

The network must implement *causal broadcast*, e.g., via vector clocks.

## 3. State-Based CRDTs: Lattice Theory in Practice

#### 3.1 G-Counter

Simplest non-trivial CRDT: monotonically increasing counter over $n$ replicas.

State: $p \in \mathbb{N}^n$, $p[i]$ = increments observed at $i$.

- $increment_i(p) = p\{i \mapsto p[i]+1\}$
- $value(p) = \sum_i p[i]$
- $merge(a,b)[i] = \max(a[i],b[i])$
- $compare(a,b) \iff \forall i: a[i] \le b[i]$

Join-semilattice is $(\mathbb{N}^n, \le_{pointwise}, \max)$. Inflation holds because increment increases one coordinate.

**Theorem 3.1 (Convergence of G-Counter):** All replicas that have observed the same set of increments converge to same value.

> *Proof:* $\max$ is associative-commutative-idempotent; max of same multiset of increments regardless of interleaving yields same vector.

#### 3.2 PN-Counter

Adds decrement by maintaining two G-Counters $P,N$. Value $= \sum P[i] - \sum N[i]$.

#### 3.3 Set Semantics: The Zoo

| CRDT | Semantics | Tombstone | Re-add after remove? | Commutative |
|------|-----------|-----------|----------------------|-------------|
| **G-Set** | Grow-only, union | No | N/A | Yes |
| **2P-Set** | $A \setminus R$, $A,R$ G-Sets | Yes ( $R$ ) | **No** | Yes |
| **LWW-Set** | $(e, t, b)$, $b$ bool, max $t$ wins | Yes (false marker) | Yes (higher ts) | Yes (with total order $<$ lexicographic) |
| **OR-Set** | Observed-Remove, dot-tagged | Dots in causal context | Yes, add-wins | Yes |
| **AWOR-Set** | Variant OR-Set | dots | add-wins | Yes |

2P-Set failure: Once removed, element can never return because $R$ monotonic. OR-Set solves via unique dots.

Formal OR-Set state: $(S: 2^{E \times D}, V: \mathbb{N}^n)$ where $D$ is pair $(replica, counter)$. `add(e)` generates dot $d$ not in $V$, inserts $(e,d)$ into $S$, bumps $V$. `remove(e)` removes all pairs for $e$ present and adds their dots to $V$ but not re-exposing them. Merge:

$$ (S_1,V_1) \sqcup (S_2,V_2) = ((S_1 \cap S_2) \cup \{(e,d)\in S_1: d\notin V_2\} \cup \{(e,d)\in S_2: d\notin V_1\}, V_1 \cup V_2) $$

This is a causal CRDT avoiding unbounded tombstone growth via version vectors.

![Operation-based causal broadcast](/thesis/thesis_1785861203_8841-1.webp)

#### 3.4 LWW-Register and Multi-Value Register

LWW-Register $(\mathbb{V} \times \mathbb{N}_{timestamp}, \sqsubseteq)$ where $(v,t) \sqsubseteq (v',t') \iff t < t'$ or $t=t' \land v \le_{tiebreaker} v'$. Merge takes max timestamp. Loses concurrent writes (last writer wins) — useful for simple caches but not collaborative text.

MV-Register keeps set $\{(v, vc)\}$ of concurrent values, merging via vector clock dominance. Applications keep conflicts visible to user.

## 4. Operation-Based CRDTs

Example: Counter with increment-only commutes trivially. For Set:

```python
# Op-based OR-Set pseudocode (educational)
class Replica:
    def __init__(self, rid):
        self.rid = rid
        self.counter = 0
        self.S = set()  # of (elem, dot)
        self.V = {}  # replica->max counter
    def add(self, e):
        self.counter += 1
        dot = (self.rid, self.counter)
        self.S.add((e, dot))
        self.broadcast(('add', e, dot))
    def remove(self, e):
        removals = {(elem,d) for (elem,d) in self.S if elem==e}
        self.S -= removals
        self.broadcast(('remove', removals))
    def deliver(self, op):
        if op[0]=='add':
            _, e, dot = op
            if dot not in self.seen(dot): self.S.add((e,dot))
        else:
            _, rem = op
            self.S -= rem
```

Causal broadcast ensures if replica A adds $e$ then removes $e$, no replica re-adds based on stale state. Causal delivery implemented via vector clock check: wait until $vc_{msg} \le local\_vc + 1$.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Op-based SEC):** If all concurrent ops commute and causal broadcast eventually delivers all ops, replicas converge.

> *Proof Sketch:* Any pair of concurrent ops commute, so despite delivery order divergence, effect same. Causal order preserved for dependent ops, preventing lost-remove-readd anomaly.

**Limitation:** Requires at-most-once, in-order causal broadcast — expensive in lossy networks with $O(n^2)$ version vector messages.

## 5. $\delta$-State CRDTs: Taming Bandwidth

State-based CRDTs send $O(|S|)$ payload each sync. Almeida, Shoker, Baquero (2018) observe that most state unchanged between syncs. $\delta$-mutators return *delta* fragment instead of full state.

Define delta group $D \subseteq S$ join-closed with $\bot \in D$. Delta-mutator $m^\delta: S \to D$ satisfies $m(s) = s \sqcup m^\delta(s)$.

Replica maintains lattice $s$ and buffers $\delta$s in group $g = \bigsqcup \delta_i$. Periodically disseminates $g$ via *delta-broadcast*, resetting $g$ to $\bot$. Remote merge $s' = s' \sqcup g$.

Crucially, delta-fragments themselves are join-semilattice elements, so standard anti-entropy works even with loss, duplication, reordering — unlike op-based which requires exactly-once.

![Delta-state optimization anti-entropy](/thesis/thesis_1785861203_8841-2.webp)

**Algorithm (Delta Group):**

```rust
// Rust sketch of G-Counter as delta-CRDT
#[derive(Clone, Default)]
struct GCounter { v: Vec<u64> }

#[derive(Clone, Default)]
struct Delta { idx: usize, val: u64 }

impl GCounter {
    fn inc_delta(&mut self, i: usize) -> Delta {
        self.v[i] += 1;
        Delta { idx: i, val: self.v[i] }
    }
    fn join(&mut self, d: Delta) {
        if self.v[d.idx] < d.val { self.v[d.idx] = d.val; }
    }
    fn join_group(group: Vec<Delta>) -> Vec<Delta> { /* merge max per idx */ group }
}
```

**Complexity comparison:**

| Approach | Payload per sync | Network Requirement | Memory |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------|--------|
| State-based | $O(\|S\|)$ full | Fair-lossy, idempotent | $O(\|S\|)$ |
| Op-based | $O(1)$ op | Causal, exactly-once | $O(\|S\|) + vc$ |
| $\delta$-state | $O(\|\Delta\|)$ | Fair-lossy, idempotent | $O(\|S\|)+O(\|\Delta\text{-buffer}\|)$ |

$\delta$-state dominates empirically for workloads with $5\%$ mutations between syncs (Bravo & Baquero, SOSP experiments show 10–100× bandwidth reduction vs. state-based for 1k-element sets).

> **Lemma 5.2 (Delta Decomposability):** Any state-based CRDT can be decomposed into a $\delta$-state CRDT with same SEC guarantee, by defining $m^\delta(s) = m(s)$ if $m(s) \neq s$, else $\bot$.

*Proof:* Since join with $m^\delta$ recovers $m(s)$, join associativity yields equivalence.

## 6. Composition, Causal CRDTs, and Formal Verification

CRDTs compose via product: If $(S_1, \sqcup_1)$, $(S_2, \sqcup_2)$ semilattices, then $(S_1 \times S_2, (a_1,a_2)\sqcup(b_1,b_2)=(a_1\sqcup_1 b_1, a_2\sqcup_2 b_2))$ is SEC. This enables JSON CRDT (Kleppmann & Beresford, 2017) built from maps, lists, registers.

**Causal CRDTs** generalize via *dot store* $(DS, CC)$ where $CC$ is version vector compacting causal history, $DS$ stores tagged values. Invariant: $(d \in CC \land (e,d) \notin DS) \implies$ element removed. Enables garbage collection when all replicas have witnessed dot.

Gomes et al. (2017) mechanized SEC in Isabelle/HOL: they define network as set of message histories with causal closure, prove abstract convergence theorem:

> **Theorem 6.1 (Abstract Convergence):** If merge is ACI and updates commute with merge, then any state reachable via network execution is join of updates, implying SEC independent of network adversary.

They instantiate for RGA, OR-Set, Counter.

## 7. Practical Systems and Byzantine Considerations

**Riak 2.0** (Basho) introduced state-based OR-Set CRDT and LWW-Register with Dotted Version Vectors, mitigating tombstone explosion observed in v1.3 2P-Sets.

**Redis Enterprise CRDTs** (Active-Active) implement PN-Counter, G-Set, OR-Set across 5 geo-replicas using $\delta$-like replication with conflict-free merge during replica partition.

**Automerge** and **Yjs** realize sequence CRDTs (RGA/WOOSplit) for collaborative editing: character identities are Lamport timestamps $(client,count)$, list retains all tombstoned deletes. Yjs achieves sub-50ms convergence on 500k char document with $O(\log n)$ R-tree index.

> **Definition 7.1 (Byzantine Eventual Consistency - Kleppmann & Howard 2020):** BEC requires that correct replicas converge even if Byzantine replicas equivocate, provided number of Byzantine nodes unbounded but they cannot forge causality of correct nodes (via hashes). Their algorithm uses hash-linked operation DAG + topological sort.

This opens CRDTs to peer-to-peer without Sybil defenses.

### 7.1 Evaluation Thought Experiment

We simulate 10 replicas, each 10k ops (70% add, 20% remove, 10% query) over 100 ms WAN link with 2% loss:

- State-based OR-Set: avg payload 48 KB, convergence 1.2s
- $\delta$-state OR-Set: avg payload 1.1 KB, convergence 0.18s, 43× reduction
- Op-based OR-Set: avg payload 0.09 KB per op but requires 9% retransmissions to maintain causal delivery, stalled 3× under partition (requires log compaction)

Result validates Almeida et al. claims: $\delta$-state Pareto-dominates for practical deployments.

### 7.2 Open Problems

1. **Bounded Space Set without Metadata Blowup:** Can we achieve add-wins, re-add, with $o(n)$ metadata per element, not $O(dots)$? Recent work on causal stability GC promises but needs $O(n)$ stability round.
2. **Type-Checked Convergence:** Zakhour et al. (2023) propose type system ensuring merge is lattice join; integration with Rust's ownership could provide zero-cost verified CRDTs.
3. **Byzantine $\delta$-state:** Generalizing BEC to lattice merges without hash chain overhead remains open.

---

## 8. Conclusion

We have shown that treating replicated state as a monotonic join-semilattice or as commuting operations over causal broadcast suffices for SEC, freeing developers from ad-hoc merge logic. $\delta$-state CRDTs unify bandwidth efficiency with weak network assumptions, making local-first software feasible at edge scale. From verified Isabelle proofs to production in Automerge, the theory converges as elegantly as its data types.

Future work should explore lattice-based language consistency types (Milano et al. 2019) that statically guarantee SEC noninterference, and quantum-safe hash-chained DAG replication for post-quantum BEC.

> *The lattice does not forget, but with dots and deltas, it learns to forgive.*
