# Hardware-Software Co-Design for Sparse Tensor Accelerators: OuterSPACE, Gamma, SIGMA Microarchitecture, Load-Balanced Merger Trees, and Compiler Mapping via TACO Sparse Iteration Theory

## Abstract

Sparse tensor algebra underpins contemporary workloads from deep neural network (DNN) training and inference to graph convolutional networks and quantum chemistry, where sparsity ratios of 70-90% arise from ReLU activations, pruning, and inherent dataset characteristics [6]. Dense systolic arrays, exemplified by the TPU and NVIDIA Tensor Cores, achieve peak efficiency by enforcing regular data movement but suffer severe under-utilization when faced with irregular, sparse general matrix-matrix multiplication (SpGEMM, SpMM). This thesis develops a unified hardware-software co-design framework that bridges three landmark accelerators—*OuterSPACE* [1], *Gamma* [2], and *SIGMA* [3]—through the formal lens of the *Tensor Algebra Compiler (TACO)* sparse iteration theory [4][5]. We dissect the architectural trade-offs between outer-product, row-wise (Gustavson), and flexible dot-product dataflows, characterize load-balanced merger trees and the Forwarding Adder Network (FAN) as irregular reduction substrates, and demonstrate compiler mappings that lower declarative Einsum specifications into accelerator dataflows via TeAAL's Einsum cascade abstraction [7]. Our analysis synthesizes 5.7x speedup over systolic baselines and asymptotic memory footprint reductions, providing design principles for next-generation sparse tensor cores.

## 1 Introduction

The ascendancy of sparse workloads has exposed a fundamental mismatch between dense accelerator microarchitecture and irregular computation. Consider $C = A \times B$ where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times K}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times N}$, and $\text{nnz}(A)/MK \ll 1$. A dense GEMM accelerator expends $O(MKN)$ multiply-accumulates regardless of zeros, while an ideal sparse engine expends $O(\text{flops}_{\text{sparse}})$ where $flops_{sparse} \propto \text{nnz}(A) \cdot \bar{d}_B$ for row-wise product [3]. The challenge lies not merely in skipping zeros but in *orchestrating data movement* when the positions of nonzeros are data-dependent and load-imbalanced.

Three architectural schools have emerged:

* **Outer-product school (OuterSPACE [1]):** Decompose SpGEMM into two phases: multiply phase generating $K$ outer-product partials $T_k = a_{:,k} b_{k,:}$, and merge phase reducing $T = \sum_k T_k$ via sorting/merging. Asynchronous SPMD PEs decouple control-divergent merge from compute-bound multiply.
* **Row-wise school (Gamma [2]):** Implements Gustavson's algorithm: for each nonzero $a_{i,k}$, fetch entire row $B_{k,:}$ and merge into $C_{i,:}$ using high-radix hardware mergers, with FiberCache and row-buffer locality to prune partials early [2][7].
* **Flexible dot-product school (SIGMA [3]):** Replaces rigid 2D systolic interconnect with Benes network for distribution and Forwarding Adder Network for irregular reductions, guaranteeing close-to-full PE utilization irrespective of shape and sparsity [3].

Compiler support remains fragmented. *TACO* [4][5] introduced the first sparse iteration theory that formulates sparse iteration spaces as lattice-ordered set expressions of nonzero coordinates, enabling code generation that co-iterates over disparate formats one dimension at a time. *TeAAL* [7] extends this to accelerator modeling, expressing OuterSPACE, Gamma, and SIGMA as Einsum cascades with operators $\mathsf{take}$, rank-swizzle, and fibertree transformations.

**Contributions:** (i) taxonomy of SpGEMM dataflows, (ii) microarchitectural deep-dive into merger trees and FAN, (iii) TACO-based lowering pipeline, (iv) empirical/E P analysis and proof sketches of traffic optimality.

> **Theorem 1 (Sparse Dataflow Duality):** Outer-product, inner-product, and row-wise SpGEMM algorithms are permutations of the loop order $(M,N,K)$ fused with different partial-output rank orders. OuterSPACE corresponds to $(K, M, N) \rightarrow (M,N,K)$, Gamma to $(M, K, N)$, and SIGMA to unrolled dot-product over $(K)$ with flexible $M,N$ mapping [7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Sparse Formats and Intensity

Sparse tensors are encoded via level formats: *dense*, *compressed*, *singleton*, *range* [4]. CSR = [dense, compressed], COO = [singleton, singleton], CSF generalizes to $N$-D. The *fiber* abstraction treats a tensor as nested lists; TeAAL's fibertree operationalizes format as traversal policy [7]. Metadata overhead varies: bitmap is never terrible and sometimes optimal for 70-90% sparsity [3]; CSR incurs pointer chase and load imbalance.

*Sparsity structure matters:* unstructured sparsity (pruned DNNs) exhibits Bernoulli $p \approx 0.1$, while block-sparse (2:4 NVIDIA STC) enforces $N$ nonzeros per $M$ block to simplify muxing. Gamma and SIGMA's value proposition is supporting *unstructured* sparsity without pattern constraints [2][3].

### 2.2 SpGEMM Algorithmic Variants

Table 1 summarizes classic algorithms.

| Variant | Loop Order | Partials | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inner-product | $i,j \rightarrow k$ | $0$ | output-local | $2\times$ input read |
| Outer-product | $k \rightarrow i,j$ | $K\cdot \bar{nnz}$ | asymptotic reuse | merge bottleneck |
| Row-wise (Gustavson) | $i \rightarrow k \rightarrow j$ | $nnz(A)$ rows | balanced compute/comm | row-length variance |
| Column-wise | symmetric | ... | ... | ... |

*OuterSPACE* adopts outer-product to expose parallelism over $K$, taming variance in $M,N$ dimensions via asynchronous PEs with software-controlled scratchpads and non-coherent caches to avoid 30-70% writeback traffic [1].

*Gamma* refines Gustavson: linear-time merge without sorting full output, exploiting concordant traversal when reduction dimension $K$ becomes innermost after rank swizzle [7]. High-radix merge (e.g., 64-way) reduces merge passes from $O(\log_{2} P)$ to $O(\log_{64} P)$.

*SIGMA* observes systolic arrays are optimal only when $M,N,K$ are multiples of array dimension [3]. Its Flex-DPE uses rich interconnect to map arbitrary $(M,K)\times(K,N)$ tiles, handling dropout-induced runtime variance.

### 2.3 Sparseloop and AESPA

Recent analytical frameworks like *Sparseloop* [8] and *AESPA* [9] quantify EDP benefits of heterogeneity: homogeneous ExTensor-like inner-product achieves 2.6x EDP vs EIE-like SpMM, while heterogeneous AESPA+ reaches 7.9x with variable parallelism dimensions [9]. This motivates co-design rather than single dataflow.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology fuses *architectural modeling* with *compiler theory*.

1. **Deconstruct Accelerators into TeAAL Einsum Cascades:** Each accelerator is expressed as:
   ```python
   # Gamma cascade (simplified TeAAL spec)
   Einsum("T[m,k,n] = A[m,k] * B[k,n] ; take intersection")
   Einsum("C[m,n] = T[m,k,n] ; merge reduction over k")
   Arch = { FiberCache(depth=2), HighRadixMerger(radix=64) }
   ```
   Operators: $\mathsf{take}$ filters $B$ rows by $A$'s nonzero pattern; rank-swizzle $T_{[M,K,N]} \rightarrow T_{[M,N,K]}$ enables K-reduction after sorting [7].

2. **Formalize TACO Sparse Iteration Theory:** Sparse iteration space $I = \{ (i,j) | A_{ij} \neq 0 \lor B_{jk} \neq 0 \}$ represented as lattice $(L, \sqcap, \sqcup)$ where $\sqcap$ = intersection (multiplication) and $\sqcup$ = union (addition) [4]. Coiteration regions partition lattice by operator annihilator properties: $0 \cdot x = 0$, $x+0 = x$. Code generator emits loops:
   ```tla
   ---- MODULE TacoCoiter ----
   VARIABLES i, j, k, A_pos, B_pos
   Coiterate(i) == \E ap \in A_i, bp \in B_k : ap.coord = bp.coord
   * refinement: lattice merge emits while(A_pos < A_len /\ B_pos < B_len)
   ```

3. **Microarchitectural Prototyping via Timeloop/Sparseloop:** Model PE array, on-chip buffer hierarchy (double-buffered scratchpad vs hardware cache), interconnect energy per bit, and merger FAN nodes. Use Sparseloop mapping to search tile sizes balancing reuse vs occupancy [8].

4. **Image-Diagram Synthesis:** Generate whiteboard-style vector diagrams to articulate dataflows, serving as visual proof scaffolding for publication-quality dissemination.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 OuterSPACE: Outer-Product Dataflow and Asynchronous Merger

OuterSPACE's insight: inner-product suffers from per-output random gather of $K$-dimension; outer-product exposes streaming over $K$ with large partials amenable to scratchpad [1]. Two phases:

* **Phase P (Multiply):** $P$ PEs each produce partial matrices $D_k = a_{:,k} \otimes b_{k,:}$ stored in linked-list format with tags (row, col). Software-controlled scratchpads prevent wasted DRAM accesses; SPMD decoupling lets control-divergent length lists run in parallel unlike SIMD which partially serializes [1].

* **Phase M (Merge):** Hierarchically merges $|K|$ sorted runs into final CSR $C$. Merge resembles $K$-way sort; reconfigurable PEs switch from multiply to merge kernels, enabling load balance when partial count skew >4x.

> **Theorem 2 (OuterSPACE Traffic):** For $\bar{d}$ avg nnz per column of $A$, outer-product generates $\bar{d}_A \cdot \bar{d}_B \cdot K$ intermediates vs. inner-product's $O(MN K)$ dense accesses. When $\rho < 0.1$, this reduces off-chip bytes by $1/\rho$ [1].

Key limitation: merge accounts for 60-70% time due to sorting [2]; Gamma's answer is eliminating global sort.

**Code perspective (Rust emitter):**
```rust
// OuterSPACE multiply emitter sketch

    let a_col = csr_col_slice(&A, k); // compressed fiber

            partials[k].push(Partial {i, j, v: aval*bval});
        }

}
```

### 4.2 Gamma: Row-Wise Dataflow, FiberCache, High-Radix Merger

Gamma builds on Gustavson but replaces heap merge (logarithmic per element) with hardware high-radix mergers that keep $F$ rows of $B$ resident in FiberCache [2]. Operational flow:

1. Distribute rows $A_i$ to PEs via work-stealing queue balancing $nnz(A_i)$ variance.
2. For each $a_{i,k} \neq 0$, *take* $B_{k,:}$ — hardware intersection unit filters zero rows.
3. Fetch $B_k$ row into cache; if evicted, spilled to DRAM with 2x bandwidth thanks to compressed format.
4. Merge $R$ cached rows via 64-way merger pipeline: $O(R/\text{radix})$ cycles vs. $O(R \log R)$ software.

**Analytical EDP:** On SuiteSparse matrices, Gamma reduces partial output traffic (number of intermediate pairs written to DRAM) by 3.1x vs. OuterSPACE [2]; high-radix merger reduces merge energy by 38% over binary merge tree due to fewer register-file spills.

*Load-balanced tree:* Gamma's merger tree is not static: when FiberCache overflows ($nnz(C_i) > \text{SRAM}$), it splits row into sub-fibers, spawning sub-tasks to idle PEs — analogous to TACO's nonzero-tiling [5].

```haskell
-- Gamma's take operator in Haskell DSL
takeRows :: CSR a -> [Row a] -> [Row a]
gammaPhi Arow Brow = 
  [ mergeRows (map (fetch B) (nzIndices Arow)) ]
  where mergeRows = foldr1 (highRadixMerge 64)
-- rank swizzle: [M,K,N] -> [M,N,K] for concordant reduction
```

> **Theorem 3 (Gamma Concordance):** If $T$ has rank order $[M,K,N]$ and reduction is over $K$, a rank swizzle to $[M,N,K]$ makes $K$ innermost, enabling streaming reduction without random gathering; TeAAL inserts this automatically when bottommost rank $\neq$ reduction rank [7].

### 4.3 SIGMA: Flexible Interconnects, Forwarding Adder Network

SIGMA tackles *irregular GEMM shapes* — e.g., transformer FC layers $M=1, N=4096, K=1024$ with 80% sparsity — where systolic arrays utilization $<10$% [3]. Innovation stack:

* **Flex-DPE:** 128 multipliers per PE, each fed via 4-stage Benes network (rearrangeably non-blocking, $O(N \log N)$ switches) distributing arbitrary $A,B$ elements. Benes guarantees any permutation with <2x contention under random traffic.
* **FAN (Forwarding Adder Network):** Conventional reduction tree produces *one* output per cycle. FAN augments tree with forwarding links that can *bypass* adders, allowing multiple independent dot-products to share tree: e.g., 4 rows of differing lengths reduced concurrently [3]. This yields close-to-full utilization even when $K$ < array size.
* **Bitmap encoding:** vs CSR/COO, bitmap's fixed 1-bit per element costs $MN$ bits but enables O(1) random access and parallel prefix-sum decode, optimal when sparsity ratio varies across model layers during training [3].

**Scaling result:** Demonstrated 10.8 TFLOPS effective at 22.33W, 65.10 $mm^2$ 28nm [3]; 5.7x over TPU-like systolic, 3x over EIE/ExTensor-like sparse baselines. 7.9x EDP gain in heterogeneous AESPA+ configuration [9].

```python
# SIGMA mapping pseudo-map via Sparseloop
import timeloop
arch = timeloop.Arch("sigma.yaml") # Benes + FAN
workload = timeloop.Workload(M=1,N=1024,K=4096,sparsity=0.8)
mapper.map(workload, arch, objective="edp")
# mapper finds tile_m=1, tile_k flexible via FAN sharing
```

*Trade-off table:*

| Metric | Systolic | OuterSPACE | Gamma | SIGMA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best dataflow | MN-stationary | K-parallel | row-parallel | flexible dot |
| Merge cost | N/A | high sort | medium radix | low FAN |
| Utilization @ irregular | <20% | 45% | 68% | 92% [3] |
| Interconnect | mesh | NoC async SPMD | crossbar + cache | Benes + FAN |

### 4.4 TACO Sparse Iteration Theory and Compiler Mapping

TACO's central abstraction: *sparse iteration theory* [4] replaces dense loop nests with lattice coiteration. Define format lattice $L = \{ (c,p) | c \in coordinates, p \in positions \}$. Set expression $S = A \cap B$ denotes intersection coiteration required for $A * B$. Compiler:

1. **Lift** tensor index expression to iteration graph $G$ with nodes = index vars, edges = dependencies [4].
2. **Construct** sparse iteration lattice via distributive law: $(A + B) * C = (A*C) \cup (B*C)$ partitions into regions with distinct merge ops [5].
3. **Emit** while loops:
   ```c
   // TACO generated kernel for row-wise SpGEMM (C = A*B)
   for (int i=0; i<M; ++i) {
     for (int ap=A_pos[i]; ap<A_pos[i+1]; ) {
       for (int k=A_idx[ap]; ; ) { // coiterate B_k
         // range scan
       }
     }
   }
   ```
   When format is CSR, inner dense exp becomes positions array walk — precisely Gamma's FiberCache fetch [5].

**Transformation API:** Senanayake et al. [5] tracks mapping functions $f: I_{orig} \rightarrow I_{derived}$ to allow `split`, `reorder`, `tiling` on sparse iteration spaces. Tiling *universe* (coordinate space) = dense tiling; tiling *nonzeros* (subset) = load-balanced nonzero blocks → Gamma's work stealing, SIGMA's Flex-DPE tile flex [5].

> **Theorem 4 (Nonzero Tiling Optimality):** Tiling nonzero space into $P$ equal-nnz blocks yields statically load-balanced $O(nnz/P)$ per PE, minimizing $\max_i load_i$ under power-law $nnz$ distribution to within $1 + \epsilon$ [5].

TeAAL binds TACO to accelerators: format spec chooses CSR for $A$, compressed for $B$; architecture spec declares mergers; binding spec maps Einsum rank orders to PE groups. This reproduces OuterSPACE's $K$-parallel cascade: two Einsums fused? No, OuterSPACE fuses via disable — Gamma fuses multiply-merge into single cascade [7].

### 4.5 Unified Co-Design View

*When to use which?* **OuterSPACE** excels when $K$ large and $M,N$ moderate (graph all-pairs). **Gamma** excels when $M$ large, row sparsity skew high (recommendation). **SIGMA** excels when $M,N,K$ irregular and sparsity dynamic during training (dropout, pruning evolution). AESPA+ [9] composes them heterogeneously: TPU-like for dense layers, ExTensor-like inner-product for moderately sparse, OuterSPACE-like outer-product for highly sparse, with Pareto frontier scheduler [3 in TACO autoscheduling].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Asymptotic Cost Model

Following Ahrens & Kjolstad asymptotic cost model [10], schedule cost $Cost = (Iter, Move)$. $Iter$ = iterations executed, $Move$ = bytes moved. OuterSPACE reduces $Move$ from $O(MK+KN)$ dense to $O(nnz(A)+nnz(B)+nnz_{part})$ but increases $Iter$ via merge. Gamma's $Move$ further prunes partials via FiberCache locality: proves $Move_{Gamma} = Move_{OuterSPACE} - \Delta_{cache}$ where $\Delta_{cache} = \sum_i \min_{cache} redundant fetches$ [2][10].

Empirically on Reddit, Amazon-0312 graphs, Gamma 1.8-3.2x speedup over Sparch, Extensor baseline [2]. SIGMA sustains >90% utilization across 400 GEMM shapes from DNN training, achieving 10.8 TFLOPS effective vs 1.9 TFLOPS systolic [3].

**Table 2: EDP benefits (heterogeneous analysis [9]):**

| Accelerator | Peak TFLOPS | EDP gain vs Homog-EIE | Parallelism bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| TPU-like GEMM | 34.56 | 0.13x | M*N |
| ExTensor-like | 9.98 | 2.6x | M or N |
| OuterSPACE-like | 24.06 | 0.4x | K |
| SIGMA heterogeneous | 16.90 | 7.9x | Variable |

### 5.2 Formal Sketch – Merger Tree Reduction

*Define* high-radix merger $M_R$ processing $R$ sorted runs of total length $L$. Complexity $T(R) = L \cdot \lceil \log_R P \rceil /R_{par}$ where $R_{par}$ = parallelism. FAN extends to multiple concurrent reductions: $T_{FAN}(R_1..R_k) = \max_i L_i /BW + forwarding latency \log N$. Proof by induction on tree depth, forwarding links bypass congested adders, reducing critical path from $O(N)$ to $O(\log N + k)$ [3].

### 5.3 Compiler Lowering Correctness

We sketch correctness of TACO lowering to Gamma: lattice expression $S = \bigcup_{k \in nz(A_i)} B_k$ maintained invariant that iterator pos $p_k$ points to smallest unseen coordinate $\geq$ output coord. While loop advances min coord; $\mathsf{merge}$ emits $C_{i,j}$ only when multiplicity $\ge 1$. Rank swizzle preserves lexicographic order equivalence [7]. QED by lattice monotonicity [4].

## 6 Limitations

* **Dynamic sparsity evolution:** SIGMA's bitmap still writes $MN$ bits when sparsity drops from 90% to 50% mid-training; adaptive re-encoding cost non-trivial. TACO dynamic formats [11] (BST, pointer-based fiber) not yet mapped to accelerator scratchpads—only array-based CSR.
* **Merge-bound tail latency:** Gamma's work-stealing handles variance but global barrier after rows complete leaves 5-8% idle at tail; OuterSPACE's non-coherent scratchpads require explicit flush, complicating consistency model [1].
* **Compiler search explosion:** Sparseloop design space Cartesian product of format, tiling, dataflow ~ $10^{12}$ points; asymptotic scheduler Pareto frontier reduces orders of magnitude [10] yet heuristics still miss optimality for >3D tensors [4][6].
* **Precision & training:** SIGMA demonstration FP32; BF16 mixed-precision training requires stochastic rounding handling not in FAN; EDP projections optimistic for INT8.
* **Tensor train (TTc) kernels [6]:** Our analysis restricted to binary SpGEMM; order-$>2$ contraction optimization (TTTc) requires loop transformation $f$ mapping non-trivial [6]; TeAAL cascade length explodes.

> *Future work:* co-search of dataflow, format, and sparsity pattern via ML-based cost predictor; heterogeneous chiplets where each chiplet implements one of OuterSPACE/Gamma/SIGMA dataflows and scheduler migrates layers dynamically.

## 7 Conclusion

We unified three sparse tensor accelerators under TACO sparse iteration theory, revealing outer-product, row-wise, and flexible dot-product as loop-order permutations unified by rank-swizzle and $\mathsf{take}$ operators. OuterSPACE's asynchronous multiply-merge demonstrates merits of decoupling compute-bound and memory-bound phases; Gamma's FiberCache and high-radix merger prune partial traffic with concordant traversal; SIGMA's Benes + FAN guarantees near-full utilization on irregular dynamic-sparse GEMMs. TACO's lattice-based code generation and transformation API provides the missing compiler substrate to target this diversity without hand-written kernels.

The synthesis suggests future sparse hardware will be *heterogeneous ensembles* [9]—not single dataflow—paired with autoschedulers that reason asymptotically about $Cost = (Iter, Move)$ [10]. By exposing sparse iteration spaces as first-class compiler constructs, we move towards accessible, performant sparse tensor compilers that finally close the gap between theoretical flop savings and wall-clock speedups.
