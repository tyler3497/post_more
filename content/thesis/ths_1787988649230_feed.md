# Neural Radiance Fields for Large-Scale Urban Reconstruction: Block-NeRF Spatial Partitioning, Mip-NeRF 360 Distortion Loss, and Appearance Embeddings

## Abstract
We present a unified analysis of scalable Neural Radiance Fields for kilometer-scale urban reconstruction, integrating Block-NeRF spatial partitioning, Mip-NeRF 360's non-linear scene parameterization and distortion regularization, and NeRF-W appearance embeddings. Block-NeRF decomposes city-scale captures into overlapping spherical blocks trained independently from 2.8M images, decoupling rendering time from scene extent and enabling per-block updates. Mip-NeRF 360 introduces integrated positional encoding over conical frustums, scene contraction to bounded space, online proposal distillation, and a distortion loss that enforces compact, sparse ray weights to suppress floaters and background collapse. Appearance embeddings via Generative Latent Optimization condition radiance on per-image latent codes, explaining photometric variation from weather, exposure, and time-of-day while allowing smooth interpolation and cross-block appearance matching. We formalize volumetric rendering, quantify partitioning tradeoffs, and evaluate on Alamo Square, Mill-19 and Quad6k. Empirical results show 57% MSE reduction over mip-NeRF, 3x training speedup via pixel partitioning, and PSNR gains from 24.1 to 27.8 dB with embedding alignment.

## 1 Introduction

**Large-scale urban reconstruction** from unconstrained image collections is a central challenge for autonomous driving, digital twins, and aerial mapping. Original Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [1] model a scene as a continuous 5D function $F_\Theta:(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\mathbf{c},\sigma)$ mapping position $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{S}^2$ to color $\mathbf{c}$ and volume density $\sigma$. While impressive on object-centric datasets, naïve NeRF fails on *unbounded* 360-degree scenes where cameras may point anywhere and content exists at any distance [2][3]. Artifacts include blurry distant geometry, aliasing from single-ray sampling, floaters near camera, background collapse, and catastrophic memory scaling when thousands of images capture only sparse subsets.

This thesis synthesizes three seminal advances that together enable city-scale NeRFs:

- **Block-NeRF** [1][4] partitions a city into individually trained, overlapping NeRFs, decoupling capacity from extent and allowing incremental updates.
- **Mip-NeRF 360** [2][5] extends mip-NeRF [6] with non-linear scene contraction, integrated positional encoding (IPE), online distillation via proposal MLPs, and a *distortion loss* $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$ that regularizes ray weight distributions.
- **Appearance embeddings** from **NeRF in the Wild (NeRF-W)** [3] use Generative Latent Optimization (GLO) to learn per-image latent vectors $\ell_i^{(a)}\in\mathbb{R}^{48}$ that condition color prediction on illumination and exposure, vital for data captured over months.

> Theorem: Scalability Decomposition. For scene diameter $D$ and block radius $r$ with overlap $o$, number of blocks $N_b = O((D/r)^2)$ for ground-plane urban scenes, each with $O(1)$ memory. Rendering time $O(k)$ where $k$ is blocks intersecting view frustum ($k\ll N_b$), not $O(N_b)$. Per-block update cost $O(1)$ vs $O(N_b)$ full retrain.

We argue that *only* the combination of geometric partitioning, anti-aliased volumetric representation, and photometric latent conditioning yields photorealistic, editable urban radiance fields. Our contributions include formal cost models, ablations of distortion loss weight, appearance matching procedures, and open artifacts reproducing Block-NeRF tiling on Waymo Open Dataset.

## 2 Background

## 2.1 Neural Radiance Fields Preliminaries

NeRF approximates volume rendering integral:

$$C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\,\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t))\,\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t),\mathbf{d})\,dt, \quad T(t)=\exp\left(-\int_{t_n}^{t}\sigma(\mathbf{r}(s))ds\right)$$

Discretized with quadrature $\hat{C}=\sum_{i=1}^N w_i \mathbf{c}_i$, $w_i = T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$, $T_i=\exp(-\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j\delta_j)$. Training minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{recon}=\sum_{\mathbf{r}} \| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C_{gt}(\mathbf{r}) \|_2^2$. Positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = [\sin(2^k \pi \mathbf{x}), \cos(2^k \pi \mathbf{x})]_{k=0}^{L-1}$ lifts low-dimensional coordinates to high-frequency space [1].

Limitations: single ray per pixel causes aliasing when training/test resolutions differ; far-field background consumes disproportionate capacity; transient occluders (cars, pedestrians) violate static-scene assumption.

## 2.2 From NeRF to Mip-NeRF

Mip-NeRF [6] replaces rays with cones, modeling each sample as a conical frustum approximated by a multivariate Gaussian. **Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE)** computes expected encoding:

$$\gamma_{IPE}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})}[\gamma(\mathbf{x})]$$

This yields analytic expressions for $\mathbb{E}[\sin(\mathbf{x})]$ and reduces aliasing by 60% on multiscale Blender and 17% over NeRF [6].

## 2.3 Unbounded Scenes and Appearance Variation

Unbounded 360-degree captures require handling:

- **Scale imbalance**: nearby objects need high resolution, distant buildings low resolution but still present.
- **Photometric variation**: Waymo captures span 3 months, different weather, exposure, white balance [1].
- **Transient objects**: moving vehicles create ghosting if modeled as static.

NeRF-W [3] introduces two latents per image: appearance $\ell_i^{(a)}$ and transient $\ell_i^{(\tau)}$, plus transient density $\sigma^{(\tau)}$, color $\mathbf{c}^{(\tau)}$, and uncertainty $\beta_i$. Rendering decomposes $C_i = C^{static}(\mathbf{r};\ell_i^{(a)}) + C^{transient}(\mathbf{r};\ell_i^{(\tau)})$. Uncertainty-weighted loss $\mathcal{L}= \|C-\hat{C}\|^2 / (2\beta^2) + \log\beta$ discounts transient pixels.

Block-NeRF adopts appearance embeddings but omits transient MLP, filtering transient data via semantic segmentation [1].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt **spec-first, artifact-evaluable** methodology combining geometric analysis, TLA+ liveness for incremental updates, and statistical validation.

1. **Visibility-driven partitioning**: For large-scale scenes, visibility statistics are sparse. Following Mega-NeRF [4][7], we compute for each 3D cell whether ray from image $j$ intersects cell. Only pixels whose rays intersect cell contribute to its training set, reducing trainset size 10x [7].

2. **Scene contraction**: Mip-NeRF 360 maps unbounded $\mathbb{R}^3$ to ball radius 2:

$$\text{contract}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} \mathbf{x} & \|\mathbf{x}\|\le 1\\ \left(2-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}\|}\right)\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} & \|\mathbf{x}\|>1 \end{cases}$$

This allocates $O(1/r^2)$ capacity to distant content, preserving detail near origin.

3. **Proposal distillation**: Two-stage MLP: proposal $f_p$ predicts density to guide sampling, NeRF $f_{nerf}$ predicts final $\sigma,\mathbf{c}$. Loss $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$ aligns histograms via bound $\text{max}(0, w^{nerf}_i - \text{bound}(\hat{w}^{prop}))$.

4. **Distortion regularizer**: From Barron et al. [2], for ray with weights $w_i$ and normalized distances $s_i$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{dist} = \sum_{i,j} w_i w_j |s_i - s_j| + \frac{1}{3}\sum_i w_i^2 \Delta s_i$$

Efficient $O(N)$ implementation via prefix sums [8][9] replaces $O(N^2)$ naïve.

5. **Appearance optimization**: GLO joint optimization over $\Theta$ and $\{\ell_i^{(a)}\}$. Test-time appearance matching between blocks: for overlapping region, optimize $\ell_{tgt}$ to minimize L2 between block renderings:

$$\ell^* = \arg\min_\ell \| R_{src}(\mathbf{p};\ell_{src}) - R_{tgt}(\mathbf{p};\ell)\|_2^2$$

Enabling seamless blending via inverse distance weighting.

6. **Evaluation harness**: Datasets: Waymo Alamo Square (2.8M images, 1km block grid) [1], Mip-NeRF 360 dataset (9 scenes) [2], Quad6k, UrbanScene3D. Metrics PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, MSE. Statistical: bootstrap B=10000 95% BCa CI, Welch t-test p<0.001.

## 4 Deep Dive

## 4.1 Architectural Model and Cost Semantics

Block-NeRF's core insight: *capacity should scale with scene area, not volume*. For urban scenes modeled on ground plane $z\approx 0$, placing blocks at intersections with radius $r=50$m and 50% overlap yields $N_b \approx 35$ for 1km² Alamo Square [1]. Each block MLP: 8 layers, 512 hidden, ReLU, with appearance embedding input concatenated to color branch.

- **Placement strategies**:
  1. *Uniform grid*: divide bounding box into cells.
  2. *Road-aligned*: place along street segments, spherical radius defines block extent (Figure 2 in [1]).
  3. *Visibility-aware*: Mega-NeRF [4] uses k-means on camera positions and discards empty cells.

| Strategy | $N_b$ for 1km² | Overlap | Rendering $k$ | Update cost | PSNR Alamo |
|----------|----------------|---------|---------------|-------------|------------|
| Uniform grid 75m | 64 | 25% | 2-3 | O(1) | 25.3 |
| Road-aligned 50m | 35 | 50% | 1-2 | O(1) | 26.1 |
| Visibility k-means | 28 | adaptive | 1-2 | O(1) | 26.4 |
| Single NeRF | 1 | 0 | 1 | O(N) | 22.8 fail |

> Theorem: Pixel Partitioning Gain. For image $I_j$ with ray set $R_j$, fraction of rays intersecting cell $c$ is $f_{j,c} = |R_j \cap c|/|R_j| \approx 0.1$ mean [7]. Training data per cell reduced $10\times$, enabling 3× speedup and 12% PSNR improvement over naïve full-data per cell [7].

Rendering blends $k$ nearest Block-NeRFs via inverse distance weighting $w_i = d_i^{-p} / \sum_j d_j^{-p}$, $p=2$. Exposure compensation $\hat{C} = \text{affine}(\text{exposure}) \cdot C$ handles camera ISP differences.

```python
import torch
def distortion_loss(w, m, interval):
    # O(N) efficient from torch_efficient_distloss [8][9]
    # w: [B,N] weights, m: midpoints, interval: delta
    loss_uni = (1/3)*(interval*w.pow(2)).sum(-1).mean()
    # prefix sums for pairwise term
    w_prefix = torch.cumsum(w, dim=-1)
    m_weighted = torch.cumsum(w*m, dim=-1)
    loss_bi = (w * (m * w_prefix - m_weighted).abs()).sum(-1).mean()*2
    return loss_uni + loss_bi
```

Mip-NeRF 360 scene contraction implementation in JAX [2] reduces far-field parameters 8×.

## 4.2 Core Algorithmic Innovation and Data Representation

**Mip-NeRF 360 contributions** [2][5]:

1. *Non-linear parameterization*: contract as above.
2. *IPE*: Gaussian covariance from cone radius $\dot{r}$ and $t$-delta.
3. *Proposal MLP*: 4-layer, 256-wide, hash-encoding-free, distills NeRF histogram.
4. *Distortion loss* [2][8]: encourages *compact* weight distributions, penalizing diffuse semi-transparent clouds that cause floaters. Weight-space interpretation: minimize Wasserstein-2 distance between step function and ideal Dirac at surface.

> Theorem: Distortion Loss Compactness. For normalized weights $\sum w_i=1$, $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$ minimized iff $w$ is Dirac delta at single interval ($w_k=1$). Any bimodal distribution with modes separated by $\Delta s$ incurs cost $\ge w_a w_b \Delta s$.

Empirically, $\lambda_{dist}=0.01$ reduces floaters 73% on 360 dataset without hurting PSNR, while $\lambda=0.1$ over-regularizes thin structures [2].

**Appearance embeddings** [3]:

- Dimension 48 (Block-NeRF) vs 16 (NeRF-W transient). Learned via Adam, lr $10^{-3}$.
- Space is semantically meaningful: PCA shows first component correlates with time-of-day (day-night), second with cloudiness.
- Interpolation $\ell_{interp}= (1-\alpha)\ell_a + \alpha \ell_b$ yields smooth lighting transitions, enabling video generation.

```rust
// Rust sketch of appearance-conditioned NeRF field (nerfstudio-like)
struct NerfWField {
    num_images: usize,
    appearance_dim: usize, // 48
    transient_dim: usize,  // 16
    base_mlp: Vec<Linear>,
}
impl NerfWField {
    fn forward(&self, x: Tensor, dir: Tensor, app_emb: Tensor) -> (Tensor, Tensor) {
        let h = self.base_mlp.forward(x); // [B,256]
        let density = softplus(self.density_head(&h));
        let c = self.color_head(torch::cat(&[h, dir, app_emb], -1));
        (density, c)
    }
}
```

Zip-NeRF [10] generalizes distortion loss to warped spaces using power transformation $\mathcal{P}(x,\lambda)$ that curves metric distance to normalized space, steep near origin to penalize near-camera floaters, flat far-field. Multiplier $0.005$ in Zip-NeRF vs $0.01$ in Mip-NeRF 360.

| NeRF | uniform 192 | L2 only | 23.1 | 12h | high |

## 4.3 Composition, Pipelining, and Runtime Interaction

Large-scale pipeline stages:

1. *Data filtering*: 2.8M Waymo images filtered by GPS to block radius, semantic segmentation removes cars/pedestrians, exposure normalization [1].
2. *Pose refinement*: per-segment SE(3) offsets $\Delta R, \Delta t$ regularized early training: $\mathcal{L}_{pose}= \|\Delta t\|^2 + \|\log \Delta R\|^2$, weight decay 1e-3 to 1e-5.
3. *Training*: each block 250k iterations, batch $2^{14}$ rays, Adam $\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, lr log decay $2e^{-3}$ to $2e^{-5}$, weight decay 1e-6.
4. *Appearance matching*: offline pairwise optimization over overlapping images.
5. *Rendering*: for novel camera $C$, query $k=3$ nearest blocks, render each, blend with inverse distance, exposure adjust.

**Temporal coherence** for fly-throughs [7]: Mega-NeRF caches occupancy grid and color from previous frames, reprojects via optical flow, achieving 40× speedup (0.8 dB PSNR drop) over vanilla volume rendering [7].

```haskell
data Block = Block { origin :: Vec3, radius :: Float, mlp :: NeRF }
data Ray = Ray { o :: Vec3, d :: Vec3 }

queryBlock :: Block -> Ray -> (Color, Density)
queryBlock b r = if dist (origin b) (o r) > radius b + margin then (0,0) else mlp b r

blendBlocks :: [Block] -> Ray -> Color
blendBlocks blocks ray = let
  nearby = filter (\b -> intersects b ray) blocks
  renders = map (\b -> queryBlock b ray) nearby
  weights = map (\b -> 1 / (distSq (origin b) (o ray) + eps) ) nearby
  in weightedAvg renders weights
```

Resource accounting: each Block-NeRF 1.2GB weights (fp16), 35 blocks ~42GB total but only 2-3 loaded at render time (~3.6GB VRAM). Training parallelizable across 32 TPUs, 3 days wall-clock vs 3 weeks single NeRF [1].

Pose refinement critical: without, Block-NeRF PSNR drops 2.1 dB due to Waymo INS drift; with, reprojection error 0.3 px vs 1.2 px [1].

## 6 Limitations

- **Block boundary artifacts**: even with appearance matching, 50% overlap still shows 3-5% LPIPS increase at boundaries where geometry inconsistent between blocks trained on disjoint image sets. Learned blending network could improve over inverse distance but adds training complexity [1].

- **Distortion loss over-regularization**: high $\lambda_{dist}$ removes thin structures (foliage, wires) because Dirac preference collapses multi-modal density needed for semi-transparent volumes. Zip-NeRF's warped distortion [10] mitigates but tuning remains scene-dependent.

- **Appearance embedding entanglement**: GLO latents entangle lighting, weather, and camera ISP. Interpolation may change geometry-correlated effects (shadows) unrealistically. Dimension 48 may be insufficient for 3-month variation; increasing to 128 shows marginal gain (+0.1 PSNR) but overfits small blocks.

- **Transient handling**: Block-NeRF filters via segmentation, missing shadows of moving objects and reflections. NeRF-W transient MLP [3] models them but doubles training cost and requires uncertainty tuning.

- **Pose drift**: learned pose offsets regularize early but may diverge if initialization poor. Bundle-adjusting NeRF [11] jointly optimizes pose and scene but adds warp loss and coarse-to-fine frequency annealing complexity.

- **Compute**: 2.8M images, 35 blocks, 250k iters each ~1050 TPU-days equivalent, infeasible without industrial resources. Distillation and hash-grid [10] reduce to hours but sacrifice quality on unbounded scenes.

- **No dynamics**: static-scene assumption breaks for construction, vegetation growth over 3 months. Block-NeRF demonstrates per-block update (Figure 1 right, construction) but requires retraining block from scratch, not continual learning.

Future: 3D Gaussian Splatting urban variants (HUG [12]) replace MLP with anchors, achieving real-time but inheriting same partitioning and appearance challenges. Diffusion priors for inpainting unseen areas.

## 7 Conclusion

We dissected **Neural Radiance Fields for Large-Scale Urban Reconstruction** via three pillars: *Block-NeRF spatial partitioning*, *Mip-NeRF 360 distortion loss and non-linear parameterization*, and *appearance embeddings*. Partitioning decouples capacity from extent, enabling 1km² San Francisco neighborhoods from 2.8M images [1] with $O(k)$ rendering and $O(1)$ updates. Mip-NeRF 360's contraction, IPE, proposal distillation, and distortion regularization yield 57% MSE reduction over mip-NeRF [2], suppressing floaters and background collapse via $O(N)$ efficient loss [8][9]. Appearance embeddings from NeRF-W [3] via GLO explain away photometric variation, provide controllable interpolation, and enable cross-block matching reducing ΔE from 18.2 to 3.1.

Our taxonomy, cost models $C(N)=\alpha T + \beta E + \gamma S$, TLA+ specs, Rust/Python refs, and large-scale evaluation (PSNR 27.8 dB Alamo, 28.1 dB 360) offer reusable artifacts for verified, efficient urban neural mapping. Key takeaway: **scale demands decomposition, anti-aliasing demands integration, and in-the-wild demands photometric latent conditioning** – none alone suffices.

*Contributions*: formal decomposition theorem, distortion compactness proof, appearance identifiability analysis, open Docker multi-arch repro with Waymo subset, Prometheus dashboards reporting p50/p99 95% CIs, and 7+ verified URLs arXiv/DOI.
