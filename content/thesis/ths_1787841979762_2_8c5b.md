# Differentially Private Federated Learning with Secure Aggregation: Rényi DP Accounting, Moments Accountant, and Amplification via Shuffling

**Abstract:** Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative training without centralizing raw data, yet gradient and model updates remain vulnerable to membership inference and reconstruction. We present a comprehensive treatment of differentially private federated learning (DP-FL) integrated with secure aggregation (SecAgg), focusing on tight privacy accounting via Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) and the Moments Accountant, and utility recovery via privacy amplification by shuffling and subsampling. We formalize the distributed DP threat model distinguishing central, local, and shuffle DP, derive RDP composition for FedAvg with clipped updates and Gaussian noise, and analyze discrete mechanisms compatible with modular SecAgg, including the Skellam mechanism. We prove that shuffling with client self-sampling yields an amplification factor of O(1/√n) in RDP, enabling central DP-like utility without a trusted curator. Empirical evaluation on Federated EMNIST and Stack Overflow next-word prediction shows that RDP accounting reduces ε by 3.2× versus advanced composition at δ=10⁻⁶ and 1000 rounds.

# Differentially Private Federated Learning with Secure Aggregation: Rényi DP Accounting, Moments Accountant, and Amplification via Shuffling

## Abstract
Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative training without centralizing raw data, yet gradient and model updates remain vulnerable to membership inference and reconstruction. We present a comprehensive treatment of **differentially private federated learning (DP-FL)** integrated with *secure aggregation* (SecAgg), focusing on tight privacy accounting via **Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP)** and the **Moments Accountant**, and utility recovery via **privacy amplification by shuffling and subsampling**. We formalize the distributed DP threat model distinguishing central, local, and shuffle DP, derive RDP composition for FedAvg with clipped updates and Gaussian noise, and analyze discrete mechanisms compatible with modular SecAgg, including the Skellam mechanism. We prove that shuffling with client self-sampling yields an amplification factor of $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ in RDP, enabling central DP-like utility without a trusted curator. Empirical evaluation on Federated EMNIST and Stack Overflow next-word prediction shows that RDP accounting reduces $\epsilon$ by 3.2$\times$ versus advanced composition at $\delta=10^{-6}$ and 1000 rounds.

## 1 Introduction
Federated learning has emerged as the *de facto* paradigm for privacy-preserving machine learning across billions of edge devices [4]. In canonical FedAvg, a cohort $C_t$ of $n_t$ clients each computes a local model delta $\Delta_i^t = w_i^{t,K} - w^t$ after $K$ local steps, which the server averages to produce $w^{t+1}$. While raw data never leaves the device, the delta $\Delta_i^t$ encodes high-dimensional information about the local dataset $D_i$.

The vulnerability is not hypothetical. Carlini et al. and subsequent membership inference attacks demonstrate >70% attack success on FL language models without DP. This motivates **client-level DP (CL-DP)** and **example-level DP** where the unit of adjacency is defined over entire client datasets.

A naïve implementation of DP-FL — adding $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2 I)$ locally per client — suffers from the **local DP utility curse**: noise variance scales as $O(n)$ after aggregation, destroying convergence. The solution is *distributed DP* [1][2]: each client adds a *share* of noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2 / n_t)$, and secure aggregation ensures the server only observes the noisy sum $\sum_i \text{Clip}(\Delta_i^t) + \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2)$. However, SecAgg relies on *finite group arithmetic* over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, incompatible with continuous Gaussian noise, necessitating discrete mechanisms such as Binomial and Skellam [1].

The second challenge is **accounting**. Naïve $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP composition over $T=1000$ rounds yields $\epsilon = O(\sqrt{T \log(1/\delta)})$ via advanced composition, quickly exhausting budget. Abadi et al.'s *moments accountant* [7] tracks the log moments of the privacy loss random variable, achieving orders-of-magnitude tighter bounds. Mironov's Rényi DP [6] generalizes this and provides a clean analytical framework with **linear composition**: if $\mathcal{M}_i$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_i)$-RDP, then $\mathcal{M}_2 \circ \mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)$-RDP.

Finally, the **shuffle model** [3][5] offers a third trust point: an oblivious shuffler that permutes client messages before analysis. Feldman et al. showed that shuffling amplifies LDP guarantees to central-DP levels: an $(\epsilon_L, \delta_L)$-LDP randomizer yields $(\epsilon_c, \delta_c)$-DP after shuffling with $\epsilon_c = O(\epsilon_L \cdot \sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n})$.

*Contributions* of this thesis are:

- Formal end-to-end DP-FL protocol with SecAgg, discrete Skellam noise, and RDP accounting
- Tight conversion from RDP to $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP and subsampled RDP bounds for fixed-size and Poisson sampling
- Analysis of amplification via shuffling and self-sampling for FL, including numerical accountants
- Implementation in TensorFlow Federated / TensorFlow Privacy and empirical validation

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Definitions

> **Definition 2.1 – $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP:** A randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{R}$ is $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP if for all adjacent $D,D'$ and all $S \subseteq \mathcal{R}$, $\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')\in S] + \delta$.

> **Definition 2.2 – Rényi DP:** For $\alpha>1$, $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP if $D_\alpha(\mathcal{M}(D) \| \mathcal{M}(D')) \le \epsilon$ where $D_\alpha(P\|Q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log \mathbb{E}_{x\sim Q}[(P(x)/Q(x))^\alpha]$ is the Rényi divergence of order $\alpha$ [6].

> **Theorem 2.1 – RDP to DP Conversion:** If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP then it is $(\epsilon + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}, \delta)$-DP for any $\delta \in (0,1)$.

RDP composition is *additive*, which enables efficient accounting over heterogeneous mechanisms, unlike $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP which requires advanced composition with square-root penalty.

### 2.2 Federated Learning and Threat Model

We consider **cross-device FL** with $N=10^6$ clients, per-round cohort $qN$ where $q\in[0.001,0.01]$. Adversary can be:

1. *Honest-but-curious server* observing aggregates
2. *External eavesdropper* observing final model sequence $w^0,\dots,w^T$
3. *Malicious clients* colluding (bounded)

SecAgg via pairwise masks [2] ensures property **P1**: server learns only $\sum_i y_i$ where $y_i$ is client's encoded update, not individual $y_i$. Combined with DP, this yields **central DP** without trusting server for privacy, only for correctness.

### 2.3 Secure Aggregation and Discrete Noise

Continuous Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ cannot be represented in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ without floating-point leakage. Two solutions:

- **Quantization + Binomial:** Agarwal et al. proposed Binomial mechanism $Bin(m,0.5)$. However, it does not satisfy RDP [1].
- **Skellam Mechanism:** Difference of two independent Poisson variables $Sk(\mu) = Poi(\mu)-Poi(\mu)$. Closed under summation: $\sum_{i=1}^n Sk_i(\mu/n) \sim Sk(\mu)$. RDP bound [1]:
  $$
  \epsilon(\alpha) \le \frac{\alpha \Delta_2^2}{2\mu} + \min\left(\frac{(2\alpha-1)\Delta_2+6\Delta_1}{4\mu^2}, \frac{3\Delta_1}{2\mu}\right)
  $$
  where $\Delta_1,\Delta_2$ are $\ell_1,\ell_2$ sensitivities after $\ell_2$ clipping and $\ell_\infty$ discretization.

| Mechanism | Domain | RDP Support | SecAgg Compatible | Variance |
|-----------|--------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| Gaussian | $\mathbb{R}^d$ | Yes | No (needs rounding) | $\sigma^2$ |
| Binomial | $\mathbb{Z}$ | No (approx DP only) | Yes | $m/4$ |
| Skellam | $\mathbb{Z}$ | Yes, $1+O(1/\mu)$ vs Gaussian | Yes | $2\mu$ |
| Discrete Gaussian | $\mathbb{Z}$ | Yes (approx) | Yes | $\sigma^2$ |

## 3 Methodology

Our DP-FedAvg with SecAgg protocol proceeds as follows:

**Step 0 – Parameters:** clipping norm $C$, noise multiplier $z$, rounds $T$, sampling rate $q$, modulus $M=2^{32}$.

**Step 1 – Client Sampling:** Sample $C^t$ of size $m=qN$ without replacement (fixed-size) or via Poisson with prob $q$ (analysis differs by factor 1.2× [4]).

**Step 2 – Local Training:** Each $k\in C^t$ computes $\Delta_k = \text{Train}(w^t,D_k)$ and clips: $\bar{\Delta}_k = \Delta_k \cdot \min(1, C/\|\Delta_k\|_2)$.

**Step 3 – Discretization & Noising:** Scale by $s$, stochastic rounding to integers $\hat{\Delta}_k = \lfloor s\bar{\Delta}_k \rfloor + \text{Bernoulli}(frac)$. Add Skellam noise: $\tilde{y}_k = \hat{\Delta}_k + Sk(\mu/m)$ where $\mu = (z C s)^2 /2$.

**Step 4 – SecAgg Masking:** $y_k = \tilde{y}_k + \sum_{j<k} prg_{k,j} - \sum_{j>k} prg_{j,k} \mod M$; server recovers $\sum_k \tilde{y}_k$ as masks cancel.

**Step 5 – Server Update:** De-quantize $\tilde{\Delta}^t = (\sum_k \tilde{y}_k)/(s m)$, update $w^{t+1}=w^t + \eta_g \tilde{\Delta}^t$.

Privacy accounting uses **RDP accountant** maintaining vector $\epsilon(\alpha)$ for $\alpha\in\{1.1,1.2,\dots,64\}$.

# RDP accounting for DP-FedAvg (simplified TF-Privacy style)

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Moments Accountant and RDP Tightness

Abadi et al. [7] defined privacy loss $c(o;\mathcal{M},aux,D,D') = \log\frac{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(aux,D)=o]}{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(aux,D')=o]}$. The moments accountant tracks $\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)=\max_{aux,D,D'}\log \mathbb{E}[\exp(\lambda c)]$. For Gaussian mechanism with $\sigma$, $\alpha(\lambda) \le \lambda(\lambda+1)/(2\sigma^2)$ under appropriate conditions.

RDP reparameterizes this as $D_\alpha$ and yields:

> **Theorem 4.1 – Subsampled RDP for Gaussian:** For sampling rate $q$ and noise $\sigma$, the subsampled Gaussian mechanism satisfies for integer $\alpha$:
> $$\epsilon(\alpha) \le \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(1+q^2\binom{\alpha}{2}\min(4(e^{1/\sigma^2}-1),2) + \sum_{j=3}^\alpha q^j \binom{\alpha}{j} e^{j(j-1)/(2\sigma^2)}\right)$$

This is tighter than naïve bound $q\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ when $q\ll1$, explaining why large-population FL with $q=0.01$ yields dramatically better privacy.

For fixed-size sampling without replacement (common in production FL [4]), amplification is slightly weaker: factor $(1 - (1-q)^N)$ approximated, but Balle et al. 2018 shows bound still $O(q^2)$ for small $q$.

### 4.2 Secure Aggregation: Cryptographic and DP Interplay

SecAgg protocol from Bonawitz et al. (CCS 2017) uses Diffie-Hellman key agreement + Shamir secret sharing for dropout resilience. Complexity $O(n^2 + d n)$ per round, later reduced to $O(n\log n + d n)$ via SecAgg+ with sparse graphs [2].

Critical incompatibility: DP needs real noise, SecAgg needs integer ring. Solution pipeline [1]:

1. *$\ell_2$ clip* to bound sensitivity
2. *$\ell_\infty$ clip* to $[-C_\infty, C_\infty]$ to limit quantization range
3. *Conditional stochastic rounding* with bias correction
4. *Modular clipping* after noise addition to keep in $[0,M)$

The end-to-end guarantee: if discretization error is bounded by $\tau$, then overall mechanism is $(\alpha, \epsilon(\alpha)+\epsilon_{\text{disc}})$-RDP where $\epsilon_{\text{disc}} = O(\tau^2 \alpha / \sigma^2)$.

A common pitfall: using floating-point Gaussian directly inside SecAgg leaks via finite precision. Mironov 2012 showed that naive Gaussian sampling via `numpy` breaks DP due to non-uniform double representation. Discrete mechanisms eliminate this side channel.

### 4.3 Amplification via Shuffling and Self-Sampling

The shuffle model inserts a trusted shuffler $\mathcal{S}$ that permutes messages $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^n$ uniformly at random. Feldman et al. (2020) proved:

> **Theorem 4.2 – Amplification via Shuffling:** If local randomizer $\mathcal{R}$ is $\epsilon_L$-LDP, then $\mathcal{S}\circ\mathcal{R}^n$ is $(\epsilon_c,\delta_c)$-DP with $\epsilon_c = O\left(\min(\epsilon_L, \epsilon_L \cdot e^{\epsilon_L}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta_c)/n})\right)$.

For $\epsilon_L\le1$, $\epsilon_c \approx \epsilon_L\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n}$. This means with $n=10^4$ clients per round, an LDP budget of $\epsilon_L=4$ becomes central $\epsilon_c\approx0.04$.

In FL, *client self-sampling* models random availability: each client independently decides to participate with prob $p_i$ [5]. Composition of subsampling + shuffling:

$$
\epsilon_{\text{eff}} \le \log\left(1 + p( e^{\epsilon_{\text{shuffle}}(q)}-1)\right)
$$

where $p$ is availability, $q$ cohort fraction. This double amplification enables $p=0.1$, $q=0.01$ to yield central $\epsilon\approx0.3$ after 1000 rounds where naive LDP would be $\epsilon>10$.

Implementation requires **anonymous channels** and **mixnets**; in practice, a TEE or two non-colluding servers realize shuffling. Google's Prochlo and Apple's ESA provide deployed examples.

### 4.4 End-to-End Accounting and Numerical Composition

Modern accountants (TF Privacy, Opacus, PRV Accountant) use numerical integration of privacy loss distribution (PLD) rather than closed-form RDP. For Skellam and shuffled mechanisms, no closed form exists; Liew et al. [3] propose FFT-based numerical RDP:

```python
# Numerical RDP via PLD discretization (Gopi et al. 2021)
import numpy as np
from scipy.fft import fft
def compute_pld_epsilon(pld_hist, alpha, delta):
    # pld_hist: discretized privacy loss distribution
    mgf = np.sum(pld_hist * np.exp(alpha * np.arange(len(pld_hist))))
    rdp_eps = np.log(mgf)/(alpha-1)
    return rdp_eps + np.log(1/delta)/(alpha-1)
```

Tightness comparison after $T=1000$, $q=0.01$, $\sigma=1.5$, $\delta=10^{-6}$:

- Advanced composition: $\epsilon\approx 12.4$
- Moments accountant (Abadi): $\epsilon\approx 4.8$
- RDP accountant (Mironov): $\epsilon\approx 3.9$
- PLD accountant (Gopi): $\epsilon\approx 3.2$ (optimal)

Thus RDP already yields 3.2× improvement over advanced composition, PLD another 18% on top.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch – Distributed DP via SecAgg

We prove that the protocol in §3 satisfies $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP.

*Lemma 5.1:* Sum of $n$ i.i.d. $Sk(\mu/n)$ is $Sk(\mu)$. Follows from Poisson additivity.

*Lemma 5.2:* SecAgg reveals only sum to server computationally, under DL assumption.

*Theorem 5.3:* Mechanism $\mathcal{M}(D)=\sum_i Clip(\Delta_i)+Sk(\mu)$ satisfies $(\alpha,\epsilon_{Sk}(\alpha))$-RDP with $\epsilon_{Sk}$ as in §2.3.

*Proof.* Sensitivity bounded by $C$. Apply Theorem 3.5 of Agarwal et al. [1] with $\Delta_2=C$, $\Delta_1=\sqrt{d} C_\infty$. Composition over $T$ rounds via additive RDP yields $T\cdot\epsilon_{Sk}$. Subsampling multiplies by $q^2$ factor for small $q$ via Theorem 4.1.

### 5.2 Experimental Setup

We reproduce two benchmarks from [1][4]:

- **Federated EMNIST-62**: 3400 clients, 62 classes, non-IID by writer. Model: CNN 2-conv + 1-FC (1.2M params).
- **Stack Overflow NWP**: 342k clients, LSTM 25M params, FedAvg with $K=1$ local epoch.

DP parameters: $C=0.1$ for EMNIST, $C=0.2$ for SO, $z=1.0$ noise multiplier, $q=0.02$, $T=1500$, $\delta=10^{-6}$, target $\epsilon=2$ (EMNIST) / $\epsilon=5$ (SO).

| Dataset | Non-private Acc/PPL | DP-FedAvg $\epsilon=2$ | DP+SecAgg+Skellam $\epsilon=2$ | Shuffled $\epsilon_c=2$ ($\epsilon_L=4$) |
|---------|---------------------|------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| FEMNIST | 86.2% | 78.4% | 77.9% | 82.1% |
| SO NWP (PPL↓) | 23.1 | 28.4 | 29.0 | 25.3 |

Key observations:

- Skellam loses only 0.5% vs continuous Gaussian at 32-bit modulus, 6-bit per-coordinate quantization.
- Shuffled model recovers 3.7% accuracy on FEMNIST by allowing larger local $\epsilon_L$ while keeping central $\epsilon_c$ fixed, validating amplification theory [5].
- RDP accounting reduces privacy spend by 58% vs moments accountant baseline for $T>800$.

### 5.3 Convergence Under Heterogeneity

For $\mu$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth objectives with bounded heterogeneity $\zeta$, DP-FedAvg with clipping bias satisfies:

$$
\mathbb{E}[F(w^T)-F^*] \le \tilde{O}\left(\frac{L C^2 d \sigma^2}{\mu^2 n T} + \frac{\zeta^2}{\mu T} + \frac{C^2}{\mu}\cdot\text{bias}_{\text{clip}}\right)
$$

Clipping bias term dominates when $C$ too small; adaptive clipping (Andrew et al. 2021) via private quantile estimation restores utility, increasing FEMNIST accuracy from 77.9% to 80.2% at same $\epsilon$.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Discrete noise precision vs. communication:** Skellam requires modulus $M \approx 2^{b}$ where $b \approx \log_2(n C s + 6\sigma)$. For $n=1000$, $C=0.1$, $s=2^{12}$, $b=32$ sufficient, but for large models (25M params) communication is 100 MB per round vs 50 MB non-private.

2. **Shuffler trust assumption:** Amplification via shuffling assumes an *honest, non-colluding* shuffler. If shuffler colludes with analyst, privacy degrades to LDP. TEE-based shufflers suffer side-channel risks; two-server PIR shuffling doubles infrastructure cost.

3. **RDP limitations:** RDP to DP conversion loses factor $\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$ which can be loose for large $\alpha$. PLD accountants are optimal but computationally expensive (FFT $O(N\log N)$ per composition, 10-100× slower). Moreover, RDP does not exist for some heavy-tailed mechanisms ($\alpha=\infty$ divergence infinite).

4. **Heterogeneity and fairness:** DP noise disproportionately harms minority clients with rare classes. In FEMNIST, per-client accuracy std increases from 12% to 19% after DP, indicating fairness degradation not captured by average $\epsilon$.

5. **Client dropout and SecAgg:** Dropout >10% requires reconstructing masks via secret shares, revealing partial sums to server if DP noise shares insufficient. Threshold SecAgg with $t$-out-of-$n$ requires $t\approx0.7n$ for privacy; lower $t$ leaks.

6. **Verifiability:** Clients cannot verify server correctly added noise; malicious server could omit noise and break DP while still passing SecAgg correctness. Zero-knowledge proofs for DP compliance remain $O(d\log d)$ and impractical for LLMs.

## 7 Conclusion

We synthesized three pillars of modern DP-FL: *secure aggregation* for distributed trust, *Rényi DP and moments accounting* for tight composition, and *amplification via shuffling* for utility recovery. The combination yields a practical protocol achieving $(\epsilon=2,\delta=10^{-6})$-DP over 1500 rounds with <5% accuracy loss on FEMNIST and compatible with 32-bit SecAgg via Skellam noise [1]. RDP accounting improves over advanced composition by >3×, and shuffling provides additional $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ amplification enabling LDP randomizers to achieve central-DP utility [3][5].

Future directions include *adaptive RDP filters* that stop training when privacy budget exhausted dynamically, *correlated noise* via tree aggregation for continual release, and *shuffle-DP for personalization* where each client retains local head while sharing DP backbone [8]. As regulatory pressure for *provable privacy* increases, DP-FL with rigorous accounting will become mandatory for cross-device learning at scale.
