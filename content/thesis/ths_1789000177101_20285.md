# State Space Models for Long Sequences: Structured SSMs, the S4 Parameterization, and Selective Scanning in Mamba

## Abstract

State space models (SSMs) have re-emerged as a first-class architecture for long-sequence modeling by unifying the parallelizability of convolutions with the constant-memory inference of recurrences. This thesis develops the theory and practice of modern SSMs in three stages. First, we derive the linear recurrence formulation and its discretization under zero-order hold (ZOH), connecting continuous-time latent dynamics to discrete token sequences. Second, we present the HiPPO theory of optimal online function approximation and show how its diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) state matrices, together with a Cauchy-kernel convolution algorithm, make the S4 parameterization computable in $\tilde{O}(N + L)$ time and space. Third, we analyze the selective scanning mechanism of Mamba, which renders SSM parameters input-dependent — recovering content-based reasoning — and compensates the loss of convolution structure with a hardware-aware parallel associative scan. We report the empirical record on the Long Range Arena (LRA), where S4 solved the 16k-length Path-X task that defeated all prior methods, prove complexity bounds, and situate SSMs against $O(N^2)$ attention, before discussing limitations and open directions toward hybrid selective architectures.

## 1. Introduction

Sequence modeling has, for the better part of a decade, been a contest between three computational motifs. **Recurrent neural networks** process tokens in $O(N)$ time with $O(1)$ memory per step but cannot be parallelized across the sequence and suffer vanishing gradients. **Convolutional networks** parallelize trivially but need depth or dilation schedules that grow with the receptive field. **Transformers** parallelize perfectly and model arbitrary token interactions, but attention costs $O(N^2)$ time and memory — a hard ceiling for long documents, audio waveforms, genomic sequences, and high-resolution temporal data [5].

State space models (SSMs) are classical objects of control theory — the *latent state* formulation $x'(t) = Ax(t) + Bu(t)$, $y(t) = Cx(t) + Du(t)$ — repurposed by Gu et al. [1] as deep sequence layers that inherit the best property of each motif: they train in parallel as *convolutions* and deploy as *recurrences* with constant state. The obstacle that kept SSMs out of deep learning for decades was not conceptual but computational: a naive parameterization cannot remember information over thousands of time steps, and a naive discretization destroys the structure that makes long-range memory possible.

> **Theorem (Informal — the SSM duality).** A linear time-invariant (LTI) SSM admits three mathematically equivalent views: (i) a continuous-time ODE, (ii) a discrete linear recurrence $x_k = \bar{A}x_{k-1} + \bar{B}u_k$, and (iii) a convolution $y = \bar{K} \ast u$ with kernel $\bar{K}_k = \bar{C}\bar{A}^k\bar{B}$. Training exploits (iii); inference exploits (ii).

This thesis traces the full arc of this duality: from linear recurrences and their discretization (§2), through the HiPPO-S4 parameterization and its $O(N \log N)$ kernel computation (§3), into the selective scan of Mamba and its hardware-aware associative scan (§4), then empirical results and formal complexity analysis (§5), limitations (§6), and conclusions (§7). Two points are developed with unusual precision: (i) a clean derivation of the Cauchy-kernel technique that reduces S4's convolution to $\tilde{O}(N+L)$ via generating functions, and (ii) a step-by-step complexity audit of Mamba's selective scan versus standard and FlashAttention.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Linear recurrences and their gradient pathologies

The vanilla RNN hidden-state update

$$h_k = \sigma(A h_{k-1} + B u_k)$$

couples nonlinearity with recurrence, which is the source of both its expressivity and its instability. Unrolling yields a product of Jacobians $\prod_{i} D_i A$ whose singular values explode or vanish exponentially in $k$, barring exceptional structure (unitary or orthogonal constraints [8]). The SSM removes the nonlinearity from the recurrence entirely: the state dynamics are *linear*, and all nonlinearity is pushed to inter-layer mixing. Linearity is what buys the convolution duality.

### 2.2 The continuous-time SSM and zero-order-hold discretization

The continuous-time SSM is the linear ODE system

$$x'(t) = Ax(t) + Bu(t), \quad y(t) = Cx(t) + Du(t),$$

with state $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$, $D \in \mathbb{R}$. To process a sampled sequence $u_0, \dots, u_{L-1}$ with step size $\Delta$, we discretize assuming the input is constant on each interval — the **zero-order hold (ZOH)** assumption. Solving the ODE in closed form gives

$$\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}(\exp(\Delta A) - I)\,\Delta B,$$

and the discrete recurrence

$$x_k = \bar{A}x_{k-1} + \bar{B}u_k, \qquad y_k = \bar{C}x_{k-1} + D u_k, \quad \bar{C} = C.$$

Unrolling the recurrence gives the convolution view: $y_k = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \bar{C}\bar{A}^{k-j}\bar{B}\,u_j$, i.e., $y = \bar{K} \ast u$ with kernel $\bar{K}_k = \bar{C}\bar{A}^k\bar{B}$. Training can therefore use FFT-based convolution in $O(L \log L)$ — *provided the kernel itself can be materialized efficiently*, which is precisely the bottleneck S4's parameterization attacks.

### 2.3 HiPPO theory: optimal online function approximation

The representational question — *what should $A$ and $B$ be so that $x(t)$ remembers the history of $u$?* — is answered by the **HiPPO framework** (High-order Polynomial Projection Operators) [3]: given a measure weighting the importance of past inputs, HiPPO produces the optimal projection of the history $u_{\le t}$ onto a polynomial basis, whose coefficients evolve as

$$\frac{d}{dt}c(t) = A(t)c(t) + B(t)u(t),$$

where $(A, B)$ are determined in closed form by the measure and basis. The flagship instance is **HiPPO-LegS**, which uses scaled Legendre polynomials with a measure weighting all history uniformly on $[0, t]$ — a *scale-invariant* memory with no prior on the timescale [3], enjoying **timescale robustness**, **bounded gradients**, and fast $O(N)$ updates. Empirically it set a new state of the art on permuted sequential MNIST (98.3%) [3].

*Why this matters for deep SSMs:* Gu et al. showed that initializing $A$ with the HiPPO-LegS matrix transforms the SSM from a model that fails simple long-range tasks into one that captures dependencies over 16,000 steps [1]. The initialization is not a trick; it is the *optimal* compression of history under a uniform measure.

## 3. Methodology: The S4 Parameterization

### 3.1 The DPLR structure and why it is necessary

S4's core insight is algebraic: the HiPPO-LegS matrix admits a **normal plus low-rank** decomposition, which S4 generalizes into the **diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR)** parameterization [1]:

> **Definition (DPLR).** $A = \Lambda - PQ^*$, where $\Lambda \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ is diagonal and $P, Q \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times r}$ are low-rank factors. Conjugating the HiPPO matrix by its eigenvector basis reduces it to this form up to factors absorbable into $B$ and $C$.The DPLR form is the *minimal* structure containing the HiPPO initialization while permitting fast kernel computation: a dense $A$ would cost $O(N^2 L)$ per kernel evaluation, whereas DPLR reduces this to $\tilde{O}(N + L)$.

### 3.2 The Cauchy kernel computation

S4 computes the kernel not by unrolling $\bar{A}^k$ but via its **generating function**. The truncated generating function evaluated at the $L$-th roots of unity $\omega$ is

$$\hat{K}(\omega) = \sum_{k=0}^{L-1} \bar{C}\bar{A}^k\bar{B}\,\omega^k = \bar{C}(I - \bar{A}^L)(I - \bar{A}\omega)^{-1}\bar{B}.$$

For the DPLR parameterization, the resolvent $(I - \bar{A}\omega)^{-1}$ is handled by the **Woodbury identity**: since the discretization preserves DPLR form, the inverse of a diagonal-plus-low-rank matrix reduces to a diagonal inverse plus a small $r \times r$ correction. The remaining core computation is a **Cauchy matrix–vector product** $\sum_{j} v_j/(\omega_i - \lambda_j)$, for which $O((N+L)\log^2(N+L))$ algorithms exist [1]. An inverse FFT then recovers $\bar{K}$ from $\hat{K}$. The total cost is $\tilde{O}(N + L)$ time and $O(N + L)$ space — *quasi-linear in both state size and sequence length*.

```python
import numpy as np

def s4_kernel(Lambda, P, Q, B, C, L, dt):
    """Materialize the S4 convolution kernel via the DPLR resolvent trick."""
    N, r = P.shape
    Abar_diag = np.exp(dt * Lambda)                     # ZOH discretization
    omegas = np.exp(2j * np.pi * np.arange(L) / L)      # roots of unity
    denom = 1.0 - Abar_diag[None, :] * omegas[:, None]  # Cauchy denominators
    Khat = np.sum(C[None, :] * (B[:, None] / denom), axis=1)  # resolvent matvec
    return np.fft.ifft(Khat).real
```

The code is schematic — the production implementation in the official `state-spaces` repository [6] fuses the Cauchy multiply with the FFT in custom CUDA kernels — but it exhibits the essential move: *never form $\bar{A}^k$ explicitly*.

### 3.3 S4D: the diagonal variant

A natural question is whether the low-rank correction is load-bearing. Gupta, Gu, and Berant [4] showed that it is not: the **Diagonal State Space (DSS)** model drops the low-rank term entirely, keeping a purely diagonal (complex) $A$, and matches S4 on Long Range Arena and speech classification while being far simpler to implement. The follow-up **S4D** variant refines this by initializing the diagonal entries as the *diagonal part* of the HiPPO matrix with a small imaginary component for oscillatory memory, recovering nearly all of S4's performance.

> **Observation.** The *spectrum* of the HiPPO matrix — eigenvalues with negative real parts spread across timescales — carries most of the benefit; the low-rank coupling is a second-order effect. Diagonal SSMs are therefore the pragmatic default, and Mamba's S6 block indeed uses a diagonal $A$.

| Model | $A$ structure | Kernel cost | LRA avg. |
|---|---|---|---|
| S4 (DPLR) | $\Lambda - PQ^*$ | $\tilde{O}(N+L)$ | 86.1% |
| DSS / S4D | diagonal | $O(N + L\log L)$ | $\approx$ S4 |
| S5 (MIMO) | diagonal, multi-input | $O(N + L\log L)$ | 87.4% |
| Mamba (S6) | diagonal, *input-dependent* | $O(NL)$ parallel scan | — (LM focus) |

*Table 1.* SSM variants, their parameterizations, and Long Range Arena averages as reported in [1, 2, 4, 7]. Values are approximate; see §5 for the benchmark details.

## 4. Deep Dive: Selectivity, Parallel Scans, and the Attention Comparison

### 4.1 Why LTI systems fail at content-based reasoning

An LTI SSM applies the *same* dynamics $(\bar{A}, \bar{B}, \bar{C})$ at every time step. Gu and Dao [2] identify this as the fundamental weakness of S4-class models on discrete modalities such as language: the model cannot **selectively propagate or forget** information based on the current token. Two synthetic tasks separate LTI SSMs from selective ones:

1. **Selective Copying.** Sparse informative tokens buried in noise must be copied; an LTI system with fixed $\bar{A}$ treats noise and signal identically and fails as noise density grows.
2. **Induction Heads.** Recalling a token $a$ whenever a trigger token $b$ appears — the mechanism underlying in-context learning — requires the state update to be *gated by content* [2].

> **Theorem (Informal — selectivity as gating).** Making the discretization step $\Delta_t = \tau_\Delta(u_t)$ input-dependent yields $\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$. Large $\Delta_t$ *overwrites* the state with the input; $\Delta_t \to 0$ *freezes* it. Thus $\Delta_t$ plays exactly the role of a continuous gate, generalizing GRU/LSTM gating [3] into the SSM framework.

### 4.2 Mamba's selective scan (S6)

Mamba [2] parameterizes the SSM matrices as *functions of the input*:

$$\Delta_t = \mathrm{softplus}(W_\Delta u_t), \qquad B_t = W_B u_t, \qquad C_t = W_C u_t,$$

while $A$ stays fixed and diagonal ($A_n = -\exp(a_n)$, as in S4D). The recurrence becomes time-varying: $x_t = \bar{A}_t x_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t u_t$, $y_t = C_t x_t$, with the simplified ZOH discretization $\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$, $\bar{B}_t = \Delta_t B_t$ [2]. Because $(\bar{A}_t, \bar{B}_t)$ vary with $t$, the convolution duality *breaks*: there is no fixed kernel $\bar{K}$, and training cannot use FFTs. This is the price of selectivity.

### 4.3 The hardware-aware parallel associative scan

The recurrence $x_t = \bar{A}_t x_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t u_t$ is a **first-order linear recurrence with time-varying coefficients**, which admits a *parallel associative scan*: define the operator on pairs $(a_i, b_i) \otimes (a_j, b_j) = (a_j a_i,\; a_j b_i + b_j)$, which is associative; a Blelloch-style scan then computes all prefixes in $O(\log L)$ depth with $O(L)$ total work [2]. A naive implementation is memory-bound, however: materializing the state of size $(B, L, D, N)$ in GPU HBM dominates runtime.

> **Key engineering result (Mamba [2]).** The selective scan is *fused* into a single GPU kernel that loads $(\Delta_t, A, B_t, C_t)$ from HBM into SRAM, performs discretization and the recurrence *entirely in SRAM*, and writes only the $O(BLD)$ outputs back. The $O(BLDN)$ intermediate state never touches slow memory — reducing IO by a factor of $N$ and making the scan compute-bound rather than memory-bound, the same roofline reasoning that motivates FlashAttention.

The net effect: Mamba trains in linear time with a small constant, and at inference it is a pure recurrence with $O(1)$ memory per step.

### 4.4 Complexity audit: SSMs versus attention

| Regime | Standard attention | FlashAttention | S4 (conv. mode) | Mamba (scan mode) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Training time | $O(N^2 d)$ | $O(N^2 d)$ | $O(N \log N)$ | $O(N d)$ |
| Training memory | $O(N^2)$ | $O(N)$ | $O(N)$ | $O(N)$ |
| Inference / token | $O(N d)$ (KV cache) | $O(N d)$ | $O(d)$ | $O(d)$ |
| Inference memory | $O(N d)$ (KV cache) | $O(N d)$ | $O(1)$ state | $O(1)$ state |

*Table 2.* Asymptotic costs in sequence length $N$ and model width $d$. FlashAttention removes the $O(N^2)$ memory term but not the $O(N^2)$ compute term; SSMs remove both. At inference, Transformers pay a KV cache linear in $N$ per token, while SSMs pay a fixed-size state.

Three corollaries deserve emphasis:

1. **Training parallelism.** S4 trains via FFT convolution — fully parallel, like attention. Mamba trains via the parallel associative scan — $O(\log N)$ depth, like a parallel prefix sum.
2. **Inference scaling.** On million-length sequences, Mamba's throughput is reported at $5\times$ that of comparable Transformers [2], because each token requires only a state update rather than a scan over the KV cache.

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Long Range Arena: S4's breakthrough

The **Long Range Arena (LRA)** benchmark was designed to stress-test long-range reasoning across six tasks — ListOps, byte-level text classification and retrieval, pixel-level image classification, and the Pathfinder/Path-X visual reasoning tasks — with lengths from 1k to 16k. Before S4, *no* model beat random guessing on **Path-X** (16,384-length).

S4 achieved state of the art on *every* LRA task, including solving Path-X, with an average of **86.1%** [1]. The ablations are as informative as the headline: replacing the HiPPO initialization with a random $A$ collapses performance, while the DPLR parameterization makes the kernel computation tractable at these lengths. DSS/S4D [4] later matched these numbers with a purely diagonal $A$, and the S5 MIMO variant [7] pushed the average to **87.4%** with 98.5% on Path-X.

On sequential CIFAR-10 (length 1,024), S4 reached **91%** accuracy with no data augmentation [1], and on language modeling it closed the gap to Transformers while generating **$60\times$ faster** [1].

### 5.2 Mamba's scaling results

Mamba-3B **outperforms Transformers of the same size and matches Transformers twice its size** on language modeling [2]. As a general backbone it achieved state of the art across language, audio, and genomics, with performance improving on real data up to **million-length sequences** [2] — a regime where quadratic attention is infeasible. On the synthetic Selective Copying and Induction Heads tasks, Mamba succeeds where LTI SSMs fail, confirming that selectivity — not just scale — is the operative ingredient [2].### 5.3 Formal complexity statements

> **Proposition 1 (S4 kernel complexity).** For state size $N$, sequence length $L$, and DPLR rank $r$, the S4 convolution kernel $\bar{K} \in \mathbb{R}^L$ can be computed in $O((N + L)\log^2(N + L))$ time and $O(N + L)$ space, via the Cauchy generating-function evaluation and inverse FFT [1, Thm. 3].

*Proof sketch.* The generating function at the $L$ roots of unity needs $L$ evaluations of $\bar{C}(I - \bar{A}\omega)^{-1}\bar{B}$. By Woodbury, each resolvent application is a diagonal solve plus an $r \times r$ correction; the dominant term is the Cauchy matrix–vector product $\sum_j v_j/(\omega_i - \lambda_j)$, computable in $O((N+L)\log^2(N+L))$ [1, §3.3]. One inverse FFT recovers $\bar{K}$. ∎

> **Proposition 2 (Selective scan complexity).** The time-varying recurrence $x_t = \bar{A}_t x_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t u_t$ over $L$ steps with state size $N$ is computed by the parallel associative scan in $O(LN)$ total work and $O(\log L)$ parallel depth [2, §3.2].

Autoregressive generation with an SSM costs $O(1)$ time and $O(1)$ memory per token (a single state update), versus $O(N)$ time and $O(N)$ memory per token for attention with a KV cache.

### 5.4 A minimal reference implementation

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def selective_scan(u, delta, A, B, C, D):
    """Mamba S6 selective scan (simplified). Sequential loop shown for
    clarity; the production kernel uses the fused parallel scan of §4.3."""
    Bsz, L, D = u.shape
    N = A.shape[1]
    dA = torch.exp(delta[..., None] * A)          # (B, L, D, N)
    dB = delta[..., None] * B[:, :, None, :] * u[:, :, :, None]
    x = torch.zeros(Bsz, D, N, device=u.device)
    ys = []
    for t in range(L):                            # fused parallel scan in practice
        x = dA[:, t] * x + dB[:, t]
        ys.append(torch.einsum('dn,bdn->bd', C[:, t], x))
    return torch.stack(ys, dim=1) + D * u
```

The loop is sequential here for clarity; the production kernel replaces it with the fused parallel associative scan of §4.3.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Initialization sensitivity.** S4's performance hinges on the HiPPO(-LegS) initialization; random $A$ initializations fail dramatically [1]. The theory explains *which* initialization is optimal for a uniform history measure, but the choice of measure itself remains a heuristic.
2. **The selectivity–parallelism trade-off.** Mamba buys content-based reasoning at the cost of the convolution duality. The selective scan demands custom fused kernels and careful SRAM budgeting.
3. **Linear dynamics ceiling.** Even selective SSMs are *linear* in the state transition; all nonlinearity lives in the mixing layers. Whether linear recurrences can fully replicate the higher-order interactions of attention remains empirically open.
4. **Interpretability gaps.** SSM states are dense $N$-dimensional vectors with no canonical token alignment, unlike attention maps. Formal results on the *expressive power* of selective SSMs relative to Transformers are still fragmentary.
5. **Hyperparameter coupling.** The learned step-size range $(\Delta_{\min}, \Delta_{\max})$ controls the effective memory horizon; miscalibrated ranges silently truncate long-range capacity, and must be tuned per modality.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The trajectory from HiPPO to S4 to Mamba shows how deep learning advances when *mathematics and systems* move together. HiPPO [3] supplied the optimal memory theory; S4 [1] supplied the DPLR parameterization and Cauchy-kernel algorithm that made the theory computable at scale; Mamba [2] supplied selectivity — the missing content-based reasoning — together with the hardware-aware scan that made selectivity affordable.

The open frontier is no longer *whether* SSMs can compete with attention but *where the hybrid boundary lies*: which computations genuinely require quadratic pairwise interactions, and which are better served by selective linear recurrences?
