## Abstract

This thesis presents a comprehensive study of quantum error mitigation for nisq variational algorithms, addressing foundational and practical challenges at the intersection of quantum error mitigation for nisq variational algorithms and modern distributed systems. We formalize the problem, survey evolution from early quantum constructions to state-of-the-art Extrapolation deployments, and introduce analytical frameworks for evaluating correctness, security, and performance under adversarial and resource-constrained conditions. Contributions include a taxonomy of design trade-offs, formal reductions to standard assumptions, and an empirical methodology bridging theory and practice. We show quantum error mitigation for nisq variational algorithms achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in throughput and latency while preserving provable guarantees, and we identify open problems in composability, verification, and hardware acceleration that guide future work. Experiments across diverse benchmarks validate scalability and robustness, with implications for cloud and edge production deployment.

fn verify_kzg(commit: G1, proof: G1, point: Fr, value: Fr, srs: &SRS) -> bool {
    let lhs = pairing(commit - srs.g*value, srs.g2);
    let rhs = pairing(proof, srs.g2_h - srs.g2*point);
    lhs == rhs
}
