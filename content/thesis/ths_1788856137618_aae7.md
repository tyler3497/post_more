# Polar Codes and Channel Polarization: From Arıkan's Butterfly Construction to Successive-Cancellation List Decoding and 5G NR Control Channels ## Abstract Polar codes, introduced by Erdal Arıkan in 2009, constitute the first *explicit* family of error-correcting codes provably achieving the symmetric capacity of binary-input discrete memoryless channels with encoding and decoding complexity $O(N \log N)$. The construction rests on *channel polarization*: a recursive combining–splitting transform that synthesizes $N$ ordered bit-channels from $N$ copies of the raw channel, such that, as $N \to \infty$, a fraction $I(W)$ of the bit-channels becomes essentially noiseless and the remainder essentially useless. This thesis develops the full arc of the theory — the butterfly combining kernel and its Kronecker powers, the Bhattacharyya parameter as a polarization martingale, frozen-bit selection via density evolution and the Gaussian approximation, successive-cancellation (SC) decoding, Tal–Vardy successive-cancellation list (SCL) decoding and its CRC-aided refinement, finite-length scaling laws with polarization exponent $\beta < 1/2$, and the industrial culmination of the theory in the 3GPP 5G NR control-channel standard. We derive the $f$/$g$ log-likelihood-ratio recursions, prove the polarization theorem's skeleton, analyze $O(LN \log N)$ list complexity, and quantify the $\approx 1.5$–$2$ dB gains of CA-SCL over plain SC at $N = 1024$ on the AWGN channel. ## 1. Introduction The Shannon capacity theorem is an *existence* proof: it guarantees that codes of rate arbitrarily close to channel capacity exist, via random coding, but it provides no recipe for constructing them. For six decades after Shannon (1948), the engineering of practical capacity-approaching codes proceeded through *ensembles* — families from which a good code is drawn at random, decoded by iterative belief propagation. Turbo codes (1993) and low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes rediscovered by MacKay and Richardson–Urbanke delivered the first codes that approach capacity at moderate blocklengths, and both rely on probabilistic constructions and suboptimal iterative decoders. Polar codes broke this pattern completely. Arıkan's 2009 paper [1] gave the first *deterministic, explicit* construction of capacity-achieving codes for symmetric binary-input memoryless channels, together with an $O(N \log N)$ encoder and an $O(N \log N)$ decoder whose analysis is exact rather than ensemble-averaged. The central mechanism — **channel polarization** — is arguably the deepest new idea in coding theory since the Viterbi algorithm: a fixed, recursive nonlinear transform on channels that concentrates all the capacity into a vanishing-entropy subset of synthesized bit-channels. The decoder does not merely decode the code; it mirrors the polarization transform itself, successively cancelling interference in exactly the order the channels were synthesized. This thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 establishes the channel model, the polarization kernel, and the combining–splitting machinery. Section 3 develops code construction: reliability ordering, density evolution, and the Gaussian approximation used for frozen-bit selection in practice. Section 4, the deep dive, derives the LLR recursions of the butterfly, the Bhattacharyya polarization martingale, SC decoding, list decoding with CRC, and finite-length scaling. Section 5 presents empirical frame-error-rate evidence and the analytical bounds that explain it. Section 6 discusses limitations, including the weak finite-length performance of plain SC that made list decoding — and eventually standardization politics — decisive. Section 7 concludes. > **Theorem (Channel Polarization, informal):** For any symmetric binary-input discrete memoryless channel $W$ and any $\delta > 0$, as the blocklength $N = 2^n \to \infty$, the fraction of synthesized bit-channels $W_N^{(i)}$ with mutual information $I(W_N^{(i)}) > 1 - \delta$ tends to $I(W)$, and the fraction with $I(W_N^{(i)}) < \delta$ tends to $1 - I(W)$. The capacity concentrates; the channels polarize. --- ## 2. Background ### 2.1 Channel Model and Parameters Let $W: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ be a *binary-input discrete memoryless channel* (B-DMC) with $\mathcal{X} = \{0,1\}$. We assume $W$ is **symmetric**: there exists a permutation $\pi$ of $\mathcal{Y}$ with $\pi^{-1} = \pi$ such that $W(y \mid 0) = W(\pi(y) \mid 1)$. Symmetry implies the uniform input distribution achieves capacity, so the relevant capacity is the *symmetric capacity* $I(W)$, the mutual information under uniform inputs. Two channel functionals govern the entire theory: - The **symmetric mutual information** $I(W) \in [0, 1]$, measuring the reliable throughput of $W$. - The **Bhattacharyya parameter** $Z(W) \in [0, 1]$, $$Z(W) = \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} \sqrt{W(y \mid 0)\, W(y \mid 1)},$$ an upper bound on the maximum-likelihood decision error probability for one uncoded bit, and — critically — the quantity that *polarizes most cleanly* under the transform. $Z(W) \approx 0$ means a nearly perfect channel; $Z(W) \approx 1$ means a nearly useless one. For the binary erasure channel $\mathrm{BEC}(\epsilon)$, the parameters coincide: $I(W) = 1 - \epsilon$ and $Z(W) = \epsilon$, with exact recursions. ### 2.2 The Polarization Kernel All of polar coding descends from a single $2 \times 2$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}_2$: $$F = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$ Channel **combining** takes two independent copies of $W$ and produces the vector channel $W_2(y_1, y_2 \mid u_1, u_2) = W(y_1 \mid u_1 \oplus u_2)\, W(y_2 \mid u_2)$. Channel **splitting** then decomposes $W_2$ back into two *bit-channels*: - $W_2^{(1)}(y_1, y_2 \mid u_1) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{u_2} W(y_1 \mid u_1 \oplus u_2)\, W(y_2 \mid u_2)$ — the "bad" channel, decoded *without* knowledge of $u_2$; - $W_2^{(2)}(y_1, y_2, u_1 \mid u_2) = \tfrac{1}{2} W(y_1 \mid u_1 \oplus u_2)\, W(y_2 \mid u_2)$ — the "good" channel, decoded *with* $u_1$ already decided. The splitting is capacity-conserving, $I(W_2^{(1)}) + I(W_2^{(2)}) = 2I(W)$, but *reliability-separating*: $I(W_2^{(1)}) \le I(W) \le I(W_2^{(2)})$, with strict inequality unless $W$ is already extreme (perfect or useless). This single step is the germ of polarization; recursion amplifies it. ### 2.3 Recursive Construction and the Generator Matrix Applying the combine–split operation recursively $n$ times to $N = 2^n$ copies of $W$ yields $N$ ordered bit-channels $W_N^{(i)}$, $i = 1, \dots, N$. The corresponding code generator matrix is $$G_N = B_N F^{\otimes n},$$ where $F^{\otimes n}$ is the $n$-fold Kronecker power of the kernel and $B_N$ is the bit-reversal permutation. Encoding maps the input vector $u_1^N$ to the codeword $x_1^N = u_1^N G_N$ in $O(N \log N)$ operations — the butterfly graph of the fast transform, structurally identical to the FFT signal-flow graph. --- ## 3. Methodology ### 3.1 Frozen-Bit Selection: The Reliability Sequence A polar code of blocklength $N$ and dimension $K$ is specified by an **information set** $\mathcal{A} \subset \{1, \dots, N\}$ of size $K$: the $K$ most reliable bit-channels carry data, and the complementary **frozen set** $\mathcal{A}^c$ is fixed to known values (conventionally zero). Under successive cancellation, the block error probability satisfies the union bound $$P_e \le \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} Z\!\left(W_N^{(i)}\right), \tag{1}$$ so construction reduces to *estimating the $N$ Bhattacharyya parameters* (or equivalent reliabilities) and sorting them. Exact computation is intractable for general channels because the output alphabet of $W_N^{(i)}$ grows doubly exponentially; three practical methodologies dominate: 1. **Density evolution (DE)** with quantized LLR distributions — accurate but expensive, $O(N \mu^2 \log \mu)$ for $\mu$ quantization levels. 2. **Gaussian approximation (GA)** — models each bit-channel's LLR density as Gaussian, propagating only the *mean* through closed-form recursions. Cost $O(N)$; accuracy excellent on the AWGN channel. 3. **Polarization weight (PW) / standardized sequences** — channel-independent orderings derived from the binary expansion of the index; the 5G NR standard uses a fixed, precomputed nested sequence $Q_0^{1023}$ (§4.5). ### 3.2 Gaussian Approximation of Density Evolution Under GA, the LLR $L_N^{(i)}$ of bit-channel $i$ is assumed distributed as $\mathcal{N}(m_N^{(i)}, 2m_N^{(i)})$ (the symmetry condition $\sigma^2 = 2m$). The mean propagates through the butterfly via [4]: $$\begin{aligned} m_N^{(2i-1)} &= \phi^{-1}\!\left(1 - \big(1 - \phi(m_{N/2}^{(i)})\big)^2\right), \ m_N^{(2i)} &= 2\, m_{N/2}^{(i)}, \end{aligned} \tag{2}$$ where $\phi(x)$ is the *capacity-like* transfer function $$\phi(x) = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi x}} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \tanh\frac{u}{2}\, e^{-\frac{(u-x)^2}{4x}}\, du, \qquad x > 0,$$ with $\phi(0) = 1$, typically evaluated through the piecewise exponential approximations of Chung–Richardson–Urbanke. The recursion is initialized at $m_1^{(1)} = 2/\sigma^2$ for the AWGN channel with noise variance $\sigma^2$, and the bit-error probability of channel $i$ is estimated as $P_b(i) \approx Q(\sqrt{m_N^{(i)}/2})$. Sorting these values yields the GA reliability ordering in linearithmic time. ```python import numpy as np def phi(x: float) -> float: """Chung-Richardson-Urbanke approximation of the GA transfer function.""" if x <= 0.0: return 1.0 # piecewise exponential fit, accurate for x in (0, ~30] return float(np.exp(-0.4527 * x**0.86 + 0.0218)) def phi_inv(y: float) -> float: lo, hi = 1e-12, 60.0 for _ in range(200): # bisection; phi is monotone decreasing mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi) if phi(mid) > y: lo = mid else: hi = mid return 0.5 * (lo + hi) def ga_reliability(n: int, design_snr_db: float) -> np.ndarray: """Mean-LLR vector m_N for N = 2^n via Gaussian approximation (AWGN).""" The list decoder (§4.4) produces $L$ candidate codewords; the CRC selects among them. This converts the decoder's tendency to place the correct codeword *somewhere in the list* (but not at rank 1) into a decisive gain — effectively a genie-aided ML decoder implemented by $m$ parity checks. --- ## 4. Deep Dive ### 4.1 The Butterfly Transform: $f$ and $g$ LLR Recursions Successive-cancellation decoding traverses the butterfly factor graph depth-first. At each node combining two child LLRs $a, b$, the decoder computes, for the upper (odd) branch: $$f(a, b) = 2\,\mathrm{atanh}\!\left(\tanh\frac{a}{2}\tanh\frac{b}{2}\right) \approx \mathrm{sign}(a)\,\mathrm{sign}(b)\,\min(|a|, |b|), \tag{3}$$ and, once the upper bit $\hat{u}_s$ is decided, for the lower (even) branch: $$g(a, b, \hat{u}_s) = (-1)^{\hat{u}_s} a + b. \tag{4}$$ The approximation in (3) is the **min-sum** form, ubiquitous in hardware. These two recursions are the *entire* SC decoder: $N \log_2 N$ node evaluations, each $O(1)$, giving $O(N \log N)$ time and $O(N)$ space. The decoding order is forced — bit $i$ is decided before bit $i+1$, using all previous decisions — hence *successive cancellation*. ### 4.2 Bhattacharyya Polarization: The Martingale Argument The cleanest path to the polarization theorem runs through $Z(W)$. Define the random process $Z_n = Z(W_{2^n}^{(B_n)})$ where $B_n$ is a uniform random bit-channel index. From the one-step recursions [1]: $$\begin{aligned} Z(W_2^{(1)}) &\le 2Z(W) - Z(W)^2, \ Z(W_2^{(2)}) &= Z(W)^2, \end{aligned} \tag{5}$$ with equality in the second line. The process $\{Z_n\}$ is a bounded **supermartingale** converging almost surely to a $\{0,1\}$-valued random variable $Z_\infty$. Because capacity is conserved, $\mathbb{E}[I_\infty] = I(W)$, and the extremality of the limit forces $$\Pr(Z_\infty = 0) = I(W), \qquad \Pr(Z_\infty = 1) = 1 - I(W). \tag{6}$$ That is the polarization theorem: the bit-channels split into a $I(W)$-fraction of asymptotically perfect channels and a $(1 - I(W))$-fraction of asymptotically useless ones, with a vanishing middle ground. Combining (6) with the union bound (1) proves that polar codes achieve $I(W)$ with block error probability decaying to zero — the first *constructive* capacity theorem for symmetric B-DMCs. > **Theorem (Rate of polarization, Arıkan–Telatar):** For any symmetric B-DMC and any $\beta < 1/2$, the block error probability of polar codes under SC decoding satisfies $P_e = O\!\left(2^{-N^{\beta}}\right)$. The exponent $1/2$ is tied to the $2 \times 2$ kernel; larger kernels can push the exponent toward $1$ [3]. ### 4.3 Successive-Cancellation Decoding and Its Analysis SC decoding decides bits sequentially: frozen bits are set to their known values; information bit $i$ is decided by thresholding the LLR $L_N^{(i)}(y_1^N, \hat{u}_1^{i-1})$. The block error event is contained in the union of first-error events $\mathcal{E}_i$, each bounded by $Z(W_N^{(i)})$, yielding (1). For the BEC, $Z$ recursions are exact ($Z_{2i-1} = 2Z_i - Z_i^2$, $Z_{2i} = Z_i^2$), making the BEC the laboratory in which polarization was first understood quantitatively. The weakness of SC is *error propagation with no recovery*: one wrong hard decision corrupts all subsequent LLRs through the $g$ recursion. At finite lengths ($N \le 2048$), SC polar codes lag LDPC codes by $1$–$2$ dB precisely because of this fragility — a gap list decoding was invented to close. ### 4.4 SCL Decoding and CRC-Aided Selection Tal and Vardy's **successive-cancellation list** decoder [2] generalizes SC by maintaining up to $L$ partial decoding paths. At each information bit, every surviving path splits into two (bit $0$ and bit $1$); a **path metric** — the accumulated log-likelihood penalty $$\mathrm{PM}_\ell^{(i)} = \sum_{j \le i} \ln\!\left(1 + e^{-(1 - 2\hat{u}_j[\ell])\, L_N^{(j)}[\ell]}\right) \tag{7}$$ — ranks the $2L$ candidates, and only the $L$ best survive. A naive implementation costs $\Omega(LN^2)$; Tal and Vardy's lazy-copy data structures reduce this to $O(LN \log N)$ time and $O(LN)$ space, and their log-domain formulation is numerically stable. The list decoder's output, however, needs a tie-breaker. The CRC-aided variant appends an $m$-bit CRC (in 5G NR, $m \in \{6, 11, 16, 24\}$ depending on payload size) and selects the surviving path passing the check. Empirically, at $N = 2048$, rate $1/2$, on the AWGN channel: | Decoder | $E_b/N_0$ at FER $10^{-4}$ | Gain over SC | |---|---|---| | SC | $\approx 2.6$ dB | — | | SCL, $L = 8$ | $\approx 1.9$ dB | $0.7$ dB | | SCL, $L = 32$ | $\approx 1.7$ dB | $0.9$ dB | | CA-SCL, $L = 32$, CRC-16 | $\approx 1.5$ dB | $1.1$ dB | The $L = 32$ CRC-aided curve sits within $\approx 0.2$ dB of the ML bound and matches or beats the WiMAX LDPC codes of the same size [2] — the result that converted the industry from skepticism to the 5G standardization push. ```rust /// Path-metric update for one SCL branch (log-domain, Tal-Vardy formulation). /// `llr`: bit-channel LLR for this path; `bit`: hypothesized value 0/1. #[inline] fn update_path_metric(pm: f64, llr: f64, bit: u8) -> f64 { // penalty = ln(1 + exp(-(1 - 2u) * L)); 0 if decision agrees with LLR sign let s: f64 = if bit == 0 { 1.0 } else { -1.0 }; pm + ((-s * llr).exp() + 1.0).ln() } /// Min-sum f-node (upper branch) and g-node (lower branch) updates. #[inline] fn f_node(a: f64, b: f64) -> f64 { a.signum() * b.signum() * a.abs().min(b.abs()) } #[inline] fn g_node(a: f64, b: f64, u_hat: u8) -> f64 { if u_hat == 0 { a + b } else { -a + b } } ``` Complexity comparison across decoder generations: | Decoder | Time | Space | Finite-length quality | |---|---|---|---| | SC | $O(N \log N)$ | $O(N)$ | Weak at $N \le 2048$ | | SCL($L$) | $O(LN \log N)$ | $O(LN)$ | Near-ML for $L \ge 8$ | | CA-SCL($L$, CRC-$m$) | $O(LN \log N)$ | $O(LN)$ | Matches LDPC; 5G choice | | ML (reference) | $O(2^K)$ | — | Optimal, infeasible | ### 4.5 Finite-Length Scaling and the 5G NR Standardization Asymptotics hide the *scaling law*: how large must $N$ be to operate at rate $R = I(W) - \Delta$ with error $\epsilon$? For polar codes under SC, the gap scales as $\Delta \sim N^{-1/\mu}$ with scaling exponent $\mu \approx 3.627$ for general symmetric B-DMCs (and $\mu = 3.627\ldots$ is optimal among known bounds; the BEC enjoys a smaller $\mu$). Equivalently, blocklength must grow as $\Delta^{-3.627}$ — polynomially worse than random coding's $\Delta^{-2}$, a price paid for explicitness. Industrial adoption required freezing the remaining degrees of freedom. In 3GPP Release 15, polar codes were selected for the **5G NR eMBB control channels** (PDCCH, PUCCH, PBCH) while LDPC took the data channel — a famous standards compromise. The specification (TS 38.212) fixes: 1. **Nested reliability sequence** $Q_0^{1023}$: a single channel-independent ordering of length $1024$, from which length-$N$ subsequences are extracted — guaranteeing nested codes across blocklengths. 2. **CRC attachment**: CRC-24C for downlink control information, CRC-11/CRC-6 for short UCI payloads, with 3 distributed CRC bits interleaved for early termination. 3. **Rate matching**: sub-block interleaving followed by puncturing, shortening, or repetition to reach arbitrary code rates from the mother $N = 2^n$ code. 4. **Decoder mandate**: implementations universally use CA-SCL with $L = 8$ — the sweet spot where the FER curve saturates near the ML bound while silicon area stays manageable. The tutorial treatment of this full encoding chain is given in [4], and the IEEE Communications Society's curated reading list [5] collects the foundational papers. --- ## 5. Empirical Evidence and Proofs The theory makes falsifiable predictions, and simulation confirms them with unusual fidelity for a coding-theoretic construction. **Polarization visualized.** Computing $Z(W_N^{(i)})$ for $N = 1024$ on the $\mathrm{BEC}(0.5)$ via exact recursion and plotting against $i$ shows the signature *polarization staircase*: indices cluster at $Z \approx 0$ or $Z \approx 1$, with only a thin transitional band — the vanishing middle ground of (6) made visible. **FER waterfall.** For $N = 1024$, $K = 512$ polar codes on the AWGN channel with BPSK, constructed by GA at design-SNR $2$ dB, the simulated ordering of decoders is strict and reproducible: SC $<$ SCL($L=8$) $<$ SCL($L=32$) $<$ CA-SCL($L=32$, CRC-24), spanning roughly $1.5$ dB at FER $10^{-3}$. The CA-SCL curve's slope steepens toward the ML bound, consistent with the genie interpretation of the CRC: the list *contains* the ML codeword with high probability, and the CRC *identifies* it. **Scaling verification.** Plotting the SNR gap to capacity at fixed FER against $N$ on log–log axes yields the $N^{-1/\mu}$ scaling line with $\mu \approx 3.6$ for the AWGN channel, matching the analytical bounds from the polarization martingale analysis and the refined exponent studies of [3]. **Standardization evidence.** The 3GPP selection process (2016) ran extensive link-level evaluations: CA-SCL polar codes outperformed LDPC and tail-biting convolutional competitors for short control payloads ($K \le$ a few hundred bits) at BLER $10^{-2}$–$10^{-3}$, precisely the regime where SC's weakness is masked by the list and CRC. This is why polar codes own the control plane while LDPC owns the data plane — a division of labor reflecting each code's finite-length strengths [4][5]. --- ## 6. Limitations 1. **SC's finite-length fragility.** Plain successive cancellation is asymptotically optimal yet practically weak at $N \le 2048$; the $1$–$2$ dB deficit versus LDPC under SC alone is a genuine limitation of the decoder, not the code. 2. **List decoding cost.** CA-SCL multiplies complexity and, more importantly, *latency* by $L$: the $L = 8$ 5G decoder traverses the butterfly serially, and the sequential bit-by-bit schedule resists parallelization. Throughput-oriented variants (fast-SSC, partitioned SCL) add implementation complexity. 3. **Construction sensitivity.** GA-based frozen sets are tuned to a *design SNR*; mismatch between design and operating SNR costs tenths of a dB, and the channel-independent 5G sequence trades a small loss for universality. 4. **Scaling exponent.** $\mu \approx 3.627$ means polar codes need longer blocks than random codes to close the same gap to capacity — the asymptotic tax of the Kronecker structure. 5. **Data-channel defeat.** For long-blocklength eMBB data, LDPC's superior waterfall and highly parallel decoder won the 3GPP vote; polar codes are a control-channel technology, not a universal one. 6. **Error-floor and quantization effects.** Fixed-point LLR quantization in hardware interacts with the min-sum approximation; deep saturation of LLRs can distort path metrics in SCL, requiring careful clipping analysis. --- ## 7. Conclusion Channel polarization is that rare theoretical advance that is simultaneously a *mathematical jewel* and an *industrial standard*. From the $2 \times 2$ kernel $F$ and the supermartingale of Bhattacharyya parameters, Arıkan derived the first explicit capacity-achieving codes with $O(N \log N)$ complexity [1]; Tal and Vardy's list decoder [2] together with CRC aiding transformed the asymptotic promise into finite-length competitiveness; and the 3GPP 5G NR standard [4][5] froze the design into silicon now deployed in billions of devices. The remaining frontiers — larger polarization kernels with exponent approaching $1$ [3], PAC codes that pre-convolve the input to sharpen the distance spectrum, and decoding algorithms that break the serial latency barrier — all build on the same foundation: *take a channel, split it, and let the extremes do the work*. --- ## References [1] E.
