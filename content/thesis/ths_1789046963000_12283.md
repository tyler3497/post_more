# Zero-Knowledge Proof Aggregation: Recursive SNARKs, Folding Schemes, and Accumulation from Nova to HyperNova

## Abstract

*Proof aggregation is the art of compressing many zero-knowledge arguments into one: given proofs $\pi_1, \dots, \pi_n$ for statements in some relation, produce a single succinct proof $\pi$ that verifies all of them. This thesis traces the lineage from early recursion in Halo through accumulation schemes to the modern folding-scheme revolution of Nova, SuperNova, HyperNova, Protostar, and Protogalaxy. We formalize the distinction between recursion, aggregation, and accumulation; develop the theory of folding schemes for relaxed R1CS and customizable constraint systems; analyze the algebraic machinery of cross-terms, relaxed relations, and polynomial evaluation arguments; and compare prover costs, recursion overhead, and verifier asymptotics across the literature. We show how CycleFold resolves the non-native-field arithmetic problem on elliptic-curve cycles, how multifolding generalizes IVC to PCD, and how compression layers reduce folding proofs to logarithmic-size SNARKs. The treatment closes with quantitative benchmarks, security considerations, and the open problems that define the frontier.*

## 1 Introduction

The central problem of this thesis is *compositional verifiability at scale*. A single zero-knowledge proof $\pi$ certifies a computation $C(x) = y$ in time that is polylogarithmic in $|C|$, but realistic workloads are not single computations: they are streams of transactions, long-running virtual-machine executions, and batches of independently-generated statements that a verifier must accept together. Naively verifying $n$ proofs costs $n$ times the cost of one; in blockchain and verifiable-computation settings, this linear blowup is fatal.

Three related but distinct paradigms address the blowup:

- **Recursion**: a proof $\pi_i$ contains a verifier circuit that checks $\pi_{i-1}$, so that $\pi_\ell$ transitively certifies $\ell$ steps. The classic route is *incrementally verifiable computation* (IVC) [9].
- **Aggregation**: $n$ independent proofs $\pi_1, \dots, \pi_n$ are combined into one $\pi$ with $|\pi| \ll \sum |\pi_i|$, often via an aggregation circuit or a single recursive layer.
- **Accumulation**: a weaker primitive in which a *decider* defers expensive checks, maintaining an accumulator of constant size that is only "decided" at the end; folding schemes are its most successful instantiation [8, 9].

The historical arc is instructive. Early recursion (e.g., the BCTV/BCCT approach [10]) required *cycles of elliptic curves* and paid enormous recursion overhead because the verifier was expressed inside its own proof system. Halo [7] removed the trusted setup and introduced *proof-carrying data* with nested amortization, but its prover remained heavy. The breakthrough of Nova [1] was to sidestep recursion-through-verification entirely: instead of embedding a SNARK verifier in a circuit, Nova *folds* two instance–witness pairs into one via a public-coin protocol whose prover cost is a constant number of multiexponentiations and whose recursion overhead is just two group scalar multiplications in-circuit — the smallest in the literature.

This thesis provides a self-contained, PhD-level treatment of the field. Our contributions are expository and analytical: a unified formal framework for folding schemes over relaxed and customizable constraint systems; a precise accounting of cross-term algebra and its generalizations; an empirical comparison of prover/verifier asymptotics; and a survey of the engineering artifacts (curve cycles, CycleFold [6], compression SNARKs) that make these systems practical.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Succinct arguments and the recursion problem

A *succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge* (SNARK) for a relation $\mathcal{R}$ is a triple $(\mathsf{Setup}, \mathsf{Prove}, \mathsf{Verify})$ such that proof size and verification time are $O_\lambda(\mathrm{polylog}(|x|))$. Knowledge soundness requires an extractor; zero knowledge requires simulatability. Classical SNARKs (Groth16 [11]) achieve constant-size proofs but need a *trusted setup* per circuit. Transparent systems (Spartan [12], STARKs) avoid trusted setup via the *sum-check protocol* and polynomial commitments.

Recursion demands that a proof system verify proofs expressed in itself. Formally, an IVC scheme [9] proves $y = F^{(\ell)}(x)$ for a step function $F$ and iteration count $\ell$, with prover work per step *independent of $\ell$*.

> **Definition (IVC).** An incrementally verifiable computation scheme consists of $(\mathsf{Gen}, \mathsf{P}, \mathsf{V})$ where $\mathsf{P}(pk, i, z_0, z_i, \Pi_i, \omega_i)$ outputs $(z_{i+1}, \Pi_{i+1})$ such that $\mathsf{V}(vk, i, z_0, z_i, \Pi_i) = 1$ iff $z_i = F^{(i)}(z_0)$ for nondeterministic witness $\omega_i$ at step $i$.

### 2.2 Accumulation schemes

Bunz, Chiesa, Mishra, and Spooner [8] introduced *accumulation schemes*: a prover and verifier interact to fold an instance $x$ into an *accumulator* $acc$ of constant size; a *decider* later checks the accumulator. Unlike a SNARK, the verifier's check is deferred, which permits recursion without succinctness at each step. Folding schemes are non-interactive, public-coin accumulation schemes with an additional *efficiency* property: folding is cheap.

### 2.3 Polynomial commitments and evaluation arguments

All modern folding schemes rely on a commitment scheme for vectors with an *evaluation argument* (inner-product argument). The workhorse is the Pedersen commitment $\mathsf{Com}(w) = \langle w, G \rangle + rH$ with the Bulletproofs-style IPA [13], which needs no trusted setup; pairing-based alternatives (KZG [14]) give constant-size evaluations at the price of a structured reference string.

## 3 Methodology

Our method is a *synthetic formal analysis*: we extract the common algebraic skeleton shared by Nova, SuperNova, HyperNova, Protostar, and Protogalaxy, prove (in sketch form) the key soundness lemmas, and derive the asymptotic cost table from the papers' own complexity claims, cross-checked against implementation benchmarks.

### 3.1 The relaxed-relation trick

The core insight of Nova [1] is *relaxation*. Folding two R1CS instances $(A,B,C), z_1, z_2$ with $(Az) \circ (Bz) = Cz$ fails naively because the Hadamard product is quadratic: folding $z = z_1 + r z_2$ produces cross-terms $Az_1 \circ Bz_2 + Az_2 \circ Bz_1$ that cannot be absorbed. Nova *relaxes* the relation with an error vector $E$ and scalar $u$:

> **Definition (Committed relaxed R1CS).** An instance is $( \bar{E}, u, \bar{W}, x )$ with witness $(E, r_E, W, r_W)$ satisfying $(AZ) \circ (BZ) = u \cdot (CZ) + E$, where $Z = (W, x, u)$, $\bar{E} = \mathsf{Com}(E, r_E)$, $\bar{W} = \mathsf{Com}(W, r_W)$.

The prover folds $(u_1, u_2)$ with random $r$ into $(u, w)$ where $u.E = u_1.E + r T + r^2 u_2.E$, absorbing the cross-term $T = AZ_1 \circ BZ_2 + AZ_2 \circ BZ_1 - u_1 CZ_2 - u_2 CZ_1$ into the error vector. This is the *entire* algebraic content of folding: quadratic relations fold cleanly once an error term is admitted.

### 3.2 From folding to IVC

Given a folding scheme for a relation, IVC is constructed by the standard bootstrapping: at step $i$, the prover folds the *running* instance $U_i$ (which encodes "all prior steps were correct") with the fresh step instance $u_i$ (encoding "$z_{i+1} = F(z_i)$ and the verifier's own checks pass"). Because folding is public-coin, the Fiat–Shamir transform yields a non-interactive IVC proof. A final compression step proves knowledge of a satisfying relaxed witness with a zkSNARK (Spartan-based [12]), reducing $O(|F|)$ group elements to $O(\log |F|)$.

### 3.3 Scope of comparison

We compare along five axes: (i) constraint system supported; (ii) prover work per fold; (iii) recursion overhead (in-circuit verifier cost); (iv) proof/accumulator size; (v) setup assumptions.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Nova: the folding-scheme blueprint

Nova's folding scheme for committed relaxed R1CS works as follows. Let $u_1, u_2$ be instances with witnesses $w_1, w_2$, and let $r$ be the verifier's challenge:

1. The prover computes the cross-term $T$ and its commitment $\bar{T}$.
2. Both parties compute the folded instance $u \gets ( \bar{E}_1 + r\bar{T} + r^2\bar{E}_2,\ u_1 + r u_2,\ \bar{W}_1 + r\bar{W}_2,\ x_1 + r x_2 )$.
3. The prover computes the folded witness $w \gets (E_1 + rT + r^2 E_2,\ \cdot,\ W_1 + rW_2,\ \cdot)$.

*Knowledge soundness* follows from a forking argument: three transcripts with distinct challenges let the extractor interpolate the witnesses. *Zero knowledge* is achieved by the "randomize-then-release" technique: fold with a freshly sampled random instance before revealing the witness.

The efficiency headline of Nova is striking [1]:

- **Recursion overhead**: constant — dominated by *two group scalar multiplications* expressed as a circuit.
- **Prover work per step**: dominated by *two multiexponentiations* of size $O(|F|)$.
- **No trusted setup, no FFTs**; instantiable over any DLOG-hard curve cycle.

The IVC proof size is $O(|F|)$ group elements, compressible to $O(\log |F|)$ via a Spartan-variant SNARK.

```rust
// Sketch of Nova's fold step (relaxed R1CS), cf. microsoft/Nova
fn fold(u1: &RelaxedInstance, u2: &Instance, r: Scalar) -> RelaxedInstance {
    let t = cross_term(&u1, &u2);          // AZ1∘BZ2 + AZ2∘BZ1 − u1·CZ2 − u2·CZ1
    let t_bar = pedersen_commit(&t);      // sent to verifier
    RelaxedInstance {
        e_bar: u1.e_bar + t_bar * r + u2.e_bar * r.square(),
        u: u1.u + r * u2.u,
        w_bar: u1.w_bar + u2.w_bar * r,
        x: u1.x.iter().zip(&u2.x).map(|(a,b)| a + r*b).collect(),
    }
}
```

### 4.2 SuperNova and non-uniform IVC

Standard IVC requires every step to use the *same* circuit $F$ — disastrous for universal machines (e.g., EVM), where each step executes one of many possible instructions and the circuit must pay for all of them. SuperNova [2] generalizes folding to *non-uniform* IVC: the step function is a set $\{F_1, \dots, F_k\}$ and each step folds into the accumulator corresponding to the instruction actually executed, while the others receive dummy folds. The prover cost per step becomes proportional to the *executed* instruction's circuit size plus a small selector overhead — an "à la carte" cost profile that makes zkVMs practical. Formally, SuperNova maintains $k$ running instances and folds $u_i$ into $U_{\varphi(i)}$ where $\varphi(i)$ selects the instruction.

### 4.3 HyperNova: multifolding and customizable constraint systems

HyperNova [3] makes three advances. First, it replaces R1CS with **CCS** (customizable constraint systems) [4], which generalize Plonkish, R1CS, and AIR *without overhead* — a constraint system defined by a set of matrices $\{M_j\}$ and a multivariate polynomial $g$:

$$\sum_{i} c_i \prod_{j \in S_i} \langle M_j \cdot z, \text{(selectors)} \rangle = 0.$$

Second, it introduces **multifolding schemes**: instances from two *different* NP relations sharing structure can be folded together, generalizing Nova's homogeneous folding. The construction is an "early-stopping" variant of Spartan's sum-check applied to a polynomial encoding claims about prior linearized CCS instances. Third, the prover's cryptographic cost per fold drops to a *single* MSM of size equal to the number of variables — optimal for MSM-based commitments — and zero knowledge is achieved "for free" by randomizing IVC proofs with the folding scheme itself, avoiding zkSNARKs in the loop.

HyperNova also contributes **nlookup**, a lookup argument tailored to folding-based recursion: $m$ lookups into a table of size $n$ cost $O(n)$ field operations plus $O(m \log n)$ degree-2 constraints — ideal for large finite-state machines such as zkVM instruction decoders.

### 4.4 Protostar, Protogalaxy, and Sangria: the folding zoo

The folding paradigm rapidly diversified:

- **Protostar** [5] generalizes folding to *arbitrary* special-sound protocols via *accumulation-friendly* encodings, folding $N$ instances at once with logarithmic verifier work, and — crucially — works over a *single* curve rather than a cycle, by expressing the verifier with *incomplete* (non-native) arithmetic deferred through the accumulator.
- **Protogalaxy** [6] (Eagen et al.) folds *Plonkish* instances with a vector of challenges $(r, r^2, \dots)$, achieving "fold $N$ into 1" with a single accumulator update and power-basis cross-terms; it is the engine behind several production zkVMs.
- **Sangria** (Geometry Research) folds *Plonk* instances directly, showing that the permutation argument and lookup structure of Plonk survive relaxation with appropriate error polynomials.

The common pattern is *relax, fold with cross-terms, defer verification*: each system differs only in the constraint language and the shape of the error term.

### 4.5 CycleFold: taming non-native arithmetic

Folding-based IVC needs a *cycle of elliptic curves* (e.g., Pallas/Vesta, BN254/Grumpkin) because the verifier circuit for curve $\mathbb{E}_1$ must be expressed over the scalar field of $\mathbb{E}_2$ and vice versa [7, 10]. But the folding verifier itself performs group operations on $\mathbb{E}_1$ — *non-native* arithmetic when expressed in $\mathbb{E}_2$'s scalar field, costing thousands of constraints per scalar multiplication. **CycleFold** [6] solves this by expressing the in-circuit group arithmetic as *another* folding instance: the expensive non-native operations are folded (not proven in-circuit), so the recursion overhead collapses back to a handful of native operations. Formally, CycleFold adds a secondary committed relaxed R1CS instance tracking the correctness of the $\mathbb{E}_1$-arithmetic, folded alongside the main instance — recursion all the way down, but with each layer paying only native costs.

> **Theorem (CycleFold, informal).** Any folding-based IVC over a 2-cycle can be transformed so that the per-step in-circuit cost is dominated by $O(1)$ native field operations plus the folding of a constant-size auxiliary instance. [6]

## 5 Empirical Evidence and Proofs

### 5.1 Asymptotic comparison

| Scheme | Constraint system | Prover / fold | Recursion overhead | Accumulator size | Setup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Halo [7] | R1CS (nested amortization) | $O(n \log n)$ MSM | large (full verifier) | $O(\log n)$ | transparent |
| Nova [1] | Relaxed R1CS | 2 MSM of size $O(|F|)$ | 2 scalar muls (constant) | $O(|F|)$ group els. | transparent |
| SuperNova [2] | Relaxed R1CS (non-uniform) | 2 MSM + selector | constant + selector | $k \times O(|F|)$ | transparent |
| HyperNova [3] | Relaxed CCS | 1 MSM of size $O(\|vars\|)$ | constant (optimal) | $O(|vars|)$ group els. | transparent |
| Protostar [5] | Special-sound protocols | $O(N)$ MSM | logarithmic | $O(\log N)$ | transparent |
| Protogalaxy [6] | Plonkish | 1 MSM + power terms | constant | $O(1)$ + challenges | transparent |
| Groth16 recursion [11] | R1CS | $O(n \log n)$ | full verifier circuit | $O(1)$ | trusted (per circuit) |

### 5.2 Benchmarks and implementations

The `nova-snark` Rust implementation (Microsoft Research) supports three curve cycles — Pallas/Vesta, BN254/Grumpkin, secp/secq — with Pedersen+IPA or HyperKZG commitments, and compresses IVC proofs with (Micro)Spartan. Reported per-step proving for a $2^{20}$-gate circuit is on the order of seconds on commodity hardware, with recursion overhead measured in *tens of thousands* of constraints — two orders of magnitude below pre-Nova recursive SNARKs. Protogalaxy-based zkVMs report folding $N = 2^{10}$ Plonk proofs into one accumulator with verifier work logarithmic in $N$.

### 5.3 Security arguments (proof sketches)

*Knowledge soundness of folding* reduces to the binding of the commitment scheme plus the forking lemma: from three accepting transcripts the extractor recovers $w_1, w_2$. *IVC soundness* follows by induction: if $U_i$ is a valid fold of all steps so far and $u_i$ certifies step $i$ (including the check that the verifier accepted $U_{i-1}$'s fold), then $U_{i+1}$ certifies steps $0..i$. The critical subtlety is *Fiat–Shamir soundness in the algebraic group model*: the challenge $r$ must bind the instance, or a malicious prover can grind cross-terms. All deployed implementations hash the full instance transcript into $r$.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Proof size before compression.** Folding accumulators are $O(|F|)$ group elements — not succinct until the compression SNARK runs, and compression itself costs a full SNARK proof over the folded witness. For short computations, plain Spartan or Groth16 is cheaper.
2. **Curve-cycle dependence.** Despite CycleFold, production deployments still need well-studied cycles (Pallas/Vesta, BN254/Grumpkin); pairing-friendly cycles with 2-adicity for FFTs remain scarce, and HyperKZG-based variants reintroduce trusted setup.
3. **Non-uniformity overhead.** SuperNova's selector logic and per-instruction accumulators multiply state by the instruction count $k$; for ISAs with hundreds of opcodes, accumulator management dominates memory.
4. **Zero-knowledge subtleties.** "ZK for free" via randomization requires careful blinding of the error vector $E$; early Nova implementations leaked the cross-term commitment pattern before the randomize-then-release fix.
5. **Post-quantum insecurity.** All folding schemes rest on discrete-logarithm assumptions; lattice- or hash-based recursive aggregation remains an open problem, with only preliminary STARK-recursion results.
6. **Formal verification gap.** The Fiat–Shamir'd folding schemes in production have machine-checked proofs only for fragments; a full verified implementation (à la seL4-style refinement) does not yet exist.

## 7 Conclusion

Proof aggregation has moved, in half a decade, from "embed a verifier in a circuit and pray the cycle exists" to a clean algebraic discipline: *relax the relation, fold with cross-terms, defer the rest*. Nova [1] supplied the blueprint; SuperNova [2] made it non-uniform; HyperNova [3] generalized the constraint language and folded many instances at once; Protostar [5] and Protogalaxy [6] extended folding to arbitrary special-sound protocols and Plonkish arithmetizations; CycleFold [6] removed the last asymptotic tax of non-native arithmetic. The practical consequence is that incrementally verifiable computation is now a commodity: zkVMs fold millions of execution steps with per-step prover work dominated by two multiexponentiations and recursion overhead of two scalar multiplications.

The frontier is clear: *post-quantum folding*, *verified implementations*, and *tighter compression* — reducing the $O(|F|)$ accumulator without a full SNARK pass, perhaps via multivariate or code-based accumulation. The algebraic core, however, is settled: folding is to recursion what the sum-check is to succinctness — the indispensable primitive.
