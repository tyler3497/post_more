# Linear Logic, Session Types, and the Curry–Howard Correspondence for Concurrency: From Proofs to Communicating Processes

## Abstract

The Curry–Howard correspondence identifies propositions with types, proofs with programs, and proof normalization with computation. Extending it from the sequential $\lambda$-calculus to concurrent interaction is a central problem of programming language theory: process calculi admit no canonical term model, and races, deadlock, and behavioral equivalence have no direct logical analogue. This thesis develops the now-canonical answer: *propositions of linear logic are session types, proofs are $\pi$-calculus processes, and cut elimination is communication*. We reconstruct the intuitionistic interpretation of Caires and Pfenning (2010), in which the sequent $\Delta \vdash P :: x : A$ reads as "$P$ provides session $A$ on channel $x$"; Wadler's classical calculus CP (2012/2014), which removes the provided/required asymmetry and hosts a type-preserving translation from Gay and Vasconcelos's functional GV; and the expressiveness hierarchy separating the two, where the classical system strictly subsumes the intuitionistic one. Session fidelity, global progress, and deadlock freedom emerge as theorems of logic rather than accidents of language design. We close with the boundaries — asynchrony, multiparty sessions, failure, recursion, decidability — and the open problems they define.

## 1. Introduction

In 1934, Haskell Curry observed that the axioms of combinatory logic correspond precisely to the typing rules of a formal system of functions; in 1969, William Howard extended the observation into a full isomorphism between intuitionistic natural deduction and the simply typed $\lambda$-calculus, published in 1980 [1][9]. Under this *formulae-as-types* correspondence, a proposition is a type, a proof is a program, and proof normalization — the elimination of detours in a deduction — computes exactly as $\beta$-reduction does. The correspondence reorganized the foundations of programming languages: type systems became logics, and program behavior became proof theory.

The sequential story is essentially complete. The concurrent story resisted for decades. The $\pi$-calculus and its relatives model interaction, but interaction has no obvious notion of "proof": processes compose in parallel, exchange names, replicate, and deadlock, and none of these operations corresponds to a rule of any standard logic in an evident way. Early attempts — Abramsky's *Proofs as Processes* (1994) and Bellin and Scott's encodings (1994) — mapped linear logic proofs into $\pi$-calculus terms and showed that cut reduction is simulated by process reduction, but the encodings were complex and indirect [3][7].

The decisive step came from an unexpected direction: *session types*. Introduced by Honda (1993) as a discipline for structured dyadic interaction, session types describe communication protocols from the viewpoint of each participant, with *duality* guaranteeing that the two endpoints of a channel always agree [4]. In 2010, Caires and Pfenning discovered that the connectives of *intuitionistic linear logic* (DILL) admit a direct reading as session types, and the proof rules of the sequent calculus are exactly the typing rules of a session-typed $\pi$-calculus [2]. Propositions became session types, proofs became processes, and cut elimination became communication.

Wadler (2012, journal version 2014) revisited the correspondence in *classical* linear logic, removing the asymmetry between "provided" and "required" channels and obtaining the calculus CP — *Classical Processes* — together with a faithful translation from a functional session language GV [3]. The two interpretations have since been compared formally: the classical system is strictly more expressive, while the intuitionistic one enforces locality disciplines on shared channels that the classical one does not [6].

This thesis presents the full arc. Section 2 reviews linear logic, the $\pi$-calculus, and session types. Section 3 states the correspondence as a methodology — a translation schema plus an operational correspondence theorem. Section 4 develops the four pillars: the intuitionistic interpretation, the classical interpretation, their expressiveness comparison, and the treatment of parallel composition via hypersequents that completes the behavioral theory. Section 5 collects the resulting guarantees — session fidelity, global progress, deadlock freedom — alongside complexity and implementation evidence. Section 6 maps the limitations and open problems.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Linear logic: logic as resource

Classical and intuitionistic logic treat hypotheses as *truths*: once assumed, a hypothesis may be used any number of times (contraction) or not at all (weakening). Girard's linear logic (1987) rejects this idealization [1]. A linear hypothesis is a *resource* — a file handle, a quantum bit, a one-shot channel — that must be consumed exactly once. Contraction and weakening are removed as global structural rules and reintroduced in controlled form through the exponentials $!A$ ("as many copies of $A$ as needed") and $?A$.

The connectives split into three families:

| Family | Connectives | Intuition |
|---|---|---|
| Multiplicatives | $A \otimes B$, $A \parr B$, $1$, $\bot$ | Independent / interacting resources |
| Additives | $A \with B$, $A \oplus B$, $\top$, $0$ | Choice: external / internal |
| Exponentials | $!A$, $?A$ | Controlled reuse |

Linear implication decomposes as $A \multimap B \equiv A^\bot \parr B$: consuming $A$ to produce $B$. The central theorem is *cut elimination*: every proof can be transformed into one without the cut rule, and the transformation terminates (strong normalization). Cut elimination is the dynamics of the logic — and, as we shall see, the dynamics of concurrent communication.

Girard himself suggested, when introducing linear logic, that it would be suited to reasoning about concurrency [8]. The suggestion took twenty years to mature into a type system.

### 2.2 The $\pi$-calculus and session types

Milner's $\pi$-calculus models mobile concurrent processes: names are channels, processes send and receive names, compose in parallel ($P \mid Q$), hide names ($\nu x.\, P$), and replicate ($!P$). Its dynamics is reduction driven by communication on a shared name.

Honda (1993) imposed *protocol discipline* on this anarchy [4]. A session type describes the entire conversation on one channel from one endpoint's perspective:

- $!T.S$ — send a value of type $T$, continue as $S$;
- $?T.S$ — receive, continue as $S$;
- $\oplus\{l_i : S_i\}$ — select label $l_i$, continue as $S_i$ (internal choice);
- $\&\{l_i : S_i\}$ — offer branches (external choice);
- $\mathsf{end}$ — terminate.

Two endpoints are safe when their types are *dual* ($\overline{!T.S} = ?T.\overline{S}$, $\overline{\oplus} = \&$, and so on): whenever one sends, the other receives. Honda, Vasconcelos, and Kubo (1998) extended this to a full language with primitives for session initiation and delegation [4][7]. The analogy with logic was suggestive — choice operators $\&$ and $\oplus$ even share their symbols with linear logic — but Honda drew no formal connection.

### 2.3 The sequential correspondence, and why concurrency is harder

The classical correspondence is a three-way isomorphism:

| Logic | Type theory | Computation |
|---|---|---|
| Proposition | Type | — |
| Proof | Program (term) | — |
| Cut elimination | — | Evaluation |

For concurrency we want a fourth column, *processes*, and a dynamics in which cut elimination *is* communication. The obstacle, identified sharply in recent work, is that no rule of linear logic corresponds directly to the parallel operator $P \mid Q$ — the most fundamental structural operator of process calculi [7]. Reconstructing the expected labelled transition system of the $\pi$-calculus from proof theory therefore requires new logical machinery, discussed in Section 4.4.

## 3. Methodology

The correspondence is established by a *translation schema* together with an *operational correspondence theorem*, in three steps.

**Step 1 — Propositions as session types.** Each linear logic connective is read as a communication action:

| Linear logic | Session type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $A \otimes B$ | $!A.B$ | Output a channel of type $A$, continue as $B$ |
| $A \parr B$ | $?A.B$ | Input a channel of type $A$, continue as $B$ |
| $A \with B$ | $\&\{A, B\}$ | External choice (branch) |
| $A \oplus B$ | $\oplus\{A, B\}$ | Internal choice (select) |
| $1$ | $\mathsf{end}$ (send) | Close the session |
| $\bot$ | $\mathsf{end}$ (receive) | Wait for close |
| $!A$ | Server | Replicated service offering $A$ |
| $A^\bot$ | $\overline{A}$ | Dual protocol |

Duality of session types *is* linear negation: $\overline{A \otimes B} = \overline{A} \parr \overline{B}$ is exactly de Morgan duality.

**Step 2 — Proofs as processes.** A derivation of $\Delta \vdash P :: x : A$ (intuitionistic) or $P \vdash \Delta$ (classical) decorates every inference rule with a process constructor: $\otimes$-right becomes output, $\parr$-right becomes input, $\with$-right becomes branching, cut becomes parallel composition under restriction, $(\nu x)(P \mid Q)$.

**Step 3 — Cut elimination as communication.** The principal cut reductions of linear logic map exactly onto the communication steps of the $\pi$-calculus:

> **Theorem (Operational correspondence).** If $\mathcal{D}$ is a derivation of a well-typed process $P$ and $\mathcal{D} \Rightarrow \mathcal{D}'$ by principal cut reduction, then $P \longrightarrow^* P'$ where $P'$ is the process decorating $\mathcal{D}'$; conversely, every communication step of $P$ is simulated by cut reductions of $\mathcal{D}$ [2][7].

Type preservation (subject reduction) is then *cut admissibility under reduction*: communication never breaks the protocol. The methodology turns concurrency metatheory into proof theory.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The intuitionistic interpretation: $\pi$DILL

Caires and Pfenning's 2010 paper *"Session Types as Intuitionistic Linear Propositions"* (CONCUR 2010; journal version with Toninho, MSCS 2016) presents a type system for the $\pi$-calculus that *exactly* corresponds to the dyadic sequent calculus for dual intuitionistic linear logic (DILL) [2].

The judgment has the form

$$\Gamma; \Delta \vdash P :: x : A$$

read: *process $P$ provides session $A$ along channel $x$, using the linear sessions in $\Delta$ and the unrestricted (shared) sessions in $\Gamma$*. The asymmetry between the single distinguished conclusion $x : A$ and the hypotheses is the price — and the power — of intuitionism: every process has a clear "offered" service.

Two constructs correspond to output ($\otimes$ on the right, $\parr$ on the left) and two to input ($\parr$ on the right, $\otimes$ on the left). The cut rule

$$\frac{\Gamma; \Delta_1 \vdash P :: x : A \qquad \Gamma; \Delta_2, x : A \vdash Q :: z : C}{\Gamma; \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \vdash (\nu x)(P \mid Q) :: z : C}$$

spawns a provider $P$ and its client $Q$, connecting them on the private channel $x$. Communication is cut reduction.

Shared channels enter through the exponential: a judgment $\Gamma, u : A; \Delta \vdash P :: x : B$ may invoke the server $u$ repeatedly. Crucially, the intuitionistic discipline enforces *locality of shared channels*: a received channel can never be used to accept new sessions in a way that breaks the provider/client stratification [2][6]. From this system Caires, Pfenning, and Toninho derive the crown jewels:

> **Theorem (Session fidelity).** If $\cdot; \Delta \vdash P :: x : A$ and $P \longrightarrow^* Q$, then $\cdot; \Delta \vdash Q :: x : A$: reduction preserves typing, hence every communication follows the declared protocol.

> **Theorem (Global progress).** A well-typed closed process is either terminated or can reduce — not merely progress on a single session, but progress of the whole system [2].

Global progress is stronger than what pure session type systems deliver and follows directly from the logical reading: a closed proof cannot be stuck.

### 4.2 The classical interpretation: CP and GV

Wadler's *"Propositions as Sessions"* (ICFP 2012; JFP 2014) revisits the correspondence in *classical* linear logic [3]. Classical sequents are one-sided — $P \vdash \Delta$, a process using the channels in $\Delta$ — which removes the provided/required distinction entirely. There is a single construct for output ($\otimes$) and a single construct for input ($\parr$), each dual to the other, with duality given by linear negation on types: the two endpoints of a channel hold types $A$ and $A^\bot$.

Wadler names the resulting calculus **CP** (*Classical Processes*) and, inspired by the linear functional language with session types of Gay and Vasconcelos (2010) [5], introduces **GV** (*Good Variation*): a functional core with session primitives whose type system is the standard presentation of session types. He then gives, for the first time, a formal translation from GV into CP:

> **Theorem (GV to CP translation).** If $\Phi \vdash M : T$ in GV then $\llbracket M \rrbracket_x \vdash \Phi^\bot, x : \llbracket T \rrbracket$ in CP: the translation preserves types [3].

The translation maps $\mathsf{send}$ to a cut-mediated output $x[y].(P \mid Q)$, $\mathsf{select}$ to labelled choice, $\mathsf{connect}$ to cut (channel creation), and $\mathsf{terminate}$ to empty input. The operational payoff is sharp: the differences between GV and Gay and Vasconcelos's original language "suffice to make GV, unlike the original, free from races and deadlock" [3]. Deadlock freedom — the bane of concurrent programming — becomes a corollary of cut elimination.

Lindley and Morris later supplied a denotational semantics for the CP/GV pair and extended CP with replication, adapting the translations accordingly [8].

### 4.3 Classical versus intuitionistic: the expressiveness gap

Are the two interpretations equivalent? No — and the difference is illuminating. Building on Girard's *Logic of Unity*, which subsumes classical, intuitionistic, and linear logic in a single system, recent work develops *United Linear Logic* (ULL) and its process reading $\pi$ULL [6]. The results are inclusions between classes of typable processes:

> **Theorem (Expressiveness).** $\pi$ULL *precisely captures* the class of processes typable under the classical interpretation, and the class typable under the intuitionistic interpretation is *strictly included* in $\pi$ULL [6].

The witness to strictness is locality: Caires and Pfenning's intuitionistic discipline enforces locality of shared channels, whereas Wadler's classical interpretation admits well-typed processes that break locality. The classical system can therefore be regarded as *more expressive* — at the cost of a weaker structural invariant. This is a genuine logical discovery about concurrency: the choice between classical and intuitionistic foundations is not aesthetic but determines which communication patterns are even typable.

### 4.4 Parallel composition, hypersequents, and behavioral theory

The correspondence had one acknowledged gap: the parallel operator. No rule of linear logic builds $P \mid Q$ directly. The fix comes from *hypersequents* — collections of sequents, studied by Avron (1991) — imported into the session setting by Montesi and Peressotti (2018): if $P$ and $Q$ implement the hyperenvironment $\Gamma \mid \Delta$, then $P \mid Q$ is typed by composing hyperenvironments in parallel, recording that the names in $\Gamma$ are used independently of those in $\Delta$ [7].

Kokke, Montesi, and Peressotti (2019) turned this into a conservative extension of linear logic supporting proof transformations from which a labelled transition system can be constructed; the "recipe" paper of 2021 systematizes the metatheory — *Linear Logic, the $\pi$-calculus, and their Metatheory: A Recipe for Proofs as Processes* [7]. Parallel lines of work connect proofs-as-processes to *choreographies* — the "Alice and Bob" notation of security protocols — giving the parallel operator a direct proof-theoretic reading as hypersequent composition (Carbone, Montesi, Schürmann 2018) [7].

With an LTS in place, the behavioral theory follows: observational equivalences under the session discipline, bisimulation, and the long-standing question of how fine-grained the logical notion of process identity is — an active research front [2][7].

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

The "experiments" of this correspondence are its theorems and its implementations. We collect the guarantees that the logical reading delivers for free:

1. **Session fidelity / subject reduction.** Communication preserves session types; a channel typed $! \mathsf{int}. ?\mathsf{bool}. \mathsf{end}$ can never receive a string where an integer is promised [2][3].
2. **Global progress.** Well-typed closed processes never get stuck: either every session has terminated or some communication is enabled [2]. Pure session systems guarantee only progress on a single session.
3. **Deadlock freedom.** GV programs — hence their CP translations — are race- and deadlock-free by construction [3].
4. **Strong normalization.** Cut elimination terminates, so well-typed processes cannot diverge through communication alone; termination is inherited from logic.
5. **Expressiveness inclusions.** Intuitionistic-typable $\subsetneq$ classical-typable $=$ $\pi$ULL-typable [6].

Implementation evidence corroborates the theory. **SILL** is a higher-order functional language integrating intuitionistic session processes via a linear contextual monad [7]. **SePi** is a $\pi$-calculus-based concurrent language with session types; **Links** natively supports binary session types for tierless web programming; **Concurrent C0** brings session-typed channels to a C-like language; and Pucella and Tov embed session types in Haskell via a parameterized monad [7]. Each implementation inherits the metatheory: write a well-typed program, and fidelity and progress hold without further proof.

Complexity results bound the logical side: multiplicative linear logic is NP-complete (Kanovich 1992), multiplicative-additive linear logic is PSPACE-complete, and full propositional linear logic is undecidable (Lincoln, Mitchell, Scedrov & Shankar 1992) [10]. Session typing — which lives in well-behaved fragments — remains decidable and, in practice, efficient.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

The correspondence is not the end of the story; its boundaries are precisely drawn.

**Asynchrony.** Cut reduction is synchronous: output and input meet in one step. Real distributed systems buffer messages. Gay and Vasconcelos (2010) model asynchronous communication with statically bounded buffers [5], but the logical account of asynchrony — where cut meets a queue rather than a partner — remains less canonical than the synchronous core.

**Multiparty sessions.** The correspondence is dyadic: one channel, two endpoints, dual types. Multiparty session types (Honda et al. 2016) describe $n$-party protocols via a global type projected to local roles [7]. A Curry–Howard reading of global types — what logic has $n$-sided sequents as proofs? — is open.

**Failure and exceptions.** Sessions in the correspondence never fail: there is no logical analogue of a dropped connection or a timed-out peer. Extensions with optional blocks and exception handling exist in the session literature but break the clean duality-as-negation story.

**Recursion and duality.** Recursive session types $\mu X. S$ are essential in practice, yet duality interacts subtly with recursion — the interplay of delegation and recursion in the definition of duality required dedicated analysis (Bernardi & Hennessy 2014) [7]. A fully logical treatment of coinductive sessions is incomplete.

**Gradual and dynamic typing.** Real systems mix typed and untyped components. *Gradual session types* extend the discipline with dynamic checks and blame, but their metatheory (the gradual guarantee, blame soundness) under the linear-logic reading is still being worked out [7].

**Liveness versus termination.** Strong normalization gives termination of communication, but realistic servers are intentionally non-terminating. Reconciling the logical guarantee with productive infinite behavior — via sized types, guarded recursion, or fair scheduling — is an active frontier.

**Undecidability.** Full propositional linear logic is undecidable [10]; any extension of the correspondence to richer logics must navigate this cliff. Which fragments keep session typing decidable while capturing realistic protocols is not fully mapped.

## 7. Conclusion

The Curry–Howard correspondence for concurrency states a precise, checkable thesis: *linear logic propositions are session types, proofs are processes, and cut elimination is communication*. From Girard's resource-sensitive logic [1] through Honda's session disciplines [4], Caires and Pfenning's intuitionistic reconstruction [2], Wadler's classical CP and the GV translation [3], to the expressiveness hierarchy of $\pi$ULL [6] and the hypersequent completion of the behavioral theory [7], each step replaced an ad hoc concurrent invariant with a theorem of logic. Session fidelity, global progress, and deadlock freedom are not properties to be proved per language; they are cut elimination, seen from the side of processes.

The open problems — asynchrony, multiparty protocols, failure, recursion, gradual typing, liveness, decidability — mark where the correspondence ends and new logic begins. If history is a guide, each will eventually be absorbed: the next Curry–Howard isomorphism for concurrency is probably already a sequent rule waiting for its process reading.
