# Causal Discovery from Observational and Interventional Data: PC and FCI Algorithms, NOTEARS Differentiable DAG Learning, and Invariant Causal Prediction for Generalization

Causal discovery infers directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) from data under faithfulness and Markov assumptions, yet observational equivalence, latent confounding, and non-convex acyclicity constraints challenge identifiability and scalability. This thesis unifies constraint-based search via PC and FCI, score-based continuous optimization via NOTEARS, and inter-environment invariance via Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP). We formalize d-separation completeness, FCI orientation soundness over partial ancestral graphs (PAGs), and NOTEARS' characterization of acyclicity through trace exponential constraints. We prove consistency conditions for PC under sparsity and strong faithfulness, derive Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions for differentiable DAG learning with L1 regularization, and show ICP's coverage guarantee for causal parents under multiple environments. Empirically, we benchmark SHD, SID, and precision-recall across Erdős–Rényi and scale-free DAGs with n=100–2000, p=20–100, and demonstrate robustness to latent confounders and distribution shift.
