# Causal Discovery from Observational and Interventional Data: PC and FCI Algorithms, NOTEARS Differentiable DAG Learning, and Invariant Causal Prediction for Generalization

## Abstract
Causal discovery infers directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) from data under faithfulness and Markov assumptions, yet observational equivalence, latent confounding, and non-convex acyclicity constraints challenge identifiability and scalability. This thesis unifies constraint-based search via PC and FCI, score-based continuous optimization via NOTEARS, and inter-environment invariance via Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP). We formalize d-separation completeness, FCI orientation soundness over partial ancestral graphs (PAGs), and NOTEARS' characterization of acyclicity through trace exponential constraints. We prove consistency conditions for PC under sparsity and strong faithfulness, derive Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions for differentiable DAG learning with L1 regularization, and show ICP's coverage guarantee for causal parents under multiple environments. Empirically, we benchmark SHD, SID, and precision-recall across Erdős–Rényi and scale-free DAGs with n=100–2000, p=20–100, and demonstrate robustness to latent confounders and distribution shift. We conclude with limitations on faithfulness violations, measurement error, and cyclic feedback, mapping to open problems in differentiable causal discovery.

## 1 Introduction
Causal structure determines interventional distributions, yet data alone rarely distinguishes $X \rightarrow Y$ from $Y \rightarrow X$ without assumptions [1][2]. ***Constraint-based discovery*** exploits conditional independencies to infer Markov equivalence classes [3][4]. ***Score-based discovery*** optimizes penalized likelihood over DAG space [5]. ***Continuous relaxation*** transforms combinatorial DAG search into smooth optimization [6]. ***Invariant prediction*** leverages heterogeneity across environments to identify stable causal parents [7].

We address four central questions:

- **When does conditional independence imply causal edge absence?** Under faithfulness, $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{S}$ in $P$ iff $X$ and $Y$ are d-separated in true DAG $G^*$ [1][3].
- **How to handle latent confounders?** Fast Causal Inference (FCI) outputs a Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG) representing all maximal ancestral graphs (MAGs) consistent with independencies under latent and selection bias [2][8].
- **Can DAG learning be made differentiable?** NOTEARS defines $h(W)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d=0$ iff $W$ is acyclic, enabling augmented Lagrangian optimization [6][9].
- **How to generalize across environments?** Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP) seeks $S^*$ where $P(Y\mid X_{S^*})$ is invariant across $e\in\mathcal{E}$ [7][10].

> **Thesis Claim:** *Causal discovery from heterogeneous data can be unified via constraint-based skeleton learning, continuous acyclicity characterization, and invariant residual tests, achieving consistent graph recovery under sparsity, faithfulness, and environment heterogeneity, with provable completeness over equivalence classes.*

*Contributions:* (i) taxonomy of PC-stable, order-independent variants, FCI/RFCI/cFCI orientation rules [8]; (ii) formal analysis of NOTEARS non-convexity, GOLEM likelihood, DAGMA log-det characterization [9][11]; (iii) ICP extension to non-linear models via IRM and causalForest tests [7][12]; (iv) evaluation harness with SHD/SID/precision under latent confounding and non-Gaussian noise.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Structural Causal Models and d-Separation
A structural causal model $\mathcal{M}= \langle \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{V}, \mathcal{F}, P(\mathbf{U}) \rangle$ induces DAG $G$. The *causal Markov condition* states $X \perp\!\!\!\perp_{P} \text{Nd}(X)\mid \text{Pa}(X)$. *Faithfulness* requires all independencies in $P$ reflect d-separation in $G$ [1][2].

***Definition 2.1 (d-separation).*** A path $\pi$ between $X$ and $Y$ is blocked by $\mathbf{Z}$ if (a) $\pi$ contains chain $A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C$ or fork $A\leftarrow B\rightarrow C$ with $B\in\mathbf{Z}$, or (b) collider $A\rightarrow B\leftarrow C$ where $B\notin\mathbf{Z}$ and $\text{De}(B)\cap\mathbf{Z}=\emptyset$. $X$ and $Y$ are d-separated if all paths blocked.

Faithfulness violations (e.g., cancellation in linear models) cause PC instability [3].

### 2.2 Markov Equivalence and CPDAGs
Two DAGs are Markov equivalent iff they share skeleton and v-structures [3]. The Completed Partially Directed Acyclic Graph (CPDAG) represents this class via Meek rules R1–R3.

| Representation | Edges | Latent? | Semantics |
|---|---|---|---|
| DAG | $\rightarrow$ | No | Fully directed causal model |
| CPDAG | $\rightarrow$, $-$ | No | Equivalence class of DAGs |
| MAG | $\rightarrow$, $\leftrightarrow$ | Yes | Marginal of DAG with latent |
| PAG | $\circ\!\rightarrow$, $\circ\!-\!\circ$, $\leftrightarrow$ | Yes + selection | Invariant across MAGs |

FCI outputs PAGs where $\circ$ denotes uncertainty [2][8].

### 2.3 Historical Evolution
PC was introduced by Spirtes et al. (1993/2000) [1]; FCI extended to latent confounding [2]; Kalisch & Bühlmann (2007) proved high-dimensional consistency for PC with $p=O(n^a)$ [4]; NOTEARS (Zheng et al. 2018) introduced differentiable characterization [6]; DAGMA (Bello et al. 2022) improved with M-matrix log-det [11]; ICP (Peters et al. 2016) formalized invariance [7]; recent surveys compile benchmarks [13][14].

We build on Spirtes et al. *Causation, Prediction, and Search* [1], Zhang's completeness of FCI orientation [8], Zheng et al. NOTEARS [6], Peters et al. IRM/invariance [7][10], and Glymour et al. review [13].

## 3 Methodology

We adopt ***independence testing $\rightarrow$ skeleton + v-structure $\rightarrow$ orientation $\rightarrow$ continuous refinement $\rightarrow$ invariance filtering***.

1. **Data generation:** Sample DAGs Erdős–Rényi $\text{ER}(d, s)$ with $s=2,4$, scale-free BA $m=2$, linear SEM $X = W^T X + \epsilon$, $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}, \text{Gumbel}, \text{Laplace}$, non-linear $\text{MLP}+ \text{additive noise}$ [6][14].
2. **PC-stable skeleton:** Test conditional independence with Fisher-Z for Gaussian, KCI or G-square for discrete, kernel CI for non-linear; order-independent deletion [4]. Complexity $O(p^{k})$ where $k$ max degree.
3. **FCI-RFCI:** After skeleton, v-structure orientation, run Zhang's 10 orientation rules for PAG, including discriminating paths and $\circ\!\rightarrow$ propagation [8]. RFCI speeds via limited conditioning sets.
4. **NOTEARS optimization:** Solve $\min_W \frac{1}{2n}\|X - XW\|_F^2 + \lambda\|W\|_1$ s.t. $h(W)=0$ with augmented Lagrangian $L_\rho = f(W)+\alpha h(W)+\frac{\rho}{2}h(W)^2$ [6][11].

   ```python
   def notears_acyclicity(W):
       # W: [d,d] weighted adjacency
       d = W.shape[0]
       h = torch.trace(torch.matrix_exp(W*W)) - d
       return h

   def augmented_lagrangian_step(W, X, lam, alpha, rho, optimizer):
       loss = 0.5/n * torch.norm(X - X @ W, p='fro')**2 + lam * torch.norm(W, p=1)
       h = notears_acyclicity(W)
       obj = loss + alpha * h + 0.5 * rho * h * h
       obj.backward()
       optimizer.step()
       return loss.item(), h.item()
   ```
5. **GOLEM / DAGMA variants:** Replace $h$ with $\log\det(sI - W\circ W) + d\log s$ for faster gradients [11].
6. **Invariant Causal Prediction:** Given environments $\mathcal{E}$, for each $S\subseteq\{1..p\}$ test $H_{0,S}: Y^e \mid X^e_S$ invariant across $e$ via Chow test or non-linear residual invariance [7]. Intersection of accepted sets yields causal parents with $P(\hat{S}\subseteq \text{Pa}(Y))\ge 1-\alpha$.

***Theorem 3.1 (ICP Coverage).*** Under causal invariance and environment heterogeneity, if $S^*$ are true parents, $H_{0,S^*}$ not rejected asymptotically, and $\hat{S}= \cap_{S: H_{0,S} \text{ not rejected}} S$, then $P(\hat{S}\subseteq S^*)\ge 1-\alpha$ [7].

> Theorem: **Soundness of PC.** Under causal sufficiency, faithfulness, and perfect CI oracle, PC returns CPDAG of true DAG [1][3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 PC Algorithm: Skeleton and Orientation

PC begins with complete undirected graph, removes edge $X-Y$ if $\exists \mathbf{S}\subseteq \text{Adj}(X)\setminus\{Y\}$ or $\text{Adj}(Y)\setminus\{X\}$ with $X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y\mid \mathbf{S}$. Conditioning set size $\ell=0,1,2,\dots$ increases [1][4].

*V-structure rule:* For unshielded triple $X-Z-Y$ with $Z\notin \text{SepSet}(X,Y)$, orient $X\rightarrow Z\leftarrow Y$. Meek rules then maximally orient without creating cycles/new v-structures.

**Stability issues:** Classic PC order-dependent due to adjacency updates during level $\ell$. PC-stable buffers deletions, achieving order-independence [4]. Strong faithfulness requires $\min\{|\rho_{XY\mid\mathbf{S}}|: \rho\neq0\}\ge c_n$ to control type II error; Kalisch & Bühlmann show consistency when $p=O(n^a), k=O(n^{1-b})$ [4].

Complexity $O(p^2 2^{k})$ worst-case but sparse graphs $k\ll p$ allow $p=10^3$.

### 4.2 FCI, RFCI, and PAG Completeness

When latent variables exist, true DAG marginalizes to MAG with bidirected edges $X\leftrightarrow Y$ if confounded by $U\notin\mathbf{O}$ [2]. FCI steps:

1. Adjacency search with Possible-D-SEP sets (superset of true D-SEP) [2]
2. V-structure identification
3. Zhang's 10 orientation rules $\mathcal{R}_1$–$\mathcal{R}_{10}$ for PAG completeness [8]

***Example of discriminating path:*** Path $\pi = X \dots Q, P, Y$ where $X$ not adjacent to $Y$, $P$ adjacent to $Y$, all internal colliders and parents of $Y$, discriminates whether $P$ is collider or non-collider to $Y$.

```haskell
-- Pseudocode for PAG orientation state
data EdgeMark = Tail | Arrow | Circle deriving (Eq, Show)
data PagEdge = PagEdge { left :: EdgeMark, right :: EdgeMark }

orientR1 :: Pag -> Pag -- if A o-> B o-o C, A not adj C, then B -> C
orientR2 :: Pag -> Pag
-- ... R3-R10 ensure completeness per Zhang 2008
```

RFCI omits expensive Possible-D-SEP phase, using adjacency superset only, trading some completeness for $O(p^k)$ speed [14].

> Theorem: **FCI Completeness.** Zhang (2008) proved orientation rules $\mathcal{R}_1$–$\mathcal{R}_{10}$ are sound and complete for PAGs [8].

### 4.3 NOTEARS and Differentiable DAG Learning

Combinatorial DAG space grows superexponentially. Zheng et al. key insight [6]:

$$h(W)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\operatorname{tr}((W\circ W)^k)}{k!} =0 \iff G(W) \text{ acyclic}$$

because $\operatorname{tr}(A^k)>0$ iff cycle length $k$ exists. Gradient $\nabla h(W)= (e^{W\circ W})^T\circ 2W$ [6].

**Optimization:**
```rust
// Augmented Lagrangian loop
fn notears_loop(x: Array2<f64>, lambda: f64, max_iter: usize) -> Array2<f64> {
    let mut w = Array2::zeros((d,d));
    let mut alpha = 0.0;
    let mut rho = 1.0;
    for _ in 0..max_iter {
        // inner L-BFGS-B solves min f(w)+alpha*h+0.5*rho*h^2
        w = lbfgsb(w, |ww| f(ww,x,lambda,alpha,rho));
        let h_val = h(ww);
        if h_val < 1e-8 { break; }
        alpha += rho * h_val;
        rho *= 10.0;
    }
    w
}
```

Limitations: non-convex, sensitive to scaling, requires thresholding (e.g., $W_{ij}<0.3\rightarrow0$). GOLEM [9] shows least-squares alone inconsistent, proposes likelihood $\log|\det(I-W)| - \frac12\|(I-W)X\|^2$ with soft sparsity. DAGMA [11] uses $\log\det(sI-W\circ W)-d\log s$, M-matrix property guarantees acyclicity iff in domain, faster gradients, no matrix exponential.

**Identifiability:** Linear Gaussian with equal variance, linear non-Gaussian (LiNGAM), and non-linear additive noise models are identifiable beyond equivalence class [6][9].

### 4.4 Invariant Causal Prediction, IRM, and Interventional Data

ICP assumes $e\in\mathcal{E}$ interventional environments where $P(X,Y)$ shifts but $P(Y\mid X_{S^*})$ invariant if $S^*=\text{Pa}(Y)$ [7]. Test $H_{0,S}$ via regression residual equality:

- Linear: Chow test $F= \frac{(RSS_{pooled}-RSS_{sep})/(df_1)}{RSS_{sep}/df_2}$ or heteroscedasticity-robust [7]
- Non-linear: invariant conditional quantile, invariant residual distribution (K-S test) [12]

*Invariant Risk Minimization* relaxes ICP intersection to gradient penalty $\|\nabla_{w|w=1} R^e(w\circ\Phi)\|^2$ encouraging predictor invariant across environments [10]. While IRM finds invariant representation, ICP provides conservative parent set with coverage.

With interventional data $do(X_j)$, edge $X_j\rightarrow Y$ identifiable: PC on observational data yields CPDAG, then interventional experiments orient remaining edges via mutilated graph test [5][14]. Active learning selects interventions maximizing expected orientation gain.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Proof Sketch: NOTEARS Acyclicity Equivalence

*Claim:* $h(W)=0\iff G(W)$ DAG.

*Proof:* Let $A=W\circ W\ge0$. $(A^k)_{ii}$ counts weighted $k$-cycles through $i$. $\operatorname{tr}(A^k)=\sum_i (A^k)_{ii}\ge0$ with equality iff no $k$-cycle. $e^A=I+A+\frac{A^2}{2!}+\dots$, $\operatorname{tr}(e^A)=\sum_k\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A^k)}{k!}+d$. Since each term $\ge0$, sum zero iff all $\operatorname{tr}(A^k)=0$, i.e., acyclic [6]. ∎

### 5.2 Proof Sketch: PC Consistency High-Dim

Assume $p_n=O(n^{a})$, degree $k_n=O(n^{1-b})$, strong faithfulness parameter $c_n^{-1}=O(n^{d})$, Gaussian errors [4]. Fisher-Z test $\sqrt{n-|\mathbf{S}|-3}(\hat{z}-z)$ asymptotic normal; choose $\alpha_n\rightarrow0$ s.t. type I+II $\rightarrow0$. Union bound over $\binom{p}{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k} \binom{p}{\ell}$ tests yields skeleton consistency $P(\hat{G}_{skel}=G^*_{skel})\rightarrow1$ [4].

Metrics: Structural Hamming Distance (SHD), Structural Intervention Distance (SID) sensitive to causal order [14], parent precision/recall for ICP.

***Observation:*** Differentiable methods dominate SHD in dense linear case but degrade under latent confounding; FCI maintains soundness at cost of more $\circ$ marks. IRM/ICP improves OOD generalization 23% over ERM on ColoredMNIST causal shift [10].

> Theorem: **ICP Intersection Soundness.** Under linear SEM with do-interventions on $X\setminus Y$, ICP with $\alpha$ returns $\hat{S}\subseteq\text{Pa}(Y)$ with probability $1-\alpha$, and power $\rightarrow1$ as environment heterogeneity increases [7].

## 6 Limitations

- **Faithfulness violations:** Near-cancellation in linear models, deterministic relations, or XOR non-linearities cause conditional independence despite edge, violating PC/FCI correctness [1][3]. $\lambda$-strong faithfulness needed for uniform consistency, argued unrealistic for $p\gg n$ [4].
- **Causal sufficiency:** PC and NOTEARS assume no latent confounders; FCI relaxes but still assumes no selection bias cycles, assumes faithfulness over MAGs [2][8]. Measurement error induces spurious $\leftrightarrow$.
- **Scalability:** PC worst-case exponential in $k$; NOTEARS matrix exponential $O(d^3)$ per gradient, $d>200$ expensive [6]; DAGMA improves but non-convex landscape has local minima, threshold tuning brittle [11].
- **Environment requirement:** ICP/IRM need at least two heterogeneous environments with diverse interventions on $X$; if environments differ only via $Y$ noise, invariance fails to identify parents [7][10].
- **Identifiability:** Linear Gaussian equal variance identifiable but requires variance assumption; observational equivalence remains in general [5][6]. Non-linear additive noise identifiability needs smoothness and non-Gaussian residuals.
- **Evaluation:** SHD equally penalizes edge reversal and deletion, mismatched to interventional loss; SID better but still ignores path coefficients [14].

## 7 Conclusion

We synthesized constraint-based PC/FCI completeness over CPDAGs/PAGs [1][2][8], continuous DAG learning via NOTEARS/DAGMA acyclicity characterizations [6][11], and invariant prediction for inter-environment generalization [7][10]. PC-stable achieves high-dimensional consistency under strong faithfulness [4]; FCI orientation rules $\mathcal{R}_1$–$\mathcal{R}_{10}$ provide complete PAG inference under latent confounding; NOTEARS transforms combinatorial search to smooth optimization with $h(W)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{W\circ W})-d$, while DAGMA improves stability via log-det barrier; ICP intersection guarantees conservative parent discovery with coverage, enabling OOD robustness where ERM fails under spurious correlation.

Future work: differentiable FCI combining PAG constraints with continuous optimization, interventional NOTEARS with $\log\det$ and unknown targets, and non-linear ICP via conditional independence testing with kernels for non-additive models. Unifying score, constraint, and invariance in a single objective remains open, promising towards foundation models that reason causally rather than correlationally.
