# The Calculus of Inductive Constructions and the Engineering of Coq: Elaboration, Universe Polymorphism, and Extraction

## Abstract

The Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC) is the dependent type theory at the heart of the Coq proof assistant, one of the most widely deployed systems for machine-checked mathematics and certified software. This thesis examines CIC as an engineered artifact: how an expressive core calculus — an impredicative sort of propositions, a cumulative predicative universe hierarchy, and primitive inductive types with dependent elimination — is made usable through three engineering mechanisms. First, *elaboration*: the bidirectional bridge between the high-level Gallina surface language, with implicit arguments, notations, and type classes, and fully explicit kernel terms. Second, *universe polymorphism*: the disciplined generalization of definitions across the hierarchy, preserving definitional transparency. Third, *extraction*: the erasure of proofs and types into executable OCaml or Haskell programs with a proved soundness argument. Drawing on the MetaCoq project [1] [2] [3] and the primary literature, we give an implementation-faithful account of the theory, its algorithms, and the metatheorems — subject reduction, confluence, principality, erasure soundness — that justify confidence in the system.

## 1. Introduction

Few software systems have borne as much intellectual weight as the Coq proof assistant. The CompCert verified C compiler, the seL4-adjacent proof developments, and the verified core of projects like ConCert smart contracts all rest on a single foundation: the kernel type checker that decides whether a purported proof term inhabits its claimed type. That kernel implements a theory — the Calculus of Inductive Constructions — whose design represents one of the deepest compromises in the history of logic between *expressive power* and *decidable checking*.

The naive dream of a dependent type theory is a single syntactic category of terms, types, propositions, and proofs, with one judgment `Γ ⊢ t : T` doing the work of both type checking and proof checking. The Calculus of Constructions (CoC), introduced by Coquand [5] and Coquand–Huet, achieves this with remarkable economy: two sorts, `Prop` and `Type`, one axiom `Prop : Type`, and four product formation rules. Yet CoC alone is impractical. Inductive definitions must be encoded via Church encodings, which are inefficient and fail to support dependent elimination. Universes must be handled explicitly, which is unbearable for users. The entire logical content of a development must be written with all types fully explicit, which no human can sustain.

> **Thesis statement:** The Calculus of Inductive Constructions succeeds as a *practical* foundation for formalized mathematics not because of any single logical innovation, but because of a carefully layered architecture: a small, decidable kernel theory (pCuIC) with proven metatheorems; an elaboration layer that reconstructs implicit information and solves universe constraints à la Hindley–Milner; universe polymorphism that generalizes definitions across the hierarchy without breaking definitional transparency; and an extraction pipeline that erases the logical content to recover executable programs with a certified computational meaning.

This thesis develops that claim. Section 2 presents the background: the pure type system of CoC, the addition of inductive types by Paulin-Mohring and Pfenning [4], and the predicative hierarchy. Section 3 describes our methodology — a synthesis of the published metatheory, the reference implementation, and the MetaCoq formalization [1] [2] [3]. Section 4 is the deep dive: elaboration with typical ambiguity and constraint solving; universe polymorphism as elaborated by Sozeau and Tabareau [6]; inductive types, their positivity conditions, and the guard checker [7]; and extraction via erasure into an untyped calculus with computational soundness. Section 5 surveys the empirical and formal results, including benchmarks and verified metatheorems. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Pure type systems and the Calculus of Constructions

The natural presentation of the Calculus of Constructions is as a *pure type system* (PTS): a triple of sorts, axioms, and product rules. In CoC, the sorts are `∗` (Prop) and `□` (Type), the axiom is `∗ : □`, and all four product rules `(s₁, s₂)` are admitted — meaning one may form dependent products whose domain and codomain live in any combination of sorts. The rules `(□, ∗)` and `(∗, □)` are the impredicative ones: the former lets one quantify over all types to form a proposition, the latter lets one form a type depending on a proposition.

The impredicativity of `Prop` is both the source of CoC's logical strength — it internalizes higher-order logic — and the source of its most delicate metatheory. Girard's paradox shows that impredicativity combined with `Type : Type` is inconsistent; Coq therefore stratifies the predicative side into a cumulative hierarchy `Type₀ ⊆ Type₁ ⊆ …` (in modern Coq, written `Type@{i}`), while `Prop` remains impredicative and proof-irrelevant.

### 2.2 From constructions to inductive constructions

In 1989–1993, Paulin-Mohring and Pfenning extended CoC with *primitive* inductive types, yielding the Calculus of Inductive Constructions [4]. The extension is not cosmetic: primitive inductives come with

1. **Constructors** as first-class term formers with declared arities;
2. **Dependent elimination** via `match` (case analysis) and `fix` (structural recursion);
3. **Positivity conditions** on inductive definitions, required for consistency;
4. A **guard condition** on fixpoints, requiring recursive calls on structurally smaller arguments, specified by Giménez and studied in Paulin-Mohring's habilitation [7].

Primitive inductives matter because Church encodings in impredicative CoC cannot express dependent elimination: the impredicative encoding of natural numbers cannot prove `0 ≠ 1` in a way that computes, nor support large elimination. Primitive `Inductive` declarations recover these, at the cost of a substantially more complex kernel.

### 2.3 The modern kernel: pCuIC

The calculus actually implemented by recent Coq kernels is the *Polymorphic Cumulative Calculus of Inductive Constructions* (pCuIC), formalized as PCUIC in the MetaCoq project [1]. Its distinguishing features:

- A **cumulative** predicative hierarchy: `Type@{i} ≤ Type@{j}` whenever `i ≤ j`, with cumulativity extending to inductive types and records through variance annotations.
- An impredicative, proof-irrelevant `Prop` (with `SProp` — strict propositions with definitional proof irrelevance — as an optional extension).
- Primitive `match` and `fix` with a syntactic guard check, plus coinductive types with `cofix`.

The kernel is deliberately small: everything else — tactics, notations, implicit arguments, modules, type classes — lives *outside* the trusted computing base and must produce kernel terms. This is where elaboration enters.

## 3. Methodology

This thesis is a *theoretical synthesis with implementation analysis*. Our method combines three strands of evidence:

1. **Primary literature analysis.** We reconstruct the theory from the foundational papers: Paulin-Mohring on inductive definitions [4], Paulin-Mohring and Werner on extraction [5], Sozeau and Tabareau on universe polymorphism [6], and the MetaCoq team's specification of pCuIC [1].
2. **Formal artifact inspection.** The MetaCoq project [1] [2] [3] provides a Coq formalization of PCUIC's syntax, typing, reduction, a verified type checker, and verified erasure. We treat these developments as the authoritative operational definition of the implemented calculus and cite their proven metatheorems (confluence, subject reduction, principality) as the empirical core of Section 5.
3. **Algorithmic reconstruction.** For elaboration and constraint solving we give the algorithms as they are implemented: bidirectional elaboration with metavariables, first-order unification with universe constraints, and the minimization/generalization discipline of universe polymorphism.

A note on scope: we do not formalize new metatheory here. Our contribution is an integrated, implementation-faithful account of how the three engineering layers — elaboration, universe polymorphism, extraction — fit together over one kernel theory, suitable as a reference for researchers entering the field.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Elaboration: from Gallina to kernel terms

The user never writes kernel terms. The Gallina surface language offers implicit arguments, coercions, notations, canonical structures, and type classes; the *elaborator* must produce fully explicit pCuIC terms. Elaboration is best understood as a constraint-generating, bidirectional type inference procedure with the following phases:

**Pretyping and metavariables.** Every omitted implicit argument becomes a metavariable (an existential variable in the unification context). The elaborator walks the pre-term in *checking* mode (given an expected type) or *inference* mode (synthesizing a type), following the bidirectional discipline that keeps unification problems small and local.

**Unification with cumulativity.** Coq's unifier is a first-order, higher-order-pattern-fragment unifier extended with universe constraints. When comparing `Type@{i}` with `Type@{j}`, it does not demand `i = j`; it records the constraint `i ≤ j`, exploiting cumulativity. This interaction between unification and subtyping is subtle: the first-order approximation rule — which short-circuits unfolding of constants with the same head — must be restricted for universe-polymorphic constants so that universe instantiation is not silently skipped [6].

**Typical ambiguity.** Following Harper and Pollack, Coq lets users write `Type` without a level annotation. The elaborator assigns each occurrence a fresh universe variable and collects constraints; if the constraint set is satisfiable, the definition is accepted at *some* level. Typical ambiguity is elaboration's quiet workhorse: it is what makes `Definition id (A : Type) (a : A) := a.` acceptable without any level arithmetic by the user.

The pipeline can be summarized as follows:

| Phase | Input | Output | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parsing | Concrete syntax | Pre-term AST | Syntax error |
| Pretyping | Pre-term + expected type | Term with metavariables | — |
| Unification | Metavariable constraints | Substitution + universe constraints | Unification failure |
| Constraint solving | Universe graph | Satisfiable / inconsistent | `Universe inconsistency` |
| Kernel check | Elaborated term | `Ok` / type error | Kernel rejection (bug if elaborator lied) |

A critical invariant, enforced by re-checking: **the kernel never trusts the elaborator**. Every elaborated term is re-type-checked by the small kernel. Elaboration bugs produce confusing error messages, not unsoundness.

> **Theorem (Elaboration soundness, informal):** If elaboration of a pre-term `t` against expected type `T` succeeds with universe constraints `ψ`, and `ψ` is satisfiable, then the produced kernel term `t'` satisfies `Γ ⊢ t' : T` in pCuIC under any valuation satisfying `ψ`.

### 4.2 Universe polymorphism: Hindley–Milner for the hierarchy

Universe polymorphism, introduced by Sozeau and Tabareau [6] and refined since, solves a concrete pain: with typical ambiguity alone, each definition is fixed at one universe level, so `list` over `Type@{i}` cannot be reused at `Type@{j}` for incompatible `i`, `j`. The fix generalizes Hindley–Milner let-polymorphism to universe levels:

1. Elaborate the body with fresh universe variables, collecting constraints `ψ`.
2. **Minimize**: solve for the least levels satisfying `ψ` that do not mention section or global variables inessential to the definition.
3. **Generalize**: abstract the remaining variables into a universe instance `@{u₁ … uₙ}` attached to the constant, storing the constraints `ψ` in the global environment.

Each use of the constant then instantiates the instance with fresh levels, checking the instantiated constraints for consistency — exactly the "definitions are transparent" principle: using a constant behaves as if its body were inlined [6].

```coq
Polymorphic Definition pidentity {A : Type} (a : A) := a.

(* Each use gets fresh levels: *)
Check (pidentity (pidentity nat 0)).
(* pidentity@{u v} ... with constraint u < v discharged by freshness *)
```

With cumulativity, application of a polymorphic constant `f` against an argument of type `Type@{j}` where `Type@{i}` was expected generates `j ≤ i` rather than `j = i`; the elaborator prefers equations over inclusions when `f`'s universes are fresh, avoiding constraint bloat [6]. Universe polymorphism is a *conservative extension* of pCIC: every polymorphic derivation erases to a monomorphic one by instantiation, so no new theorems about closed monomorphic terms become provable.

Recent work pushes further: explicit universe polymorphism in the style of Bezem, Coquand, Dybjer, and Escardó [8] internalizes level-indexed products and constraint judgments, and sort polymorphism (Poiret et al.) generalizes the idea across sorts — `Type`, `Prop`, `SProp` — addressing the duplication problem for definitions that should live at every sort.

### 4.3 Inductive types: positivity, elimination, and the guard checker

Inductive definitions are the kernel's most complex feature. A declaration `Inductive I (p : P) : A → s := c₁ : T₁ | … | cₙ : Tₙ` is accepted only if:

- **Strict positivity** holds: `I` occurs in each constructor's type only in strictly positive positions (never to the left of an arrow in a domain). This rules out definitions like `Inductive Bad := bad : (Bad → False) → Bad`, which would encode the liar paradox.
- The **universe constraints** of the arity and constructors are consistent, and the inductive lives in a level at least as large as required by its parameters (with cumulativity, inductive types carry variance annotations so that `list@{i} A ≤ list@{j} B` when appropriate).

Elimination is by `match` (with dependent return clauses, the `in`/`as`/`return` annotations of Gallina elaborating to explicit motives) and `fix`. The **guard condition** requires that every recursive call in a `fix` body be applied to a *structurally smaller* argument — a syntactic approximation of well-founded recursion. Formalizing this checker was long-standing open work; recent progress toward a verified guard checker is documented in [7], building on Giménez's codification of recursive schemes and Paulin-Mohring's correspondence between guarded fixpoints and well-founded recursion.

```coq
Inductive nat : Type := O : nat | S : nat -> nat.

Fixpoint add (n m : nat) : nat :=
  match n with
  | O => m
  | S n' => S (add n' m)   (* recursive call on n', a subterm of n: guarded *)
  end.
```

The guard checker's syntactic nature is both a strength (decidable, predictable) and a limitation: many obviously terminating programs are rejected, driving users to well-founded recursion libraries or the `Program`/`Equations` plugins — which, tellingly, elaborate *down to* guarded fixpoints, keeping the kernel small.

### 4.4 Extraction: erasing proofs, keeping programs

Extraction answers a pragmatic question: given a certified development, can we obtain an *executable program* whose behavior the proofs guarantee? The theoretical basis is due to Paulin-Mohring [5]: proofs in `Prop` are computationally irrelevant, so a translation can erase them. Letouzey's thesis extended this to full CIC, and the modern pipeline is:

1. **Erasure** (`Γ ⊢ t : T` in CIC → `t'` in `λ□`): types and proofs are replaced by a dummy constant `□`; `Prop`-sorted subterms vanish; what remains is an untyped term in a weak call-by-value lambda calculus extended with constructors and `match`.
2. **Type recovery**: because OCaml and Haskell are typed, a modified algorithm in the style of Lee and Yi re-infers ML types for the erased term; definitions whose types cannot be recovered are flagged.
3. **Pretty-printing** to OCaml, Haskell, or Scheme, with a user-extensible table mapping Coq constants to target-language primitives (e.g., `nat` to `int`).

> **Theorem (Computational soundness of erasure):** Erasure preserves observations of values: a closed well-typed term and its erasure evaluate to related values. Letouzey proved this syntactically (via operational semantics) and semantically (via realizability); the MetaCoq project has since *mechanized* erasure and its correctness proof inside Coq itself [2] [3].

The verified erasure of MetaCoq [2] is a milestone: the extraction pipeline's most subtle phase — the one whose bugs silently change program behavior — is now proved correct against the PCUIC metatheory, and composed with the CertiCoq compiler it points toward an end-to-end verified path from Gallina to assembly via CompCert.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Formal Results

We summarize the key results that turn CIC from a paper calculus into a trustworthy system.

| Result | Status | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Confluence of reduction in PCUIC | Mechanized in Coq | [1] |
| Subject reduction (type preservation) | Mechanized in Coq | [1] |
| Principality of types | Mechanized in Coq | [1] |
| Correctness + completeness of the verified type checker | Mechanized in Coq | [1] |
| Computational soundness of erasure | Mechanized in Coq | [2] [3] |
| Universe polymorphism conservativity over pCIC | Paper proof | [6] |
| Guard checker correspondence to well-founded recursion | Paper proof (habilitation) | [7] |
| Extraction to ML soundness (syntactic + realizability) | Paper proof (PhD thesis) | [5] |

Two quantitative observations are worth recording. First, the MetaCoq development comprises tens of thousands of lines of Coq formalizing exactly the calculus the OCaml kernel implements; the verified type checker can be extracted and run on real developments, differentially tested against the OCaml kernel — a rare case of a proof assistant checking its own checker. Second, the universe polymorphism elaboration was benchmarked against its predecessor on real-world developments and adopted as a drop-in replacement with favorable performance [6], demonstrating that the generalization machinery pays for itself.

The guard checker remains the least formalized kernel component: its specification exists on paper [7], and a fully verified guard checker is active ongoing work — arguably the largest remaining hole in the end-to-end verified kernel story.

## 6. Limitations

1. **The guard checker gap.** As noted, the syntactic guard condition is unverified in the kernel; all metatheorems about PCUIC in MetaCoq currently axiomatize or sidestep it. Until closed, the "verified kernel" claim carries an asterisk.
2. **Universe constraint UX.** Universe inconsistencies remain among the most cryptic errors in Coq. Minimization heuristics can pick unintuitive levels, and cumulativity's inclusion constraints occasionally over-generalize, forcing manual `@` annotations.
3. **Extraction is unverified end-to-end.** Erasure is verified [2], but type recovery, the OCaml pretty-printer, and the mapping tables are not; a bug there can still produce ill-typed or behaviorally different code. The standard extraction remains outside the fully verified pipeline.
4. **Impredicative `Prop` + classical axioms.** Developments using excluded middle or choice in `Prop` extract to code with no computational content for those axioms; extraction silently assumes constructivity, and axiom usage must be audited by hand.
5. **Performance cliffs.** Universe constraint solving is worst-case expensive, and elaboration of heavily polymorphic developments (e.g., the HoTT library) can exhibit superlinear slowdowns; typical ambiguity's freshness discipline sometimes generates far more variables than a human would.

## 7. Conclusion

The Calculus of Inductive Constructions endures because its engineering respects a single architectural insight: keep the trusted kernel minimal and push complexity into elaboration, where mistakes are caught by re-checking. Elaboration turns an unusable explicit calculus into the Gallina language mathematicians actually write; universe polymorphism turns a rigid hierarchy into a reusable one without sacrificing transparency; extraction turns proofs into programs with a proved erasure theorem behind them. The MetaCoq project [1] [2] [3] has converted much of this engineering discipline into machine-checked metatheory, and the remaining gaps — the guard checker, end-to-end extraction, cumulative universe polymorphism — are precisely demarcated. CIC is thus not merely a logic but a case study in how to build a trustworthy computational foundation: small kernel, rich elaboration, verified metatheory, and an honest account of what is not yet proved.
