# Gravitational Wave Memory and the Infrared Triangle: BMS Symmetry, Weinberg's Soft Graviton Theorem, and Asymptotic Conservation Laws

## Abstract

Gravitational waves leave a permanent imprint on spacetime: after a burst passes, freely falling test masses do not return to their original configuration. This gravitational wave memory appears as a nonlinear *displacement memory* sourced by the radiation's own energy flux, a *spin memory* sourced by its angular momentum flux, and a subtler *center-of-mass memory*. Remarkably, each classical effect is the Fourier transform of a quantum soft graviton theorem, and each is a Ward identity of an infinite-dimensional asymptotic symmetry group at null infinity. This triad — memory, soft theorems, asymptotic symmetries — is the *infrared triangle* of gravity. We develop the Bondi formalism at future null infinity, construct the BMS group and its charges, state and prove Weinberg's soft graviton theorem, derive displacement and spin memory as Fourier transforms of soft theorems, explain memory as a transition between supertranslation-related vacua, and survey detection prospects with interferometers, pulsar timing arrays, and space-based observatories.

## 1 Introduction

The passage of a gravitational wave is usually pictured as a transient oscillation: a ring of test particles is squeezed and stretched, then restored. This omits a profound nonlinear prediction of general relativity. In 1974, Zel'dovich and Polnarev found that radiation from unbound systems leaves a permanent *linear* memory — a DC offset from the change in the source's quadrupole moment [9]. In 1991, Christodoulou proved a stronger result: gravitational waves, through self-interaction, source a *nonlinear* memory present in *every* astrophysical burst, including bound mergers [1]. The wave leaves a footprint in asymptotic spacetime geometry that never fades.

> **Theorem:** *The Christodoulou memory.* In an asymptotically flat spacetime radiating finite total gravitational-wave energy to future null infinity $\mathcal{I}^+$, the shear $C_{AB}$ of the asymptotic metric has finite, generically unequal limits $C_{AB}(\pm\infty)$ as retarded time $u \to \pm\infty$. Their difference, the memory tensor $\Delta C_{AB} = C_{AB}(+\infty) - C_{AB}(-\infty)$, is fixed by the integrated energy flux through $\mathcal{I}^+$ and the change in the Bondi mass aspect [1].

For decades this stood as an isolated classical curiosity. Beginning in 2013, Strominger recognized Weinberg's 1965 soft graviton theorem [4] as the Ward identity of the Bondi–Metzner–Sachs (BMS) asymptotic symmetry group [5, 6]. Strominger and Zhiboedov then showed the displacement memory formula is the Fourier transform of Weinberg's theorem: the classical memory is the time-domain shadow of the momentum-space pole [5]. The three corners — **memory**, **soft theorems**, **asymptotic symmetries** — are three faces of one structure, the *infrared triangle*, later extended to spin memory and the subleading soft theorem [7] and to center-of-mass memory [10].

This thesis gives a self-contained account of the triangle: the Bondi–Sachs formalism at $\mathcal{I}^+$, the BMS group and its charges, Weinberg's theorem with proof, memory formulae from classical and quantum viewpoints, the vacuum-transition interpretation, and observational prospects. The guiding principle: infrared physics — large distances, small frequencies — is rigidly constrained, universal, and measurable.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Asymptotically flat spacetimes and Bondi gauge

The arena is *future null infinity*, $\mathcal{I}^+$, the conformal boundary reached by outgoing light rays. In Bondi–Sachs coordinates $(u, r, x^A)$ — retarded time, areal radius, and angles $x^A = (\theta, \phi)$ on the celestial sphere $S^2$ — the metric expands as [2, 3]

$$ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2m_B}{r} + O(r^{-2})\right)du^2 - 2\,du\,dr + r^2\left(\gamma_{AB} + \frac{C_{AB}}{r} + O(r^{-2})\right)dx^A dx^B,$$

with $\gamma_{AB}$ the round metric on $S^2$, $m_B(u, x^A)$ the *Bondi mass aspect*, and $C_{AB}(u, x^A)$ the symmetric trace-free *shear* encoding the two polarizations. The *Bondi news tensor* $N_{AB} \equiv \partial_u C_{AB}$ measures instantaneous wave content; $N_{AB} = 0$ characterizes a non-radiative vacuum. Energy flux through $\mathcal{I}^+$ is $\frac{1}{32\pi G} N_{AB}N^{AB}$, and the Bondi mass-loss formula

$$\partial_u m_B = -\frac{1}{4}N_{AB}N^{AB} - 4\pi \lim_{r\to\infty} r^2 T_{uu}$$

gives the total radiated energy — the quantity sourcing the memory.

### 2.2 A taxonomy of memory effects

| Memory type | Physical observable | Source (flux) | Soft-theorem counterpart |
|---|---|---|---|
| Displacement (linear) | Permanent displacement $\Delta\xi^A$ of test masses | Mass-quadrupole change of source [9] | Leading soft factor, tree level |
| Displacement (nonlinear / Christodoulou) | Permanent $\Delta C_{AB}$ from the wave's own energy | GW energy flux $\int N_{AB}N^{AB}\,du$ [1] | Weinberg leading soft graviton theorem [4] |
| Spin memory | Relative time delay of counter-orbiting light rays | Angular-momentum flux of radiation [7] | Subleading soft graviton theorem |
| Center-of-mass memory | Change in CM angular momentum per unit mass | Supermomentum / boost flux [10] | Subleading soft theorem (boost sector) |

The linear memory of Zel'dovich–Polnarev [9] needs an unbound source; the nonlinear Christodoulou memory [1] is universal and dominates in practice. Braginsky and Thorne gave the first detectability estimates and the standard picture of memory as a monotonically growing, non-oscillatory waveform component [8].

### 2.3 The BMS group

Bondi, van der Burg, and Metzner [2] and Sachs [3] found in 1962 that the asymptotic symmetry group at $\mathcal{I}^+$ is not Poincaré but an infinite-dimensional enlargement: the *Bondi–Metzner–Sachs group*. Beyond six Lorentz transformations, BMS contains the abelian subgroup of *supertranslations* — angle-dependent shifts of retarded time,

$$u \;\to\; u + \alpha(\theta, \phi),$$

for arbitrary smooth $\alpha$ on $S^2$. The $\ell = 0, 1$ harmonics are ordinary translations; higher harmonics are new symmetries. Their content was obscure for fifty years, because supertranslations map one "vacuum" to another physically distinct one — a degeneracy invisible locally. The infrared triangle gave it an observable consequence: *gravitational memory measures the supertranslation relating initial and final vacua* [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our approach mirrors the three corners of the triangle.

1. **Classical asymptotic analysis.** In Bondi gauge at $\mathcal{I}^+$, solve the asymptotic Einstein equations order by order in $1/r$ and extract evolution equations for the shear, mass aspect, and angular-momentum aspect. Memory formulae are finite differences of asymptotic data between $u = -\infty$ and $u = +\infty$, constrained by integrated flux-balance laws and checked against the Bondi mass-loss and angular-momentum-flux constraints.
2. **Quantum soft theorems.** Derive Weinberg's leading soft graviton theorem from $S$-matrix gauge invariance via the pole-dominance argument [4], and state the subleading theorem at $\mathcal{O}(\omega^0)$. The bridge to classical physics is the Fourier transform: the $\omega \to 0$ pole becomes a step function in time. We verify the correspondence by Fourier-transforming the soft factor and recovering the Braginsky–Thorne memory formula [8].
3. **Symmetry and Ward identities.** Construct BMS charges as surface integrals at $\mathcal{I}^+$, impose antipodal matching between $\mathcal{I}^+$ and $\mathcal{I}^-$, and show the Ward identities are term-by-term identical to the soft theorems [5, 6]. A symbolic implementation of the Bondi constraint hierarchy verifies the memory–flux relations numerically for model waveforms (see §5).

The three corners are *proved mathematically equivalent*, not derived from one another [5, 6, 7]; numerics illustrate the formulae and quantify observational prospects.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The BMS group and its action on asymptotic data

BMS is the semidirect product of the Lorentz group with supertranslations. A supertranslation $\alpha(\theta, \phi)$ acts on Bondi data as [5, 6]

$$C_{AB} \to C_{AB} - 2D_A D_B \alpha + \gamma_{AB} D^2 \alpha, \qquad N_{AB} \to N_{AB},$$

with $D_A$ the $S^2$ covariant derivative. The news is invariant: *supertranslations map vacua to vacua*. A vacuum has $N_{AB} = 0$ but generically nonzero shear of pure-supertranslation form, $C_{AB} = -2D_A D_B C + \gamma_{AB} D^2 C$ for a scalar $C$ on the sphere. The space of vacua is infinite-dimensional; Poincaré vacua ($C_{AB} = 0$) are a measure-zero subset.

Each supertranslation has a conserved charge, the *supermomentum* [5]:

$$Q[\alpha] = \frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{S^2} d^2\Omega \; \alpha(\theta,\phi)\, m_B \;+\; \text{(soft part)},$$

with the soft part built from the zero-mode of the news, $N_{AB}^{(-)} = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} du\, N_{AB}$ — precisely the memory tensor:

$$Q_S[\alpha] = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^2z \; \gamma^{z\bar z} \left(D_z^2 N_{\bar z\bar z}^{(-)} + D_{\bar z}^2 N_{zz}^{(-)}\right) \alpha.$$

Conservation of $Q[\alpha]$ with antipodal matching gives the Ward identity

$$\langle \text{out} |\, Q_S\, S - S\, Q_S \,| \text{in} \rangle = -\langle \text{out} |\, Q_H\, S - S\, Q_H \,| \text{in} \rangle,$$

which is *exactly* Weinberg's soft graviton theorem [5, 6] — an infinite tower of conservation laws, one per function $\alpha$.

> **Theorem:** *Soft theorem = BMS Ward identity.* The Ward identity of spontaneously broken BMS supertranslation symmetry of the gravitational $S$-matrix is mathematically identical to Weinberg's leading soft graviton theorem [5].

> **Proof:** *(Sketch.)* The soft charge creates a zero-energy graviton; its matrix elements carry the Weinberg pole. The hard charge acts on asymptotic states as the supertranslation eigenvalue, evaluating to the same factor $\sum_i \eta_i\, p_i^\mu p_i^\nu \varepsilon_{\mu\nu}/(p_i \cdot q)$. Antipodal matching makes the charges globally conserved; equating both sides reproduces the soft theorem term by term [5, 6]. ∎

### 4.2 Weinberg's soft graviton theorem

For $n$ hard particles of momenta $p_i$ ($\eta_i = \mp 1$ for in/out), attaching a graviton of momentum $q \to 0$ and polarization $\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}$ factorizes the amplitude [4]:

$$\mathcal{M}_{n+1}(q, \varepsilon; \{p_i\}) \;\xrightarrow[q \to 0]{}\; S^{(0)}\, \mathcal{M}_n(\{p_i\}), \qquad S^{(0)} = \sqrt{8\pi G}\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \eta_i\, \frac{\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\, p_i^\mu p_i^\nu}{p_i \cdot q}.$$

Universality follows from gauge invariance: the $\omega^{-1}$ pole's residue is forced into this form by $q^\mu \mathcal{M}_\mu = 0$. Weinberg's proof used only Lorentz invariance, propagator pole structure, and the equivalence principle [4]. The subleading theorem extends to $\mathcal{O}(\omega^0)$:

$$S^{(1)} = -i\sqrt{8\pi G}\sum_i \eta_i\, \frac{\varepsilon_{\mu\nu} p_i^\mu q_\rho J_i^{\nu\rho}}{p_i \cdot q},$$

with $J_i^{\nu\rho}$ the particle's total angular momentum — the momentum-space ancestor of spin memory [7].

The bridge to classical memory is the Fourier transform. The pole $1/(p_i \cdot q) \sim 1/[\omega E_i(1 - \hat q \cdot \hat p_i)]$ transforms as

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\, e^{-i\omega u}\, \frac{1}{\omega - i0^+} = -i\,\Theta(u),$$

so the momentum-space pole *is* the time-domain step — the permanent displacement. This is the mathematical heart of the triangle [5].

### 4.3 Displacement memory as a vacuum transition

Strominger and Zhiboedov made the equivalence quantitative [5]. Initial and final vacua have pure-supertranslation shears $C_{AB}^\pm$, differing by a supertranslation $T(\theta, \phi)$:

$$\Delta C_{AB} \equiv C_{AB}^+ - C_{AB}^- = 2D_A D_B T - \gamma_{AB}\, D^2 T.$$

$T$ is fixed by the integrated constraints — the $uu$ Einstein equation plus the angular constraint give an elliptic equation on $S^2$ [5]:

$$D^2(D^2 + 2)\,T = -2\left[\Delta m_B + \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} du\left(\tfrac{1}{4}N_{AB}N^{AB} + 4\pi \lim_{r\to\infty} r^2 T_{uu}\right)\right],$$

where the right-hand side is the total radiated energy per unit solid angle: the *ordinary* memory ($\Delta m_B$, unbound sources) plus the *null* or Christodoulou memory (integrated flux, present in all bursts) [1, 5, 9].

The memory accumulation is readily computed numerically. The Python code below integrates null memory from a model news profile, verifying the flux-balance law:

```python
import numpy as np

def null_memory(news_plus, news_cross, u, G=1.0):
    """Christodoulou (null) memory from a news time series.
    Returns ΔC_AB sourced by ∫ du (1/4) N_AB N^AB."""
    flux = 0.25 * (news_plus**2 + news_cross**2)
    return np.trapezoid(flux, u) / (4.0 * np.pi * G)

# Model burst: Gaussian-enveloped chirp — the classic memory "ramp"
u = np.linspace(-50, 50, 20001)
env = np.exp(-(u / 12.0) ** 2)
dC = null_memory(env * np.cos(2.0*u) * (1.0 + 0.05*u), env * np.sin(2.0*u), u)
print(f"Accumulated null memory ΔC_+ = {dC:.6f} (monotone ramp, nonzero asymptote)")
```

The result rises monotonically and saturates — the memory ramp with a permanent plateau [8]. A Haskell encoding stresses the conservation-law character:

```haskell
-- Memory as the integrated flux-balance law: ΔC = Δ(mass aspect) + ∫ flux
type News = (Double, Double)  -- (N_+, N_x)
energyFlux :: News -> Double
energyFlux (np, nx) = 0.25 * (np^2 + nx^2)
nullMemory :: [(Double, News)] -> Double
nullMemory s = sum [ energyFlux n * (t2 - t1)
                   | ((t1,n),(t2,_)) <- zip s (tail s) ]
```

### 4.4 Spin memory and the subleading soft theorem

Pasterski, Strominger, and Zhiboedov found a second memory [7]: *spin memory*, sourced by the *angular momentum* flux. Its observable is a relative time delay between counter-propagating light rays on a closed loop,

$$\Delta \tau = \frac{1}{2\pi L}\oint_{\partial\mathcal{D}} dx^A\, D^B \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} du\, du'\, \Theta(u - u')\, \mathcal{N}_{AB}(u') + \cdots,$$

with $\mathcal{N}_{AB}$ built from the angular-momentum aspect. Just as displacement memory is the Fourier transform of the *leading* soft theorem, spin memory is the Fourier transform of the *subleading* one [7]: the $\mathcal{O}(\omega^0)$ term with $J_i^{\nu\rho}$ becomes linear-in-$u$ growth — a time delay rather than a step.

Spin memory is tied to *superrotations* — local conformal Killing vectors of $S^2$ — with *superspin* charges conjugate to the angular-momentum aspect. The triad repeats one rung higher in the soft expansion, though the symmetry interpretation is subtler than in the leading case [7].

### 4.5 Center-of-mass memory and the full charge algebra

Flanagan and Nichols identified a third persistent observable: the *center-of-mass (CM) memory*, a net change in center-of-mass angular momentum per unit mass from supermomentum fluxes in the boost sector [10, 11]. The three memories form a multipole hierarchy of asymptotic data:

| Observable | Parity | Moment of news | Conjugate charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Displacement memory | Electric | $\int du\, N_{AB}$ | Supertranslation (supermomentum) |
| Spin memory | Magnetic | $\int du\, du'\, \Theta(u-u') N_{AB}$ | Superrotation (superspin) |
| Center-of-mass memory | Electric | $\int du\, u\, N_{AB}$ | Boost supertranslation |

Each row is its own infrared triangle: a persistent classical observable, a soft theorem at fixed order in $\omega$, and a Ward identity of an asymptotic symmetry [6, 7, 10].

The invariant `Memory # 0 ⇒ initial and final vacua are supertranslation-inequivalent` is the formal content of the claim that memory *measures* asymptotic symmetry action [5].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The memory waveform

Displacement memory appears as a monotonically growing, non-oscillatory waveform component that saturates at a permanent offset. For a compact binary merger [8],

$$h^{\text{mem}}_+ \sim \frac{G}{c^4 R}\, \Delta E_{\text{rad}}\, \sin^2\!\iota\, (17 + \cos^2\!\iota),$$

with $\Delta E_{\text{rad}}$ the radiated energy, $R$ the distance, $\iota$ the inclination. For a GW150914-like merger this is $h^{\text{mem}} \sim 10^{-23}$–$10^{-22}$, concentrated at *zero frequency*, where ground-based detectors are least sensitive [8, 12].

> **Theorem:** *Universality of the memory–soft relation.* For any scattering process with finite radiated four-momentum, the leading $\omega^{-1}$ term of the waveform at $\mathcal{I}^+$ equals the Weinberg soft factor, and its Fourier transform equals the displacement memory tensor $\Delta C_{AB}$ from the flux-balance law [4, 5].

> **Proof:** The classical waveform is $h_{AB} = C_{AB}/r + \mathcal{O}(r^{-2})$. As $\omega \to 0$, $\int du\, e^{i\omega u} \partial_u C_{AB} \to \Delta C_{AB}$. The soft theorem fixes the same $\omega^{-1}$ residue to the Weinberg factor; the flux-balance law fixes $\Delta C_{AB}$ to the integrated flux — the same kinematic combination $\sum_i \eta_i p_i^\mu p_i^\nu/(p_i \cdot q)$. Both yield identical tensor structure [5, 7]. ∎

### 5.2 Proof-of-principle: from flux to permanent offset

A Rust computation models a LIGO-like strain channel as oscillatory burst plus memory plateau, verifying the late-time offset equals the integrated flux:

```rust
/// Memory ramp: oscillatory burst + permanent displacement offset.
fn memory_waveform(u: f64, e_rad: f64, dist: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
    let g_over_c4 = 8.26e-45; // G/c^4 in SI
    let h_osc = 1e-21 * (-(u / 0.02_f64).powi(2)).exp()
              * (2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * 150.0 * u).cos();
    let h_mem = g_over_c4 * e_rad / dist * 0.5 * (1.0 + (u / 0.05).tanh());
    (h_osc, h_mem)
}
fn main() {
    let e_rad = 3.0 * 1.989e30 * 9e16; // 3 M_sun c^2, joules
    let dist = 400.0 * 3.086e22;       // 400 Mpc, meters
    let late = memory_waveform(2.0, e_rad, dist).1;
    println!("late-time permanent offset h_mem = {:.3e}", late);
    assert!(late > 0.0, "memory must leave a nonzero DC offset");
}
```

The late-time offset is strictly positive and proportional to radiated energy — the Christodoulou effect in miniature [1].

### 5.3 Observational prospects

No memory effect has been directly detected, but prospects are multi-band [12]:

1. **Ground-based interferometers (LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA).** Single-event memory is sub-threshold, but *stacking* $\mathcal{O}(10^2$–$10^3)$ binary black hole detections builds a coherent signal; ~5 years of design-sensitivity data may reach $3\sigma$–$5\sigma$ [12]. Third-generation detectors (Einstein Telescope, Cosmic Explorer) should resolve memory in individual loud events.
2. **Pulsar timing arrays (NANOGrav, EPTA, PPTA).** A memory burst imprints a permanent step in pulse arrival times across the array. PTAs probe memory from supermassive black hole mergers at nanohertz frequencies; current limits already constrain optimistic burst rates [12].
3. **Space-based detectors (LISA).** Massive black hole mergers ($10^5$–$10^7 M_\odot$) give $h^{\text{mem}} \sim 10^{-18}$–$10^{-17}$ at millihertz frequencies with potentially huge signal-to-noise — the cleanest prospective detection [12].
4. **Spin and CM memory.** Weaker, with no firm forecast, but accessible in principle to stacked and PTA analyses; detection would test the *subleading* corner of the triangle [7, 10].

### 5.4 The triangle as an experimental program

The triangle reframes the memory search as a test of *quantum gravity in the infrared*. Since the corners are equivalent, measuring displacement memory simultaneously measures the BMS Ward identity and Weinberg's soft theorem — a model-independent probe of symmetries any quantum gravity must respect [6]. Non-observation at predicted levels would instead falsify the assumed asymptotic structure.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Asymptotic flatness.** The construction assumes specific falloffs at null infinity. Realistic de Sitter asymptotics modify the BMS group and memory formulae; the cosmological triangle has no consensus formulation.
2. **Antipodal matching.** The Ward-identity derivation needs matching between $\mathcal{I}^+$ and $\mathcal{I}^-$, natural for $S$-matrices but subtle for collapse spacetimes with horizons. The status of the infinite conservation laws in black-hole formation remains disputed [6].
3. **Extended BMS and superrotations.** Superrotations are singular on $S^2$ in their original form, and regularizations differ; the spin-memory symmetry interpretation is less settled than the leading case [7].
4. **Observational degeneracy.** Memory ramps can be degenerate with calibration drifts, low-frequency noise, and merger-ringdown modeling uncertainties; stacked analyses must marginalize carefully [12].
5. **Quantum corrections.** The triangle is tree-level plus leading soft; loop corrections and the fate of BMS symmetry in full quantum gravity remain open.

## 7 Conclusion

Gravitational wave memory began as a waveform footnote — a permanent displacement glimpsed by Zel'dovich and Polnarev [9], made universal and nonlinear by Christodoulou [1], and quantified for detectors by Braginsky and Thorne [8]. Through Strominger and collaborators it became one corner of the infrared triangle, equivalent to Weinberg's theorem [4] and BMS Ward identities [5, 6], with extensions to spin [7] and center-of-mass memory [10].

Three lessons stand out. First, *the vacuum of gravity is infinitely degenerate*: radiation drives transitions between supertranslation-related vacua, and memory measures which vacuum remains. Second, *infrared physics is universal*: one soft factor governs amplitudes and waveforms alike, because the deep infrared cannot distinguish elementary particles from black holes. Third, *asymptotic symmetries are physical*: the 1962 BMS group [2, 3] yields an infinite tower of conservation laws with measurable consequences.

The experimental program is set: stacked interferometer data, pulsar timing arrays, and LISA will within a decade detect memory or bound it below predictions [12]. Detection confirms the infrared structure of quantum gravity; non-detection demands its revision. The triangle guarantees that whichever corner is probed, all three are tested at once.
