# Perfectoid Spaces and the Pro-étale Topology: Tilting, Almost Purity, and Prismatic Cohomology

## Abstract

Perfectoid spaces, introduced by Peter Scholze in 2012 [1], first let the characteristic zero and characteristic *p* worlds of *p*-adic arithmetic geometry coexist functorially. The tilting equivalence translates perfectoid spaces over a mixed-characteristic perfectoid field into perfectoid spaces of characteristic *p*, identifying their étale sites; Faltings' almost purity theorem [6], recast in the perfectoid framework, governs the ramification of integral models along towers of finite étale covers; and the pro-étale topology [2] supplies a universal affinoid perfectoid cover on which period sheaves linearize *p*-adic Hodge theory. This article develops the theory from the Fontaine–Wintenberger field-of-norms equivalence through adic spectra, almost ring theory, and the construction of the pro-étale site, culminating in the prismatic cohomology of Bhatt and Scholze [4], in which de Rham, crystalline, and étale cohomology emerge as specializations of one integral theory built from δ-rings.

##1 Intro

Arithmetic geometry over *p*-adic fields suffers from a fundamental asymmetry. The residue field of a *p*-adic field has characteristic *p*, and much of the geometry of varieties over the field is controlled by this residue characteristic — yet the field itself has characteristic zero, and classical tools of characteristic *p* geometry, above all the Frobenius endomorphism, have no direct analogue. The classical response was to work integrally: study models over the ring of integers, where the special fiber carries Frobenius, and fight to control degeneration across it.

The theory of **perfectoid spaces** proposes the opposite response [1]. Rather than avoiding the perfectness that makes characteristic *p* geometry tractable, one *perfects* the characteristic zero object itself: one passes to infinite towers in which Frobenius becomes surjective, and one discovers that the resulting objects admit a canonical avatar in characteristic *p*, the **tilt**, which remembers essentially everything of geometric interest. The tilt is not a reduction modulo *p*; it is a second incarnation of the same space in a different characteristic, joined to the original by an equivalence of étale sites strong enough to transport Galois-theoretic questions across the divide.

This article is a systematic exposition of that circle of ideas and its two great refinements. First, the **pro-étale topology** [2]: by allowing covers that are cofiltered inverse limits of étale maps, one obtains a site on which every rigid-analytic variety is locally perfectoid, so that the period sheaves of *p*-adic Hodge theory become honest sheaves with computable cohomology. Second, **prismatic cohomology** [4]: by axiomatizing the algebraic structure carried by the universal perfectoid cover — a δ-ring together with a distinguished ideal, a *prism* — Bhatt and Scholze constructed a single cohomology theory specializing to de Rham, crystalline, and étale cohomology, with integral coefficients and a built-in Frobenius action.

##2 Background

### From the field of norms to perfectoid fields

The seed of the theory is the theorem of Fontaine and Wintenberger on the *field of norms*. Let *K* be a finite extension of **Q**ₚ and let *K*∞ be the extension obtained by adjoining all *p*-power roots of unity. Fontaine and Wintenberger showed that the absolute Galois group of *K*∞ is canonically isomorphic to the absolute Galois group of a certain field of characteristic *p*, the field of norms *X*ₖ(*K*∞), a complete discretely valued field of characteristic *p* with residue field a finite extension of **F**ₚ. In modern language, the perfectoid field **Q**ₚ(ζₚ∞)∧ tilts to the perfectoid field **F**ₚ((t¹/ᵖ∞))∧, and the isomorphism of Galois groups is the zero-dimensional case of the tilting equivalence [1, Theorem 7.9].

Scholze's insight was that this phenomenon is not special to fields. The correct generality requires two ingredients that were already available: **adic spaces**, introduced by Huber in the 1990s as a refinement of rigid-analytic geometry that remembers the topology of the ring, and **almost ring theory**, developed by Faltings [6] and systematized by Gabber and Ramero [7] to handle the inevitable torsion phenomena that appear when one works with integral models.

### Almost ring theory in brief

Let *V* be a valuation ring with maximal ideal **m** satisfying **m**² = **m** — the idempotence holds for the maximal ideal of any nondiscretely valued field. A *V*-module *M* is **almost zero** if **m***M* = 0. The quotient of the category of *V*-modules by the Serre subcategory of almost zero modules is the category of **almost modules**; morphisms, flatness, étaleness, and finite presentation all have almost analogues obtained by demanding the usual property only up to multiplication by elements of **m**. The point is that statements which fail integrally by a uniformly bounded *p*-power torsion error become true in the almost category — and the error is controlled by the ramification, which is precisely what the perfectoid condition makes arbitrarily divisible [7].

### Adic spectra

A **Huber pair** (*A*, *A*⁺) consists of a topological ring *A* with an open subring *A*₀ carrying the *I*-adic topology for a finitely generated ideal *I*, and an integrally closed open subring *A*⁺ ⊆ *A*°. Its adic spectrum Spa(*A*, *A*⁺) is the space of continuous valuations. Perfectoid geometry lives inside this category: an **affinoid perfectoid** space is Spa(*R*, *R*⁺) for a perfectoid Huber pair, and a general perfectoid space is obtained by gluing such spectra [1], [5].

##3 Methodology

This article proceeds in four movements, each building on the previous:

1. **Ring level.** We define perfectoid fields and perfectoid rings, construct the tilt functor *R* ↦ *R*♭, and state the tilting equivalence as an equivalence of categories of perfectoid spaces together with an identification of étale sites.
2. **Almost level.** We explain how almost ring theory converts the tilting equivalence into Faltings' almost purity theorem: finite étale covers of the generic fiber extend to *almost* finite étale covers of integral models.
3. **Sheaf level.** We introduce the pro-étale site, show that affinoid perfectoids form a basis, and describe the period sheaves — **A**inf, **B**dR⁺ — that make the de Rham comparison isomorphism a computation on this site.
4. **Prismatic synthesis.** We axiomatize the structure present on the universal cover via δ-rings and prisms, and explain how prismatic cohomology unifies the classical *p*-adic cohomology theories.

Notation is standard: *p* is a fixed prime, *K* a perfectoid field, *K*♭ its tilt, *W*(−) the *p*-typical Witt vectors, φ the Frobenius lift. The following table fixes the principal actors.

| Object | Lives over | Role |
|---|---|---|
| *K*, perfectoid field | char 0 or *p* | base field; admits tilt *K*♭ |
| (*R*, *R*⁺), perfectoid Huber pair | *K* | affine building block |
| Spa(*R*, *R*⁺) | adic | affinoid perfectoid space |
| *X*♭, tilt of *X* | *K*♭ | characteristic *p* incarnation |
| (*A*, *I*), prism | δ-ring *A* | input datum for prismatic cohomology |
| *X*♦, diamond of *X* | pro-étale sheaf | untilts parametrized functorially |

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Perfectoid rings and the tilt functor

> **Definition.** A **perfectoid field** is a complete valued field *K* of rank one whose value group is dense in the nonnegative reals, whose residue field has characteristic *p*, and for which the Frobenius map on **O**ₖ/*p* is surjective. A **perfectoid ring** is a complete Tate ring *R* with a ring of integral elements *R*° such that the Frobenius on *R*°/*p* is surjective and *R* is *π*-adically complete for a pseudo-uniformizer π with πᵖ dividing *p* in *R*° [1].

Dense value group makes ramification infinitely *p*-divisible, which is what lets almost mathematics work; Frobenius surjectivity modulo *p* is the perfectness imported from characteristic *p*.

Given a perfectoid ring *R*, its **tilt** is the inverse limit perfection

*R*♭ = lim←ₓ↦ₓₚ *R*,

with componentwise multiplication and with addition defined by the limit formula familiar from Witt vectors: the sum of two sequences (*x*ₙ) and (*y*ₙ) is the sequence whose *n*-th term is the limit as *m* → ∞ of (*x*ₙ₊ₘ + *y*ₙ₊ₘ)ᵖᵐ. The ring *R*♭ is a perfectoid ring of characteristic *p*, and the construction is functorial. There is a canonical multiplicative **sharp map** # : *R*♭ → *R* sending (*x*ₙ) to the limit of lifts of *x*ₙᵖⁿ, which induces an isomorphism *R*♭⁺/*ϖ* ≅ *R*⁺/*p* for suitable pseudo-uniformizers — the precise sense in which the tilt remembers the reduction modulo *p* of the integral model.

> **Theorem (Tilting equivalence).** *Let K be a perfectoid field with tilt K♭. The functor X ↦ X♭ is an equivalence between the category of perfectoid spaces over K and the category of perfectoid spaces over K♭. It induces a homeomorphism |X| ≅ |X♭| identifying rational subsets, and an equivalence of étale sites X*ét *≅ X♭*ét. In particular, for affinoid perfectoid Spa(R, R⁺), finite étale covers correspond bijectively across the tilt [1, Theorem 7.9].*

The following toy implementation illustrates the inverse-limit mechanics of the tilt at the level of sequences; it is a combinatorial shadow of the true construction, which requires *p*-adic completeness to make the limits converge.

```python
def tilt_sharp_map(seq, p=5, lift=lambda x: x, prec=6):
    """Toy model of the sharp map R-flat -> R.
    seq: list [x_0, ..., x_n] with x_{k+1}^p == x_k (mod p), as integers mod p**prec.
    Returns the 'untilt' x_0 lifted coherently: lim x_k^{p^k}.
    """
    mod = p ** prec
    x = seq[-1] % mod
    # walk back down the tower, raising to p-th powers
    for x_next in reversed(seq[:-1]):
        # coherence check: x^p should agree with the recorded predecessor mod p
        assert pow(x, p, p) == x_next % p, "tower not p-coherent"
        x = pow(x, p, mod)
    return lift(x) % mod

# Example: a coherent tower in the 5-adics approximating a tilt element
tower = [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2]  # constant tower: 2^5 = 32 == 2 mod 5
print(tilt_sharp_map(tower))  # coherent 5-power tower -> sharp image mod 5^6
```

### 4.2 Almost mathematics and the almost purity theorem

The tilting equivalence is a statement about generic fibers — about *R* itself. Arithmetic, however, happens integrally: one wants to control *R*° and its finite extensions. Here almost ring theory enters. The key observation is that in a perfectoid ring, the maximal ideal **m** of topologically nilpotent elements satisfies **m**² = **m**, so the almost category is available, and the deep ramification makes the almost error arbitrarily small.

> **Theorem (Almost purity).** *Let R be a perfectoid Tate ring and let R → S be a morphism with R[1/p] → S[1/p] finite étale. Then R° → S° is almost finite étale: S° is an almost finitely generated almost projective R°-module, and the trace map is an almost isomorphism. In particular, any finite étale cover of the generic fiber extends, up to uniformly bounded p-power torsion, to the integral model [1, Theorem 7.19]; [6].*

The proof tilts to characteristic *p*, where a finite étale algebra over a perfectoid ring is itself perfectoid and the claim follows from Frobenius perfectness; tilting back transports the result. Faltings' original proof [6] used direct ramification estimates; the perfectoid proof replaces analysis with functoriality. Bhatt and Scholze later gave a still more robust version allowing ramification along arbitrary closed subsets, proved entirely within the prismatic framework without recourse to adic spaces [4, §10].

### 4.3 The pro-étale topology and period sheaves

The étale site of a rigid-analytic variety is too coarse for *p*-adic Hodge theory: the period rings one needs — **A**inf, **B**cris, **B**dR — are not sheaves for it. Scholze's solution in [2] is the **pro-étale site** *X*proét, whose covers are generated by morphisms that are cofiltered inverse limits of étale maps. The crucial geometric fact is that affinoid perfectoid spaces form a basis: every rigid-analytic variety admits a pro-étale cover by affinoid perfectoids. The standard example is the *perfectoid torus cover*: over the torus **T**ⁿ = Spa(*K*⟨*T*₁^±¹, …, *T*ₙ^±¹⟩), the tower adjoining all *p*-power roots of the coordinates,

**T**ⁿ∞ = Spa(*K*⟨*T*₁^±¹/ᵖ∞, …, *T*ₙ^±¹/ᵖ∞⟩),

is affinoid perfectoid and pro-étale over **T**ⁿ, with Galois group **Z**ₚ(1)ⁿ.

On *X*proét one defines the integral structure sheaves **Ô**⁺ ⊆ **Ô** and the period sheaves. For an affinoid perfectoid *U* = Spa(*R*, *R*⁺), one sets **A**inf(*U*) = *W*(*R*♭⁺), the Witt vectors of the tilt of the integral model, and **B**dR⁺(*U*) as the ker(θ)-adic completion, where θ : **A**inf → **Ô** is Fontaine's map. These are sheaves precisely because perfectoids are *w*-contractible for the pro-étale topology: their higher pro-étale cohomology with suitable coefficients vanishes [2], [5].

The payoff is the **de Rham comparison isomorphism** for rigid-analytic varieties: for *X* proper smooth over a *p*-adic field *k* and **L** a lisse **Z**ₚ-sheaf, there is a Galois-equivariant isomorphism

*H*ⁱ(*X*k̄, **L**) ⊗ **B**dR⁺ ≅ *H*ⁱ(*X*k̄, **L** ⊗ **B**dR⁺),

and the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence *H*ⁱ(*X*, Ωʲ) ⇒ *H*dRⁱ⁺ʲ(*X*) degenerates — with no Kähler hypothesis, a genuinely *p*-adic phenomenon [2, Theorem 1.5]. The formalism extends to diamonds: the functor *X* ↦ *X*♦, sending an adic space to the pro-étale sheaf of its untilts over perfectoid test objects, embeds rigid geometry into a category where quotients by pro-étale equivalence relations exist [5].

### 4.4 Prisms and prismatic cohomology

The constructions above all pass through a universal perfectoid cover and then descend. Bhatt and Scholze asked what algebraic structure the cover itself carries, and axiomatized the answer [4]. A **δ-ring** is a commutative ring *A* with a map δ : *A* → *A* such that φ(*x*) = *x*ᵖ + *p*δ(*x*) is a ring endomorphism lifting Frobenius — the exact structure carried by *p*-typical Witt vectors. In Haskell-flavored pseudocode, the structure is a single typeclass:

```haskell
-- A delta-ring: a commutative ring with a Frobenius lift encoded via delta.
class CommRing a => DeltaRing a where
  delta :: a -> a
  -- The Frobenius lift is derived: phi x = x^p + p * delta x
  phi :: a -> a
  phi x = x ^ p + fromInteger p * delta x
    where p = charPrime (Proxy :: Proxy a)

-- Laws: delta 0 = delta 1 = 0, and delta respects sums and products so that
-- phi is a genuine ring homomorphism lifting Frobenius mod p.
```

> **Definition.** A **prism** is a pair (*A*, *I*) where *A* is a δ-ring, *I* ⊆ *A* is an invertible ideal with *A* derived (*p*, *I*)-complete, and *p* ∈ *I* + φ(*I*)*A* [4, Definition 1.1]. The condition on *p* is a transversality: it says the prism is a "thickening" in a direction transverse to Frobenius.

Examples: the **crystalline prism** (**Z**ₚ, (*p*)); the **perfect prism** (**A**inf(*R*), ker θ) attached to a perfectoid ring *R*, where ker θ is generated by a distinguished element ξ; the **Breuil–Kisin prism** (*W*(*k*)⟦*u*⟧, (*E*(*u*))) for an Eisenstein polynomial *E*; and the **q-de Rham prism** (**Z**ₚ⟦*q*−1⟧, ([*p*]q)) with *q*-integer [*p*]q = (*q*ᵖ − 1)/(*q* − 1).

Given a bounded prism (*A*, *I*) and a smooth *p*-adic formal scheme **X** over *A*/*I*, the **prismatic site** (**X**/*A*)Δ has objects prisms (*B*, *IB*) over (*A*, *I*) with a map Spf(*B*/*IB*) → **X**; its structure sheaf cohomology *R*ΓΔ(**X**/*A*) = *R*Γ((**X**/*A*)Δ, **O**Δ) is the **prismatic cohomology**. It carries a Frobenius endomorphism φ and a Nygaard filtration, and it specializes as follows [4]:

| Prism (*A*, *I*) | Specialization | Recovers |
|---|---|---|
| (**Z**ₚ, (*p*)) crystalline | *R*ΓΔ ⊗ᴸ *A*/*I* | crystalline cohomology |
| (**A**inf, ker θ) perfect | *R*ΓΔ ⊗ᴸ *A*/φ(*I*) | de Rham cohomology |
| (**A**inf, ker θ) perfect | (φ-invariants after inverting *I*) | *p*-adic étale cohomology |
| (**Z**ₚ⟦*q*−1⟧, ([*p*]q)) *q*-de Rham | *q* = 1 fiber | *q*-de Rham cohomology |

The comparisons are isomorphisms of the expected shape: the **Hodge–Tate comparison** identifies the cohomology of *R*ΓΔ/*I* with differential forms, *H*ⁱ(*R*ΓΔ/*I*) ≅ Ωⁱ{−*i*}; the **étale comparison** identifies the Frobenius-fixed part after inverting the ideal with *p*-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber; the **de Rham** and **crystalline comparisons** identify the evident specializations with de Rham and crystalline cohomology respectively [4, §§5–9]. Prismatic cohomology is thus the long-sought integral cohomology theory unifying the *p*-adic realizations — a "motivic" cohomology for *p*-adic formal schemes, with the prism playing the role of the coefficient ring.

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We sketch the three load-bearing arguments. Full details are in [1], [2], and [4].

> **Theorem (Tilting preserves finite étale covers).** *Let R be perfectoid with tilt R♭. The functor S ↦ S♭ is an equivalence between finite étale R-algebras and finite étale R♭-algebras.*

*Proof sketch.* Reduce to the case where *R* is a perfectoid field *K*. A finite étale *K*-algebra is a finite product of finite separable field extensions. By the structure theory of valued fields, it suffices to treat finite Galois extensions *L*/*K*. The tilt *L*♭ is constructed as the completed perfection of the inverse limit, and one checks that [*L*♭ : *K*♭] = [*L* : *K*] by comparing ramification filtrations: the value groups satisfy Γ_L♭ = Γ_L and the residue fields satisfy *k*_{L♭} = *k*_L^{perf}, and a henselian valued field is determined up to isomorphism by its value group and residue field together with the extension degree. Galois-equivariance is formal, giving the categorical equivalence. ∎

> **Theorem (Almost purity, perfectoid form).** *Let R be perfectoid and R[1/p] → S[1/p] finite étale. Then R° → S° is almost finite étale.*

*Proof sketch.* By the previous theorem, tilt to characteristic *p*: *R*♭ → *S*♭ is finite étale of perfectoid rings, hence *S*♭° is finite étale over *R*♭°, in particular finite projective. Untilting a finite projective module is exact up to the kernel of θ, which is generated by a distinguished element ξ; the obstruction to descending finite étaleness is therefore annihilated by powers of the maximal ideal **m**, i.e., vanishes in the almost category. The trace pairing, which witnesses étaleness in characteristic *p* via the perfectness of Frobenius, untilts to an almost perfect pairing. ∎

> **Theorem (Hodge–Tate decomposition).** *Let X be proper smooth over an algebraically closed perfectoid field C. Then Hⁱ(X*proét*, **Z**ₚ) ⊗ C ≅ ⊕ⱼ Hⁱ⁻ʲ(X, Ωʲ)(−j).*

*Proof sketch.* Work on *X*proét with the period sheaf **Ô**. The Cartan–Leray spectral sequence for the affinoid perfectoid cover *X*∞ → *X* collapses because *R*ν∗**Ô** ≅ ⊕ⱼ Ωʲ(−*j*)[−*j*], where ν : *X*proét → *X*ét is the projection; this identification is a computation on the perfectoid torus cover, where the Galois group **Z**ₚ(1)ⁿ acts and continuous group cohomology yields the exterior algebra on the Tate twists. The pro-étale cohomology *H*ⁱ(*X*proét, **Ẑ**ₚ(1)) then computes the left-hand side, and the decomposition follows [2, §8]; [3].

##6 Limitations

The theory, for all its power, has sharp boundaries.

- **Only the prime *p*.** Everything here concerns *p*-adic coefficients. The ℓ-adic story for ℓ ≠ *p* is untouched by tilting; there is no "ℓ-perfectoid" analogue, because the mechanism depends essentially on *p*-power Frobenius.
- **Properness hypotheses.** Finiteness of *p*-adic étale cohomology and the degeneration theorems require *X* proper (or suitable compactifications). For open varieties the statements are false as stated; compactly supported variants exist but are technically demanding.
- **Non-effectivity.** Almost mathematics is inherently inexplicit: "almost finite étale" gives no bound on the *p*-power torsion killing the obstruction, and tilting is not computable from equations. The theory proves existence, not algorithms.
- **Set-theoretic overhead.** The pro-étale site and diamonds require careful handling of universes and *w*-contractible objects; the foundations in [5] are substantial, and casual use invites subtle errors about sheafification.
- **Prismatic gaps.** Prismatic cohomology is currently a theory for *p*-adic formal schemes; extending it to rigid-analytic generic fibers with the same integral precision remains active research, as does the theory of prismatic *F*-crystals as coefficients.

##7 Conclusion

Perfectoid spaces reorganized *p*-adic geometry around a single principle: *deep ramification is a feature, not a bug*. By passing to towers in which Frobenius becomes surjective, one gains a functorial bridge between characteristic zero and characteristic *p* — the tilt — that identifies étale sites and transports Galois-theoretic information across the characteristic divide. Almost purity says the bridge extends integrally up to controlled torsion; the pro-étale topology turns it into a site where period sheaves compute *p*-adic Hodge theory; prismatic cohomology axiomatizes the universal cover into δ-rings and prisms, yielding one integral theory specializing to de Rham, crystalline, and étale cohomology.

The arc from Fontaine–Wintenberger's field of norms to Bhatt–Scholze's prisms spans barely a decade, yet it has rewritten the subject's foundations: comparison isomorphisms once requiring pages of ramification estimates are now consequences of functoriality, and the period rings appear as Witt vectors of a tilt. The open frontier — integral *p*-adic Hodge theory for arbitrary varieties, prismatic coefficients, and the geometrization of the local Langlands correspondence via diamonds — suggests that the perfectoid viewpoint has not yet exhausted its power.
