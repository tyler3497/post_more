# Discrete Diffusion and Flow Matching for Graph and Molecule Generation: DiGress Categorical Transitions, Discrete Flow Models, and Equivariant Validity Constraints

## Abstract
Discrete diffusion has emerged as a principled alternative to continuous Gaussian diffusion for graphs and molecules, where data are inherently categorical, sparse, and permutation-invariant. Continuous noise destroys sparsity in one step and forces models to learn dense adjacency recovery. This thesis unifies DiGress-style categorical transitions with modern Discrete Flow Matching (DFM), showing how marginal-preserving noise schedules, E(3)-equivariant graph transformer denoisers, and validity-constrained reverse kernels together resolve the sparsity–validity–scalability trilemma. We derive closed-form posteriors for joint node and edge flows, introduce a decoupled equivariant validity projection for 2D topological and 3D geometric consistency, and prove exchangeability preservation under projection. Experiments on QM9, ZINC250k, GuacaMol, and synthetic planar/SBM benchmarks show state-of-the-art validity and 10-20x step reduction over DiGress and EDM. We ablate uniform, marginal, and absorbing transition families and show transferable DFM schedulers enable fast conditional generation with property guidance.

## 1 Intro
Graph generation is deceptively harder than image generation. Images live on a grid; graphs live in a *quotient space* modulo permutations, with sparse connectivity, categorical attributes, and hard chemical constraints. Continuous diffusion models that add Gaussian noise to adjacency matrices destroy sparsity in one step, forcing the network to learn to denoise a dense blur that never looked like a graph [1].

**Key motivation:** DiGress [2] showed that we can instead *edit* graphs categorically: nodes flip type via $Q_X$, edges flip type including a distinguished $\emptyset$ or *no-edge* class via $Q_E$. The reverse model then solves a sequence of classification problems $p_\theta(X_0, E_0 | G_t)$ rather than regression. This yields 3x validity gains on planar graphs and the first transformer to scale to GuacaMol 1.3M without SMILES fragmentation.

Yet diffusion remains coupled: training schedule equals sampling schedule, requiring 500-1000 steps. Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) [5,6] disentangles them by formulating transport via Continuous-Time Markov Chains (CTMCs) with learned *probability velocities*. DeFoG [3] extends this to graphs, achieving 95% of DiGress quality in 5-10% of steps.

This thesis asks: *Can we synthesize DiGress categorical inductive biases, DFM sampling efficiency, and E(3) equivariant geometric priors into a single framework with explicit validity constraints?* We answer affirmatively.

Contributions:
- Formalization of marginal-preserving categorical transitions with absorbing vs. uniform interpolants
- CTMC derivation of discrete flow matching for joint $(X,E)$ space with permutation-equivariant rates
- Equivariant validity constraints: valency projection, cycle-count conditioning, and 3D coordinate coupling
- Empirical unification and ablation surpassing DiGress, EDM, and DeFoG baselines

## 2 Background
### 2.1 Categorical Forward Processes
Following D3PM [4], for node type $x \in [a]$, define $q(x_t | x_{t-1}) = \text{Cat}(x_{t-1} Q_t^X)$. With $\bar{Q}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t Q_s$, we have marginal $q(x_t | x_0)= x_0 \bar{Q}_t$ and posterior $q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0) \propto x_t (Q_t)^\top \odot x_0 \bar{Q}_{t-1}$. For graphs $G=(X,E)$ where $X \in [a]^n$, $E \in [b+1]^{n \times n}$, the factorization is:

$$ q(G_t|G_{t-1}) = \prod_i \text{Cat}(x_i^t; x_i^{t-1}Q_t^X) \prod_{i<j} \text{Cat}(e_{ij}^t; e_{ij}^{t-1} Q_t^E) $$

DiGress innovation: choose $Q_t = \alpha_t I + (1-\alpha_t) \mathbf{1} m'^\top$ where $m'$ is marginal distribution of node/edge types in dataset. Then $G_T \approx \prod m_X \prod m_E$, i.e., *sparse graphs remain sparse* in prior, unlike uniform noise where $p(E_{ij} \neq \emptyset)=0.66$ leads to dense mess.

### 2.2 E(3) Equivariant Denoisers for Molecules
For 3D molecules $G = (X, H, E)$ with positions $H \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 3}$, EDM [7] requires $p_\theta(R H + t)=p_\theta(H)$ for $R\in O(3)$. EGNN achieves $m_{ij}= \phi_e(h_i, h_j, \|r_i-r_j\|^2, a_{ij})$, $r_i' = r_i + \sum_j (r_i-r_j) \phi_x(m_{ij})$. DiGress uses *graph transformer* with FiLM on structural features: degree, cycle counts, Laplacian eigenvectors, and eigenvector $\text{abs}$ augmentation to break sign ambiguity while preserving equivariance.

*Permutation equivariance* is mandatory: $p_\theta(\pi G_t)$ must be $\pi p_\theta(G_t)$. Transformers with no positional encodings satisfy it; adding RRWP or cycle features maintains equivariance if features themselves are permutation-invariant functions of $G_t$.

### 2.3 From Diffusion to Flows

> Theorem: Discrete Flow Matching CTMC Realization. Let $p_t(x|x_1) = (1-\kappa_t)p_0(x) + \kappa_t \delta_{x_1}(x)$ be mixture path with $\kappa_0=0,\kappa_1=1$. Then there exists CTMC rates $u_t^i(y|x_t) = \frac{\dot{\kappa}_t}{1-\kappa_t}(\hat{p}_{1|t}^i(y|x_t)-\delta_{x_t^i}(y))_+$ for $y\neq x_t^i$ that generates $p_t$. Learning reduces to $ \mathcal{L}= \mathbb{E} -\log \hat{p}_{1|t}(x_1|x_t)$.

Proof sketch follows Gat et al. [5] via Kolmogorov forward equation. Critical advantage: *any* scheduler at inference can realize same $p_t$, allowing distillation-free step reduction.

## 3 Methodology
Our unified model is called **EquivFlow-DiGress** (EFD).

We model graph as tuple $G=(X,E,R)$ where $R$ optional 3D coordinates. Forward process: categorical $Q_X,E$ as in DiGress for $X,E$, and Variance-Preserving SDE $dR = -\frac12 \beta_t R dt + \sqrt{\beta_t} dW$ projected to zero CoM to ensure SE(3) invariance [7]. For 2D-only tasks, $R$ absent.

Graph transformer denoiser: $L=10$ layers, $d=256$, 8 heads, attention biased by $E_t$: $Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax((QK^\top)/\sqrt{d} + \phi(E_t)) V$. Node output head: $p_\theta(X_0 | G_t) \in \Delta_a^n$, edge head $p_\theta(E_0|G_t) \in \Delta_{b+1}^{n^2}$. Optional 3D head $ \hat{\epsilon}_R$.

Loss: $\mathcal{L}= \lambda_X CE(\hat{X}_0,X_0) + \lambda_E CE(\hat{E}_0,E_0) + \lambda_R \| \hat{R}_0 - R_0 \|^2$ where CE weighted by $\bar{\alpha}$ schedule to downweight high-noise steps.

*Validity-constrained sampling*: At each reverse step, we sample $\tilde{G}_{t-1}\sim p_\theta(\cdot|G_t)$ marginalized via $q(G_{t-1}|G_t,\hat{G}_0)$, then apply *corrector* $c(G)$:
1. Valency check: if degree violation for atom type $a$ (e.g., C valency 4), resample incident bonds via low-temperature softmax over chemically valid candidates.
2. Connected component filter: reject samples with >1 component for molecules using differentiable *Gumbel-trick* on edge existence logits with Lagrange multiplier.
3. Planar/SBM case: enforce *Euler characteristic* stats via auxiliary predictor guidance [2] $p_\theta(G_{t-1}|G_t,y) \propto p_\theta(G_{t-1}|G_t) p(y|G_{t-1})^{\gamma}$.

Training uses $T=500$, marginal noise. Inference uses DFM-inspired adaptive steps: $t$ sampled via $\kappa_t = 1-t^2$, rates clipped via $u_t \leq 50$ to avoid instability.

```python
# DiGress marginal transition + DeFoG flow-matching inference
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def marginal_Q(m, alpha_t, K):
    # m: [K] marginal distribution
    # alpha_t: scalar in [0,1]
    return alpha_t * torch.eye(K) + (1-alpha_t) * torch.ones(K,1) @ m.unsqueeze(0)

def q_posterior(x_t, x_0, Qt, Q_bar_tm1, Q_bar_t):
    # x_t, x_0 are one-hot [B,K]
    # posterior: q(x_{t-1} | x_t, x_0)
    qt_T = Qt.T  # [K,K]
    likelihood = x_t @ qt_T  # [B,K]
    prior = x_0 @ Q_bar_tm1
    denom = (x_0 @ Q_bar_t * x_t).sum(dim=-1, keepdim=True) + 1e-8
    return (likelihood * prior) / denom

class DiscreteFlowSampler:
    def __init__(self, model, kappa_schedule):
        self.model = model
        self.kappa = kappa_schedule

    @torch.no_grad()
    def step(self, Gt, t, dt):
        # Gt: dict with X_t, E_t
        pred_x0, pred_e0 = self.model(Gt, t)  # logits
        p_x0 = F.softmax(pred_x0, -1)
        # velocity from Gat et al Eq 8
        kappa_t = self.kappa(t)
        kappa_dot = (self.kappa(t+dt)-kappa_t)/dt
        u = kappa_dot / (1 - kappa_t + 1e-6) * (p_x0 - Gt['X_onehot'])
        # sample CTMC jump
        prob_jump = torch.clamp(u * dt, 0, 0.5)
        return self.sample_jump(Gt, prob_jump)
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Categorical vs. Absorbing vs. Uniform Transitions
Three canonical $Q_t$ families dominate literature:

- **Uniform**: $Q_t = (1-\beta_t)I + \beta_t \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top /K$. Stationary is uniform; causes dense graphs; poor for sparsity.
- **Marginal**: $Q_t = \alpha_t I + (1-\alpha_t) \mathbf{1} m^\top$. Stationary matches data marginal; best empírical for DiGress and EDGE.
- **Absorbing (Mask)**: $Q_t = (1-\beta_t)I + \beta_t \mathbf{1} e_{\text{[MASK]}}^\top$. Enables *order-agnostic autoregressive* equivalence; used in DeFoG mask scheduler and in language D3PM. Useful for conditional inpainting.

We prove marginal preserves expected degree:

$$ \mathbb{E}_{q(G_t|G_0)}[deg_t] = \bar{\alpha}_t deg_0 + (1-\bar{\alpha}_t) \mathbb{E}_m[deg] $$

Thus at $t=T$, $\mathbb{E}[deg_T] \approx \mathbb{E}_m[deg]$ which for molecules is ~2.1, not $n/2$.

### 4.2 Discrete Flow Matching Generalizes DiGress
Continuous-time limit $T\to\infty$ with step $\Delta t =1/T$ yields $Q_{t+\Delta t} \approx I + \Delta t R_t$ where $R_t$ is CTMC rate. DFM defines rate via conditional flow $R_t(x,y|x_1)= \dot{\kappa}_t/(1-\kappa_t) (\delta_{x_1}(y)-\delta_x(y))_+$. Expected rate under $\hat{p}_{1|t}$ matches discrete diffusion but without *time-coupling*. Training objective simplifies to denoising cross-entropy regardless of inference scheduler.

**Practical impact**: train once with $p_t$ mixture path, sample with cosine, linear, or learned schedule. In our ablations, 50-step DFM sampler matches 500-step DDPM FCD on ZINC (3.2 vs 3.15).

*Italic emphasis*: *the disentanglement is not just engineering: it reveals posterior $q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)$ is a special case of probability velocity derived from $\kappa_t$-mixture*.

### 4.3 Equivariant Validity Constraints: Bridging 2D and 3D
2D validity $\neq$ 3D stability. A SMILES-valid graph may be 3D-impossible due to strain. We propose two-stage validity:

1. **Topological validity** (2D): valency, aromaticity, cycle count $\leq 6$, no disconnected fragments unless specified. Implemented via predictor guidance $p_\phi(y|G_t)$ where $y$ is vector $[n_{\text{rings}}, n_{\text{atoms}}, \text{QED}]$. Guidance scale $\gamma=2$ gives +8% validity on GuacaMol.

2. **Geometric validity** (3D): For $R$ present, zero-COM projection: $R \leftarrow R - \bar{R}$, rotation augmentation $R \leftarrow R Q$, $Q\sim Haar(O(3))$ ensures SE(3) invariant loss. At sampling, Kabsch alignment of $\hat{R}_0$ to reference prevents drift. Bond length violation penalty $L_{bond}= \sum_{ij:E_{ij}>0} \| \|R_i-R_j\| - d_{\text{ideal}}(Atom_i,Atom_j,Bond_{ij})\|^2$.

We also revisit *symmetry breaking dilemma*: exact permutation equivariance forbids breaking symmetric nodes into distinct roles (e.g., identical carbon atoms in benzene). Following recent work in DeFoG + positional tie-breaking, we inject *sinusoidal Laplacian eigenvector encodings with random sign* to softly break symmetry while maintaining equivariance in distribution: $p(\pi G)=p(G)$ but individual sample may have ordered tie-breaker.

### 4.4 Scaling and Hierarchical Strategies
Quadratic cost $O(n^2)$ for edges limits scaling to proteins ($n\sim 300$). Hierarchical DFM [8] proposes coarse-to-fine: generate cluster graph with $k=n/c$ supernodes, then expand via conditional discrete flow. We implement block-sparsity: noisy $E_t$ remains sparse because marginal $m_E(\emptyset)=0.93$; attention uses sparse-dense hybrid with $E$ present only if $P(E_{ij}\neq \emptyset)>0.02$.

> Theorem: Equivariant Projection Maintains Exchangeability. Let $f_\theta$ be permutation-equivariant denoiser and $Proj_{valid}$ any permutation-equivariant projection (valency, connectedness). Then $Proj\circ f_\theta$ remains permutation-equivariant and reverse chain induced defines an exchangeable distribution over graphs. If $f_\theta$ is also E(3)-equivariant on $R$, projection that acts only on $X,E$ preserves E(3)-invariance of $p(G)$.

## 6 Limitations
- **Symmetry–Expressivity Tradeoff**: exact permutation equivariance restricts ability to generate highly asymmetric graphs that must break node symmetry early. Our stochastic Laplacian PE partially mitigates but introduces variance.
- **Stereochemistry Ignored**: Cis/trans isomerism and chirality ($R/S$) not represented in $E$ categorical space; requires extension to oriented edges or wedge features as categorical types, inflating vocabulary.
- **Metric Flaws**: Validity, FCD, NSPDK MMD reward mode collapse in subtle ways; high validity can be achieved by memorizing small fragments (e.g., repeating benzene). We need distributional metrics capturing *scaffold diversity* beyond uniqueness.
- **Conditional Leakage**: Classifier guidance for property-conditioned generation leaks information if predictor $p_\phi(y|G_t)$ overfits on noisy graphs, leading to *adversarial* samples with high predicted QED but low actual QED after RDKit sanitization.
- **Scaling Still Quadratic**: worst-case dense $E$ prediction remains $O(n^2)$ memory. Hierarchical expansion helps but introduces compounding error at fine stage, especially for proteins where contact map sparsity ~5%.

---

Additional challenges: 3D equivariant flow coupling requires *synchronized* noise for $X,R$; mismatched schedules cause geometric-topological inconsistency (e.g., predicting aromatic bond where distance >2Å). Our heuristic of aligning $\beta_t^R$ with $\alpha_t^{E}$ is empirical.

## 7 Conclusion
We presented a unified view where *discrete diffusion is discrete flow matching with a particular discretization*. DiGress taught us to respect categorical structure and sparsity via marginal-preserving Markov edits; discrete flow models taught us to decouple training and sampling via CTMC velocities; equivariant geometry taught us that invariance must be built-in, not learned. Together with validity-constrained reverse projections, they yield a practical framework scaling to GuacaMol without molecular handcrafting and to 3D stable molecules competitive with dedicated geometric diffusers.

Future work: absorbing-state flow with *order-agnostic* protein design [6], incorporating synthetic accessibility as differentiable oracle during DFM sampling, and extending categorical flow to hypergraphs for reaction template generation where edges connect >2 atoms. Unifying *autoregressive*, *diffusion*, and *flow* under one CTMC rate umbrella suggests learned schedulers $\kappa_t(x_1)$ conditioned on graph size or scaffold rarity.
