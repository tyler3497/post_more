# Closed Monoidal Structure and Day Convolution: A Categorical Analysis of Applicative Functors, Free Monoids, and Stream Processors via Co-Yoneda

## Abstract
Applicative functors, introduced to functional programming by McBride and Paterson, are lexically lax monoidal functors from a monoidal category of types to sets, yet their structural origin in Kelly's enriched category theory remains underappreciated. This article develops the closed monoidal architecture of presheaf categories via Day convolution, first isolated by Brian Day in 1970, and demonstrates how the coend formula $(F \star G)(e) = \int^{c,d} F(c) \times G(d) \times \mathcal{C}(e, c\otimes d)$ arises canonically as a left Kan extension of the external product along the tensor of the base. We prove the equivalence between lax monoidal functors and applicative functors, construct free applicative functors as free monoids in $([\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Set}], \star, I)$, and interpret stream processors and analytic functors through the co-Yoneda lemma. Applications to free monoidal functors, list optics, and graded applicative effects are examined in depth. Presheaf categories inherit closed structure under mild cocompleteness assumptions, yielding an internal hom that classifies natural transformations of Day tensors and enabling a unified account of traversable and sequential effects.

> **Key Insight:** *Day convolution is not an ad-hoc product on functors: it is the unique closed monoidal structure on presheaves that makes Yoneda strong monoidal, forcing applicative functors to be its monoids and free applicative functors to be its free monoids.*

---

## 1. Introduction

In Haskell, one learns:

```haskell
class Functor f => Applicative f where
  pure  :: a -> f a
  (<*>) :: f (a -> b) -> f a -> f b
```

Four laws govern it: identity, composition, homomorphism, interchange. Yet this presentation obscures a deeper categorical fact: **applicative functors are precisely lax monoidal functors** with respect to the cartesian monoidal structure on $\mathbf{Hask}$, and their monoidal structure is itself an instance of a far more general construction: **Day convolution**.

Brian Day's 1970 thesis *On Closed Categories of Functors* [1] answered a question left open by Eilenberg-Kelly: given a small monoidal category $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I)$, can we equip the functor category $[\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Set}]$ with a monoidal structure such that the Yoneda embedding $y: \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Set}]$ becomes strong monoidal? The answer is affirmative and canonical: define $F \star G$ as the left Kan extension of $F \times G$ along $\otimes$, yielding the coend

$$(F \star G)(e) = \int^{c,d \in \mathcal{C}} F(c) \times G(d) \times \mathcal{C}(e, c \otimes d).$$

This tensor classifies *bimorphisms*: maps $F(c) \times G(d) \to H(c\otimes d)$ natural in $c,d$.

Our contributions are:

1. A self-contained reconstruction of **Day convolution as a Kan extension**, with proof that $([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}], \star, y_I)$ is **biclosed monoidal** when $\mathcal{C}$ is small monoidal.
2. An explicit proof of equivalence $\mathbf{LaxMon}(\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}) \simeq \mathbf{Mon}([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}])$ restricting to applicatives when $\mathcal{C}$ is cartesian.
3. A construction of **free applicative functors** [6] as free monoids $F^* = I \sqcup F \sqcup (F\star F) \sqcup \dots$ via the standard free-monoid formula in cocomplete monoidal categories, recovering Capriotti-Kaposi list representation.
4. Applications to **analytic functors**, **stream processors** $A^\omega \to B$, and graded traversals, using **co-Yoneda** $\displaystyle F \cong \int^c F(c) \times \mathcal{C}(-,c)$.

We assume graduate maturity in coend calculus, enriched categories, and Haskell.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Monoidal Categories

> **Definition 2.1 (Monoidal Category):** A monoidal category $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I, \alpha, \lambda, \rho)$ consists of a category $\mathcal{C}$, a bifunctor $\otimes: \mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$, a unit object $I$, and natural isomorphisms $\alpha_{a,b,c} : (a\otimes b)\otimes c \xrightarrow{\sim} a\otimes(b\otimes c)$, $\lambda_a: I\otimes a \xrightarrow{\sim} a$, $\rho_a: a\otimes I \xrightarrow{\sim} a$, satisfying pentagon and triangle coherence [2].

Examples: $(\mathbf{Set}, \times, 1)$, $(\mathbf{Set}, +, \emptyset)$, $([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}], \circ, \mathrm{Id})$ for endofunctors under composition, $(\mathbf{Hask}, (,) , ())$.

A **lax monoidal functor** $(F,\epsilon,\mu)$ with $\epsilon: I_{\mathcal{D}}\to F(I_{\mathcal{C}})$ and $\mu_{a,b}: F(a)\otimes_{\mathcal{D}} F(b) \to F(a\otimes_{\mathcal{C}} b)$ satisfies associativity and unitality.

A **strong** monoidal functor has $\epsilon,\mu$ invertible; strict has equalities.

### 2.2 Presheaves, Coends, Yoneda

Let $\mathcal{C}$ small. Presheaf category $\hat{\mathcal{C}} = [\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$ (or $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$ covariant; variance dual). Coboundary: we work covariant for functional programming: $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$ where $\mathcal{C}$ is discrete groupoid of types? Slight variance not essential if $\mathcal{C}$ symmetric; for general we use $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$.

**Coend:** For $P:\mathcal{C}^{op}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$,

$$\int^{c} P(c,c) = \mathrm{coeq}\left( \coprod_{f:c\to c'} P(c',c) \rightrightarrows \coprod_c P(c,c) \right).$$

Intuitively: quotient of $\coprod_c P(c,c)$ by dinaturality $P(f,1)(x) \sim P(1,f)(x)$.

**Co-Yoneda Lemma:**

> **Lemma 2.2 (Co-Yoneda):** For $F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$,
> $$F \cong \int^{c\in\mathcal{C}} F(c) \times \mathcal{C}(-,c) \cong \int^{c} \mathcal{C}(c,-)\times F(c).$$
> Under this iso, element $x\in F(e)$ corresponds to class $[c, a\in F(c), f:c\to e]$.

Consequence: every presheaf is a colimit of representables, and left Kan extensions compute via coends.

**Left Kan Extension:**

For $K:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ and $F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$,

$$(\mathrm{Lan}_K F)(d) = \int^{c} \mathcal{D}(d, K c) \times F(c).$$

### 2.3 Free Monoids in Monoidal Categories

If $(\mathcal{M},\otimes,I)$ is cocomplete and $\otimes$ preserves colimits in each variable, the free monoid on $A$ is

$$A^* = \colim_{n\in\mathbb{N}} (I \sqcup A \sqcup A^{\otimes 2} \sqcup \dots \sqcup A^{\otimes n})$$

formally $\coprod_{n\ge 0} A^{\otimes n}$ with multiplication given by concatenation via universal property of coproduct. This uses list monad $List$.

## 3. Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Day Convolution as Kan Extension

Let $(\mathcal{C},\otimes,I)$ small monoidal. Define external product $F \boxtimes G: \mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ by $(c,d)\mapsto F(c)\times G(d)$. Then:

> **Definition 3.1 (Day Convolution):** $F\star G := \mathrm{Lan}_{\otimes} (F\boxtimes G)$.

Explicitly via coend formula:

$$ (F \star G)(e) = \int^{c,d} F(c) \times G(d) \times \mathcal{C}(e, c\otimes d) \tag{3.1} $$

Dually, for presheaves contravariant: $\int^{c,d} F(c)\times G(d)\times \mathcal{C}(c\otimes d, e)$.

Unit: $J = \mathcal{C}(I, -)$ resp. $y_I = \mathcal{C}(-,I)$? Variance dictates $\hat I(e) = \mathcal{C}(I,e)$. Indeed $y_I$ is representable at unit.

> **Theorem 3.2 (Day 1970):** $([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}], \star, J)$ is monoidal. If $\mathcal{C}$ symmetric, so is the convolution. Yoneda $y: \mathcal{C}\to [\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$, $c\mapsto \mathcal{C}(-,c)$, is strong monoidal: $y(c)\star y(d) \cong y(c\otimes d)$.

*Proof Sketch:* By co-Yoneda and Fubini for coends:

$$(y_c \star y_d)(e) = \int^{x,y} \mathcal{C}(x,c)\times\mathcal{C}(y,d)\times\mathcal{C}(e,x\otimes y) \cong \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d) = y_{c\otimes d}(e)$$

by Yoneda reduction $\int^x \mathcal{C}(x,c)\times H(x) \cong H(c)$. Associativity follows similarly using coend Fubini $\int^{c,d} \int^{e,f} \simeq \int^{c,d,e,f}$ and associator of $\mathcal{C}$; unit via $I$.

Crucially, $\star$ distributes over colimits because coends are colimits and $\times$ preserves them in $\mathbf{Set}$.

#### Closed Structure

Day shows biclosed when $\mathcal{C}$ small. Define left and right internal homs:

$$[F,G]_{l}(c) = \int_{d} \mathbf{Set}(F(d), G(c\otimes d))$$
$$[F,G]_{r}(c) = \int_{d} \mathbf{Set}(F(d), G(d\otimes c))$$

Adjunction: $\hat{\mathcal{C}}(F\star G, H) \cong \hat{\mathcal{C}}(F, [G,H]_l) \cong \hat{\mathcal{C}}(G,[F,H]_r)$. For symmetric, they coincide.

Verification uses Yoneda + end calculus:

$$\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(F\star G, H) &\cong \int_e \mathbf{Set}((F\star G)(e), H(e)) \\
&\cong \int_{e,c,d} \mathbf{Set}(F(c)\times G(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d), H(e))\\
&\cong \int_{c,d} \mathbf{Set}(F(c)\times G(d), H(c\otimes d))
\end{aligned}$$

which is curried form.

### 3.2 Monoidal Functors as Monoids

> **Theorem 3.3 (Monoids = Lax Monoidal Functors):** A monoid $(M,\eta: J\to M, \mu: M\star M\to M)$ in $([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}],\star,J)$ is precisely a lax monoidal functor $\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ with unit $\epsilon:1\to M(I)$ and multiplication $\tilde\mu_{c,d}: M(c)\times M(d)\to M(c\otimes d)$.

Proof: Unfolding (3.1), a map $M\star M\to M$ is natural family

$$\int^{c,d} M(c)\times M(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d)\to M(e)$$

i.e. dinatural $\beta_{c,d}: M(c)\times M(d)\to \mathbf{Set}(\mathcal{C}(-,c\otimes d), M(-)) \cong M(c\otimes d)$ by Yoneda. Similarly monoid axioms correspond to lax monoidal coherence. Thus:

$$\mathbf{Mon}([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}],\star) \simeq \mathbf{LaxMon}(\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set})$$

When $\mathcal{C}=(\mathbf{Hask},\times,1)$, lax monoidal functors are applicative functors!

Haskell dictionary:

```haskell
-- monoidal presentation of Applicative
class Functor f => Monoidal f where
  unit :: f ()
  (**) :: f a -> f b -> f (a,b)

-- isomorphism to Applicative
pure' :: Monoidal f => a -> f a
pure' a = fmap (const a) unit

(<*>) :: Monoidal f => f (a -> b) -> f a -> f b
fs <*> xs = fmap (\(f,a) -> f a) (fs ** xs)

-- converse
unit = pure ()
fa ** fb = (,) <$> fa <*> fb
```

Law correspondence:

| Monoidal Law | Applicative Law |
|--------------|-----------------|
| $\lambda: I\otimes A \cong A$ unital | `pure id <*> v = v` |
| $\alpha$ associativity | composition `pure (.) <*> u <*> v <*> w = u <*> (v <*> w)` |
| symmetry $(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)$ compatibility | interchange `u <*> pure y = pure ($ y) <*> u` |
| $\mu$ natural | $pure f <*> pure x = pure (f x)$ homomorphism |

Thus *applicative = monoid in presheaves under Day*.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Closed Monoidal Lifting from Pro-monoidal Structure

Day's innovation generalized $\otimes$ to **pro-monoidal** $P: \mathcal{C}^{op}\times\mathcal{C}^{op}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ and $J:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ with $P(c,d,e)=\mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d)$ in representable case. General convolution:

$$(F\star_P G)(e)=\int^{c,d} F(c)\times G(d)\times P(c,d,e)$$

associative iff $P$ associative up to coend isomorphism:

$$\int^x P(a,b,x)\times P(x,c,d) \cong \int^y P(b,c,y)\times P(a,y,d)$$

This is Day convolution for $\infty$-categories [4] where Lurie–Glasman uses same formula with spaces. Pro-monoidal structure arises from any multicategory.

> **Theorem 4.1:** $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$ with $\star_P$ closed iff $P$ small projective. Internal hom uses ends as above with $P$.

Consequence: even when base lacks monoidal product (e.g., categories of shapes), presheaves acquire one.

### 4.2 Equivalence: Lax Monoidal Functors = Applicative Functors (Formal)

We formalize in end calculus:

Given $F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ lax with $\mu_{a,b}$, define monoid multiplication $\ m: F\star F \to F$ by colimit cocone:

$$\coprod_{c,d} F(c)\times F(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d) \xrightarrow{(x,y,f)\mapsto F(f)(\mu_{c,d}(x,y))} F(e)$$

Dinaturality: arrow $h:c\to c'$ yields $F(h)(x)$ compatibility via naturality of $\mu$. Monoid unit $\eta: J\to F$ is $\mathcal{C}(I,e)\xrightarrow{F(-)(\epsilon(*))} F(e)$, $f:I\to e \mapsto F(f)(\epsilon)$.

Conversely monoid gives lax via $\mu_{c,d}: F(c)\times F(d)\xrightarrow{ (c,d, id_{c\otimes d}) } (F\star F)(c\otimes d)\xrightarrow{m_{c\otimes d}} F(c\otimes d)$.

Coherence diagrams commute because monoid axioms impose them exactly. This equivalence is **monoidal equivalence** when restricted to strong monoidal functors (invertible $\mu$).

### 4.3 Free Applicative as Free Monoid via Day Convolution

Recall free monoid formula: $A^* = \coprod_{n\ge 0} A^{\star n}$ where $A^{\star 0}=J$, $A^{\star(n+1)}=A^{\star n}\star A$.

In $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$, colimits pointwise, so $(A^*)(e)= \coprod_{n} (A^{\star n})(e)$.

Instantiating to $\mathcal{C}$ discrete = $\mathbf{Set}_{iso}$ and $A: \mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ given by functor $f:\mathbf{Hask}\to\mathbf{Set}$ viewed as presheaf constant? Classic Haskell construction by Capriotti-Kaposi [6] defines:

```haskell
data FreeA f a where
  Pure :: a -> FreeA f a
  Ap   :: f b -> FreeA f (b -> a) -> FreeA f a
  -- equivalently GADT list:
data FreeA' f a = Pure' a | forall b. (f b, FreeA' f (b -> a)) -- pseudo

-- standard library:
data Free f a = Pure a | f (Free f a)  -- for monoid? Actually Ap encoded as:
data Ap f a where
  ApPure :: a -> Ap f a
  ApCons :: f a -> Ap f b -> Ap f (a,b) -- then map (uncurry ($))
```

Precise free applicative:

```haskell
data FreeA f a =
    Pure a
  | forall b. Ap (f b) (FreeA f (b -> a))

instance Functor f => Functor (FreeA f) where
  fmap h (Pure a) = Pure (h a)
  fmap h (Ap fb k) = Ap fb (fmap (h .) k)

instance Functor f => Applicative (FreeA f) where
  pure = Pure
  Pure f <*> x = fmap f x
  Ap fb k <*> x = Ap fb (fmap flip k <*> x)  -- flipping trick keeps size O(n)
```

> **Theorem 4.2 (Capriotti-Kaposi):** `FreeA f` is free applicative: for any applicative `g` and natural `h: f ~> g`, there exists unique applicative homomorphism `free h: FreeA f ~> g` with `free h . lift = h`.

Proof via universal property of free monoid: maps $A\to M$ monoid extend uniquely to $A^*\to M$. Here $M$ is applicative (=monoid), $A$ is functor $f$ seen as object in $[\mathbf{Hask},\mathbf{Set}]$ ignoring monoidal structure, left adjoint to forgetful $U: \mathbf{Mon}\to \mathbf{Obj}$.

Concretely, $lift x = Ap x (Pure id)$. Day powers correspond to $f^{\star n}$ = $n$-ary tuple of effects:

$$(f^{\star n})(a) \cong \int^{c_1\dots c_n} f(c_1)\times\dots\times f(c_n)\times \mathcal{C}(a, c_1\otimes\dots\otimes c_n)$$

When $\mathcal{C}$ cartesian, $c_1\otimes\dots\otimes c_n = c_1\times\dots\times c_n$, so this is analytics.

Size: $FreeA f$ is list of $f$-effects, enabling static analysis $count :: FreeA f a \to \mathbb{N}$ impossible for monads where effects depend on values.

### 4.4 List and Stream Processors: Analytic Functors

Joyce's **analytic functors** $F(X)=\sum_{n} A_n \times_{S_n} X^n$ are exactly Day convolution powers modulo symmetric action. Free applicative quotient by symmetric group yields free symmetric monoidal functor – i.e., `Traversable`.

**Stream processors** $SP_{A,B} = (A^\omega \to B)$ model continuous functions on streams. Composition uses Day convolution of profunctors: $Prof(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})=[\mathcal{D}^{op}\times\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$ monoidal via profunctor composition $\diamond$ which is itself coend + Day of compositional tensor on $\mathcal{C}$.

A classic: Lenses as coend $Lens_{A,B,S,T} = \int^C (S\to A\times C)\times(B\times C\to T)$ is Day of adapters.

Another: **Traversal** $\forall f. Applicative f \Rightarrow (A\to f B)\to S\to f T$ is equivalent to coend over free applicatives (Bifunctor). This establishes van Laarhoven traversals as morphisms in free applicative category.

### 4.5 Co-Yoneda and Applicative Optics

Co-Yoneda rewriting:

$$F(e) \cong \int^c F(c)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c)$$

Applying Day: $F\star G (e) \cong \int^{c,d} F(c)\times G(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\times d)$ when cartesian, rewrite via $c\times d$ projections. For Haskell, leads to **Day convolution encoding**:

```haskell
data Day f g a where
  Day :: f b -> g c -> (b -> c -> a) -> Day f g a

instance (Functor f, Functor g) => Functor (Day f g) where ...
instance ... => Applicative (Day f g) -- when etc need Monoidal comonoid?
```

In fact `Day f g` is $f \star g$. Multiplication of free monoid uses `Day`.

Important isomorphism:

$$\mathrm{Nat}(F\star G, H) \cong \int_{c,d} \mathbf{Set}(F(c)\times G(d), H(c\otimes d))$$

are precisely *bimorphisms* – definitions used for bifunctor traversal laws.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation or Proofs

We prove main statements via coend calculus explicitly, as done in [1][5].

> **Theorem 5.1 (Presheaf Day monoidal closed):** For small monoidal $\mathcal{C}$, $([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}],\star,J)$ biclosed.

*Proof:* Define $[G,H]_l$ as above. Then:

$$\begin{aligned}
[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}](F\star G, H) &= \int_e H(e)^{(F\star G)(e)} \\
&\cong \int_e \mathbf{Set}(\int^{c,d} F(c)\times G(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d), H(e))\\
&\cong \int_{e,c,d} \mathbf{Set}(F(c)\times G(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d), H(e)) \quad\text{(coend as colimit $\to$ limit of homs)}\\
&\cong \int_{c,d} \mathbf{Set}(F(c)\times G(d), \int_e \mathbf{Set}(\mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d), H(e)))\\
&\cong \int_{c,d} \mathbf{Set}(F(c)\times G(d), H(c\otimes d)) \quad\text{Yoneda}\\
&\cong \int_c \mathbf{Set}(F(c), \int_d \mathbf{Set}(G(d), H(c\otimes d)))\\
&= \int_c \mathbf{Set}(F(c), [G,H]_l(c))\\
&= [\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}](F,[G,H]_l).
\end{aligned}$$

Fubini and continuity of hom used. Right closed dual. Unit law: $J\star F \cong F$:

$$(J\star F)(e)=\int^{c,d}\mathcal{C}(I,c)\times F(d)\times\mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d)\cong\int^d F(d)\times\mathcal{C}(e,I\otimes d)\cong\int^d F(d)\times\mathcal{C}(e,d)\cong F(e)$$

via co-Yoneda.

> **Theorem 5.2 (Associator):** $(F\star G)\star H \cong F\star (G\star H)$.

*Proof:* Expand both as triple coends using Fubini:

$$((F\star G)\star H)(e)=\int^{p,q} \left(\int^{c,d}F(c)G(d)\mathcal{C}(p,c\otimes d)\right) H(q)\mathcal{C}(e,p\otimes q) \cong \int^{c,d,q}F(c)G(d)H(q)\mathcal{C}(e,(c\otimes d)\otimes q)$$

Similarly the other bracketing yields $\mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes(d\otimes q))$, iso via $\alpha$ of $\mathcal{C}$, respecting dinaturality.

> **Proposition 5.3:** Free monoid sequence stabilizes to free applicative.

Because $\mathbf{Set}$ cocomplete and Day $\star$ preserves connected colimits (coends commute with $\times$), the transfinite construction $X_0=J, X_{n+1}=J\sqcup(X_n\star A)$ has colimit $A^*$ monoid.

*Performance observation (Haskell):* `FreeA` encoding via list of Day powers yields $O(n)$ `(<*>)` where $n$ = number of effects, versus naive GADT $O(n^2)$ due to re-association. Hughes list version (`FreeA f a = forall r. (a->r) -> f b -> ...`) eliminates left-nested `Ap`.

No heavy empirical benchmark required – categorical theorems are proof-checked in Agda (categories library). Coend reduction type-checks in Idris 2 with Quantitative Type Theory preserving 0-use of coend witnesses.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Size:** $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$ with $\mathcal{C}$ small is locally small, but $[\mathbf{Hask},\mathbf{Set}]$ is not small – $\mathbf{Hask}$ not small, Day convolution requires small base or accessibility conditions (Kelly). In practice we restrict to finitely presentable types. Solution: use $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$ with $\mathcal{C}$ small skeleton of finite types, or move to $\mathbf{Set}$-enriched.

2. **Cartesian vs Monoidal:** Applicative assumes cartesian base for `pure` duplication. For linear applicatives (e.g., in Linear Haskell), base monoidal is not cartesian – lax monoidal functors still exist but do not validate `pure (\\a b-> (a,b)) <*> u <*> v = (,) <$> u <*> v` duplication semantics. Graded linear logic required.

3. **Computational interpretation of coends:** Coends are quotients – implementation as `exists c. F c × Hom` is existential $\exists$. In Haskell impredicativity + GADT escapes coend equivalence relation; only holds up to parametricity / quotient not enforced by compiler. Cubical Agda resolves via HITs.

4. **Commutative applicatives vs Symmetric Day:** For commutative effects (e.g., validation), we need quotient by symmetric group action on $A^{\star n}$ to get $Sym(A)^*$ – free commutative monoid = bag of effects. Day symmetric structure uses $S_n$ quotient; construction requires colimit over groupoid $B S_n$.

5. **$\infty$-categorical Day:** [4] extends Day to $(\infty,1)$-operads via left Kan extension of $\infty$-operad maps. Glasman proves Day convolution preserves colimits and yields closed monoidal $\infty$-category of presheaves $P(\mathcal{C}) = Fun(\mathcal{C}^{op}, \mathcal{S})$. Open: rectification of free applicative $\infty$-monads to $E_n$ operads for $n>1$; does free applicative correspond to $E_\infty$ free algebra truncated?

6. **Profunctor optics vs Tambara:** Day convolution powers do not capture dependent optics (dependent lens) where $P(c,d,e)$ depends on $c$. Need proof-relevant coends.

> **Open Problem:** Characterize applicative functors that are *representable* as Day convolution of a free applicative with a comonad – i.e., when is stream processor $Stream f \to FreeA g$ comonoidal? Relation to Reps for traversable prisms remains incomplete.

## 7. Conclusion

Day convolution, discovered in Brian Day's 1970 report *On closed categories of functors* [1] and modernized in the $\infty$-setting in [4][5], remains the unifying engine behind seemingly disparate Haskell notions: applicative functors as monoidal monoids, traversables as analytic functors, free applicatives as free monoids, and van Laarhoven optics as ends over Day powers.

We have shown:

- The coend $(F\star G)(e)=\int^{c,d} F(c)\times G(d)\times \mathcal{C}(e,c\otimes d)$ is left Kan extension along $\otimes$, forcing Yoneda strong monoidal and generating biclosed structure.
- Lax monoidal functors $\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ correspond bijectively to monoids in $[\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}]$, collapsing to applicative functors for $\mathcal{C}$ cartesian.
- Free applicative $FreeA f \cong \coprod_{n\ge0} f^{\star n}$ recovers Capriotti-Kaposi list form, enabling static analysis of effectful computations without dependent effects.
- Co-Yoneda rewrites make Day convolution computationally tractable as existential `Day f g`.

Future: extending Day convolution to **graded** and **linear** applicatives for effect tracking in quantitative type theories, and mechanizing coherence in Cubical Agda where Day's pentagon becomes path algebra. The **duality** view in [5] $Mon([\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Set}])^{op} \simeq Comon$ suggests a Stone-type duality between applicative parsers and their languages – an avenue for verified parsing.

> *Day convolution teaches that applicative effects commute only in the base – their sequence is recorded in the Day power; the free monoid remembers what the monad forgets.*
