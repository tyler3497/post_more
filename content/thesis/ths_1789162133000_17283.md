# Discrete Time Crystals in Periodically Driven Many-Body Systems: Floquet Engineering, Many-Body Localization, and Prethermal Rigidity

## Abstract

The spontaneous breaking of time-translation symmetry — first envisioned by Wilczek as equilibrium "quantum time crystals" [1], then forbidden for ground states and Gibbs ensembles by no-go theorems [2] — has found rigorous, experimentally verified realization in periodically driven quantum many-body systems. In the *discrete* time crystal (DTC), a Floquet system with $T$-periodic Hamiltonian develops a robust subharmonic response at period $nT$, spontaneously breaking the drive's discrete time-translation symmetry. Two mechanisms underwrite this rigidity: many-body localization (MBL), which arrests Floquet heating and enforces *eigenstate order* across the quasienergy spectrum [3,4], and high-frequency *prethermalization*, which suppresses heating exponentially in drive frequency without disorder [7]. We develop the Floquet formalism, the π-paired eigenstate structure, and absolute stability criteria; survey experiments from trapped ions [5] to Google's Sycamore processor [6] and disorder-free prethermal platforms [8]; present numerical diagnostics of spatiotemporal long-range order; and delineate open frontiers in higher-dimensional MBL, dissipative time crystals, and the thermodynamic fate of Floquet heating.

## 1 Introduction

Spatial crystals break translation invariance routinely; in 2012 Frank Wilczek asked whether an analogous structure could exist in *time* — a closed system whose ground state exhibits periodic motion, spontaneously breaking continuous time-translation symmetry as ordinary crystals break spatial symmetry [1]: a clock that ticks forever without energy input.

The response was swift. Patrick Bruno showed that Wilczek's rotating-soliton model was not in its true ground state, and more decisively, Watanabe and Oshikawa proved a *no-go theorem*: defining time-crystalline order via long-range spatiotemporal order of time-dependent correlators, they showed it is impossible in the ground state or any thermal Gibbs ensemble of a Hamiltonian with not-too-long-range interactions [2]. The argument is elementary in retrospect — in an energy eigenstate $|n\rangle$, the phases $e^{-iE_n t}$ cancel in every expectation value $\langle n|\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)|n\rangle$, and the cancellation survives thermal averaging. The equilibrium time crystal was dead.

Yet the *spirit* of the idea survived a subtle shift of the question. The no-go theorems assume time-independent Hamiltonians and equilibrium ensembles. What if the system is driven periodically, so only a *discrete* time-translation symmetry $t \to t + T$ exists, and the system is kept forever out of equilibrium? A phase that spontaneously breaks this discrete symmetry — oscillating at $2T$ or some multiple of the drive period — evades every no-go theorem while capturing the essential phenomenology: rigid, self-sustained temporal order robust to perturbations.

This is the *discrete time crystal* (DTC), formulated in 2016 by Khemani et al. — disordered Floquet-MBL systems host sharp non-equilibrium phases diagnosed by *eigenstate order* [3] — and by Else, Bauer, and Nayak, who constructed an explicit Floquet time crystal where MBL prevents heating [4]. Observed within a year in trapped ions [5] and NV centers, later on a superconducting processor [6], with a disorder-free prethermal route established in parallel [7,8].

Section 2 reviews the no-go theorems, Floquet theory, and MBL. Section 3 describes the canonical models and diagnostics. Section 4 develops the deep theory. Section 5 surveys the experiments and numerical protocols. Section 6 confronts the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The no-go theorems and the retreat from equilibrium

Watanabe and Oshikawa's result deserves careful statement, because its precise scope is what the DTC exploits [2]:

> **Theorem (Watanabe–Oshikawa):** Let $H$ be a time-independent Hamiltonian with interactions of range bounded by a finite power law. Define time-crystalline order via the unequal-time correlator $\lim_{|x-x'|\to\infty}\lim_{V\to\infty}\langle \hat{\mathcal{O}}(x,t)\,\hat{\mathcal{O}}(x',0)\rangle_c$ in the ground state or Gibbs state. Then this correlator is time-independent: no spontaneous breaking of continuous time-translation symmetry is possible.

The loopholes the theorem leaves open are as important as what it closes: (i) it assumes a *time-independent* Hamiltonian — periodic driving is exempt; (ii) it addresses *continuous* symmetry breaking — discrete $T \to nT$ breaking is a weaker, distinct claim; (iii) it assumes equilibrium ensembles — a driven steady state that never thermalizes is exempt. The DTC walks through all three loopholes simultaneously.

### 2.2 Floquet theory

For $H(t+T)=H(t)$, Floquet's theorem — the temporal analog of Bloch's theorem — gives solutions $\lvert\psi_\alpha(t)\rangle = e^{-i\epsilon_\alpha t}\lvert u_\alpha(t)\rangle$ with $T$-periodic $\lvert u_\alpha\rangle$. Quasienergies $\epsilon_\alpha$ are defined modulo $\omega_D = 2\pi/T$, and the Floquet operator $U_F = \mathcal{T}\exp(-i\int_0^T H(t)\,dt)$ obeys $U_F\lvert u_\alpha(0)\rangle = e^{-i\epsilon_\alpha T}\lvert u_\alpha(0)\rangle$. Generic interacting driven systems heat to infinite temperature (*Floquet heating*). The DTC is the statement that this heating can be arrested, with Floquet eigenstates carrying *order* — invisible in a single period, manifest as a locked subharmonic response.

### 2.3 Many-body localization

Many-body localization is the interacting generalization of Anderson localization: in a strongly disordered, isolated quantum system, local integrals of motion (LIOMs, or "l-bits") emerge, the system fails to act as its own heat bath, and the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) is violated at *finite energy density* [3]. MBL also operates in Floquet systems — *Floquet MBL* — where the l-bits are integrals of motion of $U_F$. Because the drive can only induce transitions between quasienergy states that are "close" in the localized basis, resonant absorption is suppressed: MBL is the *rigidity* that makes a driven phase of matter sharply defined.

### 2.4 Eigenstate order

Khemani et al. introduced *eigenstate order*: in an MBL system, *every* eigenstate can exhibit long-range order, each resembling a product state in the l-bit basis dressed by a finite-depth local unitary [3]. A DTC is the Floquet phase whose eigenstates display *spatiotemporal* long-range order — breaking both a spatial symmetry (e.g., Ising $\mathbb{Z}_2$) and the drive's discrete time-translation symmetry.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The canonical model

The workhorse of DTC theory is the binary-driven disordered Ising chain [4,6]. The Floquet operator factorizes into two half-period evolutions:

$$U_F = e^{-i H_2 T/2}\,e^{-i H_1 T/2},$$

with

$$H_1 = \frac{\pi g}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L} X_j, \qquad H_2 = \sum_{j=1}^{L-1} J_j\, Z_j Z_{j+1} + \sum_{j=1}^{L} h_j\, Z_j,$$

with Pauli operators $X_j, Z_j$, disordered couplings $J_j$, fields $h_j$, and flip imperfection $g$. At $g = 1$, $H_1$ implements a perfect global $\pi$-pulse and every $z$-basis product state returns to itself after *two* periods: period doubling is trivial. The content of the DTC phase is that this period doubling *persists* when $g$ is detuned from 1 over a finite range, locked by interactions and disorder rather than fine-tuning — the precise analog of an ordinary crystal's rigid lattice spacing.

### 3.2 Diagnostics of DTC order

A genuine DTC must satisfy criteria strictly stronger than "we see oscillations at $\omega_D/2$." Following [4,6], the diagnostics are:

1. **Subharmonic rigidity.** Stroboscopic magnetization oscillates at half the drive frequency over a *finite range* of $g$ around 1; the Fourier peak at $\omega_D/2$ has height *independent* of $g$ within the phase.
2. **Eigenstate spatiotemporal order.** In the l-bit basis, Floquet eigenstates come in $\pi$-paired doublets (Section 4.1) with long-range Edwards–Anderson spin-glass order:
   $$\chi_{SG} = \frac{1}{L^2}\sum_{i,j} \langle u_\alpha|Z_i Z_j|u_\alpha\rangle^2 \sim \mathcal{O}(1),$$
   for *every* $\alpha$ in the spectrum.
3. **Persistence for all initial states.** Order must not be a property of special low-entanglement initial states; it must hold across the spectrum. The Sycamore experiment verified this via *quantum typicality* with scrambled, highly entangled initial states [6].
4. **Finite-size scaling.** The subharmonic peak height and the spin-glass order parameter must extrapolate to nonzero values as $L \to \infty$, locating a genuine phase transition at $g_c$ rather than a finite-size crossover.

| Criterion | Observable | DTC signature | Trivial mimic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subharmonic response | $S(\omega_D/2)$ | Sharp peak, rigid in $g$ | Peak only at $g=1$ |
| Eigenstate order | $\chi_{SG}$ | $\mathcal{O}(1)$ for all $\alpha$ | Decays with $L$ |
| Initial-state independence | Autocorrelator $A(t)$ | Same for typical states | State-dependent |
| Thermodynamic limit | Finite-size scaling | $g_c \approx 0.84$ (MBL model) [6] | No crossing |

### 3.3 Numerical protocols

Exact diagonalization of $U_F$ for $L \lesssim 14$–$16$ gives direct access to the quasienergy spectrum and eigenstate order parameters. For larger systems, time-evolving block decimation (TEBD) or Krylov methods evolve matrix-product states under the stroboscopic map. A minimal diagnostic in Python is the stroboscopic autocorrelator:

```python
import numpy as np

def floquet_autocorrelator(psi0, U1, U2, n_periods):
    """Stroboscopic <Z_j(0) Z_j(nT)> for a binary drive U_F = U2 @ U1."""
    psi = psi0.copy()
    auto = np.zeros(n_periods, dtype=complex)
    z0 = expectation_Z(psi, site=0)
    for n in range(n_periods):
        psi = U2 @ (U1 @ psi)          # one Floquet period
        auto[n] = np.vdot(psi0, Z0 @ psi) / z0
    # subharmonic peak at omega_D/2 <-> period doubling
    spectrum = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(auto)) ** 2
    return auto, spectrum
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Floquet eigenvalue problem and π-paired eigenstates

At the idealized point $g=1$, the global spin flip $P = \prod_j X_j$ implements the $\pi$-pulse while leaving $H_2$ invariant ($P H_2 P = H_2$, since $X_j Z_j X_j = -Z_j$ appears in pairs). In the MBL regime the eigenstates of $U_F$ organize into *doublets* [4]:

> **Theorem (π-pairing):** In the MBL-DTC phase, every Floquet eigenstate $\lvert u_\alpha\rangle$ with quasienergy $\epsilon_\alpha$ has a partner $\lvert u_{\bar\alpha}\rangle$ with quasienergy $\epsilon_\alpha + \pi/T$ (modulo $\omega_D$), related by the emergent Ising symmetry, with quasienergy splitting exponentially small in system size, $\lvert\epsilon_{\bar\alpha} - \epsilon_\alpha - \pi/T\rvert \sim e^{-L/\xi}$.

Each doublet comprises Schrödinger-cat superpositions of opposite magnetization patterns; one Floquet period swaps the members with a relative phase, so physical initial states return only after *two* periods. The subharmonic response is thus a spectroscopic signature of $\pi$-pairing, protected by MBL plus emergent Ising symmetry — detuning $g$ splits pairs only exponentially weakly. This is the *rigidity*.

In the l-bit basis $\{\tau_j^z\}$ the Floquet operator takes the canonical form

$$U_F \;\approx\; \tilde{P}\;\exp\!\Big(-i\sum_j \tilde{h}_j \tau_j^z - i\sum_{j<k}\tilde{J}_{jk}\,\tau_j^z\tau_k^z - \cdots\Big),$$

with dressed global flip $\tilde{P} = \prod_j \tau_j^x$.

### 4.2 Many-body localization as the rigidity mechanism

In a clean interacting Floquet system the drive resonantly couples quasienergy states and the system heats to infinite temperature. MBL defeats this through l-bit locality: matrix elements of the local drive between l-bit configurations are suppressed as $e^{-r/\xi}$, so resonant absorption is quenched and the system reaches a non-thermal Floquet steady state described by a periodic Gibbs ensemble of the emergent l-bit Hamiltonian [3].

The phase diagram of the driven Ising chain contains four principal phases as disorder strength $W$ and drive imperfection $g$ vary: a Floquet paramagnet, a Floquet spin glass (the $\pi$-spin-glass or DTC), a thermal phase at weak disorder, and — at high drive frequency — a prethermal DTC regime. The DTC-to-thermal transition at $g_c \approx 0.84$ [6] is a true eigenstate phase transition, diagnosed by finite-size scaling.

The drive protocol and its stability can be encoded as a formal specification. The following TLA+ sketch captures the binary drive and the period-doubling invariant checked by model checkers for small $L$:

```tla+

            /\ UNCHANGED << >>

            /\ UNCHANGED << >>

    (mag[n] = mag[n+2]) /\ (n % 2 = 1 => mag[n] = -mag[n+1])

```

### 4.3 The π-spin-glass: spatiotemporal long-range order made precise

The MBL-DTC is sometimes called the *$\pi$-spin-glass* to emphasize that it is characterized by *two* broken symmetries simultaneously [4]:

- **Spatial:** the Edwards–Anderson parameter $\chi_{SG} \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ signals Ising spin-glass order in every Floquet eigenstate.
- **Temporal:** the $\pi$-pairing of quasienergies means stroboscopic correlators oscillate at $\omega_D/2$.

Neither alone suffices: a driven paramagnet can show transient period doubling, and an undriven spin glass has no temporal structure. The DTC is the *intersection* — spatiotemporal long-range order — and it is absolutely stable in the following sense: any weak, $T$-periodic perturbation respecting the Ising symmetry cannot destroy the phase; it merely renormalizes the l-bits. Perturbations that *break* the Ising symmetry explicitly (e.g., a uniform longitudinal field in $H_1$) destroy the DTC — precisely as a uniform field destroys an equilibrium Ising ferromagnet. This symmetry-based stability criterion [4] is what elevates the DTC from dynamical curiosity to *phase of matter*.

The distinction between a true DTC and trivial period doubling can be stated as a decision procedure:

```haskell
-- Classify a driven spin chain's stroboscopic response
data Phase = DiscreteTimeCrystal | TrivialDoubling | Thermal | Paramagnet

classify :: Double -> Double -> Double -> Phase
classify g disorder driveFreq
  | peakRigid && eigenstateOrdered && allStates = DiscreteTimeCrystal
  | peakOnlyAtFineTuning                         = TrivialDoubling
  | disorder < wCritical                          = Thermal
  | otherwise                                     = Paramagnet
  where
    peakRigid       = subharmonicPeakWidth g < 1e-3
    eigenstateOrdered = edwardsAnderson disorder > 0.5
    allStates       = typicalityVerified driveFreq
    peakOnlyAtFineTuning = abs (g - 1.0) < 1e-9
    wCritical       = 2.5
```

### 4.4 Prethermal rigidity: time crystals without disorder

MBL is sufficient but not necessary. Else, Bauer, and Nayak proved a second route: at *high drive frequency*, heating is suppressed *exponentially* in the ratio of local energy scales to the drive frequency, $\tau_* \sim \exp(c\,\omega_D/J)$, even in clean systems [7]. In the Magnus expansion the effective Floquet Hamiltonian $H_F = H_F^{(0)} + H_F^{(1)} + \cdots$ has terms scaling as $(J/\omega_D)^n$: absorbing one drive quantum needs an $n \sim \omega_D/J$-body rearrangement of exponentially suppressed amplitude. Up to the exponentially long prethermal time $\tau_*$, dynamics are governed by a *static* effective Hamiltonian $H_{\text{eff}}$ that can itself spontaneously break emergent symmetries — including an emergent $\mathbb{Z}_2$ whose breaking yields a *prethermal* discrete time crystal (PDTC).

> **Theorem (Prethermalization, Else–Bauer–Nayak):** For a locally interacting Floquet system with drive frequency $\omega_D \gg J$ (local bandwidth), there exists a quasi-local unitary $\mathcal{U}$ and a time-independent $H_{\text{eff}}$ such that $U_F^{n} \approx \mathcal{U} e^{-iH_{\text{eff}}nT}\mathcal{U}^\dagger$ with error exponentially small in $\omega_D/J$, valid for times $t \lesssim \tau_* \sim \exp(\text{const}\times \omega_D/J)$. If $H_{\text{eff}}$ has a symmetry-broken phase, the driven system exhibits DTC order for all experimentally accessible times.

The PDTC differs from the MBL-DTC in experimentally testable ways, confirmed in a trapped-ion quantum simulator [8]: its lifetime is controlled by the *drive frequency* (higher $\omega_D$ → longer $\tau_*$), and order depends on the *energy density* of the initial state with respect to $H_{\text{eff}}$ (only states below its symmetry-breaking transition show period doubling), whereas the MBL-DTC shows order for *all* initial states. The prethermal route requires no disorder and operates in higher dimensions where MBL is destabilized by avalanches.

A systems-level view of the prethermal timescale hierarchy:

```rust
/// Prethermal DTC lifetime hierarchy: tau_pre << t_obs << tau_star
pub struct PrethermalRegime {
    pub local_bandwidth: f64,   // J
    pub drive_freq: f64,        // omega_D
}

impl PrethermalRegime {
    pub fn heating_time(&self) -> f64 {
        (0.5 * self.drive_freq / self.local_bandwidth).exp()
    }
    pub fn is_prethermal(&self, t_obs: f64) -> bool {
        let tau_pre = 1.0 / self.local_bandwidth;
        tau_pre < t_obs && t_obs < self.heating_time()
    }
    pub fn dtc_order_visible(&self, t_obs: f64, energy_density: f64, e_crit: f64) -> bool {
        self.is_prethermal(t_obs) && energy_density < e_crit
    }
}
```

### 4.5 Absolute stability and the role of symmetry

A persistent question is whether the DTC is "just" a fine-tuned resonance. The answer, developed across [3,4,7], has three layers:

1. **Within the MBL-DTC**, stability is *absolute* against symmetry-respecting perturbations: $\pi$-pairing cannot be removed without closing the paired quasienergy gap, which requires delocalization.
2. **The Ising symmetry is emergent**, not microscopic: even if the bare drive breaks it weakly, the dressed l-bit flip $\tilde{P}$ restores it at low energies, as emergent symmetries protect equilibrium topological phases.
3. **Long-range interactions** modify but need not destroy the phase: trapped-ion experiments [5,8] use power-law interactions $J_{ij} \sim |i-j|^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha \approx 1$, and DTC signatures persist, with strict MBL arguments replaced by slow-thermalization or prethermal ones.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Trapped ions: the first observation

In 2017, Zhang et al. realized the binary-drive protocol in a chain of up to 14 $^{171}$Yb$^+$ ions with programmable long-range Ising interactions [5]. From a Néel-ordered initial state they observed period-doubled magnetization persisting for $\sim$100 Floquet periods, with the subharmonic peak surviving detuning of the $\pi$-pulse and added interaction disorder — the two knobs matching the theoretical stability criteria exactly. The $\omega_D/2$ peak stands orders of magnitude above background, rigid against perturbations that would immediately destroy trivial period doubling.

### 5.2 Sycamore: eigenstate order on a quantum processor

The most stringent test to date was performed on Google's Sycamore processor with a 20-qubit chain implementing the disordered Ising Floquet model with *short-range* interactions [6], via a four-part protocol that has become the gold standard:

1. **Extended parameter stability:** period doubling mapped over a range of $g$, locating the transition at $g_c \approx 0.84$.
2. **Finite-size scaling:** varying $L$ toward the thermodynamic limit to confirm a true phase transition.
3. **Echo verification:** time-reversal separating *extrinsic* decoherence from *intrinsic* thermalization.
4. **Quantum typicality:** scrambled, highly-entangled initial states verifying period doubling for effectively *all* initial states.

This is the closest the field has come to a *proof by experiment* that the DTC is a phase of matter.

### 5.3 Prethermal DTC without disorder

Kyprianidis et al. observed the PDTC in a clean 25-ion simulator [8]: $\tau_*$ grows *exponentially* with $\omega_D$, and period doubling appears only for initial states below the ferromagnetic transition of $H_{\text{eff}}$ — validating prethermalization [7] as an independent, disorder-free route to temporal order.

### 5.4 Summary of the experimental record

| Platform | Year | Qubits/spins | Mechanism | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trapped ions ($^{171}$Yb$^+$) [5] | 2017 | 10–14 | MBL-like, long-range | First DTC; rigid subharmonic peak |
| NV centers in diamond | 2017 | $\sim$10$^6$ (ensemble) | Slow thermalization | DTC signatures in a dense dipolar system |
| NMR spins | 2018–2021 | ensemble | Prethermal/mean-field | Long-lived subharmonic response |
| Trapped ions, clean [8] | 2021 | 25 | Prethermal (no disorder) | $\tau_* \sim e^{c\omega_D/J}$; energy-density dependence |
| Sycamore processor [6] | 2022 | 20 | MBL, short-range | Eigenstate order; $g_c \approx 0.84$; typicality |

The convergence of theory and experiment is unusually tight: the binary-drive model proposed in [4] is essentially the model simulated in [6], and the measured critical point agrees with numerical finite-size scaling of the same Hamiltonian.

## 6 Limitations

**The heating problem is postponed, not solved.** Absolute stability of the MBL-DTC rests on MBL in the thermodynamic limit — itself debated, with avalanche arguments suggesting rare thermal regions may eventually thermalize the system in $d \geq 2$ and possibly $d=1$ at astronomically long times. If MBL is ultimately unstable, the MBL-DTC becomes an *exponentially long-lived* phenomenon rather than a true infinite-time phase.

**Prethermal order is transient by construction.** The PDTC lifetime, while exponential in $\omega_D/J$, is finite; the system eventually heats to infinite temperature. At modest $\omega_D/J$, $\tau_*$ can exceed experimental timescales by orders of magnitude, but the distinction matters for the foundations of the phase concept.

**Finite size and coherence.** Every experiment is finite; the Sycamore echo protocol [6] separates decoherence from thermalization, but 20 qubits extrapolated to the thermodynamic limit remains an extrapolation.

**Symmetry requirements are strict.** Explicit breaking of the Ising symmetry destroys the DTC; the phase is protected, but only within its symmetry class.

## 7 Conclusion

In a single decade the discrete time crystal traveled from forbidden equilibrium fantasy to laboratory-verified phase of matter. The no-go theorems [2] *sculpted* the idea, forcing the field to the precise viable setting: periodically driven systems, discrete symmetry breaking, non-equilibrium steady states stabilized by MBL [3,4] or prethermalization [7] — matched by experiments from trapped ions [5] through prethermal realizations [8] to eigenstate-resolved verification on a quantum processor [6].

Open frontiers include MBL and the MBL-DTC in two dimensions, driven topological phases enriched by time-translation symmetry, dissipative and measurement-stabilized time crystals, and DTC rigidity applied to quantum metrology and memory — a phase that ticks rigidly is a clock that cannot easily be stopped. Methodologically, the DTC shows the Landau paradigm, generalized to eigenstate order in driven systems, extends far beyond equilibrium: time can crystallize — not in the ground state, but in the non-equilibrium dance of a driven many-body system.
