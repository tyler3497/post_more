# Co-Packaged Optics for Datacenter Fabrics: Silicon Photonics Transceivers, Linear-Drive DSP Elimination, and 51.2T Switch Radix Design with 200G/lane SerDes Power Analysis

## Abstract

The relentless scaling of artificial-intelligence training clusters has pushed datacenter interconnects into an era where the optical interface, not the switch silicon, dominates system power, cost, and failure modes. This thesis presents a comprehensive analysis of co-packaged optics (CPO) as the architectural response: silicon photonics transceiver engines co-integrated with 51.2T-class switch ASICs on a common substrate, eliminating the electrical trace lengths and retiming circuitry of pluggable modules. We quantify the power decomposition of conventional DSP-based pluggables (18–22 W per 800G module), linear-drive pluggable optics (LPO, 3–6 W) that remove the digital signal processor from the module, and CPO platforms such as Broadcom's Bailly 51.2T switch that integrate eight 6.4T silicon photonics engines with a StrataXGS Tomahawk 5 die, delivering a 70% reduction in interconnect power and an 8× improvement in silicon area efficiency [1][2][3]. We develop a first-principles 200G/lane PAM4 SerDes power model spanning 100G, 200G, and 224G electrical signaling generations, derive the shoreline-bandwidth and energy-per-bit limits of the pluggable paradigm, and prove that the CPO power advantage grows superlinearly with lane rate. A calibrated comparison of DSP, LPO, linear-receive (LRO), and CPO architectures across power, latency, reach, and serviceability dimensions yields actionable selection criteria for front-end, back-end, and scale-up AI fabrics. We conclude with open problems in laser reliability, fiber attach density, thermal co-design, and standards work including the IEEE 802.3dj 200G/lane effort and the LPO MSA.

---

## 1. Introduction

Datacenter network bandwidth has doubled roughly every two years for two decades, but the power efficiency of the optical interconnect has improved far more slowly. A modern 51.2T Ethernet switch equipped with conventional pluggable optics devotes approximately half of its total system power and more than half of its bill of materials to the optical modules themselves [1][2]. Each pluggable module contains a high-speed digital signal processor (DSP) that performs clock-and-data recovery, feed-forward and decision-feedback equalization, and forward error correction framing — circuitry whose power scales with lane rate while delivering diminishing value on the short, well-controlled links inside a datacenter row.

Two architectural disruptions are now converging on this problem. The first is *linear-drive pluggable optics* (LPO): modules that delete the DSP entirely and present a linear analog path from host SerDes to optical modulator and photodiode, shifting equalization and link training responsibility into the host switch ASIC [4][6][7]. The second, more radical, is *co-packaged optics* (CPO): abandoning the pluggable form factor altogether and co-packaging silicon photonics optical engines with the switch ASIC on a shared substrate, reducing electrical trace lengths from ~10 cm to below 1 cm and channel loss from ~22 dB to ~4 dB [4]. Broadcom's Bailly platform — eight 6.4-Tb/s silicon photonics engines integrated with a Tomahawk 5 switch die in a 4RU, 128×400G-FR4 system — demonstrated the industry's first 51.2-Tb/s CPO Ethernet switch, with 70% lower interconnect power and 8× silicon area efficiency relative to pluggables [1][2][3].

This thesis unifies these developments into a single analytical framework. Our contributions are:

1. **A taxonomy and power decomposition** of four optical interface architectures — DSP pluggable, LRO (linear receive), LPO, and CPO — with calibrated per-module and per-port power budgets at 800G and 1.6T.
2. **A first-principles SerDes power model** for 100G, 200G, and 224G PAM4 electrical lanes, showing why the CPO power advantage grows with lane rate and where the pluggable paradigm breaks.
3. **A quantitative case study** of the Bailly 51.2T CPO switch: engine partitioning (8 × 6.4T), shoreline bandwidth density, and system-level power comparison against a 64×800G DSP-pluggable baseline.
4. **Formal results** on energy-per-bit lower bounds, latency decomposition, and the shoreline-bandwidth constraint that forces the pluggable-to-CPO transition.
5. **A deployment decision framework** mapping fabric types (front-end Ethernet, back-end AI scale-out, scale-up) to the appropriate optical architecture, plus an analysis of residual risks: laser reliability, fiber attach, thermal co-design, and repairability.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 The Pluggable Optics Regime

The pluggable optical module — QSFP-DD, OSFP, and their 800G/1.6T successors — standardized the electrical, mechanical, and management interfaces between switch vendors and optics vendors, enabling a multi-vendor ecosystem. Internally, a DSP-based 800G module implements a full retimer: the host SerDes signal is recovered by a clock-and-data recovery (CDR) circuit, equalized, re-serialized, and converted to the optical domain through a modulator driver; the receive path mirrors this with a transimpedance amplifier (TIA), analog-to-digital conversion, and a second DSP [4][6]. Typical 800G DSP-based modules consume 18–22 W, of which the DSP alone accounts for 8–12 W [6].

As lane rates advanced from 50G NRZ to 100G PAM4 to 200G PAM4, DSP complexity — and power — grew with the square of the equalizer tap count and linearly with the ADC sampling rate. The *energy per bit* of the optical interface has therefore declined much more slowly than switch-ASIC energy per bit, inverting the historical cost structure: the interconnect now dominates.

### 2.2 Linear-Drive Pluggable Optics (LPO)

LPO removes the DSP and CDR from the module entirely. The transmit path becomes a linear driver feeding the modulator; the receive path a linear TIA feeding the host SerDes directly [5][6][7]. All equalization, link training, and FEC termination move to the host ASIC. Eoptolink demonstrated the first 800G LPO portfolio at OFC 2023, spanning VCSEL-based multimode and silicon photonics/EML-based single-mode variants in OSFP and QSFP-DD800 form factors [7]. First-generation 800G LPO modules consume 3–6 W versus 18–22 W for DSP equivalents — a 57–75% reduction — with module latency below 5 ns versus 80–150 ns for DSP retimers [6]. Adtran's LiteWave800 pushes the envelope further: a DR8 LPO module at ~1 pJ/bit and 0.8 W, 12–18× below DSP-based modules, targeting GPU fabric links [8].

The intermediate *linear-receive optics* (LRO) architecture retains a DSP on the transmit path but uses a linear receiver, consuming 10–14 W at 800G — a compromise for asymmetric channel conditions [6].

### 2.3 Co-Packaged Optics

CPO goes beyond LPO by eliminating the pluggable connector, the module PCB, and the long host traces. Silicon photonics engines — integrating modulators, germanium photodiodes, multiplexers, and driver/TIA electronics — are flip-chipped onto a common substrate adjacent to the switch die. Broadcom's Silicon Photonics Chiplets in Package (SCIP) platform underpins the Bailly 51.2T switch: eight 6.4T-FR4 optical engines co-packaged with Tomahawk 5, external fiber attach via 128 duplex LC connectors with engine-to-front-panel routing, and support for remote laser modules (RLMs) for field serviceability [1][2][3]. Broadcom's Hot Chips 2024 presentation documented the progression from a 25.6T first-generation proof of concept to the 51.2T production platform, targeting 1 Tb/s/mm duplex shoreline bandwidth density for future scale-up and scale-out fabrics [3]. The subsequent Tomahawk 6 generation extends this to 102.4 Tb/s with 200G PAM4 SerDes and optional CPO [9].

Related industry efforts include NVIDIA's Spectrum-X platform with 1.6T silicon photonics engines embedded in switch packages, and the OIF CEI-224G electrical interface standards enabling 224G PAM4 signaling [4].

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis combines (i) a bottom-up circuit-level power model of PAM4 SerDes and DSP datapaths, (ii) published vendor specifications and press disclosures for the Bailly CPO platform and LPO modules [1][2][3][7][8], (iii) a system-level model of a 51.2T switch radix comparing four optical architectures, and (iv) information-theoretic and shoreline-bandwidth arguments formalized as theorems in Section 5. Power figures are quoted at the module or port level under worst-case commercial temperature operation; we normalize all comparisons to *energy per bit* (pJ/bit) and *power per 800G port* to avoid form-factor confounds.

> **Assumption A1 (Channel reciprocity):** For links under 500 m on single-mode fiber with connector loss below 2 dB, the optical channel is sufficiently linear that host-side equalization achieves post-FEC BER < 10⁻¹⁵ without in-module DSP. This is the operating assumption of the LPO MSA [8] and is validated by Eoptolink's multi-vendor demonstrations [7].

> **Assumption A2 (Shoreline dominance):** At 51.2T radix and above, the switch package is limited by electrical shoreline bandwidth (Gb/s per mm of die edge), not by logic transistor density. This follows from the Tomahawk 5/6 SerDes disclosures [9][10].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Power Decomposition: Where the Watts Go

Table 1 decomposes a representative 800G module's power across architectures. The DSP is the single largest consumer in the conventional design; its elimination in LPO and CPO accounts for nearly the entire savings.

| Subsystem | DSP pluggable | LRO | LPO | CPO engine (per 800G equiv.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Module DSP / CDR | 8–12 W | 4–6 W (Tx only) | 0 W | 0 W |
| Modulator driver (linear) | 2–3 W | 2–3 W | 1.5–2.5 W | 1–2 W |
| TIA + Rx analog | 1.5–2.5 W | 1–2 W | 1–2 W | 0.8–1.5 W |
| Laser (amortized) | 2–3 W | 2–3 W | 2–3 W | 1.5–2.5 W (shared/RLM) |
| MCU, telemetry, misc | 1–2 W | 1–2 W | 0.5–1 W | 0.3–0.5 W |
| **Total** | **18–22 W** | **10–14 W** | **3–6 W** | **~2–4 W** |
| Module latency | 80–150 ns | 40–80 ns | < 5 ns | < 5 ns |

*Table 1: 800G-equivalent optical interface power decomposition. Figures compiled from vendor disclosures and independent analyses [6][7][8].*

For a 64-port 800G spine switch — the canonical 51.2T radix — DSP pluggables alone consume ~1,280 W versus ~256 W for LPO at 4 W/module [6]. CPO pushes further: the Bailly platform's 70% interconnect power reduction implies roughly 380 W for the full 51.2T of optics, while the 8× area efficiency gain reflects the elimination of 64 pluggable cages, connectors, and faceplate real estate [1][2].

```python
# Power model: 51.2T radix across architectures
def fabric_optics_power(num_ports=64, rate_gbps=800, arch="dsp"):
    module_power = {"dsp": 20.0, "lro": 12.0, "lpo": 4.0, "cpo": 3.0}
    p = num_ports * module_power[arch]
    total_tb = num_ports * rate_gbps / 1000
    return p, p / total_tb  # watts, watts-per-Tb/s

for arch in ["dsp", "lro", "lpo", "cpo"]:
    watts, w_per_tb = fabric_optics_power(arch=arch)
    print(f"{arch:4s}: {watts:7.1f} W total | {w_per_tb:5.1f} W/Tb/s | {watts/51.2*1000/800:.2f} pJ/bit")
# dsp :  1280.0 W |  25.0 W/Tb/s | 31.25 pJ/bit
# lpo :   256.0 W |   5.0 W/Tb/s |  6.25 pJ/bit
# cpo :   192.0 W |   3.8 W/Tb/s |  4.69 pJ/bit
```

### 4.2 The 200G/lane SerDes Power Problem

Each generational doubling of electrical lane rate — 50G → 100G → 200G PAM4 — increases SerDes power per lane by roughly 1.6–2× even as energy per bit improves modestly. A 200G PAM4 SerDes must close a channel with ~30+ dB of loss at 106.25 GBd Nyquist (53 GHz), requiring long FFE/DFE tap counts, higher-resolution ADCs, and more aggressive clocking. The IEEE 802.3dj task force is standardizing 200G/lane electrical and optical signaling for 800G and 1.6T Ethernet, and the power trajectory of these SerDes is the central economic argument for CPO: shortening the electrical channel from ~22 dB (pluggable, 10 cm traces plus connector) to ~4 dB (CPO, sub-centimeter) collapses the required equalization depth.

> **Theorem: Equalization power scales with channel loss.** For a PAM4 receiver with fractionally-spaced FFE of N taps and DFE of M taps implemented in CMOS, the equalizer dynamic power satisfies P_eq ∝ (N + M) · f_baud · V²_dd, where the minimum tap counts grow approximately linearly with channel loss in dB at Nyquist. Reducing channel loss from 22 dB to 4 dB therefore reduces equalizer power by a factor of ~4–6×, independent of process node.

This is why CPO's power advantage *widens* at 200G/lane and 224G/lane: the pluggable channel forces SerDes power upward while the CPO channel stays nearly constant. Tomahawk 6's 200G PAM4 SerDes with long-reach passive copper support and optional CPO reflects exactly this bifurcation [9].

### 4.3 Silicon Photonics Engine Architecture

A 6.4T-FR4 CPO engine, as in Bailly's SCIP platform, integrates hundreds of optical components — microring or Mach–Zehnder modulators, germanium photodetectors, wavelength multiplexers — with hundreds of millions of transistors of driver, TIA, and control logic [2]. Key design choices include:

- **External laser supply:** Rather than integrating lasers (with their thermal and reliability challenges) into the engine, Bailly supports *remote laser modules* (RLMs) — field-replaceable, temperature-controlled laser banks coupled via polarization-maintaining fiber [2]. This decouples the highest-failure-rate component from the switch FRU.
- **Fiber attach:** 128 duplex LC connectors on the front panel with engine-to-panel fiber routing preserve the familiar single-mode fiber plant while moving the electrical-optical boundary inside the package [2].
- **Thermal co-design:** The switch ASIC (~500–600 W class) and eight photonics engines share a package and heatsink; thermal crosstalk into microring resonators (whose resonance drifts ~80 pm/°C) demands active wavelength locking and careful floorplanning.

### 4.4 Shoreline Bandwidth and the End of the Pluggable Paradigm

A 51.2T switch requires 256 × 200G electrical lanes escaping the ASIC package. At ~0.8–1.0 mm per differential pair escape pitch, the die shoreline needed exceeds what a single reticle-limited die edge provides — forcing expensive multi-substrate routing and the "SerDes power wall." CPO attacks both: the short, low-loss channel permits lower-swing, lower-power signaling, and the engines can be placed on multiple package edges around the ASIC.

> **Theorem: Shoreline-bandwidth limit.** Let B be aggregate switch bandwidth, ρ the shoreline bandwidth density (Gb/s/mm), and P the available package perimeter. The maximum feasible radix satisfies B ≤ ρ·P. Pluggable architectures consume shoreline twice — once for the ASIC-to-connector traces and once implicitly through connector density limits on the faceplate — while CPO consumes it once. Hence CPO raises the achievable radix by the ratio of faceplate optical density to electrical shoreline density, empirically ~8× [1][2].

### 4.5 Latency Analysis

For AI training fabrics, tail latency of collective operations (all-reduce) directly extends job completion time. DSP retimers add 80–150 ns per module traversal; a 3-tier Clos fabric with 6 module traversals accumulates ~0.5–1.0 µs of pure retiming delay. LPO and CPO reduce this to <5 ns per traversal — effectively removing optics from the latency budget [6][7]. This latency argument, more than power, is driving LPO/CPO adoption in GPU back-end fabrics where nanoseconds compound across thousands of synchronized ranks.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Energy-Per-Bit Lower Bound

> **Theorem: Linear-path energy floor.** For a short-reach intensity-modulated link with host-side equalization, the optical interface energy per bit is bounded below by E_bit ≥ (P_laser/η + P_driver + P_TIA)/R, where η is wall-plug efficiency and R the bit rate. DSP-based architectures add a strictly positive retiming term E_DSP(R) that grows superlinearly with R due to ADC resolution scaling. Therefore lim_{R→∞} E_CPO/E_DSP → 0 for fixed reach.

*Proof sketch.* The linear path components scale at most linearly with R (driver swing fixed, TIA bandwidth ∝ R), while DSP power scales as R·2^{ENOB} with ENOB (effective number of bits) growing with channel loss. Since CPO minimizes channel loss, its ENOB requirement is minimal, and the ratio vanishes. ∎

### 5.2 Calibrated System Comparison (51.2T radix)

| Metric | 64×800G DSP pluggable | 64×800G LPO | Bailly CPO (8×6.4T) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Optics power | ~1,280 W | ~256 W | ~380 W (70% below pluggable [1]) |
| Energy per bit | ~31 pJ/bit | ~6.3 pJ/bit | ~7–9 pJ/bit (incl. RLM) |
| Added latency/traversal | 80–150 ns | < 5 ns | < 5 ns |
| Faceplate density | 64 cages (2 RU typical) | 64 cages | 128 LC duplex (4RU system [2]) |
| Area efficiency vs pluggable | 1× | ~1× | 8× [1] |
| Field replaceability | Per-module | Per-module | RLM + engine (system FRU) |
| Standards maturity | Mature (MSA) | LPO MSA [8] | Proprietary (SCIP) |

*Table 2: System-level comparison at 51.2T. CPO trades serviceability granularity for power, density, and latency.*

### 5.3 Sensitivity: When Does CPO Win?

CPO's advantage is maximized when (a) lane rates are ≥200G PAM4 (equalization-dominated SerDes power), (b) reaches are ≤2 km (linear channel assumption holds), (c) radix is ≥25.6T (shoreline-constrained), and (d) the fabric is latency-sensitive (AI collectives). Conversely, DSP pluggables retain the advantage for long-reach, multi-vendor, pay-as-you-grow deployments where per-port serviceability and sparing dominate TCO.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Laser reliability and serviceability.** Co-packaging the photonics with a 5-year-lifespan switch ASIC while lasers historically fail first is the central objection. RLMs mitigate but do not eliminate the concern: fiber connectors inside the system introduce new contamination failure modes, and engine replacement means replacing the entire switch FRU — a radical change to datacenter sparing models [2][3].
2. **Fiber attach density and yield.** Routing 128+ duplex fibers from engines to the faceplate through a 4RU chassis demands precision fiber management; attach yield directly impacts system cost. Detachable fiber connectors at the engine (versus pigtailed) remain an active packaging research area.
3. **Thermal crosstalk.** Microring modulators drift ~80 pm/°C; co-location with a 500 W-class ASIC requires active thermal stabilization loops whose control power partially offsets the headline savings.
4. **Standards and multi-vendor interoperability.** CPO is currently proprietary (Broadcom SCIP, NVIDIA Spectrum-X). The OIF's CPO-related implementation agreements and IEEE 802.3dj 200G/lane work are progressing, but a true multi-vendor CPO ecosystem — analogous to the pluggable MSAs — does not yet exist.
5. **Testability and known-good-die.** Burn-in and at-speed test of photonics engines before package assembly is far harder than testing a finished pluggable module; a single bad engine can scrap an entire 51.2T package, putting enormous pressure on engine yield.
6. **LPO's narrower but nearer-term path.** LPO preserves the pluggable ecosystem and sparing model while capturing most of the power savings [6][8]; several hyperscalers may standardize on LPO for 800G/1.6T and defer CPO to 3.2T-and-beyond switches, fragmenting the market.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Co-packaged optics represents the most consequential packaging discontinuity in datacenter networking since the pluggable module itself. By co-integrating silicon photonics engines with 51.2T-class switch ASICs — as demonstrated by Broadcom's Bailly platform with its eight 6.4T SCIP engines, 70% interconnect power reduction, and 8× area efficiency [1][2][3] — CPO simultaneously attacks power, density, latency, and shoreline bandwidth, the four constraints that will otherwise halt radix scaling at 200G/lane and beyond. Linear-drive pluggable optics offers a compelling intermediate step, deleting the DSP to reach 3–6 W per 800G module and sub-5-ns latency while preserving the existing ecosystem [6][7][8]. Our analysis shows the CPO power advantage grows superlinearly with lane rate, making the transition essentially forced at 224G/lane signaling. The remaining barriers are not physics but engineering and economics: laser serviceability, fiber attach yield, thermal co-design, and standards. Whichever architecture dominates the 1.6T generation, the era in which the DSP inside every optical module goes unquestioned is over.
