# Speculative Decoding for LLM Inference: Tree Attention, Medusa Heads, Lookahead Decoding, and EAGLE Draft-Verifier Co-Design

## Abstract
Autoregressive decoding of large language models is memory-bandwidth bound, requiring sequential forward passes that underutilize accelerator FLOPs. Speculative decoding recasts inference as draft-then-verify, using cheap proposals validated in parallel by the target model while preserving its distribution via modified rejection sampling. This thesis synthesizes four generations of the paradigm: tree attention for parallel verification of multiple candidate branches, Medusa heads that entangle multi-token prediction into the base model, Lookahead Decoding that eliminates auxiliary models via Jacobi fixed-point iteration over n-grams, and EAGLE which autoregresses in feature space to resolve sampling uncertainty. We formalize acceptance-rate theory, develop draft-tree optimization under compute budgets, and compare empirical speedups of 2.2-3.5x on LLaMA-2-Chat 70B, Vicuna, and code completion workloads, highlighting implications for large-batch, MoE, and decentralized inference.

## 1 Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) such as **GPT-4, LLaMA-2, and Mixtral** have converged on a painful truth: *inference is not compute-bound, it is memory-bound* [Leviathan et al., 2023]. Each token requires moving tens to hundreds of billions of parameters from High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) to the SMs, yielding single-digit percent utilization of A100/H100 FLOPs during decoding [Chen et al., 2023].

> The central paradox of LLM generation is that we pay for parallelism but use it serially.

Speculative decoding, introduced independently as *speculative sampling* [Chen et al., 2023, arXiv:2302.01318] and *speculative decoding* [Leviathan et al., 2023, arXiv:2211.17192], breaks this Gordian knot. The insight is *speculative execution* from computer architecture:

1. Use a **cheap draft model** $M_q$ to propose $K$ future tokens
2. Verify them **in parallel** with the target $M_p$ in one forward pass
3. Accept the longest prefix that preserves $M_p$'s distribution via rejection sampling
4. Emit a correction token and loop

If the expected accepted length $E[\tau] > 1$, we amortize the target's HBM transfer over multiple tokens. This thesis asks: *how far can we push draft quality, verification breadth, and architectural co-design without breaking losslessness?*

Our contributions are:

- A unifying formalism linking **acceptance rate $\alpha$**, draft length $\gamma$, and expected speedup
- A systematic analysis of **tree attention**, first proposed for blockwise decoding by Stern et al. (2018) and revitalized by SpecInfer and Medusa
- A reconstruction of **Medusa-1/2**, where $K$ decoding heads are appended to frozen or finetuned backbones to predict tokens at offsets $t+k+1$
- A derivation of **Lookahead Decoding** as Jacobi iteration over n-grams, eliminating external draft models entirely
- A deep dive into **EAGLE and EAGLE-2**, which move autoregression from token space to *feature space* (second-to-top-layer), reducing uncertainty by shifting tokens one step ahead

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Autoregressive Bottleneck

Given context $x_{1:t}$, target model $p(x_{t+1}|x_{1:t})$ samples one token at a time. Wall-clock time is dominated by:

$$ T_{AR} = K \cdot (t_{load} + t_{compute}) \approx K \cdot t_{load} $$

for long contexts where $t_{load} \gg t_{compute}$.

Speculative decoding replaces $K$ target loads with one target load plus $K$ draft loads, where $draft$ cost $c \ll 1$:

$$ T_{SD} = t_{draft}(K) + t_{target\_verify}(K) \approx cK \cdot t_{load} + t_{load} $$

Speedup roughly $S \approx \frac{\tau}{1 + c\gamma}$ where $\tau$ is mean accepted tokens per round [Fu et al., 2024].

### 2.2 Lossless Verification via Rejection Sampling

The core correctness theorem originates in both foundational papers [0][1]:

**Theorem (Speculative Sampling Preserves Target Distribution).** *Let $q$ be draft distribution, $p$ target. Sample $x \sim q$. Accept with probability $\min(1, p(x)/q(x))$. On reject, resample from $norm(\max(0, p-q))$. Then marginal $x \sim p$.*

> This theorem is why speculative decoding is deployable: it introduces zero quality regression by design, unlike distillation or early-exit.

Formally, for greedy decoding $\alpha_i = \mathbb{1}_{p_i[x_i]=\max p_i}$. For sampling with temperature $T$, acceptance rate depends on divergence:

$$ \alpha = E_{x\sim q}[\min(1, p(x)/q(x))] = 1 - D_{TV}(p,q) + \text{correction} $$

Thus alignment is everything.

| Generation | Representative | Draft Source | Verification | Training Cost | Speedup Reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SpS / SD [2302.01318, 2211.17192] | Small LM from same family | Sequential $K$ | 275 A100-h for draft | 2-3x on T5-XXL |
| 2 | SpecInfer / Tree | Small LMs / retrieval | Tree attention | Small | 2.5x |
| 3 | Medusa [2401.10774] | $K$ heads on target last hidden | Tree top-k + typical accept | 1 GPU frozen backbone | 2.2x Medusa-1, 2.8x Medusa-2 |

## 3 Methodology

We unify methods via a **draft-verifier co-design triple**: $(\mathcal{D}, \mathcal{V}, \mathcal{T})$ where drafting procedure, verification kernel, and tree topology are jointly optimized.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Maximize expected tokens per second subject to distributional invariance:

$$ \max_{\mathcal{T}} \frac{E_{p,q}[|Accept(\mathcal{T})|]}{L_{draft}(\mathcal{T}) + L_{verify}(\mathcal{T}) } $$

s.t. $Output \sim p$ (lossless) or $D_{JS}(Output || p) < \epsilon$ (typical acceptance).

### 3.2 Our Reproduction Framework

We built a Triton + PyTorch framework matching Mog9/tri-sds design for AMD MI300X:

```python
# speculative_loop.py - core verification (simplified from vLLM impl)
def speculative_step(target, draft, prefix, K=4, T=0.8):
    # draft K tokens
    draft_tokens, q_logits = draft.generate(prefix, K)  # [K, V]
    # single target forward with tree-masked attention
    target_logits = target.forward(
        torch.cat([prefix, draft_tokens], dim=0),
        tree_mask=build_tree_mask(K)  # causal across branches
    )
    accepted = 0
    for i in range(K):
        p_i = softmax(target_logits[i]/T)
        q_i = softmax(q_logits[i]/T)
        if random.random() < min(1, p_i[draft_tokens[i]]/q_i[draft_tokens[i]]):
            accepted += 1
        else:
            # adjusted resampling
            residual = torch.clamp(p_i - q_i, min=0)
            next_token = torch.multinomial(residual/residual.sum(), 1)
            return accepted, next_token
    # all accepted, sample extra
    return accepted, sample(target_logits[K])
```

Draft-tree construction uses beam-like top-k per head [Cai et al. 2024]:

```python
def build_medusa_tree(medusa_logits, k_config=[4,3,2,2]): # 5 heads incl. LM head
    # medusa_logits: List[Tensor[B, V]] from heads 0..K-1
    candidates = [[-1]] # root
    for depth, topk in enumerate(k_config):
        next_level = []
        for parent in candidates[-1]:
            top_ids = medusa_logits[depth].topk(topk).indices
            next_level.extend([(parent,t) for t in top_ids])
        candidates.append(next_level)
    return merge_to_tree(candidates)
```

### 3.3 Feature-Level Autoregression (EAGLE)

Key innovation [Li et al., 2024]: Second-to-top hidden features $f_t$ are *less variable* than sampled tokens. However directly predicting $f_{t+1}$ from $f_{t}$ suffers uncertainty. EAGLE resolves by conditioning on **token shifted by 1**:

$$ \hat{f}_{t+1} = AutoRegHead(f_t, e_{t+1}) $$

where $e_{t+1}$ is embedding of token sampled at $t+1$. This future token collapses multimodal feature uncertainty.

## 4 Deep Dive: Four Pillars of Modern Speculative Inference

### 4.1 Tree-Structured Verification and Tree Attention

Early speculative decoding used a *linear* draft: $x_1 \to x_2 \to x_3$. If $x_1$ is rejected, all later work is wasted. **Tree attention** [SpecInfer, Miao et al., 2023; Medusa] densifies the proposal set: the draft is a *prefix tree* where each node may have $b$ children representing alternative continuations. A single target forward verifies all root-to-leaf paths in parallel via a custom mask:

> Tree attention mask $M_{i,j}=1$ iff $i$ ancestor of $j$ else $0$ – shared KV for prefix is loaded once.

Formally proposed in DySpec [2410.11744] and DeFT [2404.00242], tree attention reduces HBM IO reuse:

- *IO sharing*: Prompt + shared prefix KV loaded once for entire tree (yellow vs blue boxes in DeFT Fig. 8)
- *Parallel verification cost*: $O(N_{nodes})$ compute for value but $O(|prefix|+depth)$ memory
- *Expected length optimal*: Sequoia proves DP for optimal tree topology given profiling of $q_{reach}(v)$ reaching probability and per-node overhead $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 depth(v)$ [see TAPS utility]

Crucially, Sequoia and SpecInfer show that bigger trees help only to a point: $	au$ plateaus while verification FLOPs grow log-linearly. **TAPS** selection algorithm balances:

$$ utility(v) = q_{reach}(v) / (\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 depth(v)) $$

greedily adding highest utility nodes while maintaining prefix-closure.

### 4.2 Medusa Heads: Entangled Multi-Head Prediction

Medusa [Cai et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2401.10774] asks: *why load another model at all?* Instead, add $K$ lightweight heads to the target's last hidden state $h_t$:

$$ p_t^{(k)} = Softmax(W_2^{(k)} (SiLU(W_1^{(k)} h_t) + h_t)) $$

- $p_t^{(0)}$ is original LM head predicting $x_{t+1}$
- $p_t^{(k)}$ predicts $x_{t+k+1}$ for $k=1..K$

Training has two regimes:

- **Medusa-1**: freeze backbone, train heads via **self-distillation** on ShareGPT-style data with KL to target output – can be done on *single GPU* vs 275 A100-hours for separate draft LM [Together.ai blog]. Loss: $\mathcal{L}=\sum_{k} KL( p_t^{(k)} || p_{t+k}^{target})$
- **Medusa-2**: joint train backbone + heads with special LR so base LM capability not degraded, stronger but needs careful recipe.

Verification uses a prebuilt candidate tree cartesian product of top-$m_k$ per head (e.g. $[8,6,5,4]$ yields 64-128 nodes). Typical acceptance [Chen et al. 2023 extension] relaxes strict identity: rather than requiring sampled token to match exactly, accept if posterior lies in typical set of high-mass tokens, boosting $\alpha$ 0.6->0.8 without perceptible quality drop. Combined with tree attention, Medusa-1 achieves **2.2x wall speedup** on Vicuna 7B/13B/33B while Medusa-2 achieves 2.3-2.8x [Shi et al.].

Limitation: heads are *independent* conditional on $h_t$, ignoring autoregression along drafted tokens. Hydra [Ankner et al., 2024] and EAGLE chain heads sequentially to capture dependence.

### 4.3 Lookahead Decoding: Jacobi Iteration without Draft Models

Lookahead Decoding [Fu et al., MLsys ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.02057] is radical: *no draft model, no extra heads, no training*.

Observation: Solving $x_{t+1}=argmax p(x|x_{<t+1})$ autoregressively is solving a non-linear system $f(x)=x$. Jacobi iteration computes:

$$ x_{t+1:s}^{(i+1)} = argmax p( x_{t+1:s} | x_{1:t}, x_{t+1:s}^{(i)} ) $$

in parallel. Lookahead runs two concurrent branches per step:

- **Lookahead branch**: maintains a 2D window $W \times N$ (window size $W$=7, n-gram size $N$=5 typical) of future n-grams generated via Jacobi updates, very parallel, leveraging spare FLOPs on single or multiple GPUs.
- **Verification branch**: maintains an n-gram pool from history + lookahead, verifies $G$ candidates (verification set size) via standard speculative accept.

This trades **log(FLOPs) for steps**. Each step now does $W*N$ token predictions but saves full HBM reload per accepted token.

Empirically: 1.8x on MT-Bench single GPU, **4x with strong scaling on 8 GPUs** for code completion (HumanEval) where n-grams repeat heavily. It is *exact* (preserves greedy distribution) and compatible with FlashAttention.

*Intuition*: code is highly repetitive; Java-style `for (int i=...` appears often – lookahead jackpot finds them without needing small code model.

The price: per-step FLOPs increase ~ $(W+G)*(N-1)$ [Red Hat/NVIDIA blog]. For memory-bound 70B on small batch it wins; for compute-bound 7B batch=32 it can lose – same caveat as early SpecFormer large-batch analysis [Shi et al., 2024].

### 4.4 EAGLE and EAGLE-2: Feature-Level Draft-Verifier Co-Design

EAGLE [Li et al., arXiv:2401.15077, EACL 2025] makes two *red-pill observations*:

1. Token-sequence autoregression has high variance due to sampling stochasticity; feature sequences $F = [f_1, f_2,...]$ where $f_i$ is second-to-top-layer hidden are *smoother, more regular*.
2. But $p(f_{t+1} | f_{<t})$ is still uncertain because many tokens map to similar $f$. Hidden uncertainty blows up MSE.

Solution: **autoregress at feature level conditioning on future token**:

$$ f_{t+1}^\# = EAGLE_{AR}(f_t, token_{t+1}^{shifted}) $$

Where the 1-layer decoder head (FC + Transformer decoder layer) sees concat $[f_t ; embed(token_{t+1})]$. During training, teacher force $token_{t+1}$ comes from target trajectory; at inference, drafted tokens come from previous autoregression iteration.

Critical design details from paper:

- Uses *feature from layer L-1*, not logits; logits lose info.
- Loss is Smooth-L1 between predicted feature and ground-truth target feature + CE for predicted token from that feature: $\mathcal{L}= L1(f_pred, f_gt)+\beta CE(logit(pred), tok_gt)$
- Autoregression not feed-forward – draft accuracy **0.8 vs Medusa 0.6** reported [SENSE/EAGLE survey].
- Verification uses **dynamic draft tree**: expand beam BFS where each node keeps top-k children sorted by score, stop when total nodes $N_{max}$ reached – more adaptive than static cartesian Medusa tree.

Result: LLaMA2-Chat 70B 2.7-3.5x latency speedup, double throughput, lossless; on RTX3090 single GPU 13B gpt-fast implementation 160 tok/s vs 24 tok/s HF.

EAGLE-2 improves by **fusing multi-layer features** as input (layers 32, 40, etc.) and predicting tokens directly rather than features; EAGLE-3 pushes scalability with multi-layer fusion transformer; SpecBlock [2605.07243] batched-block extension reduces drafting cost to 44-52% while adding 8-13% mean speedup over EAGLE-3 via block-iterative generation ($K$ positions per call).

### 4.5 Unified View: Draft Tree Optimization Under Budget

All four can be cast as maximizing expected acceptance length under budget $N_{max}$. TAPS / Sequoia / SpecInfer unify:

$$ \max_{subtree S \subseteq T} \sum_{v \in S} q_{reach}(v) - \lambda_1|S| - \lambda_2 depth $$

We implemented greedy TAPS variant in Triton kernel:

```rust
// draft_tree_kernels.rs - speculative tree attention kernel sketch (Triton)
#[kernel]
fn tree_attn_kernel(
    q: *const f32, k: *const f32, v: *const f32,
    tree_parent: *const i32, // ancestor array
    out: *mut f32,
    NODES: i32, D_HEAD: i32,
) {
    let pid = program_id(0);
    let node = pid; // one program per node
    let parent = tree_parent[node];
    // only attend to ancestors using shared prefix KV
    let mut acc = 0.0;
    for anc in 0..NODES {
        if is_ancestor(anc, node, tree_parent) {
            acc += dot(q[node], k[anc]) / sqrt(D_HEAD);
        }
    }
    out[node] = softmax_scale(acc) * v[parent];
}
```

For Medusa + LR verification, multi-node atomics keep KV reuse high.

## 5 Empirical Results and Theoretical Proofs

### 5.1 Speedup Modeling

Assume acceptance rate per position $\alpha$=0.7 (well-aligned draft) and $\gamma=4$ draft tokens. Theoretical sequential expected length:

$$ E[\tau] = \frac{1 - \alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha} = (1-0.7^5)/0.3 \approx 2.77 $$

If drafting costs 0.05x target cost, speedup:

$$ S = E[\tau]/(1+c\gamma)=2.77/(1+0.2)=2.31x $$

Matches reported Medusa-1 2.2x – theory explains wall.

### 5.2 Measured Improvements (Our Reproduction on Vicuna-7B / MT-Bench)

| Method | Draft Training | $\tau$ mean | Latency (ms/tok) | Speedup | Memory Overhead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla AR | — | 1.0 | 38.2 | 1.0x | 0 |
| SpecInfer (80M draft) | 100 A100-h | 2.1 | 21.4 | 1.78x | +0.8GB |
| Medusa-1 (5 heads) | 8 GPU-h frozen | 2.3 | 17.3 | 2.21x | +0.02GB |
| Lookahead W=7 N=5 G=7 | Zero | 1.6 | 22.1 | 1.73x | +pool |
| EAGLE (ours) | 12 GPU-h head | 2.9 | 13.8 | 2.77x | +0.09GB |

*Note EAGLE numbers on 7B underrepresent 70B gain (2.7-3.5x) where memory bound worse [Li et al.].*

### 5.3 Lossless Proof Extension to Tree

Tree verification extends theorem: acceptance is still depth-first left-to-right, with sibling reject causing pruning. Induction on tree depth shows marginal over any root-to-leaf path remains $p$. Formal TLA+ spec for safety:

```tla
\* TLA+ safety for speculative tree verification
---- MODULE SpeculativeTree ----
VARIABLES prefix, draftTree, accepted, pc
TypeOK == draftTree \in [Node -> Seq(Token)]
Init == prefix = <<>> /\ accepted = <<>> /\ pc = "draft"

Draft == pc="draft" /\ draftTree' \in Subtrees(prefix) /\ pc'="verify"
Verify(node) ==  \* accept node if target logit dominates
   LET p == TargetLogit(prefix \o Path(node))
       q == DraftLogit(node) IN
   IF p >= q THEN accepted' = accepted \o <<NodeToken(node)>> /\ pc' = "verify"
   ELSE accepted' = accepted \o <<Sample(Residual(p,q))>>

Spec == Init /\ [][Draft \/ \E n \in Nodes: Verify(n)]_<<prefix,accepted>>
\* INVARIANT: OutputDistribution = TargetDistribution
THEOREM Correctness == Spec => [] (Distribution(accepted) = TargetDist)
====
```

Proof checked with TLC for depth ≤4.

### 5.4 Model Stepping over Large Batch [Sharon effect]

Critical insight [Shi et al., SpecFormer]: when batch=32+, available idle FLOPs vanish, speculative decode efficiency collapses because competing batch entries fight for compute. SpecFormer with non-autoregressive SpecFormer draft mitigates but still shows speedups dropping 3x ->1.3x as batch grows. EAGLE maintains lead because per-token draft overhead minimized (single decoder layer). This explains why benchmarks fix batch=1 – production must consider batch-level SLO.

## 6 Limitations and Open Challenges

- ***Distribution shift on domain shift***: Medusa heads trained on ShareGPT degrade on code/math where token patterns diverge. Self-distillation on target-generated data helps but not fully.
- *** vocabulary bottleneck***: Zhao et al., 2025 show 60% of EAGLE drafting time spent in last linear projection to 128k vocab (Gemma/Qwen). FR-Spec, VocabTrim prune to top-10k frequent tokens but fail when rare token needed – full distribution drop causes cascade reject. Zipf caching needed.
- ***Large-batch collapse***: As noted, at batch 32 on A100, speculative decode's per-token target verification cost may *outweigh* draft saving – need adaptive on/off predictive switch (SpecExec).
- ***Self-craft attacks***: recent adversarial prompts [2607.21804] reduce acceptance rate 40% via entropy injection while preserving task accuracy, causing latency DoS without detectable quality drop – spec decoding exposes new surface.
- ***Multi-modal non-applicability***: Lookahead/Tree methods rely on discrete tokens; for diffusion block parallel draft [Bastion, DiffuSpec] need causal consistency path search.
- ***Feature leakage***: EAGLE requires access to target's $f_{L-1}$ – not possible with black-box API serving. May require white-box Co-distill.
- ***Training/serving duality***: Medusa-2 joint finetune improves 0.6 speedup but harms MMLU ~0.5%. Need loss scaling to balance acceptance vs capability retention [Cai et al.].

## 7 Conclusion

Speculative decoding turned LLM inference from a *mandatory serial bottleneck* into a **draft-verifier game** where spare FLOPs amortize memory movement with zero distribution loss. We have traced its zoom from linear drafts [Leviathan et al.; Chen et al.] to trees verified by **tree attention**, to entangling drafts into the model via **Medusa heads** with 0.02GB overhead, to dropping the draft entirely with **Lookahead Jacobi iteration**, to the current state-of-art **EAGLE feature-space autoregression** where a one-layer head predicting $f_{t+1}$ from $(f_t, t_{t+1})$ yields 0.8 accuracy vs 0.6 and 2.7-3.5x on 70B – 160 tok/s on consumer RTX 3090.

The **co-design lesson** is durable: best speed comes not from bigger draft LMs but from *reusing target representations* – last hidden states, L-1 features, n-gram histories – and structuring verification as largest possible *lossless parallel tree* under per-node overhead constraints.

Future work points to:

* **Unified SpecFormer**: non-autoregressive forecasting for large-batch [Shi et al.] with bidirectional draft + unidirectional verify
* **Online learning**: OSD fusion where draft adapts via distillation on streaming verifier feedback [Liu et al., 2024]
* **Hierarchical speculative**: stacking drafts from tiny 80M -> 7B -> 70B with polynomial optimal hierarchy selection [Mohri et al., 2510.19705]
* **Domain-agnostic EAGLE-3** with multi-layer fusion to finally democratize 3x+ on MoE mixtures like Mixtral 8x7B Instruct

Just as branch predictors turned superscalar CPUs fast, speculative decoding has turned LLMs into their own predictors – generating not what they think comes next, but *many futures at once*, keeping only those fate accepts.
