# Gravitational Wave Detection with Matched Filtering: LIGO Noise Characterization, Template Banks for Compact Binary Coalescence, and Bayesian Parameter Estimation

## Abstract

Gravitational-wave astronomy became observational reality on 14 September 2015, when the Advanced LIGO interferometers at Hanford and Livingston recorded GW150914, the coalescence of two stellar-mass black holes, with a combined matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio of 24 [1]. Extracting such signals — peak strains of order 10^−21, buried in strongly colored instrumental noise — requires the statistically optimal machinery of matched filtering against dense template banks, rigorous signal-consistency vetoes, and Bayesian parameter estimation. This thesis develops the full pipeline from first principles: the interferometric antenna and its noise power spectral density, whitening and the noise-weighted inner product, the Neyman–Pearson justification of the optimal linear filter, geometric and stochastic construction of template banks at minimal match 0.97 on the signal manifold, χ² time–frequency consistency tests that suppress non-Gaussian transients, false-alarm-rate estimation by time slides, and nested-sampling inference in the fifteen-dimensional compact-binary parameter space. Applied to GW150914, the framework yields component masses 36 and 29 M⊙, a remnant of 62 M⊙ with 3 M⊙ radiated, and a peak luminosity exceeding the visible universe — founding a new observational science [2][3].

## 1 Introduction

Einstein's 1916 prediction that accelerating masses radiate ripples in spacetime remained a theoretical curiosity for six decades [1]. The decisive turning point came from radio astronomy: the 1974 discovery by Hulse and Taylor of the binary pulsar PSR B1913+16, whose orbital decay matched the general-relativistic energy-loss rate to one part in 10^13, earned the 1993 Nobel Prize and made gravitational waves (GWs) an indirect fact [1]. Direct detection, however, demanded measuring fractional length changes of order 10^−21 — displacing 4-kilometer test masses by a thousandth of a proton diameter — and suppressing every conceivable noise source by many orders of magnitude.

The Laser Interferometer Gravitational-wave Observatory (LIGO) achieved this with kilometer-scale dual-recycled Michelson interferometers [2]. During its first Advanced LIGO observing run (O1, September 2015–January 2016), the two detectors reached a strain sensitivity of ~10^−23/√Hz near 100 Hz, and within the first sixteen days of coincident observation the product of observable volume and measurement time exceeded that of *all* previous runs combined [2]. On 14 September 2015 at 09:50:45 UTC, the Hanford (H1) and Livingston (L1) interferometers recorded a chirping transient sweeping from ~35 Hz to ~250 Hz in ~0.2 s: the gravitational-wave event GW150914, a binary black hole (BBH) merger announced in *Physical Review Letters* on 11 February 2016 [1].

The astrophysical signal, however, was only the beginning of the story. The raw detector output is dominated by instrumental noise: seismic motion, suspension thermal fluctuations, quantum shot noise, and a forest of instrumental spectral lines, punctuated by non-Gaussian glitches. Detecting a waveform buried at signal-to-noise ratios of order ten against such a background — and measuring its source parameters with credible uncertainties — is a problem in *statistical signal processing*, and this thesis presents its canonical solution:

1. **Noise characterization**: estimating the one-sided power spectral density (PSD) and whitening the data stream.
2. **Matched filtering**: correlating whitened data against a bank of relativistic waveform templates to maximize the recovered signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
3. **Signal-consistency vetoes**: χ² tests that reject glitches whose time–frequency signature disagrees with the template.
4. **Background estimation**: time-slide coincidence analysis to assign false-alarm rates.
5. **Bayesian parameter estimation**: nested sampling of the posterior over the compact-binary parameter space.

Each element is grounded in decision theory and validated against GW150914, whose recovered parameters — component masses ~36 and ~29 M⊙, final mass ~62 M⊙, ~3 M⊙c² radiated, luminosity distance ~410 Mpc — are reproduced as running examples throughout [1][7].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The Interferometric Antenna

Each Advanced LIGO detector is a *dual-recycled Michelson interferometer* with 4-km Fabry–Pérot arm cavities [2]. A 1064 nm Nd:YAG beam is split at the beam splitter; each beam resonates in an arm cavity of finesse ≈ 450, multiplying the effective path roughly three hundredfold. A *power-recycling mirror* builds the circulating power to ~750 kW, while a *signal-recycling mirror* shapes the frequency response. A passing wave of strain *h(t)* stretches the arms differentially by δL = hL/2; *DC readout* converts the fringe shift to a photodiode signal [2].

### 2.2 The Noise Budget and the Power Spectral Density

Stationary Gaussian noise in the detector output *n(t)* is fully characterized by its one-sided PSD *S_n(f)*:

$$
\langle \tilde{n}(f)\,\tilde{n}^*(f') \rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\,S_n(f)\,\delta(f-f'), \qquad f>0.
$$

The Advanced LIGO noise budget partitions into distinct physical regimes [2]:

| Frequency band | Dominant mechanism | Character |
|---|---|---|
| < 10 Hz | Seismic motion | Falls steeply above the isolation stack cutoff |
| 10–100 Hz | Suspension & coating thermal noise | Brownian motion of mirrors and fibers |
| 100–300 Hz | Quantum shot noise crossover | Minimum of the noise bucket, ~10^−23/√Hz |
| > 300 Hz | Quantum shot noise | Rises as √f, limited by laser power |

Superposed on this continuum are narrow *spectral lines* — 60 Hz mains harmonics, violin modes of the suspension fibers, calibration lines — that must be notched or modeled. The PSD is estimated empirically, typically by a Welch-median average of neighboring data segments, and is the single object that turns raw counts into a physical strain spectrum.

### 2.3 Compact Binary Waveforms

For a quasi-circular binary, the leading-order quadrupole waveform chirps upward in frequency and amplitude as the orbit shrinks:

$$
f(t) \propto \mathcal{M}^{-5/8}\,(t_c - t)^{-3/8}, \qquad h(t) \propto \frac{\mathcal{M}^{5/4}}{D}\,f(t)^{2/3}\cos\Phi(t),
$$

where the *chirp mass* ℳ = (m₁m₂)^{3/5}/(m₁+m₂)^{1/5} governs the inspiral rate, *D* is the luminosity distance, and Φ(t) the orbital phase. Full *inspiral–merger–ringdown* (IMR) models — the effective-one-body family (SEOBNR) and phenomenological family (IMRPhenom) — stitch post-Newtonian inspiral to numerical-relativity merger and quasinormal-mode ringdown [7]. These approximants, cross-checked against each other, are the *templates* of the search [8].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Whitening and the Noise-Weighted Inner Product

Because *S_n(f)* spans several orders of magnitude, raw data must be *whitened*: each Fourier component is divided by √S_n(f), so that the residual noise is white with unit variance per frequency bin. On the whitened space, the natural geometry for signal detection is the noise-weighted inner product

$$
\langle a \mid b \rangle = 4\,\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\tilde{a}(f)\,\tilde{b}^*(f)}{S_n(f)}\,df,
$$

which down-weights deaf frequency bands and up-weights the sensitive bucket — all of matched filtering is linear algebra in this inner product.

### 3.2 Template Banks on the Signal Manifold

The source parameters θ = (m₁, m₂, spins, …) are unknown *a priori*, so the data are filtered against a discrete *template bank* {h(θᵢ)} tiling the intrinsic parameter space. The *match* between two normalized waveforms,

$$
\mathcal{M}(h_1,h_2) = \max_{t_c,\phi_c}\,\langle \hat{h}_1 \mid \hat{h}_2(t_c,\phi_c) \rangle,
$$

maximized over coalescence time and phase, induces a distance on the signal manifold. A bank guarantees *minimal match* ℳ_min if every physical signal has match ≥ ℳ_min with at least one template [5]. The canonical choice ℳ_min = 0.97 bounds the SNR loss from discretization to ~1.5% and the detection-volume loss to ~10% [5]. The O1 PyCBC bank covered component masses 1–~100 M⊙ with ~250,000 templates [8].

### 3.3 The Search Pipeline

The production pipelines — **PyCBC** and **GstLAL** — share the same skeleton [4][8]:

1. Estimate *S_n(f)* per data segment; whiten data and templates.
2. Compute the matched-filter SNR time series for every template via FFT.
3. Record *triggers* above an SNR threshold, clustered in time.
4. Apply signal-consistency vetoes (χ²) and rank triggers with a reweighted statistic.
5. Demand *coincidence* across detectors within the light-travel window (~10 ms) plus template tolerance.
6. Estimate the background by *time slides* — shifting one detector's triggers by multiples of 0.1 s — and convert the loudest coincident event to a *false-alarm rate* (FAR) [8].

### 3.4 Bayesian Parameter Estimation

Detection asks *is there a signal?*; parameter estimation asks *what is it?* Bayes' theorem gives

$$
p(\theta \mid d, \mathcal{H}_S) = \frac{\mathcal{L}(d \mid \theta, \mathcal{H}_S)\,\pi(\theta \mid \mathcal{H}_S)}{\mathcal{Z}(d \mid \mathcal{H}_S)},
$$

with the Gaussian-noise likelihood ℒ ∝ exp(−½⟨d − h(θ) | d − h(θ)⟩) evaluated coherently across the detector network [7]. The posterior *p(θ|d)* over the fifteen CBC parameters (masses, six spin components, distance, inclination, sky position, polarization, time, phase) is sampled with *nested sampling* (Skilling 2006; MultiNest/dynesty), parallel-tempered MCMC, or BAMBI — three independent algorithms whose agreement on GW150914-class injections validated the LALInference library [7]. Modern LVK analyses use **Bilby** with dynesty on all catalog events [7].

A minimal whitening-plus-filter prototype illustrates the core operations:

```python
import numpy as np

def whiten(strain, psd, df):
    """Whiten a frequency-domain strain series by the noise PSD."""
    return strain / np.sqrt(psd)

def matched_filter_snr(data_f, tmpl_f, psd, df):
    """Complex matched-filter SNR time series via one inverse FFT [4]."""
    integrand = data_f * np.conj(tmpl_f) / psd
    z = 4.0 * df * np.fft.irfft(integrand)          # filter output z(t_c)
    sigma2 = 4.0 * df * np.sum(np.abs(tmpl_f)**2 / psd)  # template norm
    return z / np.sqrt(sigma2)                       # rho(t_c)

# trigger: peak over coalescence time, maximize over phase via |rho|
rho = matched_filter_snr(d_f, h_f, Sn, df)
snr, tc = np.max(np.abs(rho)), np.argmax(np.abs(rho))
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Optimal Filter: Neyman–Pearson and the Inner Product

Why correlate against a template at all? The answer is decision-theoretic. For the binary hypothesis test ℋ₀: *d = n* versus ℋ₁: *d = n + h* with stationary Gaussian noise, the *Neyman–Pearson lemma* states that the likelihood-ratio test is the most powerful test at any fixed false-alarm probability. The log-likelihood ratio is

$$
\Lambda = \langle d \mid h \rangle - \tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle h \mid h \rangle,
$$

so the optimal *linear* statistic is the inner product ⟨d|h⟩ — the matched filter — normalized by σ = √⟨h|h⟩ to form the SNR ρ = ⟨d|h⟩/σ [4].

> **Theorem:** *Among all linear filters, the matched filter maximizes the expected signal-to-noise ratio for a known waveform in stationary Gaussian noise; by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, no other linear statistic can do better.*

In the time domain this is a convolution, evaluated for all *t_c* at once by a single inverse FFT per template; the complex output *z(t_c)* simultaneously maximizes over the unknown phase, ρ(t_c) = |z(t_c)|/σ [4].

### 4.2 Noise Characterization: From PSD Estimation to Whitening

The matched filter is only optimal if the noise is *stationary and Gaussian* — and LIGO data are neither, quite. Two layers of characterization repair the gap:

- **Stationary part.** The PSD is tracked with a running median over ~10³ s of neighboring data, robust to outliers. Data-quality *veto flags* excise intervals of known instrumental misbehavior (category-2 vetoes removed from PyCBC searches [8]).
- **Non-stationary part.** Loud *glitches* — blip transients, scattered-light arches, whistle glitches — survive whitening and ring the matched filter. They are handled not by the filter but by the χ² vetoes of §4.4 and by *gating*: smoothly zeroing identified loud transients before filtering [8].

### 4.3 Template Bank Geometry: Minimal Match and the Owen Metric

The set of normalized waveforms {ĥ(θ)} forms a *signal manifold*; nearby points are compared through the *mismatch* 1 − ℳ. Expanding to second order,

$$
1 - \mathcal{M}(\theta, \theta + \Delta\theta) \simeq g_{ij}(\theta)\,\Delta\theta^i\,\Delta\theta^j,
$$

defines the *Owen metric* g_ij = −½ ∂²ℳ/∂Δθⁱ∂Δθʲ|₀ on parameter space [5][6]. A bank with minimal match ℳ_min is a *covering* of the manifold by metric balls of radius √(1 − ℳ_min) — a sphere-packing problem in curved space.

Three placement philosophies compete [5][6]:

1. **Geometric (lattice) placement.** In coordinates where the metric is approximately constant (chirp times τ₀, τ₃ for non-spinning inspiral), an A₂* hexagonal lattice is near-optimal. Elegant but brittle: curvature, spin dimensions, and parameter-space boundaries break the lattice [6].
2. **Stochastic placement.** Propose random points; accept a proposal only if its match with every accepted template is below ℳ_min — robust to curvature at the cost of ~25% more templates [5].
3. **Hybrid and tree-based placement.** The modern compromise: binary-tree decomposition of parameter space using a local metric volume estimate 𝒩_C = ∫√|det g| dV / V_T, splitting cells until each holds ≲ 1 template [6]. This yields near-stochastic robustness with an order-of-magnitude faster generation [5][6].

Banks are validated by the *fitting factor* — the fraction of optimal SNR recovered — demanding it exceed ℳ_min for injected signals across the space, including boundaries [6].

### 4.4 Glitch Rejection: The χ² Signal-Consistency Test

Real glitches can produce large matched-filter SNR — a loud blip transient has enormous raw ρ. The defense, due to Allen (2005), is a *time–frequency consistency test*: split the template into *p* frequency sub-bands, each expected to contribute ρ/p to the filter output for a true signal [4]. The statistic

$$
\chi^2 = p\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left|z_i - z/p\right|^2, \qquad \chi^2_r = \chi^2/(2p-2),
$$

follows a χ² distribution with 2p − 2 degrees of freedom for Gaussian noise plus a perfectly matching signal, but explodes for glitches whose power concentrates in one band [4]. PyCBC folds this into a *reweighted SNR*

$$
\hat{\rho} = \rho\,/\,\left[\tfrac{1 + (\chi^2_r)^3}{2}\right]^{1/6},
$$

which equals ρ for χ²_r ≤ 1 and is heavily suppressed otherwise [8]. Additional vetoes — high-frequency excess power and template-autocorrelation consistency — further prune the trigger list. For GW150914, the χ²_r values were ≈ 1 in both detectors: the signal accumulated exactly as a chirp should, across the full band [1].

### 4.5 Bayesian Parameter Estimation: Nested Sampling in Fifteen Dimensions

The likelihood surface over θ is multimodal, with strong curved degeneracies (distance–inclination, mass ratio–spin). *Nested sampling* tames it by transforming the evidence integral into a one-dimensional quadrature over prior mass:

$$
\mathcal{Z} = \int \mathcal{L}(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)\,d\theta = \int_0^1 L(X)\,dX,
$$

where *X(λ)* is the prior volume with likelihood above λ [7]. The algorithm maintains a set of *live points*, iteratively replacing the lowest-likelihood point with a higher-likelihood draw from the constrained prior, accumulating both posterior samples (weighted by likelihood × prior-volume shrinkage) and the evidence 𝒵 — the latter enabling *model selection* (e.g., GR versus massive-graviton hypotheses [7]).

> **Theorem:** *Nested sampling converges to the true Bayesian evidence with uncertainty shrinking as 1/√N_live, while its posterior samples are unbiased draws from p(θ|d) regardless of the likelihood's multimodality — provided the constrained-prior sampler mixes.*

Three independent samplers (nested sampling, parallel-tempered MCMC, BAMBI) were validated on analytic distributions and on simulated BNS, NSBH, and BBH injections with consistent results [7]. Priors are astrophysically agnostic — uniform in component masses, uniform in spin magnitude with isotropic orientations — and analyses are repeated with SEOBNR vs. IMRPhenom cross-checks to bracket waveform systematics [7].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The Detection

On 14 September 2015 at 09:50:45 UTC, both LIGO interferometers recorded an identical chirp morphology, with L1 preceding H1 by 6.9 ms — consistent with the 10 ms inter-site light-travel time and a Southern-Hemisphere source [1]. The numbers that founded the field [1][8]:

| Quantity | Value (GW150914) |
|---|---|
| Combined matched-filter SNR | 24 |
| PyCBC false-alarm rate | < 1 per 203,000 years (≳ 5.1σ) |
| Peak strain | ~1.0 × 10^−21 |
| Frequency sweep | ~35 Hz → ~250 Hz in ~0.2 s |
| Detector-frame chirp mass | ~28 M⊙ |

Two independent matched-filter pipelines (PyCBC's reweighted SNR, GstLAL's likelihood-ratio statistic) and two unmodeled burst searches all recovered the event, agreeing on a FAR below one per 200,000 years [8].

### 5.2 The Source Parameters

Bayesian PE with IMR waveforms gave (medians ± 90% credible intervals, source frame) [1][7]:

| Parameter | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Primary mass m₁ | 36.2^{+5.2}_{−3.8} M⊙ |
| Secondary mass m₂ | 29.1^{+3.7}_{−4.4} M⊙ |
| Final mass M_f | 62.3^{+3.7}_{−3.1} M⊙ |
| Radiated energy | 3.0^{+0.5}_{−0.4} M⊙c² |
| Final spin a_f | 0.68^{+0.05}_{−0.06} |
| Luminosity distance | 410^{+160}_{−180} Mpc (z ≈ 0.09) |
| Peak GW luminosity | 3.6 × 10^56 erg s^−1 |

Three features deserve emphasis. First, the *missing* 3 M⊙: the remnant mass is less than m₁ + m₂ by precisely the radiated energy — a direct calorimetric test of E = mc² in the strong field [1]. Second, the peak luminosity briefly exceeded the combined electromagnetic output of the observable universe by a factor of ~50 [1]. Third, the component masses lay squarely in the *stellar-mass black hole* range, establishing that binary black holes exist and merge within a Hubble time [1].

### 5.3 Consistency Proofs

A battery of null tests followed [1]:

1. **Waveform-systematics check.** SEOBNR and IMRPhenom analyses returned mutually consistent posteriors, ruling out single-model artifacts [7].
2. **Residual test.** Subtracting the maximum-likelihood waveform left residuals consistent with stationary noise [1].
3. **Inspiral–merger–ringdown consistency.** Inspiral-only inference predicted the remnant mass and spin measured from the merger–ringdown, testing GR's nonlinear dynamics [1].
4. **Environmental veto.** Magnetometers, seismometers, and microphones showed no coincident disturbance, and injection logs confirmed no artificial signal [1].

Together these elevate GW150914 from a *loud trigger* to a *measurement*: the full pipeline — filter, bank, veto, coincidence, Bayesian inference — closed the loop from photodiode counts to black hole masses.

## 6 Limitations

The pipeline's rigor should not obscure its approximations and failure modes:

- **Calibration uncertainty.** The conversion from photodiode counts to strain carries ~10% amplitude and ~10° phase uncertainty in O1; this propagates directly into distance and (weakly) mass estimates [2].
- **Non-stationary, non-Gaussian noise.** The χ² veto suppresses but does not eliminate glitches; rare *blip* transients can mimic short high-mass templates [8].
- **Template-bank incompleteness.** Banks assume quasi-circular, aligned-spin waveforms; precessing or eccentric signals suffer fitting-factor losses, and ℳ_min = 0.97 costs ~10% of detection volume by construction [5][6].
- **Waveform systematics.** Numerical-relativity calibration is sparse at high mass ratio and spin; PE posteriors inherit these errors, only partly bracketed by cross-approximant comparison [7].
- **Degeneracies and localization.** Distance–inclination degeneracy broadens the distance posterior; with two nearly-aligned detectors, the GW150914 sky map spanned ~590 deg², limiting electromagnetic follow-up [1].
- **Computational cost.** Filtering ~10^5 templates is a 10^6-CPU-hour-class task; PE per event takes days to weeks, motivating reduced-order models and ML acceleration [7][8].

---

## 7 Conclusion

Matched filtering turned the LIGO interferometers into *telescopes*: the noise-weighted inner product extracts the optimal linear statistic, template banks tile the signal manifold at controlled mismatch cost, χ² vetoes and time slides enforce frequentist rigor, and nested sampling converts detections into measurements [4][7][8]. GW150914 — SNR 24, two ~30 M⊙ black holes fusing into a 62 M⊙ remnant while radiating 3 M⊙c² — validated every link of this chain simultaneously [1]. The same machinery has since catalogued hundreds of compact-binary mergers and will underpin third-generation detectors, where year-long signals and overlapping sources will stress every assumption examined here. The lesson of the first detection endures: *in gravitational-wave astronomy, the instrument is the algorithm.*
