# Conflict-Driven Clause Learning: Watched Literals, VSIDS Branching, LBD Clause Reduction, Inprocessing, and Verified DRAT Proof Logging

## Abstract

Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) transformed propositional satisfiability from a combinatorial curiosity into an industrial-strength decision procedure routinely reasoning over formulas with millions of variables and clauses. This thesis provides a rigorous, end-to-end account of the modern CDCL architecture: the Decide–Deduce–Diagnose loop first articulated in the GRASP algorithm; the two-watched-literal scheme that made Boolean Constraint Propagation (BCP) amortized-constant time per unassignment; the VSIDS and VMTF branching heuristics that steer search toward conflict-dense regions of the variable space; the First Unique Implication Point (1UIP) learning scheme and non-chronological backjumping derived from cuts in the implication graph; Glucose's Literal Block Distance (LBD) metric and aggressive learnt-clause database reduction; inprocessing techniques such as vivification and blocked clause elimination that simplify the formula while solving; the revival of chronological backtracking for structured instances; and the DRAT proof format with independently verified checkers (*drat-trim*, *cake_lpr*) that elevate solver results from heuristic claims to machine-checked mathematical certainty. The synthesis yields a complete blueprint of why CDCL dominates the SAT competition landscape.

---

## 1. Introduction

The Boolean satisfiability problem (SAT) — deciding whether a propositional formula admits a satisfying assignment — occupies a singular position in computer science. As the canonical NP-complete problem, its efficient solution unlocks model checking, planning, scheduling, hardware verification, and combinatorial optimization at industrial scale. For three decades, the Davis–Putnam–Logemann–Loveland (DPLL) procedure of 1962 stood as the dominant complete search paradigm: branch on a literal, propagate unit clauses, and backtrack chronologically on conflict.

Two intellectual earthquakes reshaped the field. First, Marques-Silva and Sakallah's GRASP algorithm (1996/1999) introduced **conflict analysis with clause learning** and **non-chronological backtracking** (backjumping): when a conflict arises, the solver does not merely retreat to the most recent decision, but diagnoses *why* the conflict occurred, learns a new clause summarizing the reason, and leaps back over irrelevant decisions [1]. Second, Moskewicz et al.'s Chaff (2001) engineered GRASP's ideas into a production solver via the **two-watched-literal** BCP scheme, the **VSIDS** branching heuristic, and random restarts — improving performance on industrial instances by orders of magnitude [2].

MiniSat (2003) then distilled CDCL into ~1200 lines of clean, extensible C++ that became the *de facto* research platform for a decade [3]. Glucose (2009) added the **LBD** quality metric for learnt clauses, producing dramatic gains on unsatisfiable industrial benchmarks [4]. CaDiCaL and Kissat (Biere) pushed further with aggressive **inprocessing**, **chronological backtracking**, and **DRAT proof logging** [7]. Meanwhile, the proof-checking community responded to growing trust concerns by developing the **DRAT** format and the *drat-trim* checker, culminating in the CakeML-verified *cake_lpr* checker — machine-checked soundness for the most demanding verification contexts [5][6].

This thesis unifies these contributions into a single architectural narrative, with precise algorithmic detail, worked examples, and an honest accounting of limitations.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 SAT, CNF, and Resolution

We consider formulas in **conjunctive normal form** (CNF): conjunctions of clauses, each clause a disjunction of literals (a variable or its negation). A *partial assignment* τ maps a subset of variables to {true, false}; a clause is *satisfied* if some literal is true, *conflicting* if all literals are false, *unit* if exactly one literal is unassigned and all others false, and *unresolved* otherwise.

> **Theorem (Completeness of resolution):** A CNF formula *F* is unsatisfiable if and only if the empty clause □ is derivable from *F* by resolution. CDCL's learned clauses are each derivable from existing clauses by a sequence of resolution steps, so an UNSAT answer from CDCL induces a resolution refutation [9].

### 2.2 From DPLL to CDCL

DPLL's weakness is amnesia: the same conflicted sub-space is rediscovered exponentially many times, and backtracking always retreats one level, re-exploring irrelevant decisions. CDCL's response has three pillars [8]:

1. **Clause learning** — each conflict yields a *lemma* that prunes the search tree permanently.
2. **Non-chronological backtracking** — backjump to the *asserting level* of the learned clause, skipping decisions uninvolved in the conflict.
3. **Restarts** — periodically abandon the current branch (keeping all learned clauses) to escape heavy-tailed runtime distributions.

### 2.3 The Implication Graph

The central data structure of conflict analysis is the **implication graph**, a directed acyclic graph recording, for each implied literal, the clause (its *antecedent* or *reason*) that forced it, with edges from every other literal of the reason [1]. Decision literals have in-degree zero. A conflict vertex κ is added with edges from all literals of the conflicting clause. Learned clauses correspond to **cuts** separating the conflict vertex from the decision literals; the cut closest to the conflict that contains a single literal from the current decision level — the **First Unique Implication Point (1UIP)** — yields the standard asserting clause.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 The CDCL Main Loop

Modern solvers implement a *Decide–Deduce–Diagnose* loop:

```c
result CDCL(Formula F) {
    for (;;) {
        Clause conf = propagate(F);          // Deduce: BCP to fixpoint
        if (conf != NULL) {                  // Diagnose
            if (decision_level == 0) return UNSAT;
            Clause learnt = analyze(conf);   // 1UIP resolution
            int blevel = asserting_level(learnt);
            add_clause(F, learnt);
            backtrack(blevel);
        } else {                             // Decide
            if (all_assigned(F)) return SAT;
            Literal l = pick_branch_literal();
            decide(l);
        }
    }
}
```

Each component — `propagate`, `pick_branch_literal`, `analyze`, the clause database manager, and the proof logger — embodies decades of engineering.

### 3.2 Two Watched Literals

Naive BCP visits every clause containing a newly assigned variable, and backtracking requires undoing counter updates — together consuming over 90% of early solvers' runtime. The **two-watched-literal** scheme [2] maintains, per clause, two distinguished *watched* literals with the invariant:

> **Invariant:** At the end of BCP, either one watched literal is satisfied, or both are unassigned.

When literal *p* becomes false, only clauses watching *p* are visited; the solver seeks a non-false replacement watch. If none exists, the other watch is either satisfied (ignore), unassigned (unit — enqueue it), or false (conflict). Crucially, **backtracking is free**: unassigning variables can never violate the invariant, since watched literals are only ever non-false, so the trail pointer alone is reset. This reduced BCP's cost to near-amortized-constant and is universal in modern solvers [2][7].

```python
def propagate(watchers, trail, assign):
    while trail:
        p = trail.pop()          # newly assigned literal
        for clause in watchers[neg(p)]:
            if not try_find_new_watch(clause, p, assign):
                other = clause.other_watch(p)
                if assign[other] is True:  continue   # satisfied
                if assign[other] is False: return clause  # conflict
                trail.append(other); assign[other] = True  # unit
    return None
```

### 3.3 Branching: VSIDS and VMTF

Because watched literals hide the formula's satisfied clauses from view, classical static heuristics (MOM, JW) are unavailable. Chaff's answer was **VSIDS** — Variable State Independent Decaying Sum [2]:

1. Each variable holds an *activity*, initialized to its occurrence count.
2. On every conflict, activities of variables in the learned clause (and resolved during analysis) are bumped.
3. Periodically, all activities are multiplied by a decay factor < 1 (MiniSat instead multiplies the bump increment, achieving identical effect) [3].

The heap always yields the highest-activity unassigned variable, focusing search on recently conflicted — hence mutually constraining — variables. **Phase saving** [3] caches each variable's last assigned polarity, so re-decisions resume the prior region. MiniSat's default is VSIDS; CaDiCaL uses **VMTF** (Variable Move-To-Front), a queue-based scheme moving conflicted variables to the front, which is cheaper and excels on certain industrial suites [7].

### 3.4 1UIP Learning and Backjumping

Conflict analysis resolves the conflicting clause against antecedents of implied literals in reverse trail order until exactly one literal of the current decision level remains — the **1UIP clause** [1]. The backjump level is the second-highest decision level in the clause; the learned clause becomes unit there, immediately forcing the 1UIP literal. Empirically, 1UIP clauses are short, asserting, and dominate all other UIP schemes [8].

### 3.5 LBD and Clause Database Reduction

Unbounded learning explodes the database: solvers learn thousands of clauses per second. Glucose's breakthrough was **Literal Block Distance**: `LBD(C)` = number of distinct decision levels among C's literals [4]. A clause with LBD 2 (a *glue clause*) connects two decision blocks; after backtracking below one block it becomes unit quickly, so glue clauses propagate disproportionately. Glucose **never deletes glue clauses** and aggressively halves the remaining learnt database by LBD tier — a policy that won multiple SAT competitions and is now standard [4].

| Clause | Levels | LBD | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (¬x₇ ∨ x₁₂) | {4, 4} | 1 | unit-tier, keep forever |
| (x₃ ∨ ¬x₉ ∨ x₁₅) | {2, 5} | 2 | glue, keep forever |
| (¬x₂ ∨ x₆ ∨ ¬x₁₁ ∨ x₁₉) | {1, 3, 5, 8} | 4 | deletable |
| (x₁ ∨ ¬x₄ ∨ x₈ ∨ x₁₃ ∨ ¬x₁₇ ∨ x₂₂) | {1,2,4,6,7,9} | 6 | delete first |

### 3.6 Inprocessing: Vivification and Blocked Clause Elimination

Preprocessing (SatELite-style variable elimination) is applied once; **inprocessing** interleaves simplification *during* search [7]. Two workhorses:

- **Vivification**: for a clause *C*, temporarily assume ¬*C* and propagate; any literal of *C* implied at level 0 can be removed from *C*, and a conflict replaces *C* by the (shorter) learned clause. This is the single most effective modern inprocessing technique.
- **Blocked Clause Elimination (BCE)**: a clause *C* is *blocked* on literal *l* if every resolvent of *C* with a clause containing ¬*l* is a tautology; blocked clauses can be removed while preserving satisfiability (though not logical equivalence — proof logging must record the addition/deletion steps carefully).

CaDiCaL schedules vivification, BCE, variable elimination, and subsumption at geometrically increasing conflict intervals, re-learning a stronger formula as search proceeds [7].

### 3.7 Chronological Backtracking

Standard CDCL always backjumps. Surprisingly, selectively **backtracking chronologically** — undoing only the most recent decision level and flipping it — helps on structured (e.g., bounded model checking) instances by preserving the trail's learned propagation structure [9]. Modern solvers (CaDiCaL, Kissat) apply chronological backtracking when the backjump distance is small or the conflict level structure suggests locality, blending DPLL-style depth-first discipline into the CDCL loop.

### 3.8 DRAT Proof Logging and Verified Checking

A solver's UNSAT claim is only as trustworthy as its code — a sobering thought for verification toolchains. The community's answer is **proof emission**: every learned clause is logged as a clause *addition*, and deletions are logged too, producing a **DRAT** (Deletion Resolution Asymmetric Tautology) proof [5].

> **Theorem (DRAT soundness):** If the empty clause is derivable by the DRAT rules (each added clause is a RAT — Resolution Asymmetric Tautology — w.r.t. the current formula), the original formula is unsatisfiable. The DRAT format is *expressive*: every known CDCL technique, including preprocessing and inprocessing, is DRAT-checkable [5].

**drat-trim** checks DRAT proofs in time comparable to solving, using backward checking (Goldberg–Novikov) to trim the proof to its unsatisfiable core and emitting optimized proofs and TraceCheck⁺ dependency graphs [6]. **LRAT** enriches DRAT with *hints* (antecedent clause IDs), so checking needs no search. Finally, **cake_lpr** is a CakeML-*verified* LRAT checker: its soundness is a machine-checked theorem in HOL4, closing the trust gap from "the solver is probably right" to "a proof assistant certifies the checker" [5][8].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Amortized Cost of Watched-Literal BCP

Each clause visit during propagation either moves a watch (at most 2·|C| moves per assignment epoch before the clause is satisfied or unit) or detects unit/conflict. Since unassignment never moves watches, total watch-maintenance over an entire search is linear in the number of *assignments*, not assignments × clause size — the decisive improvement over counter-based schemes, where backtracking costs as much as propagation [2].

### 4.2 Why VSIDS Beats Static Heuristics

VSIDS is often described as "dynamic," but its deeper property is *locality*: conflicts cluster in unsatisfiable cores, and bumping recently-involved variables implements a form of online stochastic focus akin to exponential moving averages in optimization. Decay implements forgetting: stale hotspots cool, letting the solver migrate between subproblems — an effect amplified by **restarts** (Luby or glucose-style dynamic schedules), which discard the trail but keep clauses, re-entering search with fresh VSIDS ordering [2][3][4].

### 4.3 LBD as a Proxy for Proof Relevance

Why does LBD predict usefulness? A learned clause with LBD *k* requires at most *k−1* decisions to become unit or conflicting in future branches — it is a short *decision-distance* constraint. Glue clauses (LBD ≤ 2) effectively cache a binary implication between decision blocks, mimicking the role of binary clauses in propagation. Audemard and Simon showed empirically that clauses participating in future conflicts have systematically lower LBD, and that deleting high-LBD clauses barely perturbs the solver's resolution proof trajectory [4].

### 4.4 Inprocessing vs. Proof Complexity

Inprocessing complicates the proof story: techniques like BCE do not preserve logical equivalence, only satisfiability. DRAT handles this because RAT addition is *satisfiability-preserving* by definition — the checker's asymmetric-tautology test validates each step independently of the solver's internal justification. This decoupling is why DRAT (unlike pure resolution traces) survived the inprocessing era: the solver may use any satisfiability-preserving transformation, and the checker re-verifies each emitted lemma from scratch [5][6].

### 4.5 Verified Checking: From drat-trim to cake_lpr

drat-trim is a highly optimized C program — fast but itself unverified. The trust chain therefore has two links: solver → proof, proof → checker. LRAT's hints eliminate the checker's search, making the checking algorithm simple enough for **formal verification**: cake_lpr's implementation is synthesized from a CakeML program proven correct in HOL4, with the top-level theorem stating that acceptance implies unsatisfiability of the input CNF. This is the current gold standard for safety-critical verification pipelines [5][8].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

The CDCL architecture's empirical dominance is documented across two decades of SAT competitions and the Handbook of Satisfiability [8]:

- **Chaff (2001)** solved industrial benchmarks with hundreds of thousands of variables that stumped all predecessors, demonstrating that watched literals + VSIDS + restarts beat carefully tuned DPLL by 1–2 orders of magnitude [2].
- **MiniSat (2003)** became the most-cited solver artifact in the field; its descendants (MiniSat+, Glucose, CryptoMiniSat) won numerous SAT Competition tracks [3].
- **Glucose (2009)** won the SAT 2009 competition's UNSAT categories; its LBD-based aggressive deletion solved industrial instances where MiniSat-style activity-based deletion timed out [4].
- **CaDiCaL/Kissat** have dominated recent competitions, with Kissat winning SAT Competition 2020+ main tracks [7].
- **DRAT checking**: drat-trim validates competition proofs in time comparable to solving; trimmed unsatisfiable cores are frequently orders of magnitude smaller than raw proofs [5][6].

Proof-theoretically, CDCL with 1UIP learning and restarts polynomially simulates general resolution, and with nondeterministic branching choices it is *equivalent* in power to resolution — exponentially stronger than DPLL/tree-like resolution on families like pigeonhole instances [9].

## 6. Limitations

CDCL is not a panacea, and intellectual honesty demands the caveats:

1. **Resolution lower bounds bite.** CDCL is bounded by resolution proof complexity: pigeonhole, Tseitin on expanders, and clique-coloring formulas require exponential-size resolution refutations, hence exponential CDCL runtime regardless of heuristics [9].
2. **Heuristic brittleness.** VSIDS/VMTF/LBD policies are tuned on competition benchmarks; crafted or random instances (where look-ahead solvers excel) can expose pathological behavior. Portfolio solvers hedge this by running diverse strategies in parallel.
3. **Memory pressure.** Even with LBD deletion, learnt databases on hard UNSAT instances consume gigabytes; clause sharing in parallel solvers adds synchronization overhead.
4. **Proof size.** DRAT proofs for hard instances reach tens of gigabytes; checking, while linear-ish, demands comparable memory to solving [5].
5. **Chronological backtracking trade-offs.** Its gains are benchmark-specific; misapplied, it degrades to DPLL-like thrashing on instances where long backjumps prune effectively.
6. **Inprocessing soundness risk.** Every inprocessing rule must be DRAT-emittable; a bug in a single transformation silently voids the proof chain — precisely the failure mode verified checkers guard against.

---

## 7. Conclusion

CDCL's triumph is an engineering triumph built on clean theory. The implication graph gives conflict analysis a graph-theoretic foundation; watched literals make propagation essentially free under backtracking; VSIDS focuses search where conflicts live; 1UIP learning with backjumping converts each failure into permanent progress; LBD separates proof-relevant lemmas from clutter; inprocessing strengthens the formula mid-search; chronological backtracking reclaims depth-first discipline where it helps; and DRAT with verified checkers transforms solver output into trustworthy mathematical evidence. From GRASP's Diagnose engine [1] through Chaff's engineering [2], MiniSat's minimalism [3], Glucose's glue [4], CaDiCaL's inprocessing [7], to machine-checked proof validation [5][6][8], each layer addressed the previous layer's bottleneck — a thirty-year compounding of ideas that made SAT the "practical NP oracle" of modern verification.
