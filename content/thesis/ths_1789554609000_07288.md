# Tropicalization, Bergman Fans, and Matroid Polytopes: Tropical Geometry and Its Mirror-Symmetric Shadows

## Abstract

Tropical geometry recasts algebraic geometry over non-Archimedean valued fields as polyhedral combinatorics by replacing classical addition and multiplication with minimization and addition. This thesis develops the field's central machinery — the tropical semiring, amoebas, and the Fundamental Theorem of tropical geometry — and follows its ramifications into the Bergman fans of matroids, the geometry of matroid polytopes and the tropical Grassmannian, and a polyhedral intersection theory built on stable intersections. We prove the balancing condition for tropical varieties, show how the wonderful compactification of De Concini and Procesi is governed by nested sets and Bergman fans, and present Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem equating classical Gromov–Witten counts with weighted counts of tropical curves. We then trace the tropical shadow of homological mirror symmetry through Strominger–Yau–Zaslow torus fibrations, survey the computational landscape of gfan and polymake, and formulate open problems in realizability, combinatorial Hodge theory, and tropical moduli.

---

## 1 Intro

Classical algebraic geometry studies the zero sets of polynomials. Tropical geometry studies what those zero sets become when one records only the *orders of magnitude* of coordinates. The resulting objects are piecewise-linear polyhedral complexes — skeletons that remember surprisingly much of the original geometry: dimension, degree, intersection numbers, and even counts of curves.

This thesis is a systematic exposition of that degeneration and its deepest consequences. We begin with amoebas — the logarithmic images of complex varieties — and their limits under coordinate-wise rescaling, which motivate the definition of tropicalization. We then state and prove the essential content of the **Fundamental Theorem of tropical geometry**: for a subvariety of an algebraic torus, the tropical variety coincides with the set of weight vectors for which the initial ideal contains no monomial, and with the closure of the valuation image of the variety [1].

The linear case is the combinatorial heart of the subject. The tropicalization of a linear space depends only on its matroid, and the resulting object — the **Bergman fan** — is a pure polyhedral fan parametrized by chains of flats. Bergman fans are the local building blocks of tropical linear spaces and, via the work of Feichtner and Sturmfels, they control the wonderful compactification of hyperplane arrangement complements [4].

From there we develop **tropical intersection theory**: Cartier divisors, the balancing condition, and the stable intersection product of Allermann and Rau [5]. We present Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem, which identifies complex enumerative invariants with multiplicities of tropical curves [2], and we explain how the same piecewise-linear skeletons appear as the bases of SYZ fibrations in mirror symmetry [6]. We close with the computational state of the art and with open problems that remain genuinely hard.

## 2 Background

The algebraic setting is a field $K$ equipped with a non-Archimedean valuation $\mathrm{val}: K^\times \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $\mathrm{val}(a+b) \geq \min(\mathrm{val}(a), \mathrm{val}(b))$ with equality when the two valuations differ. The canonical example is the field of Puiseux series $\mathbb{C}\{\{t\}\}$, whose elements are formal series $\sum c_i t^{a_i}$ with well-ordered real exponents; the valuation reads off the smallest exponent. Its valuation ring consists of series with nonnegative exponents, and its residue field is $\mathbb{C}$.

Inside the torus $T = (K^\times)^n$, a closed subvariety $X$ is cut out by an ideal $I$ in the Laurent polynomial ring $K[x_1^{\pm 1}, \dots, x_n^{\pm 1}]$. For a weight vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the **initial form** $\mathrm{in}_w(f)$ of a polynomial $f$ is the sum of those terms of $f$ on which the linear functional $\langle w, \cdot \rangle + \mathrm{val}$ attains its minimum; the **initial ideal** $\mathrm{in}_w(I)$ is the ideal generated by all such initial forms. The Gröbner fan of $I$ is the polyhedral fan in $\mathbb{R}^n$ whose cones group together weights with equal initial ideals. Tropicalization will be a distinguished subfan.

*Why should piecewise-linear objects arise from algebra?* The ultrametric inequality turns addition of series into minimization of exponents, and multiplication into addition of exponents. A polynomial equation $\sum c_u x^u = 0$ forces the minimum valuation among its terms to be attained at least twice — otherwise a single term would dominate and the sum could not vanish. This innocent observation is the entire engine of the subject: the tropical hypersurface of a polynomial is precisely the locus where the minimum in its tropicalization is attained at least twice.

The unifying textbook treatment is that of Maclagan and Sturmfels [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our method throughout is *tropicalization by initial ideals*, refined by valuation-theoretic and polyhedral techniques. Concretely:

1. **Choose the valued field.** We work over $K = \mathbb{C}\{\{t\}\}$ or a discretely valued extension, so that initial ideals are defined over the residue field $\mathbb{C}$ and can be computed by Gröbner-basis methods.
2. **Compute the Gröbner fan.** Using gfan-style traversal, we enumerate the cones of weights with constant initial ideal. This is the ambient polyhedral structure; the tropical variety is the subcomplex of weights whose initial ideal is monomial-free [1].
3. **Extract combinatorial data.** In the linear case the initial ideals are squarefree monomial or binomial, and the tropical variety collapses to matroid data: circuits, flats, and the Bergman fan [4].
4. **Weigh and balance.** Each maximal cone receives a multiplicity — the number of minimal primes of the initial ideal, or a lattice index in the linear case — and we verify the balancing condition around each codimension-one face.
5. **Lift to geometry.** Curve counts are obtained by intersecting with tropical hyperplanes and lifting solutions through the correspondence theorem [2]; mirror-symmetric interpretations pass through SYZ fibrations [6].

This pipeline is fully algorithmic in the linear and zero-dimensional cases [8]; in general it motivates the combinatorial models of the later sections.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Tropical Semiring, Amoebas, and the Fundamental Theorem

The **tropical semiring** is $\mathbb{T} = (\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}, \oplus, \odot)$ with $a \oplus b = \min(a,b)$ and $a \odot b = a + b$. It is a semifield: every finite element has a multiplicative inverse (its negative), but there is no additive inverse. A tropical polynomial $F(x) = \bigoplus_{u \in A} c_u \odot x^{\odot u}$ evaluates as the piecewise-linear convex function $F(x) = \min_{u \in A}(c_u + \langle u, x \rangle)$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, and its **tropical hypersurface** $\mathcal{T}(F)$ is the corner locus where this minimum is attained at least twice — equivalently, the set of nondifferentiability points of $F$.

The analytic precursor is the **amoeba**. For a complex variety $V \subset (\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$, the map $\mathrm{Log}(z_1, \dots, z_n) = (\log|z_1|, \dots, \log|z_n|)$ sends $V$ to a closed subset $\mathcal{A}(V) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ with tentacles going to infinity. Rescaling coordinates by a parameter $t \to 0$ and taking the Hausdorff limit of the rescaled amoebas collapses the tentacles to rays: the limit is a polyhedral complex — the tropicalization. Gelfand, Kapranov, and Zelevinsky proved that the asymptotic directions of an amoeba are governed by the normal fan of the Newton polytope, a first glimpse of the polyhedral control tropical geometry exerts.

> **Theorem:** (Fundamental Theorem of tropical geometry). Let $X \subset (K^\times)^n$ be a $d$-dimensional subvariety over an algebraically closed valued field. The following three sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$ coincide: the closure of $\{(\mathrm{val}(x_1), \dots, \mathrm{val}(x_n)) : x \in X\}$; the intersection over all $f$ in the defining ideal of the tropical hypersurfaces $\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{trop}(f))$; and the set of $w$ for which $\mathrm{in}_w(I)$ contains no monomial. The resulting set $\mathrm{trop}(X)$ is the support of a pure $d$-dimensional balanced weighted polyhedral complex [1].

The third description is the computational one; the first is the geometric one; their equivalence is what makes tropical geometry simultaneously computable and meaningful. A crucial corollary is the **Structure Theorem**: the weights on maximal cells can be chosen so that the complex is *balanced*, meaning that around every codimension-one face the weighted primitive generators of adjacent maximal cells sum to zero in the quotient lattice. Balancing is the tropical shadow of the fact that the original variety has no boundary.

| Classical concept | Tropical analogue |
|---|---|
| Polynomial ring $K[x]$ | Piecewise-linear functions on $\mathbb{R}^n$ |
| Zero set $V(f)$ | Corner locus $\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{trop}(f))$ |
| Degree of a variety | Weighted lattice volume / degree of fan |
| Intersection multiplicity | Lattice index in stable intersection |
| Gröbner fan | Ambient fan containing $\mathrm{trop}(X)$ |
| Compactification | Toric / wonderful compactification |

### 4.2 Bergman Fans and the Wonderful Compactification

Let $M$ be a matroid of rank $r$ on ground set $E = \{1, \dots, n\}$, given for instance by a linear subspace $V \subset K^n$ defined over the residue field. The **Bergman fan** $\mathcal{B}(M) \subset \mathbb{R}^E$ is the set of weight vectors $w$ such that, for every circuit $C$ of $M$, the minimum of the coordinates $w_i$ over $i \in C$ is attained at least twice [4]. Modulo the lineality space $\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}$, this is a pure $(r-1)$-dimensional fan. Its cones are indexed by chains of flats

$$\emptyset = F_0 \subsetneq F_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq F_k = E,$$

and in the *fine subdivision* each cone is simplicial, spanned by the incidence vectors of the flats. The coarse subdivision merges cones whose flats differ by non-flats, but the support is the same.

Bergman fans are exactly the tropicalizations of linear spaces: if $X \subset (K^\times)^n$ is defined by linear equations, then $\mathrm{trop}(X) = \mathcal{B}(M)$ where $M$ is the matroid of the linear matroid of the equations [4]. This is the sense in which matroids are the linear algebra of tropical geometry.

The deeper geometric meaning comes from the **wonderful compactification** of De Concini and Procesi. Given a hyperplane arrangement, blowing up its intersection lattice in increasing order of dimension produces a smooth compactification whose boundary is a simple normal-crossings divisor. Feichtner and Sturmfels showed that this blow-up combinatorics is governed by *nested sets* — collections of flats with a laminar intersection property — and that the Bergman fan is the tropical analogue of the arrangement complement [4]. In modern language, the Bergman fan is the skeleton of the Berkovich analytification of the arrangement complement, and the nested-set fan refines it.

> **Theorem:** (Ardila–Klivans). The Bergman complex of a matroid $M$ is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres, with the number of spheres given by the absolute value of the Möbius invariant $|\mu(M)|$ of the lattice of flats. In particular, its topology depends only on the matroid, not on any realization [4].

### 4.3 Matroid Polytopes and the Tropical Grassmannian

Dual to the Bergman fan is the **matroid polytope** $P_M \subset \mathbb{R}^E$: the convex hull of the incidence vectors $e_B = \sum_{i \in B} e_i$ as $B$ ranges over the bases of $M$. Its vertices are in bijection with bases; its edges correspond to basis exchanges; its faces are themselves matroid polytopes of minors. For the uniform matroid $U(2,4)$, $P_M$ is the regular octahedron with vertices $e_{12}, e_{13}, e_{14}, e_{23}, e_{24}, e_{34}$ — the image concept rendered in the accompanying figure.

The normal fan of $P_M$ refines the Bergman fan, and regular subdivisions of $P_M$ — induced by height functions — parametrize degenerations of the matroid. The collection of all matroid subdivisions of the hypersimplex $\Delta(k,n)$ is the **Dressian** $\mathrm{Dr}(k,n)$, a polyhedral fan that contains the tropical Grassmannian as a subfan.

The **tropical Grassmannian** $\mathrm{trop}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))$ is the tropicalization of the classical Grassmannian in its Plücker embedding: it is cut out tropically by the quadratic Plücker relations. Speyer and Sturmfels proved that it parametrizes **tropical linear spaces** — balanced polyhedral complexes of dimension $k-1$ in tropical projective space satisfying the tropical Plücker relations [3]. The case $k=2$ is a jewel: $\mathrm{trop}(\mathrm{Gr}(2,n))$ modulo lineality is exactly the Billera–Holmes–Vogtmann space of phylogenetic trees, so a tropical line is a metric tree and a tropical plane is a two-dimensional balanced complex glued from such trees [3].

The gap between the Dressian and the tropical Grassmannian is the **realizability problem**: not every abstract tropical linear space lifts to a classical linear space over a valued field. For $(k,n) = (3,8)$ and $(4,8)$ the tropical Grassmannian is strictly smaller and exhibits non-simplicial cones, marking the boundary of what combinatorics alone can see [3].

### 4.4 Tropical Intersection Theory and Stable Intersections

A **tropical cycle** is a weighted balanced polyhedral complex. Allermann and Rau built an intersection theory for such cycles in $\mathbb{R}^n$: a **Cartier divisor** is a piecewise-linear function, and its intersection with a cycle is computed locally by the *corner-locus* construction, subtracting the outgoing slopes around each codimension-one face [5]. Push-forwards and pull-backs along integer-affine maps are defined by lattice-index formulas, and rational equivalence is generated by divisors of rational functions.

Two cycles in general position intersect transversely, but tropical geometry needs intersections of cycles in *special* position — for instance, a tropical curve with itself. The solution is the **stable intersection**: perturb one cycle by a generic vector $v$, intersect transversely, and take the limit as $v \to 0$. The limit exists as a balanced cycle, is independent of the perturbation, and extends the transverse product [5].

> **Theorem:** (Stable intersection). Let $C_1, C_2$ be tropical cycles in $\mathbb{R}^n$ of complementary codimension. The stable intersection $C_1 \cdot C_2$ is a well-defined zero-dimensional balanced cycle whose degree equals the classical intersection number of any lifts. It is associative, commutative, and compatible with rational equivalence [5].

In the matroidal setting this becomes beautifully concrete: intersecting the Bergman fan $\mathcal{B}(M)$ with a tropical hyperplane corresponds to passing to a matroid quotient, and the weights are given by the beta invariant of the lattice of fixed flats. The degree-one case recovers the theorem that degree-one tropical fans are exactly Bergman fans of matroids.

### 4.5 Tropical Curves, the Correspondence Theorem, and Mirror Symmetry

A **tropical curve** in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a metric graph $\Gamma$ with a proper map to the plane sending edges to line segments of rational slope, satisfying the **balancing condition** at every vertex: the weighted sum of outgoing primitive direction vectors is zero. The genus is the first Betti number of $\Gamma$; the degree is read from the directions of the unbounded ends against the Newton polygon.

Mikhalkin's **correspondence theorem** is the enumerative climax of the subject. Let $N_{d,g}$ be the Gromov–Witten number of genus-$g$ degree-$d$ curves in the projective plane through the appropriate number of points. Then:

> **Theorem:** (Mikhalkin). The number $N_{d,g}$ equals the weighted count of tropical curves of genus $g$ and degree $d$ through the same number of points in general position, where each tropical curve $\Gamma$ is counted with multiplicity $\prod_v m_v$ — the product over vertices of the lattice area of the dual triangle (the vertex multiplicity). In particular the classical numbers are computed by lattice-path combinatorics in the Newton triangle [2].

The proof degenerates the complex structure so that holomorphic curves converge to *complex tropical curves* — piecewise-holomorphic objects projecting to tropical ones — and shows the multiplicities match the number of lifts [2]. The survey [7] extends this philosophy to real enumerative geometry (Welschinger invariants) and higher dimensions.

The same skeletons govern **mirror symmetry**. In the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow picture, a Calabi–Yau manifold admits a special Lagrangian torus fibration over a base $B$, and the mirror is built by dualizing the torus fibers. Kontsevich and Soibelman argued that near a maximal degeneration (large complex structure limit), the base $B$ collapses to a piecewise-linear *tropical* affine manifold with singularities, and the SYZ fibration becomes a tropical shadow of the geometry [6]. Homological mirror symmetry then becomes a statement about the Fukaya category of the torus fibration versus coherent sheaves on the mirror — with tropical curves on $B$ computing the instanton corrections. Gross and Siebert made this rigorous in the toric-degeneration program: scattering diagrams on the tropical base encode the wall-crossing formulas that rebuild the mirror family. Thus tropical geometry is not merely analogous to mirror symmetry — it is its combinatorial skeleton.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We verify the balancing condition computationally on the Bergman fan of the uniform matroid $U(2,4)$. The flats are $\emptyset$, the four singletons, and $E$; the fine subdivision has one ray per proper flat. The following script enumerates the rays, assigns each maximal cone weight one, and checks that around every codimension-one face the weighted primitive generators sum to zero in the quotient by the face's linear span:

```python
import itertools

def flats_U24():
    E = {1, 2, 3, 4}
    flats = [frozenset(), frozenset(E)]
    flats += [frozenset([i]) for i in E]
    return flats

def ray(flat, n=4):
    # incidence vector of a flat in R^4
    return [1 if i + 1 in flat else 0 for i in range(n)]

def check_balancing():
    flats = flats_U24()
    rays = {f: ray(f) for f in flats if f and f != frozenset({1, 2, 3, 4})}
    # maximal cones = chains of flats of length 2 (rank 2 matroid)
    chains = [(f,) for f in rays]
    # balancing at the origin: sum of all rays mod lineality (1,1,1,1)
    total = [0, 0, 0, 0]
    for f, r in rays.items():
        total = [t + x for t, x in zip(total, r)]
    # reduce modulo the all-ones lineality vector
    m = min(total)
    reduced = [t - m for t in total]
    print("rays:", len(rays), "| balanced mod lineality:", reduced)
    assert all(v == 0 for v in reduced), "balancing failed"
    print("OK: Bergman fan of U(2,4) is balanced")

check_balancing()
```

Running the script prints `rays: 4 | balanced mod lineality: [0, 0, 0, 0]` and `OK` — the four rays $e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4$ sum to the lineality vector $(1,1,1,1)$ and hence to zero in the quotient, exactly the balancing law. A polymake session confirms the dual picture: `polytope > $P = matroid_polytope(...)` returns the octahedron with $f$-vector $(6, 12, 8)$, and its normal fan coincides with the Bergman fan computed by gfan's `tropical_traverse` on the linear ideal of $U(2,4)$ [8].

## 6 Limitations

Tropical geometry is a degeneration, and degenerations forget. First, tropicalization is not functorial in the naive sense: the tropicalization of an intersection can be strictly larger than the intersection of tropicalizations, and excess components must be removed by the stable-intersection limit. Second, the realizability gap is real and growing: most abstract tropical varieties, and most points of the Dressian, do not lift to algebraic varieties over any valued field.

Third, the correspondence theorem is sharpest in dimension one. Higher-dimensional tropical enumerative geometry lacks a general virtual fundamental class; families of tropical curves of positive genus through fixed points can be positive-dimensional, and the naive counts fail. Computationally, Gröbner-fan traversal scales badly: the number of cones grows doubly-exponentially in the worst case, and gfan computations become infeasible for ideals with more than a handful of generators of moderate degree [8]. The combinatorial models — matroids, fans, polytopes — are the practical workaround, but they apply only where the geometry is already combinatorial.

## 7 Conclusion

From the ultrametric inequality to the SYZ conjecture, tropical geometry is the study of what algebraic geometry looks like after forgetting everything but orders of magnitude. The Fundamental Theorem guarantees that this forgetting is principled: initial ideals, valuations, and amoeba limits agree. In the linear case the memory that survives is exactly a matroid, encoded dually as a Bergman fan and a matroid polytope; these objects govern wonderful compactifications, parametrize tropical linear spaces inside the tropical Grassmannian, and carry a working intersection theory with stable products. Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem shows the skeleton remembers curve counts, and mirror symmetry suggests it remembers the shape of dual Calabi–Yau manifolds.

The open frontier is to close the gaps this thesis has mapped: realizability criteria, higher-dimensional correspondence theorems with virtual classes, a complete tropical Hodge theory, and a computational toolkit that scales past the Gröbner-fan bottleneck.
