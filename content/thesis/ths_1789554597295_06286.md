# Diffusion on the Special Euclidean Group: Generative Protein Design from RFdiffusion to RoseTTAFold All-Atom

## Abstract

The confluence of score-based generative diffusion and deep structure prediction has produced the most capable *de novo* protein design paradigm to date. This thesis develops the formalism of diffusion on the special Euclidean group, where each residue is a rigid frame and design becomes sampling from a learned distribution over SE(3) raised to the power N [2]. We show how RoseTTAFold Diffusion (RFdiffusion) repurposes a pretrained structure prediction network as the denoising score function, achieving state-of-the-art binder design, symmetric oligomer design, and enzyme active-site scaffolding with experimental hit rates far above prior methods [1]. We trace the extension to all-atom modeling through RoseTTAFold All-Atom, which diffuses over protein, nucleic acid, and small-molecule atoms jointly, enabling binders to flexible and disordered targets [3][7]. Through case studies — a picomolar parathyroid hormone binder, designed antibodies validated by electron microscopy, de novo metallohydrolases with proficient catalytic activity, and symmetric nanomaterials confirmed by cryo-EM — we document the empirical record [1][6][8]. We contrast diffusion with the earlier hallucination paradigm [5], analyze ensemble sampling and allosteric design, and delineate current limitations.

##1 Intro

*De novo* protein design — the creation of amino acid sequences that fold into predetermined three-dimensional structures with prescribed functions — has long been the grand challenge of computational biochemistry. For two decades the field was dominated by physics-based methods built on the Rosetta energy function, which demanded expert-crafted rules for each new fold class and experimental screening campaigns in which tens of thousands of candidates might yield a single success [5]. The deep learning revolution in structure prediction, culminating in AlphaFold2 and RoseTTAFold, inverted the problem: networks trained to map sequence to structure had implicitly learned the manifold of physically realizable proteins, and this knowledge could be run in reverse to *generate* new proteins. The first demonstration of this inversion, deep network hallucination, used Monte Carlo sampling in sequence space to maximize the contrast between predicted and background inter-residue distance distributions, producing novel folds whose structures were confirmed by crystallography and NMR [5].

Diffusion models have now superseded hallucination as the generative engine of choice, and the reasons are both mathematical and empirical [1][2]. Hallucination optimizes in *sequence* space against a frozen predictor, which makes functional conditioning awkward and sampling slow. Diffusion operates directly in *structure* space: starting from pure noise, a learned score function iteratively denoises toward the data manifold, and functional constraints — a target to bind, a motif to scaffold, a symmetry to obey — enter naturally as conditioning of the reverse process [1]. The critical geometric insight is that protein backbones are most faithfully represented not as point clouds but as collections of rigid frames, one per residue, living on the special Euclidean group SE(3); diffusion on this manifold, combined with SE(3)-equivariant score networks, yields samples that respect the rigid-body geometry of the polypeptide chain by construction [2].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews score-based diffusion, the geometry of SE(3), and the hallucination baseline. Section 3 presents the RFdiffusion methodology: pretrained structure predictors as denoisers, self-conditioning, and the conditioning schemes that make binder, motif-scaffolding, and symmetric design possible, together with the all-atom extension. Section 4 develops four deep technical threads: the FrameDiff formalism for SE(3) diffusion, the denoiser-as-predictor design choice, all-atom co-generation, and ensemble/allosteric sampling. Section 5 compiles the empirical record with quantitative hit rates and a head-to-head comparison against hallucination. Section 6 confronts the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

##2 Background

**Score-based diffusion in Euclidean space.** A diffusion model defines a forward stochastic differential equation that progressively corrupts data with Gaussian noise, and a learned reverse process that denoises [4]. If $p_0$ is the data distribution and $p_t$ the marginal at noise level $t$, the reverse SDE requires only the *score* $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$, which a neural network $s_\theta(x, t)$ approximates via denoising score matching:

> **Theorem:** Under mild regularity, the reverse-time SDE $dx = (f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x))\,dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w}$ initialized at the noise prior generates exact samples from $p_0$ when the true score is used.

> **Proof:** Anderson's time-reversal theorem for diffusion processes shows the reverse drift is the forward drift minus $g(t)^2$ times the score of the marginal; substituting the learned score yields the generative SDE. ∎

**Why SE(3)?** A protein backbone of $N$ residues is well described by $N$ rigid transformations $T_n = (R_n, X_n)$ with rotation $R_n$ in SO(3) and translation $X_n$ in three-dimensional space, each frame anchoring the idealized backbone atoms N, Cα, C, and O of one residue [2]. The design space is therefore the product manifold SE(3) to the power $N$, and any generative model over backbones should be invariant to global rigid motions while its score network should be *equivariant*: rotating the input must rotate the predicted update identically. Equivariance is not cosmetic — it enforces that the model cannot memorize absolute coordinates and must learn genuinely geometric, physics-respecting local rules [2].

**Hallucination as the baseline.** Anishchenko and colleagues inverted the trRosetta network by Monte Carlo sequence optimization, obtaining 129 synthesized designs of which 27 were monodisperse with spectra consistent with the hallucinated structures, and three had experimentally determined structures matching the models closely [5]. This was a landmark, yet the approach had structural weaknesses: optimization in discrete sequence space is slow and prone to adversarial exploitation of the predictor, and conditioning on functional objectives (bind this target, scaffold this motif) required ad hoc loss engineering [1]. Diffusion addresses both weaknesses by generating in continuous structure space with conditioning built into the sampling procedure itself.

##3 Methodology

**Forward diffusion on frames.** RFdiffusion and FrameDiff define the noising process independently on rotations and translations. Translations follow the standard variance-preserving Gaussian diffusion in three-dimensional space. Rotations diffuse by Brownian motion on SO(3): at noise level $t$, each frame's rotation is perturbed by an isotropic Gaussian in the tangent space, mapped back to the manifold by the exponential map [2]. Because the prior at maximum noise is the SE(3)-invariant uniform distribution, the entire generative process is invariant to global rigid transformations, and every sampled backbone is equally valid in any reference frame.

**Denoising score matching on the manifold.** The network predicts the clean frames $\hat{T}(0)$ from noisy frames $T(t)$; the score in translation space is the usual residual, while the rotational score is computed in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ and transported appropriately [2]. Two auxiliary losses proved essential in practice: prediction of the backbone torsion angle ψ, which suppresses chain breaks, and a direct penalty on steric clashes between non-bonded atoms, which enforces physical plausibility at fine granularity [2].

**RoseTTAFold as the denoiser.** The decisive innovation of RFdiffusion is to initialize the denoising network from RoseTTAFold, a structure prediction network already trained on the protein universe, and fine-tune it for the denoising task [1]. The predictor has already internalized the intricate sequence–structure relationships of real proteins; fine-tuning redirects that knowledge toward generation. A *self-conditioning* mechanism feeds the network's own previous prediction back as an additional input, markedly improving sample quality at fixed compute [1].

**Conditioning for function.** Four conditioning modalities cover the major design challenges [1]:

1. **Unconditional and topology-constrained monomer design** — sampling novel folds, optionally constrained to a target secondary-structure topology or overall shape.
2. **Binder design** — the target protein is held fixed (or partially noised) while the binder is diffused around it, with the interface region receiving the strongest denoising guidance.
3. **Motif scaffolding** — catalytic or binding residues are fixed in space and the diffusion process builds a supporting scaffold around them; RFdiffusion2 removed the requirement to prespecify sequence positions, sampling them jointly with the backbone [8].
4. **Symmetric oligomer design** — the denoising updates are symmetrized under cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral, or icosahedral groups, producing assemblies from a single asymmetric unit [1].

**The all-atom extension.** RoseTTAFold All-Atom (RFAA) generalizes the architecture to arbitrary biomolecules: a 1D track carries chemical element types, a 2D track carries chemical bonds, and a 3D track carries coordinates with explicit chirality handling [3]. Its companion design tool, RFdiffusion All-Atom, co-generates protein structure together with bound small molecules, nucleic acids, or metal ions, so that protein–ligand complementarity is designed jointly rather than docked after the fact [3].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The FrameDiff Formalism: Principled Diffusion on SE(3)

FrameDiff, developed by Yim and collaborators, supplies the theoretical foundations that earlier frame-diffusion attempts lacked [2]. The central difficulty is that SO(3) is compact while translations are not, so a single noise schedule cannot serve both; FrameDiff therefore treats the two components with separate variance schedules and derives the denoising score matching objective on the product manifold rigorously. The translational score is the familiar Gaussian form, while the rotational score exploits the fact that Brownian motion on SO(3) has a known transition density expressed through the heat kernel, whose score can be computed in closed form via the axis-angle parameterization [2].

Equivariance is enforced architecturally through invariant point attention and frame-aligned updates: every layer's outputs transform correctly under global rotations, so the learned vector field on SE(3) to the power $N$ is itself equivariant [2]. Notably, FrameDiff achieves designable monomers up to 500 residues *without* any pretrained structure prediction network — 17.4 million parameters trained for one week on two A100 GPUs — demonstrating that the SE(3) diffusion formalism alone, given enough data, captures the protein backbone distribution [2]. Its successor FrameFlow reformulates the same geometry under flow matching, requiring five times fewer sampling steps while doubling designability, which points toward simulation-free training as the likely future of the field [4].

### 4.2 Why a Structure Predictor Makes the Best Denoiser

The RFdiffusion design choice — fine-tuning RoseTTAFold rather than training a score network from scratch — deserves scrutiny, because it is the single decision most responsible for the method's experimental dominance [1]. A structure predictor trained on the Protein Data Bank has seen every fold class, every interface geometry, every recurring motif in the known protein universe; its internal representations encode an extraordinarily rich prior over physically realizable local geometry. Viewed through the diffusion lens, *denoising a corrupted backbone toward the nearest plausible native-like structure is nearly the same task as predicting structure from a corrupted or incomplete input* — the predictor is already a denoiser, and diffusion fine-tuning merely teaches it the noise schedule [1].

Empirically, this transfer pays off dramatically. RFdiffusion generates designable backbones at rates far exceeding FrameDiff trained from scratch, and its samples generalize to topologies absent from the training set, including TIM-barrel-like structures with many more helices and strands than any natural TIM barrel [1]. The self-conditioning trick — concatenating the previous step's prediction as an auxiliary input — functions as a learned iterative refinement reminiscent of the recycling in AlphaFold2, and ablations show it contributes substantially to fine-grained physical realism [1].

### 4.3 All-Atom Co-Generation: Ligands, Nucleic Acids, and Metals

Classical protein design stopped at the backbone plus, at best, protein side chains. RoseTTAFold All-Atom extends the generative machinery to the full biomolecular menagerie by representing every atom — protein, DNA, RNA, small molecule, ion — in a unified three-track architecture where the 2D track encodes covalent connectivity and the 3D track enforces correct chirality [3]. This matters because function lives at atomic resolution: a binder's affinity is decided by hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and shape complementarity at the interface, and a catalyst's power resides in the precise electrostatic preorganization of a handful of atoms [3][8].

RFdiffusion All-Atom turns prediction into generation: given a target small molecule such as the cardiac drug digoxigenin, the model diffuses a protein around it, jointly optimizing backbone geometry and interfacial contacts [3]. Experimental characterization showed that a subset of generated proteins bound their intended small-molecule targets — the first demonstration that protein–small-molecule binding can be created by pure computation [3]. The third-generation system, RFdiffusion3, extends this to DNA binders and advanced enzymes, and has been released open source, democratizing a capability that was recently the exclusive province of a handful of laboratories [8].

### 4.4 Conformational Ensembles, Allostery, and Dynamics

Proteins are not statues; they populate ensembles of interconverting conformations, and function — catalysis, signaling, allosteric regulation — emerges from the ensemble, not from any single structure. Diffusion models are, in principle, *samplers*, and a tantalizing direction is to use them as Boltzmann-like generators of conformational ensembles rather than single-structure oracles. Early evidence supports this: varying the random seed and noise schedule of RFdiffusion produces structurally diverse yet designable backbones, and conditioning on partial structures can steer sampling toward specific metastable states [1][2].

Allosteric design — engineering a protein whose active site reorganizes upon effector binding at a distant site — is the natural grand challenge for ensemble-aware diffusion. The formalism already contains the necessary ingredients: one can diffuse two coupled conformational states simultaneously, with a shared sequence but distinct structural targets, and reward large, coordinated rearrangements between them. Success here would move the field from designing *structures* to designing *free-energy landscapes*, arguably the deepest unsolved problem in computational protein science.

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

The empirical record of diffusion-based design is best appreciated quantitatively, because the numbers mark a discontinuity with everything that came before.

**Binders.** The flagship RFdiffusion result is a *picomolar* binder to parathyroid hormone, generated by pure computation with higher affinity than any previously designed binder prior to experimental optimization [1]. A follow-up campaign against flexible and disordered targets — historically considered undruggable — produced high-affinity binders in the 3 to 100 nanomolar range against amylin, C-peptide, VP48, G3BP1, the IL-2 receptor γ-chain, and the pathogenic prion core; the amylin binders dissolved amyloid fibrils linked to type 2 diabetes in laboratory tests [7]. In the antibody domain, fine-tuned RFdiffusion generated binders against influenza hemagglutinin and a *Clostridioides difficile* toxin; electron microscopy of five antibodies showed four engaging their targets exactly as designed, and collaborator-driven affinity maturation via the OrthoRep system further improved binding strength [6].

**Symmetric assemblies.** RFdiffusion readily generated dihedral, tetrahedral, and icosahedral architectures. Size-exclusion chromatography confirmed 38 D2, seven D3, and three D4 designs at the expected molecular weights; negative-stain EM class averages and 3D reconstructions matched the design models, including the roughly 45-degree offset between tetrameric subunits of the D4 design HE0537 [1]. Among 48 icosahedral candidates, HE0902 — a 15-nanometer highly porous assembly — formed homogeneous particles whose reconstruction closely matched the design, with triangular hubs arrayed around empty fivefold axes: a purpose-built nanomaterial and prospective vaccine scaffold [1].

**Enzymes.** De novo enzyme design demands scaffolding catalytic residues with sub-angstrom precision around a transition state. RFdiffusion2 removed the requirement to prespecify catalytic residue positions, and from thousands of computational metallohydrolase designs, 96 were screened experimentally: five showed proficient hydrolysis activity, and the best design, ZETA_1, achieved catalytic efficiency several orders of magnitude above previously designed metallohydrolases, with the improved thermostability characteristic of de novo scaffolds [8].

> **Theorem (informal, designability):** A generated backbone is *designable* if some amino acid sequence folds into it; in practice this is certified when a structure prediction network, given a designed sequence, returns the target backbone with high confidence (self-consistency RMSD under 2 angstroms) [2][4].

> **Proof sketch:** Self-consistency is a fixed-point argument — the composition of sequence design and structure prediction maps the backbone to itself. High-confidence fixed points of this map concentrate on the physically realizable manifold learned from the PDB. ∎

The comparison with hallucination is instructive rather than dismissive [5]. Hallucination proved that predictors could be inverted; diffusion industrialized the inversion. Where hallucination required thousands of Monte Carlo steps per design in sequence space, diffusion generates in tens to hundreds of network evaluations in structure space, and where hallucination needed bespoke loss terms per application, diffusion absorbs each new task as a conditioning scheme [1][5]. The hit-rate economics flipped accordingly: from tens of thousands of candidates per success in the Rosetta era, to roughly a hundred in the hallucination era, to as few as *one* tested design per challenge with RFdiffusion [1].

##6 Limitations

Diffusion-based design is a breakthrough, not a solved problem, and its limitations define the research frontier.

*Static-structure bias.* The models generate single low-energy conformations, while function depends on ensembles, kinetics, and excited states. True Boltzmann sampling of the conformational landscape remains out of reach; current "ensemble" generation is heuristic rather than thermodynamically grounded.

*Atomic-resolution fidelity.* Backbone designability is largely solved, but side-chain packing, loop modeling at interfaces, and especially small-molecule pose accuracy still lag. RFdiffusion All-Atom's experimental hit rates, while historic, remain lower than backbone-only campaigns, reflecting the difficulty of jointly optimizing chemistry and geometry [3].

*Data bias.* Every model discussed here is trained, directly or by transfer, on the Protein Data Bank — a biased, incomplete sample of protein space skewed toward crystallizable, soluble, mesophilic proteins. Designs inherit these biases; membrane proteins, intrinsically disordered regions, and exotic chemistries remain frontier territory [2][3].

*Novelty versus designability.* Metrics reveal a persistent tradeoff: pushing sampling toward high novelty (low maximum TM-score to the PDB) degrades designability, and vice versa [4]. The field lacks a principled way to target the Pareto frontier rather than accepting whatever tradeoff a given noise schedule yields.

*Compute and throughput.* High-quality sampling still requires hundreds of network evaluations per design, and experimental validation — synthesis, expression, purification, biophysical assay — remains the true bottleneck. Computational hit rates mean little until the wet-lab loop accelerates commensurately.

##7 Conclusion

In under three years, diffusion on the special Euclidean group has carried protein design from a craft of expert rules to a generative engineering discipline. The FrameDiff formalism put frame diffusion on rigorous footing [2]; RFdiffusion showed that a pretrained structure predictor is the ideal denoiser and delivered picomolar binders, validated antibodies, and designed nanomaterials [1][6]; RoseTTAFold All-Atom extended the paradigm to the full atomic menagerie of biology [3]; and the RFdiffusion2/3 lineage is now producing functional enzymes from minimal catalytic specifications [8]. Against the hallucination baseline — itself a landmark — diffusion wins on speed, conditioning flexibility, and experimental hit rates by orders of magnitude [5].

The deepest open problems are now dynamical: sampling true conformational ensembles, designing allosteric free-energy landscapes, and closing the loop between computation and high-throughput experiment. If the current trajectory holds, the next discontinuity will not be a better structure generator but a *dynamics* generator — and the SE(3) diffusion machinery developed here will be its foundation.
