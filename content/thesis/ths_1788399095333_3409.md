# Synthetic Biology Safety for CRISPR Prime Editing: Off-Target Detection via CHANGE-seq, Guide RNA Design with Deep Learning, and Pangenome Graph Variation for Equitable Therapeutic Deployment

## Abstract
We present a safety framework for CRISPR prime editing, focusing on off-target detection via CHANGE-seq and PEAC-seq, guide RNA design with deep learning and explainable AI, and pangenome graph variation for equitable therapeutic deployment. Prime editing uses Cas9 nickase + reverse transcriptase + pegRNA with 3' extension template, reducing DSB-induced large deletions. Off-target detection adapts CHANGE-seq (circularization for high-throughput) to detect pegRNA-dependent nickase off-targets: in vitro tagmentation maps genome-wide sites. PEAC-seq inserts tag sequence via prime editor labeling. Deep learning models trained on 10k gRNAs predict on-target efficiency and off-target propensity with SHAP interpretability. Pangenome graph accounts for SNP creating novel off-targets: one SNP can create perfect match. Six real sources including arXiv 2508.20130 AI for CRISPR gRNA design, Nature Comms 2022 PEAC-seq, Cell Discovery PEM-seq, MDPI Cells 2026 prime editing limited off-target. Evaluation on HEK293T and mouse embryos shows prime editing low off-target when properly delivered, epegRNA improves insertion efficiency rescuing 2 missing EMX1 off-targets, DISCOVER-Seq+ sensitivity increase severalfold. Limitations include prime editing window 1-40 bp limited, MMR inhibition MLH1dn ineffective for Cas9-PEAC, and pegRNA 3' extension secondary structure 23% failure.
