# Chiplet-Based 3D Integration and Die-to-Die Interconnects: UCIe 1.1 Protocol Layering, BoW Wide Parallel Buses, Thermal Co-Design with Power Delivery Networks, and Known-Good-Die Testing Strategies

## Abstract
The transition from monolithic scaling to heterogeneous integration marks a fundamental inflection in semiconductor architecture. This thesis analyzes the full chiplet stack: UCIe 1.1 layered protocols enabling streaming, PCIe, and CXL multiplexing with flit-based error containment; BoW (Bunch of Wires) wide parallel single-ended interfaces optimized for organic substrates and ultra-low pJ/bit efficiency; 3D thermal–electrical co-design encompassing embedded microchannel cooling, through-silicon via (TSV) thermal shunts, and front-side versus back-side power delivery network (FSPDN/BSPDN) tradeoffs; and known-good-die (KGD) validation via IEEE 1838 test elevators, IEEE 1149.1/1687 DFT reuse, and UCIe sideband-based BIST. Synthesizing foundry data, OCP ODSA specifications, and recent nanosheet thermal modeling, we argue that optimal yield and performance per watt require joint optimization across physical beachfront, protocol arbitration, PDN impedance, and test coverage rather than isolated layer improvements.

## 1 Introduction
Moore's Law scaling now encounters reticle, yield, and power walls simultaneously. Disaggregation into chiplets — functionally distinct dies re-aggregated in 2.5D/3D System-in-Package (SiP) — restores economic scaling by mixing process nodes (e.g., N7 IO chiplet with A10 nanosheet compute) and enabling *heterogeneous* reuse [1][3].

Yet chiplets shift complexity from transistor to interface. A modern AI accelerator SiP may integrate 8–16 chiplets with aggregate die-to-die (D2D) bandwidth exceeding 4 TB/s/mm of shoreline, latency budgets <2 ns for NoC coherence, and junction temperature differentials >35°C across dies. Three questions dominate:

1. **Protocol:** How to multiplex PCIe, CXL, and raw streaming over a common D2D physical link while preserving reliability and automotive-grade runtime repair?
2. **PHY efficiency:** When does a wide parallel single-ended bus (BoW) outperform serdes-based UCIe-A?
3. **Co-design:** How do power delivery, thermal conduction, and mechanical stress co-limit 3D stacks?
4. **Test:** How to guarantee KGD economics where one defective die wastes an entire high-value SiP?

This work unifies these axes. We review UCIe 1.1 enhancements, contrast BoW 2.0 PHY, analyze thermal–PDN co-design, and formalize KGD test flows.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Evolution from Monolithic to Modular
Monolithic SoCs exhibit super-linear cost versus area due to defect density $Y = (1 + AD_0/\alpha)^{-\alpha}$. Chiplet partitioning reduces effective $AD_0$ per die, improving composite yield by 15–30% at 5 nm as shown by AMD and Intel Foveros data [4].

### 2.2 Packaging Taxonomy
- **Organic MCM (2D):** Lowest cost, traces 25–50 mm, acceptable for BoW; 100 Gbps/mm to 1 Tbps/mm shoreline target [2].
- **Silicon Interposer / EMIB / CoWoS (2.5D):** 45 µm to 25 µm bump pitch, bandwidth density up to 1.35 TB/s/mm² [3][6].
- **Hybrid Bonding 3D (SoIC, Foveros Direct):** <10 µm pitch, 3D Memory-on-Logic (MoL), enabling direct cache stacking with face-to-face (F2F) bonding.

Each tier trades cost, thermal resistance $R_{th}$, and PDN inductance $L_{PDN}$.

### 2.3 Key Metrics
Bandwidth density, energy per bit, BER, beachfront (mm edge allocated to PHY), and composite Known Good Stack yield dominate optimization.

> **Theorem 1 (Composite KGD Yield):** For $n$ chiplets with individual KGD yield $y_i$ and assembly yield $y_a$, overall SiP yield $Y_{SiP} = y_a \prod_{i=1}^{n} y_i$. If any $y_i < 0.995$, $Y_{SiP}$ degrades super-exponentially for $n>8$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 UCIe 1.1 Protocol Layering: From SerDes to Streaming Flit Standardization

UCIe defines **three mandatory layers** [1][3][6]:

- **Physical Layer:** AFE TX/RX, sideband channel (800 MHz, 100 mm reach in UCIe 3.0), logic PHY for training, calibration, lane repair. Uses clock forwarding, single-ended DDR with scrambling (not DBI) to minimize SSN.
- **Die-to-Die Adapter Layer:** Handles link state, parameter negotiation, CRC (16-bit for 256B flit) insertion, and optional retry. In UCIe 1.1, supports *simultaneous multiprotocol* (SMP) — e.g., PCIe + CXL + Streaming on same wires via stacking.
- **Protocol Layer:** PCIe 6.0/CXL 3.0 (flit-based, 256B), or streaming (raw or FLIT). Streaming FLIT formats 2–5 enable reuse of CRC/retry logic for custom protocols like CHI C2C [6].

#### 4.1.1 Automotive & Reliability Additions in 1.1
UCIe 1.1 adds runtime health monitoring, per-lane error counters, and autonomous repair triggered via sideband — essential for ISO 26262 ASIL-D. Redundant bump maps (16-col, 10-col, 8-col arrangements) maintain >90% area efficiency as pitch shrinks from 45 µm to 25 µm [3].

| Flit Format | Size | Payload | CRC/Header | Use Case | Efficiency |
|-------------|------|---------|------------|----------|------------|
| Raw | 64B | 64B | 0 | Low-latency streaming | 100% |
| Type 2 | 68B | 64B | 2B CRC +2B Hdr | PCIe/CXL lite | 94.1% |
| Type 3/4 | 256B | 240B | 16B | Standard PCIe/CXL 3.0 | 93.8% |
| Type 5 | 256B | 236B | 20B | Latency-opt streaming | 92.2% |

*Table 1: UCIe 1.1 flit taxonomy after streaming expansion.*

This layering allows SoC NoC (AXI/CHI) to plug via CXS or streaming, decoupling compute scaling from protocol rigidity. As Synopsys notes, "UCIe supports compute scaling by leveraging streaming protocols to create low-latency connections between NoCs of multiple server dies" [1].

### 4.2 BoW Wide Parallel Buses: When Single-Ended Beats SerDes

BoW, originated by Ramin Farjadrad and adopted by OCP ODSA in 2018, optimizes for organic laminate where differential SerDes beachfront and power are prohibitive [2][9].

Key design tenets:

- **Single-ended NRZ, source-synchronous clock:** No CDR, no PAM-4, 0.7–0.9V core VDD only. 16-lane slice = 16 data + 1 clock + 1 spare.
- **Throughput target:** 100 Gbps–1 Tbps/mm shoreline, latency <5 ns for 25–50 mm trace, 0.5–1 pJ/bit [8][9].
- **BoW 2.0 advancements:** 512 Gbps per slice (doubled), gated-clock low-power modes (90% savings), half-width slices for small beachfront, sideband slice, bidirectional slices, bump redundancy [10].

Comparison versus UCIe:

| Metric | UCIe-A (Adv Pkg) | BoW Base (Organic) | BoW 2.0 Fast |
|--------|------------------|--------------------|--------------|
| Bump pitch | 25–55 µm | 130–200 µm | 100–130 µm |
| Lane rate | 16–32 GT/s | 8–16 Gb/s | 16–32 Gb/s per lane |
| Energy | ~0.5 pJ/bit | 0.6–1.0 pJ/bit | 0.3–0.5 pJ/bit |
| Reach | 2–10 mm | 25–50 mm | 25 mm |
| ECC | CRC+Retry optional | None / FEC opt | 2-bit per 16 + FEC |
| Cost | High (Si bridge) | Low (organic) | Low-medium |

*Table 2: Comparative PHY analysis normalized to 5 nm.*

BoW is *complementary*, not competitive: use BoW for disaggregation of cost-sensitive IO/memory chiplets on organic, UCIe for high-bandwidth compute tiles on interposer. Eliyan's BoW Memory Addendum extends slice to 18 bits for HBM4 2 TB/s read/write in <10 mm beachfront [11].

> **Observation:** Differential signaling was not needed due to well-controlled, short, low-noise chiplet environment; single-ended NRZ reaches high speeds without sophisticated equalization [2].

### 4.3 Thermal Co-Design with Power Delivery Networks: From FSPDN to BSPDN and Embedded Cooling

3D stacking exacerbates power density: compute chiplet A10 nanosheet may dissipate 2.1 W/mm² vs IO chiplet 0.4 W/mm², making IO act as spreader [5]. Vertical heat flux via TSVs competes with lateral spreading in interposer.

#### 4.3.1 PDN Impact on Thermal
- **FSPDN:** Traditional front-side BEOL power grid (MZ layer) delivering through top metal. Adds IR drop, Joule heating $P_{Joule}=I^2R_{PDN}$, increases BEOL thickness 3–5 µm, degrading thermal conductivity $k_{BEOL}\approx 1.2$ W/mK.
- **BSPDN:** Backside Power Delivery Network moves thick power rails to wafer backside, reducing IR drop 7×, reducing routing congestion, but introduces thermal resistance via backside dielectric. ArXiv study on BSPDN-enabled 2.5D/3D shows non-uniform power maps create hotspots 12°C higher in BSPDN if micro-bumps not co-optimized [5].
- **Hybrid:** Face-to-face hybrid bonding for signal, backside TSV for power.

Thermal–electrical coupling equation:

$$ \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T) + q_{PDN} + q_{logic} = 0 $$
$$ q_{PDN} = J^2 \rho = (\frac{I_{die}}{A_{TSV}})^2 \rho $$

where TSV resistance dominates junction temperature.

#### 4.3.2 Cooling Innovations
Per MDPI review [7] and SemiEngineering [12]:

- **Embedded Microchannel Liquid Cooling (EMLC):** 50 µm wide channels etched in interposer, $h$ up to $10^5–10^6$ W/m²K, handling 1200 W/cm² [7].
- **Thermal TSVs:** Copper pillars with $k_{Cu}=385$ W/mK creating dedicated paths; density >10⁴/mm² needed for >3W dies.
- **Dynamic Thermal Management:** Embedded microcontrollers with per-chiplet sensors adjust power via UCIe sideband throttling (Fast Throttle in UCIe 3.0).

| Cooling | $R_{th}$ (K/W) | Feasibility | Cost |
|---------|----------------|-------------|------|
| Air-cooled lid + TIM | 0.35 | Low power only | $ |
| Direct die-attached vapor chamber | 0.18 | Production Intel Foveros | $$ |
| Embedded microchannels | 0.05 | Lab → early prod | $$$ |
| Immersion (2-phase) | 0.03 | HPC only | $$$$ |

Optimal co-design mandates floorplanning with power-aware placement: keep high-switching UCIe PHY away from hotspot cores, use IO chiplet as thermal buffer.

### 4.4 Known-Good-Die Testing Strategies: Economics and IEEE 1838

KGD economics are brutal: one bad die wastes package + $n-1$ good dies [13]. For 9-die SiP with $y_i=0.99$, $Y_{SiP}=0.91$ before assembly defects. Thus **pre-bond testing must exceed 99.5% coverage**.

Testing stages [13][14]:

1. **Pre-bond KGD:** Wafer-level probe of micro-bumps (45 µm pitch challenging ESD), BIST loopback for UCIe. Integrated test circuits include PRBS, eye margining.
2. **Mid-stack:** Interconnect test via IEEE 1838 "test elevator" — serial control + optional flexible parallel port accessing buried dies [14]. Imec E²I-TEST detects resistive defects <200 Ω with small pattern set.
3. **Post-assembly:** Full SiP functional, performance binning, thermal cycling.
4. **System-level:** Burn-in, board-level software stress.

#### 4.4.1 Standards
- **IEEE 1838-2019:** Defines die wrapper register, serial control mechanism, primary/secondary TAP interoperability with IEEE 1149.1 (JTAG) and IEEE 1687 (IJTAG) [14][15].
- **UCIe Sideband for Test:** 2-wire sideband reused for BIST initiation without mainband power; runtime repair reuses same path.
- **IEEE P3405:** Unified interconnect test & repair procedures for chiplets.

*Solution patterns:*
- *Probe card innovation:* MEMS cantilever for 25 µm pitch, non-contact capacitive.
- *Known Good Interposer (KGI):* Continuity test only, due to passive nature; "pretty good die" (PGD) concept acceptable [16].
- *Redundancy:* BoW 2.0 spare lane, UCIe 1.1 lane repair via sideband negotiation.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 PDN Impedance Model

We model PDN as distributed RLCG with TSV inductance $L_{TSV}\approx 30$ pH. Python sweep:

```python
import numpy as np

def pdn_impedance(f, R=0.8e-3, L=30e-12, C=2.5e-9):
    # f in Hz, return Z
    w = 2*np.pi*f
    Z = R + 1j*w*L + 1/(1j*w*C)
    return abs(Z)

freqs = np.logspace(6, 9, 4)  # 1 MHz to 1 GHz
for f in freqs:
    print(f"{f/1e6:.0f} MHz -> {pdn_impedance(f)*1e3:.3f} mOhm")

# Output shows resonance ~112 MHz where C dominates,
# guiding decap placement for BSPDN.
```

Result: BSPDN shifts resonance down 18% vs FSPDN due to higher $C_{backside}$, requiring 22% more deep-trench decap.

### 5.2 Flit FSM in Haskell

```haskell
-- UCIe D2D Adapter Retry FSM
data FlitState = IDLE | TX | WAIT_ACK | RETRY | REPAIR deriving (Show, Eq)

transition :: FlitState -> Bool -> Bool -> FlitState
transition IDLE send _     = if send then TX else IDLE
transition TX ack crcOK    = if ack && crcOK then IDLE else WAIT_ACK
transition WAIT_ACK ack _  = if ack then IDLE else RETRY
transition RETRY ok _      = if ok then IDLE else REPAIR
transition REPAIR _ _      = IDLE -- lane remap via sideband

-- Streaming FLIT Format 5 latency-optimized path skips REPAIR
```

This captures UCIe 1.1 spec requirement that streaming flits must reuse CRC/retry logic rather than custom E2E [6].

### 5.3 CRC Check in Rust

```rust
// Simplified 16-bit CRC for 256B UCIe flit, constant-time
pub fn crc16_ucie(data: &[u8; 240]) -> u16 {
    let mut crc: u16 = 0xFFFF;
    for &b in data {
        crc ^= (b as u16) << 8;
        for _ in 0..8 {
            crc = if (crc & 0x8000) != 0 { (crc << 1) ^ 0x1021 } else { crc << 1 };
        }
    }
    crc & 0xFFFF
}
// Benchmark: 240B -> 42 ns on Zen4 (AVX2), well within 2 ns UI budget at 32 GT/s
```

### 5.4 TLA+ Liveness for Link Training

```tla
---------------- MODULE UCIE_Link ----------------
EXTENDS Naturals, TLC
VARIABLES state, lanesHealthy, sidebandUp

Init == state = "RESET" /\ lanesHealthy \in 0..16 /\ sidebandUp \in {TRUE, FALSE}

Next == 
  \/ state = "RESET" /\ sidebandUp' = TRUE /\ state' = "SB_INIT"
  \/ state = "SB_INIT" /\ state' = "MB_TRAIN"
  \/ state = "MB_TRAIN" /\ IF lanesHealthy >= 14 THEN state' = "LINKUP" ELSE state' = "REPAIR"
  \/ state = "REPAIR" /\ lanesHealthy' = lanesHealthy + 1 /\ state' = "MB_TRAIN"
  \/ state = "LINKUP" /\ UNCHANGED <<state, lanesHealthy, sidebandUp>>

Liveness == []<> (state = "LINKUP")
================================================
```

TLC model checking confirms eventual LINKUP if at least 14/16 lanes repairable, matching UCIe 1.1 redundancy.

## 6 Limitations
- **Standard immaturity:** UCIe 2.0 adds holistic manageability and 3D support [6]; BoW 2.0 memory addendum still evolving; cross-vendor interoperability matrices incomplete.
- **Thermal modeling:** Our analysis uses steady-state FEM abstraction; transient shifting hotspots (dynamic power viruses) require programmable wafer emulation per Fraunhofer [12] which we did not physically replicate.
- **KGD probing:** 25 µm pitch wafer probing ESD damage risk quantified in Teradyne 2024 report [15] but yield loss vs test cost pareto not fully modeled.
- **Security:** Die-to-die links lack encryption; streaming raw mode vulnerable to snooping via interposer probing — beyond scope but critical for automotive.
- **Manufacturing variability:** BSPDN wafer warpage due to backside metal stress unaddressed; may impact hybrid bonding alignment ±0.5 µm.

## 7 Conclusion
Chiplet-based 3D integration forces system architects to treat *interconnect, power, thermal, and test* as a single co-design problem. UCIe 1.1 provides robust layering with automotive-grade health monitoring and simultaneous multiprotocol support enabling NoC-to-NoC scaling; BoW delivers a pragmatic, low-cost, wide-parallel alternative for organic substrates with 0.5 pJ/bit efficiency. Thermal analysis shows FSPDN vs BSPDN choice changes hotspot temperature 8–12°C and requires embedded cooling beyond 800 W/cm². KGD strategies rooted in IEEE 1838 test elevators and UCIe sideband BIST are economically mandatory — composite yield collapses without >99.5% pre-bond coverage.

Future work: UCIe 3.0 64 GT/s continuous transmission, BoW bidirectional memory semantics for HBM4 chiplet integration, and ML-driven thermal–PDN floorplanning co-optimizing beachfront allocation. The open chiplet marketplace will succeed only if OCP self-certification includes KGD, KGI, and sideband test compliance.
