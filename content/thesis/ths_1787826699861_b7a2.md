# Optical Circuit Switching for ML Collectives: RotorNet, Sirius, and Topology-Aware AllReduce Scheduling under Wavelength Constraints

## Abstract
Optical circuit switching (OCS) promises to break the power and radix walls of electrical datacenters, yet its reconfiguration latency and wavelength constraints collide with the synchronous, collective-heavy traffic of large ML training. This thesis unifies three architectural lineages—RotorNet's demand-oblivious rotor schedules [1][2], Sirius's nanosecond tunable-laser and passive grating fabric [3], and TopoOpt's topology-parallelization co-design [4]—to analyze topology-aware AllReduce scheduling under WDM and MEMS constraints. We formalize OCS as a time-expanded edge-colored multigraph, model wavelength assignment as edge-coloring of AllReduce permutations, and derive lower bounds for AllReduce completion under TotientPerms [4]. Methodology combines TLA+ verification of rotor slot safety, Python/Rust simulation of Sirius tuning, and Haskell proofs of wavelength contention freedom. Empirical synthesis from Google Palomar/Apollo production [5], Opera [6], and sub-ns soliton switching [7] shows RotorNet achieves full bisection with 1.5x stretch, Sirius demonstrates 3.84 ns end-to-end reconfiguration with <912 ps laser tuning [3], TopoOpt reduces training iteration time 3.4x vs fat-tree [4], and modern all-to-all formulations reduce completion 44% [8].

## 1 Intro

The scaling crisis is stark: datacenter traffic grows **~30% YoY**, electrical switch radix stagnates due to SerDes power $\propto$ *bandwidth*, and Moore's law slowdown makes next-gen 51.2T switches economically untenable [3]. Simultaneously, *ML collectives* dominate: a single 175B parameter AllReduce transfers **350 GB** per iteration, repeated thousands of times, creating *few-to-few*, *persistent*, *synchronous* flows ill-suited for packet-switched Clos.

**Optical circuit switching** offers an escape: photons stay photons, MEMS mirrors steer light with **~1.5 dB insertion loss** and **~100W per 300-port switch** vs 3000W electrical [5], and wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) multiplies capacity without electrical upgrade. Google's Mission Apollo has deployed *thousands* of Palomar OCS [5] as Jupiter's core, consuming **40% less power** and **50x less downtime** than EPS, validating OCS at hyperscale.

Yet OCS is not a drop-in replacement for ML. Three tensions arise:

- ***Reconfiguration latency vs collective synchrony***: MEMS reconfiguration **10-20 µs** [1] dwarfs 1 µs packet times; AllReduce barriers stall if circuits flap mid-transfer.
- ***Wavelength contention***: Arrayed Waveguide Grating Routers (AWGRs) route by $\lambda$; two simultaneous flows needing same $\lambda$ to same grating collide, requiring *graph coloring*.
- ***Demand estimation impossibility***: ML traffic is predictable at job level but *bursty* at microsecond level; centralized schedulers that collect demand matrix $D_{ij}$ every 100 µs do not scale to 10k GPUs.

This thesis argues for **demand-oblivious yet topology-aware** designs: *RotorNet* [1] and its practical successor in SIGCOMM'24 [2] for slot-based obliviousness, *Sirius* [3] for nanosecond granularity, and *TopoOpt* [4] for co-optimizing topology with TotientPerms AllReduce permutations. We unify them under a *wavelength-constrained AllReduce scheduling* framework.

> **Theorem 1 (Rotor Bisection):** A RotorNet fabric with $R$ rotor switches each cycling through $K$ matchings achieves full bisection bandwidth with stretch $\le 2 - 1/R$ for uniform traffic without central control [1].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Optical Switching Primitives

Three technologies dominate:

| Technology | Switching Time | Radix | Loss | Power | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3D MEMS (Palomar, Lumentum R300) | 10-20 µs | 136×136 (128 usable) | 1.5 dB typ | ~100W | [5] |
| Rotor switch (RotorNet/Opera) | 1.7 µs sync slot | $N$ via $R$ switches | <2 dB | Passive core | [1][2] |
| Tunable laser + AWG (Sirius) | 912 ps tuning, 3.84 ns e2e | Flat $O(N)$ gratings | <4 dB | No EPS | [3] |
| SOA + soliton microcomb | sub-ns (0.6 ns demo) | 4-20 $\lambda$ | 6 ns guard | Lab | [7] |

MEMS relies on *continuous tilt* mirrors steering collimated beams in free space. Sirius eliminates moving parts: **wavelength is the destination**. A node tunes its laser to $\lambda_i$, passive grating diffracts $\lambda_i$ to port $i$ deterministically, enabling *single-hop* nanosecond switching if all nodes coordinate time [3].

### 2.2 ML Collectives: AllReduce Taxonomy

Distributed training uses *data parallelism* where gradients $G\in\mathbb{R}^d$ must be AllReduced across $N$ GPUs:

$$
G_{\text{final}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N G_i, \quad \text{communicated to all}
$$

Algorithms:

- *Ring AllReduce*: $2(N-1)$ steps, bandwidth-optimal for large messages, latency $O(N)$. Each node talks to successor only — *perfect for fixed circuits*.
- *Tree / Double-Binary*: $2\log N$ steps, latency-optimal for small messages.
- *All-to-All*: For MoE expert parallelism, $N^2$ flows; dominates TopoOpt analysis [8].

Critical property exploited by TopoOpt [4]: **AllReduce permutations are mutable**. Ring order can be any permutation $p$ of nodes where $p$ is a single cycle; $\phi(N)$ such permutations exist where $\phi$ is Euler's totient for prime $N$. TotientPerms selects $k$ permutations that simultaneously minimize hop-count for model-parallel traffic.

### 2.3 Wavelength Constraints

WDM: single fiber carries $W$ lambdas, each $\approx$ 100 Gbps (50 Gbps in Sirius prototype). AWGR of degree $D$ routes $\lambda_j$ from input $i$ to output $(i+j) \bmod D$. Thus:

- *Contention*: two inputs cannot use same $\lambda$ to same output simultaneously.
- *Coloring*: scheduling AllReduce over $W$ lambdas is *edge-coloring* of its communication graph with $W$ colors, subject to AWGR cyclic constraint $c(i,j) = (i+j) \bmod D$ for Sirius flat fabric.
- *Circulators*: Google's custom circulators [5] halve fiber count by bidirectional use, but require odd/even $\lambda$ separation to avoid Rayleigh backscatter, adding *parity constraint*.

EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS Nodes, Switches, Slots
VARIABLES match, demand, delivered

TypeOK == match \in [Switches \times Slots -> SUBSET (Nodes \times Nodes)]
NoCollision == \A s \in Switches, k \in Slots:
  \A <<a,b>>, <<c,d>> \in match[s,k]: a=c => b=d /\ b=d => a=c  \* matching

Safety == TypeOK /\ NoCollision /\ Progress

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 RotorNet: Demand-Oblivious Rotor Fabric for ML

RotorNet [1] departs radically: **no centralized controller**. Each of $R$ rotor switches cycles independently through a fixed set of $K$ matchings, providing *uniform bandwidth* between all pairs over time, akin to Valiant Load Balancing (VLB). At $N=8,R=4,K=2$ example from original paper, each node has 4 direct uplinks (one per switch) and remainder reachable via 1-hop indirect [2].

Key innovation for ML: *packet trimming and VLB* ensure that even if AllReduce ring edge not directly connected in current slot, indirect via intermediate ToR incurs at most **one extra hop**. For $N=64$ TPU cube [5], 96 optical links per cube, stretch $1.3x$ measured in Opera [6].

> **Theorem 2 (RotorNet Throughput):** Under uniform traffic (which AllReduce approximates when load-balanced across TotientPerms), RotorNet throughput = $1 - 1/R$ of ideal. With $R=4$, 75% ideal, but with VLB + 2-hop it achieves **100% bisection** [1].

*Realizing RotorNet* [2] (SIGCOMM'24) moves from simulation to hardware: custom NIC with *synchronous routing tables* of size $N \times S$ where $S$ = number of topologies ($\approx R\cdot K$). Precomputed offline because rotor sequence is predetermined — **no control plane at runtime**. Synchronization via PTP with <50 ns jitter, admission control via token bucket to prevent buffer overflow during reconfiguration 2.25 µs + 6-7 µs ringing [2].

For ML collectives, RotorNet's *synchronization slot* aligns with AllReduce *chunk*: each AllReduce chunk = slot duration × link rate (e.g., 1.7 µs × 100 Gbps = ~21KB). Chunk size 32KB in NCCL matches well.

### 4.2 Sirius: Nanosecond Tunable-Laser Fabric

Sirius [3] pushes reconfiguration **3 orders magnitude faster** than MEMS: **<912 ps laser tuning**, **3.84 ns end-to-end** via DS-DBR lasers and passive AWG gratings. Architecture is *flat*: no spine, no hierarchy, single layer of gratings acting as *wavelength-selective crossbar*. Each grating degree $D$ ~32, many gratings in parallel to scale to 1024 nodes.

How it works:

1. **Tunable transceiver** at each node (server or ToR) emits at $\lambda_d$ to reach destination $d$ (since grating maps $\lambda \to$ port).
2. **Passive gratings** require zero power, no moving parts, no configuration — routing is *physics*.
3. **Time synchronization**: novel *sync-free* scheme using in-band reference clock distributed from switch controller, achieving **3.1 ns CDR** without BCDR receivers [3][7].
4. **Schedulerless**: nodes self-schedule via local calendars derived from global slot counter; congestion control via *NACKs + retransmit* in 43.4 ns control loop [7].

For ML, Sirius's *burstiness* handling is critical: AllReduce incast (many-to-one) is decomposed into *time-division* across lambdas. With $W=8$ lambdas per fiber, 50 Gbps each, single node can fan-out to 8 destinations *simultaneously* in same slot using WDM — effectively **8x parallelism** for reduce-scatter.

Empirical: large-scale simulations in Sirius paper show **74-77% lower power** vs ideal electrical non-blocking, and approximates its performance within 10% for hardware-driven workloads (DNN training, disaggregation) [3]. Prototype power breakdown: tunable laser 2.5W, grating 0W, NIC sync logic 1.2W vs 3000W electrical spine [5].

### 4.3 Wavelength Constraints and WDM Dimensioning

OCS fabrics face **$\lambda$-contention** at two levels:

- *AWG cyclic constraint*: In Sirius, $\lambda$ determines destination; you cannot send two different lambdas to same destination from same source simultaneously (one transceiver per node per direction). So *fan-out* limited to $W$.
- *Fiber contention*: In Palomar MEMS [5], WDM transceivers multiplex 4 lambdas (CWDM4 1310nm) over single fiber; insertion loss budget 2 dB worst-case, 1.5 dB typical, requiring *higher-power* transceivers (design point: -2 dBm launch, -11 dBm sensitivity for FR4).

We model AllReduce scheduling as:

**Input**: $N$ nodes, $W$ lambdas, $R$ switches, AllReduce algorithm $A$ = set of flows $F_t$ at step $t$.

**Constraints**:

1. $\forall t, \forall s: |\{f\in F_t: src(f)=s\}| \le W$ (transceiver limit)
2. $\forall t, \forall$ grating $g, \forall \lambda: |\{f\in F_t: g(f)=g, \lambda(f)=\lambda\}| \le 1$ (AWG no collision)
3. $\forall t, s: $ MEMS ports $\le 128$ usable per OCS (Palomar 136×136 -> 128) [5]

**Solution via edge-coloring**: Build conflict graph $G_c$ where vertices = flows, edge if they conflict on constraints 1-3. Chromatic number $\chi(G_c)$ = minimum slots needed. For Ring AllReduce, $G_c$ is collection of paths, $\chi=2$ for even $N$, $3$ for odd (Vizing). So **2 lambdas suffice** for deadlock-free ring with even nodes.

For All-to-All (MoE), $G_c$ is complete bipartite, $\chi = N-1$ in worst case [8]. Paper [8] shows lower bound via adjacency matrix powers: completion time $\ge \Omega(N^2 / (W\cdot R))$, and constructs topology sequences with expansion $\lambda_2 \le 0.3$ achieving **44% reduction** over static.

### 4.4 Topology-Aware AllReduce Scheduling: TopoOpt and TotientPerms

TopoOpt [4] insight: **network topology and parallelization are co-dependent**. Traditional Fat-tree assumes fixed topology; AllReduce algorithm assumes full bisection. TopoOpt searches jointly:

$$
\min_{T,P} \; \text{IterTime}(T,P) = \max(\text{Compute}_P, \text{Comm}_{T,P}) + \text{SyncOverhead}
$$

where $T$ = topology (set of circuits), $P$ = parallelization (DP/MP split, AllReduce order).

**TotientPerms** constructs $k$ AllReduce rings (permutations) that:

- Each ring is *bandwidth-optimal* (uses disjoint edges where possible)
- Rings are *well-positioned* for MP traffic (small hop-count for activations that need low latency)
- Rings respect *wavelength constraints* by assigning different $\lambda$ to overlapping edges across rings

Integration with NCCL: TopoOpt patches NCCL to load-balance parameter sync across $k$ rings, each mapped to distinct $\lambda$ or OCS port. Prototype 12-server A100 100 Gbps [4] with Telescent robotic OCS (10 ms reconfig, slower than MEMS but fine for job-level reconfig): **3.4x iteration speedup** vs similar-cost Fat-tree, **3.2x cheaper** than ideal full-bisection Fat-tree.

For *nanosecond* fabrics (Sirius), reconfiguration is *per-packet*, so AllReduce can interleave MP and DP transfers in same iteration without topology switch penalty. Google's Ironwood TPU (2026) integrates **3D Torus + Apollo OCS** [5]: short-reach copper intra-cube, optical inter-cube via 48 OCS, twisted torus for load balance, achieving 800G→1.6T upgrade by *only swapping transceivers* [5].

### 5.2 Lower Bound Proof Sketch

Model OCS fabric as time-expanded graph $G_t = (V, E_t)$ where $E_t$ = circuits at slot $t$. All-to-All requires each ordered pair $(i,j)$ to have path in $\cup_t E_t$ within completion time $T$. Number of pairs reachable in $T$ slots with $R$ switches each degree $d$ is bounded by adjacency matrix powers:

$$
\text{Reach}(T) \le \sum_{k=1}^{T} (A^k)_{ij}, \; A = \sum_{t=1}^{T} A_t
$$

where $A_t$ adjacency of $E_t$. For regular graphs with spectral expansion $\lambda$, mixing time $O(\log N / \log(d/\lambda))$. Paper [8] shows any sequence needs $T \ge \Omega(\log N)$ for expansion $\lambda< d/2$, and constructs sequence with $T = O(\log N)$ using Ramanujan-like lifts, achieving near-optimal.

For wavelength-constrained case, effective degree $d_{\text{eff}} = d \cdot W / \text{contention_factor}$. With Sirius AWGR, contention_factor = 1 because $\lambda$ is destination (no collision if distinct $\lambda$), so $d_{\text{eff}} = W$ per node, flat.

> **Theorem 3 (WDM AllReduce Lower Bound):** Ring AllReduce of $M$ bytes over $N$ nodes with $W$ lambdas per node, link BW $B$, reconfig $R$, needs $T_{\text{AR}} \ge \frac{2(N-1)M}{N B W} + (N-1)R$. Sirius $R=3.84$ ns negligible vs $M/B$ ~ms, so bandwidth term dominates; RotorNet $R=1.7$ µs adds 2% overhead for $M=150$MB.

## 6 Limitations

- **MEMS reliability at 10k scale**: Palomar claims *hundreds of millions* cycles no failure due to single-crystal Si eliminating hinge fatigue [5], yet production reports still cite *calibration drift* requiring closed-loop camera + injection monitoring every 10 min [5]. At 100k GPUs, 48 OCS per pod × 1563 pods = 75k OCS, camera alignment sub-micron tool complexity remains barrier.
- **Sirius laser cost**: Custom DS-DBR tunable laser chip with <912 ps tuning is *research prototype*, not commodity; volume pricing ~5x CWDM4, and temperature sensitivity ±0.1°C requires TEC cooler 1.2W extra [3].
- **RotorNet synchronization**: <50 ns PTP jitter required across 1000 nodes; Real RotorNet prototype [2] uses custom NIC FPGA, not Mellanox CX6, limiting adoption. Token bucket admission causes 3% throughput loss under incast.
- **TopoOpt search complexity**: Alternating optimization over topology $T$ ($\binom{N}{k}$ possibilities) and parallelization $P$ is *NP-hard*; TotientPerms heuristic greedy selection has no approximation guarantee for MP hop-count, and Telescent OCS 10 ms reconfig limits to job-level, not iteration-level, adaptation [4].
- **Wavelength budget**: CWDM4 4 λ typical, 8 λ max before non-linear crosstalk -18 dB; 100k GPU fabric needs 6144 links [5], requiring 48 OCS × 128 ports, fiber management nightmare (Google reports 2x fiber reduction via circulators still 3k fibers per pod).
- **ML framework integration**: NCCL topology awareness assumes static ring; dynamic $\lambda$ switching breaks NCCL's *channel* abstraction, requiring patch that is not upstreamed; PyTorch DDP timeout 30 min collides with OCS reconfig failures causing job preemption.
- **All-to-All gap**: 44% reduction in [8] assumes *unlimited buffering* at intermediate nodes for store-and-forward; real GPU memory 80GB limits to 2 hops, forcing *direct* circuits for MoE All-to-All, increasing $W$ requirement 4x.

## 7 Conclusion

OCS for ML collectives is *no longer speculative*: Google's Apollo/Palomar proves MEMS OCS scales to thousands of switches powering Jupiter [5], Sirius proves nanosecond switching is physically achievable with <1 ns tunable lasers and passive gratings [3], and RotorNet/Opera prove demand-oblivious rotor schedules achieve full bisection without central control [1][2]. The missing link is *topology-aware collective scheduling under wavelength constraints*.

Our synthesis shows: **(i)** RotorNet's fixed matchings suit Ring AllReduce chunking, stretch 1.5x acceptable for DP, **(ii)** Sirius's wavelength-as-destination enables flat 8-way parallelism for reduce-scatter, 3.84 ns reconfig makes reconfiguration overhead <1% for 32KB chunks, **(iii)** TopoOpt's TotientPerms co-design yields 3.4x training speedup by matching AllReduce permutations to MP traffic and WDM constraints [4], **(iv)** All-to-All lower bounds via adjacency powers [8] formalize why expanding sequences beat static topologies by 44%.

Future fabric for 100k GPUs likely *hybrid*: **intra-pod** copper + 3D Torus (Ironwood [5]), **inter-pod** MEMS OCS 48 units for coarse topology, **intra-ToR** Sirius-like nanosecond for fine-grained collectives, with *asynchronously-invoked hitless* partial reconfiguration [8] to avoid global barriers. Wavelength dimensioning will move from CWDM4 to **8-λ 200G/lambda** as 1.6T modules dominate >60% shipments by 2026 [5]. Control plane must evolve from centralized SDN to *schedulerless* local calendars with in-band NACKs, as Sirius demonstrates.

For practitioners: start with *job-level* OCS (TopoOpt model) for 12-64 GPUs, monitor All-to-All vs Ring ratio — if All-to-All >30% (MoE), invest in $W\ge8$; if DP-only ResNet/BERT, $W=2$ suffices (Vizing). Prototype Sirius only if you can afford custom lasers; otherwise Palomar MEMS 128-port is production-grade.
