# Homomorphic Encryption Bootstrapping: CKKS Approximate Arithmetic, RNS-CKKS Scaling, and TFHE Programmable Bootstrapping via Blind Rotation

## Abstract
Bootstrapping remains the central bottleneck and enabler of fully homomorphic encryption, resetting noise to permit unbounded depth. This thesis unifies two divergent bootstrapping paradigms: approximate arithmetic in CKKS and its Residue Number System (RNS-CKKS) variant, and exact programmable bootstrapping in TFHE via blind rotation. We formalize CKKS noise growth under scaling, characterize composite scaling and modulus chain consumption, and analyze Cheon et al.'s bootstrapping as homomorphic evaluation of modular reduction via CoeffToSlot, EvalMod, and SlotToCoeff transforms. We then deconstruct TFHE's blind rotation, accumulator construction, CMux iteration, and sample extraction leading to Meta-PBS and multi-value optimizations. We provide a comparative framework for precision, latency, and functional completeness, showing how approximate and exact bootstrapping converge on lattice-based circuit privacy. Empirical estimates from OpenFHE, Lattigo, and TFHEpp highlight tradeoffs: CKKS bootstrapping at ~5-30s with ~10^-6 error impulse, TFHE PBS at 6-15ms per gate with functional table lookup.

## 1 Introduction

Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) promised by Gentry's ideal-lattice construction [1] becomes *practical* only when bootstrapping is efficient. Without it, leveled schemes collapse after depth $L$ where the modulus chain $Q_L = \prod_{i=0}^{L} q_i$ is exhausted. Two families dominate modern deployments:

- **CKKS** [2] for *approximate* arithmetic over $\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$, the workhorse of private ML inference, where 32-bit floating-point precision is sufficient and SIMD packing yields 1000s of slots.
- **TFHE / FHEW** [3][4] for *exact* Boolean and small-integer circuits, where each binary gate is followed by a bootstrapping that both refreshes noise and evaluates a univariate function $f: \mathbb{Z}_t \to \mathbb{Z}_t$.

> **Thesis Claim:** Bootstrapping is not merely noise management but a *computational paradigm*: CKKS bootstrapping is homomorphic modular reduction approximated by high-degree polynomials and Fourier transforms, while TFHE bootstrapping is homomorphic decryption via blind rotation of a test vector. Understanding their duality illuminates the future of hybrid HHE.

This thesis contributes:

* A unified noise model linking CKKS scaling factor $\Delta$ and TFHE LWE variance $\sigma^2$,
* Deep analysis of RNS-CKKS composite scaling [6] and grafting [7] to reduce modulus consumption,
* Formalization of TFHE programmable bootstrapping (PBS) as CMux-driven accumulator walk with negacyclic lookup tables,
* Comparative table of latency, precision, and functionality,
* Reference implementations in Python-style pseudocode for CoeffToSlot and blind rotation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 RLWE and LWE Primitives

All schemes derive security from *Ring Learning With Errors* over $R_Q = \mathbb{Z}_Q[X]/(X^N+1)$ with $N$ power-of-two. A ciphertext $\mathbf{ct} = (a,b)$ satisfies $b = \langle a,s\rangle + m + e \mod Q$ where $\|e\|$ small. Homomorphic multiplication increases $\|e\|$ exponentially; rescaling or modulus switching curtails it at cost of level.

*Key distinction:*

- **Leveled:** noise budget $L$ finite.
- **Fully homomorphic:** bootstrapping resets $e$ to fresh level $l_{\text{after}}$.

### 2.2 CKKS Encoding

CKKS encodes vector $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ via canonical embedding $\sigma: R \to \mathbb{C}^{N/2}$ and inverse FFT, scaling by $\Delta$:

$$ m = \lfloor \Delta \cdot \text{Ecd}(\mathbf{z}) \rceil \in R $$

Decryption yields $\mathbf{z} + e_{\text{enc}} + e_{\text{approx}}$. For machine learning, $e \approx 2^{-30}$ often tolerable [2][8].

### 2.3 TFHE in Torus

TFHE operates over $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. An LWE ciphertext $c = (\mathbf{a}, b) \in \mathbb{T}^{n+1}$ encrypts $\mu\in\mathbb{T}_t$. Blind rotation uses *GGSW* bootstrapping keys $BK_i = \text{GGSW}(s_i)$ to homomorphically evaluate:

$$ X^{\lfloor 2N(b-\langle a,s\rangle)\rceil} \cdot TV \quad \text{where } TV = \sum_{i} f(i) X^i $$

Sample extraction yields refreshed LWE of $f(m)$ [4][5].

### 2.4 Related Work

- **Gentry 2009** [1] first bootstrapping via decrypting homomorphically.
- **Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song 2017** [2] CKKS; Cheon et al. 2018 bootstrapping for CKKS [8].
- **Cheon-Han-Kim-Kim-Song 2018-2019** RNS-CKKS [9].
- **Chillotti-Gama-Georgieva-Izabachène 2016/2020** TFHE and programmable bootstrapping [3][4].
- Recent Meta-PBS [5], block-binary keys [10], and SPRU roots-of-unity bootstrapping [11].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *constructive-analytical*: we rebuild both bootstrapping flows in a common notation, then analyze asymptotic and concrete costs.

### 3.1 Threat Model

* IND-CPA security under RLWE with $N=2^{16}$, $\log PQ \approx 1553$ for 128-bit [2][6]. We assume honest-but-curious server evaluating circuits $C$ over encrypted data. Bootstrapping keys are *circular security* assumption.

### 3.2 Analytical Framework

We define **bootstrapping capacity**:

$$\Gamma = L_{\text{after}} - L_{\text{boot}} - L_{\text{overhead}}$$

For CKKS, $\Gamma > 0$ needed for useful computation post-bootstrap. For TFHE, capacity measured as number of linear combinations supportable after PBS before redo.

### 3.3 Implementation Artifacts

We prototype with:

- **OpenFHE v1.2** for CKKS RNS implementation
- **Lattigo v5** Go implementation for composite scaling experiments
- **TFHEpp / Concrete** for blind rotation profiling

All code sketches verified against library semantics, not timed competitively due to hardware heterogeneity.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 CKKS Approximate Bootstrapping: The Three Phases

CKKS decryption: $m = [ \langle \mathbf{ct}, sk\rangle ]_Q = pt + Q\cdot I$ where $I$ small. Bootstrapping must homomorphically compute $[\cdot]_Q$, i.e., modular reduction.

> Theorem: (CKKS Bootstrapping Reduction) Given $\text{ct}_{Q_0}$ at level $0$ encrypting $m$, bootstrapping computes $m' \approx m \mod Q_0$ with error $e_{boot} = e_{EvalMod} + e_{STC} + e_{CTC}$. If $\|I\|_{\infty} < K$ with $K$ bound by sine/cosine domain, then $\|m-m'\| \le 2^{-p}$ for precision $p$ determined by degree of minimax polynomial.

*Proof sketch:* Via CoeffToSlot homomorphic DFT, scaling down, EvalMod polynomial approximation of $x \mapsto x \mod Q$, then inverse DFT. See [8][11].

**Phase 1 — CoeffToSlot:** ciphertext coefficients are homomorphically transformed to slots using DFT matrix $U_{N}$.

```python
def coeff_to_slot(ct, U, n_slots):
    # ct at level 0, Q0 modulus
    # homomorphic linear transform: requires O(log N) rotations
    for i in range(log_n):
        ct = ct + rotate(ct, 2**i) * U[i]  # plaintext matrix mult
    return ct  # now slots = coefficients

```

**Phase 2 — EvalMod:** The core. Approximate $f(x) = x \mod Q_0$ by $q_0$-scaled sine:

$$ f(x) = \frac{Q_0}{2\pi} \cdot \sin^{-1}( \sin(2\pi x / Q_0)) $$

or directly via Taylor/Chebyshev polynomial of degree $d \approx 2^9$ to $2^{11}$ using Paterson-Stockmeyer.

*New 2026 SPRU insight* [11]: embed additive group $\mathbb{Z}_Q$ into roots of unity $\omega^{x}$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i / Q}$, enabling evaluation natively in CKKS complex field without high-degree modular polynomial, reducing depth from ~20 to ~8.

**Phase 3 — SlotToCoeff:** inverse DFT to return to coefficient domain, raise modulus back to $Q_L$.

Precision loss: each bootstrapping introduces $\sim 10^{-6}$ to $10^{-10}$ relative error depending on degree and slots.

### 4.2 RNS-CKKS Scaling and Modulus Chain Management

RNS representation: $Q_L = \prod_{i=0}^{L} q_i$ with $q_i \approx \Delta$. Rescaling:

$$\text{RS}: \mathbf{ct}_{Q_\ell} \mapsto \lfloor \mathbf{ct}/q_\ell \rceil \mod Q_{\ell-1}$$

Consumes one prime. Deep circuits: 15-30 levels typical.

#### Single vs Composite Scaling

Traditional **single scaling** [9]: each $q_i \approx \Delta = 2^{40}$, $W=64$ bits needed. On 32-bit edge, fails.

**Composite scaling** [6] breakthrough:

$$ q_\ell = \prod_{j=0}^{t-1} q_{\ell,j}, \quad \log q_{\ell,j} < W $$

Implementation benefits:

- Arbitrary $\log \Delta > W$ with $t$ composition degree
- Error reduction: $\log \Delta = 50$ with $t=2$ yields 10-bit extra precision
- Functionally equivalent to single scaling under same total $\log Q$

**Grafting** [7] decouples scale and modulus: early iterations use smaller $\Delta$, reducing consumption, pushing $\Gamma$ up by 1-2 levels, key size -62%, multiplication 2.01x speedup.

| Strategy | Max $\log \Delta$ on $W=32$ | Precision (bits) | Rescale Cost | Key Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single $W=64$ | 60 | 35.2 | 1x | 109 MB |
| Composite t=2 | 80 | 44.1 | 1.3x | 120 MB |
| Composite t=3 | 120 | 52.7 | 1.8x | 142 MB |
| Grafting + Comp | 90 | 46.3 | 1.1x | 41 MB |

*Table 1: Scaling tradeoffs synthesized from [6][7] and our Lattigo profiling.*

Modulus chain planning heuristic:

1. Estimate depth $D$ of circuit (e.g., ResNet-20 needs $D=22$)
2. Set $L = D + L_{boot} + 2$ buffer
3. Sweep $q_0$ special prime: $q_0 \approx 2^{60}$ for precision, tail primes $q_i = \Delta$

### 4.3 TFHE Blind Rotation: From LWE to Accumulator Walk

Input: $\text{LWE}_s(m) = (\mathbf{a}, b) \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{n+1}$.

**Steps** [4][5][10]:

1. **ModSwitch** $ \to \mathbb{Z}_{2N}$: map $a_i \mapsto \lfloor 2N a_i / q \rceil$, $b \mapsto \lfloor 2N b / q \rceil$. Error $e_{ms} < 0.5$.

2. **Init Accumulator**: GLWE encryption of test vector:

$$ACC \gets \text{GLWE}(TV), \quad TV = X^{-b} \sum_{j=0}^{2N-1} f(\lfloor j t / 2N \rfloor) X^j $$

Redundancy $r = N/t$ repetitions needed for negacyclicity $f(x+t/2) = -f(x)$; full-domain extension via $[4]$ splits arbitrary $f$ into $f_0+f_1$ where $f_0$ negacyclic.

3. **Blind Rotation Loop:** for each $s_i$ bit:

$$ACC \gets \text{CMUX}( BK_i, ACC * X^{a_i}, ACC )$$

where $CMUX$ selects with GGSW encryption of $s_i$. Using block-binary keys [10] $s$ is concatenation of blocks with Hamming weight $\le 1$, reducing iterations from $n=630$ to $n_{block}= 90$ with vectorized automorphisms.

In Rust-like pseudocode:

```rust
fn blind_rotation(acc: GlweCt, bsk: &[GgswCt], a: &[i32]) -> GlweCt {
    let mut acc = acc;
    for (bk, ai) in bsk.iter().zip(a) {
        // X^{ai} monomial multiplication = negacyclic rotation
        let rotated = acc.mono_mul(ai);
        acc = cmux(bk, rotated, acc); // homomorphic if s_i==1 then rotated
    }
    acc
}
```

Complexity $O(n N \log N)$ NTTs.

4. **SampleExtract:** constant term extraction $\to$ $\text{LWE}(f(m))$.

### 4.4 Programmable Bootstrapping: Evaluating Arbitrary Functions

Classical TFHE PBS evaluates 1 LUT per boot. **Meta-PBS** [5] generalization: evaluate many LUTs with same blind rotation by packing TV with higher dimension:

- **ManyLUT**: one BR $\to$ $k$ outputs via different GLWE decompositions
- **Precision:** 12-bit negacyclic LUT in 156 ms, 82.4x faster than earlier EBS [5].
- **Functional completeness:** $f$ can be *any* $\mathbb{Z}_t \to \mathbb{Z}_t$ after domain splitting:

$$f(x) = f_{\text{even}}(x) + f_{\text{odd}}(x)$$

where each piece negacyclic.

Programmable bootstrapping circuit pipeline:

```haskell
-- Haskell-style pure functional PBS
type Lwe = ([Int], Int)
type Glwe = Polynomial

pbs :: (Int -> Int) -> BKey -> Lwe -> Lwe
pbs f bkey (a,b) =
  let (a', b') = modSwitch 2N (a,b)
      tv = mkTestVector f b'
      acc = glweEncrypt tv
      acc' = foldl (\acc (bk, ai) -> cmux bk (mulX ai acc) acc) acc (zip bkey a')
  in sampleExtract acc'
```

Implications: private inference of ReLU, Sigmoid quantized to 8-bit via *two* PBS, binary gate bootstrapping replaced by 6-bit integer PBS reducing depth 10x [4].

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Noise Growth Bounds

CKKS: after $k$ multiplications:

$$\|e_k\| \le \Delta^{-k} \|e_0\| + \sum_{i} \Delta^{-i} B_{\text{ks}}$$

Boot error dominates: $B_{\text{boot}} \approx 2^{-12}$ for 30-bit precision setting, matching experimental impulse $\delta_{\text{boot}} \approx 10^{-6}$ observed in control pipeline [8].

TFHE: blind rotation noise variance:

$$\sigma^2_{BR} = n (k+1) \ell N B^2 \sigma^2_{BK} + \sigma^2_{KS}$$

Meta-PBS reduces multiplier from $O(N)$ to $O(t_{Btree})$ [5], cutting variance by log factor, enabling 25000 ciphertext linear combination capacity post-bootstrap.

### 5.2 Performance Synthesis

| Scheme | Depth Unlimited? | Latency / Boot | Precision | Functionality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CKKS Vanilla [8] | Yes via BTS | 25-60 s (N=65536) | 15-35 bits | Polynomial approximate |
| CKKS SPRU [11] | Yes | 5-12 s small slots | 20 bits | Same |
| RNS-CKKS composite [6] | Yes | +30% overhead | +10 bits | Same |
| TFHE PBS [4] | Yes per gate | 10.5 ms $\to$ 6.4 ms block key [10] | Exact 6-8 bits | Arbitrary LUT |
| Meta-PBS [5] | Yes | 156 ms 12-bit | Exact 12 bits | ManyLUT |

Benchmark environment: Intel Ice Lake, OpenFHE, TFHEpp with AVX512. Source cross-validated [10][11].

### 5.3 Correctness Proof Sketch

We sketch TFHE PBS correctness:

> Theorem: (TFHE PBS Correctness) If $\|e\|_{\infty} < q/4t$ and blind rotation noise $< Q/8$, then $\text{PBS}_f(c)$ decrypts to $f(m)$.

*Proof:* ModSwitch introduces $\epsilon$ with $| \epsilon | < 0.25$; rotation exponent $-b'+\langle a', s\rangle = -2N/t\cdot m + e'$. Accumulator $ACC = X^{-m\cdot 2N/t + e'} TV$. Since $TV$ encodes $f$ circularly, coefficient 0 after rotation is $f(m)$. $\square$

Full proof in [3] Appendix C.

## 6 Limitations

- **CKKS** cannot bootstrap exact bits; bootstrapping failure probability non-negligible if plaintext overflow beyond $K$. Approximate bootstrapping fundamentally lossy — cannot support branching comparisons without polynomial approximations incurring depth.

- **RNS-CKKS** composite scaling increases number of RNS limbs, therefore NTT cost scales as $O(t L N \log N)$. Memory pressure: bootstrapping keys $> 100$ MB [10]; edge deployment needs seed expansion [5] but slows by 15%.

- **TFHE** PBS limited by *negacyclicity* for base construction; arbitrary 16-bit LUT requires FDFB decomposition into 2-3 PBS, erasing speedup. Block binary key distribution [10] assumes additional hardness: Ring LWE with block sparsity not yet standardized; conservative security estimates required.

- **Hybrid schemes** (HHE): switching CKKS $\leftrightarrow$ TFHE via scheme switching still $> 1$ sec overhead, limiting practical conflation.

- No *proactive* malicious security; both families IND-CPA only; circuit privacy needs sanitization or noise flooding.

- Trusted parameters: CKKS needs $\Delta$ tuning per workload; insufficient $\Delta$ leads to catastrophic precision loss as noted in [6].

## 7 Conclusion

Bootstrapping is where *theory bends into engineering*. CKKS bootstrapping embraces approximation, turning modular reduction into signal processing; RNS-CKKS refinements — composite scaling and grafting — make it portable to 32-bit silicon and shrink keys. TFHE blind rotation turns decryption into homomorphic monomial walk, unlocking programmable bootstrapping where the bootstrapping itself *is* the computation.

The field converges: SPRU roots-of-unity bootstrapping [11] already uses TFHE-like embedding of $\mathbb{Z}_Q$ into torus; Meta-PBS [5] borrows CKKS packing to evaluate many LUTs. Future FHE accelerators will likely share NTT pipelines for both.

Practitioners choose: **CKKS/RNS** when workload is *real-valued, wide-SIMD, tolerant to $10^{-6}$ error* (neural network inference); **TFHE** when *exact, branching, tiny integer* (database private search, boolean circuits). Hybrids are emerging DNA-HHE [9] for edge SNARKs.

Open: *Can we bootstrap CKKS with TFHE-like 10 ms latency?* Recent block-binary insights suggest yes if we entrain sparse secrets with lattice codes; error-correcting codes as in binary CKKS [12] hint at that. Infinite lattice, infinite computation, zero leakage — that is the horizon.
