# Homotopy Type Theory and Cubical Models: Univalence, Higher Inductive Types, and Computational Semantics

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf's dependent type theory through the lens of homotopy theory: types are spaces, terms are points, and elements of identity types are paths. At its center stands Voevodsky's *univalence axiom*, which identifies identifications of types with equivalences of types, $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B)$, turning isomorphic structures into equal ones. This axiom is classically consistent by the Kan simplicial set model, but that model relies on non-constructive principles and yields no computational behavior for univalence itself. *Cubical type theory*, introduced by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg, resolves this by internalizing the interval, path types, composition operations, and *Glue* types, making univalence a theorem with definitional computation rules. This thesis develops the full technical arc: the interval and face lattice of de Morgan cubes, path types and uniform Kan filling, the Glue construction that computes univalence, higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules, and the constructive presheaf semantics. We survey canonicity results, the landscape of cubical models, and open problems around general higher inductive schemas.

## 1. Introduction

In 2006, Vladimir Voevodsky discovered that the identity types of Martin-Löf type theory admit a profoundly geometric reading. In this *homotopical interpretation*, a type $A$ is a space, a term $a : A$ a point, and an element $p : a =_A b$ a *path* from $a$ to $b$ [1]. Iterated identity types are homotopies between paths, and so on upward: every type carries the structure of an $\infty$-groupoid. This was systematized during the 2012–2013 special year on *Univalent Foundations* at the Institute for Advanced Study, producing the landmark *Homotopy Type Theory* book [1].

Two principles distinguish homotopy type theory from ordinary Martin-Löf type theory (MLTT):

1. **The univalence axiom.** For types $A, B$ in a universe $\mathcal{U}$, the canonical map
$$\mathsf{idtoeqv} : (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \longrightarrow (A \simeq B)$$
is an equivalence, where $A \simeq B$ denotes the type of homotopy equivalences [1, Axiom 2.10.3]. Informally: *equivalent types may be identified*. This makes function extensionality provable and gives mathematics an *invariant* character: all constructions respect equivalence.
2. **Higher inductive types (HITs).** Ordinary inductive types are generated by *point* constructors. HITs additionally admit *path* constructors — identifications imposed between (possibly higher) terms — permitting direct, synthetic definitions of spaces such as the circle $S^1$, suspensions, truncations, and pushouts [1, Chapter 6].

Both principles posed a foundational challenge: *what do they compute?* Voevodsky's simplicial set model [1, Appendix] establishes consistency but makes essential use of classical logic (e.g., in the proof that the universe of Kan fibrations is itself Kan), so it cannot explain how a closed term of natural-number type involving univalence should reduce. The identity type, moreover, is only *propositionally* characterized by its eliminator $J$; nothing in the syntax forces $p : a = b$ to behave like a continuous path.

The decisive response was the *cubical* turn. Bezem, Coquand, and Huber constructed the first constructive model of univalence in cubical sets [4], and Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg distilled this into *cubical type theory*, in which univalence is not an axiom but a *theorem* with computational content, proved via the novel *Glue* type former [2]. Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg then added higher inductive types with *judgmental* computation rules for higher constructors [3].

This thesis presents a unified account of this development: the syntax and semantics of cubical type theory, the Glue mechanism that computes univalence, the treatment of HITs, the landscape of models, and the metatheoretic guarantees — canonicity and normalization — that make the system a genuine programming language for synthetic homotopy theory.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory and identity types

MLTT is founded on *judgments*: $\Gamma \vdash A\ \mathsf{type}$, $\Gamma \vdash a : A$, and definitional equality $\Gamma \vdash a \equiv b : A$. The identity type former is introduced by:

- **Formation:** $\dfrac{\Gamma \vdash A\ \mathsf{type} \quad \Gamma \vdash a : A \quad \Gamma \vdash b : A}{\Gamma \vdash a =_A b\ \mathsf{type}}$
- **Introduction:** $\dfrac{\Gamma \vdash a : A}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{refl}_a : a =_A a}$
- **Elimination ($J$):** given $C : \prod_{x:A} (a =_A x) \to \mathcal{U}$ and $d : C(a, \mathsf{refl}_a)$, and $p : a =_A b$, we obtain $J_{C,d}(p) : C(b, p)$, with the *computation rule* $J_{C,d}(\mathsf{refl}_a) \equiv d$.

Thus identity is inductively generated by reflexivity: the least reflexive relation on $A$. In extensional type theory, the reflection rule collapses propositional and definitional equality, making type checking undecidable. Intensional MLTT keeps them separate and remains decidable — but then *function extensionality*,
$$\mathsf{funext} : \Big(\prod_{x:A} f(x) = g(x)\Big) \to (f = g),$$
is *not* provable: one cannot identify pointwise-equal functions.

### 2.2 The homotopical interpretation and univalence

The homotopical interpretation [1, Chapter 2] assigns topological meaning to every judgment. Under this reading, univalence asserts that the *space of identifications* between types coincides with the *space of equivalences* — the universe $\mathcal{U}$ is an *object classifier* for fibrations, just as in the simplicial model.

> **Theorem:** (Univalence [1, §2.10]) For a univalent universe $\mathcal{U}$ and $A, B : \mathcal{U}$, the map $\mathsf{idtoeqv}_{A,B} : (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \to (A \simeq B)$ is an equivalence. In particular, there is a map $\mathsf{ua} : (A \simeq B) \to (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B)$ with $\mathsf{idtoeqv}(\mathsf{ua}(e)) = e$ propositionally.

Univalence implies function extensionality and propositional extensionality, and it validates the *structure identity principle*: isomorphic algebraic structures are identified, so all properties and constructions in the theory are automatically isomorphism-invariant [6].

### 2.3 Higher inductive types

HITs generalize inductive definitions by allowing constructors that produce *paths*, not just points. The paradigmatic example is the circle [1, §6.4]:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The eliminator for $S^1$ requires, besides $b : P(\mathsf{base})$, a *dependent path* $l : \mathsf{transport}^P(\mathsf{loop}, b) = b$ lying over $\mathsf{loop}$. In Book HoTT, the computation rule for $\mathsf{loop}$ holds only *propositionally*. Other HITs include propositional truncation $\|A\|$, set quotients $A/R$, suspensions $\Sigma A$, and pushouts [1, §6.9–6.10], sufficing for substantial *synthetic homotopy theory* — e.g., $\pi_1(S^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ [1, §8.1] — entirely inside type theory.

### 2.4 The computational problem

Voevodsky, Kapulkin, and Lumsdaine showed that Kan simplicial sets model MLTT plus univalence and a class of HITs. However, as Rajani emphasizes, this construction "makes fundamental use of classical logic," and therefore "cannot be used to explain the computational content of the univalence axiom, such as how to compute terms involving univalence" [5]. Concretely: if $\vdash t : \mathbb{N}$ is a closed term whose construction uses $\mathsf{ua}$, the simplicial model gives no algorithm reducing $t$ to a numeral. Worse, adding univalence as a bare axiom to MLTT *breaks* canonicity outright, since $\mathsf{ua}(e) : A =_{\mathcal{U}} B$ is a stuck term of identity type that is not $\mathsf{refl}$.

The program of *computational* univalent foundations therefore demands a type theory in which univalence *computes*. Cubical type theory is the realization of that program.

## 3. Methodology

This thesis is a *synthetic-analytic* study, combining four methodological strands:

1. **Syntactic development.** We present cubical type theory as a formal system in the style of CCHM [2]: judgments for a *pretype* interval $\mathbb{I}$, face lattice $\mathbb{F}$, path types, composition ($\mathsf{comp}$), homogeneous composition ($\mathsf{hcomp}$), and Glue types, with full definitional equalities.
2. **Semantic justification.** We explain the presheaf semantics over the *de Morgan cube category* — presheaves equipped with a uniform Kan composition operation — following CCHM [2] and the internal-topos account of Rajani [5], which reconstructs the model inside the internal type theory of an elementary topos, clarifying exactly which principles the construction requires.
3. **Comparative model analysis.** We contrast the BCH cubical-sets model [4], the CCHM model [2], Swan's identity-type construction, and the theorem of Shulman that *every* $\infty$-topos models HoTT [6, §1], situating cubical syntax among semantic alternatives.
4. **Metatheoretic and implementational evidence.** We survey canonicity (Huber), normalization results, the Nuprl formalization of the cubical model, and the cubicaltt and Cubical Agda implementations.

Our scope is the *De Morgan* cubical type theory of CCHM and its HIT extension [3]. We note variants (cartesian cubes, connections-only cubes) where relevant but do not develop them. All categorical claims are stated for presheaf toposes over small cube categories with a constructive metatheory.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The interval, faces, and the de Morgan cube category

Cubical type theory extends MLTT with an *interval pretype* $\mathbb{I}$ — a pretype because it is not itself Kan (fibrant): one cannot in general compose paths *in* $\mathbb{I}$. It comes with endpoints $0, 1 : \mathbb{I}$ and de Morgan operations [2]:

| Operation | Notation | Laws |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum | $r \land s$ | idempotent, commutative, associative |
| Maximum | $r \lor s$ | idempotent, commutative, associative |
| Reversal | $\lnot r$ | $\lnot\lnot r = r$, de Morgan dualities |
| Endpoints | $0, 1$ | $\lnot 0 = 1$, absorption |

The *face lattice* $\mathbb{F}$ is obtained from $\mathbb{I}$ by imposing $r \land \lnot r = 0$. A *system* $[\varphi_1 \mapsto u_1, \dots, \varphi_n \mapsto u_n]$ is a partial element defined on the cofibration $\varphi = \bigvee_i \varphi_i$, valid when branches agree on overlaps — geometrically, "a cube specified on some of its faces."

Semantically, contexts are interpreted in the presheaf category $\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$ over the *cube category* $\mathbb{C}$, whose objects are finite sets of dimension names and whose maps are generated by face maps, degeneracies, and the de Morgan operations — equivalently, the free de Morgan algebra on the set of names [2]. The interval is the representable $\mathbb{I} = \mathbf{y}(\{i\})$. A *cubical set* is a presheaf $X : \mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathsf{Set}$; an $n$-cube is an element of $X$ at a set of $n$ names.

The crucial semantic structure is a *uniform Kan composition operation*: for every *open box* — a cube with one face removed, specified by a system — there is a chosen *filler* and *composite* face, natural in the cube category. Types are interpreted as *fibrant* cubical sets: families equipped with such composition structure. This uniformity is what makes the model constructive, in contrast with the classical Kan condition on simplicial sets [4].

### 4.2 Path types, composition, and Kan filling

Identity types are replaced by *path types*, which are *defined* rather than axiomatized [2]:

$$\frac{\Gamma \vdash A\ \mathsf{type} \quad \Gamma \vdash a_0 : A \quad \Gamma \vdash a_1 : A}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{Path}\ A\ a_0\ a_1\ \mathsf{type}} \qquad \frac{\Gamma, i : \mathbb{I} \vdash p : A}{\Gamma \vdash \lambda i.\, p : \mathsf{Path}\ A\ (p[0/i])\ (p[1/i])}$$

Application $p\ r : A$ for $r : \mathbb{I}$ satisfies $( \lambda i.\, p)\, r \equiv p[r/i]$, with endpoint equations $p\, 0 \equiv a_0$, $p\, 1 \equiv a_1$ *definitionally*. The eliminator $J$ is *derived* from composition. Function extensionality becomes a one-liner:

```agda
funext : {f g : (x : A) → B x} → ((x : A) → f x ≡ g x) → f ≡ g
funext h = λ i x → h x i
```

Transport along a path $p : \mathsf{Path}\ \mathcal{U}\ A\ B$ is composition in the constant family: $\mathsf{transport}^p(a) = \mathsf{comp}^i\ A\ [\ ]\ a$. More generally, *heterogeneous composition*

$$\mathsf{comp}^i\ A\ [\varphi \mapsto u]\ u_0 : A[1/i]$$

fills an open box in a *line of types* $i : \mathbb{I} \vdash A$, given a cap $u_0 : A[0/i]$ and tube faces $u$ on $\varphi$. Homogeneous composition $\mathsf{hcomp}$ is the special case of a constant family. Together these operations equip every type former ($\Pi$, $\Sigma$, $\mathcal{U}$) with Kan structure [2].

> **Theorem:** (Composition for the universe [2, §6]) The universe $\mathcal{U}$ of fibrant types carries a composition operation, and hence is itself fibrant. The construction proceeds by *Glueing*: the composite of a line of universes is built by gluing the fibers along the given equivalences.

### 4.3 Glue types: the computational engine of univalence

The centerpiece of CCHM is the *Glue* type former, which internalizes the equivalence-to-path direction of univalence as a *type with definitional equalities* [2, §6]:

$$\frac{\Gamma \vdash A\ \mathsf{type} \quad \Gamma, \varphi \vdash T\ \mathsf{type} \quad \Gamma, \varphi \vdash e : \mathsf{Equiv}\ T\ A}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{Glue}\ A\ [\varphi \mapsto (T, e)]\ \mathsf{type}}$$

with the strict boundary law $\mathsf{Glue}\ A\ [1 \mapsto (T,e)] \equiv T$. Univalence is then a *theorem*. Given $e : \mathsf{Equiv}\ A\ B$, define

$$\mathsf{ua}(e) : \mathsf{Path}\ \mathcal{U}\ A\ B \qquad \mathsf{ua}(e) = \lambda i.\, \mathsf{Glue}\ B\ [(i = 0) \mapsto (A, e)].$$

At $i = 0$ this is definitionally $A$; at $i = 1$ it is $B$. Transport along $\mathsf{ua}(e)$ *computes*: for $a : A$, $\mathsf{transport}^{\mathsf{ua}(e)}(a) \equiv \mathsf{app}(e, a)$, because composition in a Glue type reduces through the equivalence [2, §7]. Hence $\mathsf{idtoeqv}(\mathsf{ua}(e)) \equiv e$ *definitionally* in one direction, and the other composite is propositionally the identity — so $\mathsf{idtoeqv}$ is an equivalence. This is the computational content the simplicial model could never provide: univalence now *runs*.

> **Theorem:** (Univalence in cubical type theory [2, Thm. 7.2]) For $A, B : \mathcal{U}$, the canonical map $\mathsf{idtoeqv} : \mathsf{Path}\ \mathcal{U}\ A\ B \to \mathsf{Equiv}\ A\ B$ is an equivalence. Moreover, transport along $\mathsf{ua}(e)$ is definitionally given by applying $e$.

Swan's construction, adapted to the cubical setting, shows that the traditional identity type $\mathsf{Id}$ (with strict $J$ computation) can be *defined* from $\mathsf{Path}$ by marking constant paths, and the two notions of contractibility coincide — so univalence transfers to $\mathsf{Id}$ as well [4].

### 4.4 Higher inductive types with judgmental computation rules

Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg extend cubical type theory with a schema of HITs — spheres, torus, suspensions, propositional truncations, and pushouts — justified by a constructive presheaf semantics in which each HIT is an initial algebra for a suitable endofunctor, and, crucially, *all constructors, including higher ones, satisfy judgmental computation rules* [3].

The circle illustrates the pattern:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : Path S¹ base base
```

The eliminator takes $b : P(\mathsf{base})$ and $l : \mathsf{PathP}\ (\lambda i → P\ (\mathsf{loop}\ i))\ b\ b$, and computes:

```agda
elimS¹ b l base      ≡ b
elimS¹ b l (loop i)  ≡ l i        -- judgmental, at every i : I
```

The second equation — computation on the *path* constructor, at every point of the interval — is what Book HoTT lacks (there it holds only up to a path). Semantically, HITs are built as higher inductive *cubical sets*: points freely generate, path constructors attach formal paths, and the fibrant replacement (Kan completion) is performed while preserving the strict equations, using the same composition machinery as ordinary types [3, §2].

Pushouts subsume suspensions and spheres; the torus admits two equivalent presentations [3, §3.3]. Universes are closed under all these formers, so large eliminations into $\mathcal{U}$ are legitimate [3, §2.5].

> **Theorem:** (Initial-algebra semantics [3, §2.4, App. A]) In the presheaf model over the de Morgan cube category, each HIT schema determines a finitary endofunctor whose initial algebra carries a composition structure; the syntactic HIT with its judgmental computation rules is sound for this semantics.

### 4.5 Semantic landscapes: presheaves, toposes, and $\infty$-toposes

The CCHM semantics is a *presheaf* model: types are families of fibrant cubical sets over a context, organized as a category with families [2]. Rajani's internal approach reconstructs this inside the internal extensional type theory of an elementary topos with suitable structure, isolating exactly which ambient principles the construction requires [5]. Orton and Pitts later axiomatized these requirements as *axioms for modelling cubical type theory in a topos*.

At the far end of generality, Shulman proved that *every* $\infty$-topos models Martin-Löf type theory with univalent universes — so every theorem of HoTT holds in all $\infty$-toposes [6, §1]. The cubical models sit between syntax and this generality: they are the models that *compute*.

| Model | Ambient logic | Univalence computes? | HITs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kan simplicial sets (Voevodsky) | classical | no | partial |
| BCH cubical sets [4] | constructive | externally (model-level) | circle, truncation |
| CCHM cubical type theory [2] | constructive (internal) | yes — Glue, definitionally | via [3], judgmental rules |
| $\infty$-toposes (Shulman) | higher-categorical | n/a (semantic) | all (as colimits) |

## 5. Empirical Results and Theoretical Guarantees

We use "empirical" here in the sense proper to formal mathematics: machine-checked artifacts and metatheorems about the implemented systems.

**Canonicity.** The flagship metatheorem is Huber's canonicity result: *every closed term of natural-number type in cubical type theory reduces to a numeral* [5]. That is, $\vdash t : \mathbb{N}$ implies $t \equiv \mathsf{suc}^k(\mathsf{zero})$ for some $k$. This is exactly the computational guarantee the simplicial model could not supply: no stuck $\mathsf{ua}$-terms, no stuck higher eliminations. The proof is a cubical adaptation of the sconing / Artin-glueing logical-relations argument.

**Decidable type checking.** Cubical type theory enjoys decidable definitional equality via normalization-by-evaluation, as implemented in cubicaltt and Cubical Agda — so it functions as a real proof-assistant kernel [2][3].

**Large-scale formalization.** The CCHM model itself was formalized in the Nuprl proof assistant — "the longest proofs ever done in Nuprl" — verifying that every rule of cubical type theory is correct with respect to the presheaf semantics and has constructive content [5, citing the Nuprl formalization effort].

**Synthetic homotopy theory, executed.** In Cubical Agda, textbook results compute: $\pi_1(S^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ is not merely proved but *evaluated*. Transport along $\mathsf{ua}$ reducing definitionally through equivalences is routinely exploited to move structures across equivalences with zero proof overhead.

**Performance characteristics.** Glue-heavy proofs incur a cost: composition in Glue types unfolds equivalence data, and deeply nested $\mathsf{hcomp}$ terms accumulate. Proof-term size remains the principal practical bottleneck across the cubicaltt and Cubical Agda codebases.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

**The interval is not fibrant.** $\mathbb{I}$ is a *pretype*: there is no composition operation on $\mathbb{I}$ itself. This is a feature (it keeps the theory consistent and the model simple) but it complicates any attempt to treat the interval as an ordinary type, e.g., for higher-dimensional pattern matching on interval variables.

**No general HIT schema.** The HITs of [3] are a fixed menu — spheres, torus, suspensions, truncations, pushouts — each justified individually as an initial algebra. A *general schema* for higher inductive-inductive types with a uniform soundness proof and judgmental computation remains open.

**Canonicity with HITs.** Huber's canonicity covers the core theory; extending the logical-relations argument to the full HIT schema of [3] with all judgmental higher computation rules is technically demanding and still being worked out.

**Coherence and strictness.** Judgmental computation for higher constructors is convenient but *strict*, complicating the relationship with the weak $\infty$-categorical semantics, where such equations hold only up to coherent homotopy.

**Universe and Glue bureaucracy.** Glue types, while computationally well-behaved, introduce proof-term overhead, and their interaction with universe polymorphism and cumulativity requires care in implementations.

**Open problems, summarized:**

1. A complete, sound schema for cubical higher inductive-inductive types with judgmental computation rules.
2. A normalization (not just canonicity) proof for full cubical type theory with the HITs of [3].
3. A precise comparison theorem between synthetic homotopy theory in cubical type theory and classical homotopy theory of spaces, at the level of models (beyond test-category results).
4. Efficient, predictable reduction strategies for Glue- and $\mathsf{hcomp}$-heavy developments.

## 7. Conclusion

Cubical type theory resolves the central computational defect of univalent foundations. By internalizing the interval as a de Morgan algebra, replacing axiomatic identity types with *defined* path types, equipping every type with a uniform Kan composition operation, and — decisively — introducing *Glue* types, it turns Voevodsky's univalence axiom from a non-computing postulate into a *theorem with definitional behavior*: transport along $\mathsf{ua}(e)$ is just application of $e$ [2]. Higher inductive types, the second pillar of HoTT, receive a constructive presheaf semantics in which even higher constructors compute judgmentally [3]. The presheaf models over cube categories are constructive where the simplicial model is classical [4][5], and they sit within a broader landscape crowned by Shulman's theorem that all $\infty$-toposes model HoTT [6].

What remains is consolidation: general HIT schemas, full normalization proofs, and the engineering of proof assistants that make cubical methods routine. Univalence no longer merely *asserts* that equivalence is identity — in cubical type theory, it *computes* that identification, and mathematics formalized in this setting inherits both the invariance of the univalent perspective and the executability of a programming language.
