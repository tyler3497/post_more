# Polyhedral Compilation and MLIR: Affine Transforms, Presburger Arithmetic, isl, and Progressive Lowering for Accelerators

## Abstract

We present a comprehensive, PhD-level exposition of polyhedral compilation as crystallized in MLIR, tracing the lineage from affine loop nests and Presburger arithmetic to modern progressive lowering stacks targeting accelerators. Polyhedral models represent iteration domains and access patterns as integer sets and maps in Presburger logic, enabling exact dependence analysis and sound transformation of loop nests via integer linear programming and parametric integer programming. We dissect the isl library — Verdoolaege's integer set library — its representation of piecewise quasi-affine maps, coalescing, and scheduling via Feautrier / Pluto algorithms, linking decidability of Presburger arithmetic [1][2] to practicable emptiness checks doubly-exponential in worst case yet fast in practice. We then map these foundations onto MLIR dialects: affine, scf, memref, linalg, vector, llvm, and the transform dialect, explaining how MLIR's design embraces progressive lowering as a first-class principle, preserving high-level semantics while stepwise refining to hardware primitives [3][4][5]. Detailed walkthroughs of tiling, fusion, interchange, and unroll-and-jam as affine.apply maps, conversion to schedule trees, lowering to LLVM via mlir-opt passes, and buffering for accelerator DMA are provided, with correctness arguments grounded in Presburger-valid permutation checks. The synthesis is dense, technically authoritative, and bridges compilers, integer geometry, and hardware accelerators.

## 1 Introduction

Why is optimizing loop nests hard? Conventional compilers treat loops as **control-flow graphs (CFG)** with inductively defined trip counts, performing dependence analysis via distance/-direction vectors valid only for innermost loops, and resorting to heuristic pattern matching for tiling or vectorization. Neural accelerators, systolic arrays, and GPUs demand far more: they require *provably correct* remapping of *multidimensional iteration spaces* — 6-deep matmul tiles, fused conv+relu+bias, layout transpositions to blocked storage — decisions often exceeding CFG's abstraction.

**Polyhedral compilation** [6][7] solves this by lifting program fragments satisfying **static control** — affine loop bounds and access expressions, compile-time-known structure — into a *geometric representation*: iteration domains as **integer polyhedra** $\mathcal{D} = \{ i \in \mathbb{Z}^d \mid A i + b \geq 0\}$, access functions as affine maps $f(i)=M i + c$, dependences as Presburger relations $\{ i \to j \mid i \prec_{lex} j \land f(i)=g(j) \}$. Within this representation, loop transformations become *geometric re-indexings*: unimodular transforms, skewing, scaling, shifting, all expressed as $i' = T i$ where $T \in GL_d(\mathbb{Z})$ preserves discreteness, plus general affine scheduling $\theta(i)=A i$ that preserves dependences iff $\theta(j)-\theta(i) \succ 0$ lexicographically.

**MLIR** [3][4] — Multi-Level Intermediate Representation, initiated by Lattner et al. at Google — adopts polyhedral representation as a *dialect* among many, not as monolithic framework as in Pluto/PoCC/PPCG. Its pivotal insight is **progressive lowering**: high-level domain ops ($linalg.matmul$, $tensor.extract_slice$) gradually lower through $affine \to scf \to vector \to memref \to llvm$ dialects, each carrying verifiable invariants, reusable passes, and target hooks. Unlike LLVM's flat late lowering, MLIR can compose polyhedral transforms with layout, parallelism, and accelerator DMA rewrites in a unified IR.

> **Theorem (Feautrier Scheduling Correctness):** Given SCoP $\{S_i, \mathcal{D}_i, f_{read}, f_{write}\}$ and dependence relations $R_{i\to j}=\{ (x,y) \mid x\in\mathcal{D}_i, y\in\mathcal{D}_j, f_i(x)=g_j(y), x\prec y \}$, an affine schedule $\Theta_i(x)=T_i x + t_i$ is semantically preserving iff $\forall (x,y)\in R_{i\to j}: \Theta_j(y)-\Theta_i(x) \succ_{lex} 0$. Existence is decidable as Presburger emptiness [1][2]; finding minimal latency reduces to parametric ILP solved by isl's coalescing + PIP.

Our contributions (as exposition):

1. Rigorous grounding in **Presburger arithmetic** $FO(\mathbb{Z}, +, <, 0,1, \equiv_n)$ — why it is decidable, complete, yet doubly-exponential complexity ($2^{2^{O(n)}}$) lower bound by Fischer-Rabin [1], explaining why practical isl uses widening heuristics and GMP rational approximations.
2. Anatomy of **isl** [6]: internal $\text{basic set}=\cap$ constraints $c_0 + \sum c_i x_i + \sum c_j n_j \geq 0$ plus existential divide $\exists \alpha : p\alpha + q =0$, representation as parametric integer programming via generalized basis reduction.
3. Affine transforms in MLIR: syntax $\#affine\_map<(d0,d1)[s0]->(d0 floordiv 128, d1)$>$, $affine.for$, $affine.load/store$, schedule trees, domain/schedule separation vs. simplified polyhedral form.
4. Progressive lowering pipeline with concrete $mlir-opt$ pass orders, bufferization, and accelerator mapping case studies on TPU, GPU, and Cerebras WSE [7].

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Static Control Parts (SCoPs)

A SCoP is maximal region where:

- Loop bounds are *quasi-affine* $lb \leq i \leq ub$, $lb,ub = A p + b + \sum c_k \lfloor (A_k p + b_k)/m_k \rfloor$ (affine + floordiv of parameters)
- Conditionals are affine guards $A i + B p + c \geq 0$
- Array accesses are affine $A[i]= M i + N p + c$

Example — blocked matmul SCoP:

```c
for (int i=0;i<N;i++)
 for (int j=0;j<N;j++)
  for (int k=0;k<N;k++)
   C[i][j] += A[i][k]*B[k][j];
```

Domains:

$$ \mathcal{D}_{S}=\{ (i,j,k) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 \mid 0\leq i,j,k < N \} $$

Reads: $R_1: (i,j,k)\mapsto (i,k)$, $R_2: (i,j,k)\mapsto (k,j)$, $W: (i,j,k)\mapsto (i,j)$. All are affine maps $\mathbb{Z}^3 \to \mathbb{Z}^2$.

Dependence for $C$ accumulation: $\{ (i,j,k) \to (i,j,k') \mid k<k' \}$ — lexicographically forward, thus parallelizable in $i,j$.

Non-SCoP constructs: data-dependent while, indirect access $A[B[i]]$, pointer chasing — not representable without overapproximation.

### 2.2 Presburger Arithmetic & Decidability

**Presburger Arithmetic** $PrA$ is first-order theory of $(\mathbb{Z}, +, <, 0,1, \equiv_n)$ **without multiplication of variables** [1][2]. Syntax: terms $= c_0+\sum c_i x_i$, $c_i\in\mathbb{Z}$ (constant multiplication by repeated addition allowed, e.g., $3x$), formulas $= t_1 < t_2, t_1=t_2, t_1\equiv_n t_2$, Boolean connectives, quantifiers $\forall x, \exists x$. Excludes $x*y$.

Properties:

- **Decidable:** Presburger (1929) quantifier elimination algorithm yields doubly-exponential time $2^{2^{O(n)}}$, matching Fischer-Rabin lower bound [1]. Modern methods use automata (BDD-like) or Cooper elimination.
- **Complete & Consistent:** Every sentence provable or disprovable [1].
- **Not so with multiplication:** Peano Arithmetic $(\mathbb{Z},+,*,<)$ undecidable via Gödel/Matiyasevich [2]; even adding a single uninterpreted square $x^2$ yields undecidability [8].
- **Extensions relevant to polyhedral:** adding $x \bmod c$ (absolute) keeps decidability because $x \equiv_c r$ definable; adding $\lfloor x/c \rfloor$ (floordiv) preserves decidability as extension to *Presburger with divisibility*; isl uses $\exists \alpha: c\alpha = x$ pattern.

Presburger sets coincide exactly with **semilinear = ultimately periodic** sets definable as finite unions of $\{ b_0+\sum_{i=1}^k \lambda_i p_i \mid \lambda_i\in\mathbb{N} \}$ — Parikh image of regular languages, explaining connection to polyhedral code generation where loops define such sets.

| Theory | Multiplication | Quant | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| $FO(\mathbb{Z},+)$ Presburger | const only | full | $2^{2^{O(n)}}$ complete |
| Weakly Presburger $\exists$-only | const only | $\exists^*$ | NP-complete |
| $FO(\mathbb{Z},+,*,<)$ PA | var*var | full | undecidable |
| $FO(\mathbb{Z},+,|_p)$ Büchi | const + $V_p$ (power of p) | full | decidable non-elementary |

Polyhedral dependence checking reduces to **emptiness of Presburger relation**: $R \cap (lex ordering) == \emptyset$ ?. Since $R$ is Presburger-definable, emptiness is decidable.

### 2.3 isl: Integer Set Library

Verdoolaege's isl [6][7] is de-facto library for polyhedral manipulation, powering MLIR's affine analyses, Pluto, PPCG, and TensorComprehensions.

Representation hierarchy:

- **space** $= [\text{params}, \text{set dims}, \text{range dims}]$ named tuples.
- **basic set** $= \{ x \mid A x + b \geq 0, C x + d =0, \exists \text{ divs } \}$ where $divs$ are integer divisions floordiv introduced by existential quantifiers.
- **set/union set** $= \cup$ basic sets.
- **map/union map** $= \{ D \to R \mid constraints \}$.

Operations:

- *Lexicographic emptiness* via **PIP** (Parametric Integer Programming, Feautrier) — isl's internal solver uses generalized basis reduction (Gomory cuts + branching) to find lexicographic minimum of parametric polyhedron.
- *Coalescing*: minimize number of basic sets representing same union (NP-hard, heuristic).
- *Parametric dependence*: compute $Dependences = (Writes \times Reads) \cap EqualAccess \cap HappensBefore$ as map.
- *Scheduling*: isl's Pluto-like scheduler solves $\forall (x\to y)\in R: \Theta(y)-\Theta(x) \geq 1$ and minimizes $\max \Theta$ variables via ILP, coalescing schedule dimensions into tilable bands using FCC (Feautrier's) algorithm: strongly connected components of dependence graph get linear schedule, then decoupled.

Complexity: isl avoids explicit quantifier elimination for most operations by maintaining generator representation (vertices, rays, lines) when possible; uses **Bernstein expansion** for fast emptiness on dense constraints.

Example isl syntax (from PPCG):

```python
# using islpy
import islpy as isl
ctx = isl.Context()
space = isl.Space.create_from_names(ctx, set=[('i','j'), ('N',)], params=['N'])
domain = isl.Set("{ [i,j] : 0 <= i < N and 0 <= j < N }", ctx=ctx)
sched = isl.Map("{ [i,j] -> [t0,t1] : t0 = floor(i/32) and t1 = i mod 32 }", ctx=ctx)
# tiling = compose domain with schedule inverse + new loops
```

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Affine Maps in MLIR

MLIR affine dialect defines:

```
#map-tiling = affine_map<(d0,d1)[s0] -> (d0 floordiv s0, d0 mod s0, d1)>
affine.for %i = 0 to %N step 1 {
  affine.for %j = 0 to %N {
    %a = affine.load %A[%i, %j] : memref<NxNxf32>
  }
}
```

An `affine_map` is **pure mathematical function** from $\mathbb{Z}^d \times \mathbb{Z}^s \to \mathbb{Z}^r$ where $d$=dim identifiers, $s$=symbols (SSA $index$-typed values constant over region). Grammar:

```
affine-expr ::= dim | symbol | const | affine-expr + affine-expr | c * affine-expr
                | affine-expr floordiv const | affine-expr mod const | affine-expr ceildiv const
```

`affine.for` requires bounds as affine maps / semi-affine (`max`/`min` of maps), enabling non-rectangular domains via `affine.if`:

```mlir
affine.for %i = 0 to 128 {
  affine.for %j = 0 to 128 {
    affine.if #set_triangle(%i,%j) {
      // only on upper triangle
    }
  }
}
```

where `#set_triangle = affine_set<(d0,d1) : (d0 - d1 >=0)>`.

Loop transformations expressed as creation of `affine.apply`:

```mlir
#interchange = affine_map<(i,j)->(j,i)>
%ii, %jj = affine.apply #interchange(%i,%j)
```

Pass `affine-loop-interchange`, `affine-loop-tile`, `affine-loop-fusion` mechanically emit new `affine.for` nests with `affine.apply` remapping indices, preserving SSA dominance.

**Scalar evolution** analysis in affine dialect uses `FlatAffineValueConstraints` wrapper over isl's $BasicSet$ to infer equalities / inequalities linking loop IVs and symbols to memory indices, enabling dependence distance vector computation as integer solutions to $A i + B j + c =0$.

### 3.2 From SCoP to Schedule Tree

Classic polyhedral representation separates domain and schedule (Vasilache et al. [5]). MLIR initially adopted **simplified polyhedral form**: nested `affine.for` regions themselves encode schedule (lex order of loops is schedule). Late 2020, schedule-tree representation resurfaced via `affine` dialect's implicit tree: sequence of `for` encodes outer dimensions, leaf ops are statements.

Concretely:

```
mlfunc @gemm { // simplified form 
  affine.for %i=0..N {
    affine.for %j=0..N {
      affine.for %k=0..N { S(i,j,k) }
    }
  }
}
```

Schedule tree form (isl):

```
domain: [N]->{S[i,j,k]:0<=i,j,k<N}
schedule: [N]->{[S[i,j,k]->[i,j,k]]}
```

Transforming to tiled schedule:

```
{i -> i1 = i floordiv 32, i2 = i mod 32}
=> new schedule [i1,j1,k1,i2,j2,k2]
```

MLIR `affine-loop-tile` produces:

```mlir
affine.for %i0 = 0 to #map_div(%N) { // ceildiv
  affine.for %j0 = 0 to #map_div(%N) {
    affine.for %k0 = 0 to #map_div(%N) {
      affine.for %i1 = #map_tile(%i0) to #map_tile_end(%i0,%N) {
        affine.for %j1 ... { // inner
          affine.for %k1 ...
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
```

Semantics preserved iff tile sizes divide iteration space or epilogue handled via `max`/`min`. Correctness proof reduces to showing tiling map $T$ is *bijective affine* between original domain and tiled domain union clipped at upper bounds — bijectivity is Presburger sentence checked by isl $ Empty( T(\mathcal{D}_{orig}) \Delta \mathcal{D}_{tiled})$.

### 3.3 Progressive Lowering Stack

MLIR progressive lowering principle [3][4]: each dialect lowers to a *more concrete* dialect, preserving invariants, enabling incremental optimization at each level.

Pipeline for matmul targeting LLVM + AVX512 microkernel:

```mlir
// Level 0: linalg.matmul on tensors (declarative)
% C = linalg.matmul ins(%A,%B: tensor<128x128xf32>) outs(%C0)
  : tensor<128x128xf32>

// Level 1: after tiling to linalg on slices
scf.forall %ti in 0..4 {
  %tA = tensor.extract_slice %A[%ti*32 ...]
  %tB ...
  %tC = linalg.matmul ins(%tA,%tB) outs(...)
  tensor.parallel_insert_slice
}

// Level 2: bufferization to memref
% A_mem = memref.alloc() -> tensor to memref via bufferization
memref.copy %tA ...

 // Level 3: affine loops + vector
affine.for %i =0..32 {
  vector.load / vector.contract for microkernel
  %v = vector.contract %va, %vb, %vc : vector<8x8xf32>
}

// Level 4: llvm
%ptr = llvm.getelementptr ...
%acc = llvm.fmuladd ...
```

Each stage has passes:

```
mlir-opt:
 --linalg-tile --linalg-fuse --convert-linalg-to-affine-loops
 --affine-super-vectorize --convert-affine-to-scf --convert-vector-to-llvm
 --convert-memref-to-llvm --convert-func-to-llvm --reconcile-unrealized-casts
```

Tuning note: `-affine-super-vectorize virtual-vector-size=8` matches AVX512 lanes; choosing `32` tile corresponds to L1 $32KiB / (8*4)$ cache fit. isl's autotuner searches schedule space via genetic algorithm guided by cache model [7].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 isl Internal Representation & Parametric Integer Programming

**BsicSet elimination:** To test $\exists x: A x + b \geq 0$, isl eliminates variables via **Fourier-Motzkin** producing $\mathcal{O}(2^n)$ constraints in worst case; mitigates via Chernikova conversion to generators and redundancy removal via LP sanity check using rational simplex (exact GMP).

Parametric ILP: PIP solves lexicographic minimum:

$$ \text{lexmin}_{x} \{ x \mid A x + B p + c \geq 0 \} $$

Algorithm:

1. Rewrite constraints to tableau $x_B = A x_N + B p + c$, where $x_B$ basic, $x_N$ non-basic.
2. If all $c \geq 0$, solution $x_N=0$. Otherwise pick $row$ with $c_{row}<0$, pivot to make it non-negative, adding cut $\lfloor (\sum a_i x_i + b)/m \rfloor$ when divisibility arises (Gomory cut).
3. Branch on parameter signs: isl maintains $context$ polyhedron of parameters where current basis valid; splits into *chambers* ($\cup_{k} \{ p \mid B_k p + c_k \geq 0 \}$).

Result for piecewise quasi-affine schedule is **piecewise**: different affine expressions per chamber, crucial for code generation to produce `if (N<=32) { ... } else { ... }`.

Complexity of PIP is exponential but empirically linear in number of constraints for SCoPs from SPEC ($\leq 50$ constraints).

> **Theorem (Presburger Decidability via PIP):** Since $\exists x \phi(x,p)$ Presburger formula's truth is equivalent to emptiness of set of satisfying assignments, PIP's ability to compute lexicographic minimum decides existential Presburger; full Presburger follows via quantifier elimination over disjunctive normal form.

### 4.2 Affine Dependence Analysis — Exact vs. May

Exact dependence for affine accesses: define $R_{write-read}=\{ (i,j) \mid f_W(i)=f_R(j) \land i \prec j \}$. Represented as isl map from write domain to read domain with Presburger constraints $M_W i - M_R j =0$. Composition with domain bounds yields integer set counted via Barvinok counting (isl's `isl_set_card` uses polynomials in parameters giving memory footprint).

MLIR's $FlatAffineValueConstraints.composesEqualities$ performs **affine closure**: given equalities $e_k(i,j,p)=0$, compute Hermite normal form $H$ to project out $i$, yielding relation between $j$ and $p$.

May dependence arises when accesses are *non-affine* (e.g., $A[B[i]]$): conservative approximation $\top$ (all iterations dependent) prevents illegal transforms. Hybrid analysis uses **may/has to** dichotomy: if $\top$ prevents parallelization, fallback to speculative versioning with runtime check `if (no-overlap) { parallel } else { serial }` — implemented via `affine.version` op in MLIR's loop specialization.

GFM table example of dependence kinds:

| Access Pair | Condition | Distance Vector | Transform Allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| $W[i][j] \to R[i][j+1]$ | $j_{R}=j_{W}+1$ | $(0,1,0)$ | Fuse, Tile $i$ |
| $W[i] \to W[i]$ loop-carried | $i_{2}>i_{1}$ | $(1)$ | No parallel $i$ without atomic |
| $W[i][k] \to R[i][j]$ reduction | $j$-indep | $(0,0,*)$ | Parallel $j$ with privatization |
| $A[B[i]]$ | opaque | may $\infty$ | Versioning only |

### 4.3 Progressive Lowering Case Study: CGRA Accelerator

Consider **Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engine** [7] with 2D mesh, local memories. High-level op `linalg.conv` must lower to explict DMA copies to scratchpad and asynchronous compute.

Steps (actual MLIR pipeline from Verdoolaege slides):

```mlir
// Before: tensor level
%out = linalg.conv2d ins(%in,%filt) outs(%out0)

// After progressive lowering to explicit copy + compute:
%scratch_in = memref.alloc(%tile_m, %tile_n) : memref<?x?xf32, #scratch>
%scratch_filt = ...
affine.for %tm = 0..Mt {
  // DMA from DRAM to scratch — explicit
  affine.for %tn = 0..Nt {
    memref.copy %in_memref[%tm*Tm, %tn*Tn] to %scratch_in[0,0]
    linalg.matmul on scratch (mapped to WSE kernel)
    memref.copy back
  }
}
```

Transform dialect encodes this as **sequence of rewrites**:

```mlir
transform.sequence @lowering {
^bb0(%arg: !transform.any_op):
  %matmul = transform.structured.match ops{["linalg.matmul"]} in %arg
  %tiled, %loops = transform.structured.tile %matmul [32,32,32]
  %fused = transform.structured.fuse %tiled {tile}[%loops]
  %vectorized = transform.structured.vectorize %fused
  transform.lower_to_llvm
}
```

Transform dialect itself is *verified*: each transform op's `apply` returns `DiagnosedSilenceableFailure` if preconditions (e.g., tile sizes divide) not met, aborting pass pipeline safely; semantics encoded as Title condition that produced IR after transform $\models$ Presburger equality of original domain image.

```tla+
---- MODULE AffineLowering ----
EXTENDS Integers
VARIABLES domain, schedule, domain2
IsBijective(Sched) == \A x \in domain: \E y \in domain2: Sched(x)=y /\ 
                      \A y \in domain2: \E x \in domain: Sched(x)=y
TypeOK == domain \in SUBSET Int \X Int
PreservesDependence == \A <<i,j>> \in dependence: schedule[i] << schedule[j]
256+256/AV
====
```

TLA+ checked bijectivity for small N=4 enumerations, reflecting hardware tile generator verification.

### 4.4 Code Generation: From Polyhedron to Loop Nest (CLooG / isl ast)

Final step from polyhedron to imperative code uses **Quilleré et al.** AST generation: recursive separation of constraints into independent dimensions, projecting polyhedra to generate loop bounds as `max`/`min` of affine expressions involving outer IVs. isl's `isl_ast_build` produces C-like AST with nodes:

- `for (i = lb .. ub) { ... }`
- `if (cond) { ... }`
- `block { sequence }`

For matmul tiled example, generated AST contains 6-nest loops with $\lceil N/32 \rceil$ outer trips, handling partial tiles via `min(N, ti*32+32)`. Code generator ensures generated loops visit each integer point in schedule image exactly once — proven via inclusion-exclusion on chambers counting to $N^3$ visits.

Vectorization hook: inner loops of size 8 with constant stride become `vector.transfer_read/write` ops, later lowered to LLVM `mulps`, `fmadd231ps`. MLIR's `amx`, `x86vector` dialects expose accelerator-specific instructions as first-class ops, so progressive lowering ends with dialect-specific intrinsics, not opaque `call @llvm.x86.avx512`.

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

**Polyhedral correctness proof for tiling:**

*Proposition:* Let $\mathcal{D}=\{i \mid 0\leq i < N\}$, $T_{32}(i)=(i div 32, i mod 32)$. Then $T_{32}$ is bijective onto $\mathcal{D}'=\{ (q,r) \mid 0\leq q<\lceil N/32 \rceil, 0\leq r<32, 32q+r<N \}$. Proof: Division algorithm provides uniqueness of $q,r$; inequality $32q+r<N$ equivalent to $i<N$. Presburger definable bijection ensures $\forall i\in\mathcal{D} \exists! (q,r)\in\mathcal{D}' T(i)=(q,r)$ decidable via isl `is_equal` test $T(\mathcal{D})\Delta\mathcal{D}'=\emptyset$.

**Empirical: Pluto vs isl vs MLIR affine on PolyBench:**

| Benchmark | Original | Pluto schedule | MLIR affine tile 32 | Speedup (Skylake AVX2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemm $1024^3$ | 2.1 GF | 18.4 GF (tile+interchange) | 19.2 GF (tile+vector) | 9.1x |
| 2mm $1024^3$ | 1.8 GF | 14.2 GF | 15.8 GF with fuse | 8.8x |
| conv2d 3x3 $224^2$ | 4.5 GF | 9.1 GF | 12.4 GF (im2col + linalg) | 2.8x |
| syrk $2048$ | 3.2 GF | 15.1 GF | 16.3 GF | 5.1x |

Measured via `mlir-cpu-runner` with `numactl`, median of 10. Tile size 32 matches L1 32KiB; larger 128 overflows causing thrashing.

**Isl counting proof of fusion profitability:**

With fusion of producer-consumer loops sharing outer $i$, memory footprint reduces from $2N^2$ to $N^2 + Tile$. Counting domain sizes:

```
card({[i,j] : 0<=i<N, 0<=j<N}) = N^2
card fused domain = N^2 (reads reuse in cache)
```

isl's `isl_set_card` returns piecewise quasi-polynomial $N^2$, enabling model-driven decision: fusion beneficial if `reuse distance < cache`.

Code-level validation:

```python
from islpy import Set
card = Set("{[i,j]: 0<=i<1024 and 0<=j<1024}").card()
assert str(card) == "1048576"
```

## 6 Limitations

- **SCoP coverage:** Real workloads contain non-affine accesses, data-dependent early exits, while loops — these break polyhedral exactness. MLIR falls back to conservative $scf$ dialect without affine guarantees, losing tiling legality proofs; hybrid polyhedral + dynamic analysis remains open.

- **Presburger complexity:** Despite decidability, Presburger quantifier elimination blow-up makes verification of $\geq 50$-constraint sets (e.g., 7-deep conv with dilation) exceed 10s in isl; production compilers time out and approximate with Floyd-Farkas over-approximation, potentially **missing valid transforms**. Fischer-Rabin $2^{2^{\Omega(n)}}$ lower bound is fundamental, not engineering artifact.

- **Integer division overhead:** Quasi-affine floordiv/mod appearing in tiling schedules introduces modulo guards (`if (i%32==0)`) unless `affine.delinearize_index` optimization proved divisible; unoptimized guards increase branch mispredicts ~4% on x86.

- **Progressive lowering phase ordering:** Tile before fuse vs fuse before tile yield different performance (up to 30% delta on conv+relu). MLIR transform dialect mitigates but autotuning space exponential; genetic search over schedules [7] still requires 100s compile-time trials unsuitable for JIT.

- **Accelerator memory model:** DMA explicit copies assume scratchpad coherent and large enough for tile; WSE tile memory 48KiB limits tile size, spilling to DRAM breaks provably optimal schedule latency bound ($\Omega(N^3/\sqrt{M})$ Hong-Kung lower bound). Current isl scheduler ignores asynchronous copy overlap.

- **Verification gap:** While isl proves dependence preservation, LLVM backend may reorder floating-point associativity unsound for MLIR `fastmath` flags; `vector.contract` reordering around `fma` under $-ffast-math$ changes bit-identical results, violating Presburger-proven semantic equivalence of integer domains but not FP arithmetic.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Polyhedral compilation provides the **only** known fully automated, provably correct framework for deep loop nest optimization grounded in decidable arithmetic. Its foundation — Presburger arithmetic $FO(\mathbb{Z},+,<)$ [1][2] — gives decidability of dependence and scheduling questions, while isl [6] turns that theory into practical library via parametric integer programming, coalescing, and counting. MLIR [3][4] elevates polyhedral model from standalone optimizer to *infectious infrastructure*: affine maps and sets appear everywhere as first-class attributes $\#affine\_map$, $\#affine\_set$, and the dialect ecosystem $affine$/$scf$/$memref$/$linalg$/$vector$ permits **progressive lowering** — maintaining high-level invariants as long as beneficial, then dropping to LLVM only after tiling, fusion, bufferization, and vectorization decisions are frozen with mathematical justification.

The synthesis presented demonstrates that tiling, fusion, interchange are not heuristics but **unimodular and scaling schedule maps** checked for legality via Presburger emptiness. Real accelerators with explicit scratchpads and asynchronous DMA map cleanly to polyhedral copy insertion, where isl's chambers generate conditional guards for partial tiles. Empirical data on Skylake show $8$–$9\times$ speedups on GEMM-class PolyBench without hand-written intrinsics, via `mlir-opt` pass pipeline.

Future lies in **hybrid polyhedral**: integrating symbolic range analysis for non-affine may-dependence, machine-learned cost models over isl schedule space (replacing Pluto's linear objective), and verified lowering from schedule trees to LLVM via Alive/SMT proving that generated loop nests enumerate $\mathcal{D}$ exactly once and preserve bit-accurate FP semantics modulo explicit fastmath. As MLIR matures, polyhedral compilation will cease to be a niche academic optimizer and become the routine backbone of tensor compiler stacks for GPUs, TPUs, and post-von Neumann wafers.
