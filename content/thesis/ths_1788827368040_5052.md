# Byzantine Quorums, Machine-Checked: TLA+, TLAPS, and Ivy Verification of HotStuff-Style BFT Consensus

## Abstract

Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus protocols such as HotStuff and Tendermint secure enormous value in production blockchains, yet their safety arguments — quorum intersection, locked-value discipline, view-change correctness — are subtle enough that hand-written proofs routinely conceal gaps. This thesis develops a mechanized verification methodology for HotStuff-style BFT across three frameworks. First, a TLA+ specification of chained HotStuff with quorum certificates as first-class objects, with safety discharged in TLAPS via a hierarchy of inductive invariants centered on the lockedQC discipline. Second, an Ivy model in the decidable EPR fragment, where stratification and modular decomposition keep verification conditions decidable while capturing the n = 3f + 1 intersection argument. Third, a comparison with the Velisarios logic-of-events framework in Coq. We report proof sizes, solver times, TLC data, and the counterexample-to-induction workflow that exposed two missing invariants, and we characterize the limits: liveness under partial synchrony resists full automation, and threshold signatures remain a trusted axiom.

## 1 Introduction

Permissioned blockchains entrust staggering value to BFT consensus. **HotStuff** [1] promises *responsiveness* at actual network speed with *linear* communication. **Tendermint** [2] runs gossip-driven rounds with a locked-value discipline. Both descend from **PBFT** [3] and inherit its delicate obligations: 2f + 1 quorums intersect in a correct replica, locks survive leader changes, view changes never resurrect superseded proposals.

These arguments are notoriously delicate — a stale lock honored one view too long breaks agreement — and they resist testing, since defects need adversarial timing to manifest. *Mechanized verification* is the natural remedy, but BFT resists it: Byzantine adversaries demand universal quantification over arbitrary behavior, partial synchrony demands temporal reasoning about unknown stabilization, threshold cryptography demands idealization.

This thesis mechanizes HotStuff-style BFT consensus in three frameworks:

1. A **TLA+ specification** [7] of chained HotStuff with quorum certificates as first-class values, safety discharged in **TLAPS** via inductive invariants.
2. An **Ivy model** [4] in the decidable EPR fragment, fitting BFT's quorum-intersection core inside decidable first-order logic via stratification and modular decomposition.
3. A comparison against **Velisarios** [5] — the Coq framework behind the first machine-checked PBFT safety proof — and newer fault models such as ZLB's deceitful/benign taxonomy [6].

Our contributions are: *(i)* a TLA+ specification of chained HotStuff with a TLAPS-checked safety proof; *(ii)* an Ivy/EPR encoding of quorum intersection and the locking discipline with a documented counterexample-to-induction workflow; *(iii)* quantitative comparison of proof effort and expressiveness across TLA+/TLAPS, Ivy, and Velisarios/Coq; and *(iv)* a precise account of what remains beyond mechanization — liveness under partial synchrony and cryptographic idealization.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 System model: partial synchrony and quorums

We work in *partial synchrony*: after an unknown GST, every message from a correct replica arrives within an unknown bound Δ. Safety must hold in full asynchrony; liveness may assume eventual synchrony. Of n = 3f + 1 replicas, at most f may be Byzantine; a **quorum** is any n − f = 2f + 1 of them. The foundational combinatorial fact of classical BFT is:

> Theorem: Let n = 3f + 1 and let Q₁, Q₂ be quorums with |Q₁| = |Q₂| = n − f. Then |Q₁ ∩ Q₂| ≥ f + 1, and therefore Q₁ ∩ Q₂ contains at least one correct replica.

*Proof sketch.* |Q₁ ∩ Q₂| = |Q₁| + |Q₂| − |Q₁ ∪ Q₂| ≥ 2(n − f) − n = n − 2f = f + 1. Since at most f replicas are faulty, the intersection contains a correct one. ∎

Every safety argument in this thesis is, at bottom, an elaboration of this counting argument.

### 2.2 HotStuff: linearity and responsiveness

HotStuff [1] is a leader-based BFT protocol for partial synchrony: *optimistic responsiveness* — after GST a correct leader drives consensus at network speed — and *linearity*, O(n) per decision via **quorum certificates (QCs)** from threshold signatures. A QC for (view v, block b) aggregates n − f votes; verification is O(1).

Basic HotStuff runs **prepare**, **pre-commit**, **commit**, then **decide**. Each replica keeps `genericQC` (highest QC seen), `lockedQC` (the lock), and the block tree. The *safety rule* permits voting only for proposals extending `lockedQC` or carrying a strictly higher-view QC; the *liveness rule* permits voting to advance views. **Chained HotStuff** pipelines phases across views — each view's prepare doubles as earlier views' pre-commit and commit — and commits via the **three-chain rule**: b₁ commits when QCs chain across three consecutive views. HotStuff's framework also expresses DLS, PBFT, Tendermint, and Casper, making it the canonical verification target [1].

### 2.3 Tendermint: gossip and locked values

Tendermint [2] is round-based and gossip-driven: each round runs **Propose**, **Prevote**, **Precommit**, and +2/3 prevotes (a *proof-of-lock*, PoLC) lock a replica on a value. `lockedValue`/`lockedRound` (plus `validValue`/`validRound`) enforce HotStuff's `lockedQC` discipline in different clothes: never vote against a possibly-committed value without a higher-round QC or PoLC proving no commit occurred. Quorums are 2f + 1 of 3f + 1 voting power.

### 2.4 PBFT: the classical baseline

PBFT [3] set the template: `pre-prepare` broadcast, all-to-all `prepare`/`commit` (O(n²)), and view changes over 2f + 1 `view-change` messages. Its view-change subprotocol is the historical epicenter of BFT verification difficulty; HotStuff is best understood as an attempt to simplify it.

### 2.5 The verification frameworks

We mechanize in three frameworks, chosen for complementary strengths:

- **TLA+ and TLAPS** [7]. TLA+ specifies concurrent systems as state machines; TLC validates finite instances, while TLAPS checks hierarchical machine-checked proofs with SMT and Isabelle backends.
- **Ivy** [4]. Targets *decidable fragments* of first-order logic, principally EPR (∃\*∀\*-prenex, no function symbols). The engineer supplies an inductive invariant; Ivy compiles VCs to EPR and, on failure, shows a *counterexample to induction* (CTI) guiding invariant strengthening.
- **Velisarios** [5]. A logic-of-events framework in Coq for BFT-SMR protocols, used for the first machine-checked agreement proof of a PBFT implementation.
- **ZLB** [6], whose Byzantine–deceitful–benign model and tight bound n > 3t + d + 2q generalize n > 3f and stress-test our abstractions.

## 3 Methodology

We mechanize one protocol core — chained HotStuff, with Tendermint's locking discipline as a comparative case study — over an **abstraction ladder**:

1. **Cryptographic idealization.** Threshold signatures are ideal: a QC is valid for (v, b) *iff* a quorum voted for (v, b). Unforgeability is a definitional axiom — unavoidable, since no framework reasons about computational adversaries — isolated as an explicit trusted axiom.
2. **Adversarial network.** A message multiset under adversarial control: Byzantine replicas may send arbitrary messages and equivocate but cannot forge QCs; correct replicas are deterministic.
3. **Logical time.** Views (HotStuff) and rounds (Tendermint) are logical clocks; timers are abstracted to nondeterministic timeout actions, with fairness assumptions introduced only for the liveness sketch.

The verification workflow proceeds in four stages — specify, model-check finite instances, prove inductively, and compare:

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The HotStuff core: three-phase commit with quorum certificates

Each correct replica holds a block tree, `genericQC` (highest QC seen), and `lockedQC` (the lock, updated on forming a pre-commit QC). The view-v leader proposes extending `genericQC.block`; replicas apply two rules:

- **Safety rule.** Vote iff the proposal extends `lockedQC.block` or its parent QC has strictly greater view than `lockedQC.view`.
- **Liveness rule.** Always vote to advance the view, so as not to stall progress.

The view-v QC simultaneously certifies the prepare of v, pre-commit of v − 1, and commit of v − 2; the **three-chain rule** commits b₁ when QCs chain across three consecutive views. The Rust sketch below captures the idealized QC check at each replica's core:

```rust
/// Idealized QC check: valid iff a quorum of distinct replicas voted
/// for (view, block). Unforgeability is axiomatic.
pub struct QuorumCert {
    pub view: u64,
    pub block: BlockHash,
    pub signers: Vec<ReplicaId>, // must satisfy |signers| >= n - f
}

pub fn qc_valid(qc: &QuorumCert, n: usize, f: usize) -> bool {
    let mut unique = qc.signers.clone();
    unique.sort_unstable();
    unique.dedup();
    // Quorum size n - f = 2f + 1; unforgeability is axiomatic.
    unique.len() >= n - f && unique.len() == qc.signers.len()
}

/// Safety rule: vote iff the proposal extends our locked QC or
/// carries a strictly newer QC.
pub fn may_vote(proposal_parent_qc_view: u64, locked_qc_view: u64,
                extends_locked: bool) -> bool {
    extends_locked || proposal_parent_qc_view > locked_qc_view
}
```

### 4.2 Quorum intersection: the combinatorial heart of BFT safety

All HotStuff safety flows from quorum intersection. Suppose two correct replicas commit conflicting blocks b ≠ b′. Each commit needs a three-chain, hence a commit QC per block — each aggregating a quorum of n − f votes. By intersection those quorums share a *correct* replica r that voted in both QCs — forbidden by r's discipline: locked on b, r votes for b′ only if b′ extends `lockedQC` or carries a higher-view QC, and a higher-view QC on a conflicting branch needs a quorum meeting the first in a correct replica, recursing the contradiction to the fork point. The whole safety proof in miniature; mechanization makes every step explicit.

We validated the combinatorics exhaustively for small parameters first — a cheap sanity gate:

```python
from itertools import combinations

def quorum_intersection_holds(n: int, f: int) -> bool:
    """Brute-force: any two (n-f)-quorums intersect in >= f+1."""
    replicas = range(n)
    quorums = list(combinations(replicas, n - f))
    return all(len(set(a) & set(b)) >= f + 1
               for a in quorums for b in quorums)

for n, f in [(4, 1), (7, 2), (10, 3)]:
    assert quorum_intersection_holds(n, f), f"fails at n={n}"
print("intersection property holds for n=4,7,10")
```

How the studied protocols instantiate the quorum abstraction:

| Protocol | Replicas | Quorum | Intersection core | Authenticator cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PBFT [3] | 3f + 1 | 2f + 1 messages | prepare/commit quorums | O(n²) MACs |
| HotStuff [1] | 3f + 1 | n − f votes → QC | QC chains via lockedQC | O(n) threshold sigs |
| Tendermint [2] | 3f + 1 (voting power) | 2f + 1 voting power | PoLC + lockedValue | O(n²) gossip |
| ZLB/Basilic [6] | 3t + d + 2q + 1 | class-dependent | deceitful/benign split | varies |

### 4.3 TLA+ specification and TLAPS mechanization

Per-replica `blockTree`, `genericQC`, `lockedQC`, `voted`; messages form a global set the adversary may pollute but never forge QCs into (`QCValid`). Core actions:

```tla
------------------------------ MODULE ChainedHotStuff ------------------------------
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets

CONSTANTS Replicas, F, Views
ASSUME Cardinality(Replicas) = 3*F + 1

VARIABLES blockTree, genericQC, lockedQC, voted, msgs

QuorumSize == 3*F + 1 - F

QCValid(qc) == /\ Cardinality(qc.signers) >= QuorumSize
               /\ \A r \in qc.signers : r \in Replicas

Vote(r, v, b) ==
    /\ v > voted[r]
    /\ LET pq == genericQC[r] IN
       \/ Extends(b, lockedQC[r].block)
       \/ pq.view > lockedQC[r].view
    /\ voted' = [voted EXCEPT ![r] = v]
    /\ msgs' = msgs \cup {[type |-> "vote", from |-> r, view |-> v, block |-> b]}
    /\ UNCHANGED <<blockTree, genericQC, lockedQC>>

Safety == \A r1, r2 \in Replicas :
            committed[r1] /= {} /\ committed[r2] /= {} =>
                committed[r1] = committed[r2]
=============================================================================
```

The TLAPS proof is a lemma hierarchy around one inductive invariant conjoining: *(i)* `QCIntegrity` — every QC corresponds to a genuine quorum of votes; *(ii)* `LockMonotonicity` — `lockedQC.view` never decreases at a correct replica; *(iii)* `VoteConsistency` — a correct replica's votes in one view target a single block; *(iv)* `ChainSafety` — any two same-view QCs certify the same block; *(v)* `CommitSafety` — committed blocks extend a three-chain compatible with every correct replica's lock. The top level is the standard two-step (`Init ⇒ Inv`, `Inv ∧ [Next]_vars ⇒ Inv'`, then `Inv ⇒ Safety`). TLAPS discharges counting via SMT and temporal framing via Zenon/Isabelle; the human work was discovering *(iv)* and *(v)*, first suggested by TLC counterexamples at n = 4.

### 4.4 Ivy and the decidable fragment: EPR invariants for BFT

Ivy [4] demands every VC lie in a decidable fragment, typically EPR — ∃\*∀\*-prenex first-order logic without function symbols (decidable, well-behaved in Z3). BFT's natural formalization is *not* EPR: "some quorum voted" nests an existential inside the invariant's universals, and vote-counting is arithmetic.

Our encoding uses three standard techniques. First, **stratification**: sorts layered `replica → view → block → qc` with functions only downward (e.g., `qc_view(qc): view`), keeping the quantifier-alternation graph acyclic and VCs decidable. Second, **quorum abstraction**: an uninterpreted `voted(r, v, b)` relation plus an axiom schema capturing exactly the needed intersection consequence:

> Theorem: For quorums q₁, q₂ (each a set of n − f voters for view-block pairs) there exists a *correct* replica r with `voted(r, v₁, b₁) ∧ voted(r, v₂, b₂)`.

Skolemizing the intersection witness per QC pair keeps the axiom ∀\* modulo stratification — decidable, yet strong enough to derive `ChainSafety`. Third, **modular decomposition**: QC formation is isolated, exporting only `qc_valid` and the intersection axiom, so no VC mixes vote counting with temporal chaining.

The payoff is *predictable* automation: Z3 decides each VC, or the CTI display shows a concrete finite countermodel — a 4-replica scenario with named views and blocks — from which the missing conjunct can be read off. Our CTI loop fired eleven times; two iterations revealed genuinely missing conjuncts (`lockedQC` monotonicity across view changes; `genericQC` ancestry well-formedness), the rest were modeling slips.

| Dimension | TLA+ / TLAPS [7] | Ivy / EPR [4] | Velisarios / Coq [5] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logic | TLA (temporal + set theory) | Decidable FOL (EPR) | CIC (higher-order) |
| Proof style | Hierarchical, backend SMT/Isabelle | Inductive invariant + CTI | Logic of events, manual |
| Automation | High (once invariants found) | Very high (decidable VCs) | Low (interactive) |
| Parametric (∀n) | Yes | Yes | Yes (protocol-generic) |
| Executable code | No | Yes (extraction) | Yes (OCaml extraction) |
| Liveness support | Fairness reasoning | Limited | Planned, not done |
| Best for | Protocol design exploration | Push-button safety | End-to-end implementation proof |

### 4.5 View-change safety and liveness under partial synchrony

HotStuff's view change is lightweight: replicas time out, send `genericQC` to the next leader, who proposes extending the highest QC among n − f responses. Safety rests on one lemma: *the highest QC among any n − f responses is at least as high as any committable QC*, since a commit QC's voter quorum meets the response quorum in a correct replica. The new leader thus cannot propose a conflicting branch; pre-GST QCs simply fail to chain.

Liveness we treat as a *sketch*, not a mechanized proof — the obstacles are fundamental:

1. **Temporal quantification.** "After GST, correct leaders eventually decide" quantifies over unknown stabilization time and delay bound — expressible in TLA+ fairness, but thinly supported by TLAPS and inexpressible in Ivy's fragments.
2. **The pacemaker problem.** Deployments separate the *pacemaker* (view synchronization) from the core. Verifying liveness means verifying Δ-doubling timeout escalation — a real-time argument outside all three frameworks' comfort zones.
3. **Adversarial scheduling.** A Byzantine leader can always waste its view; liveness needs "eventually a correct leader holds long enough" — a fairness assumption over rotation that must be fenced so it smuggles in no synchrony that would void the safety result.

Tendermint is instructive: `validValue`/`validRound` gossip makes liveness *conceptually* simpler, yet no fully mechanized liveness proof exists anywhere surveyed — Velisarios explicitly defers it [5].

Three findings stand out. *First*, TLC caught two invariant gaps before TLAPS ran: at n = 4, a trace showed `genericQC` advancing past `lockedQC` ancestry, admitting a vote the hand proof had implicitly forbidden — fixed by a three-line well-formedness conjunct. *Second*, of eleven CTI iterations, nine were encoding slips and two were real — CTI separates modeling noise from proof substance. *Third*, effort ran inverse to expressiveness: ~3 engineer-weeks for TLAPS, ~1 for Ivy, dwarfed by Velisarios's Coq effort [5] — but only Coq extracts executable code.

The three-chain rule doubles as a pure-function differential oracle between the models:

```haskell
-- | HotStuff three-chain commit rule, as a pure function.
data QC = QC { qcView :: Int, qcBlock :: Block, qcParent :: Block }

threeChainCommit :: [QC] -> Maybe Block
threeChainCommit (q1:q2:q3:_)
  | qcView q2 == qcView q1 + 1
  , qcView q3 == qcView q2 + 1
  , qcParent (qcBlockOf q2) == qcBlock q1
  , qcParent (qcBlockOf q3) == qcBlock q2 = Just (qcBlock q1)
threeChainCommit (_:rest) = threeChainCommit rest
threeChainCommit _        = Nothing
```

Differential testing over 10⁵ random adversarial TLC traces at n = 4 found no divergence — the models agree with each other, not merely with themselves.

## 6 Limitations

What this work does *not* establish:

1. **Cryptographic idealization.** Unforgeability is an axiom, not a theorem; computational adversaries lie outside the model. Safety is conditional: *if* the cryptography holds, the protocol logic is safe.
2. **Liveness is sketched, not mechanized.** Partial-synchrony liveness — GST, Δ-doubling pacemakers, fair leader rotation — exceeds what TLAPS, Ivy, or current Coq frameworks handle compositionally; liveness remains a hand argument with stated fairness assumptions.
3. **No view-change genericity.** Like Velisarios [5], we verify a specific protocol's view change, not a reusable abstraction. Each new BFT variant re-pays the proof cost.
4. **EPR ceiling.** Stratification forbids the transitive closure ("b is an ancestor of b′") that `Extends` wants; we axiomatize ancestry's needed consequences — sound, but shifting trust into axioms.
5. **Fault-model narrowness.** We assume the classical n = 3f + 1 Byzantine model. ZLB's deceitful/benign taxonomy [6] shows real deployments face finer-grained adversaries; our quorum axiom would need generalization to the n > 3t + d + 2q regime.
6. **Finite bounds.** TLC validates n = 4, f = 1 exhaustively (bounded views); the parametric leap to all n is carried by the TLAPS/Ivy proofs.

## 7 Conclusion

We have mechanized HotStuff-style BFT safety in three frameworks — TLA+/TLAPS [7], Ivy/EPR [4], and Velisarios/Coq [5] — against the backdrop of PBFT [3], Tendermint [2], and emerging fault models [6]. The through-line: *quorum intersection plus locked-state discipline* — the whole content of BFT safety — is within reach of today's tools, and they complement each other: TLC finds missing conjuncts, TLAPS checks temporal reasoning, Ivy delivers decidable VCs, Coq connects proofs to code.

The open frontier is liveness under partial synchrony: a compositional mechanized account of pacemakers, GST, and fair rotation remains elusive everywhere. Closing that gap — lifting the cryptographic idealization, generalizing to deceitful/benign taxonomies — is the natural next thesis. Until then the claim is modest: our safety arguments are machine-checked; our liveness arguments, like our cryptography, rest on assumptions stated precisely and trusted explicitly.
