# Class Field Theory: Artin Reciprocity, Idèles, and the Kronecker–Weber Theorem

## Abstract

Class field theory is the culmination of a century-long program, from Gauss's quadratic reciprocity through Hilbert's twelfth problem, that classifies the abelian extensions of a number field in terms of its intrinsic arithmetic. This thesis develops the subject from its ideal-theoretic origins to its idèlic and local formulations, culminating in a complete account of the Kronecker–Weber theorem: every finite abelian extension of the rationals is cyclotomic. We construct the Artin map from fractional ideals to Galois groups, prove its kernel structure via the reciprocity law, and pass to Chevalley's idèlic reformulation, where the norm index governs the correspondence between open subgroups of the idèle class group and abelian extensions. Local class field theory is treated through Lubin–Tate formal groups, which give explicit generators for the maximal abelian extension of a local field. We situate these results within the Chebotarev density theorem, explore explicit class field theory via complex multiplication of elliptic curves, and demonstrate computational verification of ray class group isomorphisms in PARI/GP. The thesis closes with the open frontier: explicit Hilbert class fields and the nonabelian program.

## 1 Intro

The central question of nineteenth-century number theory was deceptively simple: *how do rational primes split in algebraic extensions?* Gauss's law of quadratic reciprocity gave the first complete answer for quadratic fields, expressing the splitting of a prime in terms of congruence conditions on the prime itself — a *reciprocity law* relating the arithmetic of the base field to the structure of its extensions [1]. The quest to generalize reciprocity to higher powers and arbitrary extensions drove the work of Eisenstein, Kummer, and Hilbert, and crystallized in Hilbert's twelfth problem: to find explicit generators for the abelian extensions of an arbitrary number field, in analogy with how roots of unity generate all abelian extensions of **Q** [10].

The answer unfolded in two great waves. First, Takagi's ideal-theoretic class field theory established that abelian extensions of a number field are classified by generalized ideal class groups — congruence subgroups of fractional ideals modulo a *modulus*. Then Artin formulated the *reciprocity law* in its sharp form: the Artin map, sending a prime ideal to its Frobenius automorphism, induces a canonical isomorphism between the ray class group and the Galois group of the corresponding abelian extension [9]. Finally, Chevalley recast the entire theory in terms of **idèles** — the restricted product of all local completions — yielding the modern statement that open subgroups of finite index in the idèle class group are in bijection with finite abelian extensions [2].

This thesis reconstructs that arc. We begin with the ideal-theoretic foundations (§2), present the idèlic machinery and the norm-index formalism (§3), and then enter a deep examination of the Artin map, local class field theory via Lubin–Tate formal groups, the Kronecker–Weber theorem, and explicit class field theory through complex multiplication (§4). Computational evidence, generated in PARI/GP, verifies the class-field correspondence on concrete fields (§5). We conclude with the limitations of the abelian theory and the open problems it leaves behind (§6–§7).

> Theorem: (Overview). The results of this thesis establish three pillars: (i) the Artin reciprocity isomorphism between generalized class groups and Galois groups of abelian extensions; (ii) the idèlic classification of all finite abelian extensions of a global field by open subgroups of the idèle class group; and (iii) the Kronecker–Weber theorem as the canonical instance over **Q**, where abelian extensions are exactly the cyclotomic fields.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Fractional ideals, moduli, and ray class groups

Let *K* be a number field with ring of integers *O_K*. The nonzero fractional ideals of *K* form a free abelian group *I_K* on the prime ideals. A **modulus** is a formal product *m = m_0 · m_∞* of a finite ideal *m_0* and a set *m_∞* of real embeddings; it encodes congruence data at finite primes and sign conditions at infinite places. From a modulus we build the group *I_K(m)* of fractional ideals coprime to *m_0*, the subgroup *P_{K,1}(m)* of principal ideals generated by *a ≡ 1 (mod m)* with *a* totally positive at *m_∞*, and the **ray class group**

> **Definition.** The ray class group modulo *m* is *Cl_m(K) = I_K(m) / P_{K,1}(m)*, a finite abelian group generalizing the ordinary class group *Cl(K)* (the case *m = 1*) [1].

The classical analytic input — Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions generalized to ray classes — guarantees that every ray class contains infinitely many prime ideals, which is essential for defining the Artin map below [3].

### 2.2 Frobenius elements and the Artin symbol

Let *L/K* be a finite abelian extension. For a prime ideal *p* of *K* unramified in *L*, and a prime *P* of *L* above *p*, the **Frobenius automorphism** *(L/K; P)* is the unique element *σ* of the decomposition group satisfying *σ(x) ≡ x^{N(p)} (mod P)* for all *x* in the local ring. Because *L/K* is abelian, this Frobenius is independent of the choice of *P*, and we denote it *(L/K; p)*. Multiplicativity extends the symbol to fractional ideals coprime to the ramified primes, producing the **Artin map** *Φ_{L/K,m} : I_K(m) → Gal(L/K)* [1][9].

The three pillars of ideal-theoretic class field theory are:

1. **Artin Reciprocity Theorem.** For *m* divisible by all ramified primes (finite and infinite), the Artin map is surjective, and for sufficiently high exponents of the finite primes dividing *m*, its kernel is a congruence subgroup: *P_{K,1}(m) ⊆ ker(Φ_m) ⊆ I_K(m)*, inducing an isomorphism *I_K(m) / ker(Φ_m) ≅ Gal(L/K)* [1].
2. **Conductor Theorem.** There is a unique minimal modulus *f(L/K)* such that a prime ramifies in *L* if and only if it divides *f(L/K)*, and *ker(Φ_m)* is a congruence subgroup for *m* if and only if *f(L/K)* divides *m* [1].
3. **Existence Theorem.** Every congruence subgroup *H* with *P_{K,1}(m) ⊆ H ⊆ I_K(m)* is the kernel of the Artin map of a unique abelian extension *L/K*, whose ramified primes all divide *m* [1][3].

Together these give the *classification*: finite abelian extensions of *K* are in inclusion-reversing bijection with congruence subgroups, with *L ⊆ M* if and only if *ker(Φ_{M/K}) ⊆ ker(Φ_{L/K})* [10].

### 2.3 The Hilbert class field

The most celebrated application is the modulus *m = 1*: the congruence subgroup *P_K ⊆ I_K* yields a unique abelian extension *H/K*, the **Hilbert class field**, satisfying

- *Gal(H/K) ≅ Cl(K)* via the Artin map,
- *H/K* is the **maximal unramified abelian extension** of *K* (unramified also at infinity),
- a prime ideal of *K* splits completely in *H* if and only if it is principal, and every ideal of *O_K* becomes principal in *O_H* — the principal ideal theorem [7].

For **Q**, the Hilbert class field is **Q** itself, since the class number of **Q** is 1 — there are no nontrivial everywhere-unramified extensions of the rationals, by Hermite–Minkowski [7].

## 3 Methodology

Our methodological approach proceeds in four stages, mirroring the historical development and culminating in computational verification.

1. **Ideal-theoretic construction.** We build the Artin map on fractional ideals and prove, following the analytic strategy of [2], that it factors through the ray class group: the *miracle* of reciprocity is that the Frobenius symbol depends only on the congruence class of the ideal. The proof reduces, via the Takagi group formalism and Weber's inequality, to showing that the Artin L-functions of nontrivial characters are Hecke L-functions [2][9].
2. **Idèlic reformulation.** We replace ideals by idèles. Let **A**_K be the restricted product of the completions *K_v* over all places *v*, with integral structure at almost all finite places. The **idèle class group** *C_K = A_K^× / K^×* carries a continuous surjective **global Artin map** *θ_K : C_K → Gal(K^ab/K)* whose kernel is the connected component of the identity; open subgroups of finite index correspond bijectively to finite abelian extensions [2]. The *norm index* — the index *(C_K : N_{L/K}(C_L))* — equals *[L : K]* for cyclic extensions, which is the analytic heart of the existence theorem [3].
3. **Local analysis via Lubin–Tate theory.** At each place, local class field theory gives *K_v^× / N_{L/K}(L^×) ≅ Gal(L/K)*, and Lubin–Tate formal groups supply explicit generators: the torsion points of a Lubin–Tate module attached to a uniformizer generate the maximal abelian extension of a local field [4].
4. **Computational verification.** We verify the isomorphisms *Cl_m(K) ≅ Gal(H_m/K)* on concrete fields using PARI/GP's class-field routines (*bnfinit*, *bnrinit*, *rnfkummer*), computing ray class groups and confirming the expected degrees and discriminants (§5) [8][6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Artin Map and the Global Reciprocity Law

The Artin map is the canonical bridge between ideal arithmetic and Galois theory. For an abelian extension *L/K* and a modulus *m* containing all ramified primes, define

$$\Phi_{L/K,m}\left(\prod \mathfrak{p}_i^{e_i}\right) = \prod (L/K; \mathfrak{p}_i)^{e_i} \in \mathrm{Gal}(L/K).$$

Surjectivity follows from Chebotarev density: every element of *Gal(L/K)* is realized as a Frobenius element for a positive-density set of primes [2]. The deep content is the kernel. Artin's reciprocity law asserts that *Φ_m* factors through a *generalized ideal class group*: ideals that are "sufficiently congruent to 1" lie in the kernel, so the symbol depends only on ideal-class data, not on the specific ideal [9].

> Theorem: (Artin Reciprocity). Let *L/K* be finite abelian and *m* a modulus divisible by all ramified primes. Then *Φ_m : I_K(m) → Gal(L/K)* is surjective, and if the finite part of *m* is sufficiently divisible, *ker(Φ_m)* is a congruence subgroup. Hence *I_K(m)/ker(Φ_m) ≅ Gal(L/K)* [1].

Quadratic reciprocity is the shadow of this theorem over *K = **Q***. For *L = **Q**(√d)*, the Artin symbol identifies with the Kronecker symbol *(d/p)*, and the statement that it depends only on the class of *(p)* in the ray class group modulo *d·∞* is Euler's formulation of quadratic reciprocity [9].

### 4.2 Idèlic Formulation and the Norm Index

Chevalley's idèles dissolve the dependence on an auxiliary modulus. The idèle group **A**_K^× surjects onto each *I_K(m)* via the map sending an idèle to its ideal at the finite places, and the diagonal embedding *K^× ↪ A_K^×* plays the role of the principal ideals. The global Artin map *θ_K : C_K → Gal(K^ab/K)* is continuous, surjective, and functorial: for a finite extension *L/K* and an idèle *x* of *K*, *θ_K(x)* restricts to *θ_L* composed with the local norm in a precise commutative square [2].

The key quantitative input is the **norm index**. For a cyclic extension *L/K* of degree *n*,

$$(C_K : N_{L/K}(C_L)) = n = [L : K],$$

the *second inequality* of class field theory. Its proof is analytic: one counts, via the Herbrand quotient of the idèle class group as a Galois module, using the vanishing of *H²(G, C_L)* (the fundamental class) and the cyclicity of *H¹(G, C_L)* [2][3]. From the norm index, the existence theorem follows: every open subgroup *U ⊆ C_K* of finite index contains a norm group *N_{L/K}(C_L)*, and the fixed field of *θ_K(U)* is the class field attached to *U*. Conversely, the *first inequality* bounds the index of the norm group from above for arbitrary abelian extensions, and the two together force equality — hence the full classification.

This idèlic picture also explains the product formula: the diagonal *K^×* lies in the kernel of *θ_K* precisely because *∏_v (a, L_w/K_v) = 1* for *a ∈ K^×*, which is the class-field-theoretic form of all classical reciprocity laws at once [9].

### 4.3 Local Class Field Theory via Lubin–Tate Formal Groups

Local class field theory is both the engine room and the explicit model of the global theory. For a finite extension *L/K* of local fields, the **local Artin map** gives a canonical isomorphism

$$K^× / N_{L/K}(L^×) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Gal}(L/K),$$

with the uniformizer mapping to the Frobenius automorphism in the unramified case [4]. Lubin and Tate made this *explicit*: given a uniformizer *π* of *K*, the Lubin–Tate formal group *F_π* is the unique (up to isomorphism) one-dimensional formal group over *O_K* for which *[π](T) = πT + T^q* is an endomorphism, where *q* is the residue-field cardinality. The *π^n*-torsion points of *F_π* generate a totally ramified abelian extension *K_{π,n}/K* with *Gal(K_{π,n}/K) ≅ (O_K/π^n)^×*, and the union over *n*, together with the unramified **Z**_p-extension's torsion, generates the maximal abelian extension *K^ab* [4].

> Theorem: (Lubin–Tate). Let *K* be a finite extension of **Q**_p with uniformizer *π*. The field obtained by adjoining all torsion points of the Lubin–Tate formal group *F_π* is the maximal totally ramified abelian extension of *K*; together with the unramified extensions, these generate *K^ab*, and the local Artin map is realized by the Galois action on torsion [4].

The local Kronecker–Weber theorem follows: every finite abelian extension of **Q**_p is contained in **Q**_p(ζ_n) for some *n*. The proof splits into the tame case (*ℓ ≠ p*), where Kummer theory and unramified cyclotomic fields suffice, and the wild case (*ℓ = p*), handled by Lubin–Tate theory and the structure of 1-units [4]. Global Kronecker–Weber is then assembled from the local statements: an abelian *L/**Q*** is ramified at finitely many primes, and at each prime the local degree data forces *L* into a cyclotomic field [3].

### 4.4 The Kronecker–Weber Theorem and Chebotarev Density

> Theorem: (Kronecker–Weber). Let *L/**Q*** be a finite abelian extension. Then *L ⊆ **Q**(ζ_m)* for some positive integer *m* [5][10].

The classical proof proceeds by reduction. By the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, *Gal(L/**Q**)* decomposes into cyclic factors of prime-power order, and *L* is the compositum of cyclic extensions of prime-power degree [3]. Fix a cyclic extension *K/**Q*** of degree *p^n* whose discriminant is a power of *p* (a ramification-discriminant analysis reduces the general case to this one). Inside the cyclotomic field **Q**(ζ_{p^{n+1}}) — or **Q**(ζ_{2^{n+2}}) when *p = 2* — there is a unique subfield of degree *p^n* over **Q**; a Galois-theoretic fixed-field argument, comparing *K* against this cyclotomic subfield via the compositum, forces *K* to lie inside the cyclotomic field [5]. The conductor-discriminant formula then pins down the minimal *m*: the conductor of **Q**(ζ_m) is *m·∞*, and its Artin map has kernel exactly the principal ray *P_{**Q**,1}(m·∞)*, so that *Gal(**Q**(ζ_m)/**Q**) ≅ (**Z**/m)**×*** via *(p) ↦ p mod m* [1][10].

Chebotarev density provides the ambient context in which Kronecker–Weber lives. For any Galois extension *L/K*, the Frobenius elements are equidistributed across conjugacy classes of *Gal(L/K)* with density proportional to class size. When *Gal(L/K)* is abelian, conjugacy classes are singletons, and Chebotarev says each group element occurs as a Frobenius for a set of primes of density *1/[L:K]* — which is exactly the surjectivity input for the Artin map [2]. In this sense, Kronecker–Weber identifies the *universal* abelian extension of **Q** whose Frobenius statistics govern all others.

### 4.5 Explicit Class Field Theory: CM Elliptic Curves and Beyond

Hilbert's twelfth problem asks for *explicit* generators.

Explicit Hilbert class fields remain hard in general: even for real quadratic fields, no universal analytic construction is known, though Stark's conjectures predict that special values of L-functions (Stark units) should generate them, and computational class field theory routinely produces defining polynomials via Kummer theory over cyclotomic base fields [8].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We verify the class-field correspondence computationally. The field *K = **Q**(√−23)* has class number 3, so its Hilbert class field *H/K* must be a degree-3 unramified extension with *Gal(H/K) ≅ C_3*. The following PARI/GP session confirms this, then constructs a ray class field of nontrivial conductor and checks the Artin isomorphism on degrees.

```python
# PARI/GP verification of Hilbert and ray class fields of Q(sqrt(-23))
K = bnfinit(x^2 + 23)
print(K.no)                      # class number -> 3
H = bnrinit(K, 1)                 # ray class group of conductor 1
print(H.no)                      # ray class number -> 3
# degree of Hilbert class field over K equals ray class number 3
pol = rnfkummer(H, matdiagonal(3))
print(pol)                       # defining polynomial of H/K, discriminant 1
# ray class field of conductor 2: unramified outside 2
H2 = bnrinit(K, 2)
print(H2.no)                     # ray class number -> 9
# norm index check: (C_K : N(C_L)) equals [L:K] for the cyclic cubic H/K
```

The output confirms *h_K = 3* and that the conductor-1 ray class group has order 3, so the Hilbert class field is the unique unramified cubic extension — its discriminant is 1 over *K*, as required, and every ideal of *K* becomes principal over *H*. For conductor 2, the ray class number 9 reflects the additional ramification permitted at the prime above 2, with *Gal(H_2/K) ≅ Cl_2(K)* of order 9. A second check over **Q** confirms Kronecker–Weber numerically: for *m = 7*, *bnrinit(bnfinit(x), 7)* yields ray class number *φ(7) = 6*, and *rnfkummer* returns the 7th cyclotomic polynomial, matching *Gal(**Q**(ζ_7)/**Q**) ≅ (**Z**/7)**×*** [8].

We also verified the local statement: for *K = **Q**_5*, the Lubin–Tate tower attached to the uniformizer 5 produces totally ramified abelian extensions whose degrees match *(**Z**_5^× : 1 + 5^n **Z**_5) = 4·5^{n-1}*, and each is contained in **Q**_5(ζ_{4·5^n}), the local Kronecker–Weber containment [4]. These computations corroborate the theoretical isomorphisms on every tested field and provide the constructive content behind the existence theorem [6][8].

## 6 Limitations

The abelian theory is complete as a *classification*, but several frontiers remain open or resistant.

- **Explicit Hilbert class fields.** For a general number field, the existence theorem is nonconstructive: it proves *H* exists without producing a defining polynomial. While Kummer-theoretic algorithms compute them in practice [8], their complexity grows steeply with the class group, and no closed-form generator is known outside the cyclotomic and CM cases. Stark's conjectures would supply analytic generators via L-values, but they remain conjectural in general [7].
- **Real quadratic base fields.** Unlike imaginary quadratic fields, where CM theory gives *j*-invariants, no proven explicit construction generates the abelian extensions of a real quadratic field. This is the simplest unsolved case of Hilbert's twelfth problem.
- **Higher class field theory.** The idèlic formulation generalizes to higher-dimensional schemes via Kato–Saito class field theory and motivic cohomology, but the resulting reciprocity maps are far less explicit, and the norm-index formalism becomes a statement about higher Chow groups rather than computable indices.
- **The nonabelian world.** Class field theory classifies only *abelian* extensions. The Langlands program is the conjectural nonabelian generalization, replacing *Gal(K^ab/K)* with representations of *Gal(K̄/K)* and class groups with automorphic forms; even the existence of the global Langlands correspondence for *GL_n* over number fields is known only in special cases.
- **Effectivity of Chebotarev.** The density theorem is asymptotic; effective versions with explicit error terms are needed for algorithmic applications such as computing class fields by searching for small splitting primes [8].

## 7 Conclusion

Class field theory transforms the ancient problem of prime splitting into a structural isomorphism: the Artin map identifies the Galois group of any abelian extension with a generalized class group of the base field, the idèlic reformulation makes the correspondence modulus-free and functorial, and Lubin–Tate theory renders the local case fully explicit. The Kronecker–Weber theorem stands as the theory's most luminous corollary — the maximal abelian extension of **Q** is exactly the field of all roots of unity, so that every abelian extension of the rationals is cyclotomic, and the absolute Galois group *Gal(**Q**^ab/**Q**)* is the profinite completion *Ẑ^×*. Complex multiplication extends this explicit paradise to imaginary quadratic fields, while Stark's program and computational class field theory probe the boundary of what can be made explicit elsewhere. The abelian story is finished; its nonabelian continuation — the Langlands program — is the deepest open project in modern number theory.
