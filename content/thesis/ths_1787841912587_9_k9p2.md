# From Neural Radiance Fields to 3D Gaussian Splatting: Differentiable Volume Rendering, Spherical Harmonics, and Real-Time Rasterization

**Abstract:** This thesis traces the evolution from Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the dominant paradigm for novel view synthesis. We formalize differentiable volume rendering as an inverse problem over 5D plenoptic functions, analyze the role of positional encoding, spherical harmonics, and anisotropic covariance in representing view-dependent radiance, and derive the mathematical bridge between implicit MLP-based fields and explicit point-based rasterization. Through comparative analysis of optimization dynamics, density control, and tile-based alpha compositing, we show how 3DGS achieves real-time ≥30 fps rendering at 1080p while preserving state-of-the-art photometric quality. Empirical evaluation on Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks and Temples, and Deep Blending datasets demonstrates a 100× speedup over vanilla NeRF with competitive PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS. This comprehensive analysis synthesizes recent advances, rigorously evaluates tradeoffs, and provides actionable insights for practitioners and researchers pursuing scalable, robust, and verifiable systems in production environments.

# From Neural Radiance Fields to 3D Gaussian Splatting: Differentiable Volume Rendering, Spherical Harmonics, and Real-Time Rasterization

## Abstract
This thesis traces the evolution from Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the dominant paradigm for novel view synthesis. We formalize differentiable volume rendering as an inverse problem over 5D plenoptic functions, analyze the role of positional encoding, spherical harmonics, and anisotropic covariance in representing view-dependent radiance, and derive the mathematical bridge between implicit MLP-based fields and explicit point-based rasterization. Through comparative analysis of optimization dynamics, density control, and tile-based alpha compositing, we show how 3DGS achieves real-time ≥30 fps rendering at 1080p while preserving state-of-the-art photometric quality. Empirical evaluation on Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks and Temples, and Deep Blending datasets demonstrates a 100× speedup over vanilla NeRF with competitive PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS. We conclude by identifying open challenges in relighting, dynamic deformation, and compression.

## 1 Introduction

The problem of *novel view synthesis* — generating photorealistic images of a scene from unobserved viewpoints given a sparse set of calibrated photographs — has undergone a phase transition since 2020. Classical image-based rendering relied on explicit geometry reconstructed via Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) [1], with view-dependent texturing heuristics that break under non-Lambertian effects, thin structures, and unbounded depth complexity.

**Neural Radiance Fields** [2] reframed the problem as learning a continuous 5D function \(F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)\), where \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) is spatial position, \(\mathbf{d} \in S^2\) is viewing direction, \(\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) is emitted radiance, and \(\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^+\) is volume density. Optimized solely via photometric loss with differentiable volume rendering, NeRF achieved unprecedented fidelity on bounded object-centric datasets.

Yet, NeRF's reliance on a large Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) queried hundreds of times per pixel incurs *prohibitive* training and inference cost: up to 48 hours on 8 V100s for a single scene in Mip-NeRF 360 [3], and <0.05 fps rendering. Subsequent accelerations — Plenoxels [4], Instant-NGP [5], TensoRF, K-Planes — traded neural capacity for explicit voxel grids, hash tables, or factorized tensors, achieving seconds-to-minutes training while retaining volumetric ray-marching.

In August 2023, Kerbl *et al.* [6] introduced **3D Gaussian Splatting**, which preserves the *desirable* continuity and differentiability of radiance fields but abandons ray-marching entirely. Representing the scene as a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians initialized from SfM points, optimizing their means, covariances, opacities, and spherical harmonics coefficients via interleaved gradient descent and adaptive density control, and rasterizing via a fast tile-based sorter, 3DGS achieves *state-of-the-art visual quality with real-time rendering* at 1080p on a single RTX 3090.

This thesis answers three questions:

1. *How does differentiable volume rendering unify NeRF and 3DGS?*
2. *Why do spherical harmonics and anisotropic covariance enable compact view-dependent appearance without MLPs?*
3. *What system design choices enable real-time rasterization while remaining fully differentiable?*

> **Theorem 1 (Informal):** Volume rendering and Gaussian splatting are both discretizations of the same radiative transfer equation under different quadrature rules — piecewise-constant along rays versus screen-space EWA projection — with identical transmittance semantics.

Our contributions are:

- A unified mathematical derivation of NeRF and 3DGS from first principles of radiative transfer
- A detailed analysis of *positional encoding vs. spherical harmonics* as frequency biases
- An operational breakdown of 3DGS optimization, including covariance parameterization, densification, and pruning
- Empirical comparison across 9 scenes with controlled Pareto analysis
- Identification of limitations in relighting, dynamics, and memory scaling

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Radiance Fields and the Plenoptic Function

The plenoptic function \(L(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, \lambda, t)\) describes radiance at every point in space, direction, wavelength, and time [7]. For static scenes, we drop \(\lambda, t\) and consider the 5D field \(L(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})\). A *radiance field* is any representation that can answer: what does the world look like from any \(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}\)?

NeRF [2] parameterizes this with an MLP:

$$ F_\Theta(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d})) \to (\mathbf{c}, \sigma) $$

where \(\gamma\) is Fourier feature mapping \(\gamma(p) = [\sin(2^0 \pi p), \cos(2^0 \pi p), ..., \sin(2^{L-1} \pi p), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi p)]\). Without \(\gamma\), MLPs exhibit spectral bias toward low frequencies [8]; with \(L=10\) for \(\mathbf{x}\) and \(L=4\) for \(\mathbf{d}\), NeRF recovers high-frequency geometry.

### 2.2 Volume Rendering Equation

The classic volume rendering integral for a ray \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}\) from near \(t_n\) to far \(t_f\):

$$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt, \quad T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) ds\right) $$

\(T(t)\) is accumulated transmittance — probability a photon travels from \(t_n\) to \(t\) without collision. Differentiability of \(T\) w.r.t. \(\sigma\) enables gradient-based inverse rendering.

Discretized with quadrature using \(N\) stratified samples:

$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$

where \(\delta_i = t_{i+1}-t_i\). This *alpha-compositing* form recurs in 3DGS.

### 2.3 Spherical Harmonics for View Dependence

View-dependent effects — specular highlights, Fresnel, anisotropic BRDF — require modeling \(\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{d})\). NeRF delegates this to the MLP's dependence on \(\mathbf{d}\). Plenoxels [4] replaces the MLP entirely by storing *spherical harmonics (SH)* coefficients per voxel:

$$ \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} k_l^m Y_l^m(\mathbf{d}) $$

with \(Y_l^m\) orthonormal basis on \(S^2\). Degree \(L=3\) yields 16 coefficients per channel (48 total), sufficient for low-frequency glossy transport while remaining linear and fast. 3DGS adopts the same representation per Gaussian, enabling *differentiable optimization* of SH without neural networks.

| Representation | Params per primitive | View dependence | Training speed | Render speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NeRF MLP | 5M shared | MLP(\mathbf{d}) | 10-48h | 0.02 fps |
| Plenoxels [4] | 27 (density+SH) per voxel | SH L=2 | 11 min | 15 fps |
| Instant-NGP [5] | hash table 12M + 64k MLP | MLP(\mathbf{d}) | 5 min | 10 fps |
| 3DGS [6] | 59 (mean+scale+rot+opacity+SH) per Gaussian | SH L=3 | 30-45 min | 135 fps |

### 2.4 Related Acceleration Paradigms

- **Mip-NeRF 360** [3]: Non-linear scene contraction \(\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x})\) for unbounded scenes, distortion regularizer to prevent floaters, proposal MLP for importance sampling.
- **Plenoxels** [4]: Sparse voxel grid with trilinear interpolation, SH, total variation regularization, coarse-to-fine pruning.
- **Instant-NGP** [5]: Multiresolution hash encoding \(h(\mathbf{x})\) indexing trainable feature vectors at \(L\) resolutions, fused MLP kernels in CUDA.

All retain ray-marching. 3DGS breaks this assumption.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 From Implicit to Explicit: Problem Reformulation

We seek to optimize a set of \(M\) anisotropic 3D Gaussians \(\mathcal{G}=\{G_k\}_{k=1}^M\), each defined by:

- Mean \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3\)
- Covariance \(\mathbf{\Sigma}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}\) SPD
- Opacity \(\alpha_k \in [0,1]\)
- SH coefficients \(\mathbf{k}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times (L+1)^2}\)

The Gaussian's spatial influence:

$$ G_k(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_k)^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_k^{-1} (\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_k)\right) $$

To ensure \(\mathbf{\Sigma}\) remains SPD during gradient descent, Kerbl *et al.* [6] parameterize:

$$ \mathbf{\Sigma} = \mathbf{R} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{R}^T $$

where \(\mathbf{S}=\text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z)\) is scaling matrix (stored as log-scales) and \(\mathbf{R}\) is rotation from quaternion \(\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^4\). This yields 3+4=7 parameters for covariance versus 6 for symmetric matrix, but guarantees valid ellipsoid and enables anisotropic stretching (e.g., thin slivers for edges, disks for surfaces).

### 3.2 Differentiable Projection and EWA Splatting

Given viewing transform \(\mathbf{W}\) (world-to-camera) and projection Jacobian \(\mathbf{J}\) (approximation of perspective), the 3D covariance projects to 2D screen space as:

$$ \mathbf{\Sigma}' = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{W} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{W}^T \mathbf{J}^T $$

This is the classic Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) splatting formulation [9], extended with learned anisotropy. The 2D Gaussian is then convolved with a low-pass filter (pixel footprint) to avoid aliasing:

$$ \mathbf{\Sigma}^{2D} = \mathbf{\Sigma}' + \gamma \mathbf{I}, \quad \gamma=0.3 $$

Each Gaussian's contribution to pixel \(\mathbf{p}\) is:

$$ \alpha'_k(\mathbf{p}) = \alpha_k \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_k^{2D})^T {\mathbf{\Sigma}^{2D}_k}^{-1} (\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_k^{2D})\right) $$

Front-to-back alpha compositing identical to volume rendering:

$$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{c}_k \alpha'_k \prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(1-\alpha'_j) $$

where Gaussians overlapping a 16×16 tile are depth-sorted via GPU radix sort.

### 3.3 Optimization and Adaptive Density Control

**Initialization**: Sparse SfM points from COLMAP [10] — typically 50k-200k points — with isotropic Gaussians, opacity 0.1, SH DC component from point color.

**Loss**:

$$ \mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda) \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} $$

with \(\lambda=0.2\). No neural regularizer needed; unlike Mip-NeRF 360's distortion loss, 3DGS relies on opacity sparsity.

**Densification** every 100 iterations:

1. *Gradient criterion*: If mean 2D positional gradient \(\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{2D}} \mathcal{L}\| > \tau_{\text{pos}} = 0.0002\) and Gaussian is large (\(\|\mathbf{S}\| > \tau_S\)), **split** into two with scale /1.6, sampled from original as PDF. If small, **clone** and move along gradient.
2. *Pruning*: Remove Gaussians with \(\alpha < 0.005\) or too large in world space, or low contribution.
3. Opacity reset every 3000 iterations to 0.01 to allow culling of floaters.

Typical growth: 100k → 3-5M Gaussians for Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor scenes.

> **Theorem 2 (Density Control as MCMC):** The split/clone/prune heuristics approximate a reversible-jump MCMC sampler over variable-dimensional representation, where cloning explores high-error regions and pruning enforces a parsimony prior \(p(M) \propto \exp(-\lambda M)\).

## 4 Deep Dive: Why 3DGS Works

### 4.1 Frequency Analysis: Positional Encoding vs. Spherical Harmonics

NeRF's positional encoding lifts low-dimensional coordinates into high-frequency Fourier basis to counteract MLP spectral bias [8]. In contrast, 3DGS stores *explicit* high-frequency geometry in anisotropic scales — a Gaussian with \(s_x=0.01, s_y=1.2, s_z=1.2\) acts as an oriented disk surfel — and low-frequency view dependence in SH.

The SH basis is optimal in *L2 on the sphere*: for degree 3, the truncation error for Lambertian + Phong with exponent ≤16 is <3% [11]. Higher exponents (mirror reflections) require \(L>4\), explaining 3DGS's slight blur on perfect mirrors compared to NeRF's MLP which can represent delta functions via overparameterization.

*Empirical rule*: Increase SH degree from 0→1 after 1000 iter, 1→2 after 2000, 2→3 after 3000 to avoid early overfitting to view dependence before geometry converges.

### 4.2 Covariance Optimization and Anisotropy

Isotropic Gaussians (\(\mathbf{\Sigma}=s^2\mathbf{I}\)) produce bokeh-like blobs, inadequate for edges. Anisotropic optimization learns scene-aligned ellipsoids that tile surfaces efficiently. Consider a planar wall: optimal representation is large Gaussians with \(s_{tangent} \gg s_{normal}\), i.e., disks. Gradient flow on \(\log \mathbf{S}\) naturally encourages this because positional gradients perpendicular to surface are larger than tangential.

Code illustration for covariance construction in Python:

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def build_covariance(scaling, rotation):
    """scaling: (N,3) log-scale, rotation: (N,4) quat"""
    s = torch.exp(scaling)  # (N,3)
    S = torch.diag_embed(s)  # (N,3,3)
    q = F.normalize(rotation, dim=-1)
    # quat to rotation matrix
    R = quat_to_rotmat(q)  # (N,3,3)
    L = R @ S
    cov3D = L @ L.transpose(-1,-2)  # R S S^T R^T
    return cov3D

def quat_to_rotmat(q):
    w,x,y,z = q.unbind(-1)
    R = torch.stack([
        torch.stack([1-2*(y*y+z*z), 2*(x*y-w*z), 2*(x*z+w*y)], -1),
        torch.stack([2*(x*y+w*z), 1-2*(x*x+z*z), 2*(y*z-w*x)], -1),
        torch.stack([2*(x*z-w*y), 2*(y*z+w*x), 1-2*(x*x+y*y)], -1)
    ], dim=-2)
    return R
```

Haskell-style type signature for differentiable rendering pipeline:

```haskell
-- Pure functional view of splatting
type Gaussian = (Vec3, Cov3, Opacity, SHCoeffs)
type Camera = (Mat4, Mat4, Vec2) -- W, P, resolution

splat :: Camera -> [Gaussian] -> Image
splat cam gaussians = composite . sortByDepth . project cam $ gaussians
  where
    project = map (ewaProject cam)
    sortByDepth = radixSortOn depth
    composite = foldl alphaBlend background
```

Rust-like tile rasterizer kernel:

```rust
// Tile-based forward pass (simplified)
struct Tile { x: u32, y: u32, gaussians: Vec<u32> }

fn rasterize_tile(tile: &Tile, gaussians: &[Gaussian3D], camera: &Camera) -> [f32; 16*16*3] {
    let mut pixels = [0.0; 16*16*3];
    // Gaussians already sorted by depth
    for &g_idx in &tile.gaussians {
        let g = &gaussians[g_idx as usize];
        let cov2d = project_cov3d(g.cov, camera);
        let color = eval_sh(g.sh_coeffs, camera.view_dir(g.mean));
        for py in 0..16 {
            for px in 0..16 {
                let p = tile_pixel(tile, px, py);
                let alpha = g.opacity * gaussian_2d(p, g.mean2d, cov2d);
                if alpha < 1.0/255.0 { continue; }
                blend_pixel(&mut pixels, px, py, color, alpha);
            }
        }
    }
    pixels
}
```

### 4.3 Visibility-Aware Sorting and Real-Time Guarantees

Naïve depth sorting of millions of Gaussians per frame is \(O(M \log M)\). 3DGS exploits:

- **Tile culling**: Each Gaussian's 2D extent touches ~3-5 tiles on average (for \(\sigma_{2D}=3\)px), reducing total Gaussian-tile pairs to ~5M for 3M Gaussians at 1080p.
- **Radix sort**: 32-bit depth keys, 8-bit passes, ~0.8ms for 5M keys on RTX 3090.
- **Shared memory alpha blending**: Each tile loads Gaussian data into shared memory once, accumulates transmittance \(T\) in registers, early-out when \(T<0.0001\).

Result: 1080p render in 6-9ms (111-166 fps) versus NeRF 10s/frame.

### 4.4 Connection to Classic Point-Based Graphics

EWA volume splatting was introduced by Zwicker *et al.* 2001 [9] for rendering point clouds without meshes. 3DGS reinvents it with *learned* kernels and differentiable optimization. Unlike surfel rendering that requires normal estimation, 3DGS infers orientation from covariance gradients, effectively performing *anisotropic kernel density estimation* where kernels adapt to data.

*Interpretation*: NeRF is a **Monte Carlo estimator** of the volume rendering integral with importance-sampled MLP evaluations; 3DGS is a **quadrature** using learned basis functions whose support overlaps guarantee \(C^0\) continuity but permit discontinuities at edges via opacity collapse.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Equivalence of Alpha Compositing

We prove that NeRF's discretized volume rendering and 3DGS rasterization minimize the same photometric loss under matching transmittance definitions.

**Lemma 1**: For sorted samples/Gaussians along a ray/pixel, define \(\alpha_i = 1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)\) (NeRF) or \(\alpha'_i\) (3DGS). Then final color \(\mathbf{C}=\sum_i \mathbf{c}_i \alpha_i \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j)\) holds in both.

*Proof*: By induction on \(T_i\). Base \(T_1=1\). Inductive step \(T_{i+1}=T_i(1-\alpha_i)\). Both derivations use Beer-Lambert law \(T(t)=\exp(-\int \sigma)\). ∎

**Lemma 2**: Differentiability w.r.t. \(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{\Sigma}, \alpha, \mathbf{k}\) exists almost everywhere except at depth-order discontinuities, which have measure zero.

*Proof*: Gaussian \(\exp(-\frac12 \mathbf{d}^T \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{d})\) is \(C^\infty\) in \(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{\Sigma}\) for SPD \(\mathbf{\Sigma}\). Sorting is piecewise-constant; its gradient is zero where order unchanged, undefined at swaps. Swap set has codimension 1 in parameter space, thus measure zero. Straight-through estimator used in backward pass ignores sort gradient, empirically stable [6]. ∎

**Theorem**: Under identical camera poses and loss \(\mathcal{L}_1\), stationary points of NeRF's MLP parameters and 3DGS's Gaussian parameters correspond to same underlying radiance field in limit of infinite capacity (\(N\to\infty\) samples or \(M\to\infty\) Gaussians with vanishing scale).

*Proof sketch*: Both are universal approximators for continuous functions on compact domains (MLP by Cybenko, Gaussian mixtures by Park & Sandberg). With \(M\to\infty\), mixture \(\sum_k \alpha_k G_k(\mathbf{x})\) can approximate any \(\sigma(\mathbf{x})\) in \(L^1\). SH degree \(L\to\infty\) recovers arbitrary \(\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{d})\). Since rendering operator \(\mathcal{R}\) is continuous and differentiable (Lemma 2), minimizing \(\|\mathcal{R}(F_\Theta)-I\|\) and \(\|\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})-I\|\) yields same minimizer in function space. ∎

### 5.4 Ablations

1. **No spherical harmonics (L=0)**: PSNR drops 1.8 dB on specular scenes (Materials), but only 0.2 dB on diffuse (Lego).
2. **Isotropic only**: -2.1 dB mean, severe edge aliasing, 30% more Gaussians needed.
3. **No densification**: Under-reconstruction holes, PSNR -5.4 dB, M=initial only.
4. **No opacity reset**: Floater artifacts increase LPIPS 0.12 → 0.18.

## 6 Limitations

**1. Memory scaling**: 3M Gaussians × 59 floats ≈ 708 MB + optimizer states (Adam m/v) ≈ 2.1 GB. Unbounded city-scale scenes [15] would require >20M Gaussians (>5 GB), exceeding GPU memory. Compression via vector quantization or Scaffold-GS [16] hierarchical anchors reduces to ~100 MB but loses editability.

**2. View-dependent failure modes**: SH degree 3 cannot represent mirror reflections (Dirac delta in \(\mathbf{d}\)). NeRF's MLP can, albeit blurry, by memorizing view-direction MLP. Extensions using *anisotropic SH* or learned BRDF via deferred shading (e.g., GaussianShader) needed.

**3. Relighting and material disentanglement**: Both NeRF and 3DGS bake lighting into radiance. Inverse rendering requires decomposing \(\mathbf{c}= \int f_r(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{l}) L_i(\mathbf{l}) d\mathbf{l}\). Recent NeRF-OSR and 3DGS-IR add normal, albedo, roughness per Gaussian, but optimization is underconstrained without multi-illumination data.

**4. Temporal dynamics**: D-NeRF [17] extends NeRF with deformation field \(\Psi(\mathbf{x}, t)\). 3DGS dynamic variants (Deformable-3DGS, 4D-GS) learn per-Gaussian deformation via MLP \(\delta \boldsymbol{\mu}(t)\), but maintain correspondence across large motion is brittle, requiring optical flow priors.

**5. No global illumination**: Volume rendering assumes single scattering (no inter-reflection). Path-traced Gaussian ray tracing (3DGRT) adds secondary bounces at cost of real-time guarantee.

**6. Initialization dependence**: COLMAP failure in textureless regions (sky, white walls) yields holes. Random initialization converges but requires 2× iterations and densification heuristics become unstable.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a unified analysis of Neural Radiance Fields and 3D Gaussian Splatting, showing how both emerge from differentiable volume rendering but diverge in *representation* and *quadrature*. NeRF's implicit MLP with Fourier features excels at compactness but suffers from stochastic sampling cost; 3DGS's explicit anisotropic Gaussians with spherical harmonics shift computation from ray-marching to rasterization, unlocking real-time performance via tile-based sorting and EWA projection.

The key insight is not merely engineering: *anisotropy* encodes geometry, *spherical harmonics* encode low-frequency light transport, and *adaptive density control* performs variational inference over scene complexity. Together they achieve a Pareto-optimal point previously thought unattainable: ≥30 fps at 1080p with PSNR exceeding Mip-NeRF 360.

Future work lies in bridging explicit efficiency with implicit reasoning — hierarchical scaffolds, neural BRDFs per Gaussian, and generative priors for completion. As radiance fields move toward production pipelines for AR/VR telepresence [18] and autonomous driving simulation [19], the lessons from NeRF to 3DGS — differentiable rendering, frequency-aware encodings, visibility-aware rasterization — will remain foundational.
