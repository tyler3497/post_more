# From Persistence Diagrams to Differentiable Features: Stable Vectorizations of Persistent Homology for Machine Learning

## Abstract

Persistent homology converts point clouds, images, and graphs into multiscale topological signatures — persistence diagrams and barcodes — recording the birth and death of homological features across a filtration. Yet diagram space is a nonlinear metric space with no native inner product, so standard learning machinery cannot ingest it directly. This thesis builds the mathematical bridge from topological data analysis to statistical learning: the filtration zoo (Vietoris–Rips, Čech, alpha, cubical) and the nerve-theoretic guarantees relating them; the bottleneck and Wasserstein stability theorems bounding diagram perturbations by input perturbations; the three canonical vectorization families — persistence landscapes as Banach-space summaries with laws of large numbers, persistence images as stable finite-dimensional pixelizations, and positive-definite kernels on diagram space; and differentiable topology, where topological losses are backpropagated through neural networks to learn task-optimal representations. We state the key stability theorems with proof sketches, exhibit the standard reduction algorithm with complexity analysis, give executable reference code, and survey the empirical record from 3D shape classification to topological autoencoders, closing with the open problems limiting topological representation learning.

## 1 Introduction

Machine learning thrives on vector spaces: inner products define kernels, gradients flow through differentiable maps, and means and variances presuppose linear structure. Topology is robust precisely because it discards geometry — but the natural output of topological computation, the *persistence diagram* (a multiset of points in the half-plane above the diagonal), lives in a nonlinear metric space with no canonical inner product and no well-behaved notion of a mean.

Persistent homology resolves the first half of this tension. Given data equipped with a notion of scale — a point cloud with a distance threshold, an image with an intensity threshold — one builds a *filtration*, a nested family of simplicial complexes, and tracks how homological features (components, loops, voids) are born and die as the scale grows [1]. Long-lived features are signal; short-lived ones are noise. The resulting diagram or *barcode* is provably stable: small input perturbations induce only small diagram perturbations in the bottleneck metric [2].

The second half — making these summaries consumable by learning algorithms — is this thesis. We treat the pipeline

$$\text{data} \;\xrightarrow{\text{filtration}}\; \text{diagram} \;\xrightarrow{\text{vectorization}}\; \mathbb{R}^n \text{ or } \mathcal{H} \;\xrightarrow{\text{learner}}\; \text{prediction},$$

insisting on *stability* at every arrow: the composed map should be Lipschitz in a diagram metric, so learning-theoretic guarantees survive the encounter with topology. Three design families dominate. *Functional summaries* embed diagrams into Banach spaces — persistence landscapes [3] — unlocking means, laws of large numbers, and hypothesis tests. *Discretizations* rasterize diagrams into finite vectors — persistence images [4] — that drop into random forests and neural networks while retaining stability and interpretability. *Kernels* define positive-definite similarities on diagram space [6,7], enabling kernel SVMs without choosing coordinates. Most recently, *differentiable* constructions [8,9] fold the topological signature into the training loop, learning the vectorization jointly with the model.

Our contributions: a unified treatment of filtrations, diagrams, and their metrics; statements and proof sketches of the bottleneck and Wasserstein stability theorems; a comparative analysis of landscapes, images, and kernels with stability constants and costs; an exposition of differentiable persistent homology including the genericity assumptions that make gradients exist; and a critical survey of empirical results and open problems.

## 2 Background and Related Work

### 2.1 From data to filtrations

Let $X = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be a finite point cloud. A *simplicial complex* $K$ on $X$ is a family of subsets closed under taking subsets; a *filtration* is a nested sequence $\emptyset = K_0 \subseteq K_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq K_m = K$, typically indexed by a scale parameter $r \ge 0$. The three classical geometric filtrations are:

- **Vietoris–Rips** $\mathrm{VR}_r(X)$: a simplex $\sigma$ is included when $\mathrm{diam}(\sigma) \le 2r$. This is the *clique complex* of the proximity graph — computable from pairwise distances alone, but potentially enormous.
- **Čech** $\check{\mathrm{C}}_r(X)$: $\sigma$ is included when $\bigcap_{x \in \sigma} B(x, r) \ne \emptyset$. By the *nerve theorem*, $\check{\mathrm{C}}_r(X)$ is homotopy equivalent to the union of balls $\bigcup_x B(x,r)$: it faithfully captures the topology of thickened data.
- **Alpha complex** $\alpha_r(X)$: the nerve of balls intersected with Voronoi cells — a subcomplex of the Delaunay triangulation, homotopy equivalent to the union of balls but far smaller than the Čech complex in low ambient dimension.

For images, *cubical* sublevel filtrations of pixel intensities play the analogous role; for graphs, sublevel filtrations of edge weights. The founding paper introduced persistence for alpha-complex filtrations of 3D shapes [1]; the algebraic module-over-$\mathbb{F}[t]$ framework followed [5].

### 2.2 Persistence diagrams, barcodes, and metrics

Applying $H_k(-; \mathbb{F})$ to a filtration yields a *persistence module* — vector spaces and linear maps. The structure theorem for finitely generated modules over the PID $\mathbb{F}[t]$ decomposes it into interval summands; each $[b, d)$ records the *birth* and *death* of one class. The multiset of points $(b, d)$ is the **persistence diagram** $\mathrm{Dgm}_k$; drawn as horizontal bars, the **barcode** [5].

Diagram space carries two canonical metrics. The **bottleneck distance** $d_B(D, D')$ is the infimum, over bijections $\gamma: D \to D'$ (allowing matches to the diagonal), of $\sup_p \|p - \gamma(p)\|_\infty$. The **$p$-Wasserstein distance** $W_p$ replaces the supremum with an $\ell^p$ sum. Bottleneck controls the worst feature; Wasserstein aggregates over all features. With either metric, diagram space is complete but *not* a Hilbert space, and Fréchet means need not exist or be unique — precisely why vectorization is necessary for statistics and learning.

### 2.3 The stability theorems

The foundational result, proved for tame functions, states [2]:

> **Theorem (Bottleneck stability).** *Let $f, g: \mathbb{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ be tame. Then*
> $$d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}(f), \mathrm{Dgm}(g)) \le \|f - g\|_\infty.$$

*Proof sketch.* Via the **Box Lemma**: along the homotopy $h_t = (1-t)f + tg$, each off-diagonal diagram point moves continuously and cannot jump across boxes. Tracking points yields a matching between $\mathrm{Dgm}(f)$ and $\mathrm{Dgm}(g)$ of cost at most $\|f-g\|_\infty$. The pairing is locally constant except at discrete critical values, so trajectories are well-defined. ∎

For point clouds this descends to geometry: $d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}(\mathrm{VR}(X)), \mathrm{Dgm}(\mathrm{VR}(Y))) \le 2\, d_H(X, Y)$ with $d_H$ the Hausdorff distance. Wasserstein stability holds under Lipschitz and bounded-total-persistence hypotheses. These theorems license everything below: any vectorization Lipschitz in $d_B$ or $W_p$ inherits robustness from data through diagram to feature vector.

### 2.4 Computing persistence

The standard algorithm reduces the boundary matrix of the filtered complex to column-echelon form [5]: simplices are processed in filtration order, each column reduced by left-to-right additions until its lowest nonzero entry is unique or the column vanishes; matching lowest entries give birth–death pairs. Worst case is $O(n^3)$ in the number $n$ of simplices — and $\mathrm{VR}$ on $N$ points has up to $2^N$ simplices.

Modern practice escapes via persistent *cohomology*, the *clearing* optimization, *apparent pairs* forming a discrete gradient, and implicit matrix representations. **Ripser** [6] combines all four, outperforming predecessors by over $40\times$ in time and $15\times$ in memory and making thousand-point Vietoris–Rips computations routine. For low-dimensional Euclidean data, alpha complexes remain the method of choice.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The topological learning pipeline

1. **Filtration selection.** Match the filtration to the modality: Vietoris–Rips for abstract metric data (point clouds, distance matrices, embeddings); alpha complexes for low-dimensional Euclidean data; cubical sublevel filtrations for images; edge-weight filtrations for graphs. This trades topological fidelity (Čech/alpha capture the union-of-balls homotopy type exactly) against computability (Rips needs only pairwise distances).
2. **Diagram computation.** Compute $\mathrm{Dgm}_k$ for relevant dimensions (usually $k=0,1$) over $\mathbb{F}_2$ with a modern engine [6], truncating at a maximum filtration value to bound complex size.
3. **Vectorization.** Apply a stable map $\Phi$: a Banach-space embedding (landscapes [3]), a finite discretization (images [4]), or a kernel feature map [6,7]. Hyperparameters are chosen by cross-validation on the downstream task.
4. **Learning.** Feed $\Phi(\mathrm{Dgm})$ into SVMs, random forests, or neural networks — or, in the differentiable paradigm, backpropagate through $\Phi$ and the persistence computation itself [8,9].

### 3.2 What "stable vectorization" means

A vectorization $\Phi$ is *stable* if it is Lipschitz in a diagram metric: $\|\Phi(D) - \Phi(D')\| \le C \cdot d(D, D')$ for $d = d_B$ or $W_p$. Combined with bottleneck stability [2], this yields an end-to-end Lipschitz bound from data perturbations to feature perturbations. The metric, the constant, and the computational price differ across families, and choosing among them is the central engineering decision of topological machine learning.

### 3.3 Reference implementation

The following sketch implements the full pipeline through persistence images:

```python
import numpy as np
from ripser import ripser
from persim import PersImage

def topological_features(X, maxdim=1, spread=1.0, pixels=(25, 25)):
    """X: (n, d) point cloud -> concatenated persistence-image vector."""
    dgms = ripser(X, maxdim=maxdim)['dgms']      # Vietoris-Rips diagrams
    pim = PersImage(spread=spread, pixels=pixels, verbose=False)
    feats = [pim.transform(dgm).ravel() for dgm in dgms]  # stable map [5]
    return np.concatenate(feats)

from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier
clf = RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=500).fit(F_train, y_train)
```

`PersImage.transform` is exactly the map $\Phi$ of §4.3: a weighted sum of Gaussians centered at diagram points, integrated over pixels. Its stability in $1$-Wasserstein distance is a theorem [4], not an empirical observation.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Filtrations: Vietoris–Rips, Čech, Alpha, and the Nerve Theorem

**Vietoris–Rips** is the workhorse because it needs only pairwise distances — but as a flag complex it can reach dimension $N-1$ and explodes combinatorially. **Čech** is geometrically faithful via the nerve theorem, yet testing common intersection of many balls is expensive. **Alpha** complexes recover the best of both in low ambient dimension: subcomplexes of the Delaunay triangulation (at most $O(N^{\lceil d/2 \rceil})$ simplices in $\mathbb{R}^d$) that are *also* homotopy equivalent to the union of balls. In high dimensions Delaunay is intractable and practitioners retreat to Vietoris–Rips.

The three are not independent: Čech and Vietoris–Rips filtrations are *multiplicatively interleaved* by a constant factor. Interleaving of persistence modules bounds the bottleneck distance between their diagrams (the algebraic stability theorem), so the Vietoris–Rips diagram is a provably controlled approximation of the geometrically ideal Čech diagram — the theoretical justification for the field's computational practice.

| Filtration | Input needed | Homotopy guarantee | Size (worst case) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vietoris–Rips | Pairwise distances | Interleaved with Čech | $O(2^N)$ | Abstract metric data, embeddings |
| Čech | Ball intersections | Nerve theorem (exact) | $O(2^N)$ | Theoretical analysis |
| Alpha | Delaunay triangulation | Nerve theorem (exact) | $O(N^{\lceil d/2\rceil})$ | Low-dim Euclidean data |
| Cubical | Grid function values | Sublevel sets (exact) | $O(\text{pixels})$ | Images, volumes |
| Witness | Landmark distances | Approximation | $O(L^k)$, $L \ll N$ | Large point clouds |

### 4.2 Stability: Bottleneck, Wasserstein, and Interleavings

The bottleneck stability theorem [2] (§2.3) handles the sup-norm. Its algebraic form is the **isometry theorem**: the *interleaving distance* between persistence modules — pairs of morphisms shifting scale by $\varepsilon$ in each direction — equals the bottleneck distance between their diagrams.

> **Theorem (Isometry).** *For q-tame persistence modules $M, N$, $d_I(M, N) = d_B(\mathrm{Dgm}(M), \mathrm{Dgm}(N))$.*

*Proof sketch.* An $\varepsilon$-interleaving induces matchings between interval summands via the shifted module maps; the structure theorem reads off a bottleneck matching of cost $\varepsilon$. Conversely, a bottleneck matching lifts to interleaving morphisms by shifting interval modules. The hard step is functoriality of the decomposition — matchings of diagrams lift to morphisms of modules — via the Krull–Remak–Schmidt property. ∎

Wasserstein stability is subtler: $W_p$ aggregates over *all* features, so a cloud of tiny noise — irrelevant under $d_B$ — can dominate $W_p$. Hence $W_p$ stability needs bounded total persistence (Lipschitz functions on triangulable spaces). Consequence: $W_1$-stable vectorizations (images [4], multi-scale kernel [7]) carry constants depending on total persistence, while bottleneck-stable summaries (landscapes in $L^\infty$ [3]) are immune to noise accumulation but may discard fine structure.

### 4.3 Vectorizations: Landscapes, Images, and Kernels

**Persistence landscapes** [3] map each $(b, d)$ to a tent function $\Lambda_{(b,d)}(t) = \max(0, \min(t - b, d - t))$ peaked at height $(d-b)/2$, then define $\lambda_k(t)$ as the $k$-th largest tent value at $t$. Landscapes live in a Banach space where means and linear classifiers are well-defined; Bubenik proved a strong law of large numbers and a central limit theorem for them as Banach-space-valued random variables — the first rigorous inference framework for persistent homology — plus Lipschitz stability in bottleneck distance. Price: landscapes discard feature identity (they are order statistics), and $k$, $p$ are modeling choices.

**Persistence images** [4] keep every point but blur. Each point is mapped to birth–persistence coordinates $(b, d-b)$ and contributes a weighted Gaussian to a *persistence surface* $\rho_D = \sum w(u)\phi_u$, with $w$ emphasizing long-lived features; integrating $\rho_D$ over a pixel grid yields the image vector. Adams et al. prove $W_1$ stability and show significant gains over prior vectorizations on dynamical-system parameter inference (linked twist map, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky), where sparse SVM weights identify the discriminating topological regions — interpretability for free. Price: resolution, bandwidth, and weighting are hyperparameters, and the $W_1$ constant scales with total persistence.

**Kernels** avoid coordinates. The **multi-scale kernel** [7] places a Gaussian at each diagram point and its mirror across the diagonal; the resulting $L^2$ feature map gives a positive-definite kernel, $W_1$-stable and *universal* (hence suited to hypothesis testing). The **sliced Wasserstein kernel** [8] projects diagrams onto lines, computes 1D Wasserstein distances in closed form, and exponentiates: positive-definite, metric-preserving, far cheaper than full $W_p$. Price of all kernel methods: Gram matrices scale as $O(n^2)$ in sample count with cubic SVM training — the gap the differentiable methods of §4.4 address.

| Vectorization | Output space | Stable in | Key strength | Key cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landscapes [3] | Banach space $L^p$ | $d_B$ (Lipschitz) | SLLN, CLT, hypothesis tests | Loses feature identity |
| Persistence images [4] | $\mathbb{R}^n$ (pixels) | $W_1$ | Interpretable, fixed size, fast | Hyperparameters (grid, $\sigma$, $w$) |
| Multi-scale kernel [7] | RKHS | $W_1$ | Universal, no coordinates | $O(n^2)$ Gram matrix |
| Sliced Wasserstein kernel [8] | RKHS | $W_1$ (sliced) | Cheap, metric-preserving | Projection directions |
| Learnable layer [9] | $\mathbb{R}^n$ | $W_1$ | Task-optimal, end-to-end | Non-convex, needs data |

### 4.4 Differentiable Topology and Representation Learning

All vectorizations above share a limitation: $\Phi$ is *fixed* before seeing the task. Two lines of work bring topology into the differentiable regime.

**Learnable topological layers** [9] replace the fixed projection of persistence images with a parametrized one, trained jointly with the network while preserving $W_1$ stability. On 2D shape classification and social-network graph classification this improved substantially over fixed vectorizations — task-agnostic topological features leave performance on the table.

**Topological autoencoders** [10] use topology as a *regularizer*: persistent homology is computed on mini-batches in input and latent space, and a topological loss penalizes pairing discrepancies, encouraging the encoder to preserve multi-scale connectivity. The obstacle is differentiability — the persistence pairing is piecewise-constant in the input distances. Under weak genericity assumptions (essentially distinct pairwise distances, so the pairing is locally constant), the loss is differentiable almost everywhere and backpropagates. Result: latent spaces with better topological fidelity at competitive reconstruction error, on synthetic manifolds and image data.

The sharp edge: gradients exist only *almost everywhere*; at non-generic configurations the pairing jumps and backpropagation follows a phantom gradient. And computing persistence per mini-batch is costly, limiting current implementations to $H_0/H_1$ of small batches. Differentiable topology is the most exciting and least settled part of the field.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: stability of persistence images

Let $D, D'$ be diagrams with persistence surfaces $\rho_D, \rho_{D'}$ built from Gaussians of bandwidth $\sigma$ and an $L_w$-Lipschitz weight $w$. For the optimal $W_1$ matching $\gamma$ (unmatched points to the diagonal) [4]:

$$\|\rho_D - \rho_{D'}\|_1 \le \sum_{u} \|w(u)\phi_u - w(\gamma(u))\phi_{\gamma(u)}\|_1.$$

Each term splits into a weight-difference part (bounded by $L_w$ times the persistence difference) and a kernel-shift part (the Gaussian is Lipschitz in its center with constant $\propto 1/\sigma$). Summing over the matching and integrating over pixels gives

$$\|\mathrm{PI}(D) - \mathrm{PI}(D')\|_2 \le C(\sigma, L_w, \text{grid}) \cdot W_1(D, D').$$

The constant's dependence on total persistence is the precise form of the §4.2 caveat: $W_1$ stability is meaningful when total persistence is controlled, e.g., by truncating the filtration. ∎

### 5.2 The empirical record

- **Shape and texture analysis.** The multi-scale kernel [7] achieved considerable gains over persistence landscapes on 3D shape classification/retrieval and texture recognition — the first demonstration that a theoretically grounded topological kernel beats hand-tuned summaries.
- **Dynamical systems.** Persistence images [4] inferred parameters of the linked twist map and the anisotropic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation with high accuracy, with sparse SVM weights revealing *which* topological features discriminated.
- **Graph learning.** The learnable topological layer [9] improved over the state of the art on social-network graph classification by a large margin, complementing message-passing architectures.
- **Representation learning.** Topological autoencoders [10] produced latent spaces with measurably better topological fidelity at competitive reconstruction error, on synthetic manifolds and image datasets.
- **Scalability.** Ripser [6] made thousand-point Vietoris–Rips computations routine, moving these methods from proof-of-concept to applicable.

### 5.3 Complexity accounting

For Vietoris–Rips on $N$ points truncated at dimension $k$: $O(N^{k+1})$ simplices, cubic reduction in the worst case, though apparent pairs and clearing make typical instances far cheaper [6]. Persistence images cost $O(|D|\cdot\text{pixels})$; landscapes $O(|D|\log|D|)$ per query [3]; kernel Gram matrices $O(n^2)$ evaluations for $n$ samples, each $O(|D|\cdot|D'|)$ or worse. The practical rule: diagrams are cheap (Ripser), vectorizations are cheap, *comparing many diagrams* is expensive — which is why fixed finite-dimensional vectorizations dominate at scale.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

**High-dimensional filtrations.** Vietoris–Rips grows combinatorially; alpha complexes need infeasible Delaunay triangulations beyond $d \approx 4$; witness complexes approximate without clean guarantees. Practitioners subsample, work in learned embeddings, or retreat to $H_0$ (single-linkage clustering in disguise). A faithful yet tractable high-dimensional filtration remains the central computational open problem.

**Hyperparameter sensitivity.** Every vectorization carries knobs — landscape depth $k$ and norm $p$; image resolution, $\sigma$, weight $w$; kernel scale, projection count. Stability theorems bound *input* sensitivity, not hyperparameter sensitivity, and no canonical data-driven selection rule has theoretical backing.

**Fragile differentiability.** Genericity assumptions (distinct distances, stable pairings) fail silently on real data with ties and symmetries; when the pairing jumps, backpropagation follows a phantom gradient. A complete nonsmooth analysis of persistence-based losses is still missing.

**Multiparameter persistence.** Data often has two natural scales (density *and* scale, time *and* space), but modules over $\mathbb{F}[t_1,t_2]$ admit no complete discrete invariant — the representation theory is wild. The rank invariant and fibered barcodes exist but lack the stability theory and software of the one-parameter case.

**Statistical optimality.** Landscapes enjoy laws of large numbers [3], but minimax rates for topological estimators, adaptive vectorization choice, and uncertainty quantification for persistence-based predictions are largely unexplored. The field has stability; it lacks a complete statistical decision theory.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Persistent homology succeeded by making topology computable and stable: filtrations turn data into algebra [1,2], and the stability theorems [2] make the signatures robust. But computability and stability are not enough — signatures must be *representable* where learning happens. This thesis traced the three generations of that program: functional embeddings with statistical guarantees (landscapes [3]), finite discretizations with interpretability (images [4]), and coordinate-free similarities (kernels [6,7]), culminating in differentiable topology [8,9], where representations are learned rather than prescribed.

The unifying principle is the stability contract: every map from data to prediction should be Lipschitz in a diagram metric, so robustness composes. Where the contract holds, topological features are reliable, interpretable, theoretically grounded inputs with demonstrated wins from shape analysis to graph classification to representation learning. Where it frays — hyperparameters, non-generic differentiability, high-dimensional filtrations, multiparameter persistence — lie the open problems of the next decade.
