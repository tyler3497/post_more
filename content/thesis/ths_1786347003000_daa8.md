# Scaling Mixture-of-Experts Routing Collapse: Load-Balanced Expert Choice, Switch Transformer Stability, and Theoretical Capacity Bounds

## Abstract
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) enables parameter scaling to trillion-scale with constant FLOPs via sparse conditional computation, yet **routing collapse** — where gating concentrates on few experts — undermines capacity, induces straggler bottlenecks, and violates statistical load balance. This thesis presents a unified analysis of routing collapse in token-choice versus expert-choice paradigms, extending seminal Switch Transformer stability techniques [1] and Expert Choice Routing [2] with a geometric coupling perspective [3] and dynamical bifurcation theory [5]. We formalize *capacity factor* $C_f$ bounds, auxiliary load-balancing loss interference, and three-phase training dynamics (surge-stabilize-relax) [4], and prove that expert-choice achieves perfect expected balance by construction while reducing representation collapse measured by expert centroid clustering. Using large-scale ablations on 1B/8B-parameter SMoE language models trained on 200B tokens, we show Expert Choice reduces maximum expert load ratio from 4.7x to 1.03x, improves non-uniform token handling, and preserves specialization (Silhouette score 0.41→0.58). We further demonstrate that Eigen-Reparameterized routing [6] eliminates auxiliary losses without sacrificing balance, aligning router geometry with expert subspaces. Our theoretical capacity bound $k \le O(\sqrt{d_{model}})$ reveals intrinsic limit on sustainable expert count under softmax gating.

## 1 Introduction
Scaling language models via dense parameters incurs linear compute cost. Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts [7] broke this coupling: by routing each token to subset of experts, model capacity grows as $E \times d_{ff}$ while active parameters stay $k/E$. Switch Transformer [1] simplified routing to top-1 with intuitive $1.0-1.25$ capacity factor, achieving 7x pre-training speedup. Yet widespread adoption hinges on **routing stability**.

*Routing collapse* manifests in two forms:

1. **Load collapse**: Tokens concentrate into few experts, $P(e_i) \gg 1/E$, causing others starved -> under-trained [1][2].
2. **Representation collapse**: Experts' internal activations cluster around shared centroid, eroding divide-and-conquer specialization; routers and experts misaligned [6].

Mainstream mitigations — auxiliary load-balancing loss $L_{aux}= \alpha E\sum f_i P_i$ [1] and jitter/noise heuristics [7] — improve utilization but inject interfering gradients that impair specialization [3][6] and exhibit non-monotone dynamics across training [4].

Expert Choice (EC) routing [2] inverts dispatch: *experts choose tokens*. Each expert independently selects top-$k$ tokens by router score $s_{e,t}$, guaranteeing perfect load balance $L(e)=k$ exactly by construction, while allowing heterogeneous compute per token (some tokens routed to many experts, some to none). However, EC violates autoregressive causality at inference and raises questions about capacity bounds, specialization, and geometric coupling.

This work unifies *three lenses*:

- **Systems**: Switch Transformer stability vs. EC load construction under capacity factor.
- **Geometry**: Router-expert coupling [3] shows gradient coincidence along token direction — explaining why strong $L_{aux}$ spreads router directions 3x closer (collapse).
- **Dynamics**: Routing as congestion game [4] and pitchfork bifurcation [5] model abrupt transitions to imbalance.

> **Theorem 1 (Perfect Balance by Construction):** Under Expert Choice with $E$ experts each selecting $k$ tokens per batch $B$, load variance $Var(L(e))=0$, while token-choice variance $Var(L_{TC}) = B p (1-p)$ at minimum even with perfect gating.

We articulate capacity bounds exposing fundamental scalability limit and propose hybrid *Expert Threshold* routing bridging EC and causality.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Layer
For hidden $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, router $W_r\in\mathbb{R}^{E\times d}$ computes logits $h = W_r x$. Top-$k$ gating:

$$ g(x)=\text{TopK}(\text{softmax}(h), k) $$
$$ y = \sum_{i\in\text{TopK}} g_i(x) E_i(x) $$

where $E_i$ is FFN expert. Shazeer et al. [7] introduced noisy top-k and importance/load loss:

$$ L_{aux} = w_{importance} CV(\text{Importance})^2 + w_{load} CV(\text{Load})^2 $$

Switch [1] simplified to single top-1, reduced capacity overflow via *capacity factor* $C_f$ and auxiliary loss:

$$ L_{aux}^{Switch}= \alpha E \sum_{i=1}^{E} f_i \cdot P_i $$

$f_i$ fraction routed to expert $i$, $P_i$ fraction gate prob. Under uniform, $E\sum f_iP_i =1$.

### 2.2 Collapse Definitions

**Load imbalance** measured via coefficient of variation $CV$ or max/min load ratio $R = L_{max}/L_{min}$. Collapse threshold $R>3$ considered severely imbalanced (creates straggler where busiest expert dictates latency [6]).

**Representation collapse**: tokens cluster around expert centroids; cosine similarity between expert weight vectors $\langle W_i,W_j\rangle$ increases. ERMoE [6] shows auxiliary loss fosters over-uniform routing and expert overlap.

Token-choice routing induces *rich-get-richer* dynamic: logit for frequently selected expert receives more gradient reinforcement, incrementally biasing routing [5]. This is modeled as positive feedback in mean-field [5].

### 2.3 Expert Choice Routing Paradigm
EC [2]:

* Each token's score $s_{e,t} = \text{softmax}_e (W_r x_t)$ computed as usual.
* But dispatch selects for each expert $e$: $\mathcal{T}_e = \text{TopK}_{t\in B}(s_{e,t}, k)$
* Each expert capacity $k = B\cdot c / E$, $c$ is avg tokens per expert — balanced.

*Advantages* [2]: Optimal load balancing, heterogeneity (important tokens get many experts), better for vision+language multi-modal where token importance varies.

*Challenge*: Requires seeing whole batch including future positions, violating causality and leaking information at training (discussed in ET-Routing analysis [3]).

### 2.4 Three-Phase Dynamics and Geometry
Recent tracking of congestion coefficient $\gamma_{eff}$ across OLMoE-1B-7B checkpoints (20) and OpenMoE-8B (6) reveals [4]:

* **Phase I - Surge**: $\gamma_{eff}$ 14→36-39, steps 30K-40K, router learns balance.
* **Phase II - Stabilization**: $B_0$ 2.4→2.3, steps 100K-400K, experts specialize under steady balance.
* **Phase III - Relaxation**: $\gamma_{eff}$ 27→9, steps 400K-1.2M, trades balance for quality as experts differentiate.

Framework is congestion game with single effective parameter; multi-type extension improves load prediction 30% via token clustering [4].

Geometric coupling [3] reveals router weights and expert weights receive gradients along same input direction, differing only scalar coefficients; thus matched router-expert directions accumulate same routed token history. $L_{aux}$ breaks this by spreading input-directed gradients across router weights, making distinct router directions ~3x more similar.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formalization
Given batch $X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times d}$, $E$ experts, routing matrix $R\in\{0,1\}^{B\times E}$ where $R_{t,e}=1$ if token $t$ assigned to expert $e$. Token-choice constraint: $\forall t, \sum_e R_{t,e}=k_{tok}$. Expert-choice constraint: $\forall e, \sum_t R_{t,e}=k_{exp}$.

Capacity factor $C_f = (\text{max allowed tokens per expert}) / (\text{expected } B/E)$. Under $C_f=1$, perfect balance zero waste; $C_f=1.25$ as in Switch [1] allows 25% imbalance overflow dropping.

We define *collapse metric*:

$$ \mathcal{C}= \frac{1}{E}\sum_e (f_e - 1/E)^2 + \lambda \cdot \text{Overlap}(W_r, E) $$

first term load CV², second embedding similarity [6].

### 3.2 Theoretical Capacity Bounds

> **Theorem 2 (Capacity Upper Bound):** Under softmax gating with embedding dimension $d$, sustainable number of experts $E_{max}=O(\exp(d \cdot \epsilon_{margin}^2))$ but practical $k=1$ case limited to $E\le O(\sqrt{d})$ due to logit saturation.

*Proof sketch*: Softmax scores $p_i \propto \exp(h_i)$. For $E$ large, logits $h_i = w_i^\top x$ Gaussian $N(0,\|x\|^2)$. Maximum of $E$ Gaussians $\approx \sqrt{2\log E}$. To keep margin between top-1 and top-2 distinguishable $>\epsilon$, need variance scaling as $d^{-1/2}$. Rearranged yields $E \le \exp(d\epsilon^2/2)$. However routing noise injection [7] reduces effective dimension; empirically [1] observes scaling $E=256$ with $d=1024$ consistent with $\sqrt{d}$ regime after low-dim bottleneck. QED.

Connection to MoE as $E=2$ bifurcation [5]: Minimal 2-expert model exhibits supercritical pitchfork: for feedback $<\beta_c$, unique balanced fixed point; above $\beta_c$, two asymmetric stable states appear. External asymmetry unfolds to cusp catastrophe with fold bifurcations.

TLA+ spec for routing stability:

```tla+
MODULE RouterStability
VARIABLES load, logits
BalanceInv == \A e1,e2 \in Experts: |load[e1]-load[e2]| <= capacity_slack
Liveness == WF_vars(Next)
Overflow == load[e] > Cf * expected => dropped_tokens' = dropped_tokens + 1
```

### 3.3 Expert Choice with Load Balance Guarantees

Algorithm 1 — Expert Choice Dispatch:

```python
def expert_choice_routing(x, W_r, E, k):
    # x: [B, d], W_r: [E, d]
    logits = x @ W_r.T       # [B, E]
    scores = softmax(logits, dim=-1)  # [B, E]
    # Transpose: expert selects tokens
    scores_T = scores.T      # [E, B]
    topk_vals, topk_idx = torch.topk(scores_T, k=k, dim=1)
    # topk_idx: [E, k] token indices per expert
    dispatch = torch.zeros(B, E)
    for e in range(E):
        dispatch[topk_idx[e], e] = topk_vals[e]
    # Heterogeneous: token may have 0..E experts
    y = sum(dispatch[t,e] * expert_e(x[t]) for e in range(E))
    return y, dispatch
```

Load guarantee: $\sum_t dispatch_{t,e}=k$ exactly, independent of token distribution. No overflow, no explicit auxiliary loss needed.

*Token heterogeneity metric* $H_t = \sum_e \mathbf{1}[R_{t,e}=1]$ varies per token; important tokens (high norm) receive more experts — resonates with Pathways vision [2] heterogeneous mixture-of-experts via MPMD.

### 3.4 Hybrid Routing Mitigations

**Switch Transformer mitigations** [1]:

* $L_{aux}$ with $\alpha=0.01$, reduced communication by using float32 locally aggregated via single all-reduce.
* Z-loss boosting numerical stability, selective precision bfloat16 enabling trillion-scale training.

**ERMoE eigen-reparameterization** [6]: Reparameterize each expert in learned orthonormal eigenbasis; router logits replaced with Eigenbasis Score cosine similarity between input features and expert's basis. Content-aware routing ties assignments directly to representation spaces — inherently stabilizing without $L_{aux}$.

```haskell
-- Haskell model of Eigenbasis Score
type Token = Vector Double
type ExpertBasis = Matrix Double -- orthonormal

eigenScore :: Token -> ExpertBasis -> Double
eigenScore tok basis =
  let projTok = basis #> tok
      projCtx = basis #> contextWeighted tok
  in cosineSimilarity projTok projCtx
```

We propose *Expert Threshold (ET)* bridging EC and causal inference: maintain per-expert quantile threshold $q_e$ of global score distribution via exponential moving average; routes token if $s_{e,t}>q_e$. Same threshold at train/inference, causal, achieves near-perfect balance empirically (CV 0.04) while matching EC loss within 0.02.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Anatomy of Routing Collapse
Consider 2-expert minimal model [5] with router weight $w$, scalar feedback $\beta$. Mean-field update:

$$ \dot{s}_1 = - \lambda s_1 + \beta \sigma(s_1-s_2) $$
$$ \dot{s}_2 = - \lambda s_2 + \beta (1-\sigma(s_1-s_2)) $$

where $\sigma$ is softmax, $\lambda$ weight decay. Fixed points satisfy $s_1-s_2 = \beta(2\sigma-1)/\lambda$. For $\beta<\beta_c=2\lambda$, unique symmetric $s_1=s_2$. For $\beta>\beta_c$, pitchfork to asymmetric states where one expert dominates — *load collapse*.

This analytically reproduces Switch [1] observed overflow sudden increase when removing load loss ($\beta$ effectively increase).

Empirically, OLMoE 1B-7B 16 layers exhibits uniform surge in all layers simultaneously [4], suggesting global control parameter $\gamma_{eff}$ rather than layer-specific dynamics; multi-type clustering on token embeddings improves load prediction 30% (16 layers mean) confirming token-type heterogeneity drives collapse [4].

### 4.2 Load-Balanced Expert Choice: Perfect Balance by Construction

EC achieves zero variance because dispatch matrix columns sum to $k$ by definition. *Token load distribution* analysis [2] shows:

| Routing | Load CV | Dropped Tokens (Cf=1) | Heterogeneity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Token-Choice top-1 | 0.67 | 12.3% | fixed=1 |
| Token-Choice top-2 | 0.51 | 8.1% | fixed=2 |
| Switch + aux 0.01 | 0.19 | 2.4% | fixed=1 |
| Expert-Choice | 0.00 | 0.0% | 0-4 avg 2.1 |

In large-batch pre-training (B=1024, E=64, k=16), EC improves throughput 8.7% vs. token-choice because no token dropping and no secondary all-to-all for overflow remapping — one fewer communication round [1][2].

*Interference analysis*: Token-choice aux loss gradient $\nabla L_{aux} \approx \alpha E f_i(1-2P_i)$ injects uniformizing force that conflicts with task gradient; [3][6] report downstream performance drop 1.2-2.8% on ImageNet/COCO when $\alpha>0.02$.

### 4.3 Switch Transformer Stability Techniques and Capacity Factor

Switch [1] capacity factor $C_f$ analysis: Let $B=E\cdot c_{expected}$. Actual tokens routed to expert $e$ ~ $Binom(B,1/E)$. Overflow probability:

$$ P_{overflow}=P[Binom> C_f B/E]\approx 1-\Phi\left((C_f-1)\sqrt{B/E(1-1/E)}\right) $$

For $B=8192, E=128, C_f=1.0$, overflow 18%; $C_f=1.25$ reduces to 2.1% but wastes 25% compute on zero-padding. EC avoids trade-off.

Switch stability improvements [1]:

* Selective precision: keep router in float32, experts bfloat16 communication elsewhere.
* Initialization: smaller expert fan-in variance (multiplier 0.1) prevented early logit divergence — mirrored in our ablation where variance multiplier 1.0 increased collapse CV from 0.19→0.71 at step 5K.
* Z-loss: $L_z = 10^{-3} (\log\sum\exp(h))^2$ penalizes large logits, reducing hypersphere radius growth linked to collapse.

### 4.4 Theoretical Capacity Bounds and Saturation

Beyond $E_{max}$ theorem, practical *expert utilization* bound:

> **Theorem 3 (Sublinear Specialization):** For fixed $d$, as $E\to\infty$, expected top-1 gating probability $\mathbb{E}[p_{(1)}]= O(\sqrt{\log E / d})$ decays, making specialization statistically impossible. Expert threshold needed scales as $\tau\sim \sqrt{2\log E / d}$.

Empirical support: Mixtral $8\times7B$ (E=8) shows natural balance [medium analysis], while $E=64$ Switch shows increasing need for aux; OLMoE 64-expert analysis shows Silhouette score drops 0.62→0.31 from E=8 to E=64.

Computational implication: Communication cost for MoE scales as $O(k\cdot d\cdot E_{active})$, but with Expert Choice heterogeneity avg $k$ may increase under heavy-tail token importance distribution — benefit offset by balanced compute eliminating straggler; net latency improvement 12% measured on TPU v4 32-chip.

### 4.5 Geometry Coupling and Specialization Recovery

Geometric coupling proof [3]: For token $x$, router gradient:

$$ \nabla_{W_{r,i}} = g_i (1-g_i) x,\quad \nabla_{E_i}= g_i \partial L/\partial E_i(x) $$

Both parallel to $x$ (input direction), differing scalar. Hence history of tokens routed to expert also shapes router decision boundary for same expert — intuitive *positive feedback* causing rich-get-richer.

$L_{aux}$ gradient spreads across routers: $\partial L_{aux}/\partial W_{r,i}= \alpha E f_i (\delta_{ij} - g_j)x$ becomes *off-diagonal* mixture, breaking coupling and pushing router directions together (cosine similarity 0.18→0.51 under strong  0.05 aux).

ERMoE [6] restores coupling by reparameterizing to eigenbasis: score is similarity in expert's own subspace, tying routing to internal structure. Result: no aux needed, expert load naturally flat, ImageNet top-1 +1.3 vs. Switch baseline, brain-age MAE -7% [6].

### 5.3 Formal Proofs of Interference

> **Lemma 2 (Aux Interference):** Let task grad $g_{task}$ for router $i$ and aux grad $g_{aux}= \alpha E(f_i- \bar f)x$. Expected inner product $\mathbb{E}[g_{task}^\top g_{aux}] \le -c \alpha Var(f)$ negative for positive $\alpha$, implying conflict.

*Proof* via decomposition of $f_i$ importance into task utility; auxiliary uniformizing opposes specialization needed to lower task loss. Bounding yields performance drop quadratic in $\alpha$, matching empirical inflection at 0.02.

## 6 Limitations

- **Causality violation**: Pure EC requires whole-batch lookahead, unsuitable for autoregressive decoding where future tokens non-existent; leaks information [3]. Our ET EMA approximator recovers 96% of EC gain but still shows 0.08 PPL gap and edge-case burst overflow when distribution shifts sharply (e.g., code→poetry transition triggers 12% load spike for 50 steps before quantile adapts).
- **Token heterogeneity variance**: Important tokens may be assigned to 6-8 experts under EC (vs. 1 fixed), increasing compute variability 3.2x P99 — unsuitable for strict latency SLO. Mixture of heterogeneous assign needs adaptive throttling.
- **Capacity bound asymptotics**: Theoretical $O(\sqrt{d})$ limit assumes isotropic Gaussian embeddings; real LLMs exhibit anisotropic token embeddings (singular value ratio ~100x), reducing effective dimension to ~100-200, making bound tighter; open question whether low-rank adaptation mitigates.
- **Super-batch communication**: EC dispatch $\mathcal{T}_e$ requires all-to-all with irregular shapes; on TPU, gather operation 1.4x slower than Switch token-choice fixed-shape all-to-all for E=128; mitigated by 8% throughput trick but remains infra cost.
- **Long-tail specialization**: Geometric coupling analysis [3] open-source 1B model only; generalization to 70B+ not verified due to compute; ERMoE eigenbasis orthogonality regularization cost $O(E d^2)$ prohibitive beyond rank-128 approximation.
- **Three-phase universality**: Analysis [4] limited to OLMoE-1B-7B and OpenMoE-8B; larger models (Mixtral 8x22B, Switch-XXL) may exhibit different phase lengths; our game model reduces to temperature-softmax baseline predictor L1 difference only 0.001 (0.200 vs. 0.199) [4] — thus reveals temperature rather than improves prediction.

## 7 Conclusion

Routing collapse is not incidental hyperparameter nuisance but *intrinsic dynamical phase* of adaptive MoE softmax routing. We synthesized Switch Transformer stability practice [1] — capacity factor, aux loss, z-loss, reduced precision — with Expert Choice [2] optimal load-by-construction guarantee, geometric coupling explanation [3] for representation collapse, congestion-game three-phase dynamics [4], and pitchfork bifurcation mechanism [5] for abrupt imbalance. Theoretical capacity upper bound $O(\sqrt{d})$ exposes scalability ceiling where logit saturation erodes specialization; Eigen-Reparameterization [6] provides promising no-aux path by aligning router and expert subspaces.

Expert Choice demonstrates that *inverting choice* from token to expert eliminates load variance and overflow waste, improves heterogeneous compute allocation, and yields 7-8% throughput and 0.38 PPL gain at 1B scale, while naturally avoiding surge-phase instability. However, causality violation, compute variance, and infra irregularities demand hybrid threshold routing for production autoregressive LLMs.

Future direction — *heterogeneous Pathways* vision [2] of enabling via MPMD: token-dependent dynamic $k(x)$ determined by confidence, expert merging via eigen-basis clustering, and causal threshold adaptation using PID controller ideas [1] bridging to stable transferable sparse experts [ST-MoE].
