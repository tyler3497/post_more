# Factor-Graph Optimization in Visual SLAM: Sparse ORB Feature Tracking, Bundle Adjustment via g2o, Loop Closure Detection, and Robust Nonlinear Estimation in ORB-SLAM3 and DROID-SLAM

## Abstract

Simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) has converged on factor-graph nonlinear least squares as its dominant computational paradigm. This thesis dissects the modern visual SLAM stack from two complementary vantage points: the classical sparse feature-based pipeline of **ORB-SLAM3** [1], which fuses ORB features, inertial measurements, and multi-map place recognition within a Maximum-a-Posteriori (MAP) framework optimized by **g2o** [3], and the learning-based dense system **DROID-SLAM** [2], which replaces handcrafted correspondence with a recurrent update operator derived from RAFT [6] and performs end-to-end differentiable **dense bundle adjustment**. We derive the reprojection and inertial residual formulations, the Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt iterations on the *SE(3)* manifold, robust kernel reweighting, bag-of-words loop closure with geometric verification, and the Schur-complement structure of the dense bundle adjustment layer. Empirical evidence from EuRoC, TUM-VI, and TartanAir is surveyed, and failure modes — perceptual aliasing, scale ambiguity, and gauge degeneracy — are analyzed formally.

---

## 1. Introduction

Visual SLAM asks a camera to answer two coupled questions: *where am I?* and *what does the world look like?* Formally, given a sequence of images {I_t} and optionally inertial readings, the system must estimate the trajectory of poses **T**_t in SE(3) and a map of landmarks {**p**_j} such that the joint posterior P(**T**_1:T, **p**_1:M | Z) is maximized. Under Gaussian measurement noise, MAP estimation reduces to minimizing a sum of squared residuals — a nonlinear least-squares problem [4].

Two architectures define the state of the art:

1. **ORB-SLAM3** [1] — a sparse, feature-based system that extracts ORB keypoints, tracks them through a covisibility graph, initializes inertial biases via a closed-form MAP procedure, and reuses all past information through an *Atlas* of multiple maps merged by an improved place-recognition module.
2. **DROID-SLAM** [2] — a dense, learning-based system that eliminates explicit feature matching entirely. A learned update operator, architecturally descended from RAFT's recurrent optical-flow network [6], iteratively refines per-pixel depth and camera poses; a differentiable **Dense Bundle Adjustment (DBA)** layer then solves a full nonlinear least-squares problem over thousands of dense correspondences.

The thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 places both systems in the factor-graph tradition formalized by Dellaert and Kaess [4]. Section 3 derives the residual functions and optimization machinery. Section 4 dives into the four pillars of the topic: ORB feature tracking, g2o bundle adjustment, loop closure, and the differentiable dense counterpart. Section 5 surveys empirical results and gives formal arguments for convergence and robustness. Section 6 catalogs limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 The Factor-Graph Formulation of SLAM

A factor graph is a bipartite graph G = (V, F) relating variable nodes x_i to factor nodes f_k that encode probabilistic constraints [4]. The MAP estimate is

> **Theorem:** *Under independent Gaussian measurement models, maximizing P(X | Z) is equivalent to minimizing the sum over k of ||f_k(X_k) - z_k||^2_{Sigma_k}, i.e., a weighted nonlinear least-squares objective whose sparsity pattern mirrors the factor graph topology.*

Sparsity is everything: the information matrix H = J^T W J is sparse precisely when the factor graph is sparse, which is why both ORB-SLAM3 and DROID-SLAM design their graphs — and their optimizers — around it [3][4].

### 2.2 Classical Sparse SLAM and the ORB Lineage

The ORB lineage began with ORB-SLAM (monocular), ORB-SLAM2 (stereo/RGB-D), and culminated in ORB-SLAM3 [1]: the first system to perform visual, visual-inertial, and multi-map SLAM with monocular, stereo, and RGB-D cameras under both pinhole and fisheye models. Its two headline novelties are (i) a tightly-coupled visual-inertial system fully grounded in MAP estimation *even during IMU initialization*, and (ii) a multi-map Atlas with improved-recall place recognition that lets the system survive long episodes of poor visual information by starting new maps and seamlessly merging them later [1].

### 2.3 Loop Closure and Bag-of-Words Place Recognition

Perceptual aliasing — two distinct places that look alike — makes loop closure the most fragile module of any SLAM system. The field's workhorse is the bag-of-binary-words approach of Galvez-Lopez and Tardos [5], which converts binary descriptors into sparse visual-word vectors and evaluates place candidates through a hierarchical vocabulary tree, accelerated by a direct index and normalized scoring. ORB-SLAM3 retains this machinery while adding a geometric-verification cascade with higher recall [1].

### 2.4 Optimization Frameworks: g2o

Kuemmerle et al. [3] introduced **g2o** (*general framework for graph optimization*), an open-source C++ library expressing nonlinear least-squares problems as graphs of vertices (states on manifolds) and edges (error functions). Its extensibility — a new problem "typically can be specified in a few lines of code" [3] — made it the default backend of the ORB-SLAM family and much of robotics. g2o provides Gauss–Newton, Levenberg–Marquardt, and Powell's Dog-Leg solvers, manifold-aware parameterizations, and sparse linear backends (CSparse, CHOLMOD).

### 2.5 Dense Learning-Based SLAM

DROID-SLAM [2] belongs to the deep-learning turn in SLAM: instead of detecting and matching keypoints, it predicts dense per-pixel correspondences and depth with a neural update operator, then optimizes camera poses and depth jointly in a differentiable DBA layer. Its update operator is inherited from RAFT [6], which builds multi-scale 4D correlation volumes over *all pairs* of pixels and refines a flow field with a recurrent ConvGRU that looks up correlation features — achieving 5.10% F1-all error on KITTI and 2.855 EPE on Sintel, with strong cross-dataset generalization [6].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 State Vector and Residual Functions

In ORB-SLAM3, the estimated state per keyframe i comprises the pose **T**_i in SE(3), velocity **v**_i, IMU biases (**b**^a_i, **b**^g_i), and inverse-depth landmark parameters rho_j. The MAP objective is [1]:

X* = argmin_X ( sum_{i,j} rho( ||**r**^{proj}_{ij}(X)||^2_{Sigma_{ij}} ) + sum_i ||**r**^{IMU}_i(X)||^2_{Sigma_{IMU}} + ||**r**^{prior}_0(X)||^2_{Sigma_0} )

where rho(.) is a robust cost (Huber), and **r**^{proj}_{ij} is the reprojection residual of map point j in keyframe i:

**r**^{proj}_{ij} = **u**_{ij} - pi( **T**_i^{-1} **p**_j ),

with pi the camera projection model and **u**_{ij} the observed keypoint.

The IMU residual **r**^{IMU}_i encodes preintegrated inertial constraints between consecutive keyframes, coupled through bias random-walk models [1].

### 3.2 Manifold Optimization: Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt

Because poses live on *SE(3)*, not in a vector space, optimization uses the Lie-algebraic retraction:

**T** <- **T** * Exp(delta xi), with delta xi in se(3).

Linearizing the residual stack **r**(X [+] dx) ~= **r** + J dx, each iteration solves the normal equations:

(J^T W J + lambda I) dx = -J^T W **r**,

with lambda = 0 recovering Gauss–Newton and lambda > 0 giving Levenberg–Marquardt damping. g2o implements both, plus Dog-Leg trust-region stepping, with sparse Cholesky factorization of the block-structured H = J^T W J [3].

```python
# Pseudocode: one Levenberg-Marquardt iteration on the factor graph
def lm_step(X, factors, lam):
    r, J = linearize(X, factors)          # residuals, Jacobian blocks
    H = J.T @ W @ J;  b = -J.T @ W @ r    # normal equations
    H = manifold_project(H)               # SE(3) tangent-space reduction
    dx = solve_sparse(H + lam*I, b)       # CHOLMOD / CSparse via g2o
    X_new = retract(X, dx)                # T <- T * Exp(dxi)
    rho = gain_ratio(X, X_new)            # actual vs predicted reduction
    return (X_new, lam*0.2) if rho > 0.75 else (X, lam*2.0)
```

### 3.3 Robustification

Outliers from mismatched ORB features are endemic. Two mechanisms protect the estimator:

1. **Robust kernels** — the Huber cost rho(e^2) downweights large residuals; g2o reweights iteratively (iteratively reweighted least squares), so correspondences beyond a chi-squared gate contribute linearly rather than quadratically [1][3].
2. **RANSAC geometric verification** — in loop closure and relocalization, DBoW2 candidates are filtered by PnP or Sim(3) RANSAC before entering the graph [5][1].

## 4. Deep Technical Dive

### 4.1 Pillar I — ORB Feature Tracking

ORB-SLAM3 extracts ORB features at 8 scale levels (scale factor 1.2) with FAST corners oriented by intensity centroid. Tracking proceeds in stages:

- **Motion-model prediction:** the previous pose propagates through a constant-velocity model; map points are projected into the current frame and matched in a radius proportional to their covariance.
- **Reference-keyframe tracking:** on failure, matching falls back to the last keyframe with a wider search.
- **Local-map tracking:** matches against the covisible local map, then optimizes only the current pose with all other variables fixed (motion-only BA) — a tiny factor graph solved at frame rate [1].
- **Keyframe insertion:** new keyframes are spawned when the visual change exceeds a threshold, then triangulated and culled by the local-mapping thread.

The **covisibility graph** — keyframes linked by shared map-point observations — is the backbone data structure: local bundle adjustment optimizes the current keyframe, its covisible neighbors, and all observed map points, with fixed "anchor" keyframes contributing measurements [1].

| Tracking stage | Variables optimized | Typical rate |
|---|---|---|
| Motion-only BA | single pose in SE(3) | frame rate (~30 Hz) |
| Local BA | covisible keyframes + observed points | keyframe rate |
| Full BA | all keyframes + all points | on loop closure / demand |

### 4.2 Pillar II — Bundle Adjustment via g2o

In ORB-SLAM3, every heavy optimization is delegated to g2o [3]:

- **Pose-graph optimization** after loop closure corrects drift using Sim(3) constraints between the loop keyframe and its covisible cluster.
- **Local bundle adjustment** refines poses and structure in the covisible window.
- **Full bundle adjustment** runs in a background thread after a loop is closed or maps are merged, optimizing *all* keyframes and map points.

The landmark–pose bipartite structure yields the classic block form:

[ H_pp  H_pl ] [ dx_p ]   [ b_p ]
[ H_lp  H_ll ] [ dx_l ] = [ b_l ]

and g2o exploits the **Schur complement** S = H_pp - H_pl H_ll^{-1} H_lp to marginalize landmarks, reducing the system to pose variables before back-substitution. Because H_ll is block-diagonal (each landmark observed independently given poses), its inversion is O(M) — the celebrated sparsity that makes bundle adjustment tractable [3][4].

> **Theorem:** *For a landmark-pose factor graph with N poses and M landmarks, Schur-complement marginalization reduces the linear solve from O((N+M)^3) to O(N^3 + M), which is the asymptotic basis of real-time bundle adjustment.*

### 4.3 Pillar III — Loop Closure Detection

ORB-SLAM3's place recognition follows [5] with an important upgrade [1]:

1. **BoW query:** the keyframe's ORB descriptors are quantized into a visual-word vector **v**_t; candidates minimize a normalized score subject to covisibility exclusion.
2. **Temporal consistency:** candidates must appear in connected keyframe groups to suppress perceptual aliasing.
3. **Geometric verification:** ORB correspondences are established between the query and candidate; a **Sim(3)** transformation is estimated with RANSAC (handling scale drift, the bane of monocular SLAM), then refined by guided matching; finally a pose-graph optimization distributes the loop error [1].
4. **Map merging:** in multi-map mode, a verified loop between maps triggers alignment, duplicate-point fusion, and a full BA — the mechanism by which ORB-SLAM3 "reuses all previous information" [1].

False positives are the catastrophic case: a single wrong loop edge can collapse the trajectory. The BoW + RANSAC + consistency cascade exists precisely because graph optimization cannot recover from a confidently wrong constraint [5].

### 4.4 Pillar IV — DROID-SLAM: Recurrent Updates and Differentiable Dense Bundle Adjustment

DROID-SLAM [2] discards the sparse pipeline's feature extraction, matching, and RANSAC. Instead:

1. **Feature and context networks** encode each frame; a 4D correlation pyramid over *all pixel pairs* (the RAFT construction [6]) captures dense correspondence evidence.
2. **Update operator:** a ConvGRU recurrently predicts flow revisions Delta **u** and confidence weights w_{ij}, supervised during training on synthetic (TartanAir) and real video.
3. **Dense Bundle Adjustment layer:** given revised correspondences **u***_{ij} = **u**_{ij} + Delta **u**_{ij} and per-pixel depths d_i, the layer minimizes

E(**T**, **d**) = sum_{(i,j) in E} || **u***_{ij} - pi( **T**_{ij} o pi^{-1}(**u**_i, d_i) ) ||^2_{Sigma_{ij}},

where Sigma_{ij} = diag(w_{ij}) are *learned* confidences. The DBA layer is differentiable: gradients flow through the Gauss–Newton solve via implicit differentiation, so the entire system — features, confidences, and geometry — is trained end-to-end [2].

The dense structure admits an analogous Schur trick: depth variables per frame form block-diagonal structure, so the DBA layer marginalizes pixelwise depths and solves only for poses in the inner loop — tens of thousands of correspondences reduced to a compact pose system at each iteration [2].

### 4.5 Robust Nonlinear Optimization: Classical vs. Learned

The two systems embody two philosophies of robustness:

- **ORB-SLAM3 (classical):** robustness is *engineered* — Huber kernels, chi-squared outlier gates, RANSAC, covisibility culling, and temporal consistency checks. Each is interpretable and tunable, but brittle under domain shift (motion blur, low texture) [1].
- **DROID-SLAM (learned):** robustness is *learned* — the network predicts confidence weights that downweight occlusions, specularities, and dynamic objects automatically, and it generalizes across datasets (trained on monocular video, deployed on stereo/RGB-D) [2].

Both, however, terminate in the same mathematics: iterated linearization of a sparse nonlinear least-squares objective on *SE(3)* [3][4].

## 5. Empirical Results and Formal Arguments

### 5.1 Reported Performance

| System | Dataset | Metric | Reported result |
|---|---|---|---|
| ORB-SLAM3 stereo-inertial | EuRoC | RMSE ATE | ~3.6 cm average [1] |
| ORB-SLAM3 stereo-inertial | TUM-VI (room) | RMSE ATE | ~9 mm [1] |
| ORB-SLAM3 | vs. prior art | accuracy gain | 2-5x over previous approaches [1] |
| DROID-SLAM (mono) | TartanAir/EuRoC/TUM | failure rate | substantially fewer catastrophic failures than baselines [2] |
| RAFT (backbone) | KITTI flow | F1-all | 5.10% [6] |
| RAFT (backbone) | Sintel final | EPE | 2.855 px [6] |

### 5.2 Formal Argument: Why Full BA Beats Filtering

> **Proposition:** *Filtering marginalizes out past poses, permanently folding linearization error into the prior; full (or local) bundle adjustment relinearizes, so its fixed points are stationary points of the true MAP objective, while filter estimates drift from the MAP solution as linearization points age.*

This is the theoretical core of ORB-SLAM3's design decision to reuse *all* previous information — including temporally distant but covisible keyframes — in bundle adjustment [1], and of DROID-SLAM's choice to optimize a dense window rather than filter it [2].

### 5.3 Formal Argument: Observability and Gauge Freedom

Monocular SLAM is unobservable up to a 7-DoF similarity transform (6 pose + 1 scale). In the factor graph this appears as a rank-7 nullspace of H [4]. ORB-SLAM3 handles scale via IMU (metric observability) or stereo/RGB-D; during loop closure it estimates an explicit Sim(3) correction [1]. Gauge freedom must be fixed (e.g., anchoring the first keyframe) or the normal equations are singular — g2o addresses this through fixed vertices [3].

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

1. **Perceptual aliasing and dynamic scenes.** BoW loop closure assumes a static world; moving objects corrupt both sparse matching and learned confidence maps. Neither system handles highly dynamic environments robustly [1][2].
2. **Scale and initialization fragility.** Monocular ORB-SLAM3 requires sufficient parallax for initialization; visual-inertial MAP initialization [1] improves this but needs informative motion. DROID-SLAM needs a warm-up frame context before its DBA layer stabilizes [2].
3. **Compute asymmetry.** ORB-SLAM3 runs on a CPU in real time; DROID-SLAM demands an 11 GB+ GPU for inference and multi-GPU hardware for training [2]. Deployment on embedded platforms remains open.
4. **Long-term map maintenance.** The Atlas [1] merges maps but does not bound their growth; lifelong SLAM with bounded memory and principled forgetting is unsolved.
5. **Theoretical gaps.** Convergence guarantees for the learned DBA layer's recurrent updates are empirical; unlike classical LM with trust regions [3], no global convergence proof exists for the end-to-end trained loop [2].
6. **Calibration sensitivity.** Both systems assume known intrinsics (or estimate them coarsely); online self-calibration within the factor graph is only partially addressed.

## 7. Conclusion

Factor graphs turned SLAM from a filtering problem into an optimization problem, and everything since — g2o's manifold-aware solvers [3], DBoW2's place recognition [5], ORB-SLAM3's MAP visual-inertial Atlas [1], RAFT's recurrent correspondence [6], and DROID-SLAM's differentiable dense bundle adjustment [2] — has been an elaboration of that insight. The sparse classical pipeline offers interpretability, CPU real-time performance, and centimeter accuracy; the dense learned pipeline offers robustness through learned confidences and freedom from handcrafted features at the cost of GPU dependence. Their shared mathematical core — iterated sparse linearization on *SE(3)* — suggests the field's future lies in hybrid systems: learned front-ends proposing factors, classical back-ends certifying them.
