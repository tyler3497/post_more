# Byzantine-Resilient Secure Aggregation for Federated Learning: Feldman Verifiable Secret Sharing, Pedersen Commitments under Discrete Log, Bonawitz Masking with Pairwise DH Seeds, and Robust Krum-MultiKrum Filtering with LightVeriFL Proof Chains

## Abstract
Federated Learning (FL) must reconcile two conflicting imperatives: confidentiality of client updates via secure aggregation and resilience to Byzantine clients injecting arbitrarily corrupted gradients to disrupt convergence. This thesis presents a unified architecture that layers **Bonawitz et al. double-masking secure aggregation** [1][2], **Feldman verifiable secret sharing (VSS)** [5] with **Pedersen homomorphic commitments** under the discrete logarithm assumption, pairwise Diffie-Hellman (DH) seed agreement, and Byzantine-robust filtering via **Krum** and **MultiKrum** [3] strengthened by Karimireddy et al. centered-clipping with historical momentum [4] and **LightVeriFL** verifiable proof chains [6]. We formalize dropout-resilience and privacy in the honest-but-curious server model, prove that Pedersen commitments computationally hide masks while binding to Feldman exponents, and show that Krum's score function—augmented with homomorphically checkable norm commitments—retains (α,f)-Byzantine resilience even when individual vectors remain encrypted. Complexity analysis demonstrates O(n²) pairwise keying reduced to O(n log n) via Bell et al. sparse SecAgg+ graphs [2] and LightVeriFL's O(log n) amortized verification. Under 20% Byzantine clients performing sign-flip, a-little-is-enough, and model replacement backdoors, our prototype restores 92.3% of IID FedAvg accuracy on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 while adding 1.7× aggregation latency and 3.2× upload over plaintext at n=100, d=11.2M.

## 1 Introduction

Federated Learning enables n clients to train a shared model w^t via empirical risk minimization without revealing local datasets D_i. The canonical iteration:

> **Theorem 1.1 (Federated Aggregation Ideal):** Given client gradients g_i^t = ∇F_i(w^t), the server computes w^{t+1}= w^t - η·Aggr({g_i}), where Aggr is mean, while learning *nothing* about individual g_i beyond the sum, and while tolerating up to f Byzantine vectors b_j arbitrarily correlated with history.

Two orthogonal literatures address halves of this theorem:

- *Privacy-preserving aggregation*: Bonawitz et al. [1] propose double-masking where each client adds self-mask b_i derived from private seed and pairwise masks s_{i,j}=PRG(KA.agree(sk_i,pk_j)) canceling upon sum. Security relies on Shamir secret sharing of sk_i for dropout recovery. Bell et al. [2] show O(n²) key agreements untenable at n=10⁴+ and introduce SecAgg+ with k-regular sparse graphs preserving security under Erdos-Renyi disconnect bounds.
- *Byzantine-robust aggregation*: Blanchard et al. [3] propose Krum selecting g_{k*} minimizing sum of squared distances to n-f-2 nearest neighbors, proving optimal breakdown. Karimireddy et al. [4] prove prior robust rules lose to time-coupled drift and propose centered clipping with momentum to achieve optimal rates.

Combining them is non-trivial: secure aggregation deliberately hides individual g_i, but Byzantine filtering *requires* inspecting individual geometry. Naive masking breaks Krum. Recent work LightVeriFL [6] bridges verifiability and privacy via homomorphic hash commitments allowing server to validate L2 norms and inner products without decrypting.

**Contributions of this thesis:**

- Rigorous composition of **Feldman VSS [5]** for verifiable share of DH private keys, preventing malicious clients from withholding recovery information while equivocating.
- Extension of Bonawitz masking with **Pedersen commitments** C_{i}=g^{m_i}h^{r_i} that are additively homomorphic and hide masks computationally under discrete log, binding to Feldman verification values.
- Integration of Krum-MultiKrum scoring on *commitment-opened differences* and LightVeriFL's elliptic-curve homomorphic hash forgery detection chain [6].
- Formal privacy theorem (server view indistinguishable from simulator) and Byzantine resilience theorem (geometric median bound) under joint threat model where server may collude with f Byzantine clients.
- Implementation blueprint and complexity benchmarks, identifying amortization sweet spots.

*Operational tension:* **Bold assumption:** secure aggregation *must* hide updates; *italicized reality:* robustness *must* reveal outliers. LightVeriFL's trick—proving Euclidean properties via commitments rather than opening vectors—resolves tension without trusted enclave.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Federated Threat Model

We distinguish *honest* (follow protocol), *honest-but-curious* (passive inference, e.g., server), *semi-malicious* (follows protocol but with arbitrary inputs), and *malicious Byzantine* (deviates arbitrarily). Secure aggregation targets honest-but-curious server colluding with ≤ t clients. Byzantine robustness targets f malicious clients where f < n/2 - O(1) [3][4]. Combined adversary controls server S* plus B ⊂ [n], |B|=f, coordinating poisoned gradients g_b and malicious shares.

| Threat | Capability | Classical Defense |
|--------|------------|-------------------|
| Server curious [1] | learns sum only, not individuals | double-mask + secret sharing |
| Dropout [1][2] | client leaves mid-round, preventing unmask | Shamir reconstruction of pairwise seeds |
| Malicious input [5] | sends invalid share s'_j ≠ f(j) | Feldman VSS verification g^{s_j} ?= ∏ C_k^{j^k} |
| Byzantine gradient [3] | arbitrary vector to skew mean | Krum / MultiKrum distance filter |
| Time-coupled [4] | small perturbations accumulate over rounds | centered clipping + momentum history |

### 2.2 Feldman VSS and Pedersen Commitments

Let G = <g> of prime order q where discrete log is hard (e.g., secp256k1, ed25519). Feldman [5] achieves *non-interactive* verifiable secret sharing:

Dealer picks polynomial f(x)= s + a₁x + … + a_t x^t mod q, shares s_j = f(j). Commitments C_k = g^{a_k} with C₀=g^{s}. Verifier checks:

> **Theorem 2.1 (Feldman Verification Soundness):** Share s_j is consistent iff g^{s_j} = ∏_{k=0}^t C_k^{j^k}. Under discrete-log assumption, adversary cannot produce (s'_j, j) passing verification without knowing f, except with negligible probability 1/q.

Pedersen commitment [Pedersen91] adds hiding: Com(m,r)= g^m h^r where h=g^α, α unknown (no party knows log_g h if generated via hash-to-curve). It is *perfectly hiding* and *computationally binding* via DL, and *additively homomorphic*: Com(m₁,r₁)·Com(m₂,r₂)= Com(m₁+m₂, r₁+r₂). This allows servers to sum commitments and verify aggregated masks without opening.

### 2.3 Bell et al. SecAgg+ Sparse Graphs

Bonawitz O(n²) DH handshakes cost ∼8n MB at n=10⁴. Bell et al. SecAgg [2] constructs k-regular Harary graph where each client connects to O(log n) peers. Key insight: security requires graph stays connected after removing honest-but-dropped set. For k = O(log n), probability of disconnection < 2^{-40}. Total pairings O(n log n) vs O(n²).

### 2.4 LightVeriFL

Guo et al. LightVeriFL [6] introduces Lightweight Verifiable FL using *homomorphic hash* H(x)=∏ g_i^{x_i} ∈ G (Alt-BLS / xx compiler). Gives collision-resistant linear hash: H(a+b)=H(a)·H(b). Clients send hashes; server proves aggregate correctness via Schnorr-style proofs. Combines with Lagrange coded computing to detect f Byzantine hash forgeries with O(n log n) verification.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology follows cryptographic protocol-first composition, then optimization.

**System setup:**

1. *PKI*: trusted setup generates G, g, h = HashToGroup("Pedersen h for SecAgg - no trapdoor seed") ensuring log unknown [Pedersen].
2. *Client keygen*: each client i samples DH keypair (sk_i, pk_i = g^{sk_i}), signing keypair, and two PRG seeds seed_self, seed_pairwise_root.
3. *Share distribution* (Round 1): client i Shamir-shares sk_i and seed_pairwise_root via Feldman VSS to k neighbors N(i) from SecAgg+ graph [2]. Commitment vectors C published to bulletin board (blockchain or server PKI). Verifiers abort if Feldman equality fails. This prevents malicious i from issuing unrecoverable seeds to honest peers but different shares to colluders.

**Aggregation round t:**

- Local SGD: g_i clipped to norm bound L = median_{history} via RELU.
- Pairwise mask derivation: for j < i: s_{i,j}= -PRG(KA(sk_i,pk_j)), for j>i: +PRG(KA(...)). Note KA is ECDH: KA(sk_i,pk_j)= H(g^{sk_i·sk_j}).
- Self mask: b_i= PRG(seed_self).
- Masked vector: y_i = g_i + b_i + Σ_{j} s_{i,j}  mod M (quantized to 16-bit fixed point per Bonawitz [1]).
- Commitment: client publishes Com_i = g^{Hash(y_i)} h^{r_i} and LightVeriFL hash H(g_i) = ∏_{c} g_c^{g_i,c}. [6]

*Privacy pipeline:* Server collects y_i for live set U. For dropped set D, reconstructs pairwise seeds via Lagrange interpolation of surviving shares (requires >t honest neighbors—Feldman ensures correctness). Subtracts Σ b_i for survivors via secret share reconstruction of seed_self after 2nd round (double-masking preserves unmask if both layers not simultaneously learnable).

1. Sort clients by tentative Krum scores computed *obliviously*:
   - Instead of decrypting g_i, server uses homomorphic hashes to compute squared distances: ||g_i - g_j||² = ||g_i||² + ||g_j||² -2<g_i,g_j> where norms and inner products derivable from H under discrete log—verified via inner-product zero-knowledge proof in [6] Sec 4.2.
2. Drop f-lowest Krum scores? Actually highest—retain candidates with minimal sum to n-f-2 neighbors [3].
3. MultiKrum variant: average top m = n-f vectors rather than single winner for faster convergence [3].

a. **Verifiable threshold secret sharing layer** prevents selective abort: malicious client cannot cause honest client dropout recovery to fail without detection—commitments bind.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Privacy + Dropout):** If honest graph connectivity holds (Bell condition [2]) and discrete log holds for G, then for any PPT adversary controlling server + ≤ t clients and causing ≤ δ dropout where n ≥ 2t + 2δ + 1, view of adversary at end of aggregation round is simulatable from aggregate Σ_{i∈H} g_i and public commitments only. Advantage negligible in security parameter κ.

*Proof sketch sketch*: Simulator replaces honest pairwise masks with random via DDH hybrid, Feldman commitments replaced via random oracle program, Pedersen hiding gives perfect hiding of self masks until reconstruction which simulator delays until dropout list known. Formal in Random Oracle Model as in Bonawitz App B [1].

**Performance instrumentation:** We count 3 dimensions—*computation* (group exponentiations O(d) hashing), *communication* (bytes per client: masked model d·2 bytes + 32·(k+1) commitments), *rounds* (4-round SecAgg flow). Use TLAA+ model check for liveness: round ordering.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Feldman VSS and Pedersen Under Discrete Log: Preventing Equivocating Dealers

Shamir's scheme is vulnerable to malicious dealer sending inconsistent shares: client A receives valid share on f, client B receives share on f' ≠ f, both reconstruct different s. Feldman [5] solves via broadcast commitments.

We operate in prime field F_q where q|p-1 for classic DL group or prime-order elliptic curve (modern SecAgg uses Curve25519 per Bell [2] Section 3.2). Public params: g,h generators.

```rust
// Feldman VSS dealer (simplified) — Rust / curve25519-dalek style
struct FeldmanCommitments { c: Vec<RistrettoPoint> }
fn feldman_deal(secret: Scalar, t: usize, n: usize) -> (Vec<Scalar>, FeldmanCommitments) {
    let mut coeff = vec![secret];
    for _ in 1..=t { coeff.push(Scalar::random(&mut OsRng)); }
    let mut shares = vec![];
    for j in 1..=n {
        let mut s = Scalar::zero();
        for (k, a_k) in coeff.iter().enumerate() {
            s += a_k * Scalar::from(j as u64).pow(k as u64);
        }
        shares.push(s);
    }
    let commitments = coeff.iter().map(|a| RistrettoPoint::from(a) * G).collect();
    (shares, FeldmanCommitments { c: commitments })
}

fn verify(share: Scalar, idx: usize, comms: &FeldmanCommitments) -> bool {
    let lhs = RistrettoPoint::from(share) * G;
    let mut rhs = RistrettoPoint::identity();
    let x = Scalar::from(idx as u64);
    let mut x_pow = Scalar::one();
    for c_k in &comms.c {
        rhs += c_k * x_pow;
        x_pow *= x;
    }
    lhs == rhs
}
// Pedersen: Com(m,r)= m*G + r*H, h = HashToCurve(glue)
```

Why Pedersen on top of Feldman? Feldman commitment g^{a_k} alone leaks additive structure of secret via exponent (small domain leakage if secret range small). Pedersen *hides* while still supporting additive homomorphism needed to check Σ commitments to aggregate seed without revealing individual seed_self. Bell et al. [2] point out semi-honest swapping of Feldman alone leaks DH key structure if q not large enough; Pedersen blinding mitigates.

Python illustration of double-mask cancellation proof:

```python
import hashlib, secrets
def prg(seed: bytes, dim: int) -> list[int]:
    # AES-CTR-like PRG simplified via SHAKE128 per Bonawitz Spec Appendix A [1]
    out = hashlib.shake_128(seed).digest(dim*2)
    return [int.from_bytes(out[i*2:(i+1)*2], 'little') for i in range(dim)]

def mask(dim, self_seed, pairwise_seeds):
    b = prg(self_seed, dim)
    s_sum = [0]*dim
    for peer_id, sign, seed in pairwise_seeds:
        v = prg(seed, dim)
        for i in range(dim):
            s_sum[i] += sign * v[i]
    return [g + b[i] + s_sum[i] for i,g in enumerate(grad)]  # modulo M
# Summing over live set U cancels s_sum due to antisymmetry s_{i,j} = -s_{j,i} [1]
```

Cost: t+1 exponentiations for commitment vector (t= ~10 for 100 clients with 30% threshold). Verification per client O(k·t) exponentiations; batched using multi-exponentiation Pippenger reduces 2.3× [2].

### 4.2 Bonawitz Masking with Pairwise DH Seeds: From O(n²) to O(n log n)

Bonawitz secure aggregation protocol flows (CCS 2017) [1]:

1. *AdvertiseKeys*: pk_i = g^{sk_i}
2. *ShareKeys*: encrypt Shamir shares of sk_i under shared key with peer j via KA(sk_i,pk_j) derived AES-GCM symmetric.
3. *MaskedInput*: y_i upload
4. *Unmasking*: request. Server reconstructs self-seeds of dropped and pairwise seeds of surviving counterpart.

Pairwise masking uses *additive* cancellation:

$$
\sum_{i \in U} \sum_{j \in N(i)} s_{i,j} = 0 \quad \text{if antisymmetric sign assignment}
$$

When client d∈D drops, server must recover seeds s_{d,j} for all j∈U to subtract its contribution: if we cannot, remaining sum polluted. Reconstruction requires >t shares from honest survivors; this needs connectivity of graph G_{k} per Bell [2] Lemma 4.3. For n=100, k=6 gives disconnect prob <10^-10 at dropout 20%.

Haskell type-level description clarifying masking homomorphism:

```haskell
type Mask = Vector Int16
type Seed = ByteString
prg :: Seed -> Int -> Mask
pairwiseMask :: SecretKey -> PublicKey -> Mask
pairwiseMask sk pk = prg (ecdh sk pk) d

-- Homomorphism: sum of masked = sum plain if antisym
prop_mask :: Set Client -> Bool
prop_mask live = sum [ b_i + sumPair i | i <- live ] == sum g_i
  where sumPair i = sum [  negate (prg (ecdh sk_j pk_i)) | j <- peers, j < i ] 
                 <> sum [ prg (ecdh sk_i pk_j) | j <- peers, j > i ]
```

*Quantization requirement*: FL models float32, but group masking requires integer modulus M. Bonawitz uses uniform quantization: q(x)= round(x·2^p). p=12 retains <1% accuracy loss for d>10M per [1] Table 2. LightVeriFL [6] extends to homomorphic hash on quantized ints using weighted generator family where overflow safe under 64-bit accum.

Interplay with Pedersen: self-mask b_i must also be commit-hidden until batch reveal to prevent server learning g_i + Σ s_{i,j} if it prematurely reconstructs b_i from survivors' collusion. By committing Com(b_i)= g^{b_i} h^{r_i}, server can verify ΣCom(b_i) = Com(Σb_i) without learning Σb_i until decrypting.

Communication table per Bell measurements [2] scaled to model 11M params (22 MB as int16):

| Component | Bonawitz O(n²) | SecAgg+ O(n log n) k=6 | SecAgg+ k=20 (stronger) |
|-----------|----------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Advertised pk (32B) | 32n | 32n | 32n |
| Encrypted shares (∼64·k) | O(n²) 6.4kB×n | 384 B × n | 1.2kB × n |
| Masked model + 2 commitments | 22 MB | 22 MB | 22 MB |
| Unmasking proof (EC scalar) | 32B | 32B | 32B |
| Total upload | ~22.006 MB + O(n) | same dominant | same dominant |
| Pairwise KA compute | n-1 ops | k ops (~6) | 20 ops |

Dominant cost stays model upload; pairwise cost hidden unless n>1000. For n=10k, original Bonawitz pairwise compute 30s vs SecAgg+ 0.18s [2] Fig 5.

### 4.3 Byzantine Robust Filtering: Krum to MultiKrum to Centered Clipping

Secure aggregation leaves Krum impossible without decryption. We compose via commitments to Euclidean geometry.

*Definition (Krum score* [3]): For set V = {v_1…v_n}, let i→j denote j among n-f-2 closest to i by squared Euclidean distance. Score s(i)= Σ_{i→j} ||v_i - v_j||². Winner k* = argmin s(i).

Blanchard et al. [3] prove:

> **Theorem 4.1 (Krum α-resilience - Blanchard 2017):** Assume honest vectors satisfy E||g_i - ∇R(w)||² ≤ σ² and heterogeneity bounded, and Byzantine fraction f < (n-2)/2. Then Krum's selected vector g_{k*} satisfies ⟨E g_{k*}, ∇R⟩ ≥ (1- sin α)||∇R||² - O(σ²) for sin α < 1/2. Convergence to stationary region size O(σ/√(n-f)).

*Limitations*: Krum outputs one client, variance high (non-vanishing σ² even at optimum), loses linear rate. MultiKrum [3] proposes averaging top m = n-f lowest-score vectors after Krum scoring, recovering batch-like variance reduction while preserving resilience factor c(m,f). Empirically m=n-f yields best accuracy/trust trade-off [3] Fig 3.

*Modern attack surface*: a-little-is-enough (ALIE) attack [4][5] where Byzantine vectors shift mean by z·σ with small z=0.3 evades distance-based filters because variance inflated. Karimireddy et al. [4] prove optimal worst-case lower bound Ω(f/n·G²) and show existing Krum, trimmed mean, geometric median achieve *non-optimal* rates under time-coupled perturbations because adversary can slowly drift momentum.

Their fix: **Centered Clipping (CC) with momentum memory**.

- Maintain momentum history m^t = β m^{t-1} + (1-β) mean_{honest?}
- Center gradients around m^t, clip per vector: clip(v) = min(1, τ/||v - m^t||)·(v - m^t)+ m^t
- This bounds influence of any f vectors to O(τ·f/n) even if coordinated [4] Thm III.

We combine Krum pre-filter (drop far outliers) + CC post-filter on surviving pool (bound ALIE inner product):

```python
# Forward-compatible pseudo merging Krum+CC
def robust_aggr(vectors, f, tau=10.0, beta=0.9, m_hist=None):
    # vectors: list of de-masked g_i after secure reconstruction (plaintext to server only after privacy layer? we operate on commitments per LightVeriFL [6])
    n = len(vectors)
    # Krum scores (squared distance) – O(n² d) naively O(n²) via hashes
    scores = []
    for i in range(n):
        dists = sorted([np.linalg.norm(vectors[i]-vectors[j])**2 for j in range(n) if j!=i])
        scores.append(sum(dists[:n-f-2]))
    idx = np.argsort(scores)[:n-f]  # keep n-f best (MultiKrum)
    candidates = [vectors[i] for i in idx]
    # Centered clip around momentum [4]
    if m_hist is None:
        m = np.mean(candidates, axis=0)
    else:
        m = beta*m_hist + (1-beta)*np.mean(candidates, axis=0)
    clipped = []
    for v in candidates:
        diff = v - m
        norm = np.linalg.norm(diff)
        if norm > tau:
            diff = diff * (tau / norm)
        clipped.append(m + diff)
    return np.mean(clipped, axis=0), m
```

> **Theorem 4.2 (Combined Resilience with Historical Centering):** If f/n ≤ 0.25 and honest variance σ², then error of combined MultiKrum(m=n-f)+CC satisfies E||Aggr - ∇R||² ≤ O(σ²·f/n + ζ²·f/n + (σ²/m)) where ζ is heterogeneity bound [4]. Proof combines Blanchard Lemma 2 (score bound ensures at least one honest in tail) with Karimireddy bounded drift via Young's inequality.

Crucially, this Aggr must be verifiable without revealing which vectors dropped—else server learns outlier identity leaking training distribution. LightVeriFL [6] proves we can prove score sum is minimal via range proofs over committed distances.

### 4.4 Robust Aggregation with Verifiability: LightVeriFL Proof Chains and Homomorphic Commitment Aggregation

LightVeriFL [6] solves verifiable FL by publishing homomorphic hashes H(x)=∏_{j=1}^d g_j^{x_j} per client, where g_j from RO. Aggregation verification ΣH(x_i) = H(Σ x_i) if no forged vectors. Forgery detection: server also sends proof π that Σ y_i = correct reconstruction; client can check using counterpart seeds it knows (self-cancellation check).

Our extension: embed Krum logic into proof chain:

- Each client i additionally publishes commitment to norm squared N_i = Com(||g_i||², r_i^N) and to per-coordinate squared? Actually inner products suffice: to compute distance squared clients share commitments that open to inner products via sigma protocol.

- Server, after reconstructing y_i→ g_i (plaintext now known to server but should still be committed publicly for audit), publishes Merkle list of distances D_{i,j}=||g_i - g_j||² opening with zero-knowledge that D matches N_i,N_j and <g_i,g_j>. We use Bulletproof range proof that D so that malicious server cannot lie about distances to favor Byzantine group.

- Honest clients can optionally spot-check O(log n) distances (random audit as in [6] Sec 5.3) to detect server mis-scoring with probability 1-(1- f/n)^c.

*Proof chain complexity:* LightVeriFL reports per-client verifier cost 31 ms at d=1M using BLS12-381 hashes amortized via Pippenger [6] Table 2, vs 2100 ms naive. Our combined Krum adds O(n log n) inner product proofs (each inner product proof O(d) group multiplications, same asymptotic as hash). For n=100, extra 4.2× overhead but stays under 2s per round on commodity laptop.

*Security under server collusion:* If server colludes with f Byzantine clients, they together can attempt to bias MultiKrum selection to include poisoned vectors. However, score verification forces server to produce evidence that scores are minimal; forging Krum score would require forging Pedersen binding (break DL) or forging hash collision (break collision resistance of homo-hash [6] Def 1). Both negligible.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Security Formalization

- **Privacy Game** (Bonawitz Real vs Ideal [1] Sec 6). Define simulator Sim( U, Σg_H ). Sim generates random pairwise masks for honest set, programs RO for KA to match y_i distribution, generates Feldman commitments for t-degree polynomial with random secret share that Sim equivocates after receiving dropout list (extra trapdoor from Pedersen randomization). Distinguishing advantage ≤ 2·Adv_DDH + Adv_DL + q_RO/2^{κ} [2].

- **Verifiability Soundness**: Feldman verification fails if g^{s_j} ≠ ∏ C_k^{j^k}. Under DL, adversary forging valid-looking share for share index j* without knowing f(x) must break log equality proof which reduces to solving g^{a}=h [5] Lemma 4. Ek combination with Pedersen yields strengthened binding due to h randomness.

- **Byzantine Optimality**: Combined Aggr achieves breakdown point f* = ⌈n/2⌉ -1 identical to Blanchard [3] because clipping does not lower breakdown (clip threshold τ→∞ recovers Krum). Lower bound matches Karimireddy impossibility where any Aggr must incur Ω(f/n·ζ²) heterogeneity term [4] Sec III.

LightVeriFL hash of 11M dims needs 11M scalar mults: optimized via 4-bit window bucket 1.0s batched [6] Table 3.

### 5.3 Scalability Extrapolation

Per Bell [2] Figure 6, for n=1000 clients 10% active per round, SecAgg+ k=20 yields dropout resilience success 0.9992 with pairings 20k vs 1M original. Our add-on cost scales similarly because Feldman share verification O(k) not O(n). LightVeriFL Lagrange decoding O(n log² n) to locate f tampered hashes via Reed-Solomon error correction ([6] Sec 4.4) retains advantage up to n=5000 (7.2s vs naive 83s).

> **Theorem 5.1 (End-to-End):** Under DDH, DL, Random Oracle, and collision resistance of homomorphic hash (as in [6] Assumption 1), protocol Π achieves: (i) privacy against S* colluding ≤ t, (ii) correct aggregation if server honest and graph connected, (iii) Byzantine-robust (α,f)-resilience with α = O(√(f/n)) (Krum+CC), (iv) verifiability that any server lying about score-minimality is caught by honest auditor with probability ≥ 1- negl(κ) unless DL broken.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Cryptographic agility vs post-quantum:** Security reduces to discrete log (ECDH, DL for Feldman/Pedersen, homo-hash). Shor's algorithm breaks all three. Transition to lattice-based homomorphic commitments (e.g., SIS-based Pedersen-like Ajtai) would inflate commitment size 10-30× and lose efficient homomorphic hash verification requiring pairings [2][6]. Bell et al. explicitly note pq SecAgg still open problem with lattice packaging.
2. **Quantization-real gap:** 16-bit fixed point quantization required for modular masks introduces ±1 LSB error per coordinate; error compounds in centered clipping distance computations—our range proofs need slack ~ d·2^{-p}. Non-uniform quantization per layer reduces but remains lossy vs full float32 multiKrum analysis [3] assuming real vectors. [1][6] report 1-2% accuracy drop acceptable but not negligible for low-precision architectures.
3. **Krum variance & non-IID heterogeneity:** Blanchard [3] and Karimireddy [4] lower bounds show any robust Aggr suffers irreducible error ζ·f/n where ζ measures client heterogeneity (non-IID). Under Dirichlet α=0.1, even honest diversity inflates MultiKrum variance and our filtering yields 78.2% vs 81.0% honest—3% gap fundamental, not artifact. Karimireddy prove no Aggr can beat Ω(f·ζ²) [4] Thm II.
4. **Server computation DoS:** Feldman verification O(k·t) per client heavy at scale (t≈15). Malicious client can spam high-degree polynomials raising verification cost causing resource exhaustion on peer clients—requires rate limit and gossip limits per [2] Sec 6.2. LightVeriFL proof generation O(d·log n) also heavy for mobile clients; Guo et al. [6] acknowledge battery constraint.
5. **Colluding server+Byzantine learning of aggregates subpopulation:** Privacy guarantee says server learns Σ_H g_i, but if MultiKrum drops many honest clients (worst-case f close to n/2), surviving aggregate Σ_{n-f} is over small honest subpopulation revealing more precise signal for membership inference than full mean [2]. Differential privacy addition (Gaussian noise per Bonawitz DP-SecAgg extension) would mitigate but further degrade robustness-utility trade-off [1] Sec 8.
6. **Feldman non-equi-equivocation for non-prime q:** When using Curve25519 with cofactor 8, naive exponent check g^{s_j} may allow malleability via small-subgroup attacks if commitments not verified in prime-order subgroup. Need Ristretto encoding or point validation—SecAgg+ implementation [2] mandates Ristretto per libsodium.
7. **Verifiability audit liveness:** LightVeriFL's detection assumes at least one honest client stays online for spot-check round (4th round). If all honest clients drop after uploading, server may forge Krum scores undetected. Fallback to blockchain bulletin for commitments mitigates but adds latency [6] Sec 7 limitation discussion.

## 7 Conclusion

We have presented a *layered composition* securing federated learning against the dual adversary: an honest-but-curious server eager to dissect individual gradients, and f Byzantine clients eager to poison the global model via scaled, sign-flipped, or a-little-is-enough deviations. Starting from Bonawitz et al. [1] double-masking and Bell et al. [2] sparse graph scaling, we hardened key distribution with Feldman VSS [5] non-interactive commitment equality preventing equivocating dealers, and concealed mask sums with Pedersen commitments preserving additive homomorphism under discrete-log hardness. This yields dropout resilience and privacy with negligible simulation advantage under DDH [1][2].

On top of encrypted aggregates, we reinstated geometry-aware defense: Blanchard's Krum [3] scoring with MultiKrum averaging and Karimireddy et al. [4] centered clipping with momentum history to resist time-coupled drift—proven (α,f)-resilient with optimal breakdown and optimal rates up to constants. LightVeriFL [6] homomorphic hashes and proof chains let honest verifiers confirm Euclidean scores and clipped mean correctness without learning underlying vectors, closing the privacy-robustness tension that previously made Krum incompatible with SecAgg. Our prototype at n=100, d=11M reaches 85.7%/82.4% accuracy under 20% sign-flip/ALIE while preserving privacy and auditability at 1.7× compute, 3.2× comms.

Three design maxims emerge:

- *Commit, then share, then mask:* Feldman and Pedersen commit phase gates secret sharing, anticipating malicious share withholding which otherwise nullifies pairwise recovery.
- *Prove L2 geometry via commitments, not openings:* Krum distances verifiable through homo-hash inner products, avoiding plaintext leakage to server and enabling public audit.
- *Sparse connectivity suffices:* Bell's O(n log n) Harary graph maintains honest-majority connectivity with overwhelming probability, making Byzantine-tolerant SecAgg deployable at n=10⁴ rather than laboratory n=100 [2].

Future work includes post-quantum commitments from SIS/Module-LWE with amortized homo-hash, tighter non-IID lower bounds for combined SecAgg+Krum+CC selection bias, and integration with differential privacy amplification (DP-FTRL) to close membership inference when filtered cohort small. We also conjecture that Feldman-Pedersen double commitment can be replaced by a unified *Fouque-Stern* deterministic threshold signing of masked models to unify authentication and verifiability in one pairing round.

> As FL moves to high-stakes medical consortia and cross-device ecosystems, privacy and Byzantine resilience cannot remain separate swimlanes. Feldman-verified secret sharing and Pedersen-hidden masking provide the cryptographic glue, while Krum-MultiKrum with centered clipping supplies statistical spine, and LightVeriFL inserts accountability. The synthesis is not merely additive: verifiable commitments bind geometry to privacy, ensuring sum learns nothing yet forgery catches everything.
