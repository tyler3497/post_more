# Diffusion Probabilistic Models: Denoising Score Matching, Stochastic Differential Equations, and Sampling Guarantees

## Abstract

This thesis presents a unified treatment of diffusion probabilistic models across their three canonical formulations: denoising diffusion probabilistic models as hierarchical variational inference, score-based generative modeling through stochastic differential equations, and denoising score matching with annealed Langevin dynamics. We derive the forward Gaussian noising chain and its learned reverse, establish the equivalence between the reweighted variational bound of Ho et al. and denoising score matching via Tweedie's formula, and pass to the continuous-time SDE perspective in which variance-preserving and variance-exploding processes emerge as scaling limits of discrete schedules. Sampling guarantees are analyzed rigorously, including weak convergence of DDPM sampling to the data distribution under regularity conditions and deterministic probability-flow ODE integration. Empirical results on CIFAR-10, LSUN, and ImageNet are surveyed alongside likelihood-based refinements, and fundamental limitations — sampling cost, likelihood-sample-quality tradeoffs, and theoretical gaps — are assessed.

##1 Introduction

The central problem of *unsupervised generative modeling* is to learn a distribution $p_{\theta}(x)$ that approximates an unknown data distribution $p_{data}(x)$ over a high-dimensional space, in a way that permits both tractable evaluation and efficient sampling. For decades, this problem was partitioned among four model families — autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, normalizing flows, and generative adversarial networks — each accepting a different compromise between **likelihood tractability**, **sample quality**, and **sampling speed** [1][2][5].

Beginning in 2015 with the diffusion probabilistic models of Sohl-Dickstein et al. [2], and accelerating after the denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) of Ho et al. [1] and the SDE unification of Song et al. [3], a fifth family has emerged that dominates the empirical frontier: corrupt data with Gaussian noise through a fixed forward process, then learn to reverse it one denoising step at a time.

This thesis makes four contributions:

- **A unified derivation** of the DDPM variational bound, its reweighting, and its exact equivalence to denoising score matching [6], proved through Tweedie's formula and the tractability of the forward-process posterior.
- **A continuous-time account** of the score-based SDE framework [3], showing how variance-preserving, variance-exploding, and sub-VP SDEs arise as scaling limits of discrete schedules, and how Anderson's reverse-time SDE theorem reduces sampling to score estimation.
- **A rigorous treatment of sampling guarantees**, including weak convergence of the DDPM sampler to the data distribution as the number of steps grows [9], deterministic sampling via the probability-flow ODE [3][4], and non-Markovian acceleration through DDIM [7].
- **An empirical survey** of likelihoods and sample-quality metrics across CIFAR-10, LSUN, and ImageNet, together with an honest assessment of limitations: sampling cost, the likelihood–fidelity tradeoff, and gaps between theory and practice.

> Theorem: The DDPM simplified objective, denoising score matching at multiple noise scales, and the score-based SDE objective are — up to weighting and reparametrization — the *same* optimization problem. All three learn the Stein score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ of noise-perturbed data distributions.

##2 Background

### Score matching and the Stein score

Score matching, introduced by Hyvärinen, sidesteps the intractable partition function of energy-based models by matching the **score** $\nabla_x \log p_{\theta}(x)$ rather than the density itself. Because the score is the gradient of the log-density, it never references the normalization constant, and it suffices for sampling via Langevin dynamics. Vincent [6] showed the pivotal *denoising* variant: matching the score of data corrupted with Gaussian noise reduces to a simple regression target, $-(\tilde{x} - x)/\sigma^2$, which is directly computable from samples. This identity — learnable scores from pure supervision — is the seed from which all diffusion models grow.

### Nonequilibrium thermodynamics

Sohl-Dickstein et al. [2] reframed generation as a physical process: slowly destroy structure in data through an iterative forward diffusion, then learn a reverse diffusion that restores it, analogous to running a thermodynamic process backward. Their model trained a deep network to parametrize the mean and covariance of each reverse step, maximizing a variational lower bound. The framework was elegant but empirically weak by modern standards; the missing ingredients were the *noise-prediction reparametrization* and the *weighted bound* introduced five years later [1].

### Noise-conditional score networks

Song and Ermon [5] scaled score matching to high-resolution images with Noise Conditional Score Networks (NCSNs): a single network conditioned on the noise scale, trained by denoising score matching across a geometric ladder of noise levels, with sampling by *annealed* Langevin dynamics — running Langevin at each noise level in turn, from large noise down to small. NCSN achieved image quality competitive with GANs but sampled slowly and scored a poor FID of 25.3 on CIFAR-10, exposing the need for better discretizations and objectives [3].

The landscape before DDPM can be summarized as follows:

| Family | Training stability | Likelihood tractable | Sampling speed | CIFAR-10 FID (era) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GANs | Fragile | No | Fast | ~8–20 |
| VAEs | Stable | Bound | Fast | ~40+ |
| Autoregressive | Stable | Exact | Very slow | ~60+ |
| NCSN [5] | Stable | No | Very slow | 25.3 |
| DDPM [1] | Stable | Bound | Slow | 3.17 |

Diffusion models inherited the stability of score matching and VAEs while closing the quality gap with GANs — and then surpassing them.

##3 Methodology

### The forward process

Fix a variance schedule $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$ with each $\beta_t$ a small positive scalar, and define the *forward* (noising) Markov chain [1][2]:

$$q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\; \beta_t I\right).$$

A crucial property of Gaussian chains is that every marginal is available in closed form. With $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} \alpha_s$:

$$q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0,\; (1-\bar{\alpha}_t) I\right), \qquad x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon,\; \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).$$

With a well-chosen schedule (e.g., $T = 1000$ with $\beta_t$ linearly increasing), $x_T$ is nearly pure Gaussian noise, independent of $x_0$. The forward process *destroys all information*; the model's entire job is to learn its reversal.

### The reverse process and the variational bound

The generative model is a learned Markov chain running backward:

$$p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_{t-1}; \mu_{\theta}(x_t, t),\; \Sigma_{\theta}(x_t, t)\right), \qquad p(x_T) = \mathcal{N}(0, I).$$

Training maximizes the evidence lower bound on $\log p_{\theta}(x_0)$, which decomposes into a sum of KL divergences between the tractable forward posterior $q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)$ — Gaussian with *known* mean $\tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0)$ and variance $\tilde{\beta}_t$ — and the learned reverse step [1][2]:

$$L = \mathbb{E}_q\left[D_{KL}(q(x_T \mid x_0) \,\|\, p(x_T)) + \sum_{t>1} D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0) \,\|\, p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)) - \log p_{\theta}(x_0 \mid x_1)\right].$$

### The noise-prediction reparametrization

Ho et al. [1] reparametrized $\mu_{\theta}$ to *predict the injected noise* $\epsilon$ rather than the posterior mean:

$$\mu_{\theta}(x_t, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\,\epsilon_{\theta}(x_t, t)\right).$$

Each KL term then becomes a weighted squared error between true and predicted noise. Discarding the weights — an empirical choice that dramatically improves sample quality at the cost of likelihood calibration — yields the celebrated *simplified objective*:

$$L_{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon}\left[\left\|\epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon,\; t)\right\|^2\right], \qquad t \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(1, \dots, T).$$

Training is therefore plain supervised regression: sample a timestep, corrupt the data, predict the corruption. The full algorithm is compact:

```python
# DDPM training (Ho et al., 2020)
for x0 in dataloader:                      # x0 ~ p_data
    t = torch.randint(1, T + 1, (B,))       # uniform timestep
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)              # injected noise
    xt = sqrt(alpha_bar[t]) * x0 + sqrt(1 - alpha_bar[t]) * eps
    loss = (eps - eps_theta(xt, t)).pow(2).mean()
    optimizer.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); optimizer.step()
```

Sampling runs the learned chain backward from pure noise, adding a calibrated amount of fresh noise at each step except the last:

1. Draw $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
2. For $t = T, \dots, 1$: set $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ if $t > 1$ else $z = 0$, and update
   $$x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\,\epsilon_{\theta}(x_t, t)\right) + \sigma_t z.$$
3. Return $x_0$.

> Theorem: For the Gaussian forward kernel, the posterior mean satisfies $\tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\,\epsilon\right)$ where $\epsilon$ is the noise injected to produce $x_t$. Hence the KL terms of the ELBO are exactly weighted denoising-score-matching losses at noise scale $\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}$ [1][6].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Variational Bound and Denoising Score Matching

The ELBO decomposition above is more than a training trick: it reveals that DDPM *is* denoising score matching in variational clothing. Each term $D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0) \,\|\, p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t))$ is the KL between two Gaussians with identical variance, hence a squared error between their means — a *denoising* objective at noise level $t$. Vincent's identity [6] shows that matching the score of the perturbed distribution $q(x_t) = \int q(x_t \mid x_0) p_{data}(x_0) dx_0$ is equivalent to regressing the conditional score $\nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t \mid x_0) = -(x_t - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0)/(1-\bar{\alpha}_t)$, which is precisely a rescaled version of the noise target $\epsilon$. The weighting discarded in $L_{simple}$ corresponds to emphasizing low noise levels; Ho et al. found uniform weighting across $t$ yields the best FID [1][3].

### 4.2 Tweedie's Formula: Noise Prediction as Score Estimation

The bridge between noise prediction and score estimation is **Tweedie's formula**, the empirical-Bayes identity for Gaussian corruption. If $x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon$, then the posterior mean of the clean signal satisfies:

$$\mathbb{E}(x_0 \mid x_t) = \frac{x_t + (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\,\nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}.$$

Rearranging, the noise target and the score are related by $\epsilon = -\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t)$ in expectation. Therefore:

$$\epsilon_{\theta}(x_t, t) \approx -\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\; s_{\theta}(x_t, t), \qquad s_{\theta}(x_t, t) \approx \nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t).$$

Learning to denoise *is* learning the score field: every DDPM is secretly a score model [3]. The correspondence is interconvertible given the schedule:

```haskell
-- Tweedie correspondence: score <-> noise prediction
type Score a = a -> Double -> a        -- s_theta(x_t, t) ~= grad log q(x_t)
type NoisePred a = a -> Double -> a    -- eps_theta(x_t, t)

scoreToNoise :: Double -> Score a -> NoisePred a
scoreToNoise alphaBar s = \x t -> scale (- sqrt (1 - alphaBar)) (s x t)

noiseToScore :: Double -> NoisePred a -> Score a
noiseToScore alphaBar e = \x t -> scale (- 1 / sqrt (1 - alphaBar)) (e x t)
```

> Theorem: Under the Gaussian forward kernel, $\epsilon_{\theta}$ minimizes $L_{simple}$ if and only if $-\epsilon_{\theta}/\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}$ minimizes the weighted denoising score matching objective $\mathbb{E}[\|s_{\theta}(x_t,t) - \nabla \log q(x_t \mid x_0)\|^2]$. The two objectives have identical minimizers and identical gradients up to the scalar schedule weight [3][6].

### 4.3 The Continuous-Time Unification: VP, VE, and sub-VP SDEs

Song et al. [3] showed that DDPM and NCSN are discretizations of *one* continuous object. Consider the forward Itô SDE:

$$dx = f(x, t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw,$$

where $w$ is standard Brownian motion. Under mild regularity, Anderson's theorem gives the exact *reverse-time* SDE:

$$dx = \left[f(x, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right]dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w},$$

with $\bar{w}$ a reverse-time Brownian motion and $p_t$ the marginal density of the forward process. *Sampling reduces to score estimation*: replace $\nabla_x \log p_t$ by a learned $s_{\theta}(x, t)$ and simulate backward from the prior. The discrete DDPM and NCSN schedules emerge as follows:

| SDE | Drift $f$ | Diffusion $g$ | Discrete limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variance Preserving (VP) | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)}$ | DDPM [1] |
| Variance Exploding (VE) | $0$ | $\sqrt{d(\sigma^2(t))/dt}$ | NCSN [5] |
| sub-VP | $-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\,x$ | $\sqrt{\beta(t)(1 - e^{-2\int_0^t \beta(s)ds})}$ | Improved likelihood [4] |

The VP SDE keeps the marginal variance bounded (mirroring $\bar{\alpha}_t$ decay), the VE SDE lets variance explode geometrically (mirroring NCSN's noise ladder), and the sub-VP SDE interpolates to obtain tighter likelihood bounds [4]. For every reverse SDE there is a *deterministic* ordinary differential equation — the **probability-flow ODE** — with identical marginals:

$$dx = \left[f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\right]dt.$$

This ODE enables exact likelihood computation via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula and deterministic sampling with black-box ODE solvers [3][4]. The sampler's abstract state machine — initialize at the prior, integrate the learned reverse dynamics, terminate at $t = 0$ — can be specified precisely:

```tla
------------------------------- MODULE DiffusionSampler -------------------------------
EXTENDS Reals, Naturals
CONSTANTS T            \* number of discretization steps
VARIABLES x, t         \* current sample, current (reverse) time index
Init == x \in NoisePrior /\ t = T
ReverseStep ==
    /\ t > 0
    /\ x' = Denoise(x, t, ScoreNet(x, t))
    /\ t' = t - 1
    \/ UNCHANGED <<x, t>>   \* stuttering permitted
Next == ReverseStep
Spec == Init /\ [][Next]_<<x, t>> /\ WF_<<x, t>>(ReverseStep)
THEOREM Spec => <>(t = 0)    \* termination: a sample is always produced
========================================================================================
```

### 4.4 Acceleration: DDIM, ODE Solvers, and Few-Step Sampling

DDPM sampling requires hundreds to thousands of sequential network evaluations — prohibitive for interactive use. Song et al. [7] observed that the training objective depends only on the *marginals* $q(x_t \mid x_0)$, not on the Markov structure of the forward chain. They constructed a family of **non-Markovian** forward processes with identical marginals but deterministic, skippable reverse steps — Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM). Setting the reverse variance to zero yields a fully deterministic sampler that:

- generates high-quality samples in 20–100 steps instead of 1000,
- defines an invertible mapping between latent noise and data (enabling interpolation and editing),
- preserves the *same trained network* — no retraining required [7].

Alternatively, the probability-flow ODE [3][4] can be integrated with adaptive solvers, trading function evaluations for accuracy. The empirical payoff is dramatic:

| Model (CIFAR-10) | Steps | FID ↓ |
|---|---|---|
| DDPM [1] | 1000 | 3.17 |
| DDIM, 100 steps [7] | 100 | 4.16 |
| DDIM, 20 steps [7] | 20 | 6.84 |
| Improved DDPM [8] | 1000 | 2.90 |
| Score SDE, VPSDE [3] | 2000 | 2.41 |
| NCSN [5] | ~2000 | 25.30 |

### 4.5 Sampling Guarantees and Convergence Analysis

Early diffusion theory was almost entirely empirical. Recent work has begun to supply the missing guarantees. Nakano [9] proved that the original DDPM sampling sequence *weakly converges* to the data distribution as the number of timesteps tends to infinity, under asymptotic conditions on the variance schedule, the $L^2$ score-estimation error, and the noise-prediction network. The proof's key insight is that DDPM sampling is an **exponential-integrator discretization** of the reverse-time SDE over a finite horizon — so classical SDE numerics (strong and weak convergence of Euler–Maruyama-type schemes) can be brought to bear.

> Theorem: Let the variance schedule satisfy $\beta_t \to 0$ with $\sum_t \beta_t \to \infty$ appropriately, and let the learned score satisfy $\mathbb{E}\|s_{\theta}(x_t,t) - \nabla \log p_t(x_t)\|^2 \to 0$ in the refinement limit. Then the law of the DDPM sampler's output converges weakly to $p_{data}$ as $T \to \infty$ [9].

> Theorem: For the probability-flow ODE with exact scores, the pushforward of the prior through the ODE equals $p_{data}$ exactly at $t = 0$; with approximate scores, the total-variation error is controlled by the integrated score-matching loss plus the ODE solver's discretization error [3][4].

A single reverse step is Euler–Maruyama on the learned reverse SDE, which in Rust-like pseudocode is:

```rust
// One exponential-integrator reverse step (DDPM sampler)
fn reverse_step(x: &Tensor, t: usize, eps_theta: &Model, sched: &Schedule) -> Tensor {
    let a = sched.alpha(t);                 // 1 - beta_t
    let abar = sched.alpha_bar(t);          // cumulative product
    let eps = eps_theta.forward(x, t);      // learned noise / score
    let mean = (x - sched.beta(t) / (1.0 - abar).sqrt() * eps) / a.sqrt();
    if t > 1 {
        let z = Tensor::randn_like(x);      // fresh noise, annealed out at t = 1
        mean + sched.sigma(t) * z
    } else {
        mean
    }
}
```

These results reframe diffusion sampling as *numerical SDE integration with learned coefficients* — a viewpoint that imports classical stochastic numerics into generative modeling.

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### Benchmark results

On **unconditional CIFAR-10**, DDPM achieved an Inception Score of 9.46 and FID of 3.17, the first non-adversarial model to beat contemporary GANs on FID [1]. On **256×256 LSUN** (bedroom, church, cat), samples matched ProgressiveGAN quality [1]. Likelihoods tell a complementary story: the original DDPM bound gave NLL ≤ 3.75 bits/dim on CIFAR-10, while likelihood-weighted SDE training [4] reached 2.84 bits/dim and variational diffusion models later pushed below 2.49. Nichol and Dhariwal [8] showed that learning the reverse variances with a cosine schedule improves both metrics at once (FID 2.90, NLL 3.37).

### Proof sketch: the ELBO telescopes into denoising objectives

The variational bound's decomposition follows from the chain rule of KL divergence applied to the joint distributions $q(x_{0:T})$ and $p_{\theta}(x_{0:T})$. Because the forward posterior $q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)$ is Gaussian in closed form, each KL term reduces to a squared error between means; the noise reparametrization converts every term into $\lambda_t \|\epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}\|^2$ with explicit weight $\lambda_t$. Setting $\lambda_t = 1$ is exactly the passage from the ELBO to $L_{simple}$ [1].

### Proof sketch: Tweedie's identity

For $x_t \mid x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)I)$, differentiating the marginal $q(x_t)$ under the integral sign and applying Stein's lemma to the Gaussian kernel shows the score equals the posterior expectation of the conditional score $-(x_t - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0)/(1-\bar{\alpha}_t)$. Solving for the posterior mean of $x_0$ yields Tweedie's formula, which rearranged gives the noise–score relation of §4.2 [3][6].

### Proof sketch: weak convergence of DDPM sampling

Nakano [9] writes one DDPM reverse step as $x_{t-1} = e^{-\beta_t/2}x_t + (1 - e^{-\beta_t}) s_{\theta}(x_t, t) + \sqrt{1 - e^{-\beta_t}}\,z$, recognizing the exponential-integrator form of the reverse VP-SDE. Under Lipschitz and growth conditions on the true score, the discretization error vanishes as $\max_t \beta_t \to 0$; under $L^2$ convergence of the learned score, the statistical error vanishes too. Tightness plus convergence of finite-dimensional distributions then gives weak convergence of the sampler's law to $p_{data}$.

##6 Limitations

Diffusion models are not without deep limitations, and intellectual honesty requires stating them plainly.

- **Sampling cost.** Even with DDIM [7] and ODE solvers [3], diffusion sampling needs tens to hundreds of network evaluations per sample — orders of magnitude slower than a single GAN forward pass. For video and real-time applications this remains the binding constraint.
- **The likelihood–fidelity tradeoff.** The reweighting that makes $L_{simple}$ produce beautiful samples ($\lambda_t = 1$) provably worsens the variational bound; likelihood-weighted training [4] reverses the tradeoff. No single weighting is simultaneously optimal for both, and the *right* weighting remains an open design question rather than a derived one.
- **Theory–practice gaps.** Convergence theorems [9] assume score errors vanish in the refinement limit, but real networks are trained at fixed $T$ with fixed capacity; quantitative, non-asymptotic rates under realistic assumptions are still sparse. Mode coverage, memorization, and the geometry of the learned score field near low-density regions are poorly understood.
- **Discrete and structured data.** The Gaussian formulation is native to continuous data; text, graphs, and other discrete modalities require awkward adaptations (embeddings, absorbing states, dequantization), where autoregressive models still dominate [7].
- **Evaluation.** FID and Inception Score are proxy metrics computed against ImageNet features; they reward ImageNet-like statistics and correlate imperfectly with human judgments of quality.

##7 Conclusion

Diffusion probabilistic models unify three ideas that once looked distinct: *variational inference* over a fixed noising chain [1][2], *score matching* across noise scales [5][6], and *stochastic calculus* in continuous time [3][4]. The forward process destroys structure deterministically; the learned reverse process restores it; Tweedie's formula proves that denoising and score estimation are one operation; Anderson's theorem lifts the whole apparatus to SDEs; and the probability-flow ODE plus DDIM [7] convert the theory into fast, deterministic, likelihood-tractable samplers with emerging convergence guarantees [9].

The research frontier now lies in *efficiency* (few-step samplers), *theory* (non-asymptotic rates, score-field geometry), and *scope* (discrete modalities, scientific applications from protein design to weather forecasting). What began as a thermodynamic curiosity in 2015 [2] has become the mathematical backbone of modern generative AI — a rare case where the deepest theory and the best engineering turned out to be the same thing.
