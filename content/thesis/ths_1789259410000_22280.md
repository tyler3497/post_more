# The Conformal Bootstrap: Crossing Symmetry, the Operator Product Expansion, and the Numerical Bootstrap of the 3D Ising Model

## Abstract

We give a self-contained, technically precise account of the modern conformal bootstrap: constraining conformal field theories using only symmetry, associativity of the operator product expansion (OPE), and unitarity. From the CFT axioms we derive the crossing equation for scalar four-point functions and show how its expansion in conformal blocks — eigenfunctions of the quadratic Casimir — converts crossing symmetry into an infinite-dimensional convex feasibility problem. We explain the linear-functional method, the passage to semidefinite programming, and the arbitrary-precision SDPB solver. As the flagship application we dissect the numerical bootstrap of the critical 3D Ising model: from the first rigorous bounds [6], through the kink and central-charge minimum located at the physical exponents [3][4], to the mixed-correlator "precision islands" of Kos, Poland, Simmons-Duffin, and Vichi, which pin the leading data to Δ_σ = 0.5181489(10), Δ_ϵ = 1.412625(10), λ_σσϵ = 1.0518537(41), λ_ϵϵϵ = 1.532435(19) — two orders of magnitude beyond Monte Carlo. We cover the analytic bootstrap at large spin, the AdS/CFT translation, OPE convergence, and current limitations: SDP scaling, gap assumptions, and the open extension to gauge theories and non-unitary CFTs.

## 1 Introduction

Few problems in theoretical physics are as stubborn as the strongly coupled fixed points of the renormalization group. The three-dimensional Ising model — the lattice model of a uniaxial ferromagnet, the binary fluid, the liquid–vapor critical point — is believed to flow under coarse-graining to a non-trivial fixed point, an interacting conformal field theory (CFT) in three Euclidean dimensions. No small parameter is available: the epsilon expansion is asymptotic, and lattice Monte Carlo, while empirically superb, is a simulation rather than a derivation. The conformal bootstrap changes the rules of engagement. Rather than starting from a Lagrangian and approximating, it starts from a short list of axioms — conformal invariance, unitarity, and associativity of the operator product expansion (OPE) — and asks: *what CFT data are compatible with them?* The answer takes the form of rigorous exclusion regions in the space of scaling dimensions and OPE coefficients, and, at specially constrained points, tiny "islands" of allowed data that locate real physical theories with unprecedented precision [1][2].

The intellectual lineage is old. Polyakov's 1974 non-Hamiltonian program already proposed that the OPE associativity conditions might determine CFTs without Lagrangians. Belavin, Polyakov, and Zamolodchikov solved the two-dimensional minimal models exactly through the bootstrap in 1984, exploiting the infinite-dimensional Virasoro algebra. The modern revival — the numerical bootstrap in dimensions greater than two — was launched by Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni, and Vichi in 2008, who showed that the crossing equation for a scalar four-point function could be bounded by linear programming and produced the first rigorous universal upper bound on the dimension of the leading scalar in a 4D CFT [6]. Within four years, El-Showk, Paulos, Poland, Rychkov, Simmons-Duffin, and Vichi had aimed the same machinery at the 3D Ising model and found a sharp kink in the dimension bound sitting exactly at the physical critical exponents [3]. The kink hardened into an island when mixed correlators were bootstrapped jointly [5], and the central charge was conjectured to be minimized at the physical point [4]. This thesis is an exposition of how all of this works, why it is rigorous, and where it goes next.

> **Thesis claim:** The crossing equation, viewed as a linear constraint in the convex cone generated by conformal blocks with non-negative OPE coefficients, is algorithmically decidable (in the semidefinite-programming sense) to any desired derivative order; applied to the mixed correlator system of the 3D Ising universality class, it determines the leading CFT data to eleven significant digits, exceeding all other methods by two orders of magnitude.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Axioms of conformal field theory

A unitary CFT in *d* Euclidean dimensions is specified by a Hilbert space carrying a unitary representation of the conformal group *SO(d+1,1)*, a set of local primary operators *O_i(x)* with definite scaling dimensions Δ_i and *SO(d)* spins ℓ_i, and an operator product expansion. Conformal invariance fixes two- and three-point functions up to constants, and the OPE takes the schematic form

O_i(x) O_j(0) = Σ_k λ_{ijk} |x|^{Δ_k − Δ_i − Δ_j} [ O_k(0) + descendants ],

where the **OPE coefficients** λ_{ijk} are the dynamical data of the theory, and "descendants" denotes contributions fixed entirely by conformal symmetry (derivatives of the primary). Unitarity imposes two crucial facts: the squared OPE coefficients λ_{ijk}² are non-negative real numbers, and operator dimensions obey unitarity bounds (Δ ≥ (d−2)/2 for scalars, Δ ≥ ℓ + d − 2 for ℓ > 0). The stress tensor *T_{μν}* is always present with Δ = d and is conserved; its two-point function normalization defines the central charge *c* (in 3D, the *C_T* coefficient). Crossing symmetry — the requirement that the OPE be associative, i.e., that expanding a four-point function in different pairings ("channels") gives the same function — is the nontrivial constraint [1].

### 2.2 Four-point functions and conformal blocks

Consider four identical scalars σ of dimension Δ_σ. Conformal invariance reduces ⟨σ(x₁)σ(x₂)σ(x₃)σ(x₄)⟩ to a function of two cross-ratios *u, v*. Inserting the OPE in the (12)(34) channel yields the conformal block decomposition

⟨σσσσ⟩ = (x²₁₂ x²₃₄)^{-Δ_σ} Σ_{Δ,ℓ} λ²_{σσO_{Δ,ℓ}} g_{Δ,ℓ}(u, v),

where the sum runs over primary operators appearing in σ × σ, and the **conformal blocks** *g_{Δ,ℓ}(u,v)* package the contribution of an entire conformal multiplet (primary plus all descendants). Blocks are eigenfunctions of the quadratic Casimir of the conformal group acting on pairs of points,

D_z g_{Δ,ℓ} = c_{Δ,ℓ} g_{Δ,ℓ},  c_{Δ,ℓ} = Δ(Δ − d) + ℓ(ℓ + d − 2),

with boundary conditions fixed by the OPE limit [8]. In even dimensions, Dolan and Osborn found closed-form expressions; in odd dimensions (including the physically crucial *d* = 3), blocks are computed via power-series expansions in radial coordinates or the Zamolodchikov-type recursion relations of Kos, Poland, and Simmons-Duffin [8]. Crucially for numerics, blocks admit rational approximations whose derivatives at the crossing-symmetric point *z = z̄ = 1/2* are positive linear combinations of a fixed set of polynomials — the technical fact that makes semidefinite programming applicable [7].

### 2.3 The crossing equation

Equating the *s*-channel (12)(34) and *t*-channel (14)(23) expansions gives the crossing equation, conventionally written as a vanishing vector sum:

Σ_{Δ,ℓ} λ²_{σσO} F^{Δ_σ}_{Δ,ℓ}(u, v) = 0,   with   F^{Δ_σ}_{Δ,ℓ}(u,v) ≡ v^{Δ_σ} g_{Δ,ℓ}(u,v) − u^{Δ_σ} g_{Δ,ℓ}(v,u).

The identity operator contributes the known inhomogeneous term −F^{Δ_σ}_{0,0}. Because λ² ≥ 0 by unitarity, the crossing equation states that the vector −F_{0,0} lies in the convex cone generated by the vectors {F_{Δ,ℓ}} over all allowed (Δ, ℓ). This is the master observation: **bootstrap constraints are convex geometry** [6][1].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The linear functional method

Suppose we hypothesize a spectrum — e.g., "the leading Z₂-even scalar ϵ has dimension Δ_ϵ ≥ Δ_gap". If we can find a linear functional *α* acting on functions of *(u,v)* (in practice, a finite linear combination of derivatives evaluated at *z = z̄ = 1/2*) such that

α(F_{0,0}) = 1 (normalization),  α(F_{Δ,ℓ}) ≥ 0 for all (Δ,ℓ) in the assumed spectrum,

then applying *α* to the crossing sum gives 0 = 1 + (non-negative terms) > 0, a contradiction: the assumed spectrum is **rigorously excluded** [6]. Finding such an *α* is a linear program; allowing *α* to be a matrix-valued functional acting on systems of correlators upgrades it to a **semidefinite program (SDP)**, the formulation that unlocked the mixed-correlator era [7][5].

### 3.2 From blocks to polynomials: the SDP formulation

Derivatives of conformal blocks at the symmetric point can be approximated, via the recursion relations of [8], as

∂^m_z ∂^n_z̄ g_{Δ,ℓ}|_{1/2} ≈ χ_ℓ(Δ) P^{m,n}_ℓ(Δ),

where χ_ℓ(Δ) > 0 is a known positive prefactor and P^{m,n}_ℓ are polynomials in Δ. Positivity of *α* on all Δ above the unitarity bound then becomes a set of polynomial positivity conditions — exactly the form a semidefinite program consumes. The problem is solved in arbitrary precision by **SDPB**, a custom primal-dual interior-point solver written by Simmons-Duffin for precisely this class of problems [7]. Truncation is controlled by Λ, the maximum total derivative order; increasing Λ tightens bounds monotonically, and extrapolation in 1/Λ gives the continuum estimates.

> **Theorem (Rattazzi–Rychkov–Tonni–Vichi bound):** In any unitary 4D CFT with a scalar primary φ of dimension Δ_φ, the dimension Δ of the leading scalar in the φ × φ OPE satisfies a universal upper bound Δ ≤ f(Δ_φ) obtained from an optimal linear functional; the bound is saturated (to numerical precision) by known theories at distinguished points [6].

### 3.3 Mixed correlators and the island mechanism

A single correlator ⟨σσσσ⟩ bounds one operator at a time. The breakthrough of Kos, Poland, and Simmons-Duffin was to bootstrap the *system* {⟨σσσσ⟩, ⟨σσεϵ⟩, ⟨ϵϵϵϵ⟩} simultaneously [8]. The crossing equations couple the OPE coefficients λ_{σσϵ}, λ_{ϵϵϵ}, λ_{σσϵ'}, …, and scanning over their ratios imposes the physical requirement that only one operator sits at each dimension — eliminating the "fake" spectra that single-correlator bounds cannot rule out. In the (Δ_σ, Δ_ϵ) plane, the allowed region collapses from a one-sided bound to a **closed island** surrounding the physical point [5]. Scanning OPE-coefficient ratios at increasing Λ shrinks the island; its center converges to the true CFT data with rigorously controlled error bars.

A compact illustration of the computational pipeline:

import numpy as np

def bootstrap_bound(delta_sigma, Lambda=27, spins=range(0, 26, 2)):
    # 1. Build conformal block derivatives at z = zb = 1/2
    blocks = {l: conformal_block_derivatives(delta_sigma, l, Lambda)
              for l in spins}
    # 2. Rational/polynomial approximation: d^m g = chi(Delta) * P(Delta)
    polys = {l: rational_approximate(b) for l, b in blocks.items()}
    # 3. Assemble SDP: find functional alpha with alpha(F_id) = 1,
    #    alpha(F_{Delta,l}) >= 0 for all Delta >= unitarity_bound(l)

                       positivity=polys,
                       gaps={'scalar_even': delta_gap})
    # 4. Solve in arbitrary precision; dual feasible => spectrum excluded
    return sdp.solve(precision=1024)   # 'dual feasible' or 'primal feasible'

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Conformal blocks as Casimir eigenfunctions

The quadratic Casimir derivation underpins everything numerical. Acting with the conformal generators on points 1 and 2, the eigenvalue equation D_z g = c_{Δ,ℓ} g becomes a second-order PDE in the cross-ratios whose regular solution with the OPE boundary condition g ∼ z^{Δ} (1 + …) is unique. In *d* = 3 no elementary closed form exists, so the practical route is the recursion of [8]: blocks expand in radial coordinates (r, η) with Gegenbauer polynomials carrying the angular dependence and coefficients satisfying a three-term recursion seeded by the primary. Derivatives at the crossing-symmetric point r = 3 − 2√2 ≈ 0.1716 converge exponentially in the truncation order, and the positive prefactor χ_ℓ(Δ) factors out analytically — exposing the polynomial structure the SDP consumes [7][1].

### 4.2 The kink, the minimum, and the island

The phenomenological storyline of the 3D Ising bootstrap has three acts [2][3][4][5]:

| Era | Correlators | Key result | Δ_σ precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 — El-Showk et al. [3] | ⟨σσσσ⟩ | Kink in the Δ_ϵ bound at Δ_σ ≈ 0.518 | ~10⁻³ |
| 2014 — El-Showk et al. [4] | ⟨σσσσ⟩ + *c*-minimization | Extremal spectrum; operator decoupling at Ising point | 0.518154(15) |
| 2014 — Kos–Poland–Simmons-Duffin [8] | mixed ⟨σσσσ⟩, ⟨σσεϵ⟩, ⟨ϵϵϵϵ⟩ | First closed allowed region | ~10⁻⁶ |
| 2016 — Kos et al. [5] | mixed + OPE-ratio scan, Λ = 43 | Precision island | 0.5181489(10) |

The 2012 kink was the thunderclap: the upper bound on Δ_ϵ as a function of Δ_σ changes slope discontinuously at precisely the Monte Carlo value of Δ_σ, with Δ_ϵ saturating its bound there. El-Showk et al. then conjectured that the 3D Ising CFT *minimizes the central charge c* among unitary solutions to crossing, and showed that the extremal functional at the minimum reconstructs a full low-lying spectrum with operators visibly decoupling as the Ising point is approached — reminiscent of null-state decoupling in 2D minimal models [4].

### 4.3 Semidefinite programming at arbitrary precision

Why not ordinary double-precision SDP solvers? Bootstrap functionals require hundreds of digits: the polynomial approximations involve severe cancellations, and a merely "approximately feasible" dual solution is not a rigorous exclusion certificate. **SDPB** implements a primal-dual interior-point method with arbitrary-precision arithmetic (via GMP/MPFR-style backends), hot-starting across a scan grid, and a custom matrix algebra tuned to the block-diagonal structure of bootstrap SDPs [7]. A Λ = 43 mixed-correlator run consumes on the order of 10⁵ CPU-hours; the community has since developed *skydiving* (navigator-function optimization that follows the SDP central path while moving in parameter space) and *tiptop*-style Delaunay-triangulation scans to map islands far more efficiently [1].

### 4.4 The analytic bootstrap and large-spin systematics

Numerics dominate at low spin, but crossing symmetry also yields *analytic* control at large spin. In the lightcone limit, the identity and stress-tensor exchanges in one channel must be reproduced by infinite towers of double-twist operators [O∂^ℓO]_{n} in the crossed channel, whose anomalous dimensions admit a systematic 1/ℓ expansion fixed by the low-lying data — the *analytic bootstrap* [1]. For the 3D Ising model, the analytic predictions for the leading-twist trajectory match the numerical island data, an independent consistency check tying low-spin numerics to Regge asymptotics [1][5].

### 4.5 Holographic translation: bootstrap meets AdS/CFT

Via the AdS/CFT correspondence, a CFT with a large-*N* expansion and a sparse low-lying spectrum is dual to a weakly coupled gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter space; single-trace operators map to bulk fields and OPE coefficients to Witten-diagram couplings. Bootstrap bounds on OPE coefficients and gaps therefore translate into bounds on bulk effective field theories — e.g., universal bounds on higher-derivative corrections to Einstein gravity. The 3D Ising model itself has no weakly coupled holographic dual (it is far from large *N*), but the *methods* — crossing, convex optimization, extremal functionals — transfer wholesale, and the extremal-functional technology developed for the island now underpins holographic bootstrap studies [1].

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires stating what the bootstrap does *not* do. **(1) Computational scaling.** SDP cost grows steeply with Λ (roughly like the cube of the number of crossing equations times polynomial degree); pushing Λ = 43 mixed runs already demands supercomputer-scale resources, and each new correlator in the system multiplies the SDP dimension. **(2) Gap assumptions.** Rigorous exclusion is always conditional: "assuming no Z₂-even scalar below Δ_ϵ′ ≥ 3 …". Islands are rigorous only within their stated assumptions, and while the assumptions used for the Ising island are extremely mild (gaps far below any plausible operator), they are assumptions nonetheless. **(3) Uniqueness is conjectural.** The *c*-minimization identification of the Ising point is a conjecture supported by overwhelming numerics, not a theorem [4]. **(4) Scope.** Precision islands exist for the Ising and O(N) vector models and a handful of others; extending the technology to gauge theories (QCD-like CFTs), non-unitary theories (Lee-Yang, percolation — where λ² ≥ 0 fails), fermions at finite *N*, and defect/boundary CFTs remains largely open, as does the analytic understanding of *why* kinks coincide with physical theories [1][2]. **(5) The epsilon expansion still wins on breadth**: the bootstrap gives exquisite data at isolated points but no continuous family of theories across dimensions — though the recent 2 < d < 4 interpolation studies are closing that gap [1].

## 7 Conclusion

The conformal bootstrap has transformed the 3D Ising model from a problem of approximation into a problem of *deduction*. From the crossing equation — a single associativity condition on the operator product expansion — plus unitarity and conformal symmetry, convex optimization extracts the critical exponents to eleven significant digits, with certificates of rigor no Monte Carlo can supply. The conceptual arc runs from Rattazzi et al.'s first linear-programming bounds [6], through the kink and *c*-minimization discoveries of El-Showk et al. [3][4], the conformal-block technology of Kos, Poland, and Simmons-Duffin [8], the arbitrary-precision SDPB solver [7], and finally the precision islands [5] that stand as the sharpest determination of any strongly coupled fixed point in physics. The analytic bootstrap and the holographic dictionary extend the same ideas to large spin and quantum gravity [1]. What remains — gauge theories, non-unitary criticality, the mathematical explanation of kinks, and a fully rigorous uniqueness proof — defines the frontier. The island, however, is not going anywhere: it is a theorem about nature, proved by a computer, from symmetry alone.
