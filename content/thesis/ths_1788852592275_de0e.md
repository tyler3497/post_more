# Hardware-Algorithm Co-Design in Selective State Spaces: Kernel Fusion, Parallel Associative Scanning, and the SSD Duality Behind Million-Token Mamba Contexts

## Abstract

The resurgence of state space models (SSMs) is fundamentally a story about *algorithms designed against a memory hierarchy*. Mamba's selective state space [5] closed the quality gap between linear-time sequence models and Transformers not only by making SSM parameters input-dependent, but by fusing the recurrence into a hardware-aware kernel that never materializes the O(B·L·D·N) state in slow HBM, streaming it instead through GPU SRAM via a parallel associative scan. Mamba-2's structured state space duality (SSD) [6] recasts the selective scan as matrix multiplication against a 1-semiseparable mask, so both the recurrent and dual forms decompose into blockwise matrix multiplications. This thesis develops the continuous-time SSM and its ZOH discretization with HiPPO initialization [1], S4/S4D's structured kernels [3, 4], Mamba's selectivity and kernel fusion [5], and the SSD identity Y = (L ∘ QKᵀ)V [6]. We prove scan associativity, quantify the IO calculus of chunked computation, review evidence from Path-X to million-token training and hybrid deployments [7], and close with open limits: associative recall gaps, discretization stiffness, and contested scaling laws.

## 1 Introduction

Sequence modeling is organized around one tension: *expressivity of the sequence operator* versus *cost as a function of length L*. Transformers [Vaswani et al., 2017] spend O(L²) but model arbitrary content-based interactions; RNNs spend O(L) but parallelize poorly; linear attention [Katharopoulos et al., 2020; Choromanski et al., 2021] achieves O(L) with reduced recall capacity. The state space program—HiPPO [1], LSSL [2], S4 [3], S4D [4], Mamba [5], Mamba-2 [6]—is the most complete attempt to break this tension, and its defining insight is systems-theoretic rather than architectural:

> **Thesis:** *The asymptotic complexity of a sequence operator is only half the story; the other half is whether the operator's dataflow respects the memory hierarchy of the accelerator that executes it.*

Mamba-2's 2–8× speedup over Mamba [6] does not come from fewer FLOPs alone—it comes from restructuring the selective scan into *chunked matrix multiplications* that exploit tensor cores. This thesis therefore treats selective SSMs as an object of *co-design*: a mathematical family (continuous-time linear dynamical systems with structured parameterizations) paired with an execution strategy (fused kernels, parallel scans, SRAM tiling) without which the mathematics would be practically inert.

We proceed as follows: §2 develops the background (continuous SSM, ZOH discretization, HiPPO memory, the convolution–recurrence duality); §3 derives the three methodological pillars (S4's DPLR/Cauchy kernel, Mamba's selectivity and fused scan, SSD's matrix view); §4 decomposes the deep theory (scan associativity, semiseparable algebra, the IO calculus, the attention relation); §5 assembles empirical results and proofs; §6 discusses limitations; §7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The continuous-time state space model

A state space model is a *linear time-invariant (LTI) dynamical system* mapping a one-dimensional input signal u(t) to an output y(t) through an N-dimensional latent state x(t):

$$\dot{x}(t) = A x(t) + B u(t), \qquad y(t) = C x(t) + D u(t), \qquad (1)$$

with A ∈ ℝ^{N×N}, B ∈ ℝ^{N×1}, C ∈ ℝ^{1×N}, D ∈ ℝ. Deep variants stack many SISO systems per channel, mixing channels with projections and nonlinearities [2, 3].

The system (1) admits two complementary views. Unrolling gives the **recurrent view**:

$$x_t = \bar{A} x_{t-1} + \bar{B} u_t, \qquad y_t = C x_t, \qquad (2)$$

after discretization (see §2.2), which costs O(L) time and O(1) memory per step at inference. Alternatively, eliminating the state yields the **convolutional view**:

$$y = \bar{K} \ast u, \qquad \bar{K} = \left(C\bar{B},\, C\bar{A}\bar{B},\, \dots,\, C\bar{A}^{L-1}\bar{B}\right), \qquad (3)$$

which costs O(L log L) via FFT but parallelizes perfectly across time during training. S4's entire contribution [3] is to make (3) *numerically and computationally tractable* through a structured A.

### 2.2 Discretization: from continuous to discrete time

Deep learning processes discrete tokens, so (1) must be discretized with a step size Δ > 0. The three canonical choices are:

| Scheme | Discretized Ā | Discretized B̄ | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Euler | I + ΔA | ΔB | O(Δ) |
| Bilinear (Tustin) | (I − ΔA/2)⁻¹(I + ΔA/2) | (I − ΔA/2)⁻¹ΔB | O(Δ²) |
| Zero-order hold (ZOH) | exp(ΔA) | (ΔA)⁻¹(exp(ΔA) − I)ΔB | exact under ZOH |

Mamba uses ZOH discretization [5], where Δ itself becomes a *learned, input-dependent gating parameter*:

$$\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A), \qquad \bar{B}_t \approx \Delta_t B_t, \qquad (4)$$

the approximation for B̄ following from the first-order expansion of (ΔA)⁻¹(exp(ΔA) − I)ΔB ≈ ΔB. The exponential exp(ΔA) is the mechanism by which Δ implements **selectivity as continuous-time gating**: a large Δ_t drives Ā_t → 0, *forgetting* the past state, while a small Δ_t keeps Ā_t ≈ I, *propagating* the state forward. This is the continuous-time analog of the GRU update gate, made rigorous through HiPPO theory [1].

> **Theorem:** *Under ZOH discretization, the state transition Ā_t = exp(Δ_t A) is a contraction whenever the spectrum of A lies in the left half-plane, so that the influence of x_0 on x_t decays as ‖exp(t·Ā)‖ ≤ exp(t·max Re λ(A)). Input-dependent Δ_t therefore implements exact per-token control of the memory horizon.*

In S4 and Mamba, A is initialized from the HiPPO-LegS matrix [1], whose eigenvalues have negative real parts by construction—so the system is stable by design, and Δ modulates *how fast* the memory decays rather than whether it is stable.

### 2.3 HiPPO: optimal polynomial memory

HiPPO [1] solves the *online function approximation* problem: given a history {u(s)}_{s ≤ t}, maintain a finite coefficient vector c(t) ∈ ℝ^N such that c(t) optimally reconstructs the history in a weighted L² sense under a measure ω^(t):

$$c(t) = \arg\min_c \int_{-\infty}^{t} \left(u(s) - \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} c_n\, g_n^{(t)}(s)\right)^2 \omega^{(t)}(s)\, ds, \qquad (5)$$

where {g_n^(t)} are polynomials orthonormal under ω^(t). For the *scaled Legendre* measure ω^(t)(s) ∝ 1_{s ≤ t}, differentiating (5) yields the linear ODE

$$\dot{c}(t) = A_{\text{LegS}}\, c(t) + B_{\text{LegS}}\, u(t), \qquad (6)$$

with a specific, fixed matrix A_LegS—this is precisely an SSM of form (1) with *data-independent* parameters. The HiPPO-LegS matrix is **timescale-robust**: it remembers the entire history without committing to a fixed memory scale, which is why random initializations of A fail catastrophically on long-range tasks while HiPPO initializations succeed [1, 3]. Every subsequent SSM—LSSL, S4, S4D, Mamba, Mamba-2—inherits this initialization as its inductive bias for long-range memory.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Pillar I: S4's structured kernel — DPLR and the Cauchy computation

The convolutional view (3) requires computing the Krylov sequence (C̄B, CĀB̄, …, CĀ^{L−1}B̄). Naïvely this is O(N²L) time and numerically unstable for random A. S4 [3] restricts A to a **diagonal plus low-rank (DPLR)** form,

$$A = \Lambda - pq^\top, \qquad \Lambda\ \text{diagonal}, \qquad (7)$$

the canonical normal-plus-low-rank (NPLR) class that contains the HiPPO matrix. The key lemma is that for DPLR A, the generating function of the kernel is a **Cauchy kernel**:

$$\hat{K}(z) = C(zI - A)^{-1}B = \frac{\sum_j \frac{c_j}{z - \lambda_j}}{1 + \sum_j \frac{q_j}{z - \lambda_j}}, \qquad (8)$$

computable at L points in Õ(N + L) time, after which an inverse FFT yields K in O(L log L). Total complexity: **Õ(N + L)** time, O(N + L) memory—*nearly linear in both state size and sequence length*.

S4D [4] simplified this further: a purely *diagonal* A, initialized to the HiPPO eigenvalues, reduces the kernel to

$$K_\ell = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} C_n B_n\, e^{\lambda_n \ell \Delta}, \qquad (9)$$

a sum of decaying exponentials evaluable via a Vandermonde matrix-vector product in O(NL) (or Õ(N+L) with the same Cauchy machinery). The price of this simplification: a diagonal A cannot represent the full HiPPO-LegS dynamics exactly, and on the hardest long-range tasks S4's DPLR form retains a small edge [4].

### 3.2 Pillar II: Mamba's selectivity and the hardware-aware parallel scan

Mamba [5] identifies the central weakness of all LTI SSMs (including S4): with input-*independent* (A, B, C, Δ), the model cannot perform **content-based reasoning**—it cannot choose, per token, whether to remember or ignore it. The fix is *selectivity*: make Δ, B, C *functions of the input*:

$$\Delta_t = \tau_\Delta(\text{Linear}_\Delta(x_t)), \quad B_t = \text{Linear}_B(x_t), \quad C_t = \text{Linear}_C(x_t), \quad (10)$$

where τ_Δ = softplus ensures Δ_t > 0. Selectivity destroys the convolutional view (3): with time-varying (Ā_t, B̄_t) the system is no longer LTI, and naive recurrent training would be O(L) sequential steps—*infeasible* on parallel hardware.

Mamba's resolution is **algorithm–hardware co-design** — three fused-kernel ideas:

1. **Kernel fusion.** A naive implementation materializes the state x ∈ ℝ^{B×L×D×N} in HBM (≈1.07 GB per layer at B=8, L=4096, D=512, N=16)—pure memory traffic. The fused kernel loads (Δ, A, B, C) to *SRAM* once, runs discretization + recurrence there, and writes back only the L×D output—an O(B·L·D·N) IO reduction.
2. **Parallel associative scan.** The recurrence is an *associative* operator (§4.1), so the O(L) chain becomes an O(log L)-depth parallel scan—work-efficient and GPU-friendly.
3. **Recomputation.** Intermediate states are *not* stored for backprop; they are recomputed on demand. Memory stays O(B·L·D) at the cost of extra FLOPs—a trade IO-bound GPUs gladly accept.

The result [5]: up to **3× faster than prior SSMs on A100**, 5× higher inference throughput than Transformers, and quality scaling to **1M-length sequences**.

### 3.3 Pillar III: SSD — the structured state space duality

Mamba-2 [6] starts from a striking observation: expanding the state from a vector h ∈ ℝ^N to a *matrix* H ∈ ℝ^{N×P} (the "multi-head" SSM) and restricting A to **scalar-times-identity** (a_t·I) turns the selective recurrence

$$H_t = a_t H_{t-1} + B_t x_t^\top, \qquad y_t^\top = C_t^\top H_t, \qquad (11)$$

(where x_t plays the role of the value vector v_t and B_t, C_t are rank-one factors) is *exactly* equivalent to a matrix multiplication against a structured mask. Unrolling (11),

$$y_t = \sum_{s \leq t} C_t^\top B_s\, (a_{s+1}\cdots a_t)\, x_s, \qquad (12)$$

so that with the 1-semiseparable mask matrix L_{ts} = a_{s+1}⋯a_t (for s ≤ t, else 0),

$$Y = \big(L \circ (CB^\top)\big)\, X, \qquad (13)$$

i.e., **SSD = (L ∘ QKᵀ)V**: linear attention with a *data-dependent causal mask* L instead of softmax. This is the **structured state space duality**: attention and recurrence are two computational orderings of multiplication by the same **1-semiseparable** matrix M = L ∘ CBᵀ [6]. It is constructive: Mamba-2's **chunked algorithm** uses the quadratic dual form *within* blocks (matmul-rich, tensor-core friendly) and the linear form *across* blocks—yielding 2–8× speedups over Mamba-1 [6].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The algebra of the selective scan: associativity and the parallel prefix

Define for each time step the pair (p_t, q_t) := (Ā_t, B̄_t u_t) in an appropriate semiring. The recurrence x_t = p_t ⊗ x_{t−1} ⊕ q_t, with ⊗ matrix–vector multiplication and ⊕ addition, is the action of the affine map x ↦ p_t x + q_t. Function composition of affine maps is associative, and pairs compose as

$$(p_2, q_2) \bullet (p_1, q_1) = (p_2 p_1,\; p_2 q_1 + q_2), \qquad (14)$$

which is readily verified to be associative. Hence the entire selective recurrence reduces to a **parallel prefix (scan)** over •, computable in O(log L) depth with O(L) total work [Blelloch, 1990]. Crucially, associativity survives selectivity: nothing in (14) requires Ā_t to be time-invariant. This single algebraic fact is what rescues selective SSMs from sequential training.

> **Theorem:** *The selective SSM recurrence x_t = Ā_t x_{t−1} + B̄_t u_t with input-dependent (Ā_t, B̄_t) is exactly equivalent to a parallel prefix computation under the associative operator (14), and can therefore be evaluated in O(L) work and O(log L) span on a PRAM.*

### 4.2 Semiseparable matrices and the SSD decomposition

M = L ∘ (CBᵀ) is *1-semiseparable*: every strictly subdiagonal block has rank ≤ 1, since M_{ts} = C_tᵀB_s·L_{ts} factors into per-position terms. The deep result of [6] is that {softmax attention, linear attention, selective SSMs} are all multiplications by nested classes of *structured* matrices, and the SSD algorithm is a general fast-multiplication scheme for this class—like the FFT for circulants. Sequence modeling becomes *structured linear algebra*: architecture = matrix structure; implementation = multiplication algorithm.

### 4.3 The IO calculus: why kernel fusion beats FLOPs

Let B, L, D, N be batch, length, model dim, and state dim. The fused kernel's HBM traffic is Θ(B·L·D) while its FLOPs are Θ(B·L·D·N): arithmetic intensity Θ(N) ≈ 16 FLOP/byte—*above* the A100's ridge point, i.e. **compute-bound**. An unfused implementation streams Θ(B·L·D·N) state through HBM, collapsing intensity to Θ(1) and the kernel to **memory-bound**, up to ~16× slower on the roofline model. This quantifies why the hardware-aware algorithm, not just selectivity, determines Mamba's real-world speed [5].

Mamba-2's chunking [6] generalizes this: with chunk size Q, intra-chunk computation uses the dual (quadratic-in-Q) form with dense matmuls—maximal tensor-core utilization—while inter-chunk state passing uses the linear form. Choosing Q ≈ 64–256 balances the two, and the entire algorithm stays in SRAM per chunk, exactly mirroring FlashAttention-2's tiling strategy [Dao, 2023]. The lesson: **SSM research converged, independently, on the same IO-aware tiling discipline that made attention fast.**

| Kernel | HBM traffic | FLOPs | Bound by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive selective scan | O(B·L·D·N) | O(B·L·D·N) | memory |
| Mamba fused scan | O(B·L·D) | O(B·L·D·N) | compute |
| Mamba-2 SSD (chunked) | O(B·L·D) | O(B·L·D·N) | compute (matmul-heavy) |
| FlashAttention-2 | O(L²·d / M)† | O(L²·d) | compute |

† Tiled over SRAM size M; exact constant depends on block sizes.

### 4.4 What selectivity buys: the content-based reasoning gap

Prior subquadratic models fail on *discrete, information-dense* modalities (language) because their sequence mixing is input-independent: S4's kernel (3) treats all tokens identically, so it cannot implement the "selective copying" or "induction head" behaviors that attention performs trivially. Mamba's ablations [5] isolate this: on the *Selective Copying* synthetic (copy distinguished tokens from noise), LTI SSMs achieve ~0% accuracy while the selective SSM achieves 100%; on *Induction Heads* (complete the pattern …A B …A → B), selectivity closes most of the gap to attention. With Δ_t → ∞ the model *resets* (Ā_t → 0); with Δ_t → 0 it *ignores* the input. No LTI system can do both conditionally—selectivity, not state size, is the decisive innovation for language [5].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: the SSD duality identity

*Claim.* The scalar-A selective recurrence (11) equals the matrix form (13).

*Proof.* Unroll (11): H_t = Σ_{s≤t} (Π_{k=s+1}^{t} a_k) B_s x_sᵀ. Then y_tᵀ = C_tᵀH_t = Σ_{s≤t} C_tᵀB_s (Π_{k=s+1}^{t} a_k) x_s. Stack y_t as rows of Y and x_s as rows of X; the coefficient of x_s in y_t is exactly [L ∘ (CBᵀ)]_{ts} with L_{ts} = Π_{k=s+1}^{t} a_k for s ≤ t. ∎

Since a_t = exp(−softplus(·)·exp(a)) ∈ (0, 1] is the ZOH discretization of a scalar decay rate, L_{ts} is the *survival probability* of information from s to t—a probabilistic reading of the attention mask [6].

### 5.2 Benchmark evidence

- **Long Range Arena.** S4 solved Path-X (16k length) where all prior models failed and set SoTA on every LRA task [3]; S5 later averaged 87.4% [Smith et al., 2022].
- **Language modeling.** Mamba-3B matches 2×-larger Transformers in pretraining and downstream evals [5]; Mamba-2-8B trains stably to 1M-token contexts, competitive with attention at equal FLOPs [6].
- **Throughput.** Mamba achieves 5× higher generation throughput (no KV cache; O(1) per-step updates) [5]; SSD kernels are 2–8× faster than Mamba-1's scan [6].
- **Hybrid deployments.** Jamba interleaves Transformer and Mamba layers (1:7) to reach 256K context with a 4× smaller KV cache [7]; Zamba, Bamba, Nemotron-H, and Codestral-Mamba confirm the pattern: *dense SSM layers for throughput, sparse attention for recall* [8].

### 5.3 Complexity summary

| Model | Train time | Inference/step | Memory (train) | Selectivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer | O(L²d) | O(L·d) (KV cache) | O(L²) | yes |
| S4 / S4D | Õ(L + N) | O(N) | O(N + L) | no |
| Mamba-1 | O(L·D·N) | O(D·N) | O(L·D) | yes (Δ, B, C) |
| Mamba-2 (SSD) | O(L·D·N)† | O(D·N) | O(L·D) | yes (a, B, C, X) |

† Same FLOPs as Mamba-1 but matmul-dominant: far higher achieved TFLOP/s.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Associative recall deficit.** Pure SSMs compress history into a fixed-size state, which is information-theoretically lossy: on associative-recall-heavy benchmarks (multi-query associative recall, phonebook lookup), selective SSMs still trail attention, motivating hybrid architectures [6, 7, 8].
2. **Stiff discretization.** The ZOH exponential exp(ΔA) is sensitive to Δ's parameterization; poorly initialized Δ distributions cause vanishing/exploding effective memory horizons, and the softplus–exp(a) reparameterization of Mamba-2, while stabilizing, couples the timescale to a learned scalar with limited expressivity [6].
3. **Scalar-A restriction.** SSD's duality proof requires A = a·I (scalar-times-identity) [6]; the richer DPLR/HiPPO structure of S4 is sacrificed, and whether selectivity *plus* full HiPPO structure would recover the lost recall capacity is open.
4. **Scan kernel portability.** The fused selective-scan kernels are hand-tuned CUDA (and recently Triton/TileLang ports); on non-NVIDIA hardware the IO advantages shrink, and naive ports fall back to the memory-bound regime of §4.3.
5. **Scaling laws.** Controlled scaling-law comparisons of selective SSMs versus Transformers remain contested: some studies find SSMs scale *better* per-FLOP on long contexts but worse on knowledge-intensive short-context tasks, suggesting the optimal architecture is task-dependent rather than universal [8].

## 7 Conclusion

From HiPPO's optimal memory [1] through S4's Cauchy kernel [3], S4D's diagonal form [4], Mamba's fused selective scan [5], and Mamba-2's SSD duality [6], the lesson is that the future of sequence modeling is *co-designed*: mathematical structure (semiseparable matrices, associative scans, optimal projections) and execution strategy (kernel fusion, SRAM tiling, chunked matmuls) developed jointly, each enabling the other. The SSD framework's unification of attention and recurrence as two orderings of structured matrix multiplication suggests the field's next phase is not "SSMs versus Transformers" but a *design space of structured sequence operators*—with hybrids [7] as the pragmatic frontier and a full algebraic taxonomy as the theoretical one. The open challenges—associative recall, stiff discretization, and portable kernels—are precisely the problems where the co-design perspective is most needed.
