# Floer Homology and Pseudoholomorphic Curves: The Arnold Conjecture, the Floer Differential, and Symplectic Invariants

## Abstract

This thesis develops Hamiltonian Floer homology from first principles: a time-dependent Hamiltonian on a symplectic manifold generates a flow whose one-periodic orbits are the critical points of the symplectic action functional on the loop space. The formal negative gradient flow of this functional is the Floer equation, a nonlinear Cauchy–Riemann equation whose solutions are pseudoholomorphic cylinders asymptotic to periodic orbits [9][10]. Counting isolated solutions defines a differential on the module generated by the orbits; energy quantization, Gromov compactness, transversality, and gluing of broken trajectories combine to prove the differential squares to zero [8][11]. Continuation maps identify the resulting homology with the ordinary homology of the manifold, and a rank argument delivers the homological Arnold conjecture: a nondegenerate Hamiltonian diffeomorphism possesses at least as many contractible fixed points as the total rank of the manifold's homology [5][6]. The thesis further treats spectral invariants from the action filtration and their role in Hofer geometry [12], and closes with the passage to symplectic field theory and the Weinstein conjecture on closed Reeb orbits.

## 1 Introduction

In 1965, Vladimir Arnold conjectured a symplectic analogue of the Morse inequalities: the number of fixed points of a generic Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a compact symplectic manifold should be bounded below by a topological invariant of the manifold, in the same way that the number of critical points of a Morse function is bounded below by the topology of its domain [5][6]. The conjecture resisted direct attack for two decades because the natural variational setting — the space of contractible loops — is infinite-dimensional, and the relevant functional, the symplectic action, is strongly indefinite: its critical points have infinite Morse index and infinite co-index, so classical Morse theory is inapplicable.

The breakthrough came from Andreas Floer in the late 1980s. Floer's insight was that one need not construct a gradient flow in the classical sense; instead, one studies directly the *formal* gradient-flow equation of the action functional, which turns out to be an elliptic partial differential equation of Cauchy–Riemann type [9][10]. Solutions are *pseudoholomorphic curves* in the sense introduced by Gromov: maps from a Riemann surface into an almost complex manifold satisfying a perturbed holomorphicity condition [8]. The Fredholm theory of these equations, developed through the analytic machinery of Gromov compactness and gluing, allows one to mimic the entire Morse–Smale–Witten construction — chain complex, differential, continuation maps — in infinite dimensions.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the symplectic and Hamiltonian background, including the Conley–Zehnder index that grades the complex. Section 3 develops the methodology: the action functional, the Floer equation, and the moduli spaces of connecting trajectories. Section 4 contains the deep dive: the definition of the differential, the gluing theorem proving *d² = 0*, continuation and invariance, spectral invariants, and the bridge toward symplectic field theory and the Weinstein conjecture. Section 5 assembles the proof of the Arnold conjecture and records the principal computations. Section 6 discusses limitations — bubbling, transversality beyond the semipositive case, and the still-open strong form of the conjecture — and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Symplectic manifolds and Hamiltonian dynamics

A *symplectic manifold* is a pair *(M, ω)* where *M* is a smooth *2n*-dimensional manifold and *ω* is a closed, nondegenerate 2-form. Nondegeneracy means the map *TM → T\*M* given by contraction with *ω* is an isomorphism; closedness, *dω = 0*, is the integrability condition that makes symplectic geometry rigid. Given a smooth time-dependent Hamiltonian *H : S¹ × M → R*, the *Hamiltonian vector field* *X_H* is defined by *ω(X_H, ·) = dH*, and its flow *φ_H^t* consists of symplectomorphisms. The time-one map *φ = φ_H^1* is called a *Hamiltonian diffeomorphism*. Fixed points of *φ* correspond precisely to one-periodic orbits *x : S¹ → M* of *X_H*, and these are the objects the Arnold conjecture counts [5][6].

A fixed point *x* is *nondegenerate* when *1* is not an eigenvalue of the linearized return map *dφ* at *x*; equivalently, the corresponding periodic orbit is a nondegenerate critical point of the action functional. Nondegeneracy is a generic (open and dense) condition on *H*.

### 2.2 The Conley–Zehnder index

To grade a chain complex by periodic orbits, one needs an integer invariant of each nondegenerate orbit playing the role of the Morse index. This is the *Conley–Zehnder index* *μ_CZ(x)*, defined via the linearized flow along *x*, viewed as a path of symplectic matrices, and a choice of trivialization of *x\*TM* [11]. For a small autonomous Morse Hamiltonian, the Conley–Zehnder index of a critical point coincides with its Morse index shifted by *n*, which is the seed of the identification of Floer homology with ordinary homology. The index is additive under iteration up to a controlled error, and it governs the expected dimension of moduli spaces of Floer trajectories: a trajectory from *x⁻* to *x⁺* lives in a moduli space of virtual dimension *μ_CZ(x⁻) − μ_CZ(x⁺)*.

### 2.3 Pseudoholomorphic curves after Gromov

An *almost complex structure* *J* on *M* is *compatible* with *ω* if *g_J(v, w) = ω(v, Jw)* is a Riemannian metric. A map *u : (Σ, j) → (M, J)* from a Riemann surface is *(J, j)*-holomorphic when *du ∘ j = J ∘ du*. Gromov's 1985 introduction of such *pseudoholomorphic* (or *J*-holomorphic) curves revolutionized symplectic topology: despite the non-integrability of *J*, the equation is elliptic, solutions enjoy unique continuation and positivity of intersections, and sequences of bounded energy converge modulo the formation of *bubbles* — nonconstant holomorphic spheres or discs splitting off at points of energy concentration [8]. The compactification of moduli spaces by stable maps with bubble trees, Gromov's compactness theorem, is the analytic backbone of everything that follows. McDuff and Salamon's treatise gives the complete proofs of compactness, transversality via Sard–Smale theory, and the gluing theorems used throughout Floer theory [8].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The action functional on the loop space

Let *ℒM* denote the space of contractible loops *x : S¹ → M* (Sobolev class *W^{1,2}* for analysis). Fix a reference disc for each loop to resolve the multivaluedness of the symplectic area. The *symplectic action functional* is

$$\mathcal{A}_H(x) = -\int_{D^2} \bar{x}^*\omega + \int_{S^1} H_t(x(t))\,dt,$$

where *x̄ : D² → M* extends *x*. Its critical points are exactly the one-periodic orbits of *X_H*; its *L²*-gradient with respect to the metric induced by a compatible *J* is *−J(ẋ − X_H(x))*. The Hessian at a critical point is a self-adjoint Fredholm operator whose spectral flow computes the Conley–Zehnder index [11].

### 3.2 The Floer equation

The negative gradient-flow equation *∂_s u + ∇𝒜_H(u) = 0* for a path *u : R → ℒM* becomes, writing *u(s, t)* with *s* the flow parameter and *t ∈ S¹*,

$$\partial_s u + J_t(u)\bigl(\partial_t u - X_{H_t}(u)\bigr) = 0,$$

the *Floer equation*: a perturbed Cauchy–Riemann equation for maps *u : R × S¹ → M* asymptotic as *s → ±∞* to periodic orbits *x^±*. Finite-energy solutions converge exponentially to their asymptotes. The linearization at a solution is Fredholm, and its index equals *μ_CZ(x⁻) − μ_CZ(x⁺)*, so that for generic *(J, H)* the moduli space *ℳ(x⁻, x⁺)* of solutions modulo the *R*-translation in *s* is a smooth manifold of dimension *μ_CZ(x⁻) − μ_CZ(x⁺) − 1* [9][10][11].

### 3.3 Compactness, energy, and bubbling

The *energy* of a Floer trajectory,

$$E(u) = \int_{R \times S^1} \lvert \partial_s u \rvert^2\,ds\,dt = \mathcal{A}_H(x^-) - \mathcal{A}_H(x^+),$$

is a priori bounded by the action difference of its asymptotes. Gromov compactness asserts that a sequence of bounded-energy trajectories converges, after passing to a subsequence, to a *broken trajectory* — a chain of trajectories joined at intermediate orbits — possibly with holomorphic spheres bubbling off at finitely many points [8]. In the *semipositive* or *aspherical* setting (where *ω* vanishes on *π₂(M)*, or *c₁* is nonnegative on spheres), bubbles of negative index cannot occur for dimensional reasons, and the compactification of one- and two-dimensional moduli spaces is described purely by broken trajectories. This is the regime in which Floer's original construction runs cleanly [9][10]; the general case requires the virtual perturbation machinery of Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono [1][2][3].

### 3.4 Orientations and coefficients

Over *Z/2* no orientation questions arise. With integer coefficients, each moduli space *ℳ(x⁻, x⁺)* must be coherently oriented; the determinant line bundle of the linearized operator is orientable, and a choice of coherent orientations yields signs *ε(u) = ±1* weighting the count [2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Floer chain complex and the differential

Fix a nondegenerate Hamiltonian *H* and generic *J*. The *Floer chain complex* is the free module

$$CF_*(H) = \bigoplus_{x \in \mathcal{P}(H)} \Lambda \cdot x,$$

graded by the Conley–Zehnder index, where *𝒫(H)* is the set of contractible one-periodic orbits and *Λ* is a Novikov ring recording homology classes of cylinders. The *Floer differential* counts rigid trajectories:

$$d\,x^- = \sum_{\substack{x^+ \\ \mu_{CZ}(x^-) - \mu_{CZ}(x^+) = 1}} \#\bigl(\mathcal{M}(x^-, x^+)/R\bigr)\; x^+,$$

where the count is the signed number of points of the zero-dimensional moduli space. Because the action strictly decreases along nonconstant trajectories, the sum is finite in each action window, and the Novikov completion handles the infinite tail [9][10][11].

> **Theorem:** *(Floer)* For generic *(H, J)* on a closed symplectically aspherical manifold, *(CF_\*(H), d)* is a chain complex: *d² = 0*. Its homology *HF_\*(H)* is independent of the choices.

The proof of *d² = 0* is the heart of the theory and occupies the next subsection.

### 4.2 Gluing and the identity *d² = 0*

Consider the coefficient of *x⁺* in *d²x⁻* where *μ_CZ(x⁻) − μ_CZ(x⁺) = 2*. It counts pairs of index-1 trajectories *(u₁, u₂)* with *u₁ ∈ ℳ(x⁻, y)* and *u₂ ∈ ℳ(y, x⁺)* for intermediate orbits *y* of index one below *x⁻*. Geometrically, such a pair is a *once-broken trajectory*. The one-dimensional moduli space *ℳ(x⁻, x⁺)* has a compactification whose boundary consists exactly of these broken trajectories (bubbling being excluded by asphericity) [8].

> **Theorem:** *(Gluing)* Let *(u₁, u₂)* be a regular once-broken trajectory of total index 2. Then there exists *R₀ > 0* and a smooth embedding of *(R₀, ∞)* into *ℳ(x⁻, x⁺)*, the *gluing map*, whose image is a neighborhood of the broken end; the glued trajectories converge to *(u₁, u₂)* in the Gromov topology as the gluing parameter tends to infinity.

> **Proof:** *(Sketch.)* One constructs an approximate solution by splicing *u₁* and *u₂* with a long neck of length *R*, using cutoff functions. The linearized operator at the approximate solution is uniformly invertible for large *R* because the pieces are regular; a quantitative implicit function theorem (Newton iteration in weighted Sobolev spaces) corrects the approximation to an exact solution. Uniqueness of the correction gives the embedding property. ∎

Since a compact one-manifold has boundary points occurring in pairs, the signed count of boundary points of the compactified *ℳ(x⁻, x⁺)* vanishes — and that count is precisely the coefficient of *x⁺* in *d²x⁻*. Hence *d² = 0* [9][10][11]. The argument is a perfect infinite-dimensional analogue of the Morse-theoretic proof that the Morse–Witten differential squares to zero, with broken flow lines replaced by broken pseudoholomorphic cylinders.

A minimal computational model of this algebra over *Z/2* can be written down directly:

```python
# Toy model: Floer differential over Z/2 as a dictionary-of-sets matrix.
# Generators graded by Conley-Zehnder index; d lowers degree by 1.
def differential(generators, rigid_trajectories):
    d = {x: set() for x in generators}
    for (x_minus, x_plus) in rigid_trajectories:
        # each rigid cylinder contributes 1 mod 2
        d[x_minus] = d[x_minus] ^ {x_plus}  # symmetric difference = addition mod 2
    return d

def compose_squared(d, generators):
    # coefficient of z in d^2(x): count y with y in d(x) and z in d(y), mod 2
    d2 = {}
    for x in generators:
        targets = set()
        for y in d[x]:
            targets = targets ^ d[y]
        d2[x] = targets
    return d2

# Gluing theorem (combinatorial shadow): every index-2 broken pair (x->y, y->z)
# appears as the boundary of exactly one end of a 1-dim moduli space,
# so contributions cancel pairwise and d^2 = 0.
```

```haskell
-- Type-level sketch of the Floer package (grading by Conley-Zehnder index)
data Orbit = Orbit { czIndex :: Int, action :: Double }

-- A rigid Floer cylinder lowers the index by exactly one
data Cylinder = Cylinder { from :: Orbit, to :: Orbit }

rigid :: Cylinder -> Bool
rigid c = czIndex (from c) - czIndex (to c) == 1

-- The differential: sum over rigid cylinders (over Z/2, multiplicities mod 2)
d :: [Orbit] -> [Cylinder] -> Orbit -> [Orbit]
d _ cyls x = [ to c | c <- cyls, from c == x, rigid c ]
```

### 4.3 Continuation maps, invariance, and the PSS isomorphism

Given two Hamiltonians *H⁰, H¹*, a homotopy *H^s* induces *continuation* solutions of the *s*-dependent Floer equation; counting rigid continuation cylinders defines a chain map *Φ : CF_\*(H⁰) → CF_\*(H¹)*. Homotopies of homotopies give chain homotopies, so the induced map on homology is canonical, and *HF_\*(H)* is independent of *H* [9][11]. For a *C²*-small autonomous Morse Hamiltonian, Floer trajectories are in bijection with Morse flow lines, and the Conley–Zehnder index equals Morse index plus *n*; this yields the *PSS isomorphism* (Piunikhin–Salamon–Schwarz) *HF_\*(H) ≅ H_{\*+n}(M)* [7][11]. The rank of the chain complex is the number of periodic orbits; the rank of its homology is the total Betti number. The Arnold inequality follows immediately.

### 4.4 Spectral invariants and Hofer geometry

The differential *decreases* the action, so the subcomplexes *CF^a_\*(H)* generated by orbits with *𝒜_H < a* filter the complex. For a homology class *α ∈ H_\*(M)*, the *spectral invariant*

$$c(H, \alpha) = \inf \{\, a \mid \alpha \text{ is represented in } HF_*(CF^a_*(H)) \,\}$$

is the min-max action level at which *α* is born in the filtration [12]. These numbers are *Lipschitz* with respect to the Hofer norm, satisfy a triangle inequality under composition, and descend to invariants of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms. They define *symplectic capacities*, detect nondisplaceability, and give rise to *partial symplectic quasi-states*; Entov–Polterovich's theory builds Calabi quasi-morphisms on *Ham(M, ω)* from the asymptotics of *c(H, [M])* [12]. The filtered theory thus refines the bare existence statement of the Arnold conjecture into quantitative symplectic rigidity.

### 4.5 Toward the Weinstein conjecture and symplectic field theory

The gluing analysis of Section 4.2 reappears in SFT as the coherent compactification of moduli spaces of punctured curves; the Weinstein conjecture itself was proved in dimension three via embedded contact homology, a Floer-theoretic invariant counting embedded pseudoholomorphic curves [5].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 The Arnold conjecture, proved

> **Theorem:** *(Homological Arnold conjecture; Floer)* Let *(M, ω)* be closed and symplectically aspherical, and let *φ* be a nondegenerate Hamiltonian diffeomorphism. Then the number of contractible fixed points of *φ* satisfies
> $$|\mathrm{Fix}(\phi)| \geq \sum_{i} \dim H_i(M; \mathbb{K})$$
> for any coefficient field *K*.

> **Proof:** *(Sketch.)* Choose *H* with *φ_H^1 = φ* nondegenerate. The Floer complex *CF_\*(H)* has one generator per contractible fixed point, so its rank equals *|Fix(φ)|*. By Sections 4.1–4.2 it is a chain complex; by Section 4.3, *HF_\*(H) ≅ H_{\*+n}(M; K)*. For any chain complex, rank of the complex ≥ rank of its homology. ∎

Stronger forms hold: for the torus *T^{2n}* the bound is *2^{2n}* (Conley–Zehnder), and for *CP^n* it is *n + 1* in the nondegenerate case, with degenerate versions phrased via cup-length and Lusternik–Schnirelmann category [5][6]. The *Hofer–Zehnder conjecture* pushes further: a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of *CP^d* has either exactly *d + 1* or infinitely many periodic points — a symplectic analogue of Franks' theorem for the sphere — proved in the monotone semisimple case via barcode techniques in Floer theory [5].

### 5.2 Sample computations

|-------------------|-------------|--------------|---------|
| *T^{2n}* (standard) | *H_{\*+n}(T^{2n})*, rank *2^{2n}* | *2^{2n}* fixed points | Conley–Zehnder [6] |
| *CP^n* (Fubini–Study) | *H_{\*+n}(CP^n)*, rank *n+1* | *n+1* fixed points | Fortune; Hofer–Zehnder dichotomy [5][6] |

| Monotone *M*, monotone *L* | *HF(L, L)* via Oh's spectral sequence | Lagrangian intersections | Minimal Maslov ≥ 3 [4] |

### 5.3 The Lagrangian intersection picture

Floer's original setting was Lagrangian: for transverse Lagrangians *L₀, L₁*, intersection points generate a complex whose differential counts pseudoholomorphic *strips* with boundary on the Lagrangians [9]. Hamiltonian Floer homology is the special case *L₀ = Δ*, *L₁ = graph(φ)* in *M × M*. The general theory — obstructed by disc bubbling of codimension one — was constructed by Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono using Kuranishi structures and bounding cochains; in the monotone case with minimal Maslov number at least 3, Oh's earlier work shows the naive count already works [1][2][3][4].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Transversality and bubbling.** The clean story above requires excluding holomorphic spheres of negative Chern number. Beyond semipositive manifolds, one needs virtual fundamental chains: Kuranishi structures with multivalued perturbations [1][2][3]. The analysis is formidable and still being streamlined (e.g., via polyfolds).
2. **The strong Arnold conjecture is open.** The estimate *|Fix(φ)| ≥ crit(M)* (minimal number of critical points of a smooth function) and even the Morse-number version for nondegenerate *φ* remain unproved in general; only the homological bound and special cases (aspherical, *CP^n* via Conley index methods) are known [5][6].
3. **Degenerate Hamiltonians.** The complex as defined needs nondegeneracy; degenerate fixed points require local Floer homology and delicate perturbation arguments, which also power the Hofer–Zehnder-type multiplicity results [5].
4. **Coefficients and gradings.** Integral lifts need coherent orientations and spin structures [2]; the Conley–Zehnder grading is only well-defined up to the minimal Chern number, forcing Novikov coefficients or cyclic gradings in general.
5. **Computability.** Outside toric, cotangent, and a few other classes, *HF* is known only abstractly via the PSS isomorphism; direct chain-level computation remains rare, and spectral invariants are effectively computable only in special cases [7][12].

## 7 Conclusion

Floer homology realizes Arnold's vision: the fixed points of a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism satisfy Morse-type inequalities because they are the critical points of the action functional on the loop space, and the *formal* Morse theory of that functional is made rigorous by the elliptic theory of pseudoholomorphic curves [9][10]. The three analytic pillars — Fredholm transversality, Gromov compactness, and gluing — convert a nonlinear Cauchy–Riemann problem into a chain complex whose homology recovers the topology of the manifold, forcing the existence of periodic orbits [8][11]. The same machinery, filtered by the action, yields spectral invariants and Hofer-geometric rigidity [12]; bent toward contact boundaries, it becomes Rabinowitz–Floer theory and symplectic field theory, aimed at the Weinstein conjecture [3][5]. Open fronts — the strong Arnold conjecture, effective virtual techniques, and quantitative computability — ensure that the variational viewpoint Floer introduced will continue to drive symplectic topology [1][2][6].
