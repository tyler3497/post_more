# Discriminative Learning of RNA Secondary Structure: Reconciling Nussinov-Jacobson Maximization, Zuker-Stiegler Minimization, McCaskill Partition Functions, and CONTRAfold Conditional Log-Linear Models via the Inside-Outside Algorithm

## Abstract
RNA secondary structure prediction has progressed from combinatorial base-pair maximization to thermodynamic minimization to discriminative probabilistic learning, yet the underlying dynamic programming architecture has remained invariant. This thesis traces the lineage from the Nussinov-Jacobson O(N^3) maximal matching algorithm through the Zuker-Stiegler nearest-neighbor free-energy model parameterized by Turner enthalpies, to the McCaskill partition function which reinterprets energy minimization as equilibrium statistical mechanics yielding base-pair probabilities and ensemble diversity. We reformulate these models in the formal language of stochastic context-free grammars (SCFGs) and show that the inside-outside algorithm, originally due to Baker and refined by Lari and Young for EM training of SCFGs, is the common computational substrate unifying partition function computation, posterior decoding, and conditional log-linear training. Finally we analyze CONTRAfold of Do, Woods and Batzoglou, a conditional log-linear model that discards physics-based constraints in favor of discriminatively trained feature weights, and demonstrate why discriminative training with structure-regularized MEA decoding closes the accuracy gap over generative SCFGs and minimum free energy methods.

## 1. Introduction

Single-stranded RNA folds by forming intramolecular Watson-Crick (A-U, G-C) and wobble (G-U) pairs into a set of non-crossing helices punctuated by loops. The resulting secondary structure is both thermodynamically dominant and functionally determinative, governing tRNA adaptor function, riboswitch regulation, splice site accessibility, and viral genome packaging. Predicting this structure from sequence alone is a foundational problem of computational biology that forces confrontation with counting, energetics, and learning.

The history can be read as progressive enrichment of scoring: first counting pairs, then summing nearest-neighbor free energies, then summing Boltzmann weights of all structures, then learning arbitrary features discriminatively. At every step the optimization engine is dynamic programming over intervals, isomorphic to parsing with a context-free grammar. This thesis argues that the Inside-Outside algorithm is the conceptual bridge.

We assume familiarity with O(N^3) DP, formal grammars in Chomsky Normal Form, and exponential families. Contributions are expository and synthetic: (i) unified recurrences for Nussinov, Zuker, and McCaskill; (ii) explicit mapping to SCFGs; (iii) derivation of Inside-Outside for EM and for conditional random fields; (iv) critical analysis of CONTRAfold feature engineering and discriminative objective.

## 2. Preliminaries: Structure and Complexity

Let x = x_1 ... x_N ∈ {A,C,G,U}^N. A secondary structure y is a set of pairs (i,j) with 1 ≤ i < j ≤ N such that (i) each position participates in at most one pair, (ii) no crossing: (i,j),(k,l) ∈ y and i < k implies either j < k or l < j or j < l is forbidden if i < k < j < l, (iii) hairpin minimum loop length: j-i > 3 for typical parameterization, (iv) pair complementarity: (x_i,x_j) ∈ {AU,UA,GC,CG,GU,UG}. Non-crossing permits representation as well-balanced dot-paren string or rooted tree where internal nodes are loops closed by pairs.

Number of possible structures grows ~ 1.8^N asymptotically (Waterman), rendering exhaustive enumeration infeasible beyond N≈30. Yet non-crossing allows optimal decomposition: any structure on [i,j] either leaves i unpaired or pairs i with some k and recursively solves [i+1,k-1] and [k+1,j]. This optimal substructure yields all polynomial DP algorithms discussed herein.

Pseudoknots violating (ii) are NP-hard in general energy models and excluded from classical DP; we follow that restriction noting that CONTRAfold similarly excludes pseudoknots despite CRF extensions that could in principle model them with increased complexity.

## 3. Nussinov-Jacobson: Maximal Matching

Nussinov and Jacobson (1980) introduced the first non-trivial DP for RNA folding [0]. Their insight: maximize number of pairs rather than realistic energy.

Define S(i,j) = maximal number of pairs achievable on substring x_i..x_j. Recurrence in modern simplified form:

```
S(i,i) = S(i,i-1) = 0
S(i,j) = max { S(i+1,j),  max_{i<k≤j, complement(x_i,x_k)} [1 + S(i+1,k-1) + S(k+1,j)] }
```

Equivalent symmetric form with split:

```
S(i,j) = max { S(i+1,j-1) + δ(i,j), max_{i≤k<j} S(i,k)+S(k+1,j) }
```

where δ(i,j)=1 if complementary else 0. Matrix fill order increasing length L=j-i, O(N^3) time (O(N^2) possible with optimization of split but naive is cubic), O(N^2) storage, traceback via pointers. A related variant permits mismatched stacking ignored.

Limitations are immediate: all pairs equal, no stacking cooperativity, no loop penalties, abundant spurious short helices. Yet algorithmic scaffold endures: upper-triangular DP, inside recursion, optimal parsing. The algorithm is exactly CYK for grammar S → S S | '(' S ')' | '.' S | ε with uniform weight 1 for pair productions. Recognizing this grammatical viewpoint later enabled SCFG treatment.

Nussinov-Jacobson showed 1-2 orders of magnitude speedup over prior enumeration, enabling N=1000 searches in 1980 on long RNAs. Their "two half-matrices" construction corresponds to separation of interior and exterior fragments – precursor to inside/outside.

## 4. Thermodynamics Enters: Zuker-Stiegler and Turner Parameters

The biological inadequacy of pair counting motivated incorporation of experimental thermodynamics. Salser, Tinoco, Uhlenbeck and later Turner lab measured stacking enthalpies and entropies via optical melting of model oligonucleotides. The nearest-neighbor model posits free energy is additive over loops: stacking pair (i,j)-(i+1,j-1), hairpin closed by (i,j), bulge/internal loop with helices (i,j) and (k,l), and multibranch (multiloop) closed by (i,j) enclosing >=2 helices.

Zuker and Stiegler (1981) adapted DP to minimize ΔG°_37 [3]. Key innovation: two tables.

- W(i,j): minimum free energy of any structure on [i,j].
- V(i,j): minimum free energy of structure on [i,j] given i,j paired (closed).

```
W(i,j) = min { W(i+1,j), min_{i<k≤j} W(i,k)+W(k+1,j), V(i,j) }
V(i,j) = min { EH(i,j), ES(i,j)+V(i+1,j-1), min_{i<p<q<j} EI(i,j,p,q)+V(p,q), WM manipulation for multiloop }
```

EH hairpin energy tabulated by loop length and sequence (particularly stable tetraloops e.g. GNRA), ES stacking energy from Turner 1999/2004 tables (~ -0.9 to -3.4 kcal/mol per stack), EI internal/bulge with asymmetry penalties and length-dependent entropic term, and multibranch closure:

```
VM(i,j) = a + b*unpaired + c*branches + sum W
```

a,b,c are affine multibranch parameters fitted from data (e.g., a=3.4, b=0.4, c=0.1 kcal/mol approximations; actual Vienna RNA Package differs per implementation details across MFOLD, RNAfold, RNAstructure).

Space remains O(N^2), time O(N^3) if interior loop size bounded by L_max ≈30 (yielding O(N^2*L_max^2) worst but in practice O(N^3) worst with optimization). Inclusion of coaxial stacking and dangling ends as in Mathews-Turner 1999/2004 refinements improves MFE accuracy from ~40% to ~70% base-pair sensitivity on long ribosomal RNAs.

Zuker's algorithm also introduced suboptimal enumeration: backtrack generating structures within ΔΔG of MFE. This mitigates ill-conditioning noted by Zuker: small parameter perturbations cause large MFE shifts.

Crucially, V and W recurrences are structurally identical to Nussinov but weighted: max → min, 1 → energy terms. This anticipates algebraic generalization to semiring parsing.

## 5. Ensemble Thinking: The McCaskill Partition Function

Minimum free energy prediction commits to single structure, ignoring thermal ensemble. McCaskill (1990) pioneered equilibrium partition function approach [2]. For structure s with free energy E(s) computed via Turner model, define Boltzmann weight exp(-E(s)/RT), R gas constant, T temperature. Partition function over all structures on [i,j]:

```
Z(i,j) = Σ_{s on [i,j]} exp(-E(s)/RT)
Q^b(i,j) = Σ_{s on [i,j], i:j paired} exp(-E(s)/RT)
```

Recurrences mirror Zuker but with (+) → log-sum-exp:

```
Q^b(i,j) = exp(-EH/RT) + exp(-ES/RT)*Q^b(i+1,j-1) + Σ_{p,q} exp(-EI(i,j,p,q)/RT)*Q^b(p,q) + multibranch terms involving Q^b and Q^m
Q(i,j) = 1 + Σ_{i≤k<j} Q(i,k)*Q^b(k+1,j) ... (precise handling of linear segmentation)
```

Similar to Nussinov, the DP requires O(N^3) O(N^2) with interior loop bounding. Total partition Z = Q(1,N).

Base-pair probabilities arise by outside computation:

```
P(i,j) = Q^b(i,j) * Q^outer(i,j) / Q(1,N)
```

where Q^outer is outside partition function aggregating contributions external to interval [i,j] that co-occur with i:j closed. McCaskill derived recursive formulation for outside without explicit outside table by using auxiliary Q 'outside' decomposition or equivalently by back-propagation through DP graph: Q_outer(i,j) computed top-down decreasing length.

Thus McCaskill algorithm is exactly Inside-Outside specialised to energy-weighted grammar: Inside = partition of inside fragment, Outside = partition of context. Interpretation as probability of a production in derivation under Boltzmann distribution.

Benefits: confidence measures for MFE pairs (high P pairs have higher positive predictive value per Mathews 2004), entropy calculation: structural entropy S = -Σ p(s) log p(s) computable from partition functions and expected energy, melting curves via temperature dependence of Z, and sampling of Boltzmann ensemble (Ding & Lawrence sfold). Drew (2007) noted probabilities robust to parameter uncertainty vs MFE point estimate. Implementation dominates Vienna RNA Package RNAfold -p, sfold, CONTRAfold probabilistic features.

Logarithmic box matrix display: McCaskill suggested plotting -log10 P(i,j) as square area; this dot-plot remains standard.

## 6. SCFGs and the Inside-Outside Algorithm

Stochastic Context-Free Grammars provide probabilistic language for RNA ideal for training. Grammar G = (N, Σ, R, S) nonterminals, terminals {A,C,G,U,(, ), .} or base emission productions, rules weighted with probabilities summing to 1 per LHS. Typical toy grammar G1:

```
S → S S | a S a' P | a S | S a | a  (probabilities p1...)
P → a S a' | ...
```

where a∈Σ and a' is complementary pair. More refined G6 (common in benchmarks) distinguishes loop types.

A derivation corresponds one-to-one to secondary structure (unambiguous grammar required for probability validity; ambiguity duplicates enumeration and invalidates partition function – analogous to Nussinov vs Zuker unambiguous vs ambiguous variants noted by Janssen et al.). Likelihood of sequence x is inside probability α(S,1,N):

Inside recursion (Baker 1979, Lari & Young 1990 formulation):

```
α(v,i,j) = Σ_{v→y z} Σ_{k=i}^{j-1} P(v→y z) α(y,i,k) α(z,k+1,j) + Σ_{v→a} P(v→a) [j=i and x_i=a]
   (for binary CNF); with pair emission need A(i,j) factor
```

Outside β(v,i,j) = probability of deriving prefix x_1..x_{i-1} and suffix x_{j+1}..N plus nonterminal v spanning i,j waiting. Recursion top-down:

```
β(S,1,N)=1
β(y,i,k) += Σ_{v→y z} Σ_{j≥k+1} β(v,i,j) P(v→y z) α(z,k+1,j)
β(z,k+1,j) += similar
```

Expectation of rule usage:

```
E[c(v→y z) | x] = (1/α(S,1,N)) Σ_{i≤k<j} β(v,i,j) P(v→y z) α(y,i,k) α(z,k+1,j)
```

EM: E-step computes expected counts via inside-outside; M-step renormalizes per LHS via MLE. This is Inside-Outside, the context-free analogue of Forward-Backward for HMMs. For explanatory scope: HMM Forward-Backward is special case where grammar generates left-branching; inside-outside generalizes to bifurcating trees necessary for nested pairing.

Connection: If rule scores are exp(-energy/RT) unnormalized, inside sum becomes partition function Q. Hence McCaskill is SCFG inside-outside with Boltzmann parameterization instead of probabilistic stochastic normalization. This unifying view articulates literature: Durbin et al. "Biological Sequence Analysis" Chapter 10 explicitly frames Nussinov/Zuker as parse scores under semiring change from max to sum-product [book].

Two decoding strategies arise from posterior probabilities:

- **CYK/Viterbi:** max-product semiring finding max-prob single derivation: δ(v,i,j) = max rules. Equivalent to Nussinov/Zuker MFE.
- **Posterior/MEA:** maximum expected accuracy: maximize expected number of correctly predicted positions/pairs relative to ensemble. Given pair probabilities P(i,j) (computed from inside-outside), MEA solves DP maximizing Σ_{paired} P(i,j)*γ + unpaired contributions. Parameter γ∈[0,10] trades sensitivity vs specificity (M. Hamada et al.). CONTRAfold adopts MEA as default because it directly optimizes accuracy metric, balancing over- and under-prediction inherent to MFE's brittle point estimate.

## 7. From Generative to Discriminative: CONTRAfold and Conditional Log-Linear Models

Generative SCFGs model joint P(x,y)=P(y)P(x|y) assigning probability to sequences even though sequence is always observed. This is wasteful for structure prediction where p(x) irrelevant and leads to probability deflection onto improbable sequences sharing similar structure patterns.

CONTRAfold (Do, Woods, Batzoglou, ISMB/Bioinformatics 2006) replaces generative SCFG with conditional log-linear model (CLLM) – essentially Conditional Random Field for context-free grammars [1][2][4]:

```
P(y|x,w) = exp( w · Φ(x,y) ) / Z(x,w)
Z(x,w) = Σ_{y'∈Y(x)} exp(w·Φ(x,y'))
```

Feature map Φ decomposes into local features over grammar productions: hairpin length features, bulge/internal loop asymmetry, base-pair identity (16 possible pairs), stacking adjacent pairs (256 stacking features), terminal mismatches, helix length, multibranch degree, etc. Unlike thermodynamic tables that assume additivity and independence of loop context, CLLM can encode arbitrary overlapping features yet remain scores additive under DP decomposition – because decomposition only requires Φ(y) = Σ_{rules} φ(rule, context) linear, not independence of probabilities.

Training objective discriminative: maximize conditional log-likelihood on curated training set S=Rfam plus comparative structures (S-Processed set of 151 S-Processed RNAs, STRAND etc.) plus elastic-net regularization:

```
L(w)= Σ_{(x,y)∈D} log P(y|x,w) - λ||w||2^2
∇_w L = Σ_{(x,y)} [ Φ(x,y) - E_{P(y'|x,w)} Φ(x,y') ] -2λw
```

Expected feature counts E[Φ] are exactly inside-outside expectations but with weighted features instead of rule counts – compute via inside-outside where inside now uses exponentiated linear scores exp(w·f). Therefore gradient computation is O(N^3) per sequence same asymptotic aspartition function. Optimization via L-BFGS quasi-Newton (convex loss? actually convex? Log-linear conditional for CFGs is convex w.r.t. w because log-partition convex – unique global optimum modulo regularization unlike generative EM non-convex). This eliminates local optima plaguing Inside-Outside EM for SCFGs noted by Lari & Young.

Second innovation: maximum expected accuracy decoding instead of max conditional. Do et al. show across k-fold cross-validation ROC curve that discriminative CLLM improves sensitivity ~2-7% over generative counterpart with identical feature set, and that CONTRAfold with full feature set (corresponding to Turner-like elaborations) achieves ~0.74 sensitivity, 0.67 specificity (? dataset dependent: S-Processed; on more recent ArchiveII higher) outperforming MFOLD 0.69/0.61, ViennaRNA 0.69/0.59, PKNots 0.60/0.53, ILM 0.53/0.41, Pfold 0.49/0.75 at single γ tuning [contrafold paper Table 3]. Subsequent CONTRAfold 2.0 added multi-threshold gamma centroid tuning, improved stacking features, and re-trained.

Ablation analysis reveals helix length and terminal mismatch penalties and internal loop asymmetry contribute most (ROC AUC drop 0.029 when removed). Stacking, traditionally considered cornerstone of thermodynamics, contributes moderate 0.015.

Computational details: semi-supervised extension by Feng (Wright State thesis) uses unlabeled sequences entropy regularization H(Y|X) approximated via posterior feature expectations, increasing F-value 3.5% with unlabeled RNA – perspective overlapping with later self-training language models for RNA. RAF approach accelerates inference via sparse constraint set from CONTRAfold posterior matrices, reducing O(N^3) to essentially O(N^2) sparse feasible per pair of homologous sequences.

Potential failure: discriminative model trained on known structural databases inherits phylogenetic and experimental bias; thermodynamic models attempt to capture physics that generalizes to unseen families. Empirical assessment on family-wise cross-validation (leave-one-family-out) shows degradation but still competitive; this remains active research with recent deep networks like MXfold2, UFold mixing physics+learning.

## 8. Evaluation, Limitations, and Extensions

Evaluation metrics sensitivity (recall) = TP/(TP+FN) predicted pairs relative to reference, specificity / PPV = TP/(TP+FP). ROC via γ variation traces trade-off, area under ROC (AUC true metric?) typical reported. S-Processed benchmark for single-sequence: CONTRAfold(γ=6) 0.7377 sensitivity 0.6686 specificity vs Mfold 0.6943/0.6063 time 224s vs 62s – trade-off speed due to richer partition calculation rather than MFE pruning.

Limitations shared across DP family: exclusion of pseudoknots (at least 5-10% of bases in many RNAs), non-additive tertiary interactions, kinetic folding traps, co-transcriptional effects. McCaskill partition assumes equilibrium, violated in vivo where co-transcriptional folding and helicase activity bias pathway. SCFG/CLLM still assumes context-freeness; process of multibranch loop entropy not fully context-free due to polymer physics constraints Jacobi length dependency, handled via heuristic affine functions.

Future: Increasing incorporation of experimental probing data (SHAPE, DMS-MaPseq) as pseudo-energy terms b*w_i*(1-x_i) sloped into V recursion (Deigan et al.). Incorporation into CONTRAfold trivial: add per-position reactivity feature. Deep learned potentials like SPOT-RNA and E2Efold attempt end-to-end differentiable DP with inside-outside layer made differentiable (Differentiable Dynamic Programming via ∂α/∂w computed via autodiff through log-sum-exp). This revives EM idea but with neural feature extractor producing w(i,j) context-dependent from Transformer embeddings of sequence – full circle from manual feature engineering to learned embeddings while still enforcing grammatical validity via DP.

## 9. Conclusion

We have traced fifty years of algorithmic refinement centralized around one invariant: dynamic programming over intervals computing inside quantities, with outside computation delivering marginal probabilities needed for posterior inference and learning. Nussinov maximization instantiated Boolean semiring; Zuker minimization instantiated tropical semiring with Turner parameters; McCaskill instantiated log semiring yielding partition functions; SCFGs instantiated probabilistic semiring learned via Inside-Outside EM; CONTRAfold lifted semiring to arbitrary feature-based conditional exponential family trained discriminatively yet decoded by same MEA routine powered by inside-outside posteriors. The progression illustrates a broader lesson of computational biology: physics informs feature architecture, but discriminative optimization refines parameters when experimental energy measurements incomplete or inaccurate. The Inside-Outside algorithm, in its algebraic generality, provides computational strata upon which both physical symmetry and statistical learning can coexist.
