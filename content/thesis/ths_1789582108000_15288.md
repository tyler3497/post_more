# The Independence of the Continuum Hypothesis: Cohen Forcing, Boolean-Valued Models, and Easton's Theorem

## Abstract

In 1900 David Hilbert placed the continuum problem first among his twenty-three problems; by 1963 Paul Cohen's invention of forcing had shown that the continuum hypothesis (CH) can be neither proved nor refuted from the Zermelo-Fraenkel axioms with choice. This article gives a unified technical exposition of the three pillars of the independence proof: Cohen forcing with countable transitive ground models, the Scott-Solovay-Vopenka Boolean-valued reformulation that eliminates the metamathematical reliance on generics, and Easton's theorem, which characterizes exactly how wild the continuum function may be on regular cardinals. We develop the forcing relation, generic filters, and Boolean truth values with full proof sketches of both Con(ZFC) to Con(ZFC plus CH) and Con(ZFC) to Con(ZFC plus the negation of CH), prove the necessity of Easton's constraints via Konig's theorem, and close with the limitations imposed by singular cardinal arithmetic and large-cardinal strength.

##1 Intro

The continuum hypothesis asserts that every infinite set of real numbers is either countable or has the cardinality of the full continuum — equivalently, under the axiom of choice, that 2^{aleph_0} = aleph_1, where aleph_1 is the least uncountable cardinal. For over sixty years it resisted every attempt at resolution, until Kurt Godel showed in 1940 that CH holds in the constructible universe L, and Paul Cohen showed in 1963 that its negation is equally consistent [1]. The combined result is the first great independence theorem of modern set theory: *assuming ZFC itself is consistent, ZFC decides neither CH nor its negation.*

Cohen's method of **forcing** did far more than settle Hilbert's first problem. It became the central technique of set theory, a general machine for building universes of sets with precisely controlled properties. Three developments structure everything that follows in this article:

1. **Cohen forcing proper**: starting from a countable transitive model M of (a fragment of) ZFC, adjoin a *generic* set G — one that meets every dense subset of a partial order P lying in M — and form the extension M[G]. By choosing P cleverly, we dictate the cardinal arithmetic of M[G].
2. **Boolean-valued models** (Scott, Solovay, Vopenka, 1965-1967): replace the generic filter, which cannot exist inside M, with truth values in a complete Boolean algebra. Every sentence receives a Boolean value; quotienting by an ultrafilter recovers a classical two-valued model. The metamathematics becomes algebraic rather than combinatorial [7].
3. **Easton's theorem** (1970): the complete classification of possible behaviors of the continuum function kappa maps to 2^kappa on *regular* cardinals. Cohen showed 2^{aleph_0} could be almost anything; Easton showed the same freedom holds simultaneously at every regular cardinal, subject to exactly two provable constraints [4].

Our goal is a self-contained, proof-oriented tour: the machinery, the two directions of independence, the Boolean-valued viewpoint, Easton's global control of cardinal arithmetic, and an honest account of what the method cannot do.

##2 Background

### Cardinals, cofinality, and Konig's theorem

We work in ZFC. A *cardinal* is an initial ordinal; aleph_alpha enumerates the infinite cardinals. The *cofinality* cf(kappa) of a cardinal kappa is the least size of an unbounded subset; kappa is *regular* if cf(kappa) = kappa and *singular* otherwise. The continuum function is kappa maps to 2^kappa, the cardinality of the power set.

> **Theorem (Konig):** For every infinite cardinal kappa, cf(2^kappa) > kappa.

*Proof sketch.* If 2^kappa were the union of at most kappa many sets each of size less than 2^kappa, a diagonal argument against any purported cofinal sequence of functions from kappa to 2 produces a function dominating all of them — contradiction. This innocent-looking inequality is the *only* ZFC-provable restriction on the continuum function at regular cardinals, as Easton will show.

### Relative consistency and countable transitive models

Independence proofs are *relative consistency* proofs. We show Con(ZFC) implies Con(ZFC + phi) for phi = CH and phi = not-CH. The standard vehicle is a **countable transitive model** (ctm) M of a finite fragment of ZFC: by the reflection theorem and Lowenheim-Skolem, if ZFC is consistent then for every finite list of axioms there is a countable transitive M satisfying them [2]. Transitivity makes the membership relation absolute, so M computes ordinals, cardinals (below its height), and power sets correctly *relative to itself* — though from outside, M's "power set of omega" may miss subsets.

Godel's constructible universe L is the canonical inner model: L is the smallest transitive class containing all ordinals and closed under definable subsets. Godel proved (L, in) satisfies ZFC + GCH, hence Con(ZF) implies Con(ZFC + CH) [1]. Cohen's task was the converse direction.

### The generalized continuum hypothesis

GCH asserts 2^kappa = kappa^+ for *every* infinite cardinal kappa, where kappa^+ is the cardinal successor. Hausdorff formulated it; Godel proved it in L. Easton's theorem is best understood as the statement that GCH is *maximally violable* at regular cardinals: nothing in ZFC beyond monotonicity and Konig's inequality constrains 2^kappa there.

##3 Methodology

### Forcing posets and generic filters

A **forcing poset** is a partially ordered set (P, <=) in M with a maximum element 1_P. Intuition: conditions are *finite approximations* to the new sets we wish to add; p <= q means p carries more information than q. A set D subset of P is *dense* if every condition has a stronger condition in D. A **filter** G is a nonempty upward-closed set of pairwise compatible conditions; G is **M-generic** if it meets every dense D in M.

The crucial combinatorial fact: no M-generic filter can belong to M itself (diagonalize against the dense sets coding membership), so G is genuinely new. The **generic extension** M[G] is built from **P-names**: sets tau in M defined recursively by tau subset of { (sigma, p) : sigma a P-name, p in P }. The interpretation tau_G replaces each name by its G-evaluation, keeping (sigma, p) precisely when p in G:

```python
# Toy model: finite partial functions as forcing conditions
# P = Fn(kappa x omega, 2), ordered by reverse inclusion
def is_stronger(p, q):
    """p <= q iff p extends q as a partial function."""
    return all(p.get(k) == v for k, v in q.items())

def dense_agrees_on(n, kappa):
    """Dense set: conditions deciding the n-th Cohen real's values."""
    return lambda p: any((a, m) in p for a in range(kappa) for m in range(n))

def generic_meets_all_dense(G, dense_family):
    return all(any(is_stronger(g, d) or True for g in G) for d in dense_family)
```

### The forcing relation

The semantic heart of the method is the **forcing relation** p forces phi, written p ||- phi: every M-generic filter containing p makes phi true of the interpreted names. The **forcing theorem** (the definability half proved inside M, the truth half in the metatheory) states:

> **Theorem (Forcing theorem):** For M-generic G, M[G] satisfies phi(tau_1_G, ..., tau_n_G) if and only if some p in G forces phi(tau_1, ..., tau_n). Moreover the relation "p forces phi" is definable inside M.

Definability inside M is what lets M "talk about" the extension before it exists; truth in the metatheory is what makes M[G] a genuine model of ZFC. Verifying the ZFC axioms in M[G] is then a uniform argument: extensionality, pairing, union, power set, separation, replacement, choice all transfer, with power set and replacement requiring the most care [2].

### Boolean-valued methodology

The Boolean-valued approach replaces the poset by its **regular open algebra** RO(P), a complete Boolean algebra, and assigns every sentence a truth value [[phi]] in that algebra. The universe V^B of Boolean-valued names is defined by transfinite recursion entirely inside the ground model — no generic filter is ever assumed to exist. Only at the very end does one quotient by an ultrafilter U to obtain a classical model V^B / U, and the Los-style theorem for Boolean-valued models guarantees it satisfies exactly the sentences of Boolean value in U [7].

### Easton forcing

To control 2^kappa at *many* cardinals at once, Easton uses a **product forcing**: at each regular kappa, independently add F(kappa) many Cohen subsets of kappa, where F is the target continuum function. The product uses **Easton support** — a condition's support is bounded below each regular cardinal — which preserves just enough closure and chain condition at each coordinate to keep all cardinals intact while realizing F everywhere [4][5].

The following table summarizes the three forcing architectures used in this article:

| Forcing | Conditions | New objects | Cardinal effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fn(kappa x omega, 2) | finite binary partial functions | kappa Cohen reals | 2^{aleph_0} >= kappa |
| Coll(omega, lambda) | countable partial functions | collapse of lambda | lambda becomes countable |
| Easton product | Easton-support product of Add(kappa, F(kappa)) | F(kappa) subsets of each regular kappa | 2^kappa = F(kappa) |

##4 Deep Dive

###4.1 Cohen forcing: making the continuum large

Fix a ctm M satisfying GCH (pass to L^M if necessary) and a cardinal kappa in M with uncountable cofinality, e.g. kappa = aleph_2^M. Let P = Fn(kappa x omega, 2)^M: finite partial functions from kappa x omega to {0, 1}, ordered by reverse inclusion. A generic G determines, for each alpha < kappa, a **Cohen real** x_alpha: omega -> 2 via x_alpha(n) = 1 iff (alpha, n, 1) is in the union of G.

Two facts do all the work:

- **Distinctness.** For alpha != beta, the set D_{alpha,beta} = { p : p decides some n with p(alpha,n) != p(beta,n) } is dense; genericity forces x_alpha != x_beta. Hence M[G] contains at least kappa many distinct reals.
- **Cardinal preservation.** P has the countable chain condition (ccc): every antichain is countable. This follows from the Delta-system lemma applied to the finite domains of conditions. ccc forcings preserve all cardinals and cofinalities, so aleph_2^M remains aleph_2 in M[G].

Therefore M[G] satisfies 2^{aleph_0} >= aleph_2, i.e. not-CH [1][2]. Solovay later refined this: 2^{aleph_0} can be *any* cardinal of uncountable cofinality — "anything it ought to be," in the phrase reported in the Leiden exposition of cardinal arithmetic [6]. Uncountable cofinality is exactly Konig's constraint at kappa = aleph_0.

```haskell
-- Boolean truth values for the Cohen algebra, Haskell sketch
newtype BVal = BVal { unB :: Double -> Bool }  -- characteristic of regular open set

bAnd, bOr :: BVal -> BVal -> BVal
bAnd (BVal f) (BVal g) = BVal (\x -> f x && g x)
bOr  (BVal f) (BVal g) = BVal (\x -> f x || g x)

bVal :: String -> BVal   -- [[ phi ]] assigned by recursion on formulas
bVal _ = BVal (const True)  -- theorems of ZFC receive value 1
```

###4.2 Boolean-valued models: algebra instead of generics

Cohen's original presentation was syntactic and ramified; Scott and Solovay realized the combinatorics could be repackaged algebraically [7]. Given a complete Boolean algebra B in M, define the Boolean-valued universe:

- V^B_0 = empty set; V^B_{alpha+1} = { f : dom(f) subset of V^B_alpha, ran(f) subset of B }; take unions at limits.
- [[tau in sigma]] and [[tau = sigma]] are defined by simultaneous recursion using Boolean meets, joins, and complements over the algebra.
- Every logical axiom gets value 1_B, and modus ponens is sound: [[phi -> psi]] wedge [[phi]] <= [[psi]].

The payoff is the **quotient theorem**: for any ultrafilter U on B, the relation tau ~_U sigma iff [[tau = sigma]] in U is a congruence, and the quotient V^B / U is a classical two-valued model of precisely the sentences with Boolean value in U. Forcing becomes the special case B = RO(P): an M-generic filter G corresponds to the ultrafilter of conditions' regular-open neighborhoods, and M[G] is isomorphic to V^{RO(P)} / U_G.

Philosophically this is a genuine advance, not mere reformulation. The Boolean-valued construction never postulates a nonexistent generic object; it is *internal* to M, and the passage to a classical model is deferred to the metatheory's choice of ultrafilter. It also explains at once why the method is so robust: any complete Boolean algebra yields a model, and forcing equivalence of posets is exactly isomorphism of their regular open algebras [7].

###4.3 Easton's theorem: total control at regular cardinals

Let F be a class function on the regular cardinals. Easton's necessary and sufficient conditions [4]:

1. **Monotonicity:** kappa < lambda implies F(kappa) <= F(lambda) (a larger set has a power set at least as large).
2. **Konig's constraint:** cf(F(kappa)) > kappa for every regular kappa.

> **Theorem (Easton, 1970):** Assume ZFC is consistent and F satisfies (1) and (2). Then there is a model of ZFC with 2^kappa = F(kappa) for every regular cardinal kappa.

The forcing is the Easton-support product over regular kappa of Add(kappa, F(kappa)) — the poset adding F(kappa) many Cohen subsets of kappa with conditions of size less than kappa. The proof factors the product at each cardinal lambda into a "small" part (coordinates below lambda, which is lambda^+-cc and preserves cardinals above lambda) and a "closed" part (coordinates at and above lambda, which is lambda-closed and adds no new subsets of lambda). A counting argument then shows exactly F(lambda) many subsets of lambda appear — no more, no fewer [4].

At *singular* cardinals the model takes the minimal possible values compatible with monotonicity and Konig's inequality. Whether singular values are genuinely free is a deep question: Silver's theorem (1974) showed that if GCH holds below a singular cardinal of uncountable cofinality, it holds *at* that cardinal — so Easton-style freedom provably fails for singulars, and the full analysis requires large cardinals (Gitik's work on the negation of the Singular Cardinals Hypothesis) [6].

```rust
// Easton support: support bounded below every regular cardinal
struct EastonCondition {
    // coordinate kappa -> partial function adding Cohen subsets of kappa
    coords: std::collections::BTreeMap<Cardinal, PartialFn>,
}
fn is_easton_support(c: &EastonCondition) -> bool {
    // for each regular lambda, support intersected with lambda is bounded in lambda
    c.coords.keys().all(|&kappa| is_regular(kappa))
        && c.coords.keys().fold(true, |ok, &kappa| {
            ok && c.coords.range(..kappa).count() < kappa as usize || !is_regular(kappa)
        })
}
```

###4.4 Forcing CH: collapsing the continuum

For the other direction, start with M satisfying not-CH and *collapse* (2^{aleph_0})^M to aleph_1. The Levy collapse Coll(omega, lambda) consists of countable partial functions from omega to lambda; it is countably closed, hence adds no new reals (every new omega-sequence is already in the ground model by a closure argument). Forcing with Coll(omega, (2^{aleph_0})^M) makes the old continuum countable-sequence-surjected onto aleph_1 while the set of reals itself is unchanged — so the extension satisfies 2^{aleph_0} = aleph_1, i.e. CH [2].

Note the asymmetry: making CH true *destroys* cardinals (the old continuum collapses), while making CH false *preserves* them. This asymmetry is typical of forcing — it is easier to add sets than to remove structure.

```tla
---- MODULE GenericFilter ----
EXTENDS Naturals
CONSTANT P, Leq, DenseSets  \* poset, order, family of dense sets in M
VARIABLE G
IsFilter(G) == /\ G \subseteq P
               /\ \A p, q \in G : \E r \in G : Leq(r,p) /\ Leq(r,q)
               /\ \A p \in G : \A q \in P : (Leq(p,q) => q \in G)
IsGeneric(G) == IsFilter(G) /\ \A D \in DenseSets : G \cap D /= {}
================================
```

##5 Empirical/Proofs

We now assemble the proof sketches. "Empirical" here means *demonstrative*: each sketch is a verifiable construction whose correctness reduces to the forcing theorem and standard combinatorial lemmas.

> **Theorem:** If ZFC is consistent, so is ZFC + not-CH.

*Proof sketch.* Let M be a ctm of a finite fragment containing all axioms used in the verification, satisfying GCH. Force with P = Fn(aleph_2^M x omega, 2). By the Delta-system lemma P is ccc in M, so cardinals are preserved and aleph_2^{M[G]} = aleph_2^M. For alpha < aleph_2^M let x_alpha be the alpha-th Cohen real extracted from the generic. Density of the disagreement sets gives x_alpha != x_beta for alpha != beta, all in M[G]. Hence M[G] has at least aleph_2 many reals: 2^{aleph_0} >= aleph_2, contradicting CH. The ZFC axioms hold in M[G] by the uniform verification. Since the fragment was arbitrary, compactness yields Con(ZFC) -> Con(ZFC + not-CH) [1][2].

> **Theorem:** If ZFC is consistent, so is ZFC + CH.

*Proof sketch.* Let M be a ctm with M satisfying not-CH; put lambda = (2^{aleph_0})^M. Force with the Levy collapse Coll(omega, lambda): countable partial functions omega -> lambda. Countable closure implies no new reals are added — given a name for a real, build a descending omega-sequence of conditions deciding each bit, and take a lower bound (closure) to decide the whole real in the ground model. The generic surjection from omega onto lambda collapses lambda to aleph_1 while the reals of M[G] equal the reals of M. Thus M[G] satisfies 2^{aleph_0} = aleph_1 [2].

> **Theorem:** Easton's conditions are necessary.

*Proof sketch.* Monotonicity: if kappa < lambda there is an injection P(kappa) -> P(lambda) via X maps to X x {0} (coded appropriately), so 2^kappa <= 2^lambda in ZFC. Konig's constraint is Konig's theorem itself: cf(2^kappa) > kappa for every infinite kappa. Easton's forcing shows they are jointly sufficient on regulars [4][5].

The finite-fragment maneuver deserves emphasis: we never assume a ctm of *all* of ZFC (which would contradict Godel's second incompleteness theorem from within). For each finite list of axioms we produce a model of that list plus the desired statement; by compactness, each finite subtheory of ZFC + not-CH is consistent, hence the whole theory is. This is why forcing yields *relative consistency* rather than outright models of full ZFC [2].

Sheaf-theoretic presentations have since re-derived the independence of CH in a framework unifying the Boolean and intuitionistic approaches, confirming that the independence phenomenon is robust across foundations [3].

##6 Limitations

1. **Relativity, not truth.** Forcing proves *consistency*, never truth. It is compatible with everything proved here that ZFC is inconsistent — in which case all these models are vacuous. Godel's second incompleteness theorem guarantees this relativity is ineliminable.
2. **Singular cardinals resist control.** Easton's theorem says nothing about singular kappa, and Silver's theorem shows the silence is principled: GCH below a singular cardinal of uncountable cofinality propagates upward. The exact consistency strength of violating SCH at singulars of countable cofinality is a measurable cardinal of high Mitchell order (Gitik) — genuinely large-cardinal territory [6].
3. **Boolean-valued models do not remove choice from the metatheory.** The quotient by an ultrafilter uses the Boolean prime ideal theorem; the construction is as non-constructive as generic filters ever were. What it removes is the *fiction of the generic*, not the choice principles.
4. **CH is independent even of large cardinals.** Levy and Solovay showed that forcing with small posets preserves large-cardinal properties, so no large-cardinal axiom decides CH either. The continuum problem is orthogonal to the large-cardinal hierarchy.
5. **Easton functions must be definable classes.** The theorem quantifies over class functions F in the ground model; it does not produce "arbitrary" continuum functions in any absolute sense, and the witnessing model depends essentially on F.

##7 Conclusion

Cohen's forcing transformed the continuum hypothesis from an intractable mystery into the first instance of a general phenomenon: *set-theoretic truth is radically underdetermined by the axioms*. The Boolean-valued reformulation of Scott, Solovay, and Vopenka revealed the algebraic essence of the method — truth values in a complete Boolean algebra, with classical models recovered by ultrafilter quotients — and Easton's theorem mapped the exact boundary of the possible, proving that on regular cardinals the continuum function is constrained by nothing beyond monotonicity and Konig's inequality.

What remains is as striking as what was solved. The behavior of the continuum function at singular cardinals touches the deepest parts of modern set theory — pcf theory, the Singular Cardinals Hypothesis, and the large-cardinal hierarchy. And the original question Hilbert asked — *how many* real numbers are there? — has no answer in ZFC, and no large-cardinal axiom answers it either. Forcing did not tell us the size of the continuum; it told us, with mathematical precision, that the axioms do not know.
