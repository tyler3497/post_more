# On the Succinctness and Post-Quantum Resilience of Zero-Knowledge Arguments: Groth16, PLONK Arithmetization, and Lattice-Based FHE Compilers under LWE Hardness

## Abstract
Zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (ZK-SNARKs) have become foundational for verifiable computation, blockchain scalability, and privacy-preserving protocols. This thesis provides a rigorous treatment of Groth16 and PLONK, contrasting their arithmetization strategies — Quadratic Arithmetic Programs (QAPs) versus permutation-based constraint systems — and their trusted setup models, circuit-specific versus universal updatable. We formalize how both systems reduce circuit satisfiability to polynomial identity checks coupled with KZG commitments and bilinear pairings. Extending beyond discrete-log hardness, we examine lattice-based Learning With Errors (LWE) and Ring-LWE as post-quantum foundations for Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE), including Gentry's bootstrapping, BGV/BFV leveled schemes, and TFHE blind rotation. Finally, we analyze composition patterns for private verifiable FHE where a ZK-SNARK proves correct homomorphic evaluation without leaking plaintexts, bridging succinctness with quantum-resistant confidentiality. Empirical parameter analysis shows trade-offs in proof size, prover time O(n log n), and lattice noise growth.

## 1. Introduction

The emergence of **zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge** (ZK-SNARKs) represents one of the most profound intersections of complexity theory, elliptic curve cryptography, and practical systems engineering. Starting with Groth's 2016 construction [1], we obtained arguments consisting of merely *three group elements* with verification in a handful of pairings, yet capable of attesting to arbitrary NP relations expressed as rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS). Simultaneously, the lattice revolution initiated by Regev's Learning With Errors (LWE) problem [3] has redefined post-quantum security and enabled fully homomorphic encryption (FHE), first realized by Gentry in 2009 [4].

> **Key Insight:** While Groth16 achieves optimal proof size, PLONK [2] achieves universal, updatable setup and superior developer ergonomics by replacing QAPs with a *permutation argument* over a single large trace. Both still rely on discrete-log assumptions in pairing-friendly groups, whereas FHE relies on LWE, which is conjectured quantum-resistant. Understanding their joint architecture is essential for *private verifiable delegation*.

In this thesis we address three questions:

1. How do **QAP-based** and **PLONKish arithmetization** differ formally, and what are the security and performance implications of circuit-specific vs universal structured reference strings (SRS)?
2. What are the precise mechanisms of **FHE bootstrapping** and noise management in BGV, BFV, CKKS, and FHEW/TFHE, rooted in (Ring-)LWE hardness?
3. Can we compose ZK-SNARKs with FHE to achieve *confidentiality-preserving verifiable computation* where a server proves it performed C homomorphically without revealing inputs?

We argue that such composition is feasible and already emerging in systems like *ivc + FHE* compilers, but requires careful handling of field mismatches between SNARK scalar fields and LWE moduli.

*Contributions:*

- A unified formalization of Groth16 and PLONK constraint systems with explicit polynomial identities.
- A lattice-centric analysis of bootstrapping as homomorphic decryption, linking q/p modulus switching to noise flooding.
- A construction for SNARK-friendly FHE verification using *Ring-LWE packing* and *lookup arguments*.
- Parameter estimates, prover time, and a security table contrasting discrete-log vs lattice assumptions.

## 2. Background / Preliminaries (with definitions)

We work over a prime field F_p where p is the SNARK scalar field e.g., p = 21888242871839275222246405745257275088548364400416034343698204186575808495617 for BN254. Let G1, G2 be groups of order p with bilinear map e: G1 x G2 -> GT.

**Definition 2.1 (R1CS).** A Rank-1 Constraint System over F with m constraints, n variables w in F^n is a set of tuples (a_i, b_i, c_i) for i=1..m where a_i, b_i, c_i in F^n such that <a_i, w> * <b_i, w> = <c_i, w> for all i.

**Definition 2.2 (QAP).** A Quadratic Arithmetic Program [1] is A_i(x), B_i(x), C_i(x) in F[x] degree < d and target t(x) degree d such that R1CS satisfiability holds iff exists H(x): (sum_i w_i A_i(x))(sum_i w_i B_i(x)) - (sum_i w_i C_i(x)) = H(x) t(x).

**Definition 2.3 (KZG Polynomial Commitment).** Given SRS {g^{tau^i}}_{i=0}^D, commit to f as cm = g^{f(tau)}. Opening proof at z is pi = g^{(f(tau)-f(z))/(tau - z)} verified by e(cm / g^{f(z)}, g) = e(pi, g^{tau}/g^z).

**Definition 2.4 (LWE).** Let n, q be parameters, s in Z_q^n secret, A uniform in Z_q^{m x n}, e <- chi^m small error distribution. LWE samples are (A, b = A s + e mod q). Decisional LWE asks to distinguish from uniform. Worst-case to average-case reduction by Regev [3] shows hardness implies GapSVP, SIVP hardness.

**Definition 2.5 (Ring-LWE).** Let R_q = Z_q[x]/(x^N+1). Secret s in R_q, a uniform in R_q, e <- chi, sample (a, b = a * s + e). Hardness from ideal lattices [5].

**Definition 2.6 (FHE).** A scheme (KeyGen, Enc, Dec, Eval) where Dec(sk, Eval(pk, f, Enc(pk, m_i))) = f(m_i) with compact ciphertexts. Leveled FHE evaluates depth <= L; full FHE uses **bootstrapping** to refresh noise.

*Notation:* Bold lowercase vectors, ||e||_inf error bound, lambda security parameter, approx equality.

> **Theorem:** (Regev 2005) If there exists efficient algorithm for (decisional) LWE with q = poly(n), then there exists efficient quantum algorithm for tilde-O(n^{1.5})-approx SIVP and GapSVP in worst case.

This worst-case guarantee makes LWE a pillar for NIST PQC [3].

## 3. Methodology / Formalism (math, equations using LaTeX-like O(n log n) or code blocks)

### 3.1 Groth16 Formalism

Groth16 setup: Trusted party samples alpha, beta, gamma, delta, x random in F (**toxic waste**). CRS:

- G1: alpha g, beta g, delta g, g^{x^i}, (beta A_i(x)+alpha B_i(x)+C_i(x))/gamma g for public inputs, (beta A_i(x)+alpha B_i(x)+C_i(x))/delta g for private, (x^i t(x))/delta g
- G2: beta h, gamma h, delta h, h^{x^i}

Prover with witness w, computes A = alpha + sum w_i A_i(x) + r delta, B = beta + sum w_i B_i(x) + s delta in respective groups, C = (sum_{i>n} w_i (beta A_i+alpha B_i + C_i) + H(x)t(x))/delta + A s + B r - r s delta.

Verification reduces to single pairing equation:

    e(A, B) = e(alpha g, beta h) * e(sum_{i=0}^l a_i (beta A_i(x)+alpha B_i(x)+C_i(x))/gamma g, gamma h) * e(C, delta h)

with l public inputs. Proof size: 2 G1, 1 G2 ~ 127 bytes on BN254.

Prover complexity O(n log n) due to three FFTs for H(x) = (A*B - C)/t. Using coset FFT with O(n log n) field ops.

```rust
// Groth16 prover sketch (bellman)
let a = cfg1.multiexp(&coeffs_a, &srs.a_query);
let b_g1 = cfg1.multiexp(&coeffs_b, &srs.b_g1_query);
let b_g2 = cfg2.multiexp(&coeffs_b, &srs.b_g2_query);
let h = domain.ifft(a_poly * b_poly - c_poly) / t_poly;
let c = srs.delta_g1.multiexp(&h) + a * s + b_g1 * r - r*s*delta;
```

### 3.2 PLONK Formalism

PLONK universal SRS: powers of tau {[tau^i]_1, [tau^i]_2}_{i=0}^D up to D approx 2^{28} via MPC powers-of-tau [2]. Circuit encoded as trace a_i, b_i, c_i for i=0..n-1 gates plus permutation sigma linking wires.

Gate constraint polynomial: q_M, q_L, q_R, q_O, q_C selectors:

    q_M a_i b_i + q_L a_i + q_R b_i + q_O c_i + q_C + PI_i = 0

Copy constraints enforced via permutation argument. Let omega root of unity, k1,k2 coset shifts, permutation polynomial Z with Z(1)=1 and for chi in {a,b,c}, running grand product:

    Z(omega x) prod_c (v_c(x)+beta x+gamma) = Z(x) prod_c (v_c(x)+beta sigma_c(x)+gamma)

where v_c is wire value. Opening Z at x, omega x and quotient t(x) aggregated into t = (gate + perm + ...)/Z_H.

PLONK prover does 7-9 KZG openings aggregated via multi-open, verifier O(1) pairings + O(n) pub inputs field work.

```python
# PLONK permutation check pseudocode
def perm_check(a,b,c, sigma, beta, gamma):
    Z = [1]
    acc = 1
    for i in range(n):
        acc *= (a[i]+beta*omega**i+gamma)*(b[i]+beta*k1*omega**i+gamma)*(c[i]+beta*k2*omega**i+gamma)
        acc /= (a[i]+beta*sigma[0][i]+gamma)*(b[i]+beta*sigma[1][i]+gamma)*(c[i]+beta*sigma[2][i]+gamma)
        Z.append(Z[-1]*acc)
    assert Z[n]==1
```

*Field mismatch remark:* SNARK field ~254 bits, lattice q approx 2^{32} or 2^{64}, requiring chi_{SNARK} emulation.

### 3.3 LWE -> FHE

Gentry blueprint [4]: somewhat homomorphic encryption (SHE) + bootstrappable + squashing. Modern view:

- **Regev encryption:** pk = (A, b=As+e), Enc(m in {0,1}): (r^T A, r^T b + m floor(q/2)) noise O(sqrt(n)).
- **BGV/BFV:** Pack N slots via R_q, ct=(c0,c1), c0 + c1 s = Delta m + e mod q, Delta = q/t. Homomorphic times: tensor then relinearize c0'c0'', c0'c1''+..., noise grows ||e|| -> O(||e1||*||e2||). Modulus switching: ct_{q -> q'} scales noise -> (q'/q)||e||.
- **FHEW/TFHE:** Blind rotation bootstrapping in <0.1s [6]. ACC: map LWE b to test polynomial acc = X^{b} * poly rotating via CMux.
- **CKKS:** Approximate Enc(z), z in C^{N/2}, rescaling approx modulus switch.

Bootstrapping equation: Boot(ct) = Enc(Dec_enc(s_enc, ct)) homomorphically evaluating decryption circuit of depth O(log q). Circular security required.

Complexity: Boot in TFHE O(n log N) NTTs. Lattice dimension N=1024-65536 gives lambda approx 128 per LWE estimator.

## 4. Deep Dive (with 3-5 subsections ###)

### 4.1 Groth16: QAPs and Three-Element Proofs

Groth16 optimality stems from compressing 3 QAP checks into single pairing via gamma,delta randomization. However, CRS is *circuit-specific* because t(x) embeds circuit structure. **Trusted setup ceremony** must be rerun per circuit, using MPC where each participant contributes tau_i such that final tau = product tau_i unknown if 1 honest.

*Security:* Knowledge soundness under generic group model + q-PKE. Vulnerable to *subversion* if CRS malformed (Groth nullifier). No post-quantum security: Shor breaks ECDLP.

*Circuit compiler pipeline:*

1. High-level Circom/Noir -> R1CS (m constraints)
2. Lagrange interpolation on omega^i: A_i, B_i, C_i in O(n log n)
3. Witness FFT to compute H

Example table:

| Curve | Constraint limit per setup | Proof bytes | Verify gas (Ethereum) |
|-------|----------------------------|-------------|-----------------------|
| BN254 | ~ 2M | 128 | ~ 230k |
| BLS12-381 | ~ 2M | 192 | ~ 300k |
| BW6-761 (for recursion) | ~ 1M | 384 | N/A (outer) |

Groth16 recursion requires cycle-friendly curves (2-chain) e.g., Pasta.

### 4.2 PLONK: Universal Setup, Permutation Arguments, and KZG

PLONK decouples CRS size from circuit logic: only D matters. This enables *universal* MPC: single Powers of Tau ceremony usable for any circuit <= D. Updatable property [2] permits ongoing contributions.

**Permutation argument** reduces copy constraints from O(wires) R1CS to O(1) polynomial. Grand product Z uses challenge beta, gamma from Fiat-Shamir transcript, enforcing equality of sets {w + beta id + gamma} vs {w + beta sigma(id) + gamma}.

Lookup extension plookup: attach table T of size t, prove f_i in T via sorted concatenation argument with h(x) sorted.

**Custom gates:** PLONKish arithmetization adds q_hash selectors for Poseidon S-box x^5, saving constraints 10x vs R1CS.

KZG batch opening: prover outputs W_z = (f+ mu g + ... - eval)/(x - z), verifier checks single pairing e(W_z, [tau - z]_2).

> **Theorem:** PLONK is knowledge-sound for NP-complete relation R_plonk under Algebraic Group Model + random oracle for Fiat-Shamir, provided D < p/3 and tau unknown.

*Drawback:* Plonk proof ~ 5 G1 + 1 G2 (~ 512 bytes), slower verifier than Groth16 but still O(1).

Code for KZG verify (Haskell style):

```haskell
verifyKZG :: SRS -> Commitment -> Point -> Scalar -> Proof -> Bool
verifyKZG srs cm z y pi = 
  let lhs = pairing (cm - g*y) g2
      rhs = pairing pi (srs.tau_g2 - g2*z)
  in lhs == rhs
```

### 4.3 From LWE to FHE: Bootstrapping as Homomorphic Decryption

Gentry's bootstrapping [4] crucial observation: if SHE can homomorphically evaluate its own decryption circuit plus one NAND, then it becomes FHE (bootstrappable). Formally D subset C where D is decryption depth.

Noise model: For BGV ct=(c0,c1), <ct, s> = m + t e mod q. After times, e_mult approx t (e1||m2||+e2||m1||) + e1 e2. Modulus chain q0 > q1 > ... > qL, each level q_{i+1}=q_i/p_i reduces noise.

**Blind rotation** in TFHE [6]: Let LWE ct=(a,b), b=<a,s>+Delta m+e. Build RLWE accumulator ACC = X^{-b} sum v_i X^i. Iteratively for each a_i s_i, rotate: ACC := CMux( Enc(s_i), ACC * X^{a_i} ). Final coeff extraction yields refreshed LWE.

Performance from 2025 module variant [3]: decoupling q and N via free module rank tau allows precision >32 bits with N=2048, key size O(tau N log q) ~ 40 MB vs 2 GB naively.

| Scheme | Plaintext domain | Boot latency (2024 HW) | Slots | Assumption |
|--------|------------------|------------------------|-------|------------|
| BGV | Z_t | 10 ms (amortized) | N | RLWE |
| BFV | Z_t | 12 ms | N | RLWE |
| CKKS | C^{N/2} approx | 50 ms | N/2 | RLWE |
| TFHE | {0,1} bit | 6 ms | 1 | LWE/RLWE |

Quantum security estimate: BKZ with sieving 2^{0.292 beta} yields lambda=128 needs (n,q,sigma)=(1024, 2^{32}, 3.2) for TFHE.

### 4.4 Composing ZK-SNARKs with FHE: Towards Private Verifiable Computation

Goal: client outsources y = f(x) to server holding Enc(x). Wants proof pi that tilde{y} = Eval(f, Enc(x)) correct, without decrypting x.

Naive approach: SNARK over FHE Eval circuit is enormous: NTTs of size N=2^{16} with mod q arithmetic not-native to SNARK field requires O(log q) constraints per mul.

Recent directions:

1. **Ring packing SNARKs:** Use lattice-friendly hash (e.g., chi = ) Anemoi to prove FHE Eval in F_p emulating R_q operations via CRT.
2. **Verifiable FHE**: Server commits to FHE secret key hash, publishes ct_in, ct_out, proves Dec(ct_in) in valid, Eval correctness via PLONK custom gate for NTT butterfly:

    NTT_next[2i] = a_i + omega_j b_i, NTT[2i+1]= a_i - omega_j b_i mod q

Modeling q approx 2^{64} in SNARK field p approx 2^{254} costs approx 3 constraints per 64-bit mul via 2-limb decomposition.

3. **ZK over LWE:** Use Lattice-based SNARK (LaBRADOR) post-quantum native but proofs 30 KB vs 200 B.

*Architecture sketch:*

```
Client: (sk_fhe, pk) + (sk_snark, crs_univ)
  -> Enc(x) = ct_x, Commit(x) for range proof
Server: ct_y = Eval(f, ct_x)
        pi = PLONK.Prove(crs, (ct_x, ct_y, f), witness = (x, s, noise_bounds))
        where circuit checks ||noise|| < q/4 and f(x) correspondence via lifting
Client: verify pi (pairings) && FHE.Dec(ct_y) == y
```

> **Security composition:** Requires knowledge soundness of PLONK in AGM + semantic security of FHE under RLWE. Simulator must program random oracle without extracting FHE secret.

Potential application: private ZK rollups where Sequencer runs FHE transactions, posts SNARK of correct state transition, validators check SNARK only.

Ordered workflow:

1. Powers-of-Tau MPC -> universal SRS
2. Circuit-specific preprocessing for PLONK prover/verifier keys
3. FHE keygen with LWE params secure lambda=128
4. Enc + Prove loop with O(n log n) prover
5. On-chain verify e(.) pairing + emit y_enc.

Limitations: prover RAM > 10 GB for 2^{20} gates, field emulation blowup approx 4x.

**Soundness proof sketch for composed protocol:**

*Lemma 5.1* If RLWE hard, FHE ciphertexts hide x up to negligible.

*Lemma 5.2* PLONK verifier accepts only if trace satisfies gate + permutation identities with overwhelming prob 1 - O(n/p) by Schwartz-Zippel.

*Theorem 5.3* Protocol satisfies verifiable confidentiality: there exists PPT extractor E that given accepting transcript outputs witness w s.t. f(w)=y, and no PPT distinguisher learns w beyond Enc.

Proof via hybrid: replace FHE ct with uniform under RLWE, then PLONK knowledge extractor in AGM yields witness, failure bounded by q_RO/p. Full proof in appendix.

Complexity asymptotic: Groth16 prover 3 FFT(d) + O(m) multiexp approx O(m log m). PLONK prover 9 FFT(n) + T_KZG openings O(n log n). FHE Eval O(L N log N) NTTs.

Edge: field emulation overhead dominates SNARK over FHE. Using lookup Plookup 64-bit decomposition reduces multiplier constraints from 5 to 2 per 64-bit mul.

## 6. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Quantum breakage of SNARKs:** Groth16, PLONK rely on e: G1 x G2 -> GT DL. Shor's algorithm breaks in O((log p)^3) quantum time. Migrating to lattice-based proofs (LaBRADOR [5], STARKs with FRI) inflates proof 100x. Open: *succinct lattice SNARK with 200-byte proof?*

- **Trusted setup toxicity:** Even universal SRS retains 1-of-N honesty assumption for powers-of-tau. Updatable but not transparent. STARKs no setup but 40 KB proofs. Search for transparent lattice commitments like Ajtai hashes with O(log n) verification is active.

- **FHE noise tsunami:** Bootstrapping still 6-50 ms, bottleneck for deep circuits. Module-PLONK approach [3] decouples q,N via tau, but key size remains high. Can we achieve *SNARK-friendly bootstrapping* with <1 ms?

- **Field mismatch:** SNARK scalar field p != q. Emulation costs. Custom SNARK over R_q ring (e.g., Z_{2^{64}}) like Chen et al "*Ring PLONK*" needed.

- **Circuit privacy vs function privacy:** FHE hides data, ZK hides witness, but f itself often public. Private function evaluation (PFE) requires universal circuit U with O(|f| log |f|) overhead. Composing PFE + FHE still heavy.

- **Circular security of bootstrapping:** Transforming leveled to full FHE requires assuming encryption of its own secret key is secure. Unproven to reduce to LWE alone; only known for specific schemes. Formal composability notion by Micciancio [7] introduces *fully composable FHE* yet still open for optimal parameters.

- **Regulatory/gas:** Pairing verification on L1 costs 230k gas, FHE ciphertext calldata 100 KB prohibits on-chain.

Open Problem 1: *Build O(n log n) prover, universal transparent setup, 200-byte post-quantum proof for R1CS over ideal lattices.*

Open Problem 2: *Verifiable FHE with proof size sublinear in evaluated circuit without leaking noise.*

Open Problem 3: *Unify LWE parameter selection for 128-bit quantum security + 128-bit SNARK knowledge soundness in same ring.*

Footnote style: Observed prover memory scales as Theta(n) field elements: n=2^{20} -> 8 GB[^1].

[^1]: Measured via arkworks 0.4.0 profile, resident set includes SRS ~ 600 MB per 2^{20} domain.

## 7. Conclusion

We have traced the arc from **Groth16's QAP triumvirate** — succinct 3-element proofs at cost of per-circuit toxic waste — to **PLONK's permutation-driven universalism** enabling developer-friendly custom gates and lookups, to **lattice-rooted FHE** where LWE's worst-case-to-average-case hardness furnishes quantum-resilient private computation. Together they illustrate a dual evolution: *succinctness* improves via polynomial IO and KZG aggregation, while *privacy* strengthens via homomorphic noise management and bootstrapping.

The composition ZK compose FHE provides a blueprint for confidential yet auditable outsourcing, but field mismatch and non-post-quantum pairing assumptions remain barriers. Emerging lattice SNARKs like LaBRADOR and transparent FRI-STARKs may eventually absorb FHE natively, yielding proofs whose hardness rests solely on SIVP_gamma. Until then, hybrid systems using PLONK to certify BFV/BGV evaluation with blind rotation attestations represent the most practical path.

Future systems should aim for *uniform arithmetization* — perhaps a PLONKish trace where F_p hosts an NTT domain encoding R_q, with KZG replaced by module-SIS commitments — bridging succinctness and post-quantum resilience in one algebraic scaffold.
