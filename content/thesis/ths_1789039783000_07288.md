# Event-Based Vision and Spiking Neural Networks: Address-Event Sensing, Temporal Representations, and Gradient-Based Learning in Neuromorphic Systems

## Abstract

Event cameras abandon the synchronous frame for *asynchronous*, per-pixel sensing of logarithmic brightness change, emitting sparse timestamped event streams [1][3]. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are their natural computational counterpart: they communicate through the same discrete, event-driven currency of spikes, activating only when information arrives. This thesis develops the complete event-driven perception pipeline: dynamic vision sensor (DVS) physics and address-event representation (AER), intermediate representations — time surfaces [10] and spatiotemporal voxel grids — bridging raw events and deep networks, and the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron in exact discrete-time form. We then treat the modern supervised-learning toolkit — surrogate gradients and spatio-temporal backpropagation (STBP) [4][5] — contrasted with spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) [6] and ANN-to-SNN conversion theory [7], and quantify neuromorphic hardware (Intel Loihi [8], IBM TrueNorth [9]) in energy and latency. Synthesizing benchmarks on N-MNIST [12], DVS-Gesture, and ImageNet-scale SNNs [13], we show surrogate-trained SNNs now approach ANN accuracy at orders-of-magnitude lower energy, and we state the field's fundamental limits explicitly [2].
## 1 Introduction

Conventional cameras sample the visual world on a rigid clock: every 33 ms a global shutter freezes photons into a dense intensity matrix — mostly redundant with the previous frame — imposing three structural taxes: **latency** bounded by the exposure interval, **motion blur** under fast dynamics, and **dynamic range** compressed into ~60 dB by a single global exposure [1].

Event cameras, inspired by the transient pathways of the biological retina, invert this contract. Each pixel independently and *asynchronously* monitors its own logarithmic photocurrent and emits an event — a tuple

$$e_k = (x_k, y_k, t_k, p_k)$$

— only when the log-intensity change since its last event exceeds a threshold $C$, with $p_k \in \{+1,-1\}$ the polarity [1][3]. The output is a sparse spatiotemporal point cloud with microsecond timestamps, 120–140 dB dynamic range, and milliwatt budgets [1][3] — philosophically aligned with **spiking neural networks**, which propagate binary spikes in continuous time and integrate input only on spike arrival [4]. An event camera's output is already a spike train; a neuromorphic processor such as Intel's Loihi consumes spikes natively [8].

Three threads converge here: **sensing** — DVS physics and AER [1][3]; **representation** — time surfaces [10] and voxel grids [2] bridging events and deep networks without destroying temporal precision; and **learning** — surrogate gradients and STBP [4][5], STDP [6], ANN-to-SNN conversion [7], and neuromorphic deployment [8][9].

> **Definition: Event stream.** An event stream is a set $\mathcal{E} = \{e_k\}_{k=1}^{N}$ of events $e_k = (x_k, y_k, t_k, p_k)$ with $t_1 \le t_2 \le \cdots \le t_N$, generated by threshold crossings of per-pixel log-intensity. The stream is *sparse*: $N$ scales with scene dynamics, not with sensor resolution or wall-clock time [1].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 From frames to events

The modern DVS pixel — logarithmic photoreceptor, differencing amplifier memorizing the last transmitted log-intensity, ON/OFF comparators — was established by Lichtsteiner, Posch, and Delbrück (128×128, 120 dB, 15 µs latency) [3]. Pixels crossing threshold request the shared bus via **address-event representation (AER)**: the $(x,y)$ address and polarity travel as a digital word, with arrival time as timestamp [1]. AER readout spans MHz to GHz; bus saturation under extreme rates perturbs timestamps — an often-ignored noise source [1]. The family has diversified (DAVIS fuses events with frames [1]; commercial arrays approach megapixel scale), but the defining properties persist [1][2]:

- **High temporal resolution** — microsecond timestamps, kilohertz-equivalent pixel bandwidth;
- **High dynamic range** — ~140 dB versus ~60 dB for frame cameras;
- **Low latency and power** — no exposure wait, no redundant readout;
- **No motion blur** — events are generated in continuous time.

### 2.2 Neuromorphic datasets

The community built native benchmarks: Orchard et al. recorded MNIST/Caltech101 with a DVS undergoing saccadic motion (N-MNIST, N-Caltech101) [12]; IBM's DVS-Gesture covers hand gestures; DDD17/DDD20, N-CARS, and the Event Camera Dataset support driving and geometry tasks [2]. These are the empirical backbone of Section 5.

### 2.3 Spiking neuron models

The workhorse is the **leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF)** neuron (Lapicque, 1907); its single state variable dominates deep learning despite richer alternatives [4][5]. Information is carried in *rates*, in *timing* (notably time-to-first-spike, TTFS), or in population codes [4] — a choice that interacts deeply with the training algorithm (Section 4.4).

### 2.4 The training problem

A spiking neuron outputs $s(t) = \Theta(u(t) - \vartheta)$; its derivative is zero almost everywhere and infinite at threshold, so standard backpropagation cannot flow through it [4]. Four solution families emerged:

| Approach | Principle | Supervision | Key references |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surrogate gradients / STBP | Smooth backward pass through $\Theta$; BPTT over unfolded dynamics | Full | [4][5] |
| SLAYER / temporal credit | Error reassignment via spike-response kernels | Full | [11] |
| STDP | Local Hebbian timing rule, unsupervised | None | [6] |
| ANN-to-SNN conversion | Map trained ReLU net to firing rates | Transferred | [7] |

Surrogate-gradient methods are treated as primary — they deliver the strongest supervised results [2][5] — with STDP and conversion given full theoretical due.

## 3 Methodology

We formalize the pipeline as four maps — **transduction** (photons → events), **representation** (events → tensors/spikes), **computation** (LIF dynamics), **learning** (gradients through spikes) — fixing notation reused throughout.

### 3.1 Event generation model

Let $L(\mathbf{x},t) = \log I(\mathbf{x},t)$; each pixel stores $L(\mathbf{x},t_{\text{last}})$ from its most recent event. An event fires at time $t$ when

$$|L(\mathbf{x}, t) - L(\mathbf{x}, t_{\text{last}})| \ge C, \qquad p = \operatorname{sign}\!\big(L(\mathbf{x}, t) - L(\mathbf{x}, t_{\text{last}})\big),$$

where $C$ is the contrast threshold (typically 10–30% log units, with per-pixel mismatch) [1][3]. After firing, the pixel is briefly refractory — a hardware nonlinearity faithful simulators must model. Static scenes thus produce *zero* output, and event rate scales with $|\nabla L \cdot \mathbf{v}|$, image gradients projected onto optical flow.

### 3.2 Discrete-time LIF dynamics

For gradient-based training we simulate in discrete time with step $\Delta t$. Following the iterative LIF formulation used in STBP [5], the membrane potential $V^l[t]$ and spike output $S^l[t]$ of layer $l$ evolve as

$$V^l[t+1] = \Big(1 - \tfrac{1}{\tau_m}\Big) V^l[t] + \tfrac{1}{\tau_m}\,\mathbf{W}^l S^{l-1}[t+1], \qquad S^l[t+1] = \Theta\!\big(V^l[t+1] - V_{\mathrm{th}}\big),$$

with reset $V \leftarrow V - V_{\mathrm{th}}$ (reset-by-subtraction) or $V \leftarrow V_{\mathrm{rest}}$ after firing. The leak $\beta = 1 - 1/\tau_m$ sets memory: $\tau_m \to \infty$ recovers the perfect integrator, $\tau_m \to 1$ a memoryless coincidence detector. In continuous time,

$$\tau_m \frac{du}{dt} = -(u - u_{\mathrm{rest}}) + R\,I(t), \qquad u(t^+) = u_r \ \text{whenever}\ u(t^-) \ge \vartheta.$$

The network is thus a **recurrent system unrolled over $T$ timesteps**, with binary activations — the structure that STBP exploits.

### 3.3 Surrogate-gradient estimation

The forward pass uses the exact Heaviside $\Theta$; the backward pass replaces its derivative with a smooth surrogate $\sigma'$ [4]:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial V} \;\approx\; \sigma'(V - V_{\mathrm{th}}), \qquad \text{e.g.}\quad \sigma'(x) = \frac{1}{(\alpha |x| + 1)^2}\ \ (\text{fast sigmoid}),$$

which balances gradient fidelity against stability [4]. Rectangular, arctangent, and triangular variants share the principle that gradient mass concentrates near threshold, where spike *timing* is input-sensitive. STBP then applies the chain rule jointly over space and time [5]:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{W}^l}\mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \Big(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial V^l[t]}\Big)^{\!\top} \big(S^{l-1}[t]\big)^{\!\top}, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial V^l[t]} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S^l[t]}\frac{\partial S^l[t]}{\partial V^l[t]} + \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial V^l[t+1]}\frac{\partial V^l[t+1]}{\partial V^l[t]}.$$

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The DVS pixel and address-event representation

A DVS pixel is an analog computer: a logarithmic transimpedance amplifier converts photocurrent to log-intensity voltage (dynamic range for free, no exposure control), a switched-capacitor differencing stage subtracts the memorized value, and ON/OFF comparators issue requests to the AER arbiter on threshold crossing [1][3].

AER is a *natively spiking interconnect*: addresses, not values, travel the bus; timing is implicit in arrival. The arbiter serializes simultaneous requests, so under extreme rates timestamps jitter — precision degrades gracefully rather than dropping frames [1]. The original DVS: 128×128, 120 dB, 15 µs latency [3]; readout spans 2 MHz–1200 MHz across the sensor family [1].

> **Lemma (Event-rate scaling).** For a rigidly moving textured scene, the expected event rate satisfies $\mathbb{E}[N] \propto \int |\nabla L(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})|\,d\mathbf{x} / C$: event rate grows with contrast, speed, and inversely with threshold. *Static scenes are free.*

Bandwidth and downstream compute are thus spent only on *change*.

### 4.2 From event streams to tensors: time surfaces and voxel grids

Raw events are sets, not tensors; two representation families dominate [2]:

**Time surfaces** [10] convert recency into intensity: with $T_p(\mathbf{x})$ the latest event time of polarity $p$ at pixel $\mathbf{x}$,

$$\mathcal{T}_p(\mathbf{x}, t) = \exp\!\Big(-\frac{t - T_p(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}\Big),$$

with time constant $\tau$: recent events are bright, old ones fade, and the surface is a dense 2D image consumable by any CNN. Lagorce et al. stack these into HOTS, a hierarchy clustering time-surface patches into layer-wise features [10].

**Voxel grids / EST** [2] discretize $(x, y, t)$ into $B$ temporal bins with (often trilinear) interpolation into a $2B \times H \times W$ tensor; EST *learns* the kernel end-to-end [2]. Voxel grids preserve more temporal structure at the cost of $B$-channel memory and reintroduced quantization.

| Representation | Timing fidelity | Tensor-native | Memory | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Event frames (counts) | Lost | Yes | $2HW$ | Baselines, fast prototyping |
| Time surfaces [10] | Decayed trace | Yes | $2HW$ | CNN transfer, tracking |
| Voxel grid / EST [2] | Binned, fine | Yes | $2BHW$ | Deep learning, reconstruction |
| Raw events / spikes | Exact | No | $O(N)$ | SNNs, neuromorphic chips |

### 4.4 Surrogate gradients and spatio-temporal backpropagation

The surrogate-gradient program [4] is disarmingly simple: exact spikes forward, smooth $\sigma'$ backward — enabling full BPTT through the unfolded LIF recurrence (STBP [5]), with error flowing *sideways* (layers at fixed $t$) and *backward in time* (through the leak $\beta$).

Key refinements:

1. **Surrogate choice.** Wider surrogates ease optimization but increase bias; narrower ones sharpen timing sensitivity at the cost of dead neurons [4].
2. **Temporal credit.** SLAYER [11] reassigns error in time via spike-response kernels, supervising precise spike *timing*, not just counts.
3. **Memory.** BPTT costs $O(T)$ memory; reversible formulations and online rules (e-prop) trade exactness for constant memory [4].
4. **Normalization.** Threshold-dependent batch normalization and learnable $\tau_m$ stabilize deep SNN training [5].

> **Theorem (Surrogate bias–variance trade-off, informal).** As surrogate width $1/\alpha \to 0$, the STBP estimator converges to the true (a.e. zero) gradient — unbiased but useless; wider surrogates trade bias for tractable variance. *Training operates at finite sharpness, accepting bias as the price of learnability* [4].

Three-factor rules (SuperSpike, DECOLLE) stay closer to biology [4], and STDP needs no labels [6] — but on supervised benchmarks, surrogate STBP is state of the art [2][5].

### 4.5 ANN-to-SNN conversion

Conversion exploits rate equivalence: each ReLU unit becomes an integrate-and-fire neuron whose rate approximates its activation [7], with three critical details:

1. **Weight normalization.** Rescaling activations so the maximum maps to the saturating rate (max-norm; robust-norm uses the 99.9th percentile) prevents outliers from silencing layers [7].
2. **Reset-by-subtraction.** Subtracting $\vartheta$ instead of resetting to zero preserves threshold overshoot, sharply reducing conversion loss [7].
3. **Batch normalization folding.** BN parameters are absorbed into preceding weights and thresholds before conversion [7].

Conversion is near-lossless but needs hundreds of timesteps to resolve rates, sacrificing the latency advantage [7]. Hybrids initialize by conversion then fine-tune with surrogate gradients at few timesteps [5]; TTFS conversion maps activation to *first-spike time*, cutting spike counts ~10× at some accuracy cost [7].
## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Recognition benchmarks

On neuromorphic datasets, surrogate-trained SNNs have closed the gap to ANNs on reconstructed frames [2][5][11]:

| Dataset | Method | Accuracy | Timesteps | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N-MNIST [12] | STBP / SLAYER family | 98–99% | 10–100 | [5][11] |
| DVS-Gesture | Surrogate-gradient SNNs | >90% (SOTA mid-90s) | 20–60 | [2][5] |
| N-Caltech101 [12] | HOTS / deep SNNs | 70–80% | — | [10][12] |
| ImageNet-1K | Self-supervised Spikformer-16-512 | 70.1% top-1 | few | [13] |

On *native* event datasets, SNNs match or exceed CNNs fed with event reconstructions — preserving asynchrony helps [2]. At ImageNet scale, spiking transformers exceed 70% top-1, with self-supervised Spikformer-16-512 reporting 70.1% without labels [13]: SNNs scale beyond toy benchmarks, trailing ANNs by a narrowing margin.

### 5.2 Energy and latency

During inference, SNNs replace multiply-accumulate (MAC) with spike-gated *accumulate-only* (AC) updates; energy scales with spike count, not network size [4][8]:

- **IBM TrueNorth** [9]: 1M neurons, 256M synapses, 70 mW typical, **46 GSOPS/W** — the landmark digital-neuromorphic efficiency figure.
- **Intel Loihi** (Davies et al. [8]): 128 neuromorphic cores, on-chip learning, hierarchical AER mesh; order-of-magnitude efficiency gains on event-driven workloads, sub-millisecond spike routing.
- **Sensor**: 15 µs DVS latency [3]; event-to-decision pipelines close the loop in single-digit milliseconds versus ≥33 ms frame periods [1].

> **Lemma (Energy scaling).** For an SNN layer with mean firing rate $\bar{r}$ spikes/neuron/timestep, inference energy satisfies $E \propto \bar{r} \cdot N \cdot E_{\mathrm{AC}}$, where $E_{\mathrm{AC}} \ll E_{\mathrm{MAC}}$. *Sparsity is efficiency: halving the firing rate halves the energy, with no architectural change* [8][9].

### 5.3 STDP: the unsupervised contrast

STDP [6] remains the canonical local rule: pre-before-post spikes within ~20 ms potentiate (LTP); post-before-pre depress (LTD); the window decays over tens of milliseconds, modulated by synaptic strength and cell type [6]. STDP learns Gabor-like, motion-selective features from DVS streams label-free and maps onto Loihi's on-chip learning [8]. But as a *local, unsupervised* rule it optimizes no global objective, and deep STDP stacks trail supervised surrogate-gradient accuracy [2][6]. Consensus: STDP for adaptation and on-chip fine-tuning [8]; surrogate gradients for supervised performance [4][5].

### 5.4 Formal synthesis

We collect the thesis's formal claims:

> **Theorem (Conversion consistency).** Under reset-by-subtraction and max-norm weight scaling, the firing-rate vector of the converted SNN converges to the ANN activation vector as $T \to \infty$, with discretization error $O(1/T)$ per neuron [7].

> **Theorem (Surrogate STBP as biased BPTT).** STBP computes exact BPTT gradients of a *smoothed* network ($\Theta \to \sigma$); the estimator is biased for the true spiking network, with bias controlled by surrogate width [4][5].

The practitioner's decision procedure: convert when a trained ANN exists and latency allows long $T$ [7]; train with surrogates for sparsity, few timesteps, or temporal codes [4][5]; deploy on Loihi/TrueNorth-class hardware when energy dominates [8][9].
## 6 Limitations

What event-based SNNs do *not* yet do well [1][2]:

1. **Sensor non-idealities.** Real DVS pixels suffer threshold mismatch, background activity, refractory blindness during fast motion, and AER bus saturation under extreme rates [1][3] — all sources of sim-to-real gaps.
2. **Representation loss.** Time surfaces discard polarity sequences and velocity ambiguity; voxel grids reintroduce temporal quantization and latency; raw events resist batching. No representation is lossless and tensor-native simultaneously [2][10].
3. **Training pathologies.** Surrogate gradients are biased; narrow surrogates cause dead neurons, wide ones wash out timing; BPTT memory grows as $O(T)$; very deep SNNs still train less stably than ANNs [4][5]. STDP, while elegant, does not optimize task objectives [6].
4. **Conversion latency.** Near-lossless ANN-to-SNN conversion demands hundreds of timesteps, erasing the latency advantage; few-timestep conversion remains lossy [7].
5. **Hardware constraints.** Loihi and TrueNorth impose limited synaptic precision, fan-in/fan-out caps, and immature toolchains relative to GPUs; training still happens off-chip on power-hungry hardware, so the *system-level* energy story is incomplete [8][9].
6. **Benchmark scale.** Event datasets remain orders of magnitude smaller than ImageNet-scale frame corpora, and standardized evaluation protocols are still settling [2][12].

## 7 Conclusion

Event-based vision and SNNs are two halves of one idea: perception *driven by change, computed on arrival, silent otherwise*. This thesis traced photons to decisions — DVS pixels transducing log-intensity crossings into address-events [1][3]; time surfaces and voxel grids rendering the event cloud learnable [2][10]; LIF neurons integrating spikes in discrete time [5]; surrogate gradients and STBP differentiating the non-differentiable [4]; STDP's local plasticity [6]; conversion theory bridging ANN and SNN worlds [7]; Loihi and TrueNorth proving efficiency in silicon [8][9].

Empirically: SNNs rival frame-based pipelines on native event benchmarks, exceed 70% top-1 at ImageNet scale, and deliver 46 GSOPS/W on TrueNorth [9] with order-of-magnitude Loihi gains [8] — at millisecond closed-loop latency from 15 µs sensors [3].

The frontier is co-design: sensors, representations, learning rules, and hardware evolving *together*. Larger event datasets, sharper-yet-stable surrogates, on-chip supervised learning [8], and megapixel sub-microsecond sensors will decide whether event-driven perception stays a specialized instrument or becomes the default substrate of always-on edge intelligence. The physics — compute only on change — is on its side.
