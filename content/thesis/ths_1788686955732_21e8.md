# Sketches for Frequency Moments in the Data Stream Model: AMS Estimation, Count-Min and Count-Sketch, HyperLogLog Cardinality, and Mergeable Summaries

## Abstract

The estimation of frequency moments of a data stream is among the most consequential problems in the theory of big-data algorithms. Given a single pass over a stream σ = a₁,…,aₘ drawn from a universe [n], define the frequency vector f = (f₁,…,fₙ) and the k-th frequency moment F_k = Σᵢ fᵢᵏ. The special cases F₀ (distinct elements), F₂ (the second moment, or "surprise index"), and point-query frequency estimation all admit randomized (ε, δ)-approximations in polylogarithmic space, while matching communication-complexity lower bounds certify their near-optimality. This thesis presents a unified treatment of the four canonical sketch families—Alon–Matias–Szegedy (AMS) random-sign sketches, Charikar–Chen–Farach-Colton Count-Sketch, Cormode–Muthukrishnan Count-Min, and Flajolet–Fusy–Gandouet–Meunier HyperLogLog—deriving their bias–variance decompositions, hash-complexity requirements, and merge operators for distributed settings. We further situate these tools within the F_p estimation landscape (Indyk–Woodruff optimality), the Bar-Yossef et al. information-theoretic lower bounds, and the mergeable-summaries framework, concluding with quantitative guidance for practitioners deploying sketches at scale.

## 1. Introduction

Few abstractions have shaped modern data infrastructure as quietly and as decisively as the *sketch*. A sketch is a randomized, composable summary of a massive dataset that fits in a fraction of the space and answers a restricted family of queries with a quantified approximation guarantee. The canonical setting is the **data stream model**: items a₁, a₂, …, aₘ arrive one at a time from a universe [n] = {1, …, n}, where m and n may each be far too large for the machine's memory, and the algorithm must process each update in small time, store only polylog(m, n) bits of state, and answer queries at the end of the stream—or at any point along it. Within this model, the **frequency moments**

> *Definition.* Let fᵢ be the number of occurrences of item i in the stream. The k-th frequency moment is F_k = Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ fᵢᵏ, with F₀ = |{i : fᵢ > 0}| (the number of distinct elements), F₁ = m (the stream length), and F_∞ = maxᵢ fᵢ (the mode frequency).

are the fundamental statistics. The seminal paper of Alon, Matias, and Szegedy [1]—recognized with the 2005 Gödel Prize—initiated their systematic study and demonstrated that F₀, F₁, and F₂ can all be approximated in logarithmic space, while F_k for k ≥ 3 provably requires polynomial space. Four sketch families have since emerged as the pillars of this area:

1. **AMS sketches** [1]: random-sign linear projections estimating F₂ with an unbiased, variance-controlled estimator.
2. **Count-Sketch** [4]: sign-perturbed bucketed counters giving ℓ₂-error point queries and inner-product estimation in the turnstile model.
3. **Count-Min** [5]: monotone bucketed counters giving ℓ₁-error point queries with one-sided error and trivial mergeability.
4. **HyperLogLog** [3]: register maxima of hash bit-pattern observables giving near-optimal F₀ estimation with standard error 1.04/√m.

These sketches share a remarkable algebraic property—**linearity** or a suitable analogue—that makes them *mergeable*: sketches of disjoint stream partitions can be combined into a sketch of the union without reprocessing. This property, formalized in the mergeable-summaries framework [6], is what transports streaming algorithms into distributed systems, sensor networks, and federated analytics.

This thesis develops the full theory: the streaming model and its variants (§2), the algorithmic methodology including hashing requirements and the median trick (§3), a deep dive into each sketch family with derivations (§4), a section of proofs and theory-predicted scaling behavior (§5), the limitations and lower bounds that bound what sketches can achieve (§6), and concluding engineering guidance (§7).

## 2. Background

### The streaming models

The *cash-register model* assumes insert-only updates; the *turnstile model* permits updates (i, Δ) that add Δ ∈ ℤ to fᵢ, capturing both insertions and deletions, counters that rise and fall, and sliding aggregations. Sketches that are *linear functions* of the frequency vector—AMS, Count-Min, Count-Sketch—work identically in both models, since the sketch state is maintained by applying the update to a linear functional of the stream. A sketch algorithm is judged by three resources: **space** (bits of state), **update time** (per item), and **query time**, plus the approximation parameters ε (relative error) and δ (failure probability), with the standard *(ε, δ)-guarantee*:

> *Definition.* An estimator X̂ is an (ε, δ)-approximation of X if Pr[|X̂ − X| ≤ εX] ≥ 1 − δ.

### Why randomness is unavoidable

Deterministic algorithms are provably powerless here: any deterministic algorithm distinguishing whether F₀ ≤ n/2 or F₀ = n requires Ω(n) space [7], by a simple pigeonhole argument over the stream's support. Randomization, coupled with the median trick, is therefore not a convenience but a necessity.

### The four moments of interest

| Moment | Interpretation | Approximate in polylog space? | Foundational work |
|---|---|---|---|
| F₀ | Distinct elements (cardinality) | Yes, O(1/ε² · log n) | Flajolet–Martin [2], HyperLogLog [3] |
| F₁ | Stream length | Trivially exact | Morris counter (1978) |
| F₂ | Second moment; "surprise index" F₂/m²; Gini/heterogeneity | Yes, O(1/ε² · log n) | AMS [1] |
| F_k, k > 2 | Higher moments | No — Ω(n^{1−2/k}) needed | Indyk–Woodruff [8] |
| F_∞ | Maximum frequency | No — Ω(n) needed | [7] |

### Hashing requirements

Sketch analyses never assume fully random functions: they are implemented with **k-wise independent hash families** whose storage is O(k log n) bits. AMS requires 4-wise independence (for the variance bound), Count-Min requires only pairwise independence, and Count-Sketch requires pairwise independence for both the bucketing and sign functions. Tabulation-based 4-universal hashing gives O(1)-time evaluation in practice. For HyperLogLog, the analysis assumes a uniform random oracle, approximated in deployment by a cryptographic hash such as MurmurHash or SHA.

## 3. Methodology

### The universal skeleton

Every sketch in this thesis follows one skeleton:

1. **Hash.** Draw hash functions h from a low-independence family; the hash *implicitly* defines a random matrix too large to store.
2. **Maintain.** On update, apply a linear (or max-linear) functional: counters incremented, or registers maxed.
3. **Merge.** Because the state is a homomorphic image of the stream, states combine by addition (linear sketches) or coordinate-wise max (HLL).
4. **Estimate.** Apply a debiased aggregation—mean of squared projections, min or median over rows, harmonic mean of registers—plus small/large-range corrections where the raw estimator saturates.
5. **Boost.** Repeat the base estimator O(log(1/δ)) times and take the median; by a Chernoff bound, the failure probability drops from 1/3 to δ while the space grows only logarithmically.

The Python reference implementation below captures Count-Min's skeleton:

```python
import math, random

class CountMinSketch:
    def __init__(self, eps, delta):
        self.w = math.ceil(math.e / eps)          # width
        self.d = math.ceil(math.log(1.0 / delta)) # depth
        self.counters = [[0] * self.w for _ in range(self.d)]
        p = 10**9 + 7                             # prime > universe size
        self.hashes = [(random.randrange(1, p), random.randrange(p))
                       for _ in range(self.d)]
        self.p = p
    def _h(self, j, x):
        a, b = self.hashes[j]
        return ((a * x + b) % self.p) % self.w    # pairwise independent
    def update(self, i, c=1):
        for j in range(self.d):
            self.counters[j][self._h(j, i)] += c  # linear: merge by addition
    def query(self, i):
        return min(self.counters[j][self._h(j, i)] for j in range(self.d))
```

The same estimator expressed functionally makes the unbiasedness structure explicit:

```haskell
-- AMS estimator: E[Z^2] = ||x||_2^2 under 4-wise independent signs
amsEstimate :: [Int] -> [Double] -> Double
amsEstimate signs xs = (sum (zipWith (*) (map fromIntegral signs) xs)) ^ 2
-- F_k as a fold over the frequency vector
freqMoment :: Int -> [Int] -> Int
freqMoment k = sum . map (^ k)
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The AMS Sketch and the Second Moment

Let x ∈ ℝⁿ be the vector defined by the stream (in the turnstile model, the signed frequency vector). The AMS sketch maintains, for a random sign vector s ∈ {−1, +1}ⁿ with 4-wise independent coordinates, the scalar

Z = Σᵢ sᵢ xᵢ,   X = Z².

The analysis is a jewel of elementary probability:

> *Theorem (AMS [1]).* E[X] = ‖x‖₂² = F₂, and Var(X) ≤ 2F₂².

*Proof sketch.* Expanding, E[Z²] = Σᵢ xᵢ² + Σ_{i≠j} E[sᵢsⱼ] xᵢxⱼ = F₂ by pairwise independence. For the variance, E[Z⁴] expands into terms E[sᵢsⱼsₖsₗ] xᵢxⱼxₖxₗ; 4-wise independence kills every term whose indices cannot be paired, leaving E[Z⁴] ≤ 3F₂², whence Var(X) = E[Z⁴] − F₂² ≤ 2F₂². ∎

Averaging r = 8/ε² independent copies gives, by Chebyshev's inequality, an estimate within relative error ε with probability at least 7/8; taking the median of O(log(1/δ)) such groups boosts success to 1 − δ. Total space: O((1/ε²) log(1/δ)) counters of O(log m) bits each, plus O(log n) bits per hash function. The estimator is *unbiased*, handles arbitrary turnstile updates, and its linearity makes sketches of two streams summable into a sketch of the concatenation—a property §4.4 exploits for distributed F₂ monitoring. Beyond cardinality, F₂ measures stream *skew*: the "surprise index" F₂/m² is the collision probability of two random stream positions, used in query optimization, anomaly detection, and approximate histograms.

### 4.2 Count-Sketch and Count-Min: Point Queries in the Turnstile Model

While AMS answers an aggregate question, applications such as heavy-hitter detection and join-size estimation need *point queries*: estimate individual fᵢ. Two structures dominate.

**Count-Sketch [4].** A d × w array with w = O(1/ε²), d = O(log(1/δ)). Each row j has a bucketing hash hⱼ: [n] → [w] and a sign hash gⱼ: [n] → {−1, +1}, both pairwise independent. Update (i, c): CS[j][hⱼ(i)] += gⱼ(i)·c for all j. Query: f̂ᵢ = medianⱼ gⱼ(i)·CS[j][hⱼ(i)].

> *Theorem (Charikar–Chen–Farach-Colton [4]).* With probability ≥ 1 − δ, |f̂ᵢ − fᵢ| ≤ ε‖x‖₂ for every queried i.

The ℓ₂ guarantee is strictly stronger than ℓ₁ for skewed streams (‖x‖₂ ≪ ‖x‖₁), the estimator is unbiased, and it tolerates negative updates, making it the tool of choice for turnstile frequency estimation. Moreover, summing squared row entries estimates F₂, and the sketch supports inner-product queries ⟨x, y⟩ between two streams' sketches—a fact exploited in join-size estimation.

**Count-Min [5].** A d × w array with w = ⌈e/ε⌉, d = ⌈ln(1/δ)⌉ and pairwise independent hashes hⱼ, no signs. Update (i, c): CM[j][hⱼ(i)] += c. Query: f̂ᵢ = minⱼ CM[j][hⱼ(i)].

> *Theorem (Cormode–Muthukrishnan [5]).* For nonnegative updates, fᵢ ≤ f̂ᵢ ≤ fᵢ + ε‖x‖₁ with probability ≥ 1 − δ.

Count-Min's error is one-sided—it never underestimates—and the analysis is a single application of Markov's inequality per row, since the expected collision mass in a row is ‖x‖₁/w ≤ ε‖x‖₁/e. It uses O(1/ε) width versus Count-Sketch's O(1/ε²), making it the most space-frugal point-query sketch; it is the standard primitive for heavy-hitter (φ-frequent item) detection by thresholding, and it merges by coordinate-wise addition.

*Count-Min versus Count-Sketch.* Count-Min: smaller (O(1/ε) counters), one-sided error, needs nonnegativity for the clean guarantee. Count-Sketch: unbiased, handles deletions, ℓ₂ error, but quadratically wider. In practice the choice is dictated by the stream model: insert-only and one-sided bounds suffice → Count-Min; turnstile or unbiasedness required → Count-Sketch.

### 4.3 HyperLogLog: Near-Optimal Cardinality Estimation

F₀ estimation began with Flajolet–Martin [2]: hash each item, track the bitmap of observed bit-pattern positions ρ (the index of the least-significant 1-bit), and estimate from the leftmost zero—an idea rooted in the observation that seeing a run of k leading zeros among n distinct hashes is evidence that n ≈ 2ᵏ. Durand–Flajolet's LogLog (2003) refined this with stochastic averaging over m = 2ᵖ registers and a geometric-mean estimator with standard error 1.30/√m.

HyperLogLog [3] replaces the geometric mean with the **harmonic mean**, which is intrinsically robust to the large-register outliers that destabilize LogLog:

> *Algorithm (HLL [3]).* Hash v; let j be the first p bits (register index), w the remainder. Set M[j] ← max(M[j], ρ(w)), where ρ is the leftmost-1 position. Estimate E = αₘ m² (Σⱼ 2^{−M[j]})⁻¹.

The bias-correction constants are α₁₆ = 0.673, α₃₂ = 0.697, α₆₄ = 0.709, and αₘ = 0.7213/(1 + 1.079/m) for m ≥ 128 [3]. The analysis—an extended exercise in Mellin transforms and depoissonization—yields:

> *Theorem (Flajolet–Fusy–Gandouet–Meunier [3]).* The estimator is asymptotically unbiased with standard error 1.04/√m, using m registers of O(log log n) bits.

Two corrections handle the extremes: for small cardinalities, **linear counting** E* = m·ln(m/V) where V counts zero registers; near 2³², the **large-range correction** −2³²·log(1 − E/2³²). The paper's headline figure remains striking: cardinalities beyond 10⁹ at ~2% accuracy in 1.5 KB. HLL achieves the same accuracy as LogLog with only 64% of the memory, and it parallelizes optimally—the union of two sketches is the coordinate-wise maximum, so distributed sites merge sketches instead of shipping data. This mergeability underlies its adoption in Redis `PFCOUNT`, BigQuery `APPROX_COUNT_DISTINCT`, Presto, and Druid; Google's HyperLogLog++ adds a sparse representation and bias tables for the small-cardinality regime.

### 4.4 Mergeable Summaries and Distributed Streaming

Mergeability is the algebraic property that lets sketches leave the single machine. Formally, a summary S is *mergeable* if there is an operator ⊕ with S(A ∪ B) = S(A) ⊕ S(B) preserving the error and size guarantees [6]. Linear sketches merge by addition; HyperLogLog by coordinate-wise max; Count-Min and Count-Sketch by addition.

The mergeability contract, as a Rust trait and a TLA+ invariant:

```rust
trait Mergeable {
    fn merge(&self, other: &Self) -> Self;   // e.g. entrywise add, or register max
    fn space_bits(&self) -> usize;
    fn error_bound(&self) -> f64;            // merge must not inflate this
}
```

```tla+
---- MODULE MergeableSketch ----
EXTENDS Naturals
VARIABLES sketchA, sketchB, merged
MergePreserves ==
    \A q \in Queries : Error(merged, q) <= max(Error(sketchA, q), Error(sketchB, q))
====
```

Agarwal et al. [6] showed the property extends far beyond linear sketches: there exist deterministic mergeable summaries of size O(1/ε) for ε-approximate heavy hitters, and randomized fully-mergeable summaries of size O((1/ε)·log^{3/2}(1/ε)) for ε-approximate quantiles—plus a proof that the classic Misra–Gries and SpaceSaving summaries are isomorphic. In the distributed streaming setting pioneered by Cormode–Garofalakis ("sketching streams through the net"), monitors at network vantage points maintain local sketches and periodically ship them to a coordinator that merges them, yielding network-wide F₂, heavy hitters, and distinct counts with communication proportional to sketch size rather than data volume. Adversarially, mergeability composes with the turnstile model: signed updates flow through linear merges unchanged.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### Proof: the Count-Min guarantee

Fix a row j and query item i. The row estimate is CM[j][hⱼ(i)] = fᵢ + Σ_{k≠i} fₖ·1[hⱼ(k) = hⱼ(i)]. By pairwise independence, each collision indicator has expectation 1/w, so the expected excess is (‖f‖₁ − fᵢ)/w ≤ ε‖f‖₁/e with w = ⌈e/ε⌉. Markov's inequality gives Pr[excess > ε‖f‖₁] ≤ 1/e < 1/2. The d rows are independent; the minimum exceeds fᵢ + ε‖f‖₁ only if *all* rows fail, an event of probability ≤ 2^{−d} = δ with d = ⌈ln(1/δ)⌉. The lower bound f̂ᵢ ≥ fᵢ is immediate from nonnegativity. ∎

### Proof: AMS variance and the median trick

With r = 8/ε² averaged copies, Var ≤ 2F₂²/r = ε²F₂²/4, so Chebyshev gives Pr[|X̄ − F₂| > εF₂] ≤ 1/4 < 1/3. Partition copies into t = O(log(1/δ)) groups, average within groups, and take the median; a standard Chernoff bound on the number of "bad" groups (each bad with probability < 1/3, need > t/2 bad for the median to fail) yields failure probability ≤ δ. ∎

### Theory-predicted scaling behavior

The following table instantiates the proven error formulas—these are *analytical predictions* from the theorems, not measurements—showing how each sketch's accuracy scales with its memory budget for a stream with m = 10⁹ updates over n = 10⁸ items:

| Sketch | Memory | Proven error bound | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| HLL, m = 2¹⁰ | ~1 KB | se ≈ 3.3% | 1.04/√1024 |
| HLL, m = 2¹⁴ | ~16 KB | se ≈ 0.8% | production default |
| Count-Min, ε = 0.01 | ~1.6 KB | ±1% of ‖f‖₁ (δ = 0.01) | w = 272, d = 5 |
| Count-Sketch, ε = 0.01 | ~160 KB | ±1% of ‖f‖₂ | w = 10⁴, d = 5 |
| AMS F₂, ε = 0.05 | ~12 KB | ±5% relative | r = 3200 effective |
| FM85 bitmap | ~log n bits | ±O(1) factor | constant-factor only |

*Guidance.* For cardinality at web scale, HyperLogLog dominates: kilobyte memory, 1%-level error, exact mergeability. For per-item frequencies under insertions, Count-Min is the default; under deletions, Count-Sketch. For second-moment and join-size estimation, AMS remains the canonical choice, with the Alon–Gibbons–Matias–Szegedy refinements for tracking self-join sizes in limited storage.

## 6. Limitations

The sketch landscape is bounded on all sides by lower bounds, and an honest treatment must state them.

1. **Deterministic impossibility.** No deterministic sublinear-space algorithm approximates F₀ [7]; randomness is mandatory, and with it comes a nonzero failure probability δ that the median trick can shrink but never eliminate.
2. **The 1/ε² barrier.** Bar-Yossef et al. [7] proved via communication complexity (reductions from INDEX and multi-party set disjointness) that Ω(1/ε²) space is necessary for F₀ and F₂ estimation: the quadratic dependence on precision is inherent, not an artifact of our algorithms.
3. **F₂'s residual gap.** The AMS upper bound is O((log n)/ε²) bits while the strongest known lower bound is Ω(log n + 1/ε²) [8]; closing this logarithmic gap for small ε was the subject of recent work on the optimality of frequency-moment estimation.
4. **Higher moments are hard.** For k > 2, F_k requires Ω(n^{1−2/k}) space [8]—polynomial, not polylogarithmic—so no sketch in this thesis extends there; the Indyk–Woodruff algorithms achieve this bound up to constants, proving optimality.
5. **F_∞ needs linear space.** Estimating the maximum frequency is as hard as the problem itself in the worst case [7].
6. **Hash realism.** The clean analyses assume random oracles or full k-wise independence; deployments use fast non-cryptographic hashes, and adversarial inputs can exploit hash weaknesses (a concern addressed by recent work on adversarially robust streaming).
7. **Mergeability is not free.** Non-linear summaries (quantiles, heavy hitters) required substantial new theory [6] to become mergeable; naive merging of arbitrary summaries silently voids their guarantees.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Four ideas—random signs, sign-perturbed buckets, monotone buckets, and register maxima of hash observables—suffice to estimate every tractable frequency moment of a data stream in polylogarithmic space, with proofs that these bounds are essentially optimal. The AMS sketch turned the second moment into a linear functional with a two-line variance bound; Count-Min and Count-Sketch made point queries and inner products sketchable with complementary ℓ₁/ℓ₂ guarantees; HyperLogLog pushed cardinality estimation to kilobyte scale with a rigorously analyzed 1.04/√m standard error; and the mergeable-summaries framework lifted all of these from the single pass to the distributed fleet. For the practitioner, the decision tree is short: *cardinality* → HyperLogLog; *per-item counts, insert-only* → Count-Min; *turnstile or unbiased* → Count-Sketch; *second moment and join sizes* → AMS. The enduring lesson of [1] is that the right randomized linear projection makes the impossible routine—and that the limits of the routine are themselves theorems.
