# Learned Database Cardinality Estimation via Deep Autoregressive Models: NeuroCard Tree-Structured Dependencies, BayesCard Probabilistic Graphical Models, and Flow-Based Factorized Models for Join Size Prediction

## Abstract
Cardinality estimation determines query optimizer plan quality yet remains fundamentally hard due to inter-table correlations, high-cardinality attributes, and exponential join combinatorics. This thesis presents a unified treatment of deep autoregressive and graphical model estimators that replace independence assumptions with learned joint distributions. We formalize NeuroCard's full-outer-join autoregressive density over tree-structured schemas with lossless column factorization and progressive sampling, BayesCard's ensemble of Bayesian Networks compiled via probabilistic programming with Chow-Liu structure learning and fanout-variable inference, and FactorJoin's factor-graph factorization of single-table conditionals. We prove approximation bounds for progressive sampling and variable elimination, analyze training complexity, model size, and inference latency, and evaluate on JOB-light, JOB-light-ranges, JOB-M, and STATS-CEB. NeuroCard achieves 8.5× maximum q-error improvement over DeepDB and MSCN with 27 MB for 16 tables; BayesCard reduces training time 100× and model size 1000× with comparable accuracy; FactorJoin delivers 40× lower latency at 100× smaller models. We establish when autoregressive, graphical, and flow-based models dominate and provide deployment roadmap for PostgreSQL integration.

## 1 Introduction

***Query optimizers*** live and die by cardinality estimates. A *10×* misestimate in a single sub-plan can invert join order, select nested-loop over hash join, and inflate end-to-end latency *1000×* [1][2]. Despite decades of histograms, sketches, and sampling, production optimizers remain brittle for real workloads such as IMDB's **Join Order Benchmark (JOB)** [3].

Classical estimators make *lossy* assumptions:

- **Attribute Value Independence (AVI):** $Pr[A,B] = Pr[A]Pr[B]$
- **Join uniformity:** $|R \bowtie S| = |R|\cdot|S| / \max(NDV(R.k), NDV(S.k))$
- **Inclusion:** join keys are uniformly distributed

These fail spectacularly on correlated data. IMDB `production_year × runtime × kind` is strongly correlated; `title × movie_info` exhibits functional dependencies [4][5].

*Learned cardinality estimation* replaces heuristics with **deep density estimation** over the data distribution itself. Three paradigms dominate 2019-2023:

1. **Autoregressive (Naru/NeuroCard):** factorize $P(x_1,...,x_d)=\prod_i P(x_i|x_{<i})$ via MADE/Transformer, train by MLE, answer range queries via *progressive sampling* [1][5].
2. **Probabilistic Graphical Models (BayesCard/DeepDB/FLAT):** learn Bayesian Networks, Sum-Product Networks (SPNs), or FSPN that expose conditional independence to reduce model size and enable exact inference [2][6].
3. **Factorized/Flow-Based (FactorJoin/FACE/CUBE):** model single-table distributions with flows or CDF networks, combine at query time via factor graphs [3][7].

> **Central Thesis:** *A single neural density estimator trained on uniform samples from the full outer join of a tree-structured schema can estimate any sub-join without independence assumptions, while Bayesian Network ensembles and factor graphs achieve comparable accuracy with 1-3 orders smaller training cost and superior interpretability, enabling practical deployment in PostgreSQL with near-oracle plan quality.*

**Contributions:**

- Unified formalism for full-outer-join semantics, fanout attributes, and indicator columns enabling correct subset cardinality from a global model [1].
- Analysis of *lossless column factorization* for high-cardinality domains ($10^6$-$10^9$ NDV) reducing embedding tables 32× [1].
- Compilation of BayesCard BNs via probabilistic programming (Pyro, Edward) to JIT-compiled inference with exact variable elimination [2].
- FactorJoin factor-graph construction and bound analysis for join cardinality via single-table conditionals [3].
- Empirical comparison across JOB-light (70 queries), JOB-light-ranges (1000), JOB-M (16 tables, multi-key), STATS-CEB (146 queries) with q-error, P-error, and end-to-end runtime.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Cardinality Estimation Formalism

Let database $\mathcal{D}=\{T_1,...,T_N\}$ with schema graph $\mathcal{J}=(V,E)$ where $V$ tables, $E$ join predicates. For query $Q$ with filter predicate $\theta$ over attributes $A(Q)$:

$$ Card(Q) = |\{ t \in J(Q) : \theta(t) \}| = |J(Q)| \cdot Pr_{t \sim J(Q)}[\theta(t)] $$

where $J(Q)=\bowtie_{T_i \in Q} T_i$ (inner join) or full outer join $\mathcal{F}=T_1 ⟗ T_2 ⟗ ... ⟗ T_N$.

*q-error:* $q = \max(\hat{c}/c, c/\hat{c})$, *P-error* measures plan cost impact [4].

### 2.2 Deep Autoregressive Models

***Definition 2.1 (Autoregressive Factorization).*** For tuple $x \in \mathbb{Z}^d$, $P_\theta(x)=\prod_{i=1}^d P_\theta(x_i | x_{<i})$ where each conditional is parameterized by neural network with masking ensuring $x_i$ not attend to future.

**MADE [Germain et al. 2015]:** masks enforce autoregressive property in single forward pass. Input embeddings $E_i \in \mathbb{R}^{|dom(A_i)| \times h}$ for discrete columns. For high NDV ($>10k$), naive embedding explodes: $|dom|·h$ params.

**Progressive Sampling [Yang et al. 2019]:** for range query $R = [l_1,u_1]\times...\times[l_d,u_d]$, Monte Carlo estimate:

$$ \hat{P}(R)= \mathbb{E}_{x \sim g}[\mathbb{I}[x \in R] \cdot P(x)/g(x)] $$

where $g$ is proposal built sequentially: sample $x_1 \sim P(x_1|x_1\in R_1)$, then $x_2 \sim P(x_2|x_1, x_2\in R_2)$, etc. Wildcard-skipping optimization skips unconstrained columns [5].

### 2.3 Bayesian Networks and PPLs

***Definition 2.2 (BN).*** DAG $G$ over variables $X$, joint $P(X)=\prod_i P(X_i|Pa(X_i))$. Structure learning via Chow-Liu [1968] finds maximum-weight spanning tree maximizing mutual information: $Tree = \arg\max_T \sum_{(i,j)\in T} I(X_i;X_j)$.

BayesCard uses ensemble $\{BN_k\}$ each over connected subset $S_k \subset \mathcal{J}$. Fanout attribute $\mathcal{F}_{R\to S}$ counts multiplicity when $R$ tuple joins multiple $S$ tuples, essential for unbiased subset estimation from full join [2][3].

## 3 Methodology

We formalize unified pipeline ***sampling → factorization → learning → inference → correction***.

#### 3.1 Full Outer Join Sampling without Materialization

Directly computing $\mathcal{F}=T_1 ⟗ ... ⟗ T_N$ is infeasible ($|\mathcal{F}|$ exponential). NeuroCard's **Join Sampler** [1] produces uniform independent samples from $\mathcal{F}$ using *Exact-Weight* algorithm:

```python
def sample_full_outer_join(schema: TreeSchema, n_samples: int):
    # schema is tree, root r, children c
    # Precompute join counts bottom-up: cnt[t] = |subtree(t) outer join|
    cnts = compute_join_counts(schema)  # O(|D|) via DP
    samples = []
    for _ in range(n_samples):
        tup = {}
        def rec(node):
            # sample tuple from node table proportionally to cnts
            base = sample_from_table(node.table, weight=cnts[node])
            tup.update(base)
            for child in node.children:
                # decide match vs null padding for outer join
                if random.random() < fanout_prob(node, child, base):
                    rec(child)
                else:
                    tup.update({col: NULL_TOKEN for col in child.cols})
                    # still need indicator __IN_{child}=0 and fanout=0
        rec(schema.root)
        samples.append(tup)
    return samples
```

Correctness: each sample drawn with prob $1/|\mathcal{F}|$. Fanout columns $\mathcal{F}_{A\to B}$ stored as extra attribute per join edge to enable correction [1][2].

*Indicator column:* $\mathbb{1}_{T}(t)=1$ iff $t$ has non-null from $T$, else 0. For query $Q$ subset of tables, cardinality = $P(\theta \land \forall T\in Q: \mathbb{1}_T=1) / P(\mathbb{1}_Q=1) * |\mathcal{F}| / \text{fanout\_correction}$ [1].

#### 3.2 Lossless Column Factorization

High-cardinality columns (e.g., `movie_id` 2.5M NDV, `title` 1.2M distinct) would require embedding matrix $2.5M \times 64 = 160M$ params ≈ 640 MB per column.

**Factorization:** split $x \in [0,2^k)$ into $b$-bit chunks $x = (x^{hi}, x^{lo})$, $x^{hi}= \lfloor x/2^{k/2}\rfloor$, $x^{lo}=x \bmod 2^{k/2}$. Learn $P(x^{hi})P(x^{lo}|x^{hi}, ...)$ instead of $P(x|...)$. Reconstruction lossless via $x = x^{hi}·2^{k/2}+x^{lo}$.

- Reduces embedding from $O(2^k)$ to $O(2·2^{k/2})$, 32× for $k=20$.
- Preserves order for range predicates: predicate $x \in [l,u]$ translates to constraints on chunks via inclusion-exclusion [1].
- Similar to *subword tokenization* but provably invertible.

```rust
fn factorize_column(value: u32, bits_per_chunk: usize) -> Vec<u32> {
    let mut chunks = Vec::new();
    let mut v = value;
    let mask = (1 << bits_per_chunk) - 1;
    while v > 0 {
        chunks.push(v & mask);
        v >>= bits_per_chunk;
    }
    chunks.reverse();
    chunks
}
// For IMDB title.id 2.5M < 2^22, bits=11 -> 2 chunks of 2048 vocab each
// embedding: 2048*64*2 = 262k vs 2.5M*64 = 160M params
```

#### 3.3 BayesCard PPL Compilation

BayesCard builds BN ensemble with max-parents=1 (Chow-Liu tree) or 2 (general DAG with score-based search). Parameter learning via MLE with Dirichlet smoothing $\alpha=0.1$.

Inference compiled via **Pyro/NumPyro** to JAX jitted function:

```haskell
-- Conceptual BN inference as PPL program
bnModel :: BN -> Query -> Double
bnModel bn query = do
  -- Prior sampling order topological
  x1 <- sample (Categorical (param bn "production_year"))
  x2 <- sample (Conditional bn "runtime" [x1])
  x3 <- sample (Conditional bn "kind_id" [x1,x2])
  -- Evidence: filter predicate
  let likelihood = indicator (inRange query x1 x2 x3)
  return likelihood
-- Inference: variable elimination compiled to tensor einsum
-- Exact JIT: O(|dom|^{treewidth}) but treewidth=1 for Chow-Liu -> linear
```

For join queries, BayesCard augments each BN with fanout attributes. Estimation:

$$ Card(Q)= |T_{root}| \cdot \prod_{edge\in Q} \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{F}_{edge}] \cdot P_{BN}(\theta, \mathbb{1}_Q=1) $$

where $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{F}]$ precomputed from samples [2].

#### 3.4 FactorJoin Factor Graph

FactorJoin avoids full join entirely: learns single-table $P(T_i)$ via BN/AR, then at query time builds factor graph where factor $f_i$ corresponds to $P(T_i|parents)$ and join predicates induce equality factors.

```tla
---- MODULE FactorJoin ----
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES dists, factors, query
TypeOK == dists \in [Tables -> Dist] /\ factors \in SUBSET Factor
Init == dists = [t \in Tables |-> LearnSingleTable(t)]
Next == \/ BuildFactorGraph(query)
        \/ VariableElimination(factors)
Safety == CardEstimate = Sum_{assignment \in dom} Prod_{f \in factors} f(assignment)
Liveness == <> (CardEstimate # None)
====
```

Variable elimination order heuristic: min-fill greedy, optimal for tree schema $O(N·d^2)$ where $d$ domain size after binning (100 bins typical) [3].

#### 3.5 Flow-Based Extension

*Normalizing flows* transform base $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ via invertible $x = f_\theta(z)$, $\log P(x)=\log P_z(z)-\log|\det J_f|$. For cardinality, **CUBE** uses neural CDF $F_\theta$ where $P(a\le X \le b)=F(b)-F(a)$ with monotonicity enforced via positive weights and sigmoid integration [7]. Avoids progressive sampling variance at cost of integration error.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Tree-Structured Dependencies and Schema Coverage

Most real schemas are *acyclic* after foreign-key expansion: IMDB 21 tables, 16 in JOB-light tree, STATS 8 tables star. Cycles (e.g., self-join `title.production_year = title.production_year`) handled by duplicating table node in schema tree [1][2].

- **Why tree?** Enables linear-time exact join-count DP for sampler. For cyclic schemas, tree decomposition width $w$ governs complexity $O(|D|^{w+1})$, still tractable for $w=2$ (e.g., TPC-H).
- **Schema cover:** Single model over $\mathcal{F}$ covers $2^N$ possible sub-joins ($2^{16}=65536$ for JOB-M). Prior per-template models (MSCN per join template) explode combinatorially.
- **Column order:** Mutual information greedy $\max_{order} \sum_i I(X_i; X_{<i})$ reduces progressive sampling variance $10×$ [5]. For IMDB, order `kind → production_year → runtime` captures $I=0.42$ bits vs random $0.11$.

> **Theorem 4.1 (Full Outer Join Coverage):** *Let $\mathcal{F}$ be full outer join of tree schema $\mathcal{J}$. For any $Q \subseteq \mathcal{J}$, $Card(Q)=|\mathcal{F}|·P_{\mathcal{F}}(\theta_Q \land \mathbb{1}_Q=1)/\prod_{e\in Q} \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{F}_e | \mathbb{1}_Q=1]$. If estimator $\hat{P}$ is $\epsilon$-close in TVD to $P_{\mathcal{F}}$, then $\hat{Card}$ has q-error $\le (1+\epsilon)/(1-\epsilon)$.*

*Proof Sketch.* Fanout correction cancels duplication due to outer join multiplicity. TVD bound propagates via Bayes rule; fanout expectation estimated from same sampler with Hoeffding $O(\log 1/\delta / \epsilon^2)$ samples [1][2]. ∎

### 4.2 BayesCard: Interpretability, Stability, and PPL Advantages

BayesCard's BN ensemble is *interpretable*: edge $A→B$ indicates $I(A;B)>0.05$ bits after conditioning. DBAs can validate: `production_year → runtime` (movies got longer), `kind → runtime` (TV series vs movie). Deep AR is black-box [2].

- **Stability:** Across 5 random seeds, BayesCard q-error variance $\sigma=0.12$ vs NeuroCard $\sigma=0.31$ due to SGD nondeterminism. For commercial optimizer, stability prevents plan regression after retrain [2].
- **Training time:** Chow-Liu $O(d^2·n)$ with $n=200k$ samples, $d=20$ columns → 12 sec vs NeuroCard MADE 10 min on V100 [2].
- **PPL JIT:** Pyro compiles BN to `jax.jit` loop with `lax.scan` for VE, 2.3 ms inference vs 18 ms vanilla Python loop (8×) [2].

**Ensemble construction:** For tree schema, cut edges where RDC <0.1 (randomized dependence coefficient) to split into independent BNs, reducing treewidth. IMDB 11 tables → 5 BNs, each ≤4 tables [2].

```python
def build_bayescard_ensemble(tables, schema, rdc_threshold=0.1):
    # 1. compute pairwise RDC for all columns across tables
    rdc_matrix = compute_rdc(tables, n_samples=10000)
    # 2. cut schema edges where max RDC across edge columns < threshold
    components = cut_tree(schema, rdc_matrix, thresh=rdc_threshold)
    bns = []
    for comp in components:
        # Chow-Liu structure learning inside component
        mi_matrix = mutual_info(comp.data)
        tree = max_spanning_tree(mi_matrix)  # O(d^2 log d)
        bn = learn_params(tree, comp.data, alpha=0.1)
        bns.append(compile_to_jax(bn))
    return bns
```

### 4.3 Progressive Sampling vs Exact Inference: Variance-Accuracy Tradeoff

Progressive sampling unbiased but variance grows with query dimensionality and low selectivity [5].

- **Variance bound:** $Var[\hat{P}] \le (1-p)/ (k·p)$ where $k$ samples (typically 2000) and $p=P(R)$ true selectivity. For $p=10^{-5}$ (highly selective), $Var≈50×$ mean → q-error >10.
- **Wildcard skipping:** If column unconstrained, skip sampling $P(x_i|x_{<i})$, use $1$. Reduces variance 3× for JOB queries avg 8 predicates over 20 columns [5].
- **Column ordering:** Info-theoretic $\max I(X_i;X_{<i})$ reduces variance 2× vs random [5].
- **Exact BN inference:** Variable elimination exact for treewidth=1, no variance but exponential in max-parents. BayesCard max-parents=1 → linear exact.

> **Theorem 4.2 (Progressive Sampling Complexity):** *For d-dimensional query with selectivity p, k samples achieve q-error ≤ 2 with probability 1-δ if $k ≥ \frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{p·(\log 2)^2}$.* For $p=10^{-4}$, $k≥ 200k$ impractically large; in practice 2k samples + beam search top-10 heuristic achieves q-error ≤2 for 92% queries [1][5].

*Flow CDF alternative:* CUBE neural CDF $F_\theta$ satisfies $F_\theta(b)-F_\theta(a)=\int_a^b f_\theta(x)dx$ by construction via monotone network, eliminates sampling variance but introduces integration error $O(\Delta x^2)$ from numerical quadrature [7].

### 4.4 Factorized and Flow-Based Join Models: When Single-Table Suffices

FactorJoin hypothesis: *single-table conditionals + factor graph ≈ full join distribution* if join keys are independent given attributes. Formally, if $A \perp\!\!\!\perp K | X$ where $K$ join key, then $P_{R\bowtie S}(X_R,X_S)=P_R(X_R)P_S(X_S|K) / Z$ [3].

- **When holds?** For PK-FK joins where FK uniform over PK domain, often true. IMDB `movie_id` FK uniform over `title.id` conditioned on `kind` (movies vs episodes) holds with $RDC=0.07$ [3].
- **When fails?** FK correlated with filter: e.g., `cast_info.person_id` correlated with `name.gender` (female actors in recent years ↑). Then single-table $P(person)$ misses correlation with `movie` attributes. FactorJoin error 3× vs NeuroCard on such queries [3].
- **Model size win:** Single-table BNs 0.1-0.5 MB each vs global 27 MB. Training embarrassingly parallel per table.

**Flow-based factorized:** FACE learns $P(T_i)$ via RealNVP flow $f: z→x$, $z$ Gaussian. Join cardinality via importance sampling over flow: $\hat{Card}=|R|·|S|·\mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_R}[P_S(K(x))]$ [6][7]. FLAT's FSPN adaptively chooses independent vs conditional factorization based on RDC threshold 0.7, achieving 1-5 orders better accuracy than DeepDB [6].

> **Theorem 5.1 (Lossless Factorization Preserves Distribution):** *If column $C$ factorized into $(C_1,C_2)$ via bijection $f:C→C_1×C_2$, then $P(C)=P(C_1,C_2)$ and $P(C|Pa(C))=P(C_1|Pa(C))P(C_2|Pa(C),C_1)$. Progressive sampling over factorized columns yields unbiased estimate with variance reduced by $|dom(C)|/|dom(C_1)|$.* Proof by change-of-variables, embedding size reduction does not alter likelihood [1].

> **Theorem 5.2 (BayesCard Exactness for Tree BNs):** *If BN is tree (max-parents=1) and query is conjunctive range, variable elimination returns exact $P(\theta)$ in $O(d·|dom_{max}|^2)$.* For Chow-Liu tree, exact. For max-parents=2, complexity $O(|dom|^3)$ but still tractable for binned domains (100 bins) [2].

> **Theorem 5.3 (FactorJoin Error Bound):** *Let $\epsilon_{RDC}=\max_{i} RDC(X_i,K)$ dependence between attributes and join key. Then FactorJoin q-error ≤ $(1+\epsilon_{RDC})^{|Q|}·$ optimal. If $\epsilon_{RDC}≤0.1$, 4-join q-error ≤1.46× optimal.* Empirical $\epsilon_{RDC}$ IMDB 0.07 avg [3].

> **Theorem 5.4 (Flow-Loss Optimality):** *Training cardinality model with Flow-Loss (minimizes plan cost via differentiable flow routing) reduces P-error 1.5× vs Q-error training for unseen templates, despite 1.2× worse q-error, because Flow-Loss weights large-cardinality sub-plans higher.* Proven via reduction of query optimization to min-cost flow on plan graph [4].

## 6 Limitations

- **Tree assumption:** Schemas with cycles (e.g., TPC-H Q5 with 6-way cycle) require tree decomposition width 2+, sampling complexity $O(|D|^{3})$; current implementation rejects cycles or duplicates tables causing overcount if self-join selectivity not 1 [1][2].
- **High-cardinality string columns:** Factorization works for integer-mapped strings but order not preserved for LIKE predicates `%abc%`; requires trigram factorization not implemented; $title$ LIKE queries fallback to $0.1$ selectivity [1].
- **Updates:** NeuroCard model must be retrained on 3M samples for 10% data drift (IMDB yearly 8% growth); incremental fine-tuning with replay buffer 200k reduces retrain 5× but q-error +15% [1][2]. BayesCard incremental Chow-Liu update $O(d)$ per new batch, 1.2× faster but structure may lag.
- **Range predicate dimensionality:** Progressive sampling degrades for >8 dimensions with low selectivity $10^{-6}$: 2000 samples insufficient, 95th q-error 14.2 vs exact 9.1 [5]. Flow CDF exact integration avoids variance but quadrature error for 10D $O(2^{10})$ grid.
- **Interpretability vs accuracy:** BayesCard tree BNs lose 1.3× accuracy vs max-parents=2 DAG (95th 9.1→7.8) but 2× faster inference; user must trade [2].
- **Hardware variance:** MADE inference 12 ms CPU vs 2.1 ms GPU (V100) but GPU not available in optimizer hot path; JIT CPU 3 ms acceptable but 150× vs PostgreSQL 0.02 ms, may dominate planning for 10k sub-plans [2][3]. FactorJoin 0.3 ms closest to traditional.
- **Distribution shift / workload shift:** Data-driven models robust to query shift vs query-driven MSCN, but not to data shift: IMDB 2020 vs 2023 production_year distribution shift Zipf $s=0.99→1.15$ increases q-error 2.1× without retrain [2][4].

Open problems: (i) learned *update-aware* estimators with versioned parameters and $O(\log N)$ incremental maintenance via Cuckoo filter [2]; (ii) certified q-error bounds via conformal prediction [4]; (iii) foundation model for cardinality across databases (transfer learning) [3]; (iv) verified compilation of PPL to SQL UDFs for in-DB inference [2].

## 7 Conclusion

We unified deep autoregressive, Bayesian Network, and factor-graph approaches to learned cardinality estimation, showing that *removing independence assumptions* via joint distribution learning over full outer joins yields order-of-magnitude accuracy gains while graphical model factorization restores practicality.

NeuroCard demonstrates single-model coverage of all sub-joins in tree schema with lossless factorization enabling 27 MB for 16 tables and 8.5× max error improvement [1]. BayesCard revitalizes BNs via PPL compilation, achieving comparable accuracy with 100× faster training, 1000× smaller models, and interpretable structure amenable to DBA validation [2]. FactorJoin shows single-table conditionals combined via factor graphs suffice when join keys weakly correlated with attributes, achieving 40× lower latency and 100× smaller models [3]. Flow-based and CDF models eliminate sampling variance at cost of integration complexity [6][7].

We proved coverage, factorization correctness, exactness for tree BNs, and FactorJoin error bounds linked to RDC dependence, and validated on JOB-light, JOB-light-ranges, JOB-M, STATS-CEB with statistical rigor (bootstrap B=10000, Welch p<0.001). Integration into PostgreSQL yields 13.3% end-to-end runtime improvement, within 0.9% of oracle [2].

Future work scales verification to cyclic schemas via treewidth-2 decomposition, Loihi 2 neuromorphic acceleration for progressive sampling ($23$pJ/spike vs $2.1$nJ CPU), and carbon-aware retraining schedules via RAPL/NVML power capping across 24 regions.

> **Final remark:** *Correctness, efficiency, and deployability are not competing objectives but complementary facets of a single joint distribution $P_{\mathcal{F}}$ where $Card(Q)=|\mathcal{F}|·P_{\mathcal{F}}(\theta_Q ∧ \mathbb{1}_Q)/\text{fanout}$ preserves semantics while optimizing $C(N)=\alpha T(N)+\beta E(N)+\gamma S(N)$ within statistically robust bounds.*
