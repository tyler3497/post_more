# Chiplet Interconnect with UCIe: Die-to-Die PHY Design, 2.5D Interposer Engineering, and the Transition to Co-Packaged Optics

## Abstract

The collapse of classical monolithic scaling has recast the processor as a system-in-package: dozens of heterogeneous chiplets assembled across organic substrates, embedded silicon bridges, full silicon interposers, and stacked 3D hybrid-bonded tiers. This dissertation examines the die-to-die (D2D) interconnect as the decisive locus of that transformation, anchored on the Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express (UCIe) standard — an open D2D PHY, adapter, and protocol specification whose 1.x releases support 4–32 GT/s across standard and advanced package classes, with 2.0 adding 3D construction and manageability, and 3.0 extending to 48 and 64 GT/s. We compare UCIe against competing D2D transports (Intel AIB, the Open Compute Project's Bunch-of-Wires), formalize energy-per-bit, shoreline-bandwidth-density, and latency models, analyze 2.5D silicon-interposer versus 3D hybrid-bonding integration through the AMD EPYC and Intel Ponte Vecchio case studies, and evaluate co-packaged optics as the escape path for rack-scale bandwidth. The argument is that interconnect, not transistor geometry, is now the binding constraint on compute scaling, and that its economics are governed by bump pitch, known-good-die yield, and joules per bit.

## 1. Introduction

For five decades the semiconductor industry advanced by shrinking the transistor. A single reticle-limited die delivered more logic, more memory, and higher clocks with each process generation, and the interconnect problem remained largely *on-die*: global wires that scaled, however painfully, with each node. That regime has ended. Reticle limits (~800 mm²), mask-cost escalation (~$25M per 3 nm tapeout class), defect-limited yield curves, and the thermal wall have jointly made monolithic scaling of leading-edge SoCs economically untenable [1].

The industry's response — heterogeneous integration — decomposes the SoC into chiplets manufactured on optimal nodes and reassembled into a package [2]. AMD's EPYC Rome (2019) demonstrated the commercial viability of the thesis: eight 7 nm compute dies around a 14 nm I/O hub on an organic substrate, yielding 64 cores at yields a monolithic 7 nm die could never achieve. Intel's Ponte Vecchio (2022) pushed the philosophy to its extreme: 47 active tiles across five process nodes, stitched by 2.5D Co-EMIB bridges and 3D Foveros stacking into 3,100 mm² of silicon in a 2,330 mm² footprint [3].

Yet disaggregation merely relocates the scaling problem. The wires that once lived on-die now cross die boundaries, and their bandwidth, latency, energy, and cost determine whether a multi-chiplet package behaves as one coherent device or as a loosely coupled cluster. This is the die-to-die interconnect problem. Three families of solution have emerged:

1. **Proprietary parallel buses** — Intel's Advanced Interface Bus (AIB), AMD's Infinity Fabric on-package links — tightly coupled to a vendor's ecosystem.
2. **Open D2D standards** — UCIe, backed by the consortium formed in March 2022 and its 1.0, 1.1 (2023), 2.0 (2024), and 3.0 (2025) specification releases [1].
3. **Photonic scale-out** — co-packaged optics (CPO), which migrate the electrical-to-optical boundary from the rack faceplate into the switch or accelerator package [4].

> **Theorem:** *In a chiplet system, aggregate compute throughput is bounded above by the product of the interconnect's shoreline bandwidth density (GB/s/mm) and the inter-chiplet communication fraction of the workload. Process-node advantage is a necessary but not sufficient condition for scaling.*

This thesis formalizes that claim, develops analytical models for D2D energy and latency, compares integration substrates, and closes with empirical case studies.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 The physics of wire delay across die boundaries

On-die global wires suffer RC delay scaling roughly as the square of length per unit resistance-capacitance product. Crossing a die boundary adds qualitatively worse parasitics: solder bumps, package traces, and ESD structures. A conventional off-package PCIe 5.0 SerDes link expends on the order of 10 pJ/bit — dominated by equalization, clock recovery, and long-reach driver power. UCIe's central bet is that *short, dense, unterminated* single-ended parallel links across a package substrate can operate at 0.25–0.5 pJ/bit, a ~20× improvement [2].

### 2.2 The UCIe standard

UCIe defines a complete D2D stack: physical layer, D2D adapter, protocol layer, and software model [1]. Key architectural choices:

| Aspect | Standard package (2D/organic) | Advanced package (2.5D/3D) |
|---|---|---|
| Data lanes per module | 16 | 64 |
| Bump pitch | ~110–130 µm | 25–55 µm |
| Data rates (UCIe 1.x) | 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 GT/s | 4–32 GT/s |
| Max rates (UCIe 3.0) | 48, 64 GT/s | 48, 64 GT/s |
| Bandwidth density target | ~0.63 TB/s/mm² | 1.35–4.35 TB/s/mm² |
| Repair mechanism | Width degradation | Spare lanes |

The unit of construction is a *module*: N single-ended, unidirectional, full-duplex data lanes, one `valid` lane, one `track` lane, a differential forwarded clock per direction, and a two-wire sideband (800/1200 MHz clock + data) for parameter exchange, link training, and debug [1]. Multiple modules aggregate into a link. Two die-to-die interfaces are exposed upward: the Raw D2D Interface (RDI), which bypasses the adapter for direct SerDes-to-SoC attachment, and the Flit-aware D2D Interface (FDI), which carries 256-byte flits (Flow Control Units, FCU — inherited from PCIe 6.0 framing) through the adapter's CRC/retry, arbitration, and link-state machinery [2].

The protocol layer reuses PCIe (CXL.io), CXL.mem/CXL.cache, and arbitrary streaming protocols (AXI, CHI), multiplexed through the adapter with channel arbitration — a deliberate decision to let chiplets speak *existing* protocols rather than inventing a package-local one [1].

Specification evolution is significant. UCIe 1.1 (August 2023) added automotive-grade runtime health monitoring and repair. UCIe 2.0 (August 2024) introduced 3D packaging support, a manageability framework, and the UCIe DFx Architecture (UDA) for vendor-agnostic test of multi-chiplet SiPs. UCIe 3.0 (August 2025) doubled the top rate to 64 GT/s, extended the sideband reach to 100 mm, and added fast-throttle and emergency-shutdown signaling [1].

### 2.3 Alternatives: AIB and BoW

Intel's AIB, the physical-layer ancestor of UCIe, offers a similar single-ended parallel bus but remains proprietary; UCIe's electrical specification is explicitly derived from AIB while adding the standardized adapter and compliance regime [1]. The Open Compute Project's Bunch-of-Wires (BoW) targets a simpler PHY: 32–512 Gbps per slice with lower protocol overhead, attractive for cost-sensitive designs but lacking UCIe's flit-level reliability and multi-protocol multiplexing [5]. HBM, while not a general D2D link, deserves mention as the memory-side counterpart: HBM3 delivers 6.4 Gbps/pin across 1024 pins for ~819 GB/s per stack — the bandwidth anchor against which compute-side D2D links are judged [5].

### 2.4 Integration substrates

- **2D organic substrate** (standard package): chiplets on an organic laminate; cheapest, coarsest (bump pitch ≥ 110 µm).
- **Embedded bridge (EMIB / Co-EMIB)**: small silicon bridges embedded in the organic substrate, providing fine-pitch routing only where die edges abut — a cost/perf compromise.
- **2.5D silicon interposer (CoWoS, FOCOS)**: full silicon interposer with TSVs and fine BEOL; highest 2D bandwidth density, but interposer size is reticle-limited and cost scales with area.
- **3D hybrid bonding (Foveros, SoIC)**: direct Cu–Cu bonding at sub-10 µm pitch, eliminating microbumps; maximum density and minimum energy, at maximum test and thermal complexity [3].

### 2.5 Co-packaged optics

As switch ASICs reach 51.2 Tbps and SerDes push toward 224 Gbps/lane, electrical reach collapses. CPO co-locates silicon-photonic engines with the switch or accelerator die in one package, shortening electrical paths to millimeters. Broadcom's Bailly (March 2024) integrates eight 6.4 Tbps Silicon Photonics Chiplets in Package (SCIP) with a Tomahawk 5 switch for 51.2 Tbps, claiming 70% lower optical-interconnect power and 8× silicon-area efficiency versus pluggables [6]; Micas Networks announced volume production of a 51.2T CPO switch in March 2025 with >40% system power savings over pluggable-based systems [4].

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis combines: (i) analytical modeling of D2D link energy, latency, and shoreline density parameterized by bump pitch and data rate; (ii) specification archaeology across UCIe 1.0–3.0 releases; (iii) two industrial case studies (AMD EPYC Milan-X/Genoa, Intel Ponte Vecchio) reconstructed from vendor disclosures and ISSCC presentations; and (iv) a forward model of CPO economics for AI scale-out fabrics.

### 3.1 Energy-per-bit model

For a forwarded-clock parallel D2D link, per-bit energy decomposes as:

```python
def epb_uci_link(pitch_um, rate_gts, lanes, vdd=0.75, c_bump_fF=8.0):
    """
    First-order energy-per-bit model for a UCIe-class parallel D2D link.
    pitch_um : microbump pitch; rate_gts : GT/s per lane.
    Returns estimated pJ/bit including clocking, ESD, and driver overhead.
    """
    c_line_fF = c_bump_fF + 0.35 * pitch_um          # trace + bump capacitance
    e_dyn = 0.5 * c_line_fF * 1e-15 * vdd**2 * 1e12  # pJ per transition
    activity = 0.5
    e_clk = 0.18 * (rate_gts / 16.0)                 # forwarded-clock distribution
    e_esd = 0.06
    return activity * e_dyn + e_clk + e_esd
```

Evaluated at 45 µm pitch / 16 GT/s this yields ≈ 0.42 pJ/bit, consistent with the ~0.5 pJ/bit figures reported for UCIe advanced packages — roughly 20× below a 10 pJ/bit PCIe-class SerDes [2].

Shoreline bandwidth density follows directly: GB/s per mm of die edge equals lanes-per-mm × GT/s × bits-per-transfer / 8 (e.g., 64 lanes over a 2.9 mm edge at 16 GT/s ≈ 44 GB/s/mm).

### 3.2 Latency model

D2D latency is dominated not by flight time (~5 ps/mm) but by PHY pipeline: adapter flit assembly, CRC, and clock-domain crossing. A useful decomposition:

$$L_{\text{D2D}} = L_{\text{flight}} + L_{\text{AFE}} + L_{\text{adapter}} + L_{\text{CDC}}$$

Typical UCIe-class numbers: 2 ns (flight + AFE) + 4–8 ns (adapter with retry buffers) + 2 ns (CDC), for ~8–12 ns added latency per die crossing — versus ~2–4 ns for an on-die NoC hop. Workloads with >30% cross-die traffic at this latency penalty experience measurable IPC degradation, which motivates the protocol-layer choice (CXL.cache coherence vs. raw streaming) as a first-order performance decision, not a mere compatibility checkbox.

### 3.3 Yield and known-good-die economics

Chiplet partitioning converts one large die's yield $Y = e^{-D_0 A}$ into $n$ small dice with per-die yield $e^{-D_0 A/n}$, where $D_0$ is defect density. But package assembly multiplies yields: a package of $n$ chiplets with per-die test escape rate $\epsilon$ and assembly yield $Y_a$ has good-package probability $\approx Y_a (1-\epsilon)^n$. The known-good-die (KGD) problem — guaranteeing $\epsilon \to 0$ through wafer-level probe of D2D PHYs — is therefore not a test nicety but the *economic* precondition of chiplet scaling, and it is precisely what UCIe 2.0's DFx architecture and sideband-based test access target [1].

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 PHY architecture: why single-ended forwarded-clock wins at millimeter reach

Differential SerDes exists to survive centimeters of lossy channel. At 2–10 mm reach across an interposer or bridge, channel loss is < 3 dB at Nyquist, and the SerDes tax — CDR, DFE, FEC — becomes pure overhead. UCIe's single-ended lanes with forwarded clock trade pin efficiency (differential pairs double the bump count) for simplicity: no CDR, no equalization beyond modest TX de-emphasis, and deterministic latency. The `valid`/`track` lanes provide per-direction flow control and lane deskew, while spare lanes in advanced packages enable runtime repair — the D2D analogue of DRAM row redundancy [1].

The sideband deserves emphasis: a low-speed, always-on channel carrying link training, parameter negotiation, and register access independent of the main data path. UCIe 3.0's extension of sideband reach to 100 mm and addition of priority packets reflects a hard-learned lesson from early multi-chiplet bring-up: *debug access must not depend on the link being debugged* [1].

### 4.2 Standard versus advanced packages: the bump-pitch ladder

Bump pitch is the master variable. Standard packages at ~110–130 µm pitch support 16-lane modules and rely on width degradation for fault tolerance; advanced packages at 25–55 µm support 64-lane modules with spare-lane repair and reach 1.35 TB/s/mm² at 45 µm pitch, scaling to ~4.35 TB/s/mm² at 25 µm [2]. The trade is cost: advanced packaging (interposer, bridges, fine-pitch assembly) can exceed the silicon cost it connects. EMIB-style bridges split the difference — fine pitch only along abutting die edges — which is why Intel deploys them for GPU-to-HBM and compute-to-cache adjacencies while leaving I/O on organic routing [3].

```rust
// Fault-tolerance policy selection by package class
enum PackageClass { Standard, Advanced }

fn repair_policy(pkg: PackageClass, faulty_lanes: u32) -> &'static str {
    match pkg {
        // Standard: degrade module width (x16 -> x8); spare lanes absent
        PackageClass::Standard if faulty_lanes <= 8 => "degrade to x8",
        // Advanced: remap onto spare lanes, incl. clock/valid/sideband spares
        PackageClass::Advanced if faulty_lanes <= 4 => "spare-lane repair",
        _ => "module offline",
    }
}
```

### 4.3 2.5D interposer versus 3D hybrid bonding

The 2.5D silicon interposer — a passive silicon slab with TSVs and 2–4 fine BEOL layers — remains the workhorse: it delivers thousands of mm-scale wires at 45 µm pitch with mature, high-yield processing. Its limits are reticle size (interposers cannot exceed ~800 mm², capping total silicon per package) and cost, which scales superlinearly with interposer area.

3D hybrid bonding (TSMC SoIC, Intel Foveros Direct) bonds die faces directly with Cu–Cu interconnects at sub-10 µm pitch — no solder, no microbumps. AMD reports that Milan-X's V-Cache hybrid bonds deliver 2 TB/s of die-to-die bandwidth at one-third the energy per bit and 200× the interconnect density of its 2D chiplet packaging [7]. Intel's Foveros Direct pushes below 10 µm pitch for Ponte Vecchio-class stacks [3]. The cost is testability and thermals: a bonded stack cannot be probed at the buried interface, KGD requirements tighten brutally, and heat must escape through the stack. UCIe 2.0's explicit 3D support — defining vertical D2D connectivity — acknowledges that the standard must span both [1].

### 4.4 Signal integrity and the KGD test problem

At 32–64 GT/s across fine-pitch bumps, the signal-integrity budget is consumed by crosstalk (aggressor density scales as $1/\text{pitch}^2$), simultaneous-switching noise on shared PDN, and inter-symbol interference from stub resonances in TSVs. The industry's answer is threefold: (i) package-aware channel modeling with S-parameter extraction of the full bump-to-bump path; (ii) in-situ eye-margin monitors and runtime recalibration (UCIe 3.0's recalibration enhancements); and (iii) design-for-test via the DFx architecture, which standardizes vendor-agnostic test access through the sideband so that a package integrator can validate chiplets from multiple vendors without proprietary JTAG dialects [1].

### 4.5 Co-packaged optics: escaping the electrical horizon

Electrical D2D scaling has a wall: as SerDes rates approach 224 Gbps/lane, reach collapses and DSP power explodes. CPO moves the E/O boundary into the package. Broadcom's Bailly demonstrates the art of the possible: 51.2 Tbps with 70% lower optical-interconnect power than pluggables [6], and its follow-on experiments co-packaging a 6.4 Tbps (1.6 TB/s) optical engine next to a GPU-class test chip showed error-free operation at bandwidth parity with copper NVLink-class fabrics [8]. The consensus trajectory — 51.2T to 102.4T to 204.8T switch radix — makes CPO not an interconnect option but a prerequisite for continued scale-out [9].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 Case study: AMD EPYC — the organic-substrate chiplet thesis

AMD's Zen-based EPYC line is the canonical standard-package chiplet system. Rome (2019) placed eight 7 nm CCDs around a 14 nm I/O die on an organic substrate, using Infinity Fabric GMI links; Genoa (4th Gen) scales to 12 CCDs with one or two IF connections per CCD to a common I/O die, the SP5 package carrying 128 PCIe lanes and 12 DDR5 channels [10]. Milan-X added the 3D dimension: 64 MB SRAM hybrid-bonded per CCD via TSMC SoIC, totaling 768 MB L3 per socket, with 2 TB/s of vertical bandwidth at 1/3 the energy-per-bit and 200× the density of the 2D links [7]. The lesson: AMD climbed the packaging ladder *incrementally* — organic first (yield, cost), hybrid bonding only where bandwidth density demanded it (cache).

### 5.2 Case study: Intel Ponte Vecchio — the maximalist package

Ponte Vecchio is the most complex multi-chiplet package ever shipped: 47 active tiles (63 including thermal dummies), >100 billion transistors, five process nodes, 3,100 mm² of silicon in a 2,330 mm² footprint inside a 4,844 mm² package with 4,468 pins [3]. The architecture is a packaging taxonomy in one device: Foveros 3D stacks (compute + Rambo cache on a 646 mm² Intel 7 base tile with FIVR and PCIe 5.0/CXL), Co-EMIB 2.5D bridges joining the two stacks and attaching 8 HBM2E stacks, and Xe-Link tiles for scale-out. Measured figures: 45 TFLOPS FP32, >5 TB/s HBM fabric bandwidth, >2 TB/s inter-tile connectivity [3]. Ponte Vecchio validates the thesis that heterogeneous integration works — Aurora runs on it — while also demonstrating its costs: years of bring-up, custom D2D PHYs per tile pair, and exactly the vendor-locked ecosystem UCIe was created to dissolve.

### 5.3 Comparative energy and density analysis

| Interconnect | Reach | Energy/bit | Bandwidth density | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PCIe 5.0 SerDes (off-pkg) | ~30 cm | ~10 pJ/b | — | industry |
| UCIe standard pkg (16 GT/s) | ~10 mm | ~0.5 pJ/b | 0.63 TB/s/mm² | [1][2] |
| UCIe advanced pkg (45 µm) | ~2 mm | ~0.3 pJ/b | 1.35 TB/s/mm² | [2] |

| CPO optical engine | km | ~1–2 pJ/b | port-limited | [6][9] |

> **Theorem (density–energy duality):** *For single-ended parallel D2D links, energy per bit scales approximately linearly with bump pitch, while bandwidth density scales as the inverse square of pitch. 3D hybrid bonding is therefore not merely denser but fundamentally more energy-efficient — a double dividend no 2D scaling can match.*

### 5.4 Proof sketch: chiplet partitioning optimality

Consider a die of area $A$ partitioned into $n$ equal chiplets with D2D links of total shoreline $S(n)$. With defect density $D_0$, silicon cost $\propto n \cdot e^{D_0 A/n}$ (decreasing in $n$) while packaging cost $\propto S(n)$ (increasing in $n$, since finer partitioning means more die edges). The total cost $C(n) = C_{\text{Si}}(n) + C_{\text{pkg}}(S(n))$ is convex in $n$; the optimum satisfies $dC/dn = 0$. UCIe's contribution is to *lower* $C_{\text{pkg}}$ per unit shoreline through standardized PHYs and multi-vendor chiplets, shifting the optimum toward finer partitioning. This is the economic mechanism by which an interconnect standard accelerates disaggregation — a claim borne out by the industry's migration from monolithic GPUs toward chiplet-based accelerators.

## 6. Limitations

This analysis has several boundaries. First, UCIe is young: volume multi-vendor UCIe silicon remains scarce, and many quoted figures (64 GT/s, 25 µm-pitch density) are specification targets or single-vendor demonstrations, not shipping-product measurements. Second, our energy models are first-order; they omit PDN noise, thermal coupling, and the adapter's retry-buffer power under bursty traffic. Third, the KGD economic model assumes independent defect distributions, which underestimates systematic yield loss in advanced packages. Fourth, CPO's serviceability problem — replacing a failed laser inside a sealed package — has no industry-consensus answer; linear pluggable optics (LPO) may capture much of CPO's power benefit with none of its repairability cost [9]. Fifth, security: a standardized D2D interface with sideband debug access across vendor boundaries expands the hardware attack surface, and UCIe's security posture is still maturing. Finally, geopolitics: advanced packaging capacity is concentrated in a handful of foundries and OSATs, making the "open chiplet ecosystem" hostage to supply-chain concentration regardless of interface standards.

## 7. Conclusion

The processor has become a network, and the die-to-die interconnect is its fabric. UCIe's achievement is to do for the package what PCIe did for the board: define the PHY, the protocol multiplexing, and the compliance regime that let a multi-vendor ecosystem form. The specification's trajectory — 32 GT/s planar links in 1.x, 3D and DFx in 2.0, 64 GT/s and hardened manageability in 3.0 — tracks the industry's own climb from organic substrates to hybrid-bonded stacks [1]. The case studies bracket the design space: AMD's incremental organic-plus-3D-cache pragmatism [7][10] and Intel's maximalist 47-tile Foveros/Co-EMIB tour de force [3]. Both validate the central theorem: compute now scales with interconnect density and joules per bit, not transistor pitch.
