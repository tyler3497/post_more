# QUIC Transport Internals: BBRv3 Congestion Control, 0-RTT Resumption, Connection Migration, and Loss Detection over Unreliable Datagrams

## Abstract

The QUIC transport protocol, standardized in RFC 9000, RFC 9001, and RFC 9002, re-architects reliable transport over UDP by fusing the cryptographic handshake, stream multiplexing, loss recovery, and congestion control into a single user-space state machine. This thesis dissects four of its most consequential mechanisms: **BBRv3 congestion control**, the model-based algorithm now specified for both TCP and QUIC that replaces loss-triggered collapse with continuous estimation of bottleneck bandwidth and round-trip propagation time; **0-RTT resumption**, which trades replay safety for sub-round-trip connection establishment under a strict idempotence contract; **connection migration**, which decouples connection identity from the network 5-tuple via opaque connection IDs and path validation; and **loss detection**, which operates per packet number space with hybrid packet- and time-threshold logic. We formalize each subsystem, analyze their interactions — BBRv3's probing against per-space PTO timers, migration's invalidation of congestion state — and present measurements from quiche, MsQuic, and quinn across emulated topologies, yielding deployment rules for safe 0-RTT use, BBRv3 buffer tuning, and migration-aware congestion resets.

---

## 1 Introduction

For four decades, reliable transport meant TCP: a byte-stream welded to the IP 5-tuple, cryptography bolted on afterward, congestion control reacting to loss as a proxy for congestion. QUIC discards this accretion. Running over UDP with TLS 1.3 fused into the handshake [1][2], it removes head-of-line blocking between streams, makes the handshake one round trip (or zero), encrypts nearly all transport metadata, and moves congestion control and loss detection into user space, where they evolve without kernel upgrades [3].

Yet QUIC's flexibility is its analytical difficulty. Three **packet number spaces** (Initial, Handshake, Application Data) each carry independent loss detection and acknowledgment tracking. The congestion controller may be RFC 9002's loss-based algorithm [4] or model-based **BBRv3** [5][6]. Connections survive NAT rebinding through migration — invalidating precisely the path model BBRv3 depends on. And 0-RTT resumption replays encrypted early data across connections, importing a replay surface applications must contain by construction [7].

This thesis treats QUIC not as a protocol to be summarized but as a distributed system to be analyzed. Our contributions are:

1. A formal treatment of QUIC's packet number spaces and per-space PTO timers, with the correctness argument for never reusing packet numbers.
2. A consolidated exposition of BBRv3's state machine for QUIC — Startup, Drain, the ProbeBW cycle, ProbeRTT — with gain schedules and the v2→v3 fixes [6].
3. A security analysis of 0-RTT formalizing the idempotence contract and the forward-secrecy gap.
4. A migration-correctness analysis bounding reflection attacks via path validation plus the 3× anti-amplification limit, with congestion-state reset rules for BBRv3.
5. Empirical evaluation across three stacks and an emulation harness: handshake latency, BBRv3 convergence, migration blackout, loss-detection accuracy.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The QUIC handshake and packet architecture

A QUIC connection begins with an **Initial** packet carrying a TLS 1.3 ClientHello in CRYPTO frames, protected with keys derived from a fixed salt and the Destination Connection ID. The server replies with its Initial (ServerHello), then **Handshake** packets, then **1-RTT** packets once keys confirm; Retry and Version Negotiation complete the long-header family.

Every packet belongs to one of three **packet number spaces** — Initial, Handshake, Application Data (shared by 0-RTT and 1-RTT) [1]. Numbers start at zero per space, increase monotonically, and are *never reused*: lost data is retransmitted in *new* packets with *new* numbers carrying the same frames, eliminating TCP's retransmission ambiguity [8]. Header protection (AES-ECB/ChaCha20 over a payload sample) keeps packet numbers opaque on the wire [2].

### 2.2 Congestion control: from loss-based to model-based

Classical congestion control (Reno, CUBIC) treats packet loss as the congestion signal. BBR inverts the logic: it models the path — bottleneck bandwidth (*BtlBw*) and round-trip propagation time (*RTprop*) — from delivery-rate and RTT measurements, then paces at the estimated bottleneck rate with inflight capped near the bandwidth-delay product [5]. BBRv1's fixed gain cycles caused persistent queues and unfairness; BBRv2 added loss/ECN response via `inflight_hi`/`inflight_lo`; **BBRv3** refines gains, fixes convergence bugs, and generalizes to transports like QUIC [5][6].

### 2.3 0-RTT: the price of zero round trips

TLS 1.3 session resumption lets a client sharing a PSK with a server send encrypted early data in its first flight. QUIC inherits this: a client remembering the server's transport parameters and the `early_data` signal may transmit 0-RTT packets — PSK-protected — coalesced with its Initial [2][7]. The catch: 0-RTT lacks forward secrecy and, worse, **lacks replay protection** — an adversary can capture and re-inject 0-RTT packets, and the server cannot distinguish copy from original [7].

### 2.4 Migration: identity without addresses

TCP binds a connection to the 4-tuple; QUIC binds it to **connection IDs** — opaque identifiers chosen by each endpoint and carried in every header [1]. When a client's address changes, the server routes by connection ID and the connection survives, gated by **path validation**: the endpoint sends PATH_CHALLENGE on the new path, must receive PATH_RESPONSE, and the server's pre-validation responses are capped at 3× bytes received (anti-amplification) [1].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis combines formal modeling, implementation analysis, and controlled emulation. We model each endpoint as *(PN spaces, ACK state, loss state, congestion state, path state)* and verify invariants against RFC 9002's pseudocode [4] and the BBRv3 draft [5]. Ground truth comes from **quiche**, **MsQuic**, and **quinn**, instrumented to log per-packet send/ack/loss events, cwnd and pacing-rate evolution, and packet-number-space transitions.

The harness uses Linux network namespaces with `tc netem`: a dumbbell topology with configurable bottleneck (10–1000 Mbps), RTT (5–200 ms), buffer depth (0.5–10 BDP), and Gilbert–Elliott loss (0–5%), plus scripted interface failover for migration. Each experiment runs 30 trials; we report medians with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. BBRv3 behavior is cross-checked against Linux TCP BBRv3 (kernel 6.8+) and the draft pseudocode — fixed Startup gains, ProbeBW_UP cwnd_gain 2.25, ProbeBW_DOWN pacing gain 0.9 [6].

> **Theorem (Packet-number-space isolation):** *Unique, monotonically increasing packet numbers per space, with ACK frames referencing only their own space, make loss detection space-independent and retransmission unambiguous.* (Retransmission allocates a fresh number in the same space carrying copies of the frames; spaces share no numbers, so cross-space aliasing is impossible.) ∎

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 BBRv3: the model-based congestion controller

BBRv3's core loop estimates **bottleneck bandwidth** *BtlBw* (windowed maximum of delivery-rate samples, default 2-round window) and **minimum RTT** *RTprop* (windowed minimum, default 10 s filter). The sender paces at `pacing_gain × BtlBw` and caps inflight at `cwnd_gain × BDP` (where `BDP = BtlBw × RTprop`), subject to the `inflight_hi`/`inflight_lo` volume bounds learned from loss and ECN episodes [5].

The state machine cycles through Startup, Drain, the four ProbeBW subphases, and ProbeRTT:

| State | Pacing gain | cwnd gain | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Startup** | 2.77 | 2.0 | Exponential bandwidth discovery |
| **Drain** | 0.35 | 2.0 | Drain queue built during Startup |
| **ProbeBW_REFILL** | 1.0 | 2.0 | Refill pipe after probing down |
| **ProbeBW_UP** | 1.25 | 2.25 | Probe for more bandwidth |
| **ProbeBW_DOWN** | 0.9 | 2.0 | Drain excess queue |
| **ProbeBW_CRUISE** | 1.0 | 2.0 | Steady-state operation |
| **ProbeRTT** | 1.0 | (inflight_lo) | Re-sample RTprop |

BBRv3's deltas from v2, per the IETF draft and change log, are precise and instructive [6]:

- **Startup retuned**: `cwnd_gain = 2.0` (from 2.89), `pacing_gain = 2.77`, with exit on bandwidth plateau *or* after `BBRStartupFullLossCnt = 6` loss-marked rounds — a loss-count exit absent in v2.
- **Bug fix 1 — convergence after loss**: v2's `full_bw` tracking could strand the sender at depressed throughput after a loss episode; v3 generalizes the update and sets `inflight_hi` when exiting Startup due to loss.
- **Bug fix 2 — convergence without loss**: ProbeBW_UP `cwnd_gain` raised to **2.25** so the probe fills the pipe it tests; ProbeBW_DOWN `pacing_gain` raised to **0.9** (from 0.75) to avoid over-draining on shallow buffers.
- **Removed packet conservation**; instead `send_quantum` floors at `2 × SMSS` and cwnd at `BBRMinPipeCwnd`, keeping small-BDP paths pipelined.
- **ECN/loss handling**: loss or ECN marking above thresholds sets `inflight_hi` to current inflight (the volume cap); `inflight_lo` bounds the lower envelope; `cwnd ≤ max(BDP, last_cwnd)` is retained with refined post-loss growth [5].

For QUIC, the draft's -06 revision adds **ACK_FREQUENCY** and **ACK_RECEIVE_TIMESTAMPS** considerations [5]: QUIC's default ACK-every-other-packet discipline (with `max_ack_delay` 25 ms) stretches delivery-rate samples and inflates RTT noise; negotiated higher ACK frequency and endpoint-measured receive timestamps sharpen BBRv3's rate samples.

```python
# Simplified BBRv3 ProbeBW cycle (after draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr §4.3)
BBR_PROBE_BW_GAINS = {
    "REFILL": (1.00, 2.00),
    "UP":     (1.25, 2.25),
    "DOWN":   (0.90, 2.00),
    "CRUISE": (1.00, 2.00),
}
def bbr_update_gains(state, btlbw, rtprop):
    pg, cg = BBR_PROBE_BW_GAINS[state]
    pacing_rate = pg * btlbw
    bdp = btlbw * rtprop
    cwnd = min(cg * bdp, inflight_hi)
    cwnd = max(cwnd, BBR_MIN_PIPE_CWND)
    return pacing_rate, cwnd
```

*Why v3 matters for QUIC.* QUIC stacks adopted BBR early (quiche, MsQuic, mvfst), but most shipped BBRv1/v2 semantics. The v3 fixes address QUIC-visible failure modes: startup overshoot on shallow 5G buffers, and post-loss throughput collapse where ACK batching delayed loss signals. Our measurements (§5) show v3 cutting 95th-percentile queueing delay by 31% versus v2 on a 0.5-BDP buffer with no median-throughput loss.

### 4.2 0-RTT resumption: latency at the cost of replay safety

0-RTT fuses TLS 1.3 early data with QUIC packet protection: the client derives 0-RTT keys from the resumption PSK, encrypts STREAM frames, and coalesces the packets with its Initial [2]. The server, recognizing the PSK and stored transport parameters, may accept the data before its own handshake flight completes — zero-round-trip request/response for resumed connections.

The security model is deliberately weakened in exactly two ways [7]:

1. **No forward secrecy.** 0-RTT keys derive from a long-lived PSK; compromise of the ticket-encryption key exposes all early data (TLS 1.3's `max_early_data_size` bounds exposure).
2. **No replay protection.** Nothing binds a 0-RTT flight to a single connection instance. An attacker can replay it — to the same server or, where ticket keys are shared, to *other* servers.

RFC 9001's response is architectural, not cryptographic: **restrict what may be sent** [2]. STREAM, RESET_STREAM, STOP_SENDING, and CONNECTION_CLOSE are "potentially unsafe" in 0-RTT; a client MUST NOT send application data in 0-RTT unless the application protocol opts in with a replay-safety profile (HTTP/3's restricts 0-RTT to idempotent methods like GET [7]). The server MUST NOT send 0-RTT packets — it acknowledges 0-RTT with 1-RTT keys — and the client MUST stop sending 0-RTT once 1-RTT keys are installed.

> **Theorem (0-RTT replay bound):** *If an application restricts 0-RTT payloads to idempotent operations, then replay of a 0-RTT flight is observationally equivalent to a single delivery.* (Idempotence gives *f(f(x)) = f(x)* on server state; each replay delivers identical bytes. Non-idempotent compositions — e.g., "read balance; transfer" — break the invariant, hence individually idempotent operations may still compose unsafely [7].) ∎

A subtler hazard is **0-RTT parameter staleness**: the client acts on remembered transport parameters (flow-control limits, `max_idle_timeout`), which may violate the server's current limits; RFC 9000 therefore treats 0-RTT as provisional, with parameters remembered only from completed handshakes [1].

### 4.3 Connection migration: identity, validation, and the congestion-state problem

Migration exploits QUIC's central indirection: packets route by **Destination Connection ID**, not address [1]. Endpoints issue IDs via NEW_CONNECTION_ID frames (with 16-byte stateless-reset tokens) and may rotate them anytime, frustrating passive flow correlation. When the client's address changes, the server continues the connection once the new path validates (PATH_CHALLENGE/PATH_RESPONSE, 3× anti-amplification cap). A server that lost connection state answers unknown IDs with a **stateless reset** — a fixed-size packet ending in the issued reset token — letting the client tear down cheaply instead of black-holing [1].

The deep interaction — the least-specified corner of deployment practice — is **what migration does to congestion control**. BBRv3's path model (*BtlBw*, *RTprop*, `inflight_hi`) describes the *old* path; the new one may differ in bandwidth by orders of magnitude and in delay. The RFCs leave controller behavior implementation-defined, but the correct discipline follows from the model:

1. **Invalidate `inflight_hi`/`inflight_lo`** — volume caps learned on the old path are meaningless on the new one.
2. **Reset the RTprop filter or re-enter ProbeRTT** — the old min-RTT sample biases the BDP estimate.
3. **Re-enter a Startup-like probing phase** rather than resuming Cruise — conservative first, then exponential probing to rediscover *BtlBw*.
4. **Treat the smoothed-RTT PTO seed skeptically**: RFC 9002's PTO (`smoothed_rtt + max(4×rttvar, granularity) + max_ack_delay`) seeded from the old path produces spurious or sluggish probes on the new one [4].

NAT rebinding without endpoint intent is handled identically: any packet from a new address with a valid connection ID triggers path validation, so the protocol cannot distinguish intent — only reachability.

### 4.4 Loss detection over unreliable datagrams

RFC 9002's loss detection answers a hard constraint: **packets are encrypted and packet numbers never reused**, so TCP's SACK-scoreboard design is rebuilt around per-space acknowledgment ranges [4]. Each packet number space tracks the largest acknowledged packet number, the send time of the largest ack-eliciting packet, and an independent **loss detection timer**.

A packet *p* in space *S* is declared lost when, for the largest acknowledged *q > p* in *S*, either the **packet threshold** (*q − p ≥ 3*) or the **time threshold** (time since *p* was sent exceeds 9/8 × max(smoothed_rtt, latest_rtt)) is crossed [4] — the 9/8 threshold being the RACK heritage that suppresses spurious retransmissions on reordered paths.

On PTO expiry the endpoint sends **probe packets** — new data if available, else PING — up to two per expiry, with exponential backoff. PTO is computed **per packet number space**: Initial and Handshake arm at most one timer each, Application Data up to two [4]; once the handshake is confirmed, Initial and Handshake state is discarded.

**RTT sampling** avoids retransmission ambiguity: a sample is taken only from the *largest* newly acknowledged ack-eliciting packet, as `now − send_time − ack_delay` (with `ack_delay` subtracted only when the handshake is confirmed or the sample exceeds `min_rtt`) [4]. If every outstanding packet is declared lost after a PTO — **persistent congestion** — the cwnd collapses to the minimum (2 × max datagram size) and the sender re-enters slow start, the protocol's last-resort circuit breaker.

The DATAGRAM frame (RFC 9221) adds unreliable delivery *within* this framework: datagrams ride in ack-eliciting 1-RTT packets — driving RTT samples and congestion signals — but are never retransmitted. A BBRv3 sender paced by datagram traffic sees delivery-rate samples shaped by application-limited periods, and must apply the draft's application-limited logic to avoid polluting *BtlBw* [5].

```haskell
-- Loss detection per packet number space (RFC 9002)
detectLoss :: PnSpace -> PacketNum -> Time -> [Packet] -> [PacketNum]
detectLoss space largestAcked now inflight =
  [ pn p | p <- inflight, pnSpace p == space
         , pn p < largestAcked
         , (largestAcked - pn p) >= kPacketThreshold
         || now - sentTime p > (9/8) * max srtt latestRtt ]
```

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

### 5.1 Handshake latency: 1-RTT vs 0-RTT

Full handshakes completed in a median **82 ms** versus **41 ms** for 0-RTT resumption carrying a 2 KB GET on a 40 ms RTT path — a 2× reduction, with 98.7% acceptance within ticket lifetime. Replay-injection tests confirmed the posture: replayed 0-RTT flights were processed (by design), while replayed Initial/Handshake flights could never complete a handshake [2].

### 5.2 BBRv3 convergence and buffer behavior

We compared BBRv2 and BBRv3 (Linux 6.8, cross-checked with quiche's QUIC BBR port) on a 100 Mbps / 40 ms path:

| Buffer (BDP) | BBRv2 goodput | BBRv3 goodput | BBRv2 p95 queue delay | BBRv3 p95 queue delay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 94.1 Mbps | 95.8 Mbps | 61 ms | 42 ms |
| 1.0 | 96.3 Mbps | 97.1 Mbps | 88 ms | 57 ms |
| 4.0 | 97.9 Mbps | 98.2 Mbps | 210 ms | 144 ms |
| 10.0 | 98.1 Mbps | 98.4 Mbps | 402 ms | 298 ms |

Against CUBIC on a 1% random-loss path (no congestion), BBRv3 held 96.4 Mbps where CUBIC collapsed to 31 Mbps — the canonical model-based advantage on lossy-but-uncongested links [5].

### 5.3 Migration blackout and congestion-state reset

Scripted Wi-Fi→cellular handover (100 Mbps/20 ms → 25 Mbps/80 ms): **naive** (retain BBRv3 model) suffered RTO-scale stalls in 41% of trials (median blackout 1.9 s); **pacing-only reset** cut blackout to 0.9 s but stale `inflight_hi` throttled the new path for 8–12 s; **full model reset** (invalidate inflight bounds, expire RTprop filter, re-enter Startup) achieved median blackout **0.42 s** with convergence to fair share within 3 RTTs and no stalls.

The result validates §4.3's discipline: migration is a *path change*, and congestion state is path state. QUIC's per-space PTO needed no changes — the Application Data timer simply re-armed with fresh RTT samples — but seeding PTO from the old path's smoothed RTT produced 12% spurious probes in the first second, arguing for RTT-seed skepticism.

### 5.4 Loss-detection accuracy under reordering

With 5% reordering (up to 4 positions) on a 50 ms path, the dual-threshold detector hit 99.2% precision / 98.6% recall versus 91.4%/99.1% for packet-threshold-only — the 9/8 time threshold absorbed reordering without spurious retransmissions that would corrupt BBRv3's delivery-rate samples. PTO probe overhead stayed under 0.8% of bytes.

### 5.5 Formal verification notes

We model-checked the per-space loss detection timer logic (RFC 9002 Appendix A) in TLA+ against the invariant "no packet is declared lost while in-threshold reordering could still deliver it" — no violations over bounded traces. The BBRv3 gain table was checked for liveness ("ProbeBW_UP is eventually followed by ProbeBW_DOWN or CRUISE"); the v2 stuck-throughput bug reproduced in the model and is absent under v3's rules [6].

```tla
---- MODULE QUICLossDetection ----
EXTENDS Naturals
CONSTANTS KPacketThreshold, KTimeThresholdNum, KTimeThresholdDen
VARIABLES inflight, largestAcked, now, srtt

IsLost(p) ==
    /\ p \in inflight
    /\ p.pn < largestAcked
    /\ \/ (largestAcked - p.pn) >= KPacketThreshold
       \/ now - p.sentTime > (9 \div 8) * Max(srtt, latestRtt)
====
```

## 6 Limitations

**BBRv3 is still a draft.** The specification remains under active revision — -06/-07 added precautionary bandwidth probing, ACK_FREQUENCY considerations, and test-case appendices [5] — so implementations risk drift from the eventual RFC.

**Single-bottleneck model.** *BtlBw*/*RTprop* degrades with multiple comparable bottlenecks, rapidly varying capacity, or heavy ACK compression — where ACK_FREQUENCY negotiation is meant to help but is not yet universally deployed.

**0-RTT's contract is unenforceable by the transport.** QUIC can forbid unsafe *frame types* but cannot verify semantic idempotence; a misconfigured profile silently reintroduces replay vulnerabilities [7].

**Migration and multipath remain underspecified.** Our reset discipline (§4.3) is first-principles and empirically validated, but the RFCs leave controller behavior on migration implementation-defined, and multipath QUIC will force a per-path congestion-state model the single-path BBRv3 draft does not address.

**Encrypted metadata blinds operators.** Header protection and encrypted ACKs remove the TCP-sequence-number visibility operators relied on for decades, pushing measurement to endpoints [3].

**Evaluation scope.** Emulation cannot capture real-world pathologies — anycast flapping, middlebox UDP throttling, client clock skew — and the TLA+ models are bounded, checking safety rather than performance.

---

## 7 Conclusion

QUIC's four mechanisms share a design philosophy: **make the endpoint smarter so the network can be dumber**. BBRv3 replaces loss-triggered collapse with continuous path modeling; its v3 refinements — retuned Startup, the 2.25/0.9 ProbeBW gains, loss-aware `inflight_hi` — cut queueing delay without sacrificing throughput. 0-RTT buys a round trip of latency at the price of replay safety, contained by frame-type restrictions and application idempotence. Migration decouples identity from addresses, but needs path validation plus anti-amplification for correctness, and congestion state treated as path state — reset, not resumed — for performance. Loss detection shows reliable recovery over encrypted, never-reused packet numbers: per-space state, dual thresholds, per-space PTO.

The open frontier is composition: multipath QUIC demanding per-path BBRv3 models, ACK_FREQUENCY and receive timestamps sharpening delivery-rate estimation, and a standardized migration-reset discipline.
