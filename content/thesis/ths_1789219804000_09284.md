# Perfectoid Spaces and the Weight-Monodromy Conjecture: Scholze's Prismatic Cohomology Revolution

## Abstract

This thesis traces one of the most consequential arcs in modern arithmetic geometry: Peter Scholze's introduction of perfectoid spaces [1], his proof of the weight–monodromy conjecture for set-theoretic complete intersections in toric varieties by *tilting* across the characteristic divide, and the subsequent Bhatt–Scholze theory of prisms and prismatic cohomology [2], which unifies essentially all integral p-adic cohomology theories under a single site-theoretic framework. We explain how almost purity turns deep ramification into an analytic asset, how tilting transports ℓ-adic Galois representations from mixed to equal characteristic where Deligne's *Weil II* [6] applies, and how a prism — a δ-ring A equipped with a Cartier divisor I generated by a distinguished element — "deperfects" the perfectoid story to yield a universal cohomology theory RΓ_Δ specializing to crystalline, de Rham, étale, q-de Rham, and Hodge–Tate cohomology. We survey the Nygaard filtration, syntomic Z_p(n), and the p-adic weight–monodromy program [5], and delineate precisely what remains open.

## 1 Intro

The weight–monodromy conjecture, formulated by Deligne in the wake of the Weil conjectures, is one of the central open problems in arithmetic geometry. For a proper smooth variety X over a local field K with finite residue field of cardinality q, and a prime ℓ distinct from the residue characteristic, the ℓ-adic étale cohomology H^i(X_{K̄}, **Q**_ℓ) carries a continuous action of the Weil–Deligne group; the nilpotent monodromy operator N induces the *monodromy filtration* M_•, and the conjecture asserts the purity of its graded pieces:

> **Theorem:** *(Deligne, conjectured [6])*. The Frobenius eigenvalues on gr^M_j H^i(X_{K̄}, **Q**_ℓ) are q-Weil numbers of weight i + j for every i, j.

In equal characteristic, Deligne himself proved it as a corollary of *Weil II* [6], and Ito extended it to arbitrary equal-characteristic local fields. In *mixed* characteristic — for varieties over p-adic fields — the conjecture resisted all classical methods for decades; only the semistable case (Rapoport–Zink) and low dimensions (via de Jong's alterations and the Hodge index theorem) were known. The obstruction was fundamental: in mixed characteristic there is no good notion of "passing to characteristic p" for ℓ-adic cohomology.

Scholze's 2011 breakthrough, perfectoid spaces [1], changed this by making the characteristic divide *permeable*. A perfectoid space in characteristic 0 admits a canonical *tilt* — a perfectoid space in characteristic p with the same étale site — allowing ℓ-adic cohomology to be carried across the divide and compared with the equal-characteristic case where Deligne's theorem applies. Eight years later, Bhatt and Scholze [2] identified the *integral* shadow of this mechanism: the prism, a deperfection of the perfectoid ring, whose cohomology theory prismatic cohomology subsumes crystalline, de Rham, étale, q-de Rham, and Hodge–Tate cohomology in one framework. This thesis presents the full arc, from almost purity to prisms, and its decisive application to the weight–monodromy conjecture.

## 2 Background

The story has deep roots. Tate observed in the 1960s that *deeply ramified* extensions of local fields behave, paradoxically, like unramified ones: every finite extension of a deeply ramified extension is *almost* unramified. Faltings developed this into *almost mathematics* and proved the almost purity theorem [1]: finite étale covers of certain highly ramified rings are almost finite étale. Fontaine, meanwhile, built the period rings of p-adic Hodge theory — B_crys, B_st, B_dR — and in particular A_inf = W(**O**_C^♭), the Witt vectors of the tilt of the integers of a perfectoid field, equipped with the surjection θ : A_inf → **O**_C whose kernel is principal [3].

On the cohomological side, the Weil conjectures (Deligne [6]) established that for a smooth projective variety over a finite field **F**_q, the eigenvalues of Frobenius on H^i are Weil numbers of weight i — the gold standard of purity. The weight–monodromy conjecture is the local, bad-reduction analogue: it asks that the monodromy filtration, which measures how far the representation is from being unramified, coincide with the weight filtration. The key structural input is Grothendieck's ℓ-adic monodromy theorem: inertia acts quasi-unipotently, yielding the nilpotent operator N with the commutation relation

```
N · Frob = q · Frob · N        (so N : V → V(-1) is a morphism of Weil-Deligne representations)
```

and the monodromy filtration M_• uniquely characterized by N(M_j) ⊂ M_{j-2} and N^j : gr^M_j → gr^M_{-j}(-j) an isomorphism. The conjecture is that gr^M_j H^i is pure of weight i + j [6].

Classically, the only route to the conjecture ran through semistable reduction and vanishing cycles (Rapoport–Zink), where a weight spectral sequence degenerates — but the required non-degeneracy statement is known only in low dimensions. What was missing was a *geometric bridge* between mixed and equal characteristic. Perfectoid spaces provided exactly that.
## 3 Methodology

This thesis proceeds by *expository synthesis with technical reconstruction*: rather than proving new theorems, we reconstruct the logical spine of the perfectoid–prismatic revolution and isolate the precise mechanism by which it attacks the weight–monodromy conjecture. Our method has four stages:

1. **Almost geometry.** We define perfectoid rings and spaces, prove (in outline) the tilting equivalence and the almost purity theorem, and explain why deep ramification is an asset rather than an obstacle.
2. **Tilting reduction.** We reconstruct Scholze's argument reducing the weight–monodromy conjecture for toric complete intersections from mixed to equal characteristic, identifying the two comparison isomorphisms that make the reduction rigorous.
3. **Prismatic deperfection.** We introduce δ-rings and prisms, construct the prismatic site, and catalog the comparison theorems that make RΓ_Δ a universal integral cohomology theory.
4. **The p-adic frontier.** We survey how the same tilting strategy, combined with Hyodo–Kato cohomology and the Nygaard filtration, yields the *p-adic* weight–monodromy conjecture in the toric case [5], and we state the remaining open problems.

We assume familiarity with schemes, étale cohomology, and basic p-adic Hodge theory, but recall every nonstandard definition. All results are attributed to their original sources; the synthesis and the emphasis on the weight–monodromy thread are our own.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Perfectoid Rings, Tilting, and the Almost Purity Theorem

A *Tate ring* is a complete topological ring possessing an open subring of definition and a topologically nilpotent unit ϖ. A **perfectoid ring** is a complete Tate ring R such that:

- the Frobenius endomorphism Φ : R°/p → R°/p is *surjective*, where R° is the subring of power-bounded elements; and
- there exists a pseudo-uniformizer π ∈ R with π^p dividing p in R° (in characteristic p the second condition is vacuous, and perfectoid rings are precisely the complete Tate rings with surjective Frobenius).

The primordial example is **O**_C, the integers of a complete algebraically closed extension of **Q**_p, which contains a compatible system of p-power roots of unity — and hence of every element, up to the maximal ideal. For a perfectoid ring R, the **tilt** is

```
R^♭ = lim←_{x ↦ x^p} R/p     (a perfect ring of characteristic p)
```

and R^♭ is again perfectoid, with a canonical multiplicative (non-additive) map ♯ : R^♭ → R lifting compatible p-power roots. The construction globalizes: a *perfectoid space* is an adic space locally of the form Spa(R, R^+) with (R, R^+) perfectoid, and Scholze proves the **tilting equivalence** [1]:

> **Theorem:** *(Tilting equivalence, [1])*. For a perfectoid field K with tilt K^♭, the tilting functor X ↦ X^♭ induces an equivalence of categories between perfectoid spaces over K and perfectoid spaces over K^♭, identifying their underlying topological spaces and their étale sites: (X_ét) ≃ (X^♭_ét).

Consequently, the ℓ-adic cohomology of X and X^♭ *coincide* as Galois representations — a bridge across the characteristic divide. The second pillar is the **almost purity theorem** [1], which Scholze reproves by tilting: if A is perfectoid and B/A is finite étale, then B is perfectoid and B°/A° is *almost* finite étale (the almost mathematics being with respect to the maximal ideal of K°). The point is that in characteristic p, perfection makes finite étale covers trivial to control; tilting carries this control back to characteristic 0. Bhatt–Scholze later proved an improved version allowing ramification along arbitrary closed subsets, *without* adic spaces [2].

### 4.2 Scholze's Proof of the Weight–Monodromy Conjecture for Toric Complete Intersections

Let K be a p-adic field, X a proper smooth variety over K that is a *set-theoretic complete intersection inside a projective smooth toric variety*. Scholze's proof [1, §8] proceeds in three steps:

1. **Perfectoid covers of toric varieties.** For a smooth projective toric variety Y, Scholze constructs an affinoid perfectoid cover Ỹ → Y^ad by taking inverse limits along the toric Frobenius (multiplication by p on the fan). Because the toric Frobenius is finite flat of degree p^{dim}, the limit is perfectoid.
2. **Tilt and algebraize.** Tilting gives Ỹ^♭, a perfectoid space over K^♭. Crucially, the tilt of a toric perfectoid cover is (locally) the perfection of a toric variety over the residue field's Laurent series field **F**_q((t)) — an *equal-characteristic* object. The étale cohomology H^i(X_{K̄}, **Q**_ℓ) is identified, via the cover, with the cohomology of a subvariety of the tilt.
3. **Apply Weil II.** Over the equal-characteristic local field, the weight–monodromy conjecture is known by Deligne [6] (as extended by Ito). Since tilting identifies the Galois representations — including the monodromy operators — purity of the graded pieces transfers back to mixed characteristic.

The hypotheses are essential: the toric ambient variety supplies the perfectoid cover, and the complete-intersection condition keeps the cohomology supported where the comparison is available. The general case remains open.

### 4.3 Prisms, δ-Rings, and the Prismatic Site

Perfectoid rings are marvelous but rigid: they live at infinite level and admit few deformations. Bhatt and Scholze asked what structure *remains* when one "deperfects" — and found the **prism** [2]. A **δ-ring** is a commutative ring A with a map δ : A → A satisfying δ(0) = δ(1) = 0 and the identities making φ(x) = x^p + pδ(x) a ring endomorphism lifting Frobenius mod p. A **prism** is a pair (A, I) where:

- A is a δ-ring, (p, I)-adically complete (derived);
- I ⊂ A defines a Cartier divisor on Spec A (pro-Zariski locally principal, generated by a non-zero-divisor);
- I is generated by a *distinguished element* d, i.e. δ(d) is a unit.

Key examples:

| Prism (A, I) | Type | Role |
|---|---|---|
| (W(k), (p)), k perfect of char p | crystalline | recovers crystalline cohomology |
| (W(k)[[u]], (E(u))), E Eisenstein | Breuil–Kisin | recovers de Rham cohomology |
| (A_inf, ker θ) | perfect | recovers étale / A_inf-cohomology |
| (Z_p[[q-1]], ([p]_q)) | q-crystalline | recovers q-de Rham cohomology |

Given a bounded prism (A, I) and a p-adic formal scheme X over A/I, the **prismatic site** (X/A)_Δ has as objects prisms (B, IB) over (A, I) equipped with a map Spf(B/IB) → X, with the faithfully flat topology. **Prismatic cohomology** is

```
RΓ_Δ(X/A) := RΓ((X/A)_Δ, O_Δ)
```

the cohomology of the structure sheaf on this site — a perfect complex of A-modules with a φ-linear Frobenius [2]. The miracle is the suite of *comparison isomorphisms*:

- **Hodge–Tate:** RΓ_Δ/p ≃ ⊕_j H^j(X, Ω^j_{X/(A/I)}){-j}[-j], identifying prismatic cohomology mod I with differential forms;
- **Crystalline:** over the crystalline prism, RΓ_Δ ≃ RΓ_crys;
- **de Rham:** over the Breuil–Kisin prism, base change along S → **O**_K gives RΓ_dR;
- **Étale:** RΓ_Δ[1/μ]^∧ ≃ RΓ_ét(X, **Z**_p) ⊗ A_inf, recovering ℓ-adic (here p-adic) étale cohomology after inverting μ = [ε] − 1;
- **q-de Rham:** over the q-crystalline prism, RΓ_Δ recovers Scholze's q-de Rham complex.

Thus prismatic cohomology is the *universal* integral p-adic cohomology theory: every classical theory is a specialization [2, 3].

### 4.4 The Nygaard Filtration and Syntomic Cohomology

The comparisons above are rational shadows; integrally, prismatic cohomology carries the **Nygaard filtration**, a refinement of the Hodge filtration defined for quasiregular semiperfectoid rings and extended by descent [2]. Its graded pieces compute the cotangent complex, and the Nygaard-completed prismatic cohomology carries a Frobenius whose fixed points yield **syntomic cohomology** **Z**_p(n) — the p-adic analogue of motivic cohomology, with Z_p(n) identified with p-adic nearby cycles in mixed characteristic [4]. The odd vanishing conjecture for these twists, a K-theoretic statement, was settled via prismatic methods [2].

For the weight–monodromy story, the decisive construction is the cohomological theory of *Breuil–Kisin modules*: for a proper smooth formal scheme **X** over **O**_K, the prismatic cohomology over the Breuil–Kisin prism is a Breuil–Kisin module whose specializations give crystalline, de Rham, and étale cohomology integrally [3, 4]. This integrality is what lets the tilting argument be promoted from **Q**_ℓ-coefficients to the p-adic world: Hyodo–Kato cohomology H^i_{HK} carries a (φ, N)-module structure with Nφ = pφN, and the **p-adic weight–monodromy conjecture** predicts it is *quasipure* of weight i — i.e., gr^M_j is pure of weight i + j as a φ-module. Adapting Scholze's tilting proof to this setting, the conjecture is now known for complete intersections in toric varieties over **C**_p [5], via the "sharp" tilted Hyodo–Kato cohomology and rigid motives.
## 5 Empirical and Theoretical Results

The perfectoid–prismatic program has produced a remarkable record of *theorems*, not merely formalisms. We summarize the confirmed results relevant to the weight–monodromy thread.

**Weight–monodromy status by case.** The conjecture is now known in the following cases (all via the tilting reduction or its p-adic adaptation):

1. Equal characteristic local fields — Deligne [6], extended by Ito to the general case;
2. Semistable reduction in mixed characteristic — Rapoport–Zink, via the weight spectral sequence;
3. Dimension ≤ 2 (hence H^i for i ≤ 2 by Lefschetz) — de Jong's alterations plus the Hodge index theorem;
4. Set-theoretic complete intersections in projective smooth toric varieties over p-adic fields, ℓ-adic case — Scholze [1];
5. Same class, p-adic (Hyodo–Kato) case — Vezzani [5], adapting Scholze's argument through tilted Hyodo–Kato cohomology.

**The prismatic comparison web.** The following table records which prism recovers which classical theory [2]:

| Base prism (A, I) | Specialization of RΓ_Δ | Classical theory recovered |
|---|---|---|
| (W(k), (p)) | mod I = (p) | Crystalline cohomology |
| (W(k)[[u]], (E(u))) | S → **O**_K, u ↦ π | de Rham cohomology |
| (A_inf, ker θ) | invert μ = [ε] − 1 | p-adic étale cohomology |
| (**Z**_p[[q−1]], ([p]_q)) | q-crystalline | q-de Rham cohomology |
| any prism | mod I, Hodge–Tate | ⊕ H^j(Ω^j)[−j] |

**Computational illustration.** The purity condition at the heart of the conjecture is numerically testable in toy models. The following Python snippet verifies the Weil-number condition for Frobenius eigenvalues on a graded piece — the exact numerical content of "pure of weight w":

```python
import cmath

def is_weil_number(alpha: complex, q: int, w: int, tol: float = 1e-9) -> bool:
    """Check |alpha| == q**(w/2) for all complex embeddings (toy model)."""
    return abs(abs(alpha) - q ** (w / 2)) < tol

def check_grading(eigenvalues: dict[int, list[complex]], i: int, q: int) -> bool:
    """eigenvalues[j] = Frob eigenvalues on gr^M_j H^i; conjecture: weight i+j."""
    return all(
        is_weil_number(a, q, i + j)
        for j, evs in eigenvalues.items()
        for a in evs
    )

# Example: H^2 of a surface, monodromy filtration with j in {-1, 0, 1}, q = 5
toy = {-1: [5**0.5], 0: [5**1.0, -5**1.0], 1: [5**1.5]}
print(check_grading(toy, i=2, q=5))  # True: weights 1, 2, 3 = i + j
```

**Formalizability.** The definitions are sufficiently precise to have been formalized: Buzzard, Commelin, and Massot formalized perfectoid fields and the tilting correspondence in the Lean proof assistant [7], a striking *empirical* check on the coherence of the theory.

## 6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating what the revolution has *not* achieved:

- **The general weight–monodromy conjecture remains open.** Scholze's proof requires a toric ambient variety to produce perfectoid covers and a complete-intersection hypothesis to control the cohomology; neither is available for an arbitrary proper smooth variety over a p-adic field. No prismatic argument has yet removed these hypotheses.
- **Prismatic cohomology does not directly prove weight–monodromy.** The prismatic framework unifies cohomology theories integrally, but the purity argument still runs through tilting plus Weil II in equal characteristic. The prism is a *deperfection* of the perfectoid ring, and the weight–monodromy application leans on the *perfect* (tilting) side, not the prismatic site itself.
- **The p-adic case is fragmentary.** The p-adic weight–monodromy conjecture [5] is known only in the same toric complete-intersection class, and the argument passes through rigid motives and tilted Hyodo–Kato cohomology — machinery far heavier than the ℓ-adic original.
- **Effectivity and computability.** Prismatic cohomology is defined by a site with enormous, non-Noetherian objects; explicit computations exist only for quasiregular semiperfectoid rings and a handful of examples. There is no general algorithm computing RΓ_Δ for an arbitrary smooth formal scheme.
- **Singular and non-proper cases.** The theory is sharpest for smooth (formal) schemes; extending the comparison theorems and the weight–monodromy applications to singular varieties or to open varieties requires logarithmic prismatic cohomology, which is still under active development.

## 7 Conclusion

Scholze's perfectoid spaces did something no one had thought possible: they made the characteristic of the base field a *mutable* parameter of the geometry, so that an ℓ-adic Galois representation over **Q**_p can be studied through its tilt over **F**_p((t)), where Deligne's Weil II [6] reigns. The weight–monodromy conjecture — open in mixed characteristic for half a century — fell in the toric complete-intersection case to exactly this maneuver [1]. Bhatt and Scholze's prisms [2] then identified the integral algebraic structure underlying the maneuver: a δ-ring with a distinguished Cartier divisor, whose cohomology specializes to every known p-adic cohomology theory and whose Nygaard filtration yields Breuil–Kisin modules, syntomic cohomology, and the p-adic nearby-cycles theorems of [3, 4].

The arc is therefore complete as a *framework* but incomplete as a *conquest*: the general mixed-characteristic weight–monodromy conjecture, ℓ-adic and p-adic alike, still stands. If history is a guide, its fall will come from the same source — a new geometric bridge, perhaps logarithmic-prismatic, that makes the currently inaccessible cases as transparent as the tilted ones. The prismatic revolution taught us that cohomology theories are specializations of one universal object; the remaining task is to make purity equally universal.
