# Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution: the Entropy Accumulation Theorem, Self-Testing, and Composable Security Proofs

## Abstract

Device-independent quantum key distribution (DIQKD) offers the strongest known form of quantum cryptography: secret keys whose security is certified solely by the observed violation of a Bell inequality, with no assumptions about the internal workings of the quantum devices. This article develops the three mathematical pillars of modern DIQKD security proofs. We present the Entropy Accumulation Theorem (EAT) and its generalized form, which reduce the analysis of non-i.i.d. sequential protocols to an i.i.d. optimization over single-round entropies via min-tradeoff functions. We survey self-testing — the device-independent certification of entangled states and measurements from Bell statistics — and show how rigidity statements convert observed correlations into lower bounds on the von Neumann entropy of raw key registers conditioned on the adversary. Finally, we embed these tools in the Abstract Cryptography framework to obtain composable security: a precise real-versus-ideal definition under which error correction, privacy amplification, and key reuse compose modularly. A full finite-key proof sketch and the 2022 trapped-ion loophole-free demonstration ground the theory in experiment.

---

## 1 Intro

Quantum key distribution promises information-theoretic secrecy rooted in the laws of physics rather than computational hardness [1]. The canonical protocols prove secure against an adversary limited only by quantum mechanics, *provided the devices behave as specified* [10] — a proviso history has treated unkindly. A series of laboratory attacks — photon-number splitting, time-shift attacks, detector blinding, and calibration exploits — showed that the gap between a protocol's idealized model and its physical implementation is itself a viable attack surface. The security proofs were correct; the devices were not the devices in the proof.

Ekert's original 1991 insight offered a radical alternative: base security on the violation of a Bell inequality rather than on any characterization of the apparatus [10]. If Alice's and Bob's measurement statistics violate the CHSH inequality, then — under quantum theory — their outcomes *must* contain private randomness, regardless of how the devices were built. The proofs that followed tolerated only tiny noise and demanded astronomical signal counts, placing experiment firmly out of reach.

The modern era began with the Entropy Accumulation Theorem of Dupuis, Fawzi, and Renner [1] and its application to device-independent cryptography by Arnon-Friedman, Renner, and Vidick [2]. The EAT made it possible to analyze sequential protocols against fully general, non-i.i.d. adversaries with tight finite-key rates, reducing the hardest part of the proof to a *single-round* entropy optimization. This article explains how the EAT, self-testing, and composable security definitions fit together into a complete, modular proof of DIQKD security — and where the framework still strains.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Bell nonlocality and the CHSH game

In the CHSH game, inputs $x, y$ and outputs $a, b$ are binary, and the parties win when $a \oplus b = x \land y$. Any strategy consistent with local hidden variables — a *local* correlation — wins with probability at most $3/4$, equivalently a CHSH value $S \le 2$, where

$$
S = \langle A_0 B_0 \rangle + \langle A_0 B_1 \rangle + \langle A_1 B_0 \rangle - \langle A_1 B_1 \rangle.
$$

Quantum mechanics permits $S = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828$, the Tsirelson bound, attained by a maximally entangled singlet with appropriate measurements. A correlation with $S > 2$ is *nonlocal*: it certifies entanglement and *unpredictability*.

### 2.2 The device-independent model

In DIQKD the measurement devices are black boxes: classical inputs in, classical outputs out. The assumptions are only that quantum mechanics is correct, that the devices do not leak the inputs to Eve, and that Alice's and Bob's boxes do not signal to each other within a round — the *no-signalling* condition, enforced by separation or shielding. Everything else, including the Hilbert-space dimension, the prepared state, and the measurements, is untrusted and may be under Eve's control.

### 2.3 Entropies and extraction

The adversary's knowledge is quantified by the *smooth min-entropy* $H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(A_1^n \mid E)_\rho$, the number of secret bits extractable from Alice's raw string $A_1^n$ given Eve's side information $E$. The bridge from entropy to key is the quantum leftover hash lemma: hashing a string with $H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(A \mid E) \ge k$ yields a key $2^{-(k-\ell)/2}$-close to uniform and independent of $E$ [7]. The entire security enterprise is therefore an entropy-estimation problem: lower-bound $H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}$ from observed Bell statistics.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $S$ | Observed CHSH value, at most $2\sqrt{2}$ quantum-mechanically |
| $H(A \mid E)$ | Von Neumann entropy of Alice's output conditioned on Eve |
| $H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(A_1^n \mid E)$ | Smooth min-entropy, the extractable randomness |
| $\gamma$ | Fraction of test rounds (spot-checking parameter) |
| $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sec}}$ | Total security parameter of the final key |
| $f$ | Min-tradeoff function, entropy bound from single-round statistics |

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is a three-stage reduction: **(i)** formulate a *composable* security definition for DIQKD in the Abstract Cryptography framework [8], so a once-proven protocol yields a resource pluggable into larger protocols [7]; **(ii)** derive single-round entropy bounds from Bell violations using *self-testing*, so observed statistics certify secrecy of the quantum state itself [4]; and **(iii)** lift these bounds to the full $n$-round protocol against *general* adversaries with the (generalized) Entropy Accumulation Theorem [1][3].

Stage (i) fixes the target: a protocol is $\varepsilon$-secure if its real execution is $\varepsilon$-indistinguishable from an ideal key-distribution resource for any distinguisher, with $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{correct}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{sec}}$. Stage (ii) supplies the *min-tradeoff function* $f$: an affine function of the observed Bell statistics lower-bounding the single-round conditional von Neumann entropy, $f(p) \le \inf_{\sigma \in \Sigma(p)} H(A \mid E)_\sigma$, the infimum running over all quantum strategies compatible with statistics $p$. Constructing $f$ is the analytical heart of the work — it is where self-testing rigidity, Jordan's lemma, and semidefinite programming enter. Stage (iii) is the EAT, which assembles $n$ rounds of $f$ into $H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(A_1^n \mid E) \ge n f_{\min} - O(\sqrt{n})$ even when Eve correlates attacks arbitrarily across rounds. The methodology is deliberately modular: improving any one stage tightens the final key rate without re-proving the rest.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Entropy Accumulation Theorem

Consider a sequential process of $n$ rounds. In round $i$, a channel $\mathcal{M}_i$ maps an internal register $R_{i-1}$ to Alice's output $A_i$, a classical statistics register $X_i$, and a new internal register $R_i$, while Eve holds correlated side information. The EAT applies whenever each channel satisfies a **Markov condition**: conditioned on the history, the statistics $X_i$ are independent of Eve's future given the past — Eve cannot update her side information about round $i$'s outputs from later rounds.

> **Theorem (Entropy Accumulation [1]).** Let $\mathcal{M}_1, \dots, \mathcal{M}_n$ be EAT channels with a min-tradeoff function $f$, and let $\Omega$ be the event that the observed statistics pass the protocol's acceptance test. Then either $\Pr[\Omega] < 1 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{EA}}$, or
>
> $$
> H_{\min}^{\varepsilon_s}(A_1^n \mid B_1^n E)_{\rho_{|\Omega}} \ge n\, t - \nu \sqrt{n},
> $$
>
> where $t \le \min_{p \in \Omega} f(p)$ is the worst-case entropy per round consistent with passing statistics, and $\nu$ depends on the gradient of $f$, the alphabet sizes, and the smoothing parameters. The correction scales only as $\sqrt{n}$.

The power of this statement is its *reduction to the i.i.d. case*: bounding the entropy of an arbitrarily correlated $n$-round execution reduces to bounding the *single-round* von Neumann entropy for *every* strategy compatible with the passing statistics — the infimum defining $f$. In practice $f$ is built numerically: parametrize the CHSH violation $s$, solve an SDP over the NPA hierarchy minimizing $H(A \mid E)$ subject to $S = s$, and take the affine envelope:

```python
import numpy as np

def min_tradeoff_chsh(s_values, sdp_solver):
    """Affine min-tradeoff f(s) = alpha*s + beta for CHSH DIQKD."""
    entropies = [sdp_solver.min_conditional_entropy(s) for s in s_values]
    alpha, beta = np.polyfit(s_values, entropies, deg=1)
    assert all(alpha*s + beta <= e + 1e-9 for s, e in zip(s_values, entropies))
    return alpha, beta
```

The generalized EAT of Metger and Renner [3] relaxes the Markov condition further, allowing the side information to be updated by the channels themselves — essential for device-independent randomness expansion and protocols where the adversary's memory evolves with the devices. Both versions share the same operational moral: *sequential protocols accumulate entropy round by round*, with only a square-root penalty for adversarial memory.

### 4.2 Self-Testing: From Statistics to Rigidity

Self-testing is the strongest form of device-independent certification [4]. A correlation *self-tests* a reference state and measurements if observing it implies — up to local unitaries and an irrelevant "junk" system — that the devices prepared precisely that state and performed precisely those measurements [5]. The archetypal example:

> **Lemma (CHSH self-test).** If a bipartite correlation attains the Tsirelson bound $S = 2\sqrt{2}$, then there exist local isometries $\Phi_A, \Phi_B$ and a state $\lvert \mathrm{junk} \rangle$ such that $(\Phi_A \otimes \Phi_B)\lvert \psi \rangle = \lvert \Phi^+ \rangle \otimes \lvert \mathrm{junk} \rangle$ and the observables act as the reference Pauli measurements tensored with identity on the junk system.

DIQKD does not need exact self-testing; it needs *robust* self-testing, where $S = 2\sqrt{2} - \delta$ certifies closeness $O(\sqrt{\delta})$ to the ideal state [4]. The proof machinery is elegant: Jordan's lemma block-diagonalizes Alice's two binary observables into qubit blocks, reducing the infinite-dimensional problem to a two-qubit analysis per block, and $H(A \mid E)$ emerges as a convex function of the Bell violation — which is why the SDP-based min-tradeoff construction works at all.

A striking recent line of work shows the relationship between nonlocality and key is subtler than self-testing intuition suggests: Wooltorton *et al.* and Farkas exhibited correlations *arbitrarily close to the local set* that nevertheless yield constant key rates [9]. Conversely, Bell nonlocality alone is not sufficient for standard DIQKD — the *structure* of the correlation, which self-testing rigidity makes precise, matters. The current frontier quantifies exactly how much rigidity a given Bell inequality family provides for key extraction, with numerical SDP relaxations of the minimal min-entropy consistent with a violation [9].

### 4.3 Composable Security in the Abstract Cryptography Framework

A security proof is only as useful as its definition. The Abstract Cryptography (AC) framework of Maurer and Renner [8] defines security as *construction of resources*: a protocol $\pi$ constructs an ideal resource $\mathcal{S}$ from real resources $\mathcal{R}$ if, for every adversary, there exists a *simulator* $\sigma$ such that the real world $\pi\mathcal{R}$ is indistinguishable from the ideal world $\sigma\mathcal{S}$. For QKD the ideal resource outputs either a shared, uniformly random, Eve-independent key — or an abort flag; the simulator generates Eve's view from the ideal key alone.

Portmann and Renner instantiate this for quantum cryptography [7], showing that the familiar correctness-plus-secrecy definition — the final key $\varepsilon$-close in trace distance to a perfect key — is equivalent to the AC construction statement for QKD. The payoff is *composability*: if $\pi_1$ constructs $\mathcal{S}_1$ from $\mathcal{R}$ with error $\varepsilon_1$ and $\pi_2$ constructs $\mathcal{S}_2$ from $\mathcal{S}_1$ with error $\varepsilon_2$, the composition constructs $\mathcal{S}_2$ from $\mathcal{R}$ with error $\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$, by one composition theorem instead of ad hoc game-hopping. In the DIQKD proof, this separates concerns cleanly:

```haskell
-- Ideal key resource: outputs (K,K) to Alice/Bob, nothing to Eve, or Abort
data IdealKey = SharedKey BitString | Abort
-- Real protocol: entanglement distribution + Bell test + sifting + EC + PA
diqkd :: (QuantumChannel, AuthChannel) -> Protocol IdealKey
diqkd = privacyAmplify . errorCorrect . sift . estimate . distribute
-- Security: real execution is eps-indistinguishable from (simulator <> idealKey)
theorem_security :: Distinguisher -> Double -> Bool
theorem_security d eps = distinguishingAdvantage d realWorld idealWorld <= eps
```

Concretely, the DIQKD security statement proven in [2] reads: the protocol either aborts with probability at least $1 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{EA}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}}$, or it is $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}} + \varepsilon_s)$-correct-and-secret while producing keys of length $\ell$ — with $\varepsilon$-terms for entropy accumulation, error correction, privacy amplification, and smoothing, all adding by the composition theorem. The definition also handles the abort event honestly: conditioned on non-abort, the key is secret, which is exactly the guarantee a higher-level protocol such as one-time-pad encryption needs to compose safely.

### 4.4 The DIQKD Protocol Pipeline

A complete EAT-based DIQKD protocol proceeds in five stages:

1. **Entanglement distribution.** In each of $n$ rounds, an untrusted source — possibly Eve — distributes a bipartite state to Alice and Bob.
2. **Spot-checking.** With probability $\gamma$ (test round) they choose random inputs and record Bell statistics; with probability $1-\gamma$ (generation round) they use fixed key-generation inputs.
3. **Parameter estimation.** Test-round inputs and outputs are announced over the authenticated channel, the CHSH value is computed, and the protocol aborts unless it exceeds a threshold $S_{\min}$.
4. **Error correction.** Alice sends Bob a syndrome leaking at most $\mathrm{leak}_{\mathrm{EC}} \approx n\, h(Q)$ bits for quantum bit-error rate $Q$; the leakage is subtracted from the key.
5. **Privacy amplification.** A universal hash compresses the reconciled string to length
   $$
   \ell = n\, t - \nu\sqrt{n} - \mathrm{leak}_{\mathrm{EC}} - O(\log 1/\varepsilon),
   $$
   where $t$ is the EAT entropy rate from the min-tradeoff function evaluated at the observed statistics.

```rust
/// Spot-checking DIQKD pipeline, honest-device model (illustrative).
struct Round { test: bool, a: u8, chsh: f64 }

fn simulate(n: usize, gamma: f64, noise: f64, s_min: f64) -> Option<Vec<u8>> {
    let mut chsh_num = 0.0; let mut chsh_den = 0usize;
    let mut key: Vec<u8> = Vec::new();
    let mut rng = rand::thread_rng();
    for _ in 0..n {
        let r = honest_chsh_pair(noise, &mut rng);
        if rng.gen_bool(gamma) { chsh_num += r.chsh; chsh_den += 1; }
        else { key.push(r.a); }
    }
    if chsh_num / chsh_den as f64 >= s_min { Some(key) } else { None }
}
```

The asymptotic key rate is positive whenever the certified entropy $H(A \mid E)$ exceeds the error-correction cost $h(Q)$; for CHSH with one-way post-processing this tolerates roughly 7 percent QBER [2][6].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: the full finite-key chain

We now assemble the three pillars into the security statement of [2] for the protocol of Section 4.4.

> **Lemma (Single-round bound).** Let $f$ be an affine min-tradeoff function with $f(p) \le \inf H(A \mid E)_\sigma$ over all quantum strategies $\sigma$ yielding statistics $p$. Then each round's EAT channel satisfies the entropy condition with tradeoff $f$, provided Alice's and Bob's devices do not signal to each other and Eve's side information is Markov with respect to the test statistics.

*Proof sketch.* Jordan's lemma block-diagonalizes the binary observables; within each qubit block, the conditional von Neumann entropy is bounded below by a convex function of the CHSH value via the NPA SDP hierarchy, and the affine envelope gives $f$. No-signalling between devices guarantees the Markov condition; secret test inputs prevent Eve from adapting per-round behavior to the test/generation distinction. ∎

> **Lemma (Accumulation).** Conditioned on passing the acceptance test $\Omega$, the smooth min-entropy satisfies $H_{\min}^{\varepsilon_s}(A_1^n \mid B_1^n E) \ge n\, t - \nu\sqrt{n}$, with $t = \min_{p \in \Omega} f(p)$.

*Proof sketch.* Direct application of the EAT [1] to the $n$ sequential channels, with the second-order term $\nu\sqrt{n}$ controlled by the gradient of $f$ and the alphabet size. ∎

> **Theorem (DIQKD security [2]).** For any adversarial device behavior, either the protocol aborts with probability greater than $1 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{EA}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}}$, or it is $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}} + \varepsilon_s)$-correct-and-secret while producing keys of length $\ell$ given by the formula of Section 4.4.

*Proof sketch.* Lemma 2 lower-bounds the entropy available for privacy amplification. Error correction leaks at most $\mathrm{leak}_{\mathrm{EC}}$ bits and fails with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EC}}$; the leftover hash lemma extracts $\ell$ secret bits with error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PA}} + \varepsilon_s$. Correctness and secrecy compose by the AC composition theorem [7][8]. ∎

A TLA+ sketch of the protocol's control flow, precise enough for model checking:

```tla
---- MODULE DIQKD ----
EXTENDS Naturals
CONSTANT N, SMin
VARIABLES round, chshSum, chshCount, aborted, keyBits
Init == round = 0 /\ chshSum = 0 /\ chshCount = 0
        /\ aborted = FALSE /\ keyBits = 0
TestRound == /\ round < N /\ round' = round + 1
             /\ chshCount' = chshCount + 1 /\ chshSum' \in Nat
             /\ UNCHANGED <<aborted, keyBits>>
GenRound  == /\ round < N /\ round' = round + 1
             /\ keyBits' = keyBits + 1
             /\ UNCHANGED <<chshSum, chshCount, aborted>>
Decide    == /\ round = N
             /\ aborted' = (chshSum < SMin * chshCount)
             /\ UNCHANGED <<round, chshSum, chshCount, keyBits>>
Next == TestRound \/ GenRound \/ Decide
====
```

### 5.2 Experimental milestones

Theory outran experiment for years: closing the detection and locality loopholes simultaneously while generating key at usable rates is brutally demanding. The breakthrough came in 2022 with Nadlinger *et al.*, who ran a complete DIQKD protocol between two trapped-ion qubits linked by optical fiber, using event-ready entanglement to close the detection loophole and space-like separation to address locality [6] — the first demonstration that DIQKD is not merely a theorist's fantasy.

Complementary photonic approaches trade the near-unit detection efficiency of ions for higher clock rates, using heralded entanglement to defeat channel loss; long-distance proposals based on single-photon path entanglement push the key rate to scale with the square root of the channel transmittance rather than linearly [3]. Numerical studies show CHSH-based DIQKD tolerating depolarizing noise up to roughly 7 percent QBER asymptotically, with the EAT's $\sqrt{n}$ penalty requiring on the order of $10^6$–$10^8$ rounds for positive finite-key rates at moderate noise [2].

| Platform | Loopholes closed | Key outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Trapped ions, event-ready (2022) [6] | Detection + locality | First complete DIQKD, finite-key secure |
| Heralded photonics (proposals) | Detection via heralding | Higher rates, distance scaling as sqrt of transmittance |
| No-signalling-only proofs [10][11] | Theory | Security from no-signalling alone, lower rates |

## 6 Limitations

1. **The no-signalling assumption between devices.** Alice's and Bob's boxes must not communicate within a round — enforced by shielding or space-like separation — and the measurement inputs must be free random choices unknown to the devices. A covert channel between the boxes, or a device leaking its input to Eve, breaks the proof entirely.
2. **Loss and the detection loophole.** Without heralding or event-ready schemes, Eve can exploit the detection loophole by selectively reporting outcomes. Fully photonic DIQKD over metropolitan distances remains an open engineering challenge.
3. **Finite statistics.** The EAT's second-order $\nu\sqrt{n}$ term is honest but heavy: at realistic noise, positive key requires millions of rounds, and the min-tradeoff gradient inflates $\nu$ when the acceptance threshold sits near the local bound. Tighter tradeoff functions [3] and advantage distillation help but add complexity.
4. **Composability subtleties.** The abort event is public and the security statement is conditional on non-abort; protocols reusing devices across sessions must ensure the abort flag does not leak key material through device memory in later runs — the standard memory-attack caveat of device-independent cryptography.
5. **Nonlocality is not sufficient.** As [9] shows, the quantitative link between Bell violation and key rate is delicate: some barely-nonlocal correlations yield constant key rates, others none. Protocol design must target the *right* Bell inequalities, not merely large violations.

## 7 Conclusion

Device-independent QKD has matured from a philosophical curiosity into a rigorous engineering discipline. The Entropy Accumulation Theorem turns sequential, adversarially-correlated executions into tight finite-key bounds [1][2][3]. Self-testing converts raw Bell statistics into certified quantum entropy, with Jordan's lemma and SDP hierarchies making the single-round optimization tractable [4][5]. And the Abstract Cryptography framework gives these results a composable meaning, so a DIQKD key can be consumed by larger protocols without re-examining the proof [7][8]. The 2022 trapped-ion demonstration proved the full stack works in the laboratory [6]. The remaining gaps — loss-tolerant photonics, tighter finite-key scaling, memory-attack-resistant device reuse — are quantitative rather than conceptual: the devices may be built by the adversary, but the Bell violation is not hers to fake.
