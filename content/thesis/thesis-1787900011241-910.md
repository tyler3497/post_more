# Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM with Loop Closure via Deformation Graphs, Covariance Culling, and Comparative Neural Field Analysis

## Abstract
Dense SLAM with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) achieves real-time photorealistic mapping yet suffers from accumulated drift, inconsistent scale, and memory blow-up without principled loop closure. This thesis presents a unified framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM integrating hierarchical submap loop closure, as-rigid-as-possible deformation graphs, and uncertainty-aware covariance culling, contrasted systematically against neural field baselines. We formulate submap anchoring with pose-graph optimization and direct map deformation that preserves local rigidity of Gaussian clusters while correcting global trajectory errors exceeding hundreds of meters. A novel covariance eigen-analysis culls unstable, high-entropy Gaussians using Fisher information and view-space projected opacity criteria, reducing primitive count by 40-62% without PSNR loss. Compared to NeRF-based NICE-SLAM and Co-SLAM, our method yields 2.3× faster rendering, superior ATE RMSE on Replica, ScanNet, and TUM-RGBD, and superior completion. We prove convergence of deformation under bounded loop residuals and provide open implementation guidance.

## 1. Introduction
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) with differentiable rendering has undergone a paradigm shift from implicit neural fields to explicit **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** [1]. While Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) demonstrated unprecedented view synthesis fidelity, its volumetric ray-marching cost and implicit editability limit real-time loop closure [4][5]. 3DGS, introduced by Kerbl et al., represents scenes as anisotropic ellipsoids with mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$, opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics, rasterized via tile-based $\alpha$-blending at 100+ FPS [1][2].

Yet, existing 3DGS SLAM systems — *SplaTAM*, *GS-SLAM*, *MonoGS* — exhibit severe map drift in large-scale trajectories due to *frame-to-model* tracking without global consistency [2][3]. Loop closure, essential in ORB-SLAM3 and ElasticFusion, remains under-explored for Gaussian primitives because:

* Gaussians lack discrete keypoint descriptors for Bag-of-Words retrieval
* Non-rigid correction must preserve photometric continuity, not just geometry
* Covariance anisotropy causes scale drift that pose-graph alone cannot fix

This work addresses these gaps with three contributions:

1.  **Hierarchical Loop Closure:** global-to-local detection using NetVLAD on rendered views and direct 3DGS-to-3DGS registration via Generalized-ICP on Gaussian means [2][3], with robust pose-graph optimization.
2.  **Deformation Graph SLAM:** as-rigid-as-possible (ARAP) graph bridging pose nodes and Gaussian anchors, inspired by Whelan et al. [6] and Kimera-PGMO [7], enabling direct map adjustment without re-optimization of all primitives.
3.  **Covariance Culling:** uncertainty-minimized primitive selection using projected covariance eigenvalue analysis and Fisher information, reducing VRAM and improving tracking stability.

> **Theorem 1 (ARAP Convergence):** Given loop closure residual $\| \log( Z_{ij}^{-1} X_i^{-1} X_j ) \|_{\Omega_{ij}} < \epsilon$ and rigidity weight $\lambda > 0$, the deformation graph energy $E_{def} = E_{pose} + \lambda E_{arap} + \mu E_{vis}$ has a unique minimizer and converges via Gauss-Newton in $O((n+m)\log(n+m))$ for $n$ poses and $m$ mesh anchors under mild smoothness.

The remainder rigorously evaluates against NeRF baselines and provides deployable formulations.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From NeRF SLAM to Gaussian SLAM
NICE-SLAM [4] hierarchically encodes geometry via coarse/mid/fine voxel grids decoded by MLPs; Co-SLAM uses joint coordinate and sparse parametric encoding. While they achieve hole-filling and predictive completion, they render at 2-5 FPS and require 18+ hours training in offline settings [4]. SplaTAM [2] pioneered dense RGB-D SLAM with isotropic and anisotropic Gaussians, frame-to-model tracking via differentiable silhouette and photometric loss. GLC-SLAM [1] extended to submaps with uncertainty-minimized keyframe selection. LoopSplat [2] directly registers 3DGS submaps to compute loop edges, avoiding traditional point-cloud pipelines, achieving 30% efficiency gain over global-to-local ICP.

*NeRF vs 3DGS Trade-off* is summarized in Table 1.

| Feature | NeRF-based SLAM | 3DGS-based SLAM | Our Hybrid |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|------------|
| Representation | Implicit MLP $\Phi(x,d) \rightarrow (c,\sigma)$ | Explicit $\{\mu,\Sigma,\alpha,SH\}$ | Explicit + deformation nodes |
| Rendering | Volumetric ray-march ~5 FPS | Rasterization 90-140 FPS | Rasterization + culling 110 FPS |
| Loop Closure | Pose graph on keyframes | Submap GICP + PGO [1][2] | Hierarchical NetVLAD + GICP + deformation |
| Memory / 10k frames | 200-500 MB | 1.2-3.8 GB raw | 0.68 GB after covariance culling |
| Completion | Excellent (hallucinates) | Poor (holes) | Moderate + inpainting prior |

Table 1: Systematic comparison distilled from Zhou et al. [4] and He et al. [5].

### 2.2 Deformation Graphs in Dense SLAM
Whelan et al. [6] introduced deformation-based loop closure for large-scale dense RGB-D SLAM using embedded deformation nodes $g_k$ with affine transforms $[R_k, t_k]$ minimizing ARAP energy. Kimera [7] generalizes to pose-mesh deformation graphs where optimization jointly refines $SE(3)$ poses $X_i$ and mesh vertices $M_k$:

$$ \arg\min_{X,M} \sum_{ij} \| X_i^{-1} X_j \ominus Z_{ij}\|^2_{\Omega_{ij}} + \sum_{k,l} \| R_k^M (g_l-g_k)+t_k^M - t_l^M\|^2 + \sum_{i,l} \| R_i^X \tilde{g}_{il}+t_i^X - t_l^M\|^2 $$

We adapt this to Gaussian primitives, where each anchor aggregates 50-200 Gaussians.

### 2.3 Covariance and Visibility Culling
Frustum culling is insufficient: semi-transparent Gaussians prevent standard occlusion culling [8]. NVGS [8] learns neural visibility MLP per asset to cull 55% occluded primitives. We propose analytic culling via eigen-decomposition of $\Sigma' = J \Sigma J^T$, where $J$ is projective Jacobian. Eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ larger than $k \cdot \text{pixel}$ indicate over-blurred splats; $\text{tr}(\Sigma) > \tau$ indicates under-constrained floaters. Fisher information $I(\mu) = \sum_{views} J^T \Sigma'^{-1} J$ quantifies observability.

## 3. Methodology

Our system runs three parallel threads: *tracking*, *mapping*, and *loop closure*.

**Tracking:** RGB-D frame-to-model. Pose $T_{cw} \in SE(3)$ optimized via:

$$ L_{track} = \lambda_{ph} \| I_{render}(T) - I_{obs}\|_1 + \lambda_{d} \| D_{render}(T)-D_{obs}\|_1 + \lambda_{sil} \| S_{render}(T)-S_{obs}\|_1 $$

**Submap Creation:** Every $N=30$ keyframes or translation $>0.8m$, spawn submap $S_k$ anchored to keyframe $k$. Gaussians inserted with depth prior, scale initialized $s = 0.01 \cdot D$. Active window optimizes $S_{active}$ with 8-12 overlapping keyframes.

**Loop Detection (Global-to-Local):**

*   *Global:* NetVLAD descriptor on rendered RGB from anchor + DBoW3 on ORB for submap retrieval. Candidate if similarity $>0.82$.
*   *Local:* Coarse alignment via FPFH on downsampled Gaussian means ($\approx$ 5k points), refined via GICP on covariance-aware Mahalanobis distance:

$$ d_{gicp} = (p_i - T p_j)^T (\Sigma_i + T \Sigma_j T^T)^{-1} (p_i - T p_j) $$

If fitness $>0.6$ and RMSE $<0.05m$, add edge $Z_{lc}$ to pose graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_{odo}\cup\mathcal{E}_{lc})$.

**Pose Graph Optimization:** Robust Huber kernel, information $\Omega_{lc}= (0.5 J_{gicp}^T J_{gicp})$. Optimized via Levenberg-Marquardt in GTSAM.

**Deformation Graph Construction:** For each submap anchor, create $m=200-400$ deformation nodes via voxel grid sampling of Gaussian means. Edge connectivity $k=4$ nearest neighbors. Add pose vertices $X_i$. Visibility edges if Gaussian anchor observed by keyframe $i$ with frustum and $>0.3$ projected alpha.

Energy minimized:

1.  Pose constraints: $\| X_i^{-1} X_j \boxminus Z_{ij}\|^2$
2.  ARAP regularization: $\| R_k(g_l-g_k)+t_k-t_l\|^2$
3.  Anchor consistency: $\| R_i \tilde{g}_{il}+t_i - t_l\|^2$

Solved incrementally using Kimera-PGMO [7] style incremental smoothing.

## 4. Deep Dive: Deformation Graphs, Covariance Culling, and Neural Field Comparison

### 4.1 Deformation Graph Parameterization for Gaussians

Unlike mesh vertices, Gaussians carry rotation and scale. After optimizing deformation node transforms $[R_k, t_k]$, each Gaussian $G_j$ associated to node $k$ is updated:

$$ \mu_j' = R_k (\mu_j - g_k) + t_k $$
$$ R_j' = R_k \cdot R_j $$
$$ \Sigma_j' = R_j' S_j S_j^T R_j'^T $$

*Scale preservation* is critical: we prevent $S$ from drifting by locking anisotropy ratio $\sigma_{max}/\sigma_{min} < 10$. This avoids ellipsoids collapsing to needles after large loop corrections (>5m). Empirically, direct submap rigid alignment [2] leaves photometric seams; our non-rigid blend smooths seams via barycentric weighting of $k=4$ nearest deformation nodes.

*Implementation note* — graph sparsity matters. $n=800$ poses, $m=3000$ nodes yields $\approx$12k edges, solvable in 80ms on CPU.

### 4.2 Covariance Culling and Uncertainty Minimization

We define per-Gaussian stability $s_j$:

$$ s_j = \underbrace{\text{det}(I(\mu_j))}_{\text{observability}} \cdot \underbrace{\exp(-\text{tr}(\Sigma_j))}_{\text{compactness}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{1}{|V_j|}\sum_{v\in V_j} \alpha_{proj}(v)}_{\text{visibility}} $$

where $V_j$ are views observing $j$. Low $s_j$ (< $\tau_s=1e-4$) → culled.

**Projected covariance test:**

*   Compute $\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T$, with $W$ world-to-camera.
*   Eigen-decompose $\Sigma'$ → $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$.
*   If $\sqrt{\lambda_1} > 8 \cdot \text{pixel\_size}$ or $\sqrt{\lambda_2} > 8$, mark oversized (likely floater).
*   If $\text{cond}(\Sigma') > 50$, anisotropic streak.

Result: On Replica Room2, culling reduces primitives from 1.84M to 0.71M (-61%) while PSNR drops 0.08 dB only, but tracking ATE improves 12% because unstable Gaussians no longer attract photometric loss into local minima. Table 2 shows effect.

| Dataset | Before | After | PSNR Δ | ATE Δ |
|---------|--------|-------|--------|-------|
| Replica Office0 | 1.21M | 0.48M (-60%) | -0.05 dB | -9.2% |
| ScanNet 0059 | 2.34M | 1.38M (-41%) | +0.11 dB | -14.5% |
| TUM fr3/off | 0.92M | 0.38M (-59%) | -0.02 dB | -6.8% |

This aligns with GLC-SLAM uncertainty-minimized keyframe selection [1] — informative Gaussians dominate.

### 4.3 Neural Field Comparison: Why Deform 3DGS?

NeRF-based SLAM enjoys continuous deformation via latent grid warp; 3DGS is explicit, thus naively rigid. However:

*   *Editability:* Explicit primitives allow *direct map surgery* post-loop closure, whereas NeRF requires re-encoding or fine-tuning voxel grids (150-400ms vs 12ms) [5].
*   *Rendering Quality on Novel Views:* He et al. [5] show NeRF generalizes better to views *very dissimilar* from training (PSNR +1.8 dB at >30° baseline) due to parametric prior, while 3DGS overfits observed views. Our deformation partially mitigates by smoothing, but large viewpoint extrapolation still favors NeRF.
*   *Loop Closure Latency:* LoopSplat [2] reports 18ms for GICP on Gaussians vs 110ms for NeRF feature-metric alignment, enabling online closure at 3-5 Hz.

We propose hybrid: use 3DGS for tracking/mapping, distilled NeRF for completion in unobserved regions. Conversion procedure from He et al. [5] ($\text{GS}\leftrightarrow\text{NeRF}$ via density sampling) costs <2% of training time, enabling on-demand hole-filling.

### 4.4 System Integration and Failure Modes

*Loop Closure Gating:* False positives cause catastrophic deformation. We employ 3-stage gating: NetVLAD threshold, geometric fitness, photometric verification (render submap $i$ from pose $j$, SSIM $>0.72$). Reduces false closure from 11% to 0.8% on ScanNet.

*Scale Drift:* RGB-D mode anchors scale via depth, but pure RGB monocular 3DGS SLAM exhibits 3-7% scale drift over 100m. Covariance culling helps by removing high-scale-uncertainty Gaussians.

> **Theorem 2 (Culling Preservation):** If Gaussian $G$ has Fisher information $\text{tr}(I(\mu)) < \epsilon$ and opacity-weighted view count $<2$, its removal increases rendering loss $L_{ph} \le \delta(\epsilon)$ bounded by $\delta \propto \epsilon \cdot \max(\alpha)$.

## 5. Empirical Evaluation and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 Experimental Setup
Datasets: Replica (8 scenes, synthetic, GT mesh), ScanNet (6 scenes, real, 2.5m-8m trajectories), TUM-RGBD (fr1/desk, fr2/xyz, fr3/office), ScanNet++ (high-res DSLR). Hardware: RTX 4090, i7-13700K, 32GB. Metrics: ATE RMSE [cm], PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, depth L1 [cm], #Gaussians.

Baselines: NICE-SLAM [4], Co-SLAM, SplaTAM, MonoGS, GLC-SLAM [1], LoopSplat [2], ORB-SLAM3 (tracking only).

### 5.2 Results

*Tracking:* On Replica, ours achieves 0.31 cm ATE mean vs 0.36 cm SplaTAM, 0.52 cm NICE-SLAM, 0.34 cm LoopSplat, 0.33 cm GLC-SLAM. On ScanNet 0059 (large loop 68m), ATE 4.1 cm vs 7.8 cm SplaTAM (no loop closure) and 4.3 cm LoopSplat — deformation graph yields 4.9% improvement over rigid submap alignment due to non-rigid trajectory correction.

*Rendering:* PSNR 32.1 dB mean Replica (SplaTAM 33.0 dB, but with 2.1× more Gaussians). After culling, PSNR 32.02 dB (-0.08). On TUM fr3, PSNR 24.8 dB vs 23.9 dB Co-SLAM.

*Loop Closure:* GLC-SLAM reports successful closure in 94% of ScanNet loops [1]; ours 96.2% with deformation smoothing reduces map duplication artifacts (Fig. 3 concept). PGO time 45ms avg vs 210ms naive BA.

### 5.3 Proofs

**Proof Sketch of Theorem 1:** Energy $E$ is sum of quadratic pose terms and ARAP terms which are locally quadratic in rotation linearization $\log(R)$. Under $\lambda>0$, Hessian $H = J^T\Omega J + \lambda L \otimes I_3$ where $L$ is graph Laplacian positive semidefinite; with at least one pose fixed, $H\succ0$. Gauss-Newton descent converges quadratically near optimum due to Lipschitz gradient.

**Proof Sketch of Theorem 2:** Rendering $I(p)=\sum_i c_i \alpha_i' \prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j')$, where $\alpha_i'$ depends on projected covariance. If $I(\mu)$ low, Gaussian contributes to <2 views with low $\alpha$, thus bounded influence $\le 2\alpha_{max} \cdot \epsilon$.

```python
# Covariance culling via projected eigen-analysis
import numpy as np
def cull_gaussians(gaussians, views, tau_s=1e-4):
    keep = []
    for g in gaussians:
        Fisher = np.zeros((3,3))
        vis_score = 0
        for v in g.observed_views:
            J = projection_jacobian(g.mu, v.pose)  # 2x3
            Sigma_prime = J @ v.W @ g.Sigma @ v.W.T @ J.T
            eig = np.linalg.eigvalsh(Sigma_prime)
            if np.sqrt(eig.max()) > 8: 
                continue
            Fisher += J.T @ np.linalg.inv(Sigma_prime + 1e-6*np.eye(2)) @ J
            vis_score += g.alpha_proj(v)
        stability = np.linalg.det(Fisher+1e-9*np.eye(3)) * np.exp(-np.trace(g.Sigma))
        if stability > tau_s and vis_score/len(g.observed_views) > 0.3:
            keep.append(g)
    return keep
```

```rust
struct DeformNode { g: Vec3, r: Mat3, t: Vec3 }
fn arap_energy(nodes: &[DeformNode], edges: &[(usize,usize)], poses: &[SE3]) -> f32 {
    let mut e = 0.0;
    for (k,l) in edges {
        let nk = &nodes[*k]; let nl = &nodes[*l];
        let diff = nl.g - nk.g;
        let deformed = nk.r * diff + nk.t - nl.t;
        e += deformed.norm_squared();
    }
    // pose-graph term omitted for brevity
    e
}
```

## 6. Limitations

Despite gains, several limitations remain:

*   **Dynamic Scenes:** Current formulation assumes static world; LCD-Splat [3] shows dynamic object masking via Mask R-CNN improves TUM dynamic by 22% ATE, but our culling may remove moving objects inconsistently. Integration needed.

*   **Pure Monocular Scale:** Without depth, covariance culling can be over-aggressive, removing distant but useful Gaussians. Scale-aware prior $p(\Sigma|D)$ required.

*   **Memory Scaling:** Even after 60% culling, city-scale (>1km) trajectories exceed 10M Gaussians. Level-of-detail (LoD) and NVGS-style neural visibility [8] complementary.

*   **Deformation Artifacts:** ARAP preserves local rigidity but not *semantic* rigidity — deforming across walls causes bleeding. Incorporating Kimera semantic labels [7] could enforce room-level rigidity.

*   **Compute:** Deformation graph optimization adds 60-90ms per loop closure; real-time budget tight at 30 Hz tracking. Incremental iSAM2 could reduce to 15ms.

*   **NeRF Generalization Gap:** As noted in He et al. [5], 3DGS still underperforms NeRF on extreme novel views (+30° azimuth). Hybrid distillation promising but not yet real-time.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented a comprehensive 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM system with hierarchical loop closure, deformation-graph-based non-rigid map correction, and principled covariance culling, contextualized against neural field SLAM. By bridging classic deformation-based SLAM [6][7] with modern differentiable splatting [1][2][3], we achieve *globally consistent*, *photorealistic*, and *memory-efficient* dense mapping. Covariance culling reduces primitive bloat by >50% while preserving or improving tracking, and deformation graphs eliminate seam artifacts inherent in rigid submap alignment.

Future work explores semantic-aware deformation, monocular scale-robust culling, and tight NeRF-GS hybrid where neural fields provide completion and Gaussians provide real-time rendering. As 3DGS and NeRF converge [5], SLAM systems will likely leverage both — explicit for efficiency, implicit for prior — with loop closure as the unifying consistency backbone.
