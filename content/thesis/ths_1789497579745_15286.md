# Protein Folding with AlphaFold2: Evoformer Attention, the Structure Module, and Confidence Metrics

## Abstract

AlphaFold2 recast protein structure prediction as an end-to-end differentiable geometry problem and, in doing so, solved the fifty-year grand challenge of computational biology to near-experimental accuracy. This article dissects the three pillars of its design: the **Evoformer**, a 48-block transformer that fuses evolutionary information from multiple sequence alignments with learned pairwise geometry through axial attention and triangle-inequality-aware updates; the **structure module**, an eight-block SE(3)-equivariant network whose invariant point attention operates directly on residue-local coordinate frames to emit backbone and side-chain geometry; and the **confidence machinery** — per-residue pLDDT, the predicted aligned error matrix, and predicted TM scores — that turns a black-box network into a calibrated instrument whose self-reported uncertainty correlates with genuine predictive accuracy. We derive the key operators mathematically, trace how losses such as the frame-aligned point error couple supervision to geometry, examine empirical evidence from CASP14 and the 200-million-structure AlphaFold database, and close with the method's principled limitations. No prior familiarity with structural bioinformatics is assumed beyond graduate-level machine learning.

##1 Introduction

For half a century, predicting the three-dimensional structure of a protein from its amino-acid sequence stood as the central unsolved problem of computational biology. The difficulty is not conceptual — Anfinsen's dogma tells us the sequence determines the fold — but computational: the conformational space of even a modest chain is astronomically large, and the energy landscape is rugged with kinetic traps. Classical approaches attacked the problem through physics-based simulation (molecular dynamics, fragment assembly) or through template-based homology modelling, each hitting a ceiling well below experimental accuracy [1].

The CASP14 blind-prediction experiment of 2020 changed the field overnight: DeepMind's AlphaFold2 achieved a median GDT_TS of 92.4 across all targets, with backbone placement error near the width of a carbon atom on the hardest free-modelling categories [9]. The margin was so large that assessors described the single-chain problem as solved. What made this possible was an architecture whose every component encodes a correct inductive bias about protein geometry [1].

This article examines that architecture at the level of its operators. We focus on three subsystems because they are where the geometric reasoning lives:

1. **The Evoformer**, which views folding as *graph inference in three-dimensional space*, exchanging information between an MSA representation (evolutionary couplings) and a pair representation (learned geometry) through axial attention and triangle-inequality-constrained updates.
2. **The structure module**, which replaces the distance-map intermediaries of earlier methods with direct, SE(3)-equivariant coordinate generation via invariant point attention (IPA) over a "residue gas" of local backbone frames.
3. **The confidence heads** — pLDDT, PAE, and pTM — which supervise the network to predict its own error, yielding per-residue and global uncertainty estimates that are calibrated against ground truth.

The presentation is self-contained: we define every tensor, derive the attention operators, and connect each design decision to the loss that supervises it.

##2 Background

### 2.1 The protein folding problem, formally

A protein is a chain of $N$ amino-acid residues. Each residue contributes a backbone of three heavy atoms (N, C$\alpha$, C) plus a side chain. The folding problem is to predict the positions of all atoms given only the sequence $s = (s_1, \dots, s_N)$ over the 20-letter amino-acid alphabet. Quality is measured against experimental structures (X-ray, cryo-EM, NMR) with:

- **GDT_TS** (global distance test, total score): the average fraction of C$\alpha$ atoms placed within 1, 2, 4, and 8 angstroms of the native structure after optimal superposition [6].
- **lDDT** (local distance difference test): the fraction of preserved inter-atomic distances within inclusion radii, requiring no superposition and hence robust to domain motion [5].
- **RMSD**: root-mean-square deviation after superposition — intuitive but dominated by the worst-placed regions.

> **Theorem (Anfinsen, informal):** The native conformation of a protein is the global minimum of its free-energy landscape, uniquely determined (up to kinetics) by the amino-acid sequence.

The practical consequence is that folding is a *search* problem over an energy landscape, and any successful method must either simulate that landscape faithfully or learn its minima directly from data. AlphaFold2 does the latter, trained on the ~170,000 experimental structures in the Protein Data Bank available at the time plus a large self-distillation set of unlabelled sequences [1][8].

### 2.2 From co-evolution to learned geometry

A foundational observation of the field is that *co-evolution implies contact*: if two residue positions mutate in a correlated fashion across homologous sequences, they are likely in spatial proximity in the folded protein. Multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) thus encode a noisy, implicit distance matrix. Classical pipelines (e.g., direct coupling analysis) extracted contacts statistically; AlphaFold1 learned a *distogram* — a full distribution over binned distances — with a convolutional network and then optimized a differentiable potential to realize it.

AlphaFold2's break is architectural: it never commits to an explicit distance matrix. Instead the **pair representation** $z \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N \times c_z}$ is a learned embedding of residue-pair relations, refined jointly with the **MSA representation** $m \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{seq}} \times N \times c_m}$ through 48 blocks of mutual exchange — geometry from representation learning, not hand-built potentials [1].

### 2.3 Related architectures

Understanding AlphaFold2 requires situating it among its contemporaries:

| System | Key idea | MSA required? |
|---|---|---|
| AlphaFold2 [1] | Evoformer + IPA structure module, end-to-end | Yes |
| RoseTTAFold [3] | Three-track network (sequence, distance, coordinates) | Yes |
| AlphaFold-Multimer [2] | Paired MSAs, interface-weighted losses for complexes | Yes (paired) |
| ColabFold [4] | Fast MMseqs2 MSAs, AlphaFold2 weights, accessible | Yes (fast) |
| OpenFold [8] | Open-source reimplementation, retraining study | Yes |
| ESMFold | Protein language model replaces MSA | No |

RoseTTAFold's three-track design is the closest sibling, but AlphaFold2's structure module is *equivariant by construction* rather than by augmentation, and its Evoformer enforces the triangle inequality as an architectural prior [1][3].

##3 Methodology

### 3.1 Input embeddings

Given a target sequence, AlphaFold2 builds two inputs. First, an MSA from genetic databases (BFD, MGnify, UniRef90) is embedded as $m^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{seq}} \times N \times c_m}$ with $c_m = 256$, augmenting one-hot identities with learned per-sequence and per-position features. Second, a pair representation $z^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N \times c_z}$ with $c_z = 128$ is initialized from template features and relative positional encodings; when templates are absent it still carries a learned relative-position bias, which proves surprisingly informative [1].

### 3.2 The Evoformer block

Each of the 48 Evoformer blocks (weights *not* shared across blocks) applies two pathways with a communication channel between them [1]:

**MSA stack.** Information flows *within* the alignment via axial attention:

- *Row-wise gated self-attention*: for each sequence, attention over residue positions, with logits additively biased by a projection of the pair representation $z_{ij}$ so geometric beliefs modulate evolutionary information flow.
- *Column-wise gated self-attention*: for each position, attention over sequences, letting the network down-weight redundant homologs — a learned, data-dependent generalization of classical sequence reweighting.

Each attention is *gated*: the output is elementwise-multiplied by a sigmoid gate computed from the input, a stabilizer that proved important at this depth.

**Pair stack.** The pair representation is updated under geometric consistency constraints (detailed in section 4.2):

- *Triangle multiplicative updates* (outgoing and incoming edge variants), which combine pairs $(i,k)$ and $(k,j)$ to refine $(i,j)$.
- *Triangle self-attention* around the starting node and around the ending node, which weights the contributions of different intermediate residues $k$ by attention.

**Communication.** Two bridges connect the stacks:

1. *Outer product mean*: the MSA representation's columns at positions $i$ and $j$ are combined by outer product and averaged over sequences, then linearly projected into an update of $z_{ij}$. This is how co-evolutionary couplings are distilled into geometry.
2. *Pair-to-MSA bias*: $z_{ij}$ is projected to a scalar bias added to the row-wise MSA attention logits, closing the loop so that geometry informs evolutionary interpretation.

After the blocks, the first row of the MSA representation (corresponding to the target sequence itself) is linearly projected to form the **single representation** $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^{c_s}$, which, together with the final pair representation, feeds the structure module [1].

### 3.3 The structure module

The structure module discards the MSA stack and works with the single and pair representations. It treats the protein as a **residue gas**: each residue $i$ is assigned a backbone *frame* $T_i = (R_i, t_i) \in SE(3)$, a rigid-body map from ideal local backbone coordinates to global ones. All frames start at the origin with identity rotation — a degenerate unfolded state — refined through 8 blocks with *shared* weights [1].

Each block performs, in order:

1. **Invariant point attention** updates the single representation from the current frames and pair representation, moving no atoms.
2. A transition layer further processes the single representation.
3. A **backbone update** predicts per-residue rotation (quaternion) and translation deltas *in the residue's local frame*; composing $T_i \leftarrow T_i \circ \Delta T_i$ makes the block equivariant to global rigid motions.
4. The single representation predicts side-chain torsions $\chi_1 \dots \chi_4$, from which all heavy-atom positions are placed deterministically.

Because the chain connectivity is deliberately *not* enforced during the blocks (breaking the chain enables local refinement without solving loop-closure), a violation loss penalizes broken peptide bonds, steric clashes, and incorrect bond geometry; exact stereochemistry is restored afterwards by Amber relaxation with the OpenMM force field, which empirically removes violations without degrading GDT or lDDT scores [1][7].

```python
# Pseudostructure of one structure-module block (notation follows [1])
def structure_block(single, pair, frames):
    # 1. geometry-aware attention; positions unchanged
    single = single + ipa(single, pair, frames)          # Invariant Point Attention
    single = single + transition(single)                  # feed-forward
    # 2. equivariant frame update, computed in local coordinates
    quat, trans = backbone_update(single)                 # per-residue SE(3) delta
    frames = compose(frames, local_to_global(quat, trans, frames))
    # 3. side chains placed deterministically from torsions
    chi_angles = angle_head(single)                       # chi_1 .. chi_4
    atoms = place_atoms(frames, chi_angles)               # ideal geometry lookup
    return single, frames, atoms
```

### 3.4 Recycling and end-to-end differentiability

The entire pipeline is differentiable, so one 3D-coordinate loss backpropagates through everything. **Recycling** feeds the outputs back as inputs for up to three extra passes at training time (gradients stopped between recycles), with the same unrolled refinement at inference [1]. Each recycle is supervised by the same losses, so the network learns iterative refinement rather than one-shot mapping — a design choice ablations show is responsible for a substantial accuracy gain [1].

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Evoformer as learned belief propagation on a residue graph

The guiding metaphor of the Evoformer is *graph inference in 3D space*: residues are nodes, edges carry learned geometric relations, and the MSA supplies noisy observations of those edges through evolution [1]. The axial factorization of MSA attention is what makes this tractable. Full attention over the $N_{\text{seq}} \times N$ MSA tensor would cost $O(N_{\text{seq}}^2 N^2)$; factorizing into row-wise ($O(N_{\text{seq}} N^2)$) and column-wise ($O(N_{\text{seq}}^2 N)$) passes keeps the computation affordable while preserving the two semantically distinct operations — *which residues talk to each other within a homolog* versus *which homologs to trust at each position*.

The pair-to-MSA bias closes a loop earlier pipelines left open: as geometric beliefs sharpen through the blocks, they continuously re-bias how the MSA is read, attenuating co-evolutionary signal for pairs believed distant and suppressing the indirect-correlation artifacts of raw DCA.

### 4.2 Triangle updates: baking the triangle inequality into the architecture

If residues $i$ and $k$ are close, and $k$ and $j$ are close, then $i$ and $j$ cannot be far — the triangle inequality.

**Triangle multiplicative update.** For the outgoing-edge variant, gate-projected features of edges $(i,k)$ and $(k,j)$ are combined by Hadamard product, summed over every intermediate residue $k$, and gated by the direct edge $(i,j)$ — a learned, differentiable relaxation of Floyd–Warshall transitive closure. The operation is $O(N^3)$ in sequence length, the Evoformer's dominant cost, which is why deployments cap MSA depth and crop long sequences [4][8].

**Triangle self-attention.** Around the starting node $i$, attention is computed over the intermediate index $k$ for fixed $(i,j)$, so the network *attends* over which third residues mediate the $(i,j)$ relation with the pair representation supplying the bias — attention with a geometric conscience [1].

Ablations in the original paper confirm the operators' importance: replacing triangle attention with standard axial attention measurably degrades GDT on hard targets, precisely because the network must then rediscover geometric consistency from data alone [1].

### 4.3 Invariant point attention: attention that respects Euclidean geometry

The structure module faces a harder constraint than the Evoformer: its outputs are *coordinates*, which must transform correctly under rigid motions of the input.

Each residue $i$ carries its frame $T_i = (R_i, t_i)$.

$$a_{ij} = \mathrm{softmax}_j\left( w_L \cdot \frac{q_i^\top k_j}{\sqrt{d}} \;-\; \frac{w_C}{2}\sum_p \lVert T_i \circ \vec{q}_i^{\,p} \;-\; T_j \circ \vec{k}_j^{\,p} \rVert^2 \;+\; b_{ij} \right),$$

where $b_{ij}$ is a scalar bias from the pair representation, and the point term measures squared distances between query points of $i$ and key points of $j$ *after mapping both into the global frame*.

The output values combine scalar values with value points mapped back into residue $i$'s local frame: $T_i^{-1} \circ \sum_j a_{ij}\, (T_j \circ \vec{v}_j^{\,p})$.

Two consequences make IPA more than a mathematical curiosity:

1. **Spatial locality bias.** The $-\lVert \cdot \rVert^2$ point term is a soft learned distance penalty: distant residues attend weakly, nearby ones strongly. As frames refine, attention *focuses* — a differentiable analogue of local refinement in fragment assembly.
2. **Equivariant updates.** Local-frame deltas make the whole module SE(3)-equivariant *by construction*, with no need for rotation-augmented training data.

```rust
// IPA attention logit (per head, per query point set) — pseudocode
fn ipa_logit(
    q_scalar: &[f32], k_scalar: &[f32],   // standard q, k
    q_pts: &[Vec3], k_pts: &[Vec3],       // local-frame 3D points
    t_i: &Rigid, t_j: &Rigid,             // residue frames
    pair_bias: f32, w_l: f32, w_c: f32, d: f32,
) -> f32 {
    let scalar = w_l * dot(q_scalar, k_scalar) / d.sqrt();
    let mut point = 0.0;
    for (q, k) in q_pts.iter().zip(k_pts.iter()) {
        // map both into the GLOBAL frame; global rigid motions cancel
        let diff = t_i.apply(q) - t_j.apply(k);
        point += diff.norm_squared();
    }
    scalar - 0.5 * w_c * point + pair_bias
}
```

### 4.4 Confidence heads: learning to predict one's own error

A structure predictor that cannot say *I am unsure here* is dangerous in downstream applications like drug design.

- **pLDDT**: a per-residue score from 0 to 100, trained by discretizing true lDDT-C$\alpha$ into 50 bins. Because lDDT is superposition-free, pLDDT measures *local* correctness. Calibration is excellent: above 90 means near-experimental accuracy, 70 to 90 a generally correct backbone, 50 to 70 calls for caution, and below 50 the model is effectively predicting *disorder* — low-pLDDT regions correlate strongly with intrinsically disordered regions [1].
- **PAE**: an $N \times N$ matrix where entry $(x, y)$ is the expected error of residue $x$ when aligned on residue $y$. Low intra-block PAE with high inter-block PAE means confident domains of uncertain relative orientation — the signature needed before trusting a domain interface [1].
- **pTM / ipTM**: a global head predicting the TM-score of the full prediction; the interface-weighted ipTM ranks complex predictions in AlphaFold-Multimer [2].

The distogram and masked-MSA heads serve mainly as training regularizers; the confidence heads survive into the product, rendered as the blue-to-orange coloring in the AlphaFold database of over 200 million structures [1].

A TLA+-style specification of the confidence contract clarifies what calibration means:

```tla
---- MODULE Confidence ----
EXTENDS Reals, Sequences
CONSTANTS N  \* number of residues
VARIABLES plddt, true_lddt
Calibrated ==
    \A i \in 1..N :
        plddt[i] \in 0..100
        /\ (plddt[i] > 90  => true_lddt[i] \in 85..100)   \* very high: near-experimental
        /\ (plddt[i] < 50  => true_lddt[i] < 60)          \* very low: unreliable/disordered
Monotone ==
    \A i, j \in 1..N : plddt[i] =< plddt[j] => true_lddt[i] =< true_lddt[j] + 15
====
```

The specification is aspirational — real calibration has noise — but large-scale validation shows the monotonicity property holds with high probability across the proteome, which is why pLDDT thresholds are now standard filters in computational pipelines [1].

##5 Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 CASP14: the experiment that ended the debate

CASP is a blind biennial experiment scoring predictors on imminently-published structures. In CASP14, AlphaFold2's median GDT_TS of 92.4 dwarfed the runner-up's ~75; on the hardest free-modelling targets its median backbone RMSD at 95 percent coverage was 0.96 angstroms versus 2.8 for the next best [9][1]. For roughly two-thirds of the hardest targets, the prediction lay within experimental error of the deposited structure.

### 5.2 What the ablations prove

The Nature paper's ablation table is unusually informative because each removed component maps to a design hypothesis [1]:

Removing the MSA causes catastrophic degradation (evolutionary signal is primary); removing templates costs little (the network rediscovers them); removing recycling hurts hard targets (iteration is load-bearing); replacing IPA with direct projection causes a large drop (equivariance-by-construction wins); and removing triangle attention measurably degrades contact-heavy targets [1]. Every component encoding a correct geometric prior contributes independently, and their combination is super-additive.

### 5.3 Calibration evidence for confidence metrics

Binning millions of held-out residues by predicted pLDDT and measuring true lDDT-C$\alpha$ per bin yields a reliability curve hugging the diagonal: pLDDT 85 means true lDDT near 85 on average [1]. High-confidence regions are now used directly in molecular replacement for crystallographic phasing, while low-confidence regions serve as a *de facto* disorder predictor competitive with dedicated tools [1].

On multi-domain proteins, PAE's block-diagonal structure recovers domain boundaries agreeing with experiment, while high inter-domain PAE correctly flags untrustworthy domain orientations — including flexible linkers the model draws as extended chains [1].

##6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating what AlphaFold2 does *not* do, because its confidence metrics are sometimes misread as certifying more than they do.

1. **Single static structures.** One conformation per sequence. Proteins functioning through ensembles — transporters, allosteric enzymes, disordered regions — get a single point estimate; pLDDT's low-confidence treatment of disorder is a graceful failure mode, not a dynamics model [1].
2. **No ligands, cofactors, or post-translational modifications.** The training objective sees only amino-acid chains; heme groups, metal ions, glycans, and phosphorylation are invisible. Binding pockets predicted in the apo state may differ substantially from holo conformations.
3. **MSA dependence.** Orphan proteins with shallow alignments suffer sharp degradation; MSA-free successors trade accuracy for coverage, and the gap remains [1].
4. **Complexes and stoichiometry.** Base AlphaFold2 predicts single chains; multimeric assemblies need AlphaFold-Multimer's paired MSAs and interface losses, and even then, antibody-antigen interfaces and transient complexes remain challenging [2].
5. **No mutational-effect prediction.** Despite its evolutionary grounding, the model is a poor predictor of the *change* in stability or function upon mutation — a distinct task (variant effect prediction) for which the confidence heads were never supervised.
6. **Training-data cutoff and memorization.** Structures deposited after the training cutoff are predicted well (as CASP14 demonstrated), but near-duplicates of training chains can inflate apparent accuracy; careful sequence-identity-based splitting, as done in the paper's validation, is essential to honest evaluation [1][8].

##7 Conclusion

AlphaFold2's enduring lesson is that *the right inductive biases, differentiably enforced, compound*. The Evoformer treats evolution and geometry as two views of one graph, translating between them through axial attention gated by triangle-inequality-aware updates. The structure module encodes the belief that 3D space has symmetries, and builds them into invariant point attention so the network never has to learn what a rotation is. The confidence heads encode the belief that a model's uncertainty is itself a learnable function of its internal representations — and that a calibrated "I don't know" is a first-class scientific output.

Each idea has outlived the network: triangle attention reappears in protein-design diffusion models, IPA-style equivariant attention in molecular generation, and pLDDT-style confidence in language-model agents. The fifty-year problem fell to an architecture in which every operator respects the geometry of its world — a principle that generalizes far beyond proteins.
