# Diffusion Probabilistic Models: From Denoising Markov Chains to Score-Based SDEs and Latent Diffusion at Scale

## Abstract

This thesis develops a unified mathematical treatment of diffusion probabilistic models, the family of generative models that has dominated high-fidelity image synthesis since 2020. We begin from the thermodynamic origins of diffusion modeling [5] and the noise-conditional score estimation framework [6], and derive the two complementary formulations that structure the modern literature: the discrete-time denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) of Ho et al. [1], whose training reduces to a weighted variational bound on the data log-likelihood, and the continuous-time score-based stochastic differential equation (SDE) of Song et al. [3], whose reverse-time dynamics depend only on the time-dependent score function of the perturbed data distribution. We prove, via the variational analysis of Meng et al. [9], that the DDPM objective and denoising score matching are exactly equivalent, and that the noise-prediction network is a scaled score estimator. We then analyze Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) [2], which generalize the Markovian forward process to a non-Markovian family sharing the same training objective while permitting deterministic, accelerated sampling. Finally, we examine latent diffusion models (LDMs) [4], which migrate the diffusion process into the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder to achieve high-resolution synthesis at manageable cost, and discuss the empirical record — including unconditional FID of 3.17 on CIFAR-10 [1], FID 2.20 for score-SDE models [3], and GAN-surpassing sample quality [8]. We conclude with limitations: sampling cost, the likelihood–quality tension, and the architectural bottlenecks of iterative refinement.

## 1 Introduction

Generative modeling seeks to learn a distribution $p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})$ over high-dimensional observations from finite samples, and to draw novel samples from the learned distribution. Prior to 2020, the field was partitioned between adversarial approaches (GANs), which offered high sample quality but suffered from unstable training and mode collapse, and likelihood-based approaches (VAEs, autoregressive models, normalizing flows), which offered stable training at the cost of blurred samples or intractable sampling. **Diffusion probabilistic models** introduced a third paradigm: define a fixed *forward* stochastic process that gradually destroys structure in the data, transforming it into a simple noise distribution; then learn a *reverse* process that iteratively restores structure. Because the forward process is fixed and analytically tractable, training reduces to a sequence of well-conditioned denoising regression problems rather than a saddle-point game [1].

> **Theorem:** The forward diffusion process is fixed and independent of the parameters; the only learned object is the reverse conditional. This asymmetry is what makes diffusion training unusually stable relative to adversarial training.

The intellectual lineage begins with Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) [5], who imported tools from nonequilibrium thermodynamics — Jarzynski equalities and annealing — into probabilistic modeling, but whose models did not scale to natural images. The modern era was launched in parallel by two 2019–2020 lines: (i) Noise-Conditional Score Networks (NCSN) by Song and Ermon [6], which estimated gradients of the data distribution at multiple noise scales and sampled by annealed Langevin dynamics; and (ii) Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) by Ho, Jain, and Abbeel [1], which introduced the weighted variational bound and the noise-prediction parameterization that made thousand-step Markovian diffusion practical on CIFAR-10 and LSUN. Song et al. (2021) [3] then unified both lines under continuous-time stochastic differential equations, showing that DDPM and NCSN are discretizations of two SDEs — the *variance preserving* (VP) and *variance exploding* (VE) SDEs — and derived the reverse-time SDE, the probability-flow ODE, and a predictor–corrector sampling framework. Sampling speed was addressed by Song, Meng, and Ermon (2021) [2] via DDIM, and the computational cost of pixel-space diffusion was addressed by Rombach et al. (2022) [4] via latent diffusion, which underpins contemporary text-to-image systems.

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 formalizes the forward process and the score function. Section 3 derives the DDPM variational objective and its equivalence to score matching. Section 4 treats four deep technical themes: the score-SDE framework and probability flow ODE; the DDIM non-Markovian generalization; Tweedie's formula and the ε–score correspondence; and latent diffusion architecture with cross-attention conditioning. Section 5 surveys the empirical record and provides key proof sketches. Section 6 discusses limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The forward diffusion process

Let $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim p_{\text{data}}$ be a data point. The forward process defines a discrete-time Markov chain $\mathbf{x}_0 \to \mathbf{x}_1 \to \cdots \to \mathbf{x}_T$ with Gaussian transitions of prescribed variance schedule $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$ [1]:

$$q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}) := \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I}).$$

Crucially, the marginal at arbitrary time $t$ admits a closed form. Defining $\alpha_t := 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t := \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$, repeated application of the Gaussian reparameterization yields [1]:

$$q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I}), \qquad \mathbf{x}_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}).$$

When $\bar{\alpha}_T \approx 0$, the terminal marginal $q(\mathbf{x}_T)$ is approximately standard Gaussian — a tractable prior from which sampling is trivial.

### 2.2 The score function and denoising score matching

The *score* of a distribution $p(\mathbf{x})$ is the gradient of its log-density, $\mathbf{s}(\mathbf{x}) := \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p(\mathbf{x})$. Score matching estimates $\mathbf{s}_\theta$ without evaluating the normalization constant, but fails in low-density regions where the score is poorly determined [6]. The remedy is to perturb the data with Gaussian noise at multiple scales $\sigma_1 < \cdots < \sigma_L$ and match the score of each perturbed distribution $p_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}) = \int \mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{x}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})\,p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}$. Vincent's *denoising score matching* identity gives the tractable objective [6]:

$$\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{x}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})}\left\lVert \mathbf{s}_\theta(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}, \sigma) + \frac{\tilde{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x}}{\sigma^2} \right\rVert_2^2,$$

which, up to a constant, equals the explicit score-matching loss for $p_\sigma$. Samples are then generated by *annealed Langevin dynamics*: starting from the largest noise scale and descending, iterate $\tilde{\mathbf{x}} \leftarrow \tilde{\mathbf{x}} + \frac{\eta^2}{2}\mathbf{s}_\theta(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}, \sigma) + \eta \mathbf{z}$ with $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$.

| Concept | Symbol | Role in diffusion models |
|---|---|---|
| Noise schedule | $\beta_t$, $\bar{\alpha}_t$ | Controls signal-to-noise ratio along the chain [1] |
| Perturbed density | $p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$ or $q(\mathbf{x}_t)$ | Time-indexed marginals of the forward process |
| Score | $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$ | Vector field the network estimates [3] |
| Noise predictor | $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ | Practical parameterization; proportional to score |
| Reverse kernel | $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ | Learned Gaussian transition for sampling |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The DDPM reverse process and variational bound

The generative (reverse) process is a learned Markov chain with the same functional form as the forward process, initialized at the prior $p(\mathbf{x}_T) = \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ [1]:

$$p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}) := p(\mathbf{x}_T)\prod_{t=1}^{T} p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t), \qquad p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t) := \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t), \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)).$$

Training maximizes a variational bound on the negative log-likelihood. With the posterior $q(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_0)$ — Gaussian and analytically tractable — the ELBO decomposes into a sum of KL divergences between Gaussians [1]:

$$L_{\text{vlb}} := L_0 + \sum_{t=2}^{T} L_{t-1} + L_T, \quad L_{t-1} := D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(q(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_0) \,\|\, p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t)\right).$$

### 3.2 The simplified noise-prediction objective

Parameterizing the reverse mean through a noise-prediction network, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(\mathbf{x}_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\right)$, and fixing the variance, the KL terms collapse — up to time-dependent weights — into the now-canonical *simplified objective* [1]:

$$L_{\text{simple}}(\theta) := \mathbb{E}_{t \sim [1,T],\,\mathbf{x}_0,\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\left\lVert \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta\!\left(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon},\, t\right)\right\rVert^2\right].$$

This is a *reweighted* variational bound that down-weights the low-noise (high-$t$-small) terms where the denoising task is easy, and empirically yields substantially better sample quality than the unweighted ELBO. Ho et al. report an unconditional CIFAR-10 FID of 3.17 and Inception score 9.46 [1].

### 3.3 Equivalence to score matching

Meng et al. [9] provide the variational perspective that closes the loop between DDPM and score matching: under the plug-in reverse SDE, minimizing the score-matching loss is *exactly equivalent* to maximizing a lower bound on the likelihood. The key identity, Tweedie's formula applied to the Gaussian perturbation kernel, gives:

$$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) \approx -\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_t(\mathbf{x}_t).$$

Thus the noise predictor is a *scaled score estimator*, and DDPM training is denoising score matching on the forward marginals [3, 9]. A minimal training step in PyTorch notation:

```python
def ddpm_loss(x0, model, alphas_cumprod, T):
    t = torch.randint(1, T + 1, (x0.shape[0],), device=x0.device)
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    a_bar = alphas_cumprod[t].view(-1, 1, 1, 1)
    x_t = torch.sqrt(a_bar) * x0 + torch.sqrt(1 - a_bar) * eps
    eps_hat = model(x_t, t)                      # noise predictor = scaled score
    return F.mse_loss(eps_hat, eps)
```

> **Theorem (Score–noise correspondence):** For the VP forward process, $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)/\!\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} = -\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)$. Hence every DDPM is a score-based model, and every score estimate yields a denoiser via $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 = (\mathbf{x}_t + (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t))/\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}$.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The score-based SDE framework

Song et al. [3] lift diffusion to continuous time. Let $\{\mathbf{x}(t)\}_{t \in [0,T]}$ solve the forward SDE

$$d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{w},$$

where $\mathbf{w}$ is standard Brownian motion. Anderson's time-reversal theorem gives the *reverse-time SDE* [3]:

$$d\mathbf{x} = \left[\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})\right]dt + g(t)\,d\bar{\mathbf{w}},$$

where $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ is a reverse-time Brownian motion and $p_t$ is the marginal density of the forward process. The drift correction depends *only* on the score — everything else is known analytically. Two canonical SDEs instantiate the framework:

- **VP SDE** (continuous DDPM): $d\mathbf{x} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathbf{x}\,dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\mathbf{w}$.
- **VE SDE** (continuous NCSN): $d\mathbf{x} = \sqrt{d[\sigma^2(t)]/dt}\,d\mathbf{w}$.

From the reverse SDE, Song et al. derive the **probability-flow ODE** [3]:

$$d\mathbf{x} = \left[\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})\right]dt,$$

an ordinary differential equation whose trajectories share the *same marginals* $p_t$ as the SDE at every time. This yields (i) *exact likelihood computation* via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula with a Hutchinson trace estimator, and (ii) a deterministic, invertible encoding of data into latents — enabling latent manipulation and near-exact reconstruction. The authors also introduce a **predictor–corrector** sampler: each reverse-SDE predictor step is followed by Langevin corrector steps using the score, substantially improving sample quality at fixed compute [3].

### 4.2 DDIM: non-Markovian forward processes and accelerated sampling

DDPM sampling is slow because generation must simulate the full $T$-step Markov chain (typically $T = 1000$). Song, Meng, and Ermon [2] observe that the *only* properties of the forward process used in training are the marginals $q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0)$ — not the Markovian factorization. They define a family of *non-Markovian* inference distributions

$$q_\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\mid\mathbf{x}_0) := q_\sigma(\mathbf{x}_T\mid\mathbf{x}_0)\prod_{t=2}^{T} q_\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_0),$$

where each $q_\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{x}_0)$ is Gaussian with mean constructed so that the marginals $q_\sigma(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\mathbf{x}_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I})$ are unchanged. The variational objective $J_\sigma$ then coincides, term by term, with the DDPM objective whenever the variance parameter $\sigma_t$ is matched to the DDPM variance — so **a network trained as a DDPM can be sampled as a DDIM with no retraining** [2].

The DDIM update with stochasticity parameter $\eta$ (and $\sigma_t = \eta\sqrt{(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})/(1-\bar{\alpha}_t)}\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t/\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}$) is:

$$\mathbf{x}_{t-1} = \underbrace{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}\right)}_{\text{predicted }\mathbf{x}_0\text{ term}} + \underbrace{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)}_{\text{direction toward }\mathbf{x}_t} + \underbrace{\sigma_t \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t}_{\text{noise}}.$$

Setting $\eta = 0$ yields a **fully deterministic** sampler: the generative process becomes an implicit model whose latent code $\mathbf{x}_T$ maps deterministically to a sample, enabling semantically meaningful interpolation and near-exact inversion. Because the process can be strided over a subsequence $\tau \subset \{1,\dots,T\}$ of timesteps, DDIM produces high-quality samples $10\times$–$50\times$ faster than DDPM in wall-clock time [2].

```python
@torch.no_grad()
def ddim_sample(model, shape, timesteps, alphas_cumprod, eta=0.0):
    x = torch.randn(shape)
    for i, t in enumerate(reversed(timesteps)):
        t_prev = timesteps[i - 1] if i > 0 else 0
        a_t, a_prev = alphas_cumprod[t], alphas_cumprod[t_prev]
        eps = model(x, torch.full((shape[0],), t))
        x0_hat = (x - torch.sqrt(1 - a_t) * eps) / torch.sqrt(a_t)
        sigma = eta * torch.sqrt((1 - a_prev) / (1 - a_t)) * torch.sqrt(1 - a_t / a_prev)
        dir_xt = torch.sqrt(torch.clamp(1 - a_prev - sigma**2, min=0.0)) * eps
        x = torch.sqrt(a_prev) * x0_hat + dir_xt + sigma * torch.randn_like(x)
    return x
```

### 4.3 Variance schedules, prediction targets, and improved training

The original DDPM used a linear $\beta$ schedule and fixed reverse variance. Nichol and Dhariwal [7] showed that *learning* the reverse variance via an interpolation parameter, together with a cosine noise schedule, dramatically improves log-likelihood (e.g., 3.75 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 with $T=4000$) while hybridizing $L_{\text{simple}} + \lambda L_{\text{vlb}}$ preserves sample quality. The cosine schedule keeps $\bar{\alpha}_t$ near 1 for longer at small $t$, allocating model capacity to the perceptually critical low-noise regime. Dhariwal and Nichol [8] further demonstrated that with classifier guidance — perturbing the reverse mean by the gradient of a classifier, $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log p_\phi(y\mid\mathbf{x}_t)$ — diffusion models *surpass GANs* on ImageNet synthesis (FID 3.94 at 256×256 with guidance), ending GAN dominance of the image-quality frontier [8].

| NCSN [6] | 2019 | Denoising score matching, multi-scale | Annealed Langevin | No |
| Improved DDPM [7] | 2021 | Hybrid $L_{\text{simple}} + L_{\text{vlb}}$, cosine schedule | 1000–4000 | Yes (learned variance) |

| LDM [4] | 2022 | Diffusion in autoencoder latent space + cross-attention | 50–200 (DDIM) | Approximate |

### 4.4 Latent diffusion: scaling beyond pixel space

Pixel-space diffusion at $512\times512$ resolution is prohibitively expensive: self-attention in the UNet scales quadratically with the number of pixels. Rombach et al. [4] decompose generation into two stages. First, train a perceptual autoencoder — a VQ-regularized or KL-regularized convolutional encoder $\mathcal{E}$ and decoder $\mathcal{D}$ — that compresses images $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ to latents $\mathbf{z} = \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c}$ with downsampling factor $f = H/h$ (typically $f \in \{4, 8, 16\}$), balancing compression against reconstruction fidelity. Second, train the diffusion model *entirely in latent space*:

$$L_{\text{LDM}} := \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}=\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{x}),\,y,\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon},\,t}\left[\left\lVert \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t, \tau_\theta(y))\right\rVert_2^2\right],$$

where $\tau_\theta(y)$ embeds conditioning inputs (text, semantic maps, bounding boxes) and is injected into the UNet via **cross-attention** layers, $\text{Attention}(Q, K, V)$ with $Q$ from the UNet features and $K, V$ from $\tau_\theta(y)$. Because the latent space is perceptually equivalent but spatially compressed, LDMs reduce training cost by orders of magnitude while reaching state-of-the-art on inpainting, class-conditional synthesis, and text-to-image generation, and enable convolutional high-resolution synthesis beyond the training resolution [4]. This architecture is the direct ancestor of Stable Diffusion.

## 5 Empirical Analysis and Proofs

### 5.1 Proof sketch: score-matching ≡ variational bound

We sketch the argument of Meng et al. [9] connecting score matching to the DDPM ELBO. Let $q(\mathbf{x}_{0:T})$ be the forward chain and $p_\theta$ the reverse chain. The ELBO is

$$\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\mid\mathbf{x}_0)}\left[\log \frac{p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{0:T})}{q(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}\mid\mathbf{x}_0)}\right] =: \mathcal{L}_{\text{ELBO}}.$$

For Gaussian transitions with matched variances, each KL term is a squared error between the true posterior mean $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_t(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_0)$ and the parameterized mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$. Substituting the noise parameterization and applying Tweedie's formula $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_t = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(\mathbf{x}_t + \beta_t \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0))$, the squared error becomes proportional to $\lVert \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta \rVert^2 = (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\lVert \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t}\log q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0) + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta/\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\rVert^2$, which is exactly the denoising score-matching integrand. Summing over $t$ with the ELBO weights shows the DDPM objective *is* a weighted sum of score-matching losses — the two literatures optimize the same functional [3, 9].

### 5.2 Proof sketch: DDIM objective equivalence

Fix marginals $q(\mathbf{x}_t\mid\mathbf{x}_0)$. For the non-Markovian family $q_\sigma$, the variational bound is $J_\sigma = \mathbb{E}[\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0\mid\mathbf{x}_1)] - \sum_t D_{\text{KL}}(q_\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{x}_0)\,\|\,p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t))$. Each KL is between Gaussians whose means differ only through the predicted noise; choosing $\sigma_t^2 = \tilde{\beta}_t := \frac{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\beta_t$ makes every KL term proportional to $\lVert \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta \rVert^2$ with identical weights to DDPM's $L_\gamma$ for a specific weighting $\gamma$. Hence $J_\sigma$ and the DDPM objective share the *same* optimum in $\theta$ — a DDPM checkpoint is a valid DDIM without retraining [2].

### 5.3 Empirical record

- **DDPM** [1]: CIFAR-10 unconditional, IS 9.46, FID 3.17 (state of the art at publication); LSUN 256×256 quality comparable to ProgressiveGAN.
- **Score-SDE** [3]: CIFAR-10 IS 9.89, FID 2.20 — a record at the time — with 3.10 bits/dim likelihood, and first high-fidelity $1024\times1024$ generation from a score-based model.
- **DDIM** [2]: $10\times$–$50\times$ wall-clock acceleration over DDPM at comparable FID using 20–100 strided steps; deterministic inversion with reconstruction error far below DDPM's stochastic chain.
- **ADM with classifier guidance** [8]: ImageNet 256×256 FID 3.94, surpassing BigGAN-deep — the first likelihood-based family to beat GANs on a standard image benchmark.
- **LDM** [4]: state-of-the-art inpainting and class-conditional synthesis at a fraction of pixel-space compute; enabled open text-to-image models trained on hundreds of millions of image–text pairs.

> **Theorem (Universality of the score):** Given the exact time-dependent score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ for all $t$, the reverse SDE generates *exact* samples from $p_{\text{data}}$, the probability-flow ODE computes *exact* likelihoods, and the DDIM update with $\eta=0$ computes a *deterministic* transport map. All error in diffusion models is therefore score-estimation error plus discretization error — the theory is exact; only the numerics are approximate [3].

## 6 Limitations

1. **Sampling cost.** Even with DDIM striding, diffusion models require tens of sequential network evaluations per sample, versus one for GANs or VAEs. Distillation (progressive, consistency models) narrows but does not close this gap.
2. **Likelihood–quality tension.** The reweighting in $L_{\text{simple}}$ that produces good samples degrades likelihood; the hybrid objectives [7] only partially reconcile the two. Exact likelihood via the probability-flow ODE [3] requires expensive ODE solves with trace estimators.
3. **Score estimation in low-density regions.** Denoising score matching is only as good as the noise coverage; manifold overfitting and memorization of training data at low noise levels remain active concerns, particularly for large text-to-image models.
4. **Discretization and schedule sensitivity.** Both SDE solvers and DDIM striding introduce bias that interacts with the noise schedule; there is no universally optimal schedule, and aggressive striding degrades fine detail.
5. **Conditioning fidelity.** Classifier-free guidance trades diversity for prompt adherence in a poorly understood way, and cross-attention conditioning [4] can ignore fine-grained compositional instructions.
6. **Evaluation.** FID and Inception score are known to be misaligned with human judgment at the current quality frontier, yet remain the de facto standards in the cited works.

## 7 Conclusion

Diffusion probabilistic models reframe generation as *iterative denoising*: a fixed forward process converts data into noise along an analytically tractable path, and learning reduces to estimating the score of the perturbed marginals — equivalently, predicting the injected noise [1, 3, 9]. This single insight supports the DDPM variational formulation, the score-SDE continuous-time unification with its reverse SDE and probability-flow ODE, the DDIM non-Markovian acceleration family, and the latent diffusion architecture that made high-resolution conditional synthesis computationally feasible [2, 4]. Empirically, the family progressed from CIFAR-10 records [1, 3] to GAN-surpassing ImageNet quality [8] to open text-to-image systems [4] within two years — a pace explained by the theory: once the score is estimated, sampling, likelihood, inversion, and interpolation are all consequences of the same reverse dynamics. The remaining challenges are computational (sequential sampling), statistical (score estimation off-manifold), and methodological (evaluation). Future work will likely compress the iterative process — through distillation, consistency, or flow-matching formulations — while preserving the stability and mode coverage that made diffusion the dominant generative paradigm.
