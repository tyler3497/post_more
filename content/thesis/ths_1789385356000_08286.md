# The Prediction Machinery: AlphaFold2's Evoformer, MSA Transformers, and the CASP14 Verdict

## Abstract

The protein structure prediction problem — computing a protein's three-dimensional fold from its amino-acid sequence — stood for five decades as the canonical grand challenge of computational biology. AlphaFold2's performance at CASP14 in 2020 ended it as a scientific contest: a median backbone accuracy of 0.96 angstroms r.m.s.d.95, against 2.8 angstroms for the next-best method, placed roughly two-thirds of predictions within experimental error of the solved structures [1]. This thesis dissects the machinery behind that leap. We analyze the Evoformer, AlphaFold2's joint attention architecture over alignments and residue-pair representations; the invariant point attention module that renders SE(3)-equivariant coordinates directly; the MSA Transformer, which proved that coevolutionary signal can be learned as attention maps rather than hand-fit Potts models [3]; and the single-sequence ESMFold line trading alignment depth for scale [4]. We survey the sibling RoseTTAFold three-track architecture [5], derive how fine-tuned predictors became generative models in RFdiffusion [8], and read the CASP14 assessment record as community-scale validation [6]. Structure prediction fell not to one trick, but to the synthesis of evolutionary statistics, geometric deep learning, and self-estimated confidence.

## 1 Intro

In December 2020, the biennial Critical Assessment of Structure Prediction (CASP14) announced results that structural biologists described as a discontinuity rather than a progression. DeepMind's AlphaFold2 had produced models with a median backbone deviation of 0.96 angstroms from experiment — the width of a carbon atom is roughly 1.4 angstroms — across targets for which no structural template existed [1]. The system's predecessor, AlphaFold1, had won CASP13 with a median accuracy of 6.6 angstroms on the hardest targets, respectable but clearly short of experimental resolution. The new system compressed the gap between computation and crystallography by nearly an order of magnitude in a single cycle.

This thesis asks *how*, at the level of architectural mechanism. The answer decomposes into four innovations that this document treats as the core literature:

1. **End-to-end learned coevolution.** Instead of hand-engineered covariation features fed to a convolutional network, AlphaFold2 processes the raw multiple sequence alignment (MSA) with an attention stack — the Evoformer — that exchanges information between sequence rows and residue-pair columns.
2. **Geometric structure generation.** The structure module builds 3D coordinates via invariant point attention (IPA), a mechanism provably invariant to global rigid motions, so the network never has to learn rotation and translation as statistical regularities.
3. **MSA-native language modeling.** The MSA Transformer [3] demonstrated that masked language modeling over alignments recovers contact maps in its attention heads, unifying the Potts-model tradition of direct coupling analysis with the transformer paradigm.
4. **Calibrated self-confidence.** AlphaFold2 predicts its own error (pLDDT) per residue, turning a black-box fold into a reliability-annotated instrument.

We then connect prediction to its mirror image, *design*: RFdiffusion's fine-tuning of a prediction network into a denoising generative model over backbones [8]. Finally, we examine the CASP evaluation machinery itself — the blind experiment that certified the revolution [6].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The problem and the coevolution insight

A protein's sequence determines its fold — Anfinsen's principle — but the mapping is a high-dimensional constraint satisfaction problem over tens of thousands of atomic degrees of freedom. Levinthal's paradox notes that exhaustive search is impossible; nature solves it in milliseconds. For decades, computational methods approximated the answer in two stages: extract evolutionary constraints, then search conformational space.

The key biological observation is coevolution: residues in physical contact tend to mutate in a correlated fashion across a protein family, because a mutation at one position is compensated by mutations at contacting positions. Given a deep multiple sequence alignment, statistical methods detect these correlations. Early approaches used mutual information; the modern foundation was laid by **direct coupling analysis (DCA)**, which fits a pairwise Markov random field — the Potts model — to the MSA by pseudolikelihood maximization, disentangling direct from transitive correlations [3]. Contact maps derived from DCA, fed as distance restraints to folding engines like Rosetta, powered the best pre-deep-learning predictors and the trRosetta pipeline.

The limitation was representational: a Potts model is fit *per family*, sees only pairwise statistics, and cannot transfer knowledge across families.

### 2.2 Deep learning enters: distograms and AlphaFold1

AlphaFold1 (CASP13, 2018) replaced hand-rolled restraint generation with a deep residual convolutional network predicting a full **distogram** — a probability distribution over inter-residue distances — plus torsion-angle distributions, from MSA-derived features. Gradient descent on the resulting differentiable potential then folded the chain. It won decisively but remained a two-stage system: features in, potential out, optimization separate.

The field sensed that the MSA featurization was the bottleneck. What if the network could attend over the alignment itself?

## 3 Methodology

AlphaFold2's methodology can be summarized as *iterative, end-to-end refinement of two coupled representations*. The system maintains:

- **MSA representation** $m_{si}$: an embedding for each sequence $s$ at residue position $i$ (a set of aligned sequences × positions).
- **Pair representation** $z_{ij}$: an embedding for each residue pair $(i,j)$, encoding the network's current belief about their spatial relationship.

These are processed jointly by the Evoformer stack (48 blocks), then the structure module converts the pair representation into 3D coordinates, and the whole pipeline is *recycled* — outputs re-fed as inputs — typically three times. Training optimizes a frame-aligned point error (FAPE) loss directly on coordinates, plus auxiliary losses on the distogram, masked-MSA reconstruction, and confidence heads.

The methodological break from AlphaFold1 is completeness: there is no separate folding stage. The network learns to extract coevolutionary signal from raw alignments, reason geometrically about it, and emit coordinates with calibrated uncertainty, all under one objective.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Evoformer: attention over alignments and pairs

The Evoformer is arguably the single most consequential architecture in computational biology since the transformer itself. Each of its 48 blocks updates the MSA and pair representations with a choreography of attention operations [1]:

- **Row-wise gated self-attention with pair bias.** Within each sequence row, residues attend to one another; the attention logits are biased by the pair representation, injecting the current geometric hypothesis into sequence processing.
- **Column-wise gated self-attention.** Across sequences at a fixed alignment column, rows attend to each other — this is the learned generalization of coevolutionary coupling, computed by the network rather than by a Potts fit.
- **Outer product mean.** Each pair $(i,j)$ is updated by the average, over sequences, of the outer product $m_{si} \otimes m_{sj}$ projected linearly. This operation explicitly constructs pairwise statistics from per-residue embeddings — a differentiable, learned analogue of the covariance matrices that DCA models by hand.
- **Triangular updates.** The pair representation is refined by triangle multiplicative updates and triangle self-attention, which enforce a soft form of the triangle inequality: information about pair $(i,k)$ and $(k,j)$ flows into pair $(i,j)$, the exact kind of consistency that a true distance matrix must satisfy.

Conceptually, the Evoformer *learns to do contact prediction inside the folding network*, with the pair representation acting as a working memory of the emerging structure. The ablation record in the paper shows the triangular components and the recycling are among the largest contributors to accuracy — geometric consistency, not just more parameters, drives the gains.

### 4.2 The structure module: invariant point attention and SE(3) frames

The structure module must convert abstract pair embeddings into coordinates, and it must do so in a way that respects the symmetries of physics: rotating the input must rotate the output identically (equivariance), never change it. AlphaFold2 achieves this with **invariant point attention** (IPA).

Each residue is assigned a local frame in the special Euclidean group SE(3): a rotation $R_i$ constructed from backbone atom vectors via Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization, and a translation $x_i$ at the alpha carbon. In IPA, a query residue generates a set of 3D query points in its own local frame; these points are transformed into the global frame and compared against key points generated in the *target* residue's frame. Because attention weights are computed from scalar products of points expressed in a common global frame, they are invariant under any global rigid transformation, while the aggregated output transforms equivariantly [1].

> **Theorem:** *If attention weights are computed from SE(3)-invariant scalar products of frame-local points and the output values are SE(3)-equivariant functions of the frames, then the structure module's coordinate updates commute with arbitrary global rotations and translations of the input — the network's geometry is coordinate-free by construction.*

The module predicts backbone frames directly, then side-chain torsions, and is applied with shared weights across recycling iterations — a form of learned iterative refinement reminiscent of a learned optimizer. The FAPE loss, which aligns predicted and true frames residue-by-residue before measuring deviation, makes the objective itself invariant to global pose.

### 4.3 The MSA Transformer: coevolution as attention

Parallel to AlphaFold2, Rao and colleagues asked a sharper question: what can a transformer learn from alignments *alone*, with no structural supervision? The **MSA Transformer** interleaves row-wise and column-wise attention across an input MSA and trains with a masked language modeling objective — predict randomly masked residues from the surrounding alignment context [3].

The results were striking:

- **Unsupervised contact prediction.** With no structural training, symmetrized attention maps from the model predict residue contacts more accurately than the previous state of the art, with far greater parameter efficiency than single-sequence protein language models.
- **Column attention encodes phylogeny.** A follow-up study showed that column-attention heads capture Hamming distances between sequences — the model disentangles phylogenetic correlation (shared ancestry) from functional covariation (structural contact), the exact confound that has plagued DCA since its inception [3].
- **Parameter sharing across families.** Unlike Potts models fit per family, one set of weights processes all families, enabling transfer of statistical regularities across the protein universe.

The MSA Transformer thus provided the theoretical justification for the Evoformer's column attention: coevolutionary signal is most naturally expressed as attention over alignments, and a network can learn to separate its phylogenetic and structural components.

### 4.4 Sibling architectures: RoseTTAFold and ESMFold

The Baker laboratory's **RoseTTAFold** arrived at a strikingly similar design independently, guided by the sparse architectural hints DeepMind had released. It is a three-track network in which 1D sequence, 2D distance/orientation, and 3D coordinate information flow back and forth, allowing the network to collectively reason about sequence–structure relationships [5]. Its importance was practical and political as much as scientific: open weights and code let the entire community build on the paradigm within weeks, and its three-track formulation became the substrate for RFdiffusion.

**ESMFold** pushed the opposite frontier: what happens with *no* alignment at all? By scaling a single-sequence protein language model (ESM-2, up to 15 billion parameters) and attaching a folding head, the Meta team showed that a sufficiently large language model internalizes enough evolutionary statistics in its weights to fold many proteins without an explicit MSA — and does so an order of magnitude faster [4]. The trade is accuracy on the hardest targets, where deep alignments still dominate. The lesson is architectural: the MSA is a data structure for evolutionary information, and there are two ways to obtain it — retrieve it (AlphaFold2) or memorize a compressed version (ESMFold).

### 4.5 From prediction to design: the RFdiffusion inversion

A structure predictor maps sequence to structure. Invert the arrow and you get design: *which sequences fold into a desired structure?* **RFdiffusion** operationalized this inversion by fine-tuning the RoseTTAFold prediction network as a denoising diffusion model over SE(3) residue frames [8]. Starting from random frames, the network iteratively denoises toward coherent backbones, conditioned on functional requirements — a motif to scaffold, a symmetry to obey, a target to bind. Two technical choices proved decisive: self-conditioning on the previous step's predicted structure (echoing AlphaFold2's recycling), and a mean-squared-error training loss that, being frame-dependent, promotes continuity across denoising steps.

The experimental record — hundreds of characterized designs, a cryo-EM binder structure matching its design model to 0.63 angstroms backbone RMSD — confirmed the deep thesis of the prediction revolution: a sufficiently powerful predictor implicitly encodes a generative model of protein space [8]. Prediction and design are not two fields; they are the forward and inverse maps of one learned energy landscape.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 The CASP14 verdict

CASP is the community's blind experiment: targets are recently solved structures withheld from the PDB, so no method can have trained on them; independent assessors score predictions [6]. At CASP14 the headline numbers were [1]:

| Metric | AlphaFold2 | Next-best method |
|---|---|---|
| Median backbone r.m.s.d.95 (Cα) | 0.96 Å (95% CI 0.85–1.16) | 2.8 Å (95% CI 2.7–4.0) |
| Median all-atom r.m.s.d.95 | 1.5 Å (95% CI 1.2–1.6) | 3.5 Å (95% CI 3.1–4.2) |

For perspective, AlphaFold1's median on the hardest CASP13 targets was 6.6 Å — not competitive with experiment. AlphaFold2 placed nearly two-thirds of its predictions at accuracy comparable to experimental determination, and produced accurate side chains whenever the backbone was accurate. Even on the free-modeling (FM) category — targets with no detectable template, the traditional graveyard of predictors — AlphaFold2 folded more than a third of targets to experimental accuracy, where previous CASPs had seen essentially none [6].

A subtle but crucial result concerned **confidence calibration**: the per-residue pLDDT score correlated tightly with true local accuracy, and the predicted TM-score ranked models reliably. A predictor that knows what it does not know is deployable; one that does not is a curiosity. ColabFold later showed that early stopping on pLDDT ≥ 85 sacrifices little accuracy while cutting compute dramatically, confirming that the confidence head carries genuine signal [7].

### 5.2 Proteome-scale deployment

The CASP result could have been a competition artifact. The human-proteome study answered that: AlphaFold2 predicted full-length chains for the human proteome, with 70% of long multi-domain chains (over 800 residues) achieving TM-score above 0.7 on a template-filtered test set — a stringent regime where inter-domain packing, never directly trained, had to emerge from the architecture [2]. The subsequent AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, built with EMBL-EBI, extended coverage to hundreds of millions of sequences. The epistemic status of "unknown structure" changed permanently: the null hypothesis for a new sequence is now a confident prediction, not a blank.

## 6 Limitations

Honesty requires the failure modes, which the literature documents plainly:

- **Single static conformations.** The network predicts one structure, typically the dominant crystallographic state. Allosteric transitions, intrinsically disordered regions, and folding pathways are outside its training distribution; pLDDT helpfully flags disorder as low confidence, but that is a warning label, not a model of dynamics.
- **MSA dependence.** Accuracy degrades for orphan proteins, designed sequences, and viral proteins with shallow alignments. ESMFold's single-sequence approach mitigates this at the cost of peak accuracy [4].
- **Complexes and context.** The original CASP14 system predicted monomers; multimers, protein–nucleic acid complexes, and ligand-bound states required later extensions, and interfaces remain less reliable than cores.
- **Not a simulator.** High structural accuracy does not imply correct energetics: predicting the *effect* of a point mutation on stability or function remains substantially harder than predicting the wild-type fold, because the training objective rewards coordinates, not free energies.
- **Evaluation gaming risk.** As predictions saturate databases, the boundary between training and test data demands vigilance; CASP's blind protocol remains the gold standard precisely because post-hoc benchmarks on public structures can leak.

## 7 Conclusion

AlphaFold2's triumph decomposes into a small number of ideas with large consequences: treat the alignment as a first-class object and attend over it; maintain a pair representation as the working memory of geometry; generate coordinates with provably equivariant attention; recycle; and predict your own error. The MSA Transformer showed the coevolutionary half of this story could be learned unsupervised [3]; RoseTTAFold proved the architecture was reproducible and extensible [5]; ESMFold mapped the alignment-free frontier [4]; RFdiffusion showed the predictor inverts into a designer [8]; and CASP supplied the blind verdict that turned claims into facts [6].

The deeper lesson is methodological. For fifty years, structure prediction progressed by hand-crafting better approximations to physics — energy functions, fragment libraries, restraint potentials. AlphaFold2 succeeded by learning the *statistics* of the folded universe so completely that physics emerged as a special case: triangle inequality enforced by attention, rigid-body symmetry enforced by construction, thermodynamics approximated by a confidence head. The protein folding problem, as CASP defined it for single chains, is solved. What remains — dynamics, design, function — is harder, and the machinery described here is the foundation on which it will be built.
