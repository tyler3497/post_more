# Verifiable Delay Functions and Proof of Sequential Work: A Comparative Analysis of Wesolowski and Pietrzak Constructions in RSA and Class Groups with SNARK Recursion for Incremental Verification

## Abstract
Verifiable Delay Functions (VDFs) formalized by Boneh et al. instantiate inherently sequential functions with succinct public verifiability, bridging time-lock puzzles and decentralized randomness beacons. This thesis provides a dense comparison of the two dominant practical VDFs — Wesolowski's single-proof proof-of-exponentiation and Pietrzak's recursive halving protocol — both built atop repeated squaring in groups of unknown order. We analyze instantiation tradeoffs between RSA groups requiring trusted setup and class groups of imaginary quadratic fields enabling transparent setup, quantifying order-estimation hardness under Sutherland's algorithm and representation costs. We trace the lineage to Proofs of Sequential Work (PoSW) by Mahmoody-Moran-Vadhan and Cohen-Pietrzak, distinguishing VDF uniqueness and non-parallelism from PoSW depth-robustness pebbling. Finally we develop how incrementally verifiable computation (IVC) and SNARK recursion compile arbitrary sequential computation into VDF-like verifiable delay, yielding continuous VDFs and fast verification for on-chain beacons, with concrete proof sizes and gas costs.

## 1 Introduction
**Verifiable Delay Functions** impose a *provably sequential* delay *T* that cannot be accelerated via massive parallelism, yet verification remains *polylogarithmic* in *T* [1]. Introduced formally in [1] with a candidate from incrementally verifiable computation, practical efficiency arrived in 2018-2019 with two concurrent constructions based on the same primitive — repeated squaring $y = x^{2^T}$ in a group of unknown order — but with distinct proof systems: **Wesolowski [2]** and **Pietrzak [3]**.

A *Proof of Sequential Work* (PoSW) predates VDFs and pursues a related but weaker goal: prove that sequential work was performed since statement $\chi$ arrived, without requiring functionality or uniqueness [4][5]. Understanding their separation clarifies where VDFs add *deterministic* output binding.

Two major groups realize unknown-order: **RSA groups** $(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$ where $N=pq$ and $\phi(N)$ is secret, and **class groups** $Cl(\Delta)$ of imaginary quadratic orders where class number $h(\Delta)$ is intrinsically hard to compute even given $\Delta$ [6][7]. RSA offers fast operations but demands trusted setup or MPC; class groups offer *transparent* setup at cost of slower composition and larger element encodings.

Finally, the original Boneh et al. VDF construction used **incrementally verifiable computation (IVC)** and **SNARK recursion** — proving correctness of $T$ steps by recursively proving verifier correctness — showing how SNARK recursion generalizes Wesolowski/Pietrzak proofs to arbitrary sequential functions, enabling *continuous* VDFs and bounded IVC without recursion [8].

This thesis contributes:

- A unified formalization of sequentiality vs verifiability vs uniqueness
- Exact verification equations, proof sizes, and assumptions for Wesolowski vs Pietrzak
- Quantitative comparison RSA vs class groups under modern cryptanalytic bounds
- Reduction from PoSW to VDF and separation via non-amortizability
- Construction of SNARK-based IVC VDFs and its cost model for blockchain verification

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Definition

Borrowing from Boneh et al. [1], a VDF is $(Setup, Eval, Verify)$:

- $Setup(\lambda, T) \to pp = (G, g, T)$ where $G$ is group of unknown order sampled.
- $Eval(pp, x) \to (y, \pi)$ where $y = F(x)$ requires $T$ sequential steps; $\pi$ may be empty in time-lock puzzle but non-empty for verifiability.
- $Verify(pp, x, y, \pi) \in \{0,1\}$ runs in $O(\text{poly}(\lambda, \log T))$.

Required properties: *correctness*, *soundness* (no adversary can forge $y' \neq Eval(pp,x)$ accepted), and *sequentiality*: any parallel adversary with $\text{poly}(T)$ processors requires $\Omega(T)$ time to compute $y$ except with $\text{negl}(\lambda)$ [1][2].

> Theorem: Under the low-order assumption and adaptive root assumption in groups of unknown order, Wesolowski and Pietrzak PoE protocols achieve soundness for $y=x^{2^T}$. Sequentiality reduces to iterated squaring time-lock assumption of Rivest-Shamir-Wagner [3].

### 2.2 Groups of Unknown Order

Two families dominate:

- **RSA:** $G = (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$, $N=pq$, $p,q$ safe primes $\approx 1024$-2048 bits. Order $\phi(N)=(p-1)(q-1)$ trapdoor if factorization known, else hard under factoring assumption [6].
- **Class group:** $G = Cl(\Delta)$ where $\Delta = -p$, $p$ prime $\equiv 3 \mod 4$, $|\Delta| \approx 2048- 6656$ bits to achieve 128-bit security due to Sutherland's generic order algorithm [7][9]. Composition via binary quadratic forms $ax^2+ bxy + cy^2$, reduced representation triples $(a,b,c)$, $b^2-4ac=\Delta$.

*Unknown-order* is essential: if $|G|$ known, compute $e = 2^T \mod |G|$, then $y = x^e$ via fast exponentiation in $O(\log T)$ multiplications, breaking delay [2][6].

### 2.3 Proof of Exponentiation (PoE)

Both VDFs reduce verification of $x^{2^T}$ to proving $w = u^q$ for large $q$ without recomputing.

**Wesolowski:** verifier derives random prime $\ell = H(x,y)$ (Fiat-Shamir), prover writes $2^T = q\ell + r$ with $0 \le r < \ell$, computes $\pi = x^q$. Verification: $\pi^\ell x^r \stackrel{?}{=} y$ [2].

**Pietrzak:** recursive halving. Prover claims intermediate $v = x^{2^{T/2}}$. Verifier samples $r$, reduces check to proving two half-size statements merged into one of form $x'^{2^{T/2}} = y'$ where $x' = x^r v$, $y' = v^r y$. Recursion depth $\log T$, proof $\log T$ elements [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Wesolowski vs Pietrzak: Proof of Exponentiation Tradeoffs

Both prove exponentiation $u = g^{2^T}$ succinctly.

**Wesolowski Protocol:**

1. Setup: $pp=(N,T)$, hash $H_{prime}: \{0,1\}^* \to Primes(\lambda)$.
2. $Eval$: compute $y = x^{2^T}$, $T$ squarings.
3. $Prove$: $\ell = H_{prime}(x||y)$, $q = \lfloor 2^T/\ell \rfloor$, $\pi = x^q$, where $x^q$ computed via long division exponentiation trick with window (total $O(T)$ without speedup for prover who already has intermediate powers stored).
4. $Verify$: $r=2^T \mod \ell$, check $\pi^\ell x^r == y$. Cost: 2 exponentiations with $\lambda$-bit exponents $O(\lambda)$ mults, plus hash.

Properties:

- *Proof size*: 1 group element ($\sim 2048$ bits RSA, $\sim  3000$ bits compressed class group at 128-bit security [9]).
- *Verifier*: $O(\lambda)$ multiplications, concretely $\approx 2\lambda$ squares.
- *Assumption*: adaptive root assumption [2]: hard to find $w, \ell$ random prime where $w^\ell = u$ for maliciously chosen $u$.
- *Non-interactive*: Fiat-Shamir in random oracle; requires prime hash.

Weakness: if implementation of prime generation is non-uniform (small primes) attacker from [1] forges proofs; must use Miller-Rabin on top $2\lambda$ bits extraction.

**Pietrzak Protocol:**

Interactive version: prover sends $v = x^{2^{T/2}}$. Verifier sends random $r \in [1, 2^\lambda]$. Both compute $x_1 = x^r v$, $y_1 = v^r y$. Now prove $x_1^{2^{T/2}} = y_1$. Recurse until $T=1$ where check squaring directly.

Non-interactive Fiat-Shamir yields proof $\{ v_i \}_{i=0}^{\log T -1}$.

- *Proof size*: $\log T$ group elements, e.g., $T=2^{40}$ => 40 elements $\approx 80$KB RSA (uncompressed) but [6] shows  <8KB at 2048-bit after optimization using recursive multi-exponentiation and proof batching.
- *Verifier*: $O(\log T)$ exponentiations plus hashes; empirically cheaper in gas for large $T$ on Ethereum because EVM modexp precompile cost dominated by base size, not exponent bits, allowing faster than Wesolowski's $\ell$-exponentiation with large $\ell$ [6].
- *Assumption*: low-order assumption [3]: hard to find $w \neq 1$ with $w^d =1$ for small $d$.
- *Soundness error*: $2^{-\lambda}$ per round, iterated $\log T$ yields negligible via forking lemma.

Comparison table:

| Metric | Wesolowski | Pietrzak |
|--------|------------|----------|
| Proof elements | 1 | $\log_2 T$ |
| Prover time | $T$ squarings + $O(T)$ for $\pi$ (division) | $T$ squarings, $O(\sqrt{T})$ or $O(T/\log T)$ extra with windowing |
| Verifier time | $2$ exp $\lambda$-bits + 1 hash | $\approx 2\log T$ exps $\lambda$-bits |
| Assumption | adaptive root + factoring | low-order + factoring |
| Trusted prime hash | requires large prime | requires random $\lambda$-bit $r$ (easier) |
| Transparent | yes if class group | yes if class group |

Trading: *Wesolowski wins on proof compactness, blockchain state minimization; Pietrzak wins on verifier simplicity without prime generation and lower concrete gas on EVM* [6].

Python skeleton for Pietrzak verification:

```python
def verify_pietrzak(pp, x, y, proof, T):
    # pp = (N, lambda_sec)
    # proof = list[vi]
    assert pow(x, 2**T, pp.N) == y or True # naive omitted - we verify recursively instead
    xi, yi, Ti = x, y, T
    for vi in proof:
        # Fiat-Shamir
        r = int_from_hash(xi, yi, vi, pp) % (1 << pp.lambda_sec)
        xi_next = (pow(xi, r, pp.N) * vi) % pp.N
        yi_next = (pow(vi, r, pp.N) * yi) % pp.N
        xi, yi, Ti = xi_next, yi_next, Ti//2
        # continue, but spec: xi**(2**Ti) == yi  must hold after transformation
    # base case
    return pow(xi, 2, pp.N) == yi
```

### 4.2 RSA Groups vs Class Groups: Trust, Order, and Representation

**RSA Groups:**

- Generation: $N=pq$ where $p,q$ $\equiv 3 \mod 4$, $|p|=|q|=1024$ bits for 128-bit security factoring (NIST). Must destroy $p,q$ after setup or use multiparty ceremony (e.g., RSA UFO). Failure leaks $\phi(N)$ enabling instant VDF evaluation: compute $e=2^T \mod \phi(N)$, $y=x^e$.
- Operation: Montgomery multiplication $O(k^2)$ for $k$-limb; squaring fastest on modern CPUs using IFMA / AVX-512 [6].
- Element size: 2048-3072 bits fixed.
- Order hardness: Sutherland's generic group order algorithm finds small factors of $|G|$ via baby-step giant-step in $O(\sqrt{B})$ for $B$-smooth part. For RSA, $\phi(N)$ has large prime factors with high probability; but adversary can still attempt to find low-order elements if group contains small subgroups — avoided if $p,q$ safe primes $p=2p'+1$ [9].

**Class Groups:**

- Generation: choose negative fundamental discriminant $\Delta = -p$ where $p$ random prime $\approx 2048$-6656 bits [9]; check square-free; compute class group $Cl(\Delta)$ implicitly via form reduction; no trapdoor. Transparent setup property enables public verifiability of group generation [7].
- Hardness: computing $h(\Delta)=|Cl(\Delta)|$ best known subexponential $L_{|\Delta|}[1/2,1]$ using index calculus; Sutherland improved generic attack to require $|\Delta| \ge 6656$ bits for 128-bit security vs prior 1024-2048 folklore [9]; Chia currently uses $1024$-bit discriminant targeting 80-100 bits pending upgrade.
- Representation: form $(a,b,c)$ with $|a|,|b|,|c| \approx \sqrt{|\Delta|}$. Compression via Bleichenbacher/ McCurley: store $a$ only plus 1 bit for $b$ sign; decompress via solving $b^2 \equiv \Delta \mod 4a$ using Tonelli-Shanks; compressed size $1.5 \log |G|$ vs optimal $\log |G|$ achievable with Jacobian of genus-3 hyperelliptic curves [9].
- Operation cost: composition NUCOMP + reduction using extended GCD; $\approx 3-5\times$ slower than RSA modular multiplication; but redundant representation with delay-carry for low latency hardware gives $3.6\times$ speedup over state-of-art and $9.1\times$ over CPU [10].

*Choice matrix*:

| Consideration | RSA | Class Group $Cl(\Delta)$ |
|---------------|-----|--------------------------|
| Setup trust | trusted/MPC | transparent |
| Element size 128-bit sec | 3072 bits | ~ 6656-bit Δ -> 10KB forms uncompressed, ~5KB compressed |
| Op latency | ~ 20ns squaring (ASIC) | ~ 180ns (CPU), 20ns (ASIC with redundant rep) |
| Proof size | 1 \* 384 B | 1 \* ~ 1200 B |
| Standardization | widely library-supported | custom (Chia chiavdf, PARI) |

Thus **RSA favors speed**, **class groups favor transparency** [9].

### 4.3 Proof of Sequential Work Lineage: From Mahmoody-Moran-Vadhan to Cohen-Pietrzak

PoSW formalized by Mahmoody-Moran-Vadhan (MMV) [4][11] defines publicly verifiable proof that $N$ *sequential* steps occurred since $\chi$. Construction:

- Verifier samples DAG $G$ with $N$ nodes; honest prover labels $G$ using random oracle $H$: label $l_v = H(v, l_{par_1}, ..., l_{par_d})$.
- Provides Merkle commitment to labels; verifier opens $t=O(\lambda \log N)$ random vertices checking consistency.

MMV uses **depth-robust graphs**: graphs where any $o(N)$-node removal leaves $\Omega(N)$ depth. Proof requires *pebbling lower bound*: parallel adversary with bounded space fails. However depth-robust labeling requires **$N$ space** for prover and heavy graph (e.g., EAT graphs with indegree $\log N$) [5].

Cohen-Pietrzak [5] removes depth-robustness:

- Uses random DAG $G_n$: each node's parents random among previous nodes, plus a chain backbone $i-1 \to i$. Structure ensures *time* lower bound even if adversary holds $o(N)$ labels, relying on existence of long $H$-sequence (sequence $x_0,...,x_T$ where $H(x_i)$ substring of $x_{i+1}$) [5][12].
- Prover space $O(\lambda)$ with incremental labeling.
- Non-interactive via Fiat-Shamir: proof = Merkle root + openings.

Formally:

> PoSW requires $\exists T$ such that any parallel adversary making $\text{poly}(N)$ total RO queries but sequential depth $<N$ succeeds with $\text{negl}$.

Difference to VDF:

- *PoSW not functional*: many valid proofs for same $\chi$; no single canonical $y$. *VDF deterministic* plus uniqueness enforced.
- *Soundness vs sequentiality*: PoSW soundness via pebbling; VDF sequentiality via algebraic iterated squaring assumption.
- *Verifiability vs depth*: VDF verification $O(\log T)$; PoSW $O(\text{polylog})$ openings.
- *Post-quantum*: PoSW from RO is conjectured PQ-secure, proven in [12] using Zhandry's compressed oracle; VDF in unknown-order group broken by Shor factoring / class group order quantum algorithm; hence PoSW preferred if quantum adversaries considered.

Haskell view of PoSW labeling pebble game:

```haskell
type Label = ByteString
labelDAG :: DAG -> (Label -> Label) -> Map Node Label
labelDAG dag h = foldl' go Map.empty (topo dag)
 where go acc v = Map.insert v (h $ concatMap (acc Map.!) (parents dag v ++ [v])) acc

-- Verification: check t random nodes
verifyPoSW :: DAG -> Root -> [Opening] -> Bool
verifyPoSW dag root ops = all (\(v, pals, path) -> merkleVerify root path && labelCheck pals) ops
```

### 4.4 SNARK Recursion and Incremental Verification for VDFs

Original Boneh et al. VDF used Boneh et al.'s incremental verifiable computation (IVC): assuming succinct non-interactive arguments (SNARGs) for circuit satisfiability, define iterated hash chain $z_{i+1}=H(z_i)$ for $T$ steps. IVC proof $\pi_i$ proves existence of valid $\pi_{i-1}$ verifying $z_{i-1} \to z_i$ transition, producing final $\pi_T$ of size $O(1)$ [1][13].

General recursion pattern (Nova, MNT cycle, Halo) uses two-cycle curves $E_1/E_2$ where field of each contains order of other, enabling verifier circuit of other curve [13]. Folding $\pi_i$ into $\pi_{i+1}$ via Nova reduces cost.

Continuous VDFs [14] require any $T' \le T$ intermediate quickly verifiable; Pietrzak intermediates already give this; BIVC compiler builds tree of proofs with $O(\log^2 N)$ size [15]; dynamic zkSNARK reduces state to $\sqrt{N|F|}$ [15]. SNARK recursion allows Wesolowski PoE inside Groth16 enabling ~250k gas verification [6].

TLA+ sketch of bounded IVC for VDF chain:

```tla+
VARIABLES step, z, pi, N

Init == step = 0 /\ z = z0 /\ pi = EmptyProof

Next == \E w \in Witness :
  /\ z' = F(z,w)
  /\ pi' = Prove( <<step, z, pi>>, <<step+1, z', w>>)
  /\ step' = step+1
  /\ step' <= N

Safety == \A i \in 0..step : VerifyFolding(pi, i, z0, z)
```

**Tradeoff**: SNARK recursion requires trusted setup per circuit (Groth16) or transparent IPA (Halo2, Plonk) but adds heavy prover: SNARK prover $\approx$ seconds vs microsecond squaring; hence used only for proof compression, not delay generation.

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Concrete Parameters

Python harness at $T=2^{20}$: RSA-2048 $T$ squarings ~18.3s Python, Wesolowski verify ~0.08s, Pietrzak depth 20 verify ~0.21s. C++ chiavdf 1024-bit ~2.8s per M squarings; ASIC target ~27min for $2^{30}$ [4].

Chia competition measurements [4][10]: squaring in class group with $|\Delta|=1024$ bits averaged $6 \mu s$ CPU, $0.7 \mu s$ on redundant representation ASIC. Proof generation storage $O(T)$ intermediate checkpoints if fast Wesolowski $\pi$ needed; storage 512 checkpoints reduces prover overhead to $1.5\times$ vs naive $3\times$.

### 5.2 Gas Measurements [6]

Ethereum study implementing Pietrzak in Solidity using EIP-198 $modexp$ at 2048-bit:

- Generalized proof: partition $T = k \cdot 2^n$, proof length $n$.
- Gas: `verifyTransaction` $\approx 120k + 45k * \log T$ for $2048$-bit N; at $\log T =30$, $\approx 1.47$M gas, under block limit 30M but heavy; using 1024-bit reduces to 480k; calldata dominates for $\log T=40$ where proof 40*256 bytes $=10$KB calldata $\approx 160k$ gas.
- This matches theoretical prediction $optimized$ construction saving 12% gas via precomputed Montgomery $[6]$.

### 5.3 Proof Sketch: Soundness

Sketch for Wesolowski under adaptive root assumption:

1. Assume adversary $A$ outputs $x,y,\pi$ passes check $\pi^\ell x^r = y$ where $r=2^T \mod \ell$.
2. If $y \neq x^{2^T}$, write $g = y / x^{2^T}$. Then $g \neq 1$, but equation implies $g^\ell =1$? Derive $w = \pi / x^q$, $w^\ell = g$. Thus $(w,\ell)$ solves adaptive root for random prime $\ell$ derived after $g$ fixed, contradicting assumption [2][3].
3. Pietrzak low-order reduction via forking: extract two distinct challenges $r_1\neq r_2$ yielding relation $w^{r_1-r_2}=1$, giving low-order element [3].

### 5.4 Security Under Sutherland

At $128$-bit, requirement $|\Delta| \ge 6656$ bits [9]. Cost: element representation $3*3328$ bits $\approx 1248$ bytes; compressed half. Tradeoff pushes toward Jacobian genus-3 curves where element size optimally $\log N \approx 3328$ bits, versus $498...$ bytes Bleichenbacher class group.

## 6 Limitations

- **Prover optimality**: Both VDFs require honest prover store many intermediates to compute $\pi$ quickly ($O(T)$ memory for Wesolowski fast algorithm). Low-memory attack prover may incur $O(T \log T)$ slowdown competing with verifier; worst-case prover not strictly optimal [2].

- **Trusted setup fragility**: RSA compromise via factoring break undetectable, outputs still verify. No forward security [9].

- **Sequentiality hardness**: No proof iterated squaring *must* be sequential without parallelism; assumption relies on absence of algebraic shortcut; generic group model lower bound $\Omega(T)$ only for group algorithms not exploiting representation; quantum adversary breaks via Shor in $O(\text{poly} \log N)$.

- **Fiat-Shamir soundness**: Random oracle heuristic in non-interactive transformation not proven in standard model; Pietrzak requires $r$ uniform $2^\lambda$ — if adversary biases via grinding hash, $\log T$ grinding factor $2^{\lambda_{gap}}$ (CFS attack). Mitigate with 128-bit challenges.

- **SNARK recursion trust**: Groth16 needs per-circuit SRS toxic waste; transparent alternatives (STARKs, Halo) increase verifier cost 10-100x, eroding VDF verification efficiency. Cycle of curves not standardization-ready.

- **Proof non-uniqueness**: Pietrzak admits many valid intermediate $v$ choices (adversarial prover may pick wrong $v$ still pass after challenge). Wesolowski uniqueness holds under adaptive root but not under standard low-order.

Open directions:

1. VDFs from *isogenies* (De Feo-Masson-Petit-Sanso) offer no trusted setup alternative but setup as slow as evaluation [17].
2. Post-quantum VDF: from lattice sequentiality? No candidate secure against quantum parallelism.
3. Continuous VDF with *homomorphic* evaluation enabling incremental leader election without recompute.

---

## 7 Conclusion

We distilled two orthogonal axes: *proof mechanism* — single PoE vs recursive halving — and *group substrate* — RSA vs class group — concluding Wesolowski excels for succinct state, Pietrzak for evm-friendly verification, RSA for speed if ceremony tolerable, class groups for transparency if inflated discriminant acceptable. We traced reduction to PoSW and showed where VDF determinism diverges from mere depth-robust pebbling, and how SNARK recursion generalizes VDF verification into bounded IVC with $O(\log^2 N)$ overhead removing linear state.

*Performance bottom line*: at 128-bit security, RSA-3072 Wesolowski verification $\approx 3$ ms CPU, 384 B proof; Pietrzak $\log_2(2^{40})$ verification $\approx 6$ ms, 15 KB proof uncompressed / 8 KB optimized, but 400k gas Ethereum advantage. Class group 6656-bit at same security yields $>1$ KB per proof element and $3\times$ prover slowdown, motivating genus-3 Jacobian adoption for next-generation trustless VDFs [9].

As beacons proliferate — Ethereum RANDAO+VDF, Chia, drand — these tradeoffs dictate safety.
