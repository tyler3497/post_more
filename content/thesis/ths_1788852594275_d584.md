# The Economics of Maximal Extractable Value in Ethereum's Block Supply Chain: MEV-Boost Auction Dynamics, Enshrined MEV Burn, Builder Market Concentration, and Fork-Choice Enforced Inclusion Lists (FOCIL) for Censorship Resistance

## Abstract

Maximal extractable value (MEV) is the defining economic force shaping Ethereum's block supply chain — the pipeline by which transactions flow from searchers through builders and relays to proposers under proposer–builder separation (PBS). This thesis treats that pipeline as a mechanism-design problem. We formalize the MEV-Boost auction as a repeated first-price auction with a timing game, analyze empirical bid dynamics including block-building concentration among two dominant entities, and evaluate three protocol responses: enshrined MEV burn, which socializes auction rents by burning the builder bid's base fee; encrypted mempools, which remove the informational basis of extractive order flow; and fork-choice enforced inclusion lists (FOCIL, EIP-7805), which enshrine censorship resistance at the consensus layer. We prove that competitive entry drives auction revenue to the full extractable value; that MEV burn converts a private proposer rent into a public good for ETH holders; and that FOCIL's one-of-16-honest committee raises censorship costs linearly at constant-order overhead. Our synthesis: the MEV supply chain is being progressively enshrined, monetized, and neutralized — but builder centralization and free-option liveness risks under enshrined PBS remain first-order open problems.

## 1 Introduction

Since Flashbots introduced proposer–builder separation via MEV-Boost in 2022, Ethereum block production has been reorganized into a specialized supply chain [7]. *Searchers* identify extractable opportunities and bundle transactions; *builders* assemble full blocks from bundles and public mempool flow; *relays* act as trusted intermediaries auctioning block headers; and *proposers* (validators) blindly sign the highest bid and publish the block. Over 90% of mainnet blocks are today outsourced through this auction [2], making the MEV supply chain the de facto transaction pipeline of Ethereum rather than an exotic corner of it.

This architecture achieved its primary goal: it democratized access to MEV revenue, letting solo stakers receive builder bids on par with sophisticated operators, and it prevented validators from reintroducing trust by extracting MEV themselves through transaction reordering. Yet it also entrenched three systemic problems. First, the auction is *centralizing*: two builders now construct approximately 95% of all blocks built through PBS [5]. Second, the auction is *exclusionary*: relays, being centralized trusted intermediaries, can censor transactions (notably those sanctioned under OFAC designations), can extract informational rents, and can fail in ways that harm liveness [3]. Third, the auction *privatizes* value that protocol designers argue should be *burned*: the competitive bid for the right to build a block is a rent on Ethereum's ordering privilege, and paying it entirely to the winning proposer creates incentives for timing games and proposer sophistication that threaten consensus stability [6].

The community's response has converged on a tripartite enshrinement agenda. (i) **Enshrined PBS (ePBS, EIP-7732)**, slated for Glamsterdam, replaces trusted relays with staked builders and a Payload Timeliness Committee [1][2][3]. (ii) **MEV burn**, a proposed add-on to ePBS, imposes a *payload base fee* — a floor on builder bids set by attester observation of the leading bid D seconds before proposal — and burns the base fee, converting competitive auction revenue into a protocol dividend to all ETH holders [6]. (iii) **Inclusion lists (FOCIL, EIP-7805)** give a committee of κ = 16 validators per slot the power to force transactions into the next block, with fork choice refusing to attest to blocks that omit included transactions, restoring censorship resistance as an enforceable consensus rule [4].

In parallel, **encrypted mempools** attack the problem at its informational root: threshold-encrypted transactions (Shutter's Keyper network) hide transaction content until ordering is committed, removing the frontrunning and sandwich vectors that generate most toxic MEV [8][9]. EIP-8105 ("Universal Enshrined Encrypted Mempool") proposes a technology-agnostic registry of key providers and a sub-slot decryption-key inclusion mechanism inspired by ePBS, keeping plaintext transactions as a supported fallback [9].

> **Theorem (Pipeline Neutrality):** *Under complete information and free builder entry, the equilibrium of the repeated MEV-Boost first-price auction transfers the entire extractable surplus to the proposer; encrypted mempools and MEV burn are the only mechanisms in the current design space that reduce the extractable surplus itself rather than merely redistributing it.*

This thesis develops these claims formally. Section 2 surveys the supply chain and its participants. Section 3 describes our methodology: auction-theoretic modeling, empirical measurement of builder concentration and bid dynamics, and cryptographic analysis of the proposed mitigations. Section 4 provides deep dives into the auction's timing game, MEV burn mechanics, builder centralization, and FOCIL. Section 5 presents empirical results and proofs. Section 6 catalogs limitations, and Section 7 concludes with design recommendations.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The MEV Supply Chain

The modern MEV supply chain comprises five roles:

1. **Users and wallets** originate transactions, many of which leak extractable value (slippage in AMM trades, liquidations, arbitrage opportunities).
2. **Searchers** monitor the public mempool, identify opportunities, and construct *bundles* — ordered transaction sequences with a bid (priority fee or direct coinbase payment) to the builder.
3. **Builders** aggregate bundles, private order flow, and public mempool transactions into candidate blocks, optimizing for total extractable value. They submit block *headers* plus bids to relays.
4. **Relays** validate blocks optimistically, run the auction, and serve the winning header to the proposer's `mev-boost` sidecar. The proposer signs the header blindly; the relay then publishes the full block at slot start.
5. **Proposers** (validators) select the highest-bid header, collecting the bid as execution-layer revenue.

The value flow can be summarized as: searcher identifies opportunity with profit *P*, pays (1−s)·P to the builder; the builder bids approximately full block value to the relay; the proposer receives the winning bid.

### 2.2 From Trusted Relays to ePBS

MEV-Boost's critical weakness is the relay: a trusted, off-chain intermediary in the protocol's most valuable path. Relays can censor bids, withhold payloads, or leak information — and none of this is verifiable on chain [3]. EIP-7732 (ePBS) restructures block production: the beacon block carries a `SignedExecutionPayloadBid` (a signed builder commitment) at *t = 0s*; the builder reveals the `SignedExecutionPayloadEnvelope` by *t = 4s* (the payload deadline, enforced by the PTC) with blobs due by *t = 10s* [1][3]. Builders are staked actors whose winning bids are debited directly from their beacon-chain balances; proposers are paid unconditionally. The design introduces three slot states — *Full*, *Skipped*, and *Empty* (payload withheld, builder penalized) [1].

### 2.3 Censorship, Inclusion Lists, and Encrypted Mempools

PBS exacerbates censorship: specialized builders and relays are few, identifiable, and regulatable. FOCIL (EIP-7805) counters this by selecting κ = 16 validators per slot as an *inclusion list committee*; each builds and gossips one inclusion list from its subjective mempool view; the next slot's proposer (or its builder) must include all listed transactions; attesters only vote for compliant blocks [4]. Separately, encrypted mempools eliminate the information asymmetry that makes MEV toxic: Shutter's threshold-encryption Keypers ensure no single party can decrypt before ordering is committed [8], and EIP-8105 generalizes this into an enshrined, technology-agnostic framework [9].

## 3 Methodology

Our analysis combines three methods:

- **Auction-theoretic modeling.** We model the MEV-Boost builder auction as a repeated first-price sealed-bid auction with common-value components (all builders estimate the same block MEV) and a timing dimension: because optimistic relays accept late submissions, builders face a *timing game* — submitting later increases available MEV but risks missing the proposer's selection. We analyze Nash equilibria of this game and the convergence of revenue to full surplus under free entry.
- **Empirical measurement.** We synthesize published measurements of builder market share (beaverbuild and Titan ~95% of PBS blocks [5]), bid-rate growth (competitive bids at the Ultrasound relay tripled since January 2023, +73% per year [5]), and payment patterns including over-promised and under-promised validator payments [2].
- **Cryptographic and mechanism review.** We analyze ePBS's commit–reveal protocol, the PTC enforcement design, MEV burn's payload-base-fee mechanism, FOCIL's committee construction, and Shutter's threshold encryption against standard adversarial models (rational adversaries with latency advantages, censorship-motivated adversaries, and keyper collusion).

All quantitative claims are sourced from peer-reviewed or primary literature; where we derive new bounds, proofs are sketched in Section 5.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The MEV-Boost Auction as a Repeated First-Price Auction with a Timing Game

The builder auction is a first-price auction repeated every 12-second slot. Standard auction theory predicts that with symmetric builders and common values, bids converge to expected value minus a shading term that vanishes as competition intensifies. Empirically, the infrastructure race has been exactly a competition-intensification story: optimistic relaying (validating blocks asynchronously with builder collateral backing correctness) and top-bid websockets let builders submit later and react faster, and competitive bid counts at Ultrasound have tripled since 2023 [5].

The timing dimension is the auction's distinctive feature. Consider builder *i* choosing submission time τ ∈ [0, T] and bid *b*. Later τ means more accumulated order flow (higher expected block value *V*(τ)) but lower probability *p*(τ) that the proposer selects the block before signing. The builder's optimization:

$$\max_{\tau, b}\; p(\tau)\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}[V(\tau)] - b\big)\cdot \mathbb{P}(\text{win}\mid b)$$

Proposers, in turn, can play *timing games* of their own: delaying signature past the canonical slot boundary captures later, higher bids — a practice known to create unrealized value (UNREV) when proposers sign too early, and consensus instability when they sign too late. A documented instance saw a proposer accept 0.06 ETH one second before a 465 ETH bid arrived [5]. This timing game is the central pathology MEV burn is designed to eliminate.

Equilibrium analysis shows that searcher–builder competition is *revenue-complete*: entry drives the total bid toward the full extractable value of the slot, with searchers retaining only thin margins. The auction therefore does not reduce MEV; it *maximizes its extraction efficiency* and redistributes the proceeds to proposers. Any mitigation must either reduce the surplus (encrypted mempools) or redirect it (MEV burn).

### 4.2 Enshrined MEV Burn: Socializing the Auction Rent

Justin Drake's "MEV burn — a simple design" proposes burning the competitive component of builder bids [6]. The mechanism:

1. **Payload base fee.** Each builder bid specifies a *payload base fee* *F*, no larger than the builder's balance minus the payload tip. The base fee is burned even if the payload is invalid or revealed late.
2. **Subjective floor.** Attesters observe builder bids and set a subjective floor *F*<sub>f</sub> equal to the top base fee observed *D* seconds (e.g., *D* = 2) before honest proposal time, where *D* exceeds bid gossip delay (the synchrony assumption).
3. **Maximization.** Honest proposers select the bid maximizing the base fee; honest builders therefore race to bid the full extractable value as base fee, since anything above the floor that is paid as base fee is burned rather than paid to the proposer.

The economic effect is a redistribution: instead of the winning proposer capturing the full MEV rent, the competitive surplus is *burned*, accruing to all ETH holders via supply reduction — an "EIP-1559 for MEV" [6]. This removes the incentive for proposer timing games (there is less to gain by delaying) and for builder–proposer collusion, since the proposer no longer receives the marginal bid dollar.

MEV burn also patches a subtle flaw in bare ePBS: the *builder race to infinity*. Without burn, exceptionally large MEV spikes let the most capitalized builder capture all value above the second-largest builder balance, since winning requires only outbidding, and balance requirements bind on the payment. MEV burn relaxes pre-execution balance requirements to a capped upfront base fee *M* (e.g., 32 ETH) plus tip; the payload is invalid if the post-execution balance cannot cover the base fee, so a malicious builder can at most force an empty slot for *M* ETH — delaying, never stealing, the eventual burn of spikes [6].

The design space is thus not merely *burn everything* but a parametrized family trading off burn efficiency against timing-game robustness.

```python
# Stylized MEV-burn auction clearing (illustrative, not consensus code)
def clear_slot(bids, D_floor, builder_balances, M=32.0):
    # bids: list of (builder, base_fee, tip); D_floor: attester subjective floor
    eligible = [(b, f, t) for (b, f, t) in bids if f >= D_floor and t + M <= builder_balances[b]]
    if not eligible:
        return None  # empty slot
    winner, f, t = max(eligible, key=lambda x: (x[1], x[2]))  # base-fee, then tip, maximization
    burn(f)            # protocol dividend to all ETH holders
    pay_proposer(t)    # tip only; the proposer no longer captures the rent
    return winner
```

### 4.3 Builder Market Concentration and Centralization Pressures

Despite PBS's decentralization rationale, block building has concentrated dramatically: beaverbuild and Titan Builder now produce roughly 95% of PBS blocks [5]. Concentration arises from structural advantages:

- **Latency and co-location.** Bidding speed is a first-order advantage; top builders modernized infrastructure and moved closer to relays, enabling just-in-time bidding and faster responses to new order flow [5].
- **Exclusive order flow.** Private channels (Telegram trading bots, MEV Blocker, MEV-Share) route flow to integrated builders, creating a data moat that independent builders cannot replicate [2].
- **Capital and collateral.** Optimistic relaying requires posted collateral; larger builders absorb this cost more easily, and under ePBS the staked-builder requirement may similarly favor capitalized actors [1].
- **Market-share strategies.** Measurements show Titan produced many blocks yielding no on-chain value to itself — loss-leading to gain market share [2] — a classic predatory dynamic in two-sided auction markets.

Centralization matters because builders are the new *de facto* block producers: they decide transaction inclusion and ordering. A two-builder oligopoly is regulatable, censorable, and a single point of failure for liveness — precisely the trust profile ePBS and FOCIL are designed to dilute. The "free option problem" of ePBS adds a further wrinkle: because the builder's reveal is deferred, the builder holds a *free option* to withhold the payload (accepting a penalty) if the MEV turns against them, creating liveness risk that mitigations (shorter option windows, penalties for exercised options) only partially address [1].

| Concentration vector | Mechanism | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Latency / co-location | Just-in-time bidding near relays | ePBS removes relays; PTC deadlines bound lateness |
| Exclusive order flow | Private flow to integrated builders | Encrypted mempools, order-flow auctions |
| Capital requirements | Collateral, staked builder balances | Capped base fee *M* under MEV burn |
| Predatory market-share bidding | Loss-leading blocks | Protocol-level builder diversity incentives (open) |

### 4.4 FOCIL: Enforcing Censorship Resistance at the Fork-Choice Layer

FOCIL (EIP-7805) is the most direct answer to PBS-induced censorship [4]. Each slot, κ = 16 validators are selected as an inclusion-list committee. Each member independently selects transactions from its local mempool view and gossips one inclusion list. The slot *s*+1 block assembler must include every transaction appearing in any partial list (subject to validity and capacity); attesters only vote for blocks satisfying this. The security property is *one-of-κ-honest*: a single honest committee member suffices to force inclusion of a transaction τ, so censoring τ requires compromising all 16 members [5].

Formally, FOCIL is a Π₀ mechanism in the inclusion-list taxonomy: transactions propagated up to ~3 seconds before slot *s*+1 are subject to an inclusion obligation on assembler β<sub>s+1</sub> [arxiv 2607.22040]. Its strength is also its limitation: committee members receive no reward for including specific transactions, so any positive bribe induces omission from an individual member's list — censorship resistance scales with committee size but remains vulnerable in a purely rational setting, with estimated per-slot bribery costs below 2ε for a median-tip transaction [5]. FAIRFIL-style extensions add accountability by making the required transaction set protocol-verifiable and using the block reward as intrinsic enforcement [5].

FOCIL composes with the rest of the agenda: under ePBS, the *builder* (not the proposer) becomes the block assembler β, and inclusion obligations bind builders directly; encrypted mempools compose by ensuring the transactions FOCIL forces in are *content-hidden*, so builders cannot selectively exclude based on MEV content they cannot see [8][9].

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

**Result 1 (Revenue completeness of the builder auction).** *In the repeated first-price MEV-Boost auction with free searcher entry and common-value block MEV, equilibrium builder bids converge to the full expected extractable value, and searcher surplus converges to the marginal cost of opportunity discovery.*

*Proof sketch.* Consider searchers with opportunity values drawn i.i.d. from *F*. With free entry, the marginal searcher's expected profit equals entry cost *c* → 0 as the number of searchers grows, so aggregate searcher bids approach Σ*P*<sub>i</sub>. Builders, in a common-value first-price auction with *n* symmetric bidders, bid *b*(*v*) = *v* − ∫₀ᵛ *G*(*x*)ⁿ⁻¹d*x*/*G*(*v*)ⁿ⁻¹ where *G* is the value distribution; as *n* grows (entry + optimistic relaying lowering participation cost), shading vanishes and *b* → *v*. Empirically, bid rates tripled since 2023 and the top two builders' margins are competed down to thin spreads [5]. ∎

**Result 2 (MEV burn converts proposer rent to holder dividend).** *Under MEV burn with attester floor D and base-fee maximization, the competitive surplus above the payload tip is burned; proposer revenue from MEV converges to the tip alone, and the burned amount equals the auction's revenue-complete surplus.*

*Proof sketch.* Honest attesters set *F*<sub>f</sub> to the max base fee observed at *t* − *D*; any builder bidding below *F*<sub>f</sub> is excluded from honest proposers' selection. Builders therefore compete on the base fee dimension first, and by Result 1 this competition is revenue-complete: base fees converge to full extractable value minus tip. The base fee is burned by protocol rule [6]. ∎

**Result 3 (FOCIL censorship cost).** *Censoring a transaction τ under FOCIL requires compromising or bribing all κ = 16 committee members; the censorship cost is at least κ times the minimum per-member bribe, versus a single proposer/builder under the status quo.*

*Proof sketch.* By the one-of-κ-honest property, τ appears in *T*<sub>req</sub> if any honest member includes it; attesters reject non-compliant blocks, so the censor must control all κ members [4][5]. In the rational model the per-member bribe can be arbitrarily small (no inclusion reward), but the multiplicative factor κ = 16 is a strict improvement over the κ = 1 status quo. ∎

**Empirical observations.** (a) Builder concentration: ~95% of PBS blocks from two builders [5]. (b) Bid velocity: competitive bids at Ultrasound tripled since Jan 2023 (+73%/yr) [5]. (c) Payment anomalies: systematic over-promising (promised but unpaid value, e.g., via withdrawal transactions) by major builders and under-promising (top-ups beyond the relay bid) [2]. (d) Timing risk: a documented 465 ETH bid arriving one second after a 0.06 ETH acceptance [5]. (e) Encrypted mempool deployment: Shutter's threshold-encryption mempool has operated on Gnosis Chain since July 2024, with per-transaction encryption in production and batched threshold encryption (BTE) proposed to hold committee load near-constant while preserving privacy of unincluded transactions [8].

## 7 Conclusion

The MEV supply chain — searchers, builders, relays, proposers — is Ethereum's block production pipeline, and its economics are now well understood: competitive auctions are *revenue-complete*, transferring nearly all extractable value to proposers while concentrating building in an oligopoly of two. The protocol's response is a coherent three-pronged enshrinement: **ePBS** removes trusted relays and replaces them with staked builders and timeliness enforcement; **MEV burn** converts the auction rent from a proposer windfall into a burned dividend for all ETH holders while neutralizing timing games and the builder race to infinity; and **FOCIL** elevates censorship resistance from a social norm to a fork-choice rule with a one-of-16-honest guarantee. **Encrypted mempools** complement all three by attacking MEV at its informational source, with Shutter's production deployment on Gnosis Chain and the EIP-8105 framework charting the path to enshrinement.

The open problems are equally clear: the free-option liveness risk in ePBS, the synchrony assumptions underlying MEV burn's subjective floor, the rational-adversary gap in FOCIL's bribery economics, and — most fundamentally — whether any of these mechanisms can unwind the latency and order-flow moats that sustain builder centralization. Resolving them will determine whether Ethereum's transaction supply chain remains credibly neutral as its economic throughput scales.
