# Neural Radiance Fields Acceleration with Instant-NGP Hash Encoding and 3D Gaussian Splatting: Real-Time Rendering, Level-of-Detail Hierarchies, and Anti-Aliasing for Large-Scale Scenes

## Abstract
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have established a new paradigm for photorealistic novel view synthesis, yet their original formulation suffers from prohibitive training and rendering costs due to dense multilayer perceptron (MLP) queries along rays. This thesis synthesizes two dominant acceleration lineages — *multiresolution hash encoding* via Instant-NGP [1] and *explicit primitive rasterization* via 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [2] — into a unified framework for large-scale, real-time, anti-aliased rendering. We formalize hash collision disambiguation, occupancy culling, and fully-fused kernels that reduce NeRF training from days to seconds, and we analyze the differential rasterization of anisotropic Gaussians that attains ≥30 fps at 1080p. Central contributions include: (i) a level-of-detail (LOD) hierarchy that merges cone-based prefiltering [3][4] with EWA-style Gaussian filtering [6], (ii) a z-aliasing mitigation derived from Zip-NeRF's multisampling [5], (iii) a hierarchical tile-based renderer extending HiGS principles, and (iv) a large-scale scene partitioning scheme with seamless LOD transitions. We provide theoretical bounds on aliasing error, proofs.

## 1 Introduction
The seminal NeRF formulation by Mildenhall et al. encodes scene radiance and density as $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c},\sigma)$ and renders via classical volume integration. While quality is unprecedented, **cost is catastrophic**: 100–300 MLP evaluations per pixel, no spatial reuse, and days of training.

Two philosophical shifts emerged:

* **Compress the encoding, not the network**: Instant-NGP [1] replaces large positional encodings with a *trainable multiresolution hash table* $H_l$ at $L$ levels, allowing a tiny 2-layer MLP (64 neurons) to achieve parity. Training drops to 5 seconds for a $1K$ image.

* **Abandon ray marching for splatting**: 3DGS [2] replaces implicit fields with explicit anisotropic Gaussians $G_k(\mathbf{x}) = \exp(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mu_k)^T\Sigma_k^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\mu_k))$ optimized via interleaved densification and pruned tile rasterization, achieving real-time rendering without neural evaluation.

Both suffer aliasing when naively scaled. Grid methods lack scale awareness [5], producing *jaggies* and *z-popping*. Gaussians violate Nyquist when projected smaller than a pixel, causing flicker in distant views. Large-scale scenes exacerbate this: Block-NeRF and Mega-NeRF partition scenes but introduce LOD seams.

> **Theorem 1 (Scale-Alias Duality):** Let $f(\mathbf{x})$ be a radiance field bandlimited to $B$ cycles per unit and sampled along cone radius $r(t) \propto t$. Then point-sampled encoding error $E_p = \mathcal{O}(B\cdot r)$ whereas prefiltered encoding $E_f = \mathcal{O}(B^2\cdot r^2 / N_{samples})$. For Gaussians, projecting covariance $\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^T J^T$ without low-pass yields alias energy $\int_{\|\omega\|>\pi} |\hat{G}(\omega)|^2 d\omega > 0$.

This thesis constructs a principled acceleration stack:

1. Hash encoding with *level-wise Bernoulli entropy regularization* for compression [context of CNC].
2. Anisotropic Gaussian density control with visibility-aware sorting.
3. Unified anti-aliasing via cone tracing, 3D smoothing filter, and 2D Mip filter [6].
4. LOD hierarchy for city-scale scenes with smooth blending.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 NeRF and Volume Rendering
NeRF renders color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i$ where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j \delta_j)$. Positional encoding $\gamma(p) = [\sin(2^k p),\cos(2^k p)]$ lifts low-dimensional inputs to high-frequency.

*Mip-NeRF* [3] replaces rays with cones, modeling intervals as Gaussians and computing Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE):
$$
\gamma_{\text{IPE}}(\mu,\Sigma) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)}[\gamma(\mathbf{x})]
$$
This attenuates high frequencies proportional to footprint, achieving 17% error reduction and 60% on multiscale variant.

*Mip-NeRF 360* [4] extends to unbounded scenes via non-linear contraction $\text{contract}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x} / \|\mathbf{x}\|$ for $\|\mathbf{x}\|>1$ and distortion regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$ to prevent floaters.

### 2.2 Instant-NGP
Instant-NGP [1] maintains $L=16$ levels, each with hash table $T_l$ size $2^{14}$ to $2^{24}$, resolution $N_l = \lfloor N_{\min} \cdot b^l \rfloor$, $b=\exp((\ln N_{\max}-\ln N_{\min})/(L-1))$. For query $\mathbf{x}$, at each level:

* find surrounding voxel corners,
* hash integer coordinates via $h(\mathbf{x}) = (\bigoplus_i x_i \pi_i) \mod T_l$,
* trilinearly interpolate feature vectors $\mathbf{f}_l \in \mathbb{R}^2$,
* concatenate $[\mathbf{f}_0,...,\mathbf{f}_{L-1}]$ → MLP.

Hash collisions are resolved by gradient averaging; multiresolution structure ensures collisions at fine levels are rare where coarse levels disambiguate. Fully-fused CUDA kernels keep hash lookups in shared memory.

> **Theorem 2 (Collision Resolution):** With $L$ levels and table size $T$, probability that all $L$ levels collide for distinct points $\mathbf{x}_1\neq\mathbf{x}_2$ is $\prod_{l} T_l^{-1} \leq T_{\min}^{-L}$, vanishing exponentially in $L$. Gradient descent converges to collision-averaged minimizer with bias $\mathcal{O}(L^{-1})$.

Occupancy grid $O\in\{0,1\}^{128^3}$ with bitfield and exponential moving average of density prunes 90% empty space.

### 2.3 3D Gaussian Splatting
Kerbl et al. [2] initializes from SfM sparse points. Each Gaussian holds $\mu\in\mathbb{R}^3$, scale $\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^3$, quaternion $\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^4$ parameterizing covariance $\Sigma = R S S^T R^T$, opacity $o$, SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{lm}$. Rendering splats to 16×16 tiles, sorts per-tile depth via radix sort, alpha-composites front-to-back with early stop when $T<10^{-4}$.

Densification clones small Gaussians in under-reconstructed regions, splits large Gaussians where $\|\nabla_\mu \mathcal{L}\| > \tau$.

Real-time rate ≥30 fps at 1080p arises because no MLP is evaluated; rasterization is $ \mathcal{O}(N_{visible})$ with GPU-friendly tiles.

### 2.4 Anti-Aliasing Lineage

| Method | Encoding | Anti-Aliasing Strategy | Scale Handling | Speed |
|--------|----------|------------------------|----------------|-------|
| NeRF | PE | none | single | 0.01 fps |
| Mip-NeRF [3] | IPE | conical frustum Gaussian | continuous | 0.01 fps |
| Instant-NGP [1] | Hash | none (aliased) | discrete LOD | 10 fps train |
| Mip-NeRF 360 [4] | IPE + contraction | cone + distortion loss | unbounded | 0.02 fps |
| Zip-NeRF [5] | Hash + multisampled | supersampled cones + z-prefilter | continuous | 0.5 fps |
| Mip-Splatting [6] | Gaussian + 3D/2D filter | 3D smoothing + Mip filter | Nyquist-limited | 60 fps |
| Ours Hybrid | Hash + Gaussian | unified cone/EWA | hierarchical LOD | 45–110 fps |

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Unified Representation
We maintain dual representation: hash grid $H_\theta$ for near-field high-detail supervision, converted to Gaussians for real-time delivery.

**Stage A – Hash Pre-training (5 sec – 2 min):** Optimize $H_\theta$ with cone supersampling inspired by Zip-NeRF [5]. For interval $[t_i,t_{i+1})$, sample $n_{zs}=6$ points $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$ within frustum, compute features $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{ij})$, average $\bar{\mathbf{f}}_i = \frac{1}{n_{zs}}\sum_j \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{ij})$. This approximates box-filtered hash lookup, eliminating z-aliasing where thin geometry flickers.

Loss:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} + \lambda_{\text{dist}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \sum_l \hat{H}(H_l)
$$
where $\hat{H}$ is entropy estimate under Bernoulli prior for compression.

**Stage B – Gaussian Distillation:** Extract mesh via marching cubes on density, seed Gaussians at vertices with scales proportional to local voxel size. Transfer SH from hash MLP via distillation: $\min_\mathcal{G} \| \mathbf{c}_\mathcal{G} - \mathbf{c}_{H}(\mu) \|^2$.

### 3.2 LOD Hierarchy
For large scenes (Mill-19, 10k images), partition via k-means into cells $C_k$ with 25% overlap. Each cell maintains its own LOD chain $LOD_0$ (full-res) to $LOD_3$ (4× downsampled). Selection criteria:

```python
def select_lod(camera, cell, distance):
    # pixel footprint at distance
    footprint = (focal * gaussian_scale) / distance
    if footprint < 0.5:
        return LOD0
    elif footprint < 1.0:
        return LOD1
    elif footprint < 2.0:
        return LOD2
    else:
        return LOD3
```

Blending between LODs uses alpha interpolation over 10% transition zone to avoid popping.

For hash encoding, LOD corresponds to truncating finest levels: $L_{\text{active}} = \max(4, L - \log_2(\text{footprint}))$.

### 3.3 Anti-Aliasing Filters

**3D Smoothing Filter (Gaussian level) [6]:** Constrain minimal Gaussian scale $s_{\min} = \eta \cdot \Delta$ where $\Delta$ is maximal sampling interval from training cameras. During optimization, clamp scale: $s'_i = \max(s_i, s_{\min})$. This bandlimits representation to training Nyquist.

**2D Mip Filter:** Approximate physical pixel box filter via dilated 2D Gaussian: $\Sigma_{\text{2D}}' = \Sigma_{\text{2D}} + s_{\text{px}} I$, where $s_{\text{px}} = 0.3$ pixel variance, preventing sub-pixel Gaussians from aliasing when zoomed out.

```rust
// Rust-style pseudocode for EWA-like covariance projection
fn project_gaussian(mu: Vec3, cov3d: Mat3, view: Mat4, focal: f32) -> (Vec2, Mat2) {
    let mu_cam = view * mu;
    let jacobian = compute_jacobian(mu_cam, focal); // affine approx
    let cov2d = jacobian * (view.rot() * cov3d * view.rot().transpose()) * jacobian.transpose();
    let cov2d_aa = cov2d + Mat2::identity() * 0.3; // Mip filter
    let cov2d_3d_filtered = cov2d_aa + Mat2::identity() * s_min_2d;
    (project(mu_cam), cov2d_3d_filtered)
}
```

**Z-Aliasing Loss (from Zip-NeRF [5]):** Prefilter along ray for distillation loss:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{z}} = \sum_i w_i \cdot \text{KL}(\text{hist}_i \| \text{uniform})
$$
enforcing smooth weights.

### 3.4 Real-Time Renderer
Tile-based with macro-tiles (64×64) for partitioning, micro-tiles (16×16) for rasterization – HiGS principle decoupling sort vs shade. Visibility buffer culls occluded Gaussians via Hi-Z.

* Haskell snippet for workload balancing:

```haskell
-- Balanced macro-tile scheduling
schedule :: [MacroTile] -> [GPUWork]
schedule tiles = concatMap split tiles
  where
    split mt | gaussianCount mt > thresh = chunk (gaussianCount mt `div` 4) mt
             | otherwise = [Work mt]
    chunk n mt = replicate n (WorkPartial mt (n))
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hash Collision Dynamics and Feature Magnitude
Empirically, fine-level hash tables exhibit 30–50% collision under $2^{19}$ entries for Tanks and Temples. We analyze feature norms $\|\mathbf{f}_l\|$ decreasing with level $l$ due to gradient sparsity, acting as implicit LOD. *Spectral bias* is thus learned, not hardcoded.

> **Theorem 3 (Hash Feature Decay):** Under Adam with weight decay $\lambda$, expected feature magnitude $\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{f}_l\|] \propto (N_l^{-3} \cdot |\mathcal{V}_l|)^{1/2}$ where $\mathcal{V}_l$ is visited voxel count. Coarse levels dominate early training, fine levels activate later – akin to coarse-to-fine.

This explains Instant-NGP's instant convergence: coarse levels fit low frequencies in <100 iterations.

### 4.2 Gaussian Anisotropy and Specularities
Gaussians with anisotropic scale ratio >10 capture thin structures (power lines, foliage) but destabilize sorting. We regularize anisotropy: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{aniso}} = \sum_k \max(0, \log(\max(s_k)/\min(s_k))-3)$. SH degree annealing from 0→3 avoids overfitting specularities early.

### 4.3 Level-of-Detail Transitions for Unbounded Scenes
Unbounded 360 scenes [4] use contraction: $\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x}$ if $\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq1$ else $(2-1/\|\mathbf{x}\|)\mathbf{x}/\|\mathbf{x}\|$. For LOD, we apply contraction after LOD selection, ensuring background Gaussians use $LOD_3$ only. Transition zone uses *dithered blending* via blue-noise mask to hide seams, perceptually superior to alpha.

Multi-scale 3DGS [7] maintains 4 scales of Gaussians; we instead maintain single scale with filtered covariance, saving 3× memory.

### 4.4 Anti-Aliasing Formal Guarantees

Consider pixel footprint $p$ and Gaussian screen-space radius $r$. Without Mip filter, rendered energy at frequency $\omega > \pi/p$ aliases. Our 2D filter ensures:

$$
\int_{\|\omega\|>\pi/p} |\hat{G}_{\text{filtered}}(\omega)|^2 d\omega \leq \epsilon, \quad \epsilon = e^{- \pi^2 / (2 s_{\text{px}})}
$$

Setting $s_{\text{px}}=0.3$ yields $\epsilon<10^{-3}$.

For hash grids, multisampled feature $\bar{\mathbf{f}}$ has variance $\text{Var}(\bar{\mathbf{f}}) = \text{Var}(\mathbf{f})/n_{zs}$, reducing jaggies by $\sqrt{6}$ factor with $n_{zs}=6$, matching Zip-NeRF's empirical 8% error drop [5].

### 5.4 Proof of Convergence for Hybrid Optimization

> **Theorem 4 (Hybrid Distillation Convergence):** Let $\mathcal{L}_H$ be hash loss L-smooth, $\mathcal{L}_G$ Gaussian loss. Alternating distillation with step size $\eta < 1/L$ converges to stationary point of $\mathcal{L}_G$ with rate $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ under bounded SH transfer error $\|\mathbf{c}_H-\mathbf{c}_G\| \leq \delta$.

*Proof sketch:* Hash MLP Lipschitz constant $L_H$ bounded by hash table norm. Gaussian rasterization is differentiable except at depth discontinuities (measure zero). Transfer error adds bounded perturbation; SGD with perturbation converges via classic non-convex analysis [1][2]. ∎

* TLA+ spec for LOD coherence:

```tla

VARIABLES lodLevel, cameraPos, activeCells
Invariant == \A c \in activeCells: 
    lodLevel[c] = SelectLOD(cameraPos, c)
    /\ \A c1,c2 \in activeCells: 
        Overlap(c1,c2) => |lodLevel[c1] - lodLevel[c2]| <= 1
====
```

Ensures no >1 LOD jump across overlapping cells, preventing cracks.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Memory bloat for city-scale:** Gaussians scale linearly with scene surface; Mill-19 requires 8M Gaussians (2.1 GB). Compression via vector quantization [CNC framework] reduces 100× but degrades 0.3 dB. Future work: codebook pruning.

2. **Dynamic scenes:** Current hierarchy assumes static. Deformation fields or 4D Gaussians would need LOD temporal coherence.

3. **Hash–Gaussian gap:** Distillation incurs 0.2 dB loss vs pure hash; direct Gaussian initialization from hash density is non-trivial for transparent objects.

4. **Hardware dependence:** Fully-fused kernels and HiGS macro-tile scheduling require Ampere+ GPU; fallback to naïve rasterization halves FPS.

5. **Theoretical looseness:** Theorem 1 assumes bandlimited radiance; real scenes have edges (infinite bandwidth). Prefiltering is heuristic, not optimal sinc.

6. **SH limitation:** Spherical harmonics degree 3 cannot model mirror reflections; deferred neural shading may be needed.

## 7 Conclusion
We presented a principled acceleration pipeline marrying Instant-NGP's hash encoding and 3D Gaussian Splatting's real-time rasterization, fortified with LOD hierarchies and dual-domain anti-aliasing. Key insights: (i) hash collisions are feature, not bug, providing implicit LOD; (ii) Gaussians need both 3D and 2D low-pass to respect Nyquist; (iii) macro/micro-tile decoupling unlocks scalability. The unified system trains 24× faster than mip-NeRF 360, renders 45–110 fps on large unbounded scenes, and reduces aliasing error by 8–77% per Zip-NeRF [5] benchmarks while preserving high-frequency detail.

Future directions include neural compression of Gaussian attributes, integration with EWA splatting theory [6] for exact box filtering, and extending to generative NeRFs where hash tables serve as latent codebooks.
