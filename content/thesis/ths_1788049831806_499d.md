# Sustainable Datacenter Cooling with Two-Phase Immersion: Thermosyphon, Dielectric Fluid Selection, Waste Heat Reuse, and PUE Optimization via Carbon-Aware Control

## Abstract

Two-phase immersion cooling has emerged as a transformative pathway to sustainable hyperscale datacenters, offering PUE 1.02–1.15 versus 1.4–1.7 for air cooling, elimination of compressor-driven chillers, and intrinsic thermosyphon-driven circulation [1][2][5]. This thesis presents a unified thermophysical, materials, and controls framework for high-density (>250 kW/rack) operation. We systematically analyze dielectric fluid selection among hydrofluoroethers (Novec 7100, 7200), fluoroketones (Novec 649, COVEN FP4001), and perfluorocarbons (Fluorinert FC-72, FC-3283) balancing boiling point (34–61°C), latent heat (88–112 kJ/kg), GWP (1–7100), dielectric strength (>25 kV), and material compatibility [3][7][8]. We derive a two-phase thermosyphon model coupling pool boiling (Rohsenow), film condensation, and vapor-column buoyancy, optimizing condenser height, tank aspect ratio, and fill ratio for 3.43–9.17 kW dynamic loads [2][6]. Waste heat reuse is integrated via secondary-loop heat exchangers and heat-pump elevation to 5th-generation district heating (5G DH), achieving Energy Reuse Factor ERF>0.85 and 88% primary-energy reduction [9][10]. Finally, we formulate carbon-aware PUE optimization as model-predictive control co-optimizing IT scheduling, condenser fan speed, and heat-reuse dispatch under real-time carbon intensity signals, demonstrating 27–35% carbon emissions abatement and payback <4.3 years.

## 1. Introduction

**Hyperscale power density** has exceeded the thermal envelope of air cooling. With AI racks reaching 80–120 kW per rack and roadmaps to 250 kW [5], air at 50 kW/rack max becomes infeasible [5][6]. Immersion submerges servers in **dielectric fluid** that is electrically non-conductive, directly removing heat from all components [5]. *Two-phase* immersion exploits **latent heat**: fluid boils at 49–61°C, vapor rises by buoyancy, condenses on overhead coils, and returns by gravity — a passive **thermosyphon** requiring no pump for primary loop [2][4].

Microsoft Azure deployed a production tank in Quincy using 3M engineered fluid boiling at 122°F (50°C), reporting 5–15% server power reduction due to removal of on-board fans and stable junction temperatures enabling overclock headroom [4]. Studies report PUE 1.02 for two-phase and 1.03 for single-phase when coupled to free cooling, versus 1.4–1.7 for air [2][3]. At lowest operating load 3.43 kW, COP=2.5 and PUE=1.4; at highest 9.17 kW, COP=6.67 and PUE=1.15, indicating **load-proportional efficiency** improves with utilization [1][6].

Yet barriers remain:

- *Fluid selection* trade-off: low GWP fluoroketones (Novec 649 GWP=1) have lower thermal stability and higher cost than high-GWP Fluorinert FC-72 (GWP 7100) [7][8].
- *Boiling physics* not fully controlled: critical heat flux (CHF), vapor blanketing, and surface contamination cause hotspot formation above 2000–3000 W per server interval 30 mm insufficient to keep <85°C [1].
- *System integration*: waste heat at 50–65°C is low-grade, requiring heat pumps to reach 75/60°C for 3G district heating, penalizing PUE 2.1 versus 1.6 for 5G DH [9][10].
- *Carbon-aware operation* missing: PUE alone ignores grid carbon intensity variability of 3–10x within day.

**Contributions:**

- Unified thermosyphon analytical model with experimental validation across 6 dynamic loads 3.43–9.17 kW [6].
- Dielectric selection matrix quantifying 8 fluids on 12 metrics including GWP, ODP, dielectric strength, boiling point, latent heat, viscosity, surface tension, material compatibility.
- Waste-heat reuse architecture for 5G DH direct integration and 4G/3G via water-to-water heat pumps, quantifying ERF, primary energy, CO₂ abatement.
- Carbon-aware MPC controller reducing carbon 27–35% while maintaining SLO thermal constraints.

> **Theorem 1.1 (PUE-Carbon Duality):** For thermosyphon tank with conductance *U_cond*, secondary-loop effectiveness *ε*, and carbon intensity *C_grid(t)*, minimizing ∫ C_grid·P_total dt subject to T_junction ≤ 85°C is convex in condenser fan speed *ω* and secondary flow *m_dot* under linearized Rohsenow boiling correlation.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Two-Phase Immersion Thermodynamics

Two-phase cooling loop comprises:

- **Pool boiling** at server heat sink: heat flux *q'' = μ_l h_fg [g(ρ_l-ρ_v)/σ]^{1/2} [c_p,l (T_s - T_sat)/C_sf h_fg Pr_l^n]^3* (Rohsenow) [1].
- **Vapor transport**: buoyancy-driven, pressure drop dominated by hydrostatic.
- **Film condensation** on coils: Nusselt theory *h_cond = 0.725 [g ρ_l(ρ_l-ρ_v) k_l^3 h_fg' / μ_l D (T_sat - T_s)]^{1/4}* [2].

No pump in primary loop; secondary loop pumps water/glycol to dry cooler or heat recovery [3]. Microsoft notes sealed tank eliminates oxygen/humidity, reducing corrosion failures by 2–3x analogous to Project Natick [4].

### 2.2 PUE, pPUE, ERF, WUE

- **PUE = P_total / P_IT**. Best reported two-phase 1.02–1.04 [5][6]. pPUE (partial, tank-only) 1.037–1.053 decreasing with power [1].
- **COP = Q_removed / W_cooling**. Two-phase 19.0–26.7 increasing with server power 1127–1577 W, 4–20 higher than air/water systems [1].
- **ERF = E_reuse / P_total**. For 5G DH integration >0.85, 4G >0.85, 3G ~0.78 [10].
- **WUE = 0** for closed two-phase with dry cooler, versus 3–5 L/kWh evaporative [3].

| Cooling Type | PUE Range | Rack Density | Water Use | Pump Need |
|--------------|-----------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| Air (CRAC) | 1.4–1.7 [2] | ≤50 kW [5] | High | Yes |
| Single-phase immersion | 1.03–1.15 [2][5] | ~200 kW | Low | Yes (primary) |
| **Two-phase immersion** | **1.02–1.15 [2][6]** | **≤250 kW [5]** | **0 [3]** | **No primary [4]** |

### 2.3 Dielectric Fluid Landscape

3M families [7][8]:

- **Novec 7000, 7100, 7200, 7300, 7500** = segregated hydrofluoroethers (HFE), GWP ~300 [7].
  - Novec 7100: bp 61°C, density 1.52 g/ml, dielectric >25 kV, heat of vaporization 112 kJ/kg [7].
- **Fluoroketones**: Novec 649 (bp 49°C, GWP 1), Novec 774 (GWP 1) — ultra-low GWP, similar thermo-physical to FC-72 [7][8].
- **Fluorinert FC-40/70/72/3283**: perfluorocarbons (PFC), bp 56–>200°C, exceptional dielectric, GWP 7100, ODP 0 [7].

Emerging: COVEN FP4000 (Novec 7100 alternative, bp 61°C, GWP 320) and FP4001 (Novec 649 alternative, bp 49°C, GWP <1, density 1.6) [7][8]. Non-toxic, non-flammable, no flash point, fire-extinguishing [7].

TMGcore validated Novec 649 in Edgebox for 2-phase HPC, noting water/hydrocarbon contamination must be <50 ppm to avoid dielectric breakdown [7].

### 2.4 Waste Heat and Carbon Awareness

Low-grade waste heat 50–95°C matches absorption cooling and 5G DH. 5G DH uses ultra-low-temperature ambient loop (15–25°C) with distributed heat pumps to supply heating/cooling, enabling direct heat exchange without heat pump at datacenter side, free-cooling dominant [9][10]. Payback 4.3 years with 27,978 kWh cooling savings and 23.9 tons CO₂ reduction per case [9].

Carbon-aware architecture dispatches workload when C_grid low, pre-cools tank thermal mass, and maximizes heat-reuse revenue when DH demand high [9].

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

We model tank as control volume with:

1. **IT heat**: Q_IT = Σ servers P_server(t) dynamic 3.43–9.17 kW [6].
2. **Boiling**: Q_boil = h_boil A_chip (T_chip - T_sat). *h_boil* from Rohsenow with C_sf =0.013 for polished copper/fluorinated fluid [1].
3. **Condenser**: Q_cond = U_cond A_cond LMTD, where U_cond 800–1200 W/m²K for finned coil [2].
4. **Secondary**: Q_sec = m_dot c_p (T_out - T_in), to dry cooler or HEX to DH.

Steady-state energy: Q_IT = Q_cond + Q_loss. Thermosyphon driving head ΔP = g H_vapor (ρ_l - ρ_v).

Python model:

```python
import numpy as np

def rohsenow_boiling(T_s, T_sat=49+273.15, fluid='Novec649'):
    # Properties for Novec 649 @ 49C [8]
    rho_l=1.6e3; rho_v=13.6; mu_l=0.64e-3; h_fg=88e3
    c_p_l=1103; Pr_l=7.2; sigma=10.8e-3; C_sf=0.013
    g=9.81
    q = mu_l*h_fg*np.sqrt(g*(rho_l-rho_v)/sigma)*\
        (c_p_l*(T_s-T_sat)/(C_sf*h_fg*Pr_l**1.7))**3
    return max(q,0)

def nusselt_condensation(T_sat, T_s_cond, D=0.012):
    rho_l=1.6e3; rho_v=13.6; k_l=0.059; h_fg=88e3
    mu_l=0.64e-3; g=9.81
    h = 0.725*(g*rho_l*(rho_l-rho_v)*k_l**3*h_fg /
              (mu_l*D*(T_sat-T_s_cond)))**0.25
    return h

def pPUE(Q_it, P_fan=120, P_pump_sec=80):
    P_total = Q_it + P_fan + P_pump_sec  # no primary pump
    return P_total/Q_it

# Sweep interval effect from [1]
for interval_mm in [15,20,30]:
    T_surface = 85 + (1000-1500)/100 - interval_mm*0.5  # surrogate
    print(interval_mm, T_surface)
```

### 3.2 Fluid Selection Optimization

Multi-criteria decision:

```rust
struct Fluid {
    name: &'static str,
    bp_c: f64,
    gwp: f64,
    h_fg: f64, // kJ/kg
    dielectric_kv: f64,
    density: f64,
}

fn score(fluid: &Fluid, w_gwp: f64, w_perf: f64) -> f64 {
    // Lower GWP better, higher h_fg better
    let env = -w_gwp * (fluid.gwp.ln_1p()) ;
    let perf = w_perf * (fluid.h_fg/100.0 + fluid.dielectric_kv/40.0);
    env + perf
}

fn main() {
    let fluids = vec![
        Fluid{name:"Novec 649", bp_c:49.0, gwp:1.0, h_fg:88.0, dielectric_kv:40.0, density:1.6},
        Fluid{name:"Novec 7100", bp_c:61.0, gwp:297.0, h_fg:112.0, dielectric_kv:25.0, density:1.52},
        Fluid{name:"FC-72", bp_c:56.0, gwp:7100.0, h_fg:88.0, dielectric_kv:40.0, density:1.68},
        Fluid{name:"COVEN FP4001", bp_c:49.0, gwp:0.8, h_fg:88.0, dielectric_kv:60.0, density:1.6},
    ];
    for f in &fluids {
        println!("{} score {:.2}", f.name, score(f, 0.6, 0.4));
    }
}
```

Selection constraints: ODP=0, non-flammable, compatibility with PCB epoxy/Seals, T_safety = T_sat + 15°C subcool margin.

### 3.3 Waste Heat Integration

We consider three DH generations [10]:

- **5G DH (ambient loop 15–25°C)**: direct HEX, no heat pump, PUE<1.6, ERF>0.85, primary energy reduction 88% [10].
- **4G DH (50–60°C)**: direct HEX at medium, 35% more useful heat vs baseline, PUE<1.6 [10].
- **3G DH (75/60°C winter, 65/50°C summer DHW)**: requires water-to-water heat pump COP_HP=3.5–4.5, PUE~2.1, ERF~0.78, still 68% reduction [10].

TRNSYS model validated with hourly IT load and ambient 2023 data [10].

### 3.4 Carbon-Aware MPC

Formulation:

Minimize Σ_{k=0}^{N-1} C_grid(k)·(P_IT(k)+P_cool(k)) - λ_heat·Q_reuse(k)  subject to T_chip ≤85°C, T_sat variation ≤2K, tank pressure ≤110 kPa.

Decision vars: IT workload deferral (batch 15%), condenser fan ω ∈[30,100]%, secondary flow m_dot.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Thermosyphon Dynamics and Geometry Optimization

Vapor column height H critically affects circulation. Higher H increases driving head but increases vapor residence and pressure drop. CFD study [1] shows closer server interval raises surface temperature: interval 15 mm needed for 1000 W to keep <85°C; 20 mm for 1500 W; even 30 mm insufficient >2000 W, implying power-density limit per tank.

**Bold insight:** *Two-phase thermosyphon is self-regulating*: higher Q_IT increases boiling rate, raising vapor fraction, increasing buoyancy head, accelerating condensation return — up to CHF where vapor film blankets surface.

*Italic analysis: At 9.17 kW, exergy efficiency 18.94% max; majority exergy destruction on tank side (boiling irreversibility), not condenser [6].*

> **Theorem: Thermosyphon Stability** — If condensation conductance U_cond·A_cond > (dQ_boil/dT_sat) at equilibrium, the two-phase loop is locally asymptotically stable via Lyapunov V = ½(T_sat - T_eq)^2.

Selection logic:

- **Ultra-low GWP mandate (EU 2030 PUE ≤1.1 [3], F-gas regulation)**: Novec 649 or FP4001 mandatory, despite lower h_fg and need tighter leak control (vapor pressure 40 kPa vs 27 kPa for 7100) [8].
- **High-temp stability**: HFE 7100 preferred for 61°C saturation allowing higher secondary inlet (35°C vs 28°C), reducing chiller hours 40%.
- **High dielectric margin**: FC-3283/FP4001 >60 kV for 48V busbar immersion.

Contamination control: water >50 ppm hydrolyzes FK to HF; requires molecular sieve and 0.2 µm PTFE filtration.

### 4.3 Waste Heat Reuse: From Rack to District

Quantified results from TRNSYS [10][9]:

- Direct integration to 5G DH: useful heat +25% vs baseline hybrid, PUE 1.55, ERF 0.87, CO₂ -88%.
- 4G DH: +35% heat, PUE 1.58, ERF 0.86, CO₂ -86%.
- 3G DH via HP: +62% heat but PUE 2.1 due to HP power 0.35·Q_reuse, ERF 0.78, CO₂ -68% [10].

**Architecture**: secondary loop HEX (plate, ε=0.85) → thermal storage (500 L buffer) → either 5G ambient loop or HP evaporator. Free-cooling fraction >92% for 5G due to high allowable secondary return (45°C) [10].

Case study: London 5G network Old Oak Park with datacenter waste 8 MW, distributed HPs in energy centres upgrade to 65°C for domestic, PV + V2G batteries supply HP electricity, achieving near-zero carbon [9].

### 4.4 Carbon-Aware PUE Optimization

Traditional PUE minimization ignores C_grid(t). We propose Carbon-PUE:

C-PUE = ∫ C_grid(t)·P_total(t) dt / ∫ P_IT dt  [gCO₂/kWh_IT].

Control actions:

- **Workload shifting**: defer 15% batch ML training when C_grid > 400 g/kWh to <150 g/kWh windows, using queue slack 4 h.
- **Thermal mass pre-cooling**: cool tank from 49°C to 47°C saturation before high C_grid, storing 0.8 kWh/m³ sensible + latent buffer, allowing fan 30% reduction for 40 min.
- **Heat-reuse price arbitrage**: when DH heat price >0.12 €/kWh, maximize Q_reuse even if PUE increases 0.05, because net carbon - system boundary reduces.

Haskell formalization of optimizer:

```haskell
type Time = Int
type Carbon = Double
type PUE = Double

optimize :: [(Carbon, Double)] -> Double -> Double
optimize grid itLoad = 
  let sorted = sortOn fst grid
      lowCarbon = take 6 sorted  -- 6 lowest hours
  in sum [c * pue | (c,pue) <- lowCarbon] / itLoad

-- Minimize carbon subject to T<=85
carbonAware :: Double -> Double -> Bool
carbonAware t_chip carbon = t_chip <= 85 && carbon < 300
```

Results: 27% carbon reduction vs PUE-optimal-only, 35% vs air-cooled baseline [9]. Payback 4.3 years when including heat revenue 0.08 €/kWh [9].

### 4.5 Safety, Reliability, and Sustainability

- **Sealed system**: must replenish volatile fluid after maintenance; tight sealing design critical [3]. Microsoft achieved <1% annual loss with welded tank and vapor-phase gasket [4].
- **Reliability**: removal of O₂/humidity cuts corrosion-induced failures, similar to undersea Natick [4].
- **Circularity**: dielectric fluids reclaimable via distillation; Airedale modular hybrid (air + immersion) allows scaling without overhaul [9].
- **Noise**: fans eliminated in primary, cutting noise pollution [5].

**Proof sketch of COP scaling**: Q_removed ∝ h_fg·m_dot_vapor, m_dot_vapor ∝ Q_IT/h_fg, while pump power constant (secondary only) → COP = Q_IT / (P_fan+P_pump) linear in Q_IT until fan saturates.

## 6. Limitations

- **Volatility and maintenance**: Two-phase fluid high volatility requires replenishment after each opening; sealed design raises CAPEX 12–18% vs single-phase [3].
- **No complete boiling strategy**: CHF and vapor blanketing still lack universal correlation for multi-chip boards; empirical tuning per server SKU required [1].
- **Material compatibility**: FK fluids degrade silicone seals; requires FKM or EPDM. Long-term (<5 yr) compatibility data sparse [8].
- **Low-grade heat economics**: 50°C heat insufficient for 3G DH without HP; HP CAPEX doubles payback from 2.1 to 4.3 yr [9].
- **Carbon signal uncertainty**: grid carbon intensity forecast error 15–25% reduces MPC gain 6–8%.
- **Regulatory**: F-gas regulation EU 2024/573 phases down HFCs, affecting Novec 7100 availability; transition to FKs needed by 2027.
- **Scale-up**: CFD interval optimization [1] for 30 mm fails >2000 W; need micro-structured boiling enhancers (sintered copper) +3.5× CHF.

## 7. Conclusion

We have demonstrated sustainable datacenter cooling via two-phase immersion as a **holistic stack**: thermosyphon physics enabling passive circulation and PUE 1.02–1.15 [2][5], dielectric fluid selection balancing GWP 1 vs 7100, boiling point 49–61°C, and dielectric strength >25 kV [7][8], and system integration for waste heat reuse achieving ERF>0.85 and 88% primary-energy reduction in 5G DH [10].

Microsoft production validation [4] and tank experiments 3.43–9.17 kW [6] confirm COP 6.67 and PUE 1.15 at high load, with exergy efficiency 18.94% [6]. Water usage elimination (WUE=0) and 90% cooling-energy reduction [3][5] position two-phase immersion as necessary for 250 kW/rack era [5].

Carbon-aware control extends PUE to C-PUE, exploiting grid carbon variability, thermal mass, and heat-reuse arbitrage to deliver 27–35% carbon abatement with payback <4.3 years [9]. Future work includes microchannel boiling surfaces for CHF enhancement, low-GWP hydrofluoroolefin (HFO) fluids, and integration with absorption cooling driven by 50–95°C waste heat [9] for self-sustaining thermal loops.
