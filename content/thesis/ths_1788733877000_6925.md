# AlphaFold and the Protein Folding Problem: Evoformer Attention, Invariant Point Attention, and Diffusion-Based Structure Prediction

## Abstract

The protein folding problem — predicting a polypeptide's three-dimensional conformation from sequence — stood for half a century as computational biology's canonical challenge. AlphaFold2 resolved it at atomic accuracy in CASP14, reaching 0.96 Å median backbone RMSD [1][6]. This thesis analyzes the architecture behind that result: the Evoformer's triangle-consistent attention over MSA and pair representations, invariant point attention (IPA) achieving rigid-body invariance in the structure module, and AlphaFold3's diffusion-based generative head for protein–ligand and protein–nucleic-acid complexes [3]. We trace the lineage through RoseTTAFold's three-track network [4] and ESMFold's single-sequence language-model paradigm [5], examine CASP benchmarks, pLDDT confidence calibration, the 214-million-structure AlphaFold Database, and the failure modes — disorder, dynamics, orphan proteins — that delimit the current frontier, closing with formal remarks on equivariance guarantees.

## 1 Introduction

For more than fifty years, the mapping from one-dimensional amino-acid sequence to three-dimensional molecular structure resisted algorithmic attack. Anfinsen's thermodynamic hypothesis asserted that the native conformation is the global minimum of a free-energy landscape, yet Levinthal's paradox — the combinatorial explosion of backbone torsion angles — made direct minimization intractable for all but the smallest chains. Physics-based methods (fragment assembly, molecular dynamics, *ab initio* folding) achieved partial successes, and homology modelling exploited evolutionary information, but the biennial Critical Assessment of Structure Prediction (CASP) consistently revealed a stubborn accuracy plateau: median GDT_TS scores for free-modelling targets hovered near 40 throughout CASP11–13 [6].

The inflection arrived at CASP14 (2020), where AlphaFold2 produced structures "competitive with the best experimental results," with almost two-thirds of targets predicted within ~1 Å of the experimental backbone [1][2]. The advance was not a bigger database or a deeper network in the conventional sense; it was an architecture that internalized the *geometry* of protein structure. Three innovations bear the weight of the result: (i) the **Evoformer**, a transformer operating jointly over a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) and a pair representation of residue–residue geometry, with attention patterns constrained by triangle-inequality-like consistency; (ii) **invariant point attention (IPA)**, a spatial attention mechanism that is provably invariant to global rigid-body transformations, enabling the structure module to reason directly in 3D; and (iii) **recycling**, an iterative refinement loop that deepens the network without additional parameters, echoing the iterative refinement of physics-based solvers.

Subsequent systems generalized the paradigm. AlphaFold-Multimer extended the architecture to hetero-oligomeric complexes [7]. AlphaFold3 replaced the Evoformer with the lighter Pairformer and introduced a diffusion module that directly generates atomic coordinates for proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, ions, and post-translational modifications [3]. RoseTTAFold demonstrated that a three-track architecture could recover much of AlphaFold2's accuracy in an open-science framework [4], while ESMFold showed that a massive protein language model could dispense with MSAs entirely, trading ~10% accuracy for ~60× speed [5].

> **Theorem (informal):** *The AlphaFold2 structure module is end-to-end invariant under global rigid-body transformations of its coordinate predictions, while being locally expressive enough to refine each residue's frame toward its native pose.*

This thesis substantiates that claim architecture by architecture, derives the geometric constraints encoded in triangular attention, explains the diffusion formulation of AlphaFold3, and presents the empirical evidence with the care the claims require.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The folding problem and Levinthal's paradox

A protein of $n$ residues has approximately $2n$ backbone torsion angles $(\phi_i, \psi_i)$. Discretizing each into just three bins yields $3^{2n}$ conformations — $3^{200}$ for a 100-residue chain — which no physical search could enumerate in the age of the universe. Yet ribosomes fold such chains in seconds. The resolution of the paradox is that folding follows a funnel-shaped energy landscape, not uniform random search. Computational prediction therefore required either faithful energy models (physics-based folding) or learned surrogates (template-based and, eventually, deep-learning methods).

### 2.2 Coevolution and contact prediction

A decisive biological signal predates deep learning: *coevolution*. When two residues are in spatial contact, a mutation at one often induces a compensatory mutation at the other, creating correlated columns in a multiple sequence alignment. Direct coupling analysis (DCA) formalized this with Potts models; contact predictors such as RaptorX and trRosetta learned to map MSAs to distograms and orientations using residual convolutional networks. AlphaFold1 (CASP13 winner, 2018) exemplified this lineage, predicting pairwise distance distributions and optimizing them by gradient descent. It reached GDT_TS ~70 but was limited by a two-stage design: the network never reasoned in 3D.

### 2.3 The CASP benchmark

CASP's blind protocol — targets are experimental structures withheld from the public until prediction deadlines pass — makes it the gold standard for fair evaluation [6]. Primary metrics include **GDT_TS** (global distance test, total score: the fraction of Cα atoms within 1, 2, 4, 8 Å cutoffs, averaged) and **lDDT** (local distance difference test, superposition-free). AlphaFold2's CASP14 median GDT_TS of 92.4 versus ~70 for the runner-up constituted a single-generation discontinuity of a magnitude the organizers had not anticipated for a decade [1][2][6].

## 3 Methodology

We analyze the AlphaFold family as a family of *geometric deep-learning architectures* — networks whose inductive biases encode the symmetries of Euclidean space — and evaluate them against three methodological lenses: representational design, equivariance guarantees, and generative formulation.

### 3.1 Architectural decomposition of AlphaFold2

AlphaFold2 consumes an MSA (up to $N_{seq}$ sequences × $N_{res}$ residues) and templates, and produces all-atom coordinates. Its trunk, the **Evoformer**, maintains two representations updated for 48 blocks:

- **MSA representation** $m_{si} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_m}$: per-sequence, per-residue embeddings.
- **Pair representation** $z_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_z}$: per-residue-pair embeddings encoding co-evolutionary and geometric relationships.

The pair representation is initialized from the MSA outer product and template distograms; it is the network's internal "distance matrix," progressively denoised toward the true geometry.

### 3.2 Triangular attention and triangle consistency

The key geometric insight: for any three residues $i, j, k$, the distances must satisfy the triangle inequality $d_{ik} \le d_{ij} + d_{jk}$. Rather than hoping the network discovers this, the Evoformer enforces it architecturally. In **triangle multiplicative updates**, $z_{ij}$ is updated by contracting over the third index:

$$
z_{ij} \leftarrow z_{ij} + \sum_k \sigma(a_{ik}) \odot \sigma(b_{jk}),
$$

where $a, b$ are linear projections of $z$. The **triangle self-attention** variants (around starting and ending nodes) compute attention weights $w_{ijk}$ over $k$ conditioned on $z_{ik}, z_{jk}$, explicitly routing information through the triangle $(i, j, k)$ [1]. This is a *soft relaxation* of metric consistency: the network learns to project its pair beliefs onto the manifold of realizable geometries, a operation that classical methods performed by expensive semidefinite or metric-matrix-completion routines.

### 3.3 Invariant point attention

The **structure module** predicts, per residue, a rigid-body frame $T_i = (R_i, t_i) \in SE(3)$ for the backbone. IPA computes attention in a way that is invariant to a global transformation $T_{global}$ applied to all frames, without requiring spherical-harmonic tensor products. For query residues, IPA forms attention weights from scalar features, then aggregates *points* expressed in each residue's local frame:

$$
\text{IPA}(T) = \sum_j a_{ij} \, R_i^{-1}(x_j - t_i),
$$

where $x_j$ are learned 3D points attached to residue $j$. Under $T_i \mapsto T_{global} \circ T_i$, the local-frame coordinates are unchanged — hence invariance. The loss (frame-aligned point error, FAPE) is likewise invariant, comparing predicted and true atom positions after aligning each residue's frame. Recycling feeds the predicted frames back into the Evoformer input for up to 3 additional passes, effectively increasing depth at constant parameter count.

### 3.4 AlphaFold3: diffusion over atomic coordinates

AlphaFold3 discards the Evoformer in favor of the **Pairformer** — pair-only attention without the MSA track's full machinery — and replaces the IPA structure module with a **diffusion module** [3]. Given noisy coordinates $x_t$ at diffusion time $t$, the network predicts the clean coordinates $\hat{x}_0$, and a standard Gaussian diffusion SDE is reversed to sample structures. The diffusion head naturally handles *variable atomic composition*: proteins, DNA/RNA, ligands, ions, and modified residues are all just atoms with type embeddings. Training losses combine denoising score matching with the familiar FAPE-style and distogram terms. Notably, AlphaFold3 achieves state-of-the-art docking accuracy (success rates ~76% on PoseBusters versus ~52% for the best classical baseline) without MSAs for the small-molecule component [3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Evoformer: joint attention over sequence and geometry

The Evoformer's MSA stack applies row-wise and column-wise gated self-attention: rows attend across residues within one sequence (capturing intra-chain dependencies), columns attend across homologous sequences at one position (capturing conservation). The **MSA-to-pair outer-product mean** then injects coevolutionary statistics into $z_{ij}$:

```python
# Simplified Evoformer pair update (schematic)
def outer_product_mean(msa):            # msa: [S, L, c_m]
    a = linear_a(msa)                   # [S, L, c]
    b = linear_b(msa)                   # [S, L, c]
    z = einsum('s i c, s j d -> i j c d', a, b)
    return z.mean(dim=0).reshape(L, L, -1)
```

Information flows *back* from pair to MSA through triangle updates, so geometric beliefs refine sequence interpretation — a bidirectional reasoning loop absent from AlphaFold1's feed-forward distogram stage. Ablations in the Nature paper show that removing the triangle multiplicative updates or the IPA structure module degrades CASP14 GDT_TS by double-digit margins, confirming that geometry, not scale alone, is the operative ingredient [1].

### 4.2 Invariance, equivariance, and the FAPE loss

IPA's elegance lies in achieving 3D reasoning *without* equivariant tensor machinery (cf. Tensor Field Networks, SE(3)-Transformers). The predicted frames are updated by the network in a global-frame-invariant manner, while the *outputs* (atom positions) transform equivariantly by construction: positions are generated as $R_i p + t_i$ with local offsets $p$. FAPE aligns each predicted residue frame to the corresponding true frame, then penalizes clamped Euclidean distances:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{FAPE}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i,j} \min\left(d_{\text{clamp}},\, \|T_i^{-1} \circ \hat{x}_j - T_i^{-1,\text{true}} \circ x_j^{\text{true}}\| \right).
$$

Because every comparison happens in aligned local frames, the loss is invariant to global rigid motions, removing the need for Kabsch superposition during training and stabilizing gradients on large proteins [1].

### 4.3 Recycling as unrolled iterative refinement

Classical structure refinement (e.g., Rosetta relax) iterates energy minimization; AlphaFold2 *learns* the iteration. Recycling re-embeds the previous prediction's distogram into the pair representation and re-runs the full Evoformer + structure module. Empirically, recycling converts marginal predictions into confident ones (median pLDDT gains of several points on hard targets) at the cost of 3–4× inference time [1]. The procedure is a learned analogue of unrolled optimization, with the network's weights shared across "iterations."

### 4.4 RoseTTAFold and ESMFold: alternative design points

RoseTTAFold's **three-track network** processes 1D sequence, 2D distance/orientation, and 3D coordinate tracks with attention-based information flow between them, achieving CASP14-adjacent accuracy with open weights and training code [4]. Its significance is methodological and sociological: it proved the geometric-attention paradigm was reproducible outside DeepMind and catalyzed the open protein-design ecosystem (RFdiffusion descends directly from this lineage).

ESMFold takes the opposite trade: it replaces the MSA with embeddings from ESM-2, a 15-billion-parameter protein language model trained on 65 million sequences [5]. With no sequence search and no recycling, ESMFold runs ~60× faster than AlphaFold2 while recovering most of its accuracy on single chains (median TM-score 0.68 vs. 0.75 on CAMEO in the original report). The result demonstrates that evolutionary information can be *amortized into weights* rather than retrieved per-query — at the cost of reduced accuracy on deep-homology targets where explicit coevolution signals dominate.

### 4.5 Confidence: pLDDT and PAE

AlphaFold2 predicts its own error. **pLDDT** (predicted lDDT-Cα) is a per-residue confidence score trained against the true lDDT; regions with pLDDT > 90 are expected to be within ~1 Å, while pLDDT < 50 reliably flags disorder or unmodeled linkers. The **predicted aligned error (PAE)** matrix estimates positional error between residue pairs, diagnosing domain-packing confidence. Calibration studies on the human proteome found that 58% of residues were predicted confidently, and low-confidence stretches correlated strongly with known intrinsically disordered regions [2]. This self-awareness is what made proteome-scale deployment scientifically responsible: users can distinguish atomic-grade predictions from speculative ones.

## 5 Empirical Results and Formal Proofs

### 5.1 CASP14: the discontinuity

On 87 CASP14 domains, AlphaFold2 achieved a median GDT_TS of 92.4; the next-best group scored ~72 [1][6]. For 59% of targets, the backbone RMSD was below 1 Å — within experimental error of the deposited structures. A formal statement of the invariance property underpinning the structure module:

> **Theorem:** *Let $T = (T_1, \dots, T_L)$ be residue frames and $T_{\text{global}} \in SE(3)$. The IPA update satisfies $\text{IPA}(T_{\text{global}} \circ T) = \text{IPA}(T)$ as a function of the frames, and the predicted coordinates transform as $T_{\text{global}} \circ \hat{x}$.*
>
> *Proof sketch.* Attention weights depend only on scalar (frame-independent) features. The aggregated quantity $R_i^{-1}(x_j - t_i)$ is the local-frame representation of a world point; left-multiplying all frames by $T_{\text{global}}$ leaves every local-frame coordinate unchanged, hence weights and outputs are invariant. Coordinates are emitted as $R_i p + t_i$, which inherits equivariance. ∎

### 5.2 Proteome scale and the AlphaFold Database

Applying AlphaFold2 to the human proteome covered 98.5% of human proteins, with 58% of residues at confident pLDDT [2]. The AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (DeepMind/EMBL-EBI) subsequently released over **214 million** predicted structures covering UniRef90 — effectively the known protein universe — free for academic and commercial use. Downstream impact includes accelerated cryo-EM model building (predictions as molecular-replacement search models), variant-effect interpretation, and the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry shared by Hassabis and Jumper "for protein structure prediction" alongside Baker for computational protein design.

### 5.3 AlphaFold3 and molecular interactions

AlphaFold3's diffusion formulation extends beyond the polypeptide. On the PoseBusters benchmark for protein–ligand docking, it reports 76% success (RMSD < 2 Å) versus 52% for the strongest classical baseline; on protein–nucleic-acid interfaces it exceeds specialized tools [3]. The Pairformer retains triangular-style pair updates while dropping the MSA track's quadratic-in-sequences cost, and MSA information enters through a lighter conditioning path. The diffusion head's ability to model *joint distributions* over heterogeneous atoms is the architectural reason one model now covers covalent modifications, ions, and multi-chain assemblies.

### 5.4 Reproducibility and open implementations

ColabFold repackaged AlphaFold2 with MMseqs2-based MSA search, cutting database-search time from hours to minutes and enabling a 1,762-protein archaeal proteome in 48 hours on a single GPU [8]. OpenFold reimplemented the training pipeline in PyTorch, verifying that the published method — not undisclosed engineering — accounts for the performance. These replications matter epistemically: they converted a corporate demo into a scientific result.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs).** AlphaFold2 predicts a single static conformation; disordered regions receive low pLDDT and often collapse into arbitrary compact states. The physics of disorder — ensembles, not structures — is outside the model's hypothesis class.
2. **Conformational dynamics.** Allostery, induced fit, and multiple functional states are poorly captured. Sampling strategies (MSA subsampling, dropout ensembles) recover *some* alternative states but without thermodynamic weighting.
3. **Novel folds and orphan proteins.** Accuracy degrades sharply with MSA depth; single-sequence methods (ESMFold) partially mitigate this but at lower ceiling [5].
4. **Mutation effects.** Point-mutation ΔΔG prediction remains unreliable; the network's energy landscape is too smooth to resolve single-residue destabilization, limiting direct use in variant pathogenicity scoring.
5. **Training-data cutoff and memorization.** Templates and PDB-derived training data introduce subtle leakage risks; CASP's time-split protocol mitigates but does not eliminate them.
6. **AlphaFold3's closed weights.** Unlike AlphaFold2, AF3's parameters were initially restricted, slowing independent verification — a step backward for open science partially remedied by later releases and reimplementations.

---

## 7 Conclusion

AlphaFold's contribution is best understood not as "deep learning applied to proteins" but as the discovery of the right *geometric inductive biases* for molecular structure: triangle-consistent pair reasoning, frame-invariant spatial attention, and — in the third generation — diffusion over heterogeneous atomic sets. The empirical record (CASP14's 92.4 median GDT_TS, 214 million database structures, docking accuracy surpassing classical baselines) justifies the claim that single-structure prediction of folded domains is, for practical purposes, solved [1][2][3][6]. What remains — ensembles, dynamics, disorder, and the quantitative energetics of mutation — defines the next decade. The architectures reviewed here will likely serve as the *initialization*, not the endpoint, of that program: as priors inside generative models of conformational ensembles and as feature extractors for the physics the field has yet to learn.
