# Game Semantics: The Hyland–Ong Fully Abstract Models of Programming Languages

## Abstract

The full abstraction problem for PCF — the demand that denotational equality coincide exactly with observational equivalence — resisted solution for nearly two decades before its resolution in 2000 through game semantics. This thesis reconstructs the Hyland–Ong (and independently Abramsky–Jagadeesan–Malacaria) construction: types as *arena games*, programs as *strategies*, and the two combinatorial constraints — **innocence** and **well-bracketing** — that isolate precisely the definable behaviours of a purely functional, call-by-name language with recursion. We develop the technical machinery in full: arenas, justified sequences, legal plays, the Player-view and Opponent-view, composition via parallel interaction and hiding, and the intensional full abstraction theorem stating that every compact innocent well-bracketed strategy is definable. We then examine the second phase of the construction — the quotient by the intrinsic preorder that collapses the intensional model to an extensional, order-theoretic one — and explain how relaxing each constraint independently classifies state, control, and other computational effects, with applications to Idealized Algol and call-by-value languages.

## 1 Introduction

Denotational semantics aspires to assign to every program a mathematical meaning such that replacing a subterm by one with the same meaning never changes observable behaviour. A model satisfying this converse — programs are observationally equivalent *if and only if* their denotations coincide — is **fully abstract** [1]. Full abstraction is the gold standard of denotational correctness: it guarantees the model contains no "junk" distinguishing observationally indistinguishable programs, and no "noise" conflating programs a context could tell apart.

For PCF (Programming Computable Functions), Plotkin's simply typed λ-calculus enriched with natural numbers, booleans, conditionals and general recursion, the problem was open from 1977 [2]. Scott's domain-theoretic models were adequate but never fully abstract: the continuous-functions model contains "parallel or" and other non-sequential elements with no PCF denotation. Sequentiality — the idea that a higher-order functional evaluates its arguments one at a time, in some order — resisted order-theoretic capture.

Game semantics broke the impasse by changing the ontology. Instead of *values*, the primitive semantic objects are *dialogues*: a computation is a structured exchange between a Player (the program) and an Opponent (the environment). Types become **games** — protocols specifying which moves may be played when — and programs become **strategies** — rules prescribing how Player responds. The key insight of Hyland and Ong [3], and independently of Abramsky, Jagadeesan and Malacaria [4], is that *restricting the memory a strategy may use* captures exactly the sequential, purely functional character of PCF. Innocence — dependence only on the Player-view of the play — plus well-bracketing — answers must follow the discipline of a stack — characterise PCF-definability.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 PCF and observational equivalence

PCF types are generated from base types `nat` and `bool` by the function arrow. Terms include constants, `succ`, `pred`, `iszero`, conditionals, λ-abstraction, application, and the fixed-point combinator `Y_σ` at every type. The operational semantics is a small-step (or big-step) evaluation relation on closed terms of ground type. Two terms `M`, `N` of the same type are **observationally equivalent**, `M ≅ N`, if for every closing context `C[−]` of ground type, `C[M]⇓v` iff `C[N]⇓v`. A model `⟦−⟧` is **fully abstract** when `⟦M⟧ = ⟦N⟧ ⇔ M ≅ N`.

### 2.2 The domain-theoretic impasse

The classic cpo model interprets `σ → τ` as the continuous function space. It is computationally adequate but contains elements such as the "parallel-or" functional, which evaluates both arguments concurrently. No PCF term denotes it, so the model fails full abstraction. The obstruction is *extensionality of the wrong kind*: order-theoretic models record input–output behaviour but not the *order and interleaving of interrogations* that sequential evaluation performs. Stable domains and sequential algorithms narrowed the gap but never closed it.

### 2.3 Dialogue games

Blass connected games with linear logic; Abramsky, Jagadeesan and Malacaria, and independently Hyland and Ong, placed compositionality — the inductive definition of strategies on syntax — at the centre and solved PCF. The Hyland–Ong variant uses **arena games** and **innocent strategies** presented through *justification pointers* [5].

## 3 Methodology

Our method is mathematical reconstruction with verification against primary sources. We fix the Hyland–Ong presentation (dialogue games, pointer sequences) and note where the Abramsky–Jagadeesan–Malacaria presentation (history-free strategies on games with explicit causal structure) differs. All definitions below are standard; proofs are sketched with references to the full arguments in [3][4]. Technical claims about definability, composition, and the quotient construction are cross-checked against the Stanford Encyclopedia survey [1].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Arena games, moves, and justification

An **arena** is a triple `A = (M_A, λ_A, ⊢_A)` where:

- `M_A` is a set of *moves*;
- `λ_A : M_A → {O, P} × {Q, A}` labels each move as Opponent/Player and Question/Answer;
- `⊢_A ⊆ ({⋆} ∪ M_A) × M_A` is the *enabling* (justification) relation: `⋆ ⊢ m` means `m` is initial (necessarily an Opponent question), and `m ⊢ n` means `m` may justify `n`, subject to: justification alternates between O and P, answers are justified by questions, and an answer may only be played while its justifying question is pending (the *well-bracketing* condition on plays, enforced at the level of legal sequences).

A **justified sequence** is a sequence of moves in which every non-initial move carries a pointer to an earlier move that enables it. A **play** is a justified sequence satisfying alternation (O and P strictly alternate, O moving first) and *visibility*: every move's justifier must occur in the appropriate *view* of the prefix. Plays are the complete dialogues the protocol admits.

```haskell
-- The arena for PCF's bool: one initial question, two answers
data Move = Q | TT | FF
enables :: Move -> Move -> Bool
enables Q TT = True   -- question justifies each answer
enables Q FF = True
enables _ _  = False
initial :: Move -> Bool
initial Q = True      -- only the question may start a play
initial _ = False
```

Types are interpreted as arenas by induction: `⟦nat⟧` and `⟦bool⟧` are flat arenas (one question, answers for each value), and `⟦σ → τ⟧ = ⟦σ⟧ ⇒ ⟦τ⟧` is the function-space arena, whose moves are the disjoint union of the argument and result arenas with polarities of the argument component flipped — a move by the *environment* inside the argument subgame becomes a Player move at the outer level, because it is the program that interrogates its argument. This polarity reversal is the semantic heart of higher-order interaction.

### 4.2 Strategies, views, and innocence

A **strategy** `σ` for Player on an arena `A` is a non-empty, prefix-closed set of even-length plays (plays ending with a Player move) that is *deterministic*: if `s·m` and `s·n` are both in `σ` with `m, n` Player moves, then `m = n`. Determinism encodes that Player's response to a given history is unique — the strategy is a *function* from Opponent continuations to Player responses, not a relation.

The **Player-view** `⌜s⌝` of a play `s` is computed recursively: it retains the justifier-chain of the last move, discarding everything Opponent did "off to the side". Formally, `⌜s·m⌝ = ⌜s⌝·m` when `m` is a Player move, and `⌜s·m⌝ = ⌜s₀⌝·m` when `m` is an Opponent move justified by a move in `s = s₀·…`. Intuitively, the Player-view is the subsequence of the dialogue that is *causally relevant* to Player's next decision: the pending questions and the answers already received along the current thread of control.

> **Definition (Innocence).** A strategy `σ` is *innocent* if its response depends only on the Player-view: whenever `s·m, t ∈ σ` with `⌜s⌝ = ⌜t⌝` and `t·m'` is a legal extension, then `m = m'`.

Innocence is a *memory restriction*: Player may not remember arbitrary history, only the current view. This is exactly what rules out state: a strategy modelling a mutable cell must react differently to the *same* view depending on what values were written *outside* the view — a non-innocent behaviour. Well-bracketing adds the stack discipline: every answer must answer the most recently asked unanswered question in the view, which rules out control operators such as `call/cc` that capture and abandon continuations.

### 4.3 Composition: interaction plus hiding

Strategies compose. Given `σ : A ⇒ B` and `τ : B ⇒ C`, their composite `σ;τ : A ⇒ C` is formed in two stages. First, the **parallel interaction** `σ ∥ τ` collects all interleavings of plays of `σ` and `τ` that *synchronise* on the shared arena `B`: whenever one strategy plays a move in `B`, the other must be ready to receive it as an Opponent move, like two processes communicating over a channel. Second, **hiding** deletes all moves in `B`, leaving a strategy on `A ⇒ C` that records only the externally visible dialogue.

This is composition as *internal communication*: the function and its argument converse privately in `B`, and the outside world observes only the resulting input–output interaction. Hiding is what makes the category work — without it, composition would leak internal chatter into the interface.

> **Theorem (Innocence is preserved by composition) [3].** If `σ` and `τ` are innocent (and well-bracketed), then `σ;τ` is innocent (and well-bracketed).

The proof is non-trivial: one must show that the view of the hidden interaction sequence determines the view of the composite play. It proceeds by a careful analysis of how pointers thread through the synchronisation, establishing that the Player-view of the composite is computed from the views of the components. This closure property is what makes innocent strategies form a category — the intensional model of PCF.

### 4.4 Intensional full abstraction and definability

The intensional model interprets every PCF term as an innocent, well-bracketed strategy. The central theorem is a *definability* or *universality* result:

> **Theorem (Intensional full abstraction, Hyland–Ong [3], Thm. 7.1).** For every PCF type `σ = σ₁ → ⋯ → σₙ → κ` with `κ ∈ {nat, bool}`, every *compact* innocent well-bracketed strategy on `⟦σ⟧` is the denotation of a PCF term.

Compactness is the finiteness condition: the strategy's behaviour is determined by finitely many plays (equivalently, it is a finite element in the strategy order). The proof decomposes an arbitrary compact innocent strategy into a finite decision tree and synthesises a PCF term — using conditionals at ground type and recursion — that realises it. Abramsky, Jagadeesan and Malacaria prove the parallel result for history-free strategies [4], where "history-free" plays the role of innocence in their differently-structured games.

Intensional here means the model distinguishes programs with different *interactive behaviour* even when they are observationally equivalent — it is *too fine*. Two terms may denote different strategies while no context separates them. Full abstraction in the standard sense requires a further step.

### 4.5 The extensional collapse: quotient by the intrinsic preorder

Abramsky, Jagadeesan and Malacaria's distinctive contribution is the construction of the extensional model from the intensional one *without syntax*. They define an **intrinsic preorder** `≲` on strategies: `σ ≲ τ` if, roughly, `τ` is at least as defined as `σ` in every observable respect — formalised via tests against strategies that play the role of contexts. The remarkable fact they prove is that on function types, the intrinsic preorder coincides with the *pointwise* preorder: `σ ≲ τ : A ⇒ B` iff for all "arguments" the applications are related. Quotienting the intensional category by the equivalence induced by `≲` yields an order-extensional model.

> **Theorem (Extensional full abstraction [4], Thm. 3.2).** The quotient of the intensional model by the intrinsic preorder is fully abstract for PCF: `⟦M⟧ = ⟦N⟧` in the quotient iff `M ≅ N`.

Hyland and Ong obtain the same collapse via their own route (a quotient by observational equivalence of strategies). The conceptual moral: game semantics naturally produces an *intensional* universe of interactive behaviours; extensionality — the identification of behaviourally indistinguishable programs — is recovered as a *quotient*, and the quotient can be described purely semantically, giving the first syntax-independent description of the fully abstract model of PCF. This also yields a universality theorem for the effective model: every recursive strategy in the quotient is definable up to observational equivalence [4].

### 4.6 Beyond PCF: Idealized Algol, call-by-value, and the constraint landscape

The deepest payoff of the Hyland–Ong analysis is its *modularity*. Each constraint corresponds to a programming discipline, and relaxing constraints classifies computational effects [1]:

| Constraints | Language characterised |
|---|---|
| Innocent + well-bracketed | PCF (pure, call-by-name) |
| Well-bracketed, not innocent | Idealized Algol (block-structured state) |
| Innocent, not well-bracketed | PCF + control operators (`call/cc`) |
| Neither | Unrestricted higher-order store |

Dropping innocence admits strategies that consult history outside the view — precisely the power needed to model mutable references. Abramsky and McCusker's fully abstract model of Idealized Algol keeps well-bracketing (block structure enforces the stack discipline) but allows *knowing* strategies [6]. Conversely, dropping well-bracketing while keeping innocence models control: answers may target non-top pending questions, mirroring captured continuations.

For **call-by-value**, Abramsky and McCusker reformulated the games so that value-passing is explicit in the protocol [7]; Honda and Yoshida gave a parallel game-theoretic analysis of call-by-value computation, showing the innocent fragment corresponds to call-by-value PCF. Murawski and collaborators later refined the landscape with *block-innocent* strategies, situated strictly between innocent and knowing, characterising call-by-value Idealized Algol [8]. The "semantic square" of Abramsky and students maps these combinations systematically [1].

## 5 Empirical Results / Proofs

Game semantics is a mathematical theory; its "empirical" content is the body of theorems it has produced and the verification tools they enabled:

1. **Definability (universality).** Every compact innocent well-bracketed strategy is PCF-definable [3]; every compact history-free strategy is definable in PCF extended with a simple non-sequential constant [4]. These are the intensional full abstraction theorems.
2. **Extensional collapse.** The intrinsic-preorder quotient is order-extensional and fully abstract, with a universality theorem for recursive strategies [4].
3. **Effect classification.** The constraint lattice above has been validated across languages: Idealized Algol with active expressions [6], call-by-value languages [7], and refined fragments via block-innocence [8].
4. **Applied impact.** The 2017 Alonzo Church Award citation credits these models with "fundamentally revolutionising the field of programming language semantics, and for the applied impact of these models" — including software model checking of higher-order programs, where strategies become finite automata over which observational equivalence is decidable [9].

The proofs rest on combinatorial lemmas about views and pointers: the *view extraction* lemmas showing interaction preserves views, and the *decomposition lemma* factoring compact strategies into syntactic constructors. Their intricacy — acknowledged even by practitioners [5] — is the price of a fully abstract model.

## 6 Limitations

The framework has genuine boundaries. **Complexity:** proving that constraints are preserved under composition is a major undertaking for each new variant, and the pointer combinatorics do not obviously connect to algebra or mainstream type theory [5]. **Decidability:** Loader proved finitary PCF observational equivalence undecidable, so no effective procedure can decide strategy equivalence in general — the model is fully abstract but not computationally tractable at higher types. **Concurrency:** the alternating, sequential dialogue structure does not naturally model true concurrency; concurrent games require substantial reworking. **Totality and effects mixing:** combining state, control, and polymorphism in one fully abstract model remains delicate, and each combination demands its own constraint analysis. Finally, the models are *denotational* in a strong sense — extracting efficient operational intuitions or compilers from strategies is an open engineering challenge.

## 7 Conclusion

The Hyland–Ong construction solved the full abstraction problem for PCF by relocating semantics from values to *interactions*. Arenas formalise the protocol of a type; justified plays record dialogues with explicit causal pointers; innocence and well-bracketing carve out exactly the sequential, purely functional behaviours; composition as interaction-plus-hiding builds a category; and the intrinsic-preorder quotient collapses intension to extension. Beyond PCF, the same architecture — relax a constraint, gain an effect — became the organising principle for the semantics of state, control, and evaluation order, from Idealized Algol to call-by-value languages. Game semantics thus did more than close a twenty-year open problem: it supplied a *periodic table* of programming disciplines, indexed by the memory a strategy is allowed to keep.
