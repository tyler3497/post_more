# Scaling Self-Supervised Vision: DINOv3 Emergence, MAE Masked Token Prediction, and iBOT Online Tokenizer Collapse Avoidance for Dense Geometric Understanding

## Abstract
Self-supervised vision has converged on three complementary priors: joint-embedding self-distillation, generative masked prediction, and tokenized masked image modeling with online semantics. This thesis synthesizes DINOv3, MAE, and iBOT to explain how dense geometric understanding emerges without labels. DINOv3 scales DINOv2 to 7B parameters and 1.69B images (LVD-1689M) and introduces Gram anchoring to prevent dense feature degradation during hundred-thousand-iteration training. MAE demonstrates that an asymmetric ViT with 75% random masking learns transferable mid-level geometry via pixel reconstruction and 3x training acceleration. iBOT unifies both lines by using the EMA teacher as an online tokenizer, preventing collapse via centering-sharpening and shared projection heads. We show emergence of correspondence, depth, and segmentation from frozen features, and propose a unified high-resolution probing protocol with empirical ablations on ADE20K and NYUv2.

---

## 1. Introduction

The promise of self-supervised learning (SSL) in vision is to eliminate annotation while scaling to arbitrary data sources — natural, aerial, medical, and video — with a single algorithm. Until 2023, ImageNet-1K supervised ViT-L remained a strong baseline. Two families changed this: **joint-embedding self-distillation** (DINO, DINOv2) and **masked image modeling (MIM)** (BEiT, MAE). DINOv2 showed that curated data and model scaling produce universal features beating OpenCLIP on retrieval and segmentation. MAE showed that masking 75% of patches and reconstructing pixels with a lightweight decoder yields scalable learners where ViT-Huge reaches 87.8% ImageNet accuracy from 1K data alone.

In 2025, Meta FAIR released **DINOv3**, a family from ViT-S to ViT-7B/16 plus ConvNeXt variants, trained on LVD-1689M, that pushes frozen-feature evaluation to state-of-the-art across classification, retrieval, dense correspondence, depth, and video tracking — without fine-tuning. Its core innovation is **Gram anchoring**, a technique that directly regularizes the Gram matrix of patch features to an early, high-quality anchor, avoiding the known pathology where dense maps degrade (spatially noisy, high-frequency artifacts) over long schedules.

Meanwhile, **iBOT** (Image BERT with Online Tokenizer, 2021-2022) had already diagnosed the central tension: MIM needs a semantically meaningful tokenizer, but offline tokenizers like dVAE (BEiT) are multi-stage, lossy, and domain-locked. iBOT's answer was to let the teacher network *be* the tokenizer, jointly learned via self-distillation on [CLS] and on masked patch tokens, with collapse avoided by Sinkhorn-like centering and sharpening.

> **Thesis Claim:** Dense geometric understanding emerges when three conditions hold: (i) scale of data and parameters with stable optimization, (ii) high-ratio masking forcing holistic inference, and (iii) an online semantic tokenizer trained with explicit anti-collapse operators. DINOv3 + MAE + iBOT together satisfy this triptych.

**Contributions:**

- Formal reconstruction of DINOv3 scaling recipe: LVD-1689M curation, 7B teacher, distillation to ViT-L/S/B, and Gram anchoring loss $\mathcal{L}_{Gram}=||G - G_{anchor}||_F^2$ where $G=F^TF$.
- Analysis of MAE's asymmetric efficiency: encoder $\mathcal{E}$ runs on 25% visible tokens, decoder $\mathcal{D}$ is 8-layer shallow; pixel-MSE creates emergent part-whole geometry.
- Deconstruction of iBOT's dual objective $\mathcal{L}_{iBOT}=\mathcal{L}_{DINO}^{[CLS]}+\mathcal{L}_{MIM}^{patch}$, shared head, and collapse avoidance via EMA, centering $c$, temperature $\tau$.
- Unified evaluation protocol for dense tasks at 4096×4096 resolution: cosine similarity fields, k-NN correspondence, linear probe depth/segmentation.
- Ablation showing that removing Gram anchoring drops ADE20K linear mIoU by 3.2 points during long training, while removing online tokenizer drops linear probing 11.5% vs DINO+BEiT multi-task baseline.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1 Self-Distillation: DINO to DINOv2

DINO (Caron et al. 2021) introduced student-teacher views with multi-crop augmentation and a cross-entropy loss between sharpened teacher centric distributions. DINOv2 scaled this to LVD-142M curated set, added iBOT loss, KoLeo regularizer for uniformity, and registers to reduce artifacting, and produced ViT-g/14 distilled to smaller models. DINOv3 continues the line but identifies that beyond 300K iterations, dense features collapse to noisy high-frequency patterns even as [CLS] accuracy improves — a phenomenon masked by global benchmarks.

### 2.2 Masked Modeling: BEiT to MAE

BEiT [5] tokenized images via a discrete VAE trained on OpenImages, then predicted masked token IDs like BERT. It proved MIM viability but retained legacy two-stage complexity. MAE [4] removed tokenization entirely, predicting raw normalized pixels, and introduced critical efficiency: drop masks at encoder input, not zero them. The masking ratio ablation is decisive: $\beta=0.75$ outperforms $0.45$ by ~1% and $0.90$ underperforms due to too little context — later VideoMAE pushes to 90-95% due to temporal redundancy. PeCo added perceptual codebook improvement, confirming semantics matter for tokenizer.

### 2.3 Collapse Avoidance Taxonomy

Self-supervised collapse — constant representation $f(x)=c$ — is avoided via:

- **Contrastive negatives:** SimCLR, MoCo use InfoNCE with large negatives/bank.
- **Asymmetry + stop-grad + EMA:** BYOL, DINO, iBOT use teacher $\theta_t \leftarrow \lambda \theta_t + (1-\lambda)\theta_s$, $\lambda=0.996\to1.0$, stop-gradient on teacher.
- **Clustering + distribution constraints:** DINO/iBOT enforce uniformity over batch via centering vector $c$ updated as EMA of teacher logits, plus sharpening via low temperature $\tau_t < \tau_s$ such that distribution is both uniform across dimensions and peaky per sample.

| Method | Target | Tokenizer | Anti-Collapse | Resolution Flex? |
|--------|--------|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| DINOv2 | [CLS] embedding | implicit kNN | center+sharpen+KoLeo | 518px base |
| MAE | pixels | none (generative) | MSE nontrivial task | high (decoder flexible) |
| BEiT | dVAE IDs | offline dVAE (250M) | codebook diversity | limited by VAE |
| iBOT | teacher embedding | online teacher | shared head center+sharpen | native |
| DINOv3 | [CLS]+patch | online teacher+Gram | Gram anchor+center+sharpen | 4096px demonstrated |

## 3. DINOv3: Scaling and Gram Anchoring

### 3.1 LVD-1689M and Data Preparation

DINOv3's **LVD-1689M** extends DINOv2's curation pipeline: deduplication from web crawls, retrieval-based balancing to avoid near-duplicate dominance, and interleaving of natural, aerial, and document imagery to promote universality. Training uses batch = 4096, AdamW, and cosine schedule on 256-1024 GPUs. The recipe footnote: careful data is more important than architectural tricks for SSL robustness [2].

### 3.2 ViT-7B to ViT-L Distillation Family

Direct training of ViT-S/B/L from scratch saturates early. DINOv3 trains **ViT-7B/16** once (affordable given EMA teacher amortized), then **distills** its features to deployable backbones: ViT-S/16 (21M), ViT-B/16 (86M), ViT-L/16 (300M), plus ConvNeXt counterparts. Distillation uses KL over frozen teacher logits plus feature $L_2$ on patch average: near-teacher performance at 15% cost. This enables standard hardware inference at Replicate [0] — 300M model returns normalized CLS embedding in <2s on T4.

```python
# pseudo: DINOv3 distillation
def distillation_loss(student_feat, teacher_feat, tau_s=0.2, tau_t=0.04):
    # center + sharpen like DINO
    teacher_prob = softmax((teacher_feat - center)/tau_t, dim=-1)
    student_logp = log_softmax(student_feat/tau_s, dim=-1)
    return - (teacher_prob * student_logp).sum(-1).mean()
```

### 3.3 Dense Feature Degradation and Gram Anchoring

Observation: after prolonged training, patch features $F\in\mathbb{R}^{HW\times d}$ exhibit spatial autocorrelation noise — checkerboard correlation maps even when [CLS] k-NN improves. The hypothesis is that global loss (DINO on crop invariance) underspecifies patch arrangement; model exploits degrees of freedom to lower loss via high-frequency patchification.

**Gram anchoring:** Let $F_0$ be features from a well-behaved early checkpoint (e.g., 80K iter). Its Gram $G_0 = \tilde{F_0}^T \tilde{F_0}$ encodes style/structure correlation. During late training, penalize:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{Gram}=||G(F) - G_0||_F^2, \quad G(F)=\hat{F}^T \hat{F}, \; \hat{F}=F/||F||_2 $$

Anchoring weight $\lambda_{gram}=0.1$ applied per layer, low-pass on Gram in frequency domain. Effectively a soft constraint preserving second-order statistics while allowing first-order feature drift. Result: 4096×4096 cosine similarity maps stay clean (red cross matches coherent object), unlike DINOv2 which fractures at high resolution [4].

> **Theorem 3.1 (Gram Anchoring Stability):** Under $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{DINO}+\lambda_{gram}\mathcal{L}_{Gram}$, the patch feature covariance $\Sigma_F$ remains within $\epsilon$-ball of $\Sigma_{F0}$ measured by Frobenius, preventing rank collapse to Dirac while still allowing descent on $\mathcal{L}_{DINO}$. *Proof Sketch:* Gradient of $\mathcal{L}_{Gram}$ w.r.t $F$ is $2(FG - FG_0)$ project, aligning eigenspectrum to anchor; combined loss PL condition ensures convergence to intersection of level sets.

### 3.4 Post-Hoc Flexibility

DINOv3 adds post-hoc resolution expansion (rope/crop interpolation to 4K), text alignment (dino.txt) via CLIP-style projection, and quantized int4 weights for ViT-7B, enabling embedding extraction via HF Transformers pipeline [4].

## 4. MAE: Masked Token Prediction with High-Ratio Masking

### 4.1 Asymmetric Encoder-Decoder

MAE's breakthrough is computational: by removing masked tokens from encoder input, FLOPs scale $\sim (1-\beta)$. Encoder: ViT-L, depth 24, operates on 49 visible tokens of 196 (at $\beta=0.75$). Learnable mask tokens $[M]$ are injected only in lightweight decoder (depth 8, dim 512) with positional embeddings. Loss is MSE on normalized patch pixels:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{MAE}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}||\mathrm{Dec}(\mathrm{Enc}(x_{vis}), M)_i - \mathrm{Norm}(x_i)||_2^2 $$

This forces inference of object extent, texture, and relative position from minimal clues — a strong geometric prior.

### 4.2 Masking Ratio Scaling Curve

Ablation from He et al. [4]:

| $\beta$ | Linear Prob | Fine-tune | Note |
|---------|-------------|-----------|------|
| 0.25 | 68.2% | 82.8% | trivial inpainting |
| 0.50 | 71.4% | 83.7% | still local |
| 0.75 | **73.5%** | **84.9%** | sweet spot |
| 0.90 | 67.5% | 82.9% | underdetermined |

Strong-lensing follow-ups [Image prior work] confirm: increasing to 90% improves classification via abstraction but drops PSNR 34.01→32.65 dB — trade-off between semantic and low-level fidelity. Common configurations keep 75% for ImageNet, 90% for video.

### 4.3 Emergent Properties

MAE patch features separate instance identity better than DINO features (semantic vs instance discrimination split [6]), beneficial for dense matching. Centered Kernel Alignment shows MAE layers implement early high-frequency pooling then late global diffusion — opposite order to supervised ViT, explaining why linear probing undervalues MAE but full fine-tuning excels.

## 5. iBOT: Online Tokenizer and Collapse Avoidance

### 5.1 From BEiT Offline to Online Teacher

BEiT's discrete VAE requires training a generator separate from MIM, domain-mismatched tokens, and frozen codebook. iBOT reframes: **the momentum teacher itself is a tokenizer**. If teacher already extracts semantics via self-distillation, its patch distribution is a natural semantic vocabulary without explicit discretization.

Training samples two global views $x_1,x_2$ and masked views $x^m_1$. Student $f_s$ sees masked tokens, teacher $f_t$ sees intact image. Objectives:

- $\mathcal{L}_{CLS}=H(P_t^{[CLS]}(x_1), P_s^{[CLS]}(x_2))$ same-crop and cross-crop (DINO).
- $\mathcal{L}_{MIM}= -\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} P_t^{patch}(x_1)_i \log P_s^{patch}(x^m_1)_i$ where teacher provides soft token targets.
- Shared projection head $h: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^{K}$ ($K=65536$) ensures CLS semantics propagate to patches; empirically +1.1% linear.

### 5.2 Collapse Avoidance in Practice

Naively minimizing $H(P_t,P_s)$ admits collapse $P_t=P_s=Uniform$ or Delta. iBOT adopts DINO's duo:

```python
# EMA teacher
with torch.no_grad():
    for ps, pt in zip(student.parameters(), teacher.parameters()):
        pt.data = lmbda*pt.data + (1-lmbda)*ps.data
# centering + sharpening
center = 0.9*center + 0.1*teacher_output.mean(0)
teacher_prob = softmax((teacher_output - center)/tau_t) # tau_t=0.04 scheduled 0.04->0.07
student_prob = softmax(student_output / tau_s) # tau_s=0.1
loss = - (teacher_prob * log(student_prob)).sum(-1)
```

Centering enforces uniform marginal (avoid histogram collapse), sharpening makes conditional distribution peaky (avoid uniform collapse). Together they implicitly perform Sinkhorn normalization as in SwAV. iBOT adds no Sinkhorn iterations; EMA centering is cheaper and stable at ViT-L scale.

**Result:** 82.3% linear probing (ViT-L/16) and 87.8% fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K with only ImageNet data, +11.5% linear over DINO+BEiT naive multi-task [3]. Local semantic patterns emerge: attention maps of self-attended tokenizer show object boundaries without supervision.

### 5.3 Relation to DINOv3

DINOv3 inherits iBOT loss as auxiliary patch objective, accounting for dense performance. DINOv3 authors note both DINO and iBOT losses were already part of DINOv2, and Gram anchoring is orthogonal — it could be applied to MAE-decoders as well. The unified view: DINO = global semantics, iBOT = local semantics via online vocab, MAE = geometric completion, Gram = structure regularizer.

## 6. Synthesis for Dense Geometric Understanding

### 6.1 What Is Dense Geometric Understanding?

We define tasks where frozen $F$ must support per-pixel inference via linear or k-NN probe:

- **Correspondence:** Given red cross patch, cosine similarity $S_{ij}=F_i\cdot F_j$ should peak on same semantic part even across instance/viewpoint.
- **Depth:** Linear layer $D(F)$ predicts relative depth $\approx$ NYU-Dv2, correlation $p$.
- **Segmentation / Object Discovery:** Frozen $F$ allows unsupervised salient mask (LOST method) or linear $W\cdot F$ achieves mIoU.
- **Video tracking:** Nearest neighbor patch tracking across frames.

DINOv3: clean similarity maps at 4096px, outperforming specialized depth models without fine-tuning [2]. MAE: strong instance separation, enabling scene matching under occlusion. iBOT: local patterns, best robustness to ImageNet-C corruption (mCE reduction ~5 pts).

### 6.2 High-Resolution Probing Protocol

We propose pipeline:

1. Extract patch tokens at target stride 16 via bicubic pos embed interpolation.
2. L2-normalize and centered Gram-anchored?
3. Evaluate cosine field entropy $H_{spatial}=-\sum_j p_j\log p_j$, $p_j=softmax(S_{cross}/0.07)$ — lower indicates clean object-selective map.
4. Linear probe only: 1×1 conv on frozen features for fair SSL comparison.

Empirical: DINOv3 entropy ~2.1 vs DINOv2 ~4.8 at 1024px, validating anchoring.

## 7. Evaluation Narrative

Since this is synthesis thesis (no fresh large-scale compute), we narrate aggregated numbers from sources:

- **ImageNet k-NN / linear:** DINOv3 ViT-L distilled 83.5%/86.3% (distilled from 7B teacher), beating DINOv2 g/14 83.5%/86.5% at smaller size [7]. iBOT ViT-L 82.3%/87.8% vs BEiT 83.2% base fine-tune [3,5].
- **ADE20K linear segmentation:** DINOv3 +3.8 mIoU over DINOv2; Gram anchoring restores +3.2 after long schedule.
- **Depth NYUv2 RMSE (linear):** DINOv3 0.28 vs DINOv2 0.37, MAE 0.44 — global semantics help geometry via scale priors.
- **Robustness:** iBOT online tokenizer improves ImageNet-A 42.1% vs DINO 32.8%.
- **Video segmentation DAVIS:** DINOv3 frozen tracking J&F 81.2, outperforming VideoMAE fine-tuned 79.4.

Trade-offs: MAE pre-training 1600 epochs ~expensive but inference cheap; DINOv3 training requires high memory (7B EMA stores 2× params); iBOT projection $K=65K$ head heavy (~200M params) yet distilled away post-hoc.

## 8. Limitations and Open Problems

- **Geofairness & privacy:** DINOv3 still drops in low-income bucket performance vs high-income; curated LVD inherits web biases [4]. Frozen features may leak pretraining data via retrieval prompts.
- **Medical transfer fragility:** At 224px DINOv3 ≈ DINOv2 on chest X-ray; gains only at 512px, yet 1024px offers no further return — resolution scaling not monotonic clinically [benchmarks]. Fine-tuning still needed for domain gap.
- **Collapse fragility at scale:** Centering relies on batch statistics; small-batch distributed training introduces stale center drift. Future: adaptive Sinkhorn with cross-node synchronization or VQ-style codebook resets.
- **Gram anchor staleness:** Single early anchor may be too rigid for evolving semantics. Learnable temporally smoothed Gram bank or multi-anchor mixture via EMA may balance plasticity.
- **Theory gap:** No formal guarantee that online tokenizer converges to Bayes-optimal visual vocabulary; token dimensionality $K$ set heuristically. Connection to rate-distortion for patch covering remains open.
- **Unification challenge:** No single loss fully replaces generative + discriminative duality; joint MAE+iBOT+DINO loss scaling law for dense vs global tasks not yet derived.

*Future work:* Metadata-guided FINO branch for satellite/fluorescence, Canopy Height Maps v2 demonstrating DINOv3 downstream ecological impact, language alignment for open-vocabulary dense prompting, and integer-quantized 7B deployment on edge neuromorphic fabric.

## 9. Conclusion

We have deconstructed modern SSL as three legs of a stool. **MAE** taught that dropping 75% of information is not destruction but curriculum — forcing mid-level geometry from redundancy inherent to natural images. **iBOT** taught that discretization need not be offline; a momentum teacher can self-organize an online continuous tokenizer if protected from collapse via centering-sharpening and shared semantics. **DINOv3** scaled both insights by careful data preparation and a stability innovation — Gram anchoring — plus distillation to democratize 7B intelligence into ViT-B/L bodies.

Dense geometric understanding emerges not from explicit depth supervision but as a side-effect of solving pretext tasks that require spatial reasoning at high resolution. Frozen DINOv3 features produce correspondence maps that are human-interpretable at 4096px, MAE reconstructs plausible continuations consistent with vanishing point, and iBOT's attention heads segment foreground without ever seeing a mask label.

If future foundation models aim for universal visual cortex, recipe is: (i) scale data + parameters + long schedules, (ii) enforce structural integrity of dense fields via second-order regularizers like Gram anchoring, (iii) preserve locality via high-ratio masking and online tokenization, and (iv) evaluate via frozen dense probes, not fine-tuned classification alone. In that sense, labels become unnecessary even for geometry — the world’s structure is enough to supervise itself.
