# High-Throughput Permissioned Ledgers with Narwhal-Bullshark DAG and Sui Move Object Model: Equivocation Resistance and Fast Path Execution

## Abstract
This thesis presents a comprehensive analysis of high-throughput permissioned ledger construction via the Narwhal mempool and Bullshark consensus DAG, integrated with the Sui Move object-centric data model for equivocation resistance and fast-path execution. We formalize Narwhal's reliable broadcast and certificate-based DAG as a decoupled availability layer that tolerates up to f Byzantine faults among 3f+1 validators while achieving 130k+ tx/s at sub-second latency in geo-distributed deployments. We then dissect Bullshark's partially-synchronous, anchor-based ordering over Narwhal's DAG, proving liveness under eventual synchrony and zero-message overhead over mempool dissemination. Extending to Sui, we characterize its object model — owned vs. shared objects, single-writer fast path with Byzantine consistent broadcast, and multi-writer consensus path via Bullshark — as a hybrid architecture that sidesteps total ordering for causally independent transactions. We prove safety of Sui's fast path under equivocation checks and provide complexity bounds, TLA+ specifications, and empirical validation strategies. The synthesis yields a principled blueprint for permissioned ledgers that separate data dissemination from ordering and exploit object semantics for latency-optimal execution.

## 1 Introduction

Permissioned ledgers underpin enterprise settlement, central bank digital currency pilots, and consortium supply-chain provenance, where validator sets are known but not trusted. Classical Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) state machine replication (SMR) protocols such as **PBFT** [1], **HotStuff** [2], and their pipelined variants entangle *data dissemination* with *ordering*, forcing a leader to broadcast blocks through consensus messages. This coupling caps throughput to leader bandwidth and introduces head-of-line blocking under equivocation.

**Narwhal-Bullshark**, introduced by Danezis et al. at Mysten Labs [3][4], decouples these concerns: *Narwhal* is a high-throughput DAG-based mempool providing reliable availability of transaction batches, while *Bullshark* imposes a total order over Narwhal's DAG without extra communication. Complementarily, **Sui** [5][6] reimagines state as *objects* with explicit ownership in **Move**, enabling a **fast path** for single-owner transactions via Byzantine consistent broadcast (BCB) and relegating only shared-object contention to the Bullshark consensus path.

This thesis makes four contributions:

- Formalizes Narwhal's certificate DAG, quorum intersection, and censorship-resilience, proving equivocation resistance under 2f+1 certificate thresholds.
- Provides a safety/liveness proof sketch for Bullshark under partial synchrony, with anchor election, vote types, and garbage collection analysis.
- Characterizes Sui's object model: owned, shared, immutable, and wrapped objects; Move's linear types; and the *fast-path / consensus-path* duality that achieves ~0.5s finality for 90%+ workloads.
- Presents TLA+, Rust, and Python executable specifications, empirical evaluation methodology, and limitations for permissioned deployments.

> **Theorem 1 (Decoupling Gain):** Separating mempool availability from consensus ordering increases throughput by a factor of Theta(n) over leader-based BFT where leader bandwidth is bottleneck, while preserving safety under n=3f+1 [3].

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Permissioned BFT Landscape

Permissioned ledgers assume n = 3f+1 validators with known identities via PKI, partial synchrony after Global Stabilization Time (GST) [7], and authenticated point-to-point channels. PBFT achieves O(n^2) message complexity; HotStuff reduces to O(n) with linear leader rotation and threshold signatures. Yet both remain leader-bottlenecked: transactions must flow through the leader's proposal.

DAG-based BFT, pioneered by **Hashgraph**, **Aleph**, **DAG-Rider** [8], and **Tusk** [3], replaces leader broadcast with all-to-all DAG dissemination. Each validator proposes vertices containing transaction batches; edges reference 2f+1 prior vertices, ensuring causality and availability.

### 2.2 Narwhal: Mempool as DAG

Narwhal [3] validators continuously build a round-based DAG:

1. **Batching:** Transactions are grouped into 500KB-1MB batches.
2. **Reliable Broadcast:** Each batch is disseminated via best-effort broadcast + 2f+1 acknowledgments forming a *certificate of availability* (CoA).
3. **DAG Vertex:** A vertex v at round r contains CoAs for 2f+1 vertices of round r-1 plus new batches.
4. **Advancement:** Validators advance round upon collecting 2f+1 certified vertices of current round.

Key invariants:

- **Availability:** If a CoA exists, at least f+1 honest validators store the data, enabling retrieval despite f faults.
- **Non-equivocation:** A validator cannot produce two certified vertices for same round without exposing double-signing, detectable via 2f+1 quorum intersection.
- **Integrity:** Every certified vertex's causal history is retrievable.

### 2.3 Bullshark: Ordering without Messages

Bullshark [4] is a *zero-overhead* consensus layered over Narwhal. No extra messages beyond Narwhal's DAG are needed. Periodically, pre-defined *anchors* (e.g., round r where r mod 2 == 0) are elected. Validators deterministically interpret DAG to commit anchors when 2f+1 vertices of round r+1 contain vote links supporting the anchor.

Two Bullshark variants exist:

- **Partially-synchronous Bullshark:** Requires 2f+1 steady-state votes; anchor commit after 2 rounds, providing predictable latency post-GST.
- **Asynchronous Bullshark (DAG-Rider style):** Uses random coin for anchor election, ensuring liveness even under asynchrony at cost of higher latency.

Sui Lutris [5] adopts partially-synchronous Bullshark for production.

### 2.4 Sui and Move Object Model

Sui [5][6] diverges from account-based (Ethereum) or UTXO models. State is a collection of *objects* with unique ID, owner field, version, and Move type:

- **Owned objects:** Single owner (Address or Object), linear ownership prevents double-spend. Fast-path executable via BCB.
- **Shared objects:** No exclusive owner, require consensus ordering (e.g., AMM pool, auction).
- **Immutable objects:** No owner, readable by all, never mutated.
- **Wrapped / dynamic fields:** Objects can own other objects, enabling composability.

**Move** [9] enforces linearity, no aliasing, and resource safety: objects cannot be duplicated or implicitly discarded. Sui Move extends Core Move with ID, transfer, and object lifecycle primitives.

> **Theorem 2 (Object Safety):** If an owned object O at version v is consumed by transaction T, any conflicting transaction T' != T consuming O_v will fail equivocation check at honest validators because quorum intersection requires 2f+1 signatures on distinct T, impossible without f+1 honest double-signs.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 System Model

We assume:

- n=3f+1 validators, f Byzantine, PKI, partial synchrony.
- Adversary can reorder/delay messages pre-GST but cannot break crypto.
- Sui validators maintain object store with versions.

### 3.2 Narwhal Formalization

Let DAG_r denote set of certified vertices at round r. Certificate C(v) is 2f+1 signatures on (digest(v), r, author). Quorum intersection lemma: any two quorums intersect in at least f+1 honest validators, ensures at most one certified vertex per (author, r) is retrievable without detection.

**TLA+ skeleton:**

```tla
----------------------------- MODULE NarwhalDAG -----------------------------
EXTENDS Integers, FiniteSets
CONSTANTS N, F, Validators
VARIABLES dag, certs, round

TypeOK == dag \in [Validators -> SUBSET [r: Nat, batches: SUBSET STRING]]
Certify(v) == Cardinality({s \in Validators: s signs v}) >= 2*F+1

Next == \/ \E v \in Validators: \E b \in Batches: 
           certs' = certs \cup {{v,b}}
        \/ \E v: round' = round[v]+1

Safety == \A v1,v2 \in certs: v1.author=v2.author /\ v1.r=v2.r => v1=v2
=============================================================================
```

### 3.3 Bullshark Ordering Rule

Anchors A_k at round r_k = 2k. Commit rule: A_k committed if >= f+1 vertices at r_k+1 with edges to A_k and >= 2f+1 vertices at r_k+2 supporting. This two-round pattern ensures:

- **Safety:** If honest commits A_k, no honest commits conflicting ordering of A_k's causal history.
- **Liveness:** Post-GST, every anchor eventually gets 2f+1 support, advancing commit.

### 3.4 Sui Fast Path vs Consensus Path

```rust
// Rust: equivocation check for owned object fast path
use std::collections::HashMap;

#[derive(Debug)]
struct ObjectVersion { id: String, version: u64, owner: String }

fn fast_path_verify(
    tx: &Transaction,
    object: &ObjectVersion,
    sigs: Vec<Signature>,
    quorum: usize
) -> bool {
    // 1. Check no double-spend in lock table
    if lock_table_contains(&object.id, &object.version) { return false; }
    // 2. Verify 2f+1 signatures on tx digest
    let valid_sigs = sigs.iter().filter(|s| s.valid_for(tx)).count();
    if valid_sigs < quorum { return false; }
    // 3. Ensure tx sender == object.owner
    tx.sender == object.owner
}

fn consensus_path(tx: &Transaction) -> bool {
    // Shared object → enqueue to Bullshark
    tx.inputs.iter().any(|o| o.is_shared())
}
```

Fast-path execution (owned objects only):

1. Client sends tx to validators.
2. Validators lock owned objects, sign tx, return cert.
3. Client aggregates 2f+1 cert -> *effects certificate* -> finality ~400-600ms.

Consensus path (>=1 shared object):

1. Tx forwarded to Narwhal, included in DAG.
2. Bullshark orders DAG, assigns shared-object version ordering.
3. Validators execute deterministically.

Hybridity ensures *causal independence* -> no global ordering.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Equivocation Resistance in Narwhal Certificates

Equivocation — a Byzantine author signing two conflicting vertices for same round — is central to DAG safety. Narwhal's *certificate* threshold 2f+1 ensures:

- Any two quorums intersect in at least f+1 honest validators.
- An equivocating vertex cannot obtain 2f+1 honest signatures without exposing f+1 honest double-votes, impossible.

**Detection protocol:** Validators gossip certificates; upon receiving two certificates C1, C2 for same (author, round) with different digests, they produce *equivocation proof* and slash/ban author in permissioned setting via governance vote.

**Table: Equivocation Mitigation Comparison**

| Protocol | Detection Mechanism | Cost | Finality Impact |
|----------|---------------------|------|-----------------|
| PBFT | View-change + 2f+1 | O(n^2) msgs | 3-phase commit delay |
| HotStuff | QC intersection | O(n) | 3-chain latency |
| Narwhal | CoA quorum (2f+1) | O(n) ack only | No ordering delay |
| Sui Fast Path | Lock table + cert | O(n) sigs | 1 RTT (~0.5s) |
| Sui Consensus | Bullshark anchor | Zero extra msgs | 2 DAG rounds |

### 4.2 Bullshark Anchor Commit Analysis

Bullshark's partially-synchronous path achieves:

- **Latency:** 2 DAG rounds ~ 2s in 100-validator WAN, vs. 3-4 rounds for Tusk.
- **Throughput:** Inherits Narwhal's 130k tx/s (512B tx) measured on t2.medium [3].
- **Fairness:** Leaderless — any validator's vertices can be anchors, preventing censorship.

**Vote types:** Bullshark distinguishes *steady-state* vs. *fallback* votes to avoid deadlock when anchors timeout. Fallback uses random coin (DAG-Rider style) for liveness under asynchrony.

> **Theorem 3 (Bullshark Safety):** If honest validator v commits anchor A_k, then any honest v' committing anchor A_{k'} with k' >= k will order A_k before A_{k'} and with identical causal histories. Proof via quorum intersection and DAG completeness lemma [4] (Lemma 4.2).

### 4.3 Sui Object Model: Fast Path Optimality

Sui's insight: *most* transactions in permissioned ledgers are *single-writer* — payment, NFT mint, capability transfer. These commute with other owners' transactions.

Fast-path characteristics:

- No consensus needed, only BCB: O(n) messages, 1 RTT.
- Equivocation prevented by per-validator lock table: lock_table[(object_id, version)] = tx_digest | None.
- Effects certificate is final: client can present it as proof of execution.

Shared-object path:

- AMM swap touches shared pool object -> needs total order to prevent race.
- Bullshark orders shared-object versions linearly, ensuring determinism.
- Sui Lutris [5] checkpoint protocol batches ~10k consensus txs into checkpoint, amortizing signature verification.

**Empirical from Sui mainnet [6]:** ~70-80% txs are owned-only, achieving ~0.5s finality; shared txs ~2-3s.

### 4.4 Permissioned Deployment Considerations

Permissioned ledgers demand:

- **Governance:** Validator rotation via on-chain ValidatorSet shared object, updated only via consensus path.
- **Compliance:** Move packages can enforce KYC via capability objects; private data via owned objects never exposed to consensus ordering.
- **Throughput isolation:** Narwhal's workers (batch makers) scale horizontally separate from primaries (DAG coordinators), enabling 10+ workers per validator in enterprise cloud.

**Complexities:**

- Memory: DAG retains history until garbage collected beyond committed anchor + causal horizon; Sui prunes via checkpoint snapshots.
- WAN: 100 validators across 5 regions still sustains >100k tx/s because Narwhal pipelines broadcast, unlike leader bottleneck.

### 4.5 Integration Blueprint

Proposed permissioned stack:

```
Client -> Sui JSON-RPC -> Narwhal Workers (batch) -> Narwhal Primary (DAG cert)
        -> Fast-path BCB (owned) -> Effects Cert
        -> Consensus (Bullshark over DAG) -> Checkpoint -> Object Store
```

Move package example (permissioned token with fast-path transfer):

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Safety Proofs (Sketch)

**Lemma 1 (Narwhal Availability):** If CoA for v exists, at least f+1 honest validators store v's batches. *Proof.* 2f+1 signatures include f+1 honest; honest stores upon signing [3].

**Lemma 2 (Bullshark Anchor Uniqueness):** No two conflicting anchors can be committed at same round. *Proof.* Requires f+1 honest votes each; quorum intersection forces overlap [4].

**Theorem 4 (Sui Fast-Path Safety):** Two conflicting fast-path txs consuming same owned object version cannot both obtain effects certificates. *Proof.* First tx locks object at f+1 honest; second tx's lock check fails at those honest, insufficient signatures.

### 5.2 Liveness

Post-GST, Narwhal advances rounds at network speed; Bullshark commits an anchor every 2 rounds; Sui checkpoint protocol commits every 3-4 anchors, ensuring liveness for shared objects.

### 5.3 Performance Model

Throughput:

T_Narwhal = min( n * BW_worker , CertRate / AvgBatchSize )

Measured [3]: n=50, BW=1Gbps, T ~ 130k tx/s, latency 2s.

Fast-path latency:

L_fast = 2*Delta + SigVerify ~ 0.4-0.6s

Consensus-path:

L_cons = L_Narwhal + 2*roundTime ~ 2-3s

### 5.4 Python Simulation: DAG Growth

```python
import random
from collections import defaultdict

def simulate_dag(n=4, f=1, rounds=10):
    dag = defaultdict(list)
    for r in range(rounds):
        # each validator creates vertex referencing 2f+1 previous
        for v in range(n):
            if r==0:
                dag[r].append((v, []))
            else:
                parents = random.sample(dag[r-1], min(2*f+1, len(dag[r-1])))
                dag[r].append((v, parents))
        print(f"Round {r}: {len(dag[r])} vertices, avg parents {sum(len(p) for _,p in dag[r])/len(dag[r]):.1f}")
    # commit analysis: anchor at even rounds
    committed=0
    for r in range(0, rounds-1, 2):
        # simplistic: if 2f+1 support in r+1
        if len(dag[r+1]) >= 2*f+1:
            committed+=1
    print(f"Anchors committed: {committed}/{rounds//2}")

simulate_dag(n=7, f=2, rounds=8)
```

Output shows linear DAG growth and predictable commit rate.

## 6 Limitations

- **Permissioned trust assumption:** n=3f+1 PKI; dynamic validator churn requires shared-object governance tx, adding latency.
- **Garbage collection:** Narwhal DAG cannot prune below last committed anchor's causal history without risking availability proofs; Sui's checkpoint snapshots mitigate but increase storage to ~TB-scale over months.
- **Shared-object contention:** High contention workloads (e.g., popular AMM) revert to consensus bottleneck; fast-path benefit limited to 70-80% workload mix.
- **Move expressiveness:** Linear types prevent some patterns (e.g., temporary aliasing) requiring dynamic_field indirection, adding gas cost.
- **WAN fairness:** Under uneven bandwidth, low-bandwidth validators' vertices may be consistently excluded from 2f+1 quorum, causing censorship; requires worker load balancing.
- **Formal verification gap:** Bullshark's TLA+ spec covers safety but not full liveness under asynchrony; Sui Lutris machine-checked proofs remain partial [5].

## 7 Conclusion

We have dissected high-throughput permissioned ledgers built on **Narwhal-Bullshark DAG** with **Sui Move object semantics**. By decoupling dissemination from ordering and exploiting object ownership for fast-path execution, the architecture achieves throughput an order of magnitude above leader-based BFT while preserving equivocation resistance via quorum certificates and lock tables.

Key takeaways:

- Narwhal's 2f+1 CoA provides provable availability and non-equivocation without consensus.
- Bullshark imposes ordering with zero extra messages, committing anchors every two DAG rounds post-GST.
- Sui's owned/shared object duality yields latency-optimal execution: ~0.5s for single-writer, ~2-3s for shared.
- Permissioned deployments benefit from worker scaling, governance via shared ValidatorSet, and Move's linear safety for compliance tokens.

Future work includes adaptive anchor selection under WAN variance, zero-knowledge Move objects for privacy-preserving permissioned ledgers, and full Lean4 formalization of Sui Lutris checkpoint safety.
