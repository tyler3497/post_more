# Circuit Quantum Electrodynamics: Transmon Qubits, Dispersive Readout, and Surface-Code Integration

## Abstract

Circuit quantum electrodynamics (cQED) has emerged as the dominant physical platform for scalable quantum computation, wedding the quantum optics of the Jaynes–Cummings model to lithographically defined superconducting circuits. This thesis examines the transmon qubit — a Cooper-pair box operated at large Josephson-to-charging energy ratio $E_J/E_C$, where charge dispersion is suppressed exponentially while anharmonicity decays only as a weak power law — its dispersive coupling to microwave resonators for quantum-non-demolition readout, and the embedding of transmon lattices into the surface code. We derive the dispersive Hamiltonian via the Schrieffer–Wolff transformation, analyze the measurement-induced ionization bounding photon number and readout fidelity, quantify the Purcell limit on relaxation, and survey tunable-coupler two-qubit gates. We then map device physics onto fault tolerance: syndrome-extraction cycles, real-time decoding under 1.1 µs cycle constraints, and empirical below-threshold demonstrations achieving $\Lambda > 2$ at distances 5 and 7. Drawing on coherence milestones from 2007 through 2026 — relaxation times growing from microseconds to milliseconds — we assess the distance to practical fault tolerance and the remaining walls: correlated error floors, leakage, readout crosstalk, and the fixed-versus-tunable frequency trade-off.

---

## 1. Introduction

The question that organizes modern superconducting quantum engineering is deceptively simple: *how does one measure a qubit without destroying it?* Quantum error correction demands repeated extraction of error syndromes — classical parity bits — while preserving an encoded logical state the measurement must never collapse [1][7].

Circuit quantum electrodynamics supplies the answer that has scaled best. In cQED, an artificial atom — a nonlinear superconducting circuit built from Josephson junctions — interacts with the quantized field of a microwave resonator. Because the dipole coupling $\hbar g$ reaches several percent of the photon energy, the system realizes strong-coupling cavity QED with *engineered* parameters [1]. When qubit and resonator are far detuned, $|\Delta| = |\omega_q - \omega_r| \gg g$, the interaction takes the dispersive form $H_{\text{int}} = \hbar \chi a^\dagger a \,\sigma_z$: the resonator frequency depends on the qubit state, and the two commute [1][2]. A microwave tone reflected from the resonator therefore reports the qubit state in a quantum-non-demolition (QND) fashion [3][9].

The transmon, introduced by Koch *et al.* in 2007, made this regime practical [2]. Shunting the junction of a Cooper-pair box with a large capacitance ($E_J/E_C \sim 20$–$100$) trades a small, power-law loss of anharmonicity for *exponential* suppression of charge-noise sensitivity — the dominant dephasing channel of earlier charge qubits [2][3]. The device became the workhorse of the field: it underlies Google's Sycamore and Willow processors [6], IBM's Eagle and Condor lines, and record devices with $T_1$ beyond a millisecond [4][5].

But the surface code — the leading practical code — demands more: *repeatable, high-fidelity syndrome measurements* on a 2D lattice, decoded in real time on microsecond timescales [6][7]. The 2023–2025 demonstrations of below-threshold surface-code memories on transmon processors, with $\Lambda > 2$ at distances 5 and 7 [6], mark where cQED crossed from "improving components" to "fault tolerance demonstrated." This thesis traces that arc: from the transmon Hamiltonian, through dispersive measurement physics, to surface-code integration and the empirical record.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 From Cooper-pair box to transmon

The Cooper-pair box consists of a superconducting island connected to a reservoir via a Josephson junction, with Hamiltonian

$$H = 4E_C (\hat{n} - n_g)^2 - E_J \cos \hat{\varphi},$$

where $E_C = e^2/2C_\Sigma$ is the charging energy, $E_J$ the Josephson energy, $\hat{n}$ the transferred Cooper-pair number, and $n_g$ the offset charge [2]. At $E_J/E_C \sim 1$, the bands are strongly dispersive in $n_g$, making the qubit exquisitely sensitive to ubiquitous $1/f$ charge noise — coherence was limited to hundreds of nanoseconds [4].

Koch *et al.* recognized that the spectrum of this Mathieu equation flattens exponentially with $E_J/E_C$: the charge dispersion of level $m$ scales as

$$\epsilon_m \propto E_C \left(\frac{2^{4m+5}}{m!}\right)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} \left(\frac{E_J}{2E_C}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+\frac{3}{4}} e^{-\sqrt{8E_J/E_C}},$$

while the anharmonicity $\alpha = \omega_{12} - \omega_{01} \approx -E_C$ decays only algebraically [2]. At $E_J/E_C = 50$, charge dispersion falls below 1 kHz — negligible against typical linewidths — while $\alpha \sim -200$ to $-350$ MHz remains sufficient for selective control [2][3]. Schreier *et al.* verified the exponential suppression experimentally, observing homogeneously broadened transitions with microsecond coherence [3].

> **Theorem (Exponential charge insensitivity):** For a transmon with $E_J/E_C \gg 1$, the sensitivity of every eigenenergy to offset charge $n_g$ vanishes as $\exp(-\sqrt{8E_J/E_C})$, whereas the anharmonicity magnitude $|\alpha| \approx E_C \propto (E_J E_C)^{1/4}\cdot (E_C/E_J)^{1/4}$ degrades only as a power law. Hence charge dephasing can be driven below all other dephasing channels with modest loss of addressability [2].

Two variants dominate practice [4]:
- **Fixed-frequency transmons** — single-junction devices, immune to flux noise; two-qubit gates rely on microwave-activated interactions (cross-resonance) or tunable couplers.
- **Tunable transmons** — a SQUID loop gives $E_J(\Phi) = E_{J,\max} |\cos(\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)|$; fast flux pulses realize high-fidelity CZ gates, at the price of $1/f$ flux-noise sensitivity away from the sweet spot.

### 2.2 The Jaynes–Cummings Hamiltonian in circuits

A transmon coupled capacitively to a microwave resonator realizes, after truncating to the qubit subspace, the Jaynes–Cummings model [1]:

$$H_{\text{JC}} = \hbar \omega_r a^\dagger a + \frac{\hbar \omega_q}{2}\sigma_z + \hbar g (a \sigma_+ + a^\dagger \sigma_-),$$

with $\omega_r/2\pi \sim 5$–$8$ GHz, $\omega_q/2\pi \sim 4$–$6$ GHz, and $g/2\pi \sim 50$–$200$ MHz — couplings roughly $10^3$ times larger than in atomic cavity QED [1]. The multi-level nature of the transmon matters: virtual transitions through $|f\rangle$ and higher states contribute to the effective parameters, and at specific detunings the partial contributions to the dispersive shift add constructively, producing the *straddling regime* with anomalously large $\chi$ [8].

## 3. Methodology

Our analysis follows the standard perturbative reduction of circuit QED and confronts it with published experimental data, in three stages:

1. **Hamiltonian reduction.** From the circuit Lagrangian [1], we quantize, truncate the transmon to its lowest levels, and apply a Schrieffer–Wolff transformation $U = e^{S}$ with $S \propto (g/\Delta)(a\sigma_+ - a^\dagger\sigma_-)$ to eliminate direct exchange to first order in $g/\Delta$. Terms to $\mathcal{O}((g/\Delta)^2)$ yield the dispersive Hamiltonian; fourth-order corrections [8] set the breakdown scale.

2. **Measurement theory.** Readout is modeled as homodyne/heterodyne detection of the resonator field, with added noise characterized by the quantum efficiency $\eta = 1/(N_T + 1) \le 1$ [1]. Readout fidelity is $F = 1 - [P(e|g) + P(g|e)]/2$, bounded by Purcell decay, photon-shot-noise dephasing, and measurement-induced transitions to non-computational states [9].

3. **Code integration and empirical synthesis.** We describe the surface-code stabilizer cycle on square transmon lattices [6][7], then compile published coherence, gate, and logical-error data (2007–2026) to test the chain "device physics $\to$ physical error rates $\to$ logical performance" against the threshold theorem.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Transmon Hamiltonian and Charge-Noise Insensitivity

In the phase representation, $[\hat{\varphi}, \hat{n}] = i$, the transmon wavefunction spreads over many charge states and the $-E_J\cos\hat{\varphi}$ potential binds it near $\varphi = 0$ with plasma frequency $\omega_p = \sqrt{8E_J E_C}/\hbar$. Expanding,

$$H \approx 4E_C \hat{n}^2 + \frac{1}{2}E_J \hat{\varphi}^2 - \frac{E_J}{24}\hat{\varphi}^4,$$

a weakly anharmonic (Duffing) oscillator: nearly harmonic levels split by the quartic Kerr term $K \approx -E_C/2$ per quantum [2]. The relative anharmonicity $\alpha/\omega_{01} \approx -\sqrt{E_C/8E_J} \sim -5\%$ is the price paid; in exchange, $1/f$ charge noise — the channel that limited the Cooper-pair box — is eliminated to first order at *every* operating point, not just at a sweet spot [2][3]. This "set and forget" character is what made frequency-multiplexed readout of dozens of qubits on one feedline practical.

A key subtlety: the transmon is *not* a true two-level system. The $|e\rangle \leftrightarrow |f\rangle$ transition, detuned from the resonator by $\Delta + \alpha$, shifts the dispersive physics quantitatively — and when the resonator frequency lies between $\omega_{ge}$ and $\omega_{ef}$, both virtual transitions contribute to $\chi$ with the same sign, the *straddling regime* with anomalously large dispersive shifts [8].

```python
# Exact diagonalization of the transmon in the charge basis (truncated)
import numpy as np

def transmon_hamiltonian(EJ, EC, ng, N=25):
    """H = 4EC (n - ng)^2 - EJ cos(phi), charge basis, N states."""
    n = np.arange(-N, N + 1)
    H = np.diag(4 * EC * (n - ng) ** 2).astype(complex)
    # cos(phi) = (e^{iphi} + e^{-iphi})/2 couples n <-> n+1
    off = np.ones(2 * N)
    H += -EJ / 2 * (np.diag(off, 1) + np.diag(off, -1))
    return H

def charge_dispersion(EJ, EC, m=1):
    """Peak-to-peak dispersion of level m over ng in [0, 0.5]."""
    Es = []
    for ng in np.linspace(0, 0.5, 41):
        w = np.linalg.eigvalsh(transmon_hamiltonian(EJ, EC, ng))
        Es.append(w[m])
    return max(Es) - min(Es)

for ratio in [10, 20, 50]:
    EJ, EC = ratio, 1.0
    print(f"EJ/EC={ratio:3d}  dispersion(E1)={charge_dispersion(EJ, EC):.3e} EC")
```

The output reproduces the exponential collapse of dispersion with $E_J/E_C$ and the $\sim E_C$ saturation of anharmonicity — the core trade defining the device [2][3].

### 4.2 Jaynes–Cummings Coupling and the Dispersive Regime

In the dispersive limit $|\Delta| \gg g\sqrt{\bar{n}+1}$, the Schrieffer–Wolff transformation yields [1][8]

$$H_{\text{disp}} \approx \hbar(\omega_r + \chi \sigma_z) a^\dagger a + \frac{\hbar}{2}(\tilde{\omega}_q + \chi)\sigma_z,$$

with the dispersive shift $\chi = g^2/\Delta$ (two-level atom) and Lamb-shifted qubit frequency $\tilde{\omega}_q = \omega_q + g^2/\Delta$. For a multilevel transmon,

$$\chi = \frac{g_{ge}^2}{\Delta_{ge}} - \frac{g_{ef}^2}{2\,\Delta_{ef}}, \qquad \Delta_{ge} = \omega_{ge}-\omega_r,\; \Delta_{ef} = \omega_{ef}-\omega_r,$$

capturing the competing virtual transitions [8]. The interaction $\hbar\chi a^\dagger a\, \sigma_z$ is the workhorse of cQED: it *commutes with $\sigma_z$*, so probing the resonator extracts qubit information without exchanging energy with the qubit — a QND measurement [1][9].

The approximation breaks down near the critical photon number $n_{\text{crit}} = \Delta^2/(4g^2)$, where the expansion in $(g/\Delta)\sqrt{n}$ fails and the QND character degrades [1].

> **Theorem (Dispersive QND measurement):** In the regime $|\Delta| \gg g\sqrt{\bar{n}+1}$ with $\bar{n} \ll n_{\text{crit}}$, the effective interaction $H_{\text{int}} = \hbar\chi a^\dagger a\,\sigma_z$ satisfies $[H_{\text{int}}, \sigma_z] = 0$; repeated measurements of the resonator field therefore return identical qubit outcomes up to backaction-free photon-shot-noise dephasing. Violations scale with multiphoton resonances that drive measurement-induced transitions [1][9].

This theorem is the foundation of everything that follows: because the readout observable commutes with the computational basis, syndrome measurements in the surface code can be repeated indefinitely without disturbing the encoded logical state [6].

### 4.3 Dispersive Readout: Quantum Efficiency and the Purcell Limit

A practical readout chain sends a microwave tone at $\omega_r$ into the resonator; the reflected phase encodes $\sigma_z$, since the resonator presents $\omega_r + \chi$ for $|e\rangle$ and $\omega_r - \chi$ for $|g\rangle$. After a circulator, the signal passes through a quantum-limited parametric amplifier, a cryogenic HEMT, mixer, and ADC [1]. The integrated-quadrature SNR grows as

$$\text{SNR} \propto \eta \, \kappa \, \bar{n} \, \tau_m \left(\frac{2\chi}{\kappa}\right)^2 \Big/ \left[1 + \left(\frac{2\chi}{\kappa}\right)^2\right],$$

peaked at $2\chi/\kappa \approx 1$: the dispersive shift should match the resonator linewidth [1][4]. State-of-the-art chains reach $\eta \sim 0.3$–$0.6$ and single-shot fidelities above 99% in $\sim 100$–$300$ ns [4][9].

Two fundamental ceilings bound this performance:

- **The Purcell effect.** Resonator coupling opens a radiative decay channel: $T_1^{\text{Purcell}} \approx (\Delta/g)^2 \kappa^{-1}$ [1]. Purcell filters — microwave band-stop structures between qubit and readout line — suppress this decay while passing the measurement tone, and are standard on high-coherence devices [4][5].
- **Measurement-induced ionization.** At moderate drive strengths, multiphoton resonances drive the transmon into highly excited states, degrading the QND character the dispersive approximation promised [9]. Chapple *et al.* showed this follows generically from the non-QND parent Hamiltonian, and that *longitudinal* coupling ($H_{\text{int}} \propto \sigma_z (a + a^\dagger)$) can suppress these transitions because its strength is detuning-independent — enabling fast, high-fidelity QND readout in principle [9]. For conventional dispersive readout, ionization sets the practical ceiling on $\bar{n}$ and hence on speed.

```haskell
-- Pauli-frame model of a dispersive measurement round
data Qubit = G | E deriving (Eq, Show)
data Outcome = ClickG | ClickE deriving (Eq, Show)

-- readout fidelity: F = 1 - (P(e|g) + P(g|e)) / 2
fidelity :: Double -> Double -> Double
fidelity p_e_given_g p_g_given_e = 1 - (p_e_given_g + p_g_given_e) / 2

-- QND check: ideal dispersive readout preserves the pre-measurement state
qndRepeat :: Qubit -> [Outcome] -> Bool
qndRepeat G os = all (== ClickG) os
qndRepeat E os = all (== ClickE) os
```

### 4.4 Two-Qubit Gates, Tunable Coupling, and Crosstalk

Single-qubit control is the solved part: DRAG-shaped microwave pulses suppressing leakage to $|f\rangle$ achieve fidelities above 99.99% [5]. Entanglement is harder. The leading approaches on transmon lattices are [4][5]:

1. **Flux-tuned CZ.** Tunable transmons are brought near an avoided crossing with $|ee\rangle \leftrightarrow |gf\rangle$; conditional phase accumulates at tens of megahertz, yielding CZ gates in 20–60 ns with fidelities 99.7–99.85% on Willow-class hardware [6] and up to 99.95% with leakage-reduction protocols [5].
2. **Cross-resonance (CR).** On fixed-frequency qubits, driving the control qubit at the target's frequency induces an effective $ZX$ interaction; echoed CR sequences produce CNOT-equivalent gates without flux lines [4].
3. **Parametric gates.** Modulating a tunable coupler or qubit frequency at the difference frequency activates $i$SWAP or CZ interactions while idle qubits stay far detuned, mitigating always-on $ZZ$ crosstalk [4].

The common enemy is *crosstalk and frequency crowding*: spectator qubits, residual $ZZ$ coupling, and drive-line leakage convert single-qubit operations into correlated multi-qubit errors — the error class surface-code decoders handle worst [6].

```rust
// Minimal model: CZ error budget on a tunable-transmon pair
struct CzBudget {
    decoherence: f64,   // T1/T2 during gate time
    leakage: f64,       // population leaving {|g>,|e>}
    control: f64,       // pulse distortion, flux noise
    crosstalk: f64,     // spectator ZZ, drive bleed
}

impl CzBudget {
    fn total_error(&self) -> f64 {
        // first-order: independent error channels add
        self.decoherence + self.leakage + self.control + self.crosstalk
    }
    fn fidelity(&self) -> f64 { 1.0 - self.total_error() }
}

fn main() {
    let willow_class = CzBudget { decoherence: 0.0012, leakage: 0.0004,
                                  control: 0.0006, crosstalk: 0.0003 };
    println!("CZ fidelity: {:.4}", willow_class.fidelity()); // ~0.9975
}
```

### 4.5 Surface-Code Stabilizer Extraction on a Transmon Lattice

The surface code encodes one logical qubit in a $d \times d$ patch of data qubits ($d^2$ data, $d^2 - 1$ measure qubits), with $X$- and $Z$-type stabilizers on alternating plaquettes [6][7].

1. **Reset** measure qubits (active or unconditional reset).
2. **Entangle** each measure qubit with its four data-qubit neighbors via CZ gates in fixed N–W–E–S order — scheduling avoids hook errors that would halve the effective distance.
3. **Measure** all measure qubits simultaneously via frequency-multiplexed dispersive readout [1][6].
4. **Decode** the syndrome history in real time — on Willow, the 1.1 µs cycle budget forced decoder latency into the microsecond regime, met with an integrated real-time decoder [6].

> **Theorem (Threshold):** If every elementary operation fails independently with probability $p < p_{\text{th}}$, the logical error rate of a distance-$d$ surface code satisfies $\varepsilon_L = \mathcal{O}\!\left((p/p_{\text{th}})^{(d+1)/2}\right)$: exponential suppression in $d$. For circuit-level depolarizing noise, $p_{\text{th}} \approx 0.75$–$1\%$ [6][7].

The threshold theorem is an *asymptotic* promise; the engineering question is whether physical error rates sit far enough below threshold that modest $d$ suffices.

A TLA+ sketch of the syndrome cycle's safety property — stabilizers commute with the logical operators, so measurement never disturbs the encoded state:


## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

The empirical record of cQED is a twenty-year exponential in coherence, punctuated by architectural leaps [4][5]:

| Year | Milestone | $T_1$ / fidelity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2007 | Transmon proposed (Koch *et al.*) | theory: charge dispersion $<1$ kHz at $E_J/E_C=50$ | [2] |
| 2008 | First transmon device (Schreier *et al.*) | $\mu$s-scale $T_1, T_2$ | [3] |
| 2011 | 3D transmon (Paik *et al.*) | $T_1 > 60\,\mu$s | [4] |
| 2019 | Sycamore, 53 qubits | computational advantage demo | [5] |
| 2021 | Tantalum transmon (Place *et al.*) | $T_1 > 300\,\mu$s planar | [5] |
| 2023 | Distance-5 surface code below threshold | $\Lambda = 2.14$, 49 data qubits | [7] |
| 2024/25 | Willow: distance-5 & 7 below threshold, real-time decode | $\Lambda > 2$; CZ 99.7–99.85% | [6] |
| 2025 | Tantalum-on-silicon 2D transmon (Bland *et al.*) | $T_1$ up to $1.68$ ms, $Q$ to $1.5\times10^7$ | [5] |
| 2025 | Aalto planar transmon (Tuokkola *et al.*) | $T_{2,\text{echo}} = 1.06$ ms | [5] |
| 2025 | IQM CZ with leakage reduction | 2Q fidelity 99.95% (max) | [5] |

Three results deserve emphasis as *proofs* rather than milestones:

1. **Below-threshold scaling is real.** Willow's logical error per cycle *decreased* with distance — consistent with $\Lambda > 2$ — on processors with mean $T_1 = 68\,\mu$s and $T_{2,\text{CPMG}} = 89\,\mu$s [6]. This is the empirical content of the threshold theorem: hardware whose logical qubit outlives its constituents.
2. **Real-time decoding at speed.** Fast superconducting cycles (1.1 µs) are an advantage for code rate but brutal for the decoder. Below-threshold operation with an *integrated real-time* decoder — not offline post-processing — removed the last "in principle" qualifier [6].
3. **The exponential was materials-driven.** The jump from ~20 µs to millisecond coherence came from surface-loss engineering — tantalum films, substrate cleaning, participation-ratio optimization — not new qubit topologies [5].

## 6. Limitations

The same experiments that proved below-threshold operation exposed the walls [6]:

- **Correlated error floors.** High-distance repetition codes on Willow revealed logical errors dominated by *correlated* events occurring roughly once per hour, of unknown origin, setting a floor near $10^{-10}$ [6]. The threshold theorem assumes independent errors; a distance-independent correlated floor would eventually halt exponential suppression. Cosmic rays, chip-scale phonon bursts, and TLS avalanches are suspects — identifying them is the field's most pressing experimental mystery.
- **Leakage.** Transmons leak to $|f\rangle$ during CZ gates and readout ionization [9]; leakage is neither Pauli nor caught by standard stabilizers, and it propagates. Leakage-reduction units and the PALEA protocol mitigate it [5], at cycle-time cost.
- **Readout crosstalk and ionization.** Multiplexed readout couples measurement photons into spectator qubits; readout drives can ionize *neighboring* qubits and lower their ionization thresholds [9]. Hundreds of simultaneous readouts demand ever-more-careful frequency planning.
- **Fixed vs. tunable trade-off.** Tunable transmons pay flux-noise dephasing for fast CZ gates; fixed-frequency devices pay gate-speed and crowding costs for coherence. Tunable couplers split the difference but add calibration complexity growing with lattice size [4][5].
- **Overhead.** A distance-7 logical qubit consumed 105 physical qubits for a *memory* — no computation. Practical algorithms need thousands of logical qubits plus magic-state factories; million-physical-qubit estimates assume error rates somewhat better than today's.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Circuit QED's arc — from the Jaynes–Cummings model on a chip, through the transmon's exponential charge-noise suppression [2][3], dispersive QND readout [1][9], and into below-threshold surface-code memories with real-time decoding [6] — is the most complete realization to date of the fault-tolerance program. The enabling physics is perturbative and well understood: a Schrieffer–Wolff transformation, a commuting measurement interaction, and a topological code whose stabilizers respect it [1][7][8].

The remaining challenges are no longer about whether quantum error correction *works* — Willow settled that — but about the *correlated, non-Pauli, and leakage* channels the standard theory assumes away [6][9], and the brutal arithmetic of overhead. Longitudinal readout [9], millisecond planar transmons [5], and 99.95% two-qubit gates [5] each attack one wall. The next decisive experiment will not be a larger $\Lambda$ but a logical *gate* — lattice surgery between two below-threshold memories — executed faster than the errors it must survive. When that succeeds, cQED crosses from fault-tolerant memory to fault-tolerant computation.
