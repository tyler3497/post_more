# The Cosmic Web: Large-Scale Structure Formation, the Halo Model, Baryon Acoustic Oscillations, and Redshift-Space Distortions in Survey Cosmology

## Abstract

The large-scale distribution of galaxies traces a web of filaments, walls, nodes, and voids whose statistics encode gravitational instability, the nature of dark matter and dark energy, and the expansion history of the Universe. This thesis unifies four pillars of survey cosmology. First, linear and mildly nonlinear gravitational instability supplies the language of density perturbations, the growth factor, and the matter power spectrum. Second, the halo model decomposes nonlinear clustering into one-halo and two-halo terms, bridging perturbation theory and N-body simulations [1][7]. Third, baryon acoustic oscillations imprint a standard ruler at the sound-horizon scale, anchoring expansion-history measurements to sub-percent precision in surveys such as DESI [2][8]. Fourth, redshift-space distortions break statistical isotropy in observed catalogs, yielding the Kaiser enhancement on large scales and fingers of God on small scales, and thereby measuring the growth rate of structure [4][5]. Together these tools convert galaxy redshift catalogs into precision tests of Lambda-CDM and beyond [3].

## 1 Introduction

When we map the three-dimensional positions of millions of galaxies, a striking architecture emerges: luminous matter arranges itself into a *cosmic web*, a sponge-like network of dense filaments and sheets enclosing vast, nearly empty voids, with galaxy clusters sitting at the web's nodes. This pattern is not decorative; it is a fossil record of gravitational instability acting over cosmic time on tiny primordial density fluctuations seeded by inflation. Understanding the cosmic web quantitatively is the central enterprise of large-scale structure (LSS) cosmology, and it underpins the most stringent current tests of the standard Lambda-CDM model.

This thesis synthesizes four interlocking subjects that any practitioner of survey cosmology must master. **Gravitational instability** provides the dynamical engine: linear perturbation theory predicts how density contrast fields grow, while N-body simulations extend predictions into the deeply nonlinear regime [7]. **The halo model** gives these dynamics a remarkably economical analytic form by positing that all mass resides in virialized dark matter halos, so that any correlation statistic decomposes into intra-halo and inter-halo contributions [1]. **Baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO)** are relic sound waves from the primordial photon-baryon plasma, frozen into the matter distribution at a known comoving scale of approximately 150 Mpc; measuring their apparent size at various redshifts yields a standard ruler for cosmic distances [2][9]. **Redshift-space distortions (RSD)** arise because observed redshifts mix cosmological expansion with peculiar velocities, imprinting anisotropy that directly measures the growth rate of structure [4][5].

> **Theorem (Gravitational Amplification):** In a matter-dominated universe, sub-horizon adiabatic density perturbations in the linear regime grow proportionally to the cosmic scale factor, so that primordial fluctuations of amplitude one part in one hundred thousand amplify by three orders of magnitude between recombination and the present.

The remainder of this thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 reviews the essential background: the statistics of random fields, the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) cosmology, and halo statistics. Section 3 describes the methodology of modern survey cosmology, from spectroscopic redshift catalogs through mock universes to covariance estimation. Section 4 develops the four pillars in depth. Section 5 marshals empirical evidence, including the DESI Data Release 1 BAO results that now challenge Lambda-CDM at the two-to-four sigma level [2][3]. Section 6 discusses limitations honestly, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The cosmological stage

The background Universe is described by the FLRW metric with scale factor *a(t)*, Hubble rate *H = ȧ/a*, and energy budget dominated today by matter (fraction Omega_m ≈ 0.3), a cosmological constant or dynamical dark energy (Omega_Lambda ≈ 0.7), and trace radiation and neutrinos. Density contrasts are defined as delta(**x**, t) = (rho(**x**, t) - rho-bar(t)) / rho-bar(t). On large scales these are small and evolve linearly; on scales below a few Mpc they reach order unity and collapse into gravitationally bound halos.

The fundamental summary statistic is the **matter power spectrum** P(k), the Fourier transform of the two-point correlation function xi(r):

- P(k) quantifies variance of the density field as a function of wavenumber k.
- Its shape on large scales preserves the imprint of inflation and the transfer function; its amplitude grows as the square of the linear growth factor D(a).
- Galaxy surveys measure a biased tracer version, P_g(k) = b^2 P(k) plus nonlinear corrections, where b is the linear bias.

### 2.2 Statistical language

Modern LSS is a field-theoretic and statistical discipline. The density field is treated as a random realization of an underlying ensemble. Key objects include:

1. **Two-point functions** xi(r) and P(k) — the workhorses of BAO and RSD analyses.
2. **Three-point functions and bispectra** — sensitive to nonlinear gravitational evolution and primordial non-Gaussianity.
3. **Higher-order moments** — counts-in-cells and Minkowski functionals probe topology beyond perturbation theory.

Because galaxies are biased, discrete tracers, the mapping from the dark matter field to the observed galaxy field is parameterized through the halo occupation distribution (HOD): the probability that a halo of mass M hosts a central and a set of satellite galaxies, constrained by clustering measurements [6].

### 2.3 Halos as the unit of nonlinear structure

Numerical N-body simulations establish that collisionless dark matter collapses into quasi-universal virialized halos with density profiles well described by the Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) form, rho(r) proportional to the inverse of (r/r_s)(1 + r/r_s)^2, where r_s is a scale radius. Halo abundances follow mass functions calibrated from simulations, and halo clustering is biased relative to matter by a mass-dependent factor b_h(M) derived from extended Press-Schechter theory. These three ingredients — abundance, profile, bias — are the complete input to the halo model [1].

| Component | Typical halo mass (solar masses) | Bias | Typical occupation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dwarf galaxies | 10^10 to 10^11 | less than 1 | 1 central |
| Milky-Way-like galaxies | 10^11 to 10^12 | about 1 | 1 central plus few satellites |
| Galaxy groups | 10^13 to 10^14 | 1 to 3 | tens of satellites |
| Rich clusters | above 10^14 | 3 to 5 | hundreds of satellites |

## 3 Methodology

A modern survey cosmology analysis proceeds through a disciplined pipeline whose every stage must be controlled to extract percent-level cosmological constraints.

1. **Catalog construction.** Spectroscopic surveys such as DESI measure redshifts for tens of millions of targets — bright galaxies, luminous red galaxies (LRGs), emission-line galaxies (ELGs), quasars, and Lyman-alpha forest sightlines — converting each spectrum into a three-dimensional comoving position under a fiducial cosmology [2].
2. **Reconstruction.** Nonlinear displacements that blur the BAO feature are partially reversed by estimating the Lagrangian displacement field and shifting galaxies back toward their initial positions, sharpening the acoustic peak [2].
3. **Two-point estimation.** The correlation function or power spectrum is measured with Landy-Szalay-type estimators against large random catalogs encoding the survey selection function, decomposed into multipoles (monopole, quadrupole, hexadecapole) that separately constrain isotropic and anisotropic information.
4. **Modeling.** The measured clustering is fit with a template combining the halo model or effective field theory of LSS for broadband shape, a BAO feature model with Gaussian damping of the acoustic peak, and RSD models (Kaiser plus finger-of-God damping) [4][5][8].
5. **Mock universes.** Thousands of synthetic catalogs (e.g., Abacus and EZmock suites for DESI) validate the pipeline end to end and furnish covariance matrices [2].
6. **Blinding.** To mitigate confirmation bias, DESI applied a catalog-level blinding shift to redshifts, with analysis choices frozen before unblinding [2].
7. **Cosmological inference.** BAO distance measurements D_M/r_d and D_H/r_d in redshift bins, combined with CMB and supernova data, constrain the expansion history and dark energy models [2][3].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Gravitational Instability: From Perturbations to the Cosmic Web

The linearized continuity, Euler, and Poisson equations for a pressureless fluid in an expanding universe combine into a single second-order equation for the density contrast of each Fourier mode:

delta-double-dot + 2H delta-dot = 4 pi G rho-bar delta.

The growing solution D(a) is the linear growth factor; its logarithmic derivative f = d ln D / d ln a is the linear growth rate, well approximated in Lambda-CDM by f ≈ Omega_m(a)^0.55 with the growth index gamma ≈ 0.55 distinguishing general relativity from modified-gravity alternatives [4].

As fluctuations exceed order unity, mode coupling generates the cosmic web. Lagrangian perturbation theory — the Zel'dovich approximation and its higher-order extensions — predicts pancake and filament collapse from the deformation tensor eigenvalues, explaining why the web's geometry is triaxial rather than spherical. Full N-body simulations resolve this regime exactly for cold dark matter and serve both as truth data and as training sets for emulators that interpolate cosmological parameter grids [7].

> **Theorem (Kaiser 1987):** In the distant-observer limit, linear redshift-space distortions enhance the angle-dependent galaxy power spectrum by the factor (1 + beta mu^2)^2, where mu is the cosine of the angle between the wavevector and the line of sight, and beta = f/b is the redshift-distortion parameter. Angle-averaging yields an enhancement of (1 + 2 beta/3 + beta^2/5) [4][5].

The linear growth rate is not merely a fitting parameter: it is the bridge between geometry and dynamics. BAO measures distances; RSD measures how fast structure grows at those distances. Their combination tests whether cosmic acceleration is driven by dark energy within general relativity or by a modification of gravity itself.

### 4.2 The Halo Model: An Analytic Theory of Nonlinear Clustering

The halo model begins from a radical simplification: *all mass is associated with virialized halos* [1]. Given three calibrated ingredients — the halo mass function dn/dM, the halo bias b(M), and the normalized halo density profile u(r|M) with Fourier transform u(k|M) — every clustering statistic follows by decomposition into pairs residing in the same halo and pairs in distinct halos:

P(k) = P^{1h}(k) + P^{2h}(k),

with the one-halo term

P^{1h}(k) = integral dM (dn/dM) (M/rho-bar)^2 |u(k|M)|^2,

and the two-halo term

P^{2h}(k) = P_lin(k) [integral dM (dn/dM) (M/rho-bar) b(M) u(k|M)]^2.

On large scales u(k|M) approaches unity and the bias integral is normalized to one, so P^{2h} recovers linear theory exactly; on small scales P^{1h} dominates, capturing virialized nonlinearities analytically. The framework extends naturally to galaxies via the HOD [6], to velocities and pressure fields, to weak lensing, and to secondary CMB anisotropies [1].

The model is not exact — halo exclusion, assembly bias, and profile scatter require corrections — but it reproduces simulation two-point and higher-order statistics at the ten-percent level across a remarkable dynamic range, and modern extensions such as the Web-Halo Model combine perturbation theory on large scales with halo-model resummation on small scales without free parameters, reaching percent-level accuracy needed by Euclid and DESI analyses [7].

```haskell
-- Cosmological halo model ingredients in Haskell
data HaloIngredient = MassFunction   { dn_dM :: Double -> Double }
                    | HaloBias       { bOfM  :: Double -> Double }
                    | HaloProfile    { uOfkM  :: Double -> Double -> Double }

-- One-halo integrand: (dn/dM) (M/rhobar)^2 |u(k|M)|^2
oneHaloIntegrand :: HaloIngredient -> HaloIngredient -> HaloIngredient
                 -> Double -> Double -> Double -> Double
oneHaloIntegrand mf _ prof k m rhoBar =
    dn_dM mf m * (m / rhoBar)^2 * (uOfkM prof k m)^2
```

### 4.3 Baryon Acoustic Oscillations: A Relic Standard Ruler

Before recombination, photons and baryons were tightly coupled into a single relativistic plasma. Primordial overdensities launched spherical sound waves propagating at c_s ≈ c/sqrt(3); at the drag epoch (z_d ≈ 1020) photons decoupled and the waves stalled, imprinting a characteristic shell of excess baryon density at the sound horizon r_s ≈ 150 Mpc comoving [9]. Because the feature's physical scale is fixed by early-universe physics — depending only on the physical densities Omega_m h^2 and Omega_b h^2 — its observed angular and redshift extent at later epochs directly measures the comoving angular-diameter distance D_M(z) and the Hubble distance D_H(z) = c/H(z) relative to r_s [2].

In the correlation function the BAO appear as a localized bump near 100 h^-1 Mpc; in the power spectrum as a decaying series of wiggles with characteristic frequency set by r_s. Nonlinear structure formation damps the wiggles, an effect well approximated by Gaussian smoothing of the acoustic feature in both real and redshift space [8]. Density-field reconstruction sharpens the peak and recovers most of the lost precision [2].

DESI's first data release measured BAO in seven redshift bins spanning z = 0.1 to 4.2 using over six million extragalactic tracers, achieving near-percent distance precision. The DR1 results are consistent with flat Lambda-CDM (Omega_m = 0.295 ± 0.015 from BAO alone), but in combination with CMB and supernova data they prefer evolving dark energy — w_0 > -1 and w_a < 0 — discrepant with a cosmological constant at up to 3.9 sigma depending on the supernova compilation [2]. Whether this hints at dynamical dark energy or residual systematics is the defining question of contemporary survey cosmology [3].

```rust
// Trapezoidal estimate of the sound horizon r_s(z_d) in Mpc
fn sound_horizon(omega_m: f64, omega_b: f64, h: f64, z_d: f64) -> f64 {
    let c = 299792.458_f64; // km/s
    let n = 20000usize;
    let mut sum = 0.0_f64;
    for i in 0..n {
        let z = z_d * (i as f64) / (n as f64);
        let r_ratio = 3.0 * omega_b / (4.0 * 2.469e-5 / h / h) / (1.0 + z);
        let cs = c / (3.0_f64 * (1.0 + r_ratio)).sqrt();
        let e = (omega_m * (1.0 + z).powi(3) + (1.0 - omega_m)).sqrt();
        sum += cs / (h * 100.0 * e);
    }
    sum * z_d / (n as f64)
}
```

### 4.4 Redshift-Space Distortions: Velocities Written on the Sky

Galaxy redshifts conflate Hubble flow with peculiar velocities. Coherent infall toward overdensities compresses apparent structure along the line of sight — the Kaiser squashing — amplifying large-scale clustering anisotropically. Within virialized clusters, random orbital motions stretch structure along the line of sight into *fingers of God*, producing the characteristic elongated contours in the xi(r_perp, r_parallel) plane [4][5].

The streaming model and its perturbative refinements write the redshift-space correlation as a convolution of the real-space correlation with the pairwise velocity distribution. In Fourier space the standard phenomenological model is:

P_s(k, mu) = (1 + beta mu^2)^2 P_r(k) D_FoG(k, mu),

where D_FoG is a Lorentzian or Gaussian damping term encoding small-scale velocity dispersion, with dispersion scale often parameterized by sigma_v [4].

Crucially, the BAO feature survives redshift-space distortions: the acoustic scale is robust against velocity smearing, so BAO and RSD analyses of the same survey volume yield complementary, nearly independent cosmological information — geometry and growth — from the same data [2][5].

    /\ k \in 0.01 .. KMax

## 5 Empirical Evidence and Proofs

The theoretical edifice above is validated by a convergence of independent measurements:

1. **N-body simulations** reproduce the halo model decomposition quantitatively: one-halo dominance on small scales and biased linear clustering on large scales emerge from first-principles gravitational dynamics, and emulators trained on simulation grids now predict the nonlinear power spectrum to percent accuracy across parameter space [1][7].
2. **The BAO feature is detected** in galaxy correlation functions and power spectra across multiple surveys — SDSS LRGs (the original detection), BOSS, eBOSS, and now DESI — at precisely the sound-horizon scale predicted from CMB-calibrated early-universe physics, a non-trivial cross-epoch consistency test [2][8][9].
3. **DESI DR1** delivers BAO distances in seven redshift bins with aggregate sub-percent precision: transverse comoving distance D_M/r_d and radial Hubble distance D_H/r_d measured from over six million tracers, with a blinded analysis and a systematics budget validated against Abacus and EZmock suites [2].
4. **RSD growth measurements** from BOSS, eBOSS, and DESI constrain f sigma_8(z) at multiple epochs, consistent with Lambda-CDM growth to within current errors, while the Kaiser anisotropy is visually unmistakable in two-dimensional correlation function contours [4][5].
5. **The HOD framework** fit to galaxy clustering reproduces luminosity- and color-dependent clustering, confirming that the halo model plus occupation statistics correctly describe the galaxy-matter connection [6].
6. **The emerging tension**: DESI DR1 BAO combined with Planck CMB and supernova data favors dynamical dark energy (w_0 w_a CDM) over Lambda-CDM at 2.5 to 3.9 sigma. Physics-focused parameterizations — thawing, emergent, and mirage classes — fit the data at least as well as Lambda-CDM, suggesting the signal deserves serious investigation rather than dismissal [2][3].

## 6 Limitations

Intellectual honesty requires stating what these methods cannot yet do:

- **The halo model's approximations.** Real halos are triaxial, substructured, and environmentally dependent; assembly bias — the dependence of clustering on formation history at fixed mass — violates the model's clean factorization, and halo exclusion must be imposed by hand. Precision cosmology increasingly relies on simulation-calibrated emulators rather than pure halo-model analytics [1][7].
- **Nonlinear RSD modeling.** The Kaiser formula is linear; on quasi-nonlinear scales, perturbation-theory-based RSD models carry nuisance parameters and restricted validity ranges, and the finger-of-God damping is phenomenological. Different modeling choices shift f sigma_8 at the few-percent level — comparable to the statistical precision of next-generation surveys [4][5].
- **BAO systematics.** Reconstruction assumes a fiducial cosmology; HOD-dependent systematics, observational systematics, and covariance misestimation all enter the error budget. The LRG1/LRG2 monopole features driving part of DESI's tension with Planck illustrate how single bins can steer global conclusions [2].
- **The tension's ambiguity.** The preference for evolving dark energy is prior- and dataset-dependent: model-agnostic reconstructions find it less significant, and it softens or strengthens with the supernova compilation used. It may signal new physics, underestimated systematics, or both [3].
- **Simulation cost.** Full N-body suites for covariance estimation and emulator training remain computationally expensive, limiting the cosmological parameter volume that can be explored and motivating approximate methods whose fidelity must be continuously audited [7].

## 7 Conclusion

The cosmic web is the largest structure in nature and the most precise laboratory for fundamental physics. Gravitational instability explains its origin; the halo model explains its statistics in closed analytic form; baryon acoustic oscillations provide a calibrated ruler to measure the Universe's expansion; and redshift-space distortions encode the growth of structure and the law of gravity itself. From the two-halo term's recovery of linear theory to DESI's sub-percent distance ladder across seven redshift bins, the arc of the field is one of steadily shrinking error bars and steadily more demanding questions [1][2][7].

The central open question is now explicit: does the DESI DR1 preference for evolving dark energy survive the full survey, Euclid, and the Vera Rubin Observatory, or will systematics reclaim it? Either answer is a triumph — a discovery of new physics, or a demonstration that the cosmic web, understood through the halo model, BAO, and RSD, is even more consistent with Lambda-CDM than we dared hope. The next decade of survey cosmology will decide.
