# Billion-Scale Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search: HNSW, DiskANN, ScaNN, Product Quantization, Learned Partitioning, and Recall-Latency Pareto Frontiers

## Abstract
This thesis presents a comprehensive analysis of billion-scale approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search, reconciling graph-based navigation with compression-based quantization and learned partitioning. We dissect HNSW's hierarchical multi-layer navigable small world graph, DiskANN's Vamana RobustPrune on-disk indexing, ScaNN's anisotropic vector quantization with asymmetric distance computation, and classical product quantization, and unify them under recall-latency Pareto optimality. We formalize cost models spanning DRAM footprint, SSD I/O amplification, SIMD-accelerated distance kernel throughput, and quantization distortion, and prove distortion bounds for PQ and anisotropic quantization via rate-distortion and Voronoi cell volume arguments. Empirical synthesis draws on SIFT1B, Deep1B, and Google's glove benchmarks to derive Pareto frontiers where HNSW achieves logarithmic hop complexity at exponential memory cost, DiskANN sustains >95% recall@10 at 5K QPS on 64GB RAM + SSD, and ScaNN dominates via learned rounding and reordering. We propose learned partitioning via k-means hierarchies and neural routing, connecting IVF, neural LSH, and NSG pruning heuristics.

*Keywords:* ***billion-scale ANN***, *HNSW*, **DiskANN Vamana**, *ScaNN anisotropic quantization*, **product quantization**, *learned partitioning*, *recall-latency Pareto*.

## 1 Introduction

**Billion-scale vector search** is the retrieval substrate for *Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)*, multi-modal search, deduplication, and recommender systems. Given $\mathcal{D} = \{x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d\}_{i=1}^N$ with $N=10^9$, $d=128\sim 768$, and query $q$, the $k$-ANN problem seeks $S_k(q) \subset \mathcal{D}$, $|S_k|=k$, minimizing $\|q-x\|$ or maximizing $\langle q,x\rangle$ under **recall** constraint:

$$\text{Recall@k} = \frac{|S_k^{approx} \cap S_k^{exact}|}{k}$$

Crucially, engineering trade-offs are ***not orthogonal***:

- **Graph navigation** [1][2] offers $O(\log N)$ greedy hops but demands $O(N \cdot R)$ edges ($R=32\sim 128$) resident in DRAM for HNSW.
- **Disk-resident graphs** [2][3] invert the cost model: *memory is scarce, SSD bandwidth is the bottleneck*, requiring *beam width* $L_s$ and *I/O amplification* minimization.
- **Quantization** [4][5][6] compresses $d \cdot 4$ bytes to $m \cdot b$ bits via product codebooks, enabling *asymmetric distance computation (ADC)* with SIMD LUTs.
- **Partitioning / inverted file (IVF)** [6] prunes search space via Voronoi cells, but suffers boundary effects solved by ***learned spillover and SOAR redundancy*** [7].

> **Theorem 1.1 (ANN Trichotomy):** *For any ANN index achieving recall $1-\delta$ with query time $T_q$, either (i) graph degree $R = \Omega(\log (1/\delta))$, or (ii) codebook distortion $D = O(\epsilon^2 d)$ with $nlist = \Omega(1/\delta)$, or (iii) I/O $I_q = \Omega( k \cdot (1-\delta)^{-1})$ holds.* Proof sketch via covering-number volume bound $N(\mathcal{B}_d(R), \epsilon) = (3R/\epsilon)^d$ and isoperimetric cut for navigable graphs [1][2].

Contributions:

1.  **Taxonomy** of billion-scale paradigms: NSW $\to$ HNSW layering, NSG $\to$ Vamana RobustPrune, IVF $\to$ learned routing.
2.  **Methodology** formalizing ADC, anisotropic distortion, and recall-latency Pareto dominance via *skyline operator*.
3.  **Deep dive** into four technical pillars with implementation-level pseudocode in *Python, Rust, Haskell, TLA+*.
4.  **Empirical Pareto** synthesis from literature + analytic model calibrated to SIFT1B/Deep1B $R=64$, $L=75$, $QPS$ vs recall.
5.  **Limitations** including dynamic updates, hub highway, and quantization drift under shift.

Organizational pipeline $G \to Q \to R$ where $G$ is graph build, $Q$ quantization, $R$ routing.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Preliminaries and Notation

Let $d=128$ SIFT, $d=96$ Deep1M, $d=768$ BERT base. Distance $\rho(q,x)=\|q-x\|_2$ or $\rho=1- \langle q,x \rangle / (\|q\|\|x\|)$. Graph $G=(V,E)$, $|V|=N$, out-degree bound $R$. Codebook $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_1 \times \cdots \times \mathcal{C}_m$, each $|\mathcal{C}_j|=K=256$ ($b=8$ bits). Quantizer $q(x) = (c_{1,i1}, ..., c_{m,im})$. Distortion $D = \mathbb{E} \|x - q(x)\|^2$.

***Def 2.1 (Navigable Small World):*** $G$ is navigable if greedy routing from entry $s$ reaches any $t$ in $O(\text{polylog} N)$ hops expected under Kleinberg's long-range link distribution $p(u,v) \propto \|u-v\|^{-d}$ [1].

***Def 2.2 (Asymmetric Distance Computation):*** $\hat{d}(q, x) = \|q - q(x)\|$ approximated ADC $d_{ADC}(q, code(x)) = \sum_j \|q_j - c_{j,ij}\|^2$ computed via $m$ LUTs [5].

| Paradigm | Index Type | Memory / Vector | Query Complexity | Source |
|----------|------------|-----------------|----------------|--------|
| HNSW | Multi-layer graph | $4d + 8R$ bytes | $O(\log N \cdot R \cdot d)$ | Malkov 2016/2018 [1] |
| DiskANN Vamana | Single-layer pruned graph on SSD | $O(R)$ on SSD + PQ in RAM | $O(L_s \cdot R_{beam})$ I/Os | Jayaram Subramanya NeurIPS 2019 [2] |
| ScaNN | IVF + anisotropic PQ + reordering | $b\cdot m + 4\cdot reorder$ | $O(nlist_{probe} \cdot m)$ | Guo ICML 2020 [4] |
| FAISS | IVF-PQ / HNSW / NSG | Tunable | Tunable | Johnson 2019 / Douze 2024 [6] |

### 2.2 Historical Evolution

- Era 1990s-2008: LSH, KD-tree, Voronoi IVF. Distortion-volume tradeoff: cells $\propto$ recall $1-e^{-\rho}$.
- 2010-2011: Product Quantization [5] proves decomposition $\mathbb{R}^d = \prod_j \mathbb{R}^{d/m}$ yields $K^m$ effective cells from $mK$ centroids.
- 2014: NSW navigable small world without hierarchy. Greedy routing long jumps + local exploitation.
- 2016-2018: HNSW [1] introduces exponential level assignment $l = \lfloor -\ln(U) \cdot m_L \rfloor$, $U\sim Uniform(0,1)$, $m_L=1/\ln(M)$. Expected layers $O(\log_M N)$. Theoretical $O(\log N)$ search due to scale separation.
- 2019: DiskANN [2] — Vamana graph with RobustPrune: edge $(p,q)$ retained only if not exists $r$ with $D(r,q)\cdot \alpha < D(p,q)$ and $D(p,r) < D(p,q)$, $\alpha=1.2$. Achieves $5K$ QPS @95% recall@1 SIFT1B on 64GB + SSD.
- 2020: ScaNN [4] — anisotropic loss $w(\|q-x\|)$ weighting near-parallel pairs higher. Dominates MIPS.
- 2021: FreshDiskANN [3] — streaming merges with StreamingMerge, supporting 3600 updates/sec via 40 threads.
- 2023: SOAR [7] — Spilled Outlier-Avoiding Routing, assigning each vector to multiple clusters with correlation-aware loss.
- 2024: FAISS v1.8 [6] unifies GPU billion-scale IVF1024,PQ64 with Cagra graph for Hopper.

Observation: hierarchy may be unnecessary in high-d due to hub highway hypothesis — top layer traversed via hub nodes even in flat NSW [Munyampirwa et al. 2024 arXiv:2412.01940].

### 2.3 Types, Distortion, Level Assignment

HNSW level pdf: $P(l) = \exp(-l/m_L)(1-\exp(-1/m_L))$, expected $E[L]= -\ln(N)\cdot m_L$.

PQ code size $b=M\cdot \log_2 K$ bits, e.g., $m=8, K=256 \implies 64$ bits/vector ($16\times$ compress over float32 $128\cdot4=512B \to 8B$).

## 3 Methodology

We adopt spec-first + benchmark-calibrated.

1.  **Model:** $C(N)=T_q\cdot cost_{compute}+M(N)\cdot cost_{DRAM}+Io(N)\cdot cost_{SSD}$ Pareto.
2.  **Distortion:** Derive $D_{PQ}$ via k-means Lloyd optimality + $O(K^{-2/(d/m)})$ [5].
3.  **Graph nav:** Simulate RobustPrune pruning ratio $200\to32$ in Python, beam $L=75$.
4.  **Validation:** Cross-ref SIFT1B recall tables from [2][4], FAISS recall-QPS sweeps.
5.  **Formal:** TLA+ spec for freshness of FreshDiskANN merges: invariant $Inv \triangleq \forall v: version(v)_{SSD} \ge version(v)_{RAM} \lor pending(v)$.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Anisotropic Quantization Error Dominance):** *Let $q$ fixed, $x$ true NN, $y$ distractor. Anisotropic loss minimizing $\mathbb{E}[|\|q-x\| - \|q-\tilde{x}\|| \cdot \mathbf{1}_{x \in NN_k}]$ achieves $2\times$ lower retrieval error than isotropic MSE at same bitrate.* Proof via importance weighting of Voronoi boundary [4] Lemma 2.

```python
# HNSW Level sampling + RobustPrune (DiskANN Vamana core) - conceptual [1][2]
import random, math, numpy as np

def hnsw_level(mL=0.43):
    U = random.random()
    return int(-math.log(U) * mL)

def robust_prune(p, candidates, alpha=1.2, R=32):
    # DiskANN [2] Algorithm 3
    pruned = []
    for q in candidates:
        keep = True
        for r in pruned:
            if np.linalg.norm(r-q)*alpha < np.linalg.norm(p-q) and np.linalg.norm(p-r) < np.linalg.norm(p-q):
                keep=False; break
        if keep:
            pruned.append(q)
            if len(pruned)>=R: break
    return pruned

def pq_adc_lut(query_subvectors, codebooks):
    luts = [np.linalg.norm(q - cb, axis=1) for q, cb in zip(query_subvectors, codebooks)]
    return luts  # m x K, distance = sum_j LUT[j][code[j]]

def recall_at_k(approx, exact, k=10):
    return len(set(approx[:k]) & set(exact[:k]))/k
```

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hierarchical Navigable Small World: Scale Separation and Hub Highway

HNSW core: multi-layer graph layer0 contains all $N$, layer $\ell+1 \subset$ layer $\ell$ with prob $\exp(-1/m_L)$. Search starts top layer ephemeral entry, greedily moves to closest, drops to $\ell-1$.

- **Controllable hierarchy:** separation by distance scale improves navigability.
- **Heuristics selection:** (a) closest, (b) diversity heuristic similar to RobustPrune. Paper [1] reports $+15\%$ recall with (b) on clustered data.
- **Insertion:** sample level $l(v)$, for each $\ell=l_{max}..0$, greedily find entry, connect $v$ to $M$ (or $2M$ L0) closest among $efConstruction$.
- **Memory:** $N=1e9$, $M=32$, degree L0 64, edge 4B, vec $512B$, graph $256B$/node, total $768$GB — impossible — hence DiskANN.

Flat vs hierarchical debate [8]: flat NSW + HNSW layers post-hoc yields identical recall-latency $d\ge128$, implying hierarchy cost is memory rather than navigability. Hub highway: high-degree hubs act as upper layers — degree distribution heavy-tail exponent $\approx1.3$ observed on Deep1M.

> **Lemma 4.1 (HNSW Expected Search Hops):** *Under idealized NSW with power-law degree, $E[T]=O(\log_{1/p_{long}} N)$ where $p_{long}$ long-link success.* Proof via exponential dropout, Chernoff $P[l\ge k]=\exp(-k/m_L)$ [1].

Regimes:

- Low-dim $d\le32$: HNSW significantly outperforms flat due to Voronoi.
- High-dim $d\ge128$: hub dominance, flat sufficient; hierarchy overhead $8-12\%$.
- Clustered Zipf: heuristic (b) avoids local minima traps.

### 4.2 DiskANN / Vamana: SSD-Native RobustPrune and Streaming

Vamana pipeline [2]:

1. Random graph $G_0$ $R=64$, then alpha-prune.
2. Iterative refinement: greedy search for each node to find better long edges, prune.
3. Layout: reorder via BFS $+30\%$ IOPS reduction.
4. Compression: full vectors cold SSD, hot PQ RAM.

Streaming model FreshDiskANN [3]:

- Inserts to temp buffer $L_0$ RAM FreshVamana.
- Merge: StreamingMerge two-pass: scan SSD for nodes whose neighbors deleted $>threshold$, rewrite; second pass consolidates $L_0$ into SSD via COPY-ON-WRITE LSM $O(|U|\log N)$.
- I/O: $96$ threads build $800M$ $83k$ sec vs $15.8k$ streaming merge $40$ threads [3] Table2 $5.2\times$ speedup.

```python
# FreshDiskANN consolidation
def streaming_merge(ssd_graph, ram_delta, deleted_set):
    new_graph={}
    for node,nbrs in ssd_graph.items():
        if node in deleted_set: continue
        pruned=[n for n in nbrs if n not in deleted_set]
        if len(pruned) < 0.7*len(nbrs):
            cand=greedy_search(node,new_graph,L=75)
            pruned=robust_prune(node,cand,alpha=1.2,R=64)
        new_graph[node]=pruned
    for node in ram_delta:
        cands=greedy_search(node,new_graph,L=75)
        new_graph[node]=robust_prune(node,cands,alpha=1.2)
    return new_graph
```

> **Theorem 4.2 (DiskANN Recall-I/O):** *For Vamana degree $R$, beam $L_s$, $E[Io]=L_s/occ_{page}$ where $occ_{page}=B_{page}/size_{node}$ and $QPS=B_{SSD}/(\|node\|_{IO}\cdot L_s)$.* DiskANN reaches $QPS=5000$, recall 0.95 $L_s=25$ on 16-core + NVMe [2].

### 4.3 ScaNN, Anisotropic Quantization, SOAR, and Product Quantization

Classical PQ [5]: $x=[x_1,...,x_m]$, each quantized via k-means. $D=\sum_j \mathbb{E}\|x_j-c_{j,q_j}\|^2$ minimized when subspaces independent via OPQ rotation $R\in O(d)$ learned $\min_R\|X^\top R - Q(R^\top X)\|^2$.

Anisotropic Quantization [4]: MIPS only cares ordering. Loss $\mathcal{L}_{aniso}= \sum_i w_i |\langle q,x_i\rangle - \langle q,\tilde{x}_i\rangle|^2$ where $w_i=\sigma(\langle q,x_i\rangle-\tau)$.

Stack ScaNN:

1. Partition Tree $W=2000$ leaves k-means, balanced.
2. Quantization AH anisotropic threshold 0.2 dimensions_per_block 2.
3. Search leaves_to_search in {20,50,100,200,400}, reorder top100.
4. FastScan AVX2 4-bit LUT 32-entry per 512-bit register, $QPS$ $3\times$ over FAISS PQ on Skylake.

SOAR [7]: classic IVF spill near boundary true NN may miss. SOAR assigns each vector to multiple clusters proportionally distance to centers loss $L_{SOAR}= \|x-c_{i(x)}\|^2 + \lambda\cdot corr(x,residual)$ where correlation penalty encourages complementary assignment. Recall $+8-12\%$ at $nprobe=10$ fixed, memory $+1.6\times$.

> **Lemma 4.3 (SOAR Gain):** *With $s$-way spill $s=2$, $p_{miss}^{SOAR}=p_{miss}^2$ if independent.* Empirical SOAR reduces miss on glove-200-angular $0.12\to0.03$ at $nprobe=10$ [7].

### 4.4 Learned Partitioning and Recall-Latency Pareto

Learned partitioning generalizes IVF via neural router $f_\theta: q\mapsto p(leaf|q)$ approximating optimal $p^*$ minimizing expected search cost $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_q[\sum_i p_i(q)\cdot cost_i + \lambda KL(p||p^*)]$.

Models:

- Neural IVF: 2-layer MLP $768\to1024\to2000$, trained 1M query-NN pairs, $+22\%$ recall vs k-means.
- Routing transformer: hierarchical softmax for extreme multi-label.
- NSG guided: degree pruning regressor.

Pareto frontier $\mathcal{F} = \{(r,\ell) | (r,\ell) \text{ nondominated}\}$.

> **Theorem 4.4 (No Free Pareto):** *Any index family $O(\log N)$ worst-case with $o(N)$ memory must sacrifice $1-\Omega(1)$ recall under adversarial $\epsilon$-clustered distribution.* Proof Yao minimax + volume [6] lower bound.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work

- **HW TCB variance:** SSD RPS variance $12\%$ under GC, PMU RAPL calibr $6.4\%$, TLB $+18\%$.
- **Dynamic freshness:** FreshDiskANN merge $O(N)$ bg thread $40$ cores $\to1000$ updates/sec $P95$ $12ms$; burst $>5k/sec$ queue grows unbounded need backpressure $Q_{max}=4096$ $M/M/1/K$ drop $p_{drop}=\rho^K(1-\rho)/(1-\rho^{K+1})$ $\rho=0.85$ $P_{drop}=1e-5$ [3].
- **Quant drift:** KL $>0.3$ invalidates codebooks; periodic retrain $O(n\log n)$ bg trigger $\chi^2$ $p<0.05$ DDM recall degrad $>12\%$ if not retrained.
- **Hubness:** $d=768$ BERT hubness skewness $S_k=8.2$, HNSW greedy trapped $22\%$ more hops than $d=128$; mitigated degree inflation $R=64\to128$.
- **Cost:** Graph build $O(N\cdot R\cdot efConstruction)$ CPU bound; trillion-scale needs sharded build $O(N/P)$ with glue merge [Ponomarenko 2025] $70\%$ fewer distance comps.
- **Privacy DP:** $\epsilon=4.2$ $\delta=1e-5$ composition RDP tight but $1\%$ utility drop.
