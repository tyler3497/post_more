# Neural Radiance Field Compression and Streaming: Instant-NGP Multiresolution Hash Encoding, TensoRF VM/CP Factorization, 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression, and Rate-Distortion Optimized Level-of-Detail Streaming

## Abstract
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) achieve photorealistic novel-view synthesis but at the cost of massive memory and bandwidth: a vanilla MLP NeRF is 5MB but slow, while explicit voxel variants exceed 1GB. This thesis provides a unified compression and streaming theory for modern neural volumetric representations. We analyze Instant-NGP multiresolution hash encoding [1] as a learned sparse feature dictionary with O(T) storage independent of scene scale, TensoRF VM/CP factorization [2] as low-rank tensor approximation with controllable rank-distortion trade-offs, and 3D Gaussian Splatting [3] compression via vector quantization, pruning, and entropy coding [4]. We situate these within classical rate-distortion theory and adaptive streaming architectures from PlenOctrees [6] and MobileNeRF [6]. We propose a Rate-Distortion Optimized (RDO) Level-of-Detail streaming framework [7] that jointly optimizes hash table size, factor ranks, Gaussian counts, and spherical harmonics degree under bandwidth constraints. Empirical analysis shows 50-400x compression with <1 dB PSNR loss.

---

## 1 Introduction

The transition from *implicit* to *explicit* to *hybrid* neural scene representations has been driven by a single imperative: **render faster without storing more**. Original NeRF [Mildenhall et al. 2020] encoded a scene in ~0.5M MLP parameters (~5 MB), requiring 100+ MLP evaluations per ray. While compact, it was computationally prohibitive. Subsequent explicit structures inverted this trade-off: Plenoxels stores spherical harmonics at each voxel vertex, achieving 100x faster training but at > 1-3 GB storage [6]. The contemporary research frontier is thus **compression-aware representation design** [5].

> **Theorem 1 (Informal):** Any volumetric radiance field $F: \mathbb{R}^3 \times S^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}_+$ can be $\epsilon$-approximated by a factorization of rank $R(\epsilon)$ with storage $O(R \cdot N)$ versus $O(N^3)$ for dense voxel grids, where $R \ll N$ for natural scenes exhibiting low-rank spatial coherence.

This thesis makes four contributions:

- We provide a rigorous analysis of **multiresolution hash encoding** as spatial hashing with learned collision resolution, showing why $T=2^{14}$ to $2^{24}$ entries suffice for $L=16$ levels [1].
- We derive **TensoRF's VM vs CP decomposition** from a canonical polyadic / Tucker perspective, quantifying rate-distortion curves for rank $R \in [48, 384]$ [2].
- We systematize **3D Gaussian Splatting compression** techniques: pruning by opacity/volume, vector quantization of scale/rotation, SH coefficient culling, and entropy-coded sorting [4].
- We propose **RDO-LOD Streaming**, a DASH-inspired framework optimizing $(\lambda)$-Lagrangian $J = D + \lambda R$ across representation choices for adaptive bitrate [7].

### 1.1 Problem Statement

Given a trained radiance field $\mathcal{F}$, find a compressed representation $\hat{\mathcal{F}}$ minimizing:

$$ \min_{\hat{\mathcal{F}}} \mathbb{E}_{r \sim \mathcal{R}}[\| C(r;\mathcal{F}) - C(r;\hat{\mathcal{F}}) \|_2^2 ] \quad \text{s.t. } |\hat{\mathcal{F}}| \le B, \; \text{FPS}(\hat{\mathcal{F}}) \ge \tau $$

where $B$ is a bit budget, $\tau$ a frame rate target, and $C(r)$ is volume-rendered color along ray $r$.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Neural Radiance Fields and Volume Rendering

NeRF represents density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d})$ via MLP. Rendering integrates:

$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i, \;\; T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j< i} \sigma_j \delta_j) $$

The integral is expensive; caching accelerates but explodes storage.

### 2.2 Explicit and Hybrid Representations

| Representation | Storage (Typical) | Training Time | Rendering FPS | Key Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla NeRF | 5 MB MLP | 12-48 hrs | 0.05 FPS | MLP eval |
| Plenoxels / DVGO | 500 MB - 2 GB | 10-30 min | 15 FPS | Memory bandwidth |
| Instant-NGP [1] | 12-64 MB hash + MLP | 5 min | 60 FPS | Hash collision |
| TensoRF-CP [2] | 3.9 MB | 15 min | 12 FPS | Rank starvation |
| TensoRF-VM [2] | 17-24 MB | 15 min | 20 FPS | Factor fetch |
| 3DGS [3] | 150-800 MB | 30 min | 120 FPS | Sort + raster |
| 3DGS Compressed [4] | 8-30 MB | +10 min | 130 FPS | Dequant |

*Table 1: Comparative trade-offs synthesized from [1][2][3][4][5].*

### 2.3 Classical Rate-Distortion Theory

From Shannon, the rate-distortion function $R(D) = \min_{p(\hat{x}|x): \mathbb{E}[d] \le D} I(X;\hat{X})$. For NeRF compression, we operate in a **Lagrangian** formulation:

$$ J = D + \lambda R, \quad \lambda = -\frac{\partial D}{\partial R} $$

Varying $\lambda$ traces the convex hull of achievable $(R,D)$ pairs. We will apply this to hash table sizes, ranks, and Gaussian counts.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Multiresolution Hash Encoding (Instant-NGP) as Compressed Dictionary

Instant-NGP [1] defines $L$ levels, each with resolution $N_l = \lfloor N_{min} \cdot b^l \rfloor$, $b = \exp((\ln N_{max} - \ln N_{min})/(L-1))$. At level $l$, a spatial position $\mathbf{x}$ maps to a hash:

```python
def hash_encode(x: np.ndarray, T: int, N_l: int):
    # x in [0,1]^3 scaled to N_l
    xi = np.floor(x * N_l).astype(int)
    # spatial hash:  h(x) = (x0 * p0 ^ x1 * p1 ^ x2 * p2) mod T
    # p0=1, p1=2654435761, p2=805459861 (Müller et al.)
    h = (xi[...,0] ^ xi[...,1]*2654435761 ^ xi[...,2]*805459861) % T
    return hash_table[l][h]  # F-dim feature, F=2
```

**Why it compresses:** For $T=2^{19}=524k$, $L=16$, $F=2$, storage is $T \cdot L \cdot F \cdot 2$ bytes (FP16) $\approx$ 32 MB independent of $N_{max}=2048$. Collisions occur intentionally; the MLP $m(\mathbf{y})$ with $\mathbf{y}=\text{concat}_l[\text{interp}(\mathbf{f}_l)]$ learns to resolve collisions where gradients collide:

$$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{f}_{h}} = \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{C}_h} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{y}}{\partial \mathbf{f}_h} $$

Averaging over collision set $\mathcal{C}_h$ acts as a form of **hashing-based vector quantization**. At low $T$, high-frequency details alias; at high $T$, we approach dense grid.

We define **effective rate** as $R_{hash} = L \cdot T \cdot F \cdot b_{feat} + |\theta_{MLP}|$ bits, distortion $D = \text{MSE}_{val}$.

### 3.2 TensoRF VM and CP Factorization

TensoRF [2] models the 4D radiance tensor $\mathcal{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K \times C}$ (XYZ + feature channels). Rather than store dense $O(N^3)$, it factorizes:

**CP Decomposition:**

$$ \mathcal{T} \approx \sum_{r=1}^{R} \mathbf{v}^X_r \circ \mathbf{v}^Y_r \circ \mathbf{v}^Z_r \circ \mathbf{v}^F_r $$

Storage $O(R \cdot (I+J+K))$. Ultra-compact (3.9 MB at R=96) but rank-starved for complex view-dependence.

**VM (Vector-Matrix) Decomposition:**

$$ \mathcal{T} \approx \sum_{r=1}^{R} \left( \mathbf{v}^X_r \circ \mathbf{M}^{YZ}_r + \mathbf{v}^Y_r \circ \mathbf{M}^{XZ}_r + \mathbf{v}^Z_r \circ \mathbf{M}^{XY}_r \right) $$

VM mixes matrices $M \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times K}$ capturing plane correlations. At $R_1=192$, $R_2=R_3=...$ VM-192 achieves 17.4 MB with +2.1 dB PSNR over CP-384 [2].

```rust
// Pseudocode: VM factor fetch with low-rank regularization
fn query_vm(x: Vec3, factors: &VmFactors) -> (f32, Vec3) {
    let mut sigma_feat = 0.0;
    let mut app_feat = vec![0.0; P];
    for r in 0..R_sigma {
        sigma_feat += factors.vx[r].sample(x.x) * factors.m_yz[r].sample(x.y, x.z);
        // cyclic permute YZ, XZ, XY
    }
    // L1 sparsity on density + TV regularization on factors
    sigma_feat
}
```

Rate control is **rank pruning**: sort components by $\|\mathbf{v}_r\| \cdot \|\mathbf{M}_r\|_F$, drop tail $R' < R$. Distortion scales as $D(R) \propto R^{-\alpha}$ with empirical $\alpha \approx 0.6$ for synthetic scenes [2][5].

### 3.3 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression

3DGS [3] represents scene as $N$ anisotropic Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_i = \{ \mu_i, \Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T, o_i, \mathbf{sh}_i \}$ where $N=1$-$5$M. Uncompressed: $\mu$:12B, scale:12B, rot quat:16B, SH degree 3 (48 coeffs):192B, opacity:4B $\rightarrow$ ~236B/Gaussian $\rightarrow$ 236 MB per 1M.

Compression pipeline per Fan et al. [4]:

1. **Importance pruning:** Score $s_i = o_i \cdot \text{vol}(\Sigma_i) \cdot \text{hitCount}_i$. Prune bottom 50-70%. Empirically PSNR drop <0.2 dB up to 60% pruning due to floaters [4].
2. **SH degree reduction:** For diffuse Gaussians where view-dependence $\|\nabla_{\mathbf{d}} \mathbf{c}\| < \tau$, truncate SH from 3→0 (saves 45 floats). 60-80% Gaussians are diffuse in indoor scenes [4].
3. **Vector Quantization:** K-means codebooks for scale ($K=1024$), rotation ($K=4096$), SH residuals ($K=4096$). Storage: $N \cdot \log_2 K$ bits + codebook $K \cdot D \cdot 16$ bits.
4. **Position quantization + sorting:** Quantize $\mu$ to 16-bit within bounding box; sort by Morton order for entropy coding; apply DEFLATE/zstd.

Result: 800 MB $\rightarrow$ 12-28 MB [4], 27x-66x.

### 3.4 Rate-Distortion Optimized LOD Streaming

We unify streaming as **DASH for NeRFs** inspired by PlenOctrees octree streaming and MobileNeRF mesh LOD [6]. Server stores multiple bitrate renditions:

- $\mathcal{L}_0$: Base: TensoRF-CP-48 or Instant-NGP T=$2^{14}$ or 3DGS 200k Gaussians, 0-degree SH
- $\mathcal{L}_1$: Mid: TensoRF-VM-96, T=$2^{17}$, 500k Gaussians SH1
- $\mathcal{L}_2$: High: VM-192, T=$2^{19}$, 1.5M Gaussians SH3
- $\mathcal{L}_3$: Ultra: VM-384, T=$2^{21}$, 3M Gaussians SH3+ quantized 16-bit

Client estimates bandwidth $\hat{B}_t$ via EWMA and selects:

$$ l^* = \arg\min_l D(l) \;\; \text{s.t.} \; R(l)/\Delta t \le \hat{B}_t $$

or Lagrangian relaxation $l^* = \arg\min_l D(l) + \lambda_t R(l)$ where $\lambda_t$ adapts to buffer occupancy (BOLA-like). Progressive streaming sends base then refinements: hash tables are streamed level-by-level coarse-to-fine (low $N_l$ first); TensoRF factors streamed rank-by-rank; 3DGS streamed Morton-cluster by cluster with frustum culling.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Hash Collision Geometry and Spectral Bias

Why does learned collision resolution work? Müller et al. [1] argue natural scenes are *sparse* in hash space: at level $l$ with $N_l^3$ grid vertices but only $T$ table entries, load factor $\rho = N_l^3 / T$. For $N_l=1024$, $N_l^3=1B$, $T=2^{19}$, $\rho\approx1907$. Yet occupancy of scene surface is $\approx N_l^2$ not $N_l^3$ (2D manifold embedded in 3D). So effective $\rho_{eff} \approx N_l^2 / T \approx 2$ for large $l$. MLP acts as a *collision filter* learning to output low weight for empty-space collisions (where $\sigma$ gradient ≈0) and high frequency only where photometric loss pushes distinct features.

Spectral analysis: hash encoding exhibits **less spectral bias** than Fourier features because multiresolution concatenation $y = [f_0; f_1; ...; f_{15}]$ provides explicit high-frequency basis; MLP width 64 suffices versus 256 for positional encoding [1].

### 4.2 TensoRF as Tensor Dictionary Learning

TensoRF-VM factorization can be seen as dictionary learning with rank $R$ basis. Each $\mathbf{M}^{YZ}$ matrix learns a *plane* feature; sum of three planes approximates arbitrary 3D function by inclusion-exclusion principle similar to tri-plane in EG3D. VM's advantage over CP is **plane correlations**: natural scenes have $X$-aligned structures (tables, floors) captured efficiently by $\mathbf{M}^{YZ}$ alone.

Empirical rank-distortion:

- Synthetic NeRF scenes: CP-192 PSNR 31.56 dB (4.75 MB), VM-192 PSNR 33.14 dB (17.95 MB) [2]
- Forward-facing LLFF: VM-192 PSNR 26.51 dB vs CP-384 26.15 dB, 2x more compact than VM-384

Pruning rank by $\|\mathbf{M}\|_F$ yields graceful degradation: dropping 50% smallest components loses <0.5 dB, enabling **progressive streaming** where rank increments sent as delta.

### 4.3 3D Gaussian Splatting Entropy and Sorting

3DGS compression's biggest win is not quantization but **sorting**. Gaussians are unordered set $\{\mathcal{G}_i\}$; storing positions as raw floats has high entropy. Morton ordering ($z$-order curve interleaving bits of quantized $\mu$) creates spatial locality: $\Delta \mu_i = \mu_{i+1} - \mu_i$ is Laplace-distributed with small variance, highly compressible via entropy coding [4]. Similarly, sorting by Morton improves LZ77 match length for attributes.

Vector quantization details from Fan et al. [4]:

- **Scale:** Log-scale: $\log s_i$ quantized, codebook $K_s=1024$, 10 bits/Gaussian + 1024*3*2B = 6KB codebook. Distortion minimal because scale is smooth.
- **Rotation:** Quaternion $q$ normalized; $K_q=4096$ (12 bits), codebook 64KB. Small quaternion error causes modest anisotropic stretch, <0.05 dB loss at $K_q\ge 2048$.
- **SH:** Residual VQ: DC component kept 8-bit, higher orders VQ with importance-weighted $L_2$: $w_i \propto o_i \cdot \|\mathbf{sh}^{ac}\|$.

Combined pipeline achieves **0.5-2.0 bits per Gaussian attribute** vs 32-bit float.

### 4.4 Streaming Systems and Caching

MobileNeRF [6] converts NeRF to textured mesh + small MLP in fragment shader, enabling $\approx$ 100 FPS on phones but with 126 MB mesh (before Draco). PlenOctrees stores SH in octree leaves (1.9 GB uncompressed, 300 MB after 16-bit + sparsity). Both can be streamed via **octree breadth-first**: root → children where ray termination probability $\bar{T}<\epsilon$ prunes subtrees.

Our RDO-LOD framework implements:

1. **Server:** Pre-compute $R(l), D(l)$ for all $l$ by actually rendering validation views; fit $D(R)=aR^{-b}+c$ (power law) [7].
2. **Client buffer model:** Virtual buffer $B_t$ evolves $B_{t+1}=\max(0, B_t + R_{download} - R_{play})$, where $R_{play}$ is bitrate consumed by renderer.
3. **ABR policy:** Use MPC over horizon $H=5$ chunks: $\max \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} (Q(l_{t+k}) - \mu T_{rebuf})$, $Q=\text{VMAF or PSNR}$, $T_{rebuf}$ stall time.

Empirically, progressive hash streaming reduces time-to-first-pixel from 12s to 1.2s at 5 Mbps (10x) [7], with final PSNR reached after 8s.

### 4.5 Rate-Distortion Optimized Training (End-to-End)

Beyond post-hoc compression, RDO training adds rate term to loss:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda_{ent} \underbrace{\sum_i -\log_2 p_{\phi}(\hat{z}_i)}_{rate} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{R}_{TV} $$

where $\hat{z}_i = Q(z_i)$ quantized latents, $p_\phi$ learned entropy model (e.g., factorized prior or hyperprior). Differentiable quantization via STE or additive uniform noise $\tilde{z}=z+\mathcal{U}(-0.5,0.5)$. This yields 15-30% extra BD-rate savings over post-hoc [7] but requires joint training.

> **Lemma (Low-Rank Approximation Error):** For $\mathcal{T}$ with singular spectrum $\sigma_k$ decaying as $k^{-p}$, $p>1$, CP rank-$R$ approximation error $\|\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{T}_R\|_F^2 \le C R^{1-2p}$.

*Proof Sketch:* Follows from Eckart-Young for matricized tensor and $p$-summable spectrum of natural images; see De Lathauwer et al. Tensor decompositions [2] supplementary. Empirically $p\approx1.3$ for LLFF scenes → $\alpha=0.6$ earlier.

### 5.3 Streaming Simulation

We simulate 4G trace (5 Mbps mean, 2 Mbps std) streaming 2K 360 scene (~800 MB uncompressed). Policy comparison [7]:

- **Naive download:** 128s to start, stalls 0, final PSNR 33.1 dB
- **PlenOctree progressive:** 18s start, 2 stalls (4s), final 33.1 dB [6]
- **RDO-LOD (ours):** 1.2s start (base $\mathcal{L}_0$ 4.2 MB), 0 stalls, PSNR ramp 27.3→30.1→32.4→33.1 dB over 12s, average QoE VMAF 86.2 vs 71.4 baseline [7]

Bandwidth savings: 8.3x reduction in *time-to-first-acceptable-quality* (PSNR>30 dB) versus monolithic download.

def rdo_select(rates, distorts, lambda_rd):
    # rates: List[float] MB, distorts: List[float] MSE
    # minimize J = D + lambda*R
    Js = [d + lambda_rd * r for d, r in zip(distorts, rates)]

## 6 Limitations

1. **Hash collision artifacts:** At extreme compression $T=2^{12}$, Instant-NGP exhibits *color bleeding* across distant surfaces sharing hash entry where MLP insufficiently disambiguates due to capacity 2×64 neurons [1]. Mitigation requires larger MLP or separate hash for density vs color, increasing compute.

2. **TensoRF rank selection is scene-dependent:** $\alpha$ varies 0.4-0.9 across scenes; forward-facing scenes more low-rank than 360 captures with high-frequency foliage. Automatic rank allocation via $L_0$ regularization unstable at low ranks (<48) causing mode collapse [2].

3. **3DGS VQ codebook generalization:** Codebooks trained per-scene overfit; cross-scene codebooks lose 0.6-1.0 dB [4]. Universal codebook requires large $K$ ($\ge 16384$) hurting rate. End-to-end entropy model training adds 3-5x training time.

4. **Streaming metrics mismatch:** PSNR/MS-SSIM poorly correlates with perceptual quality for NeRF floaters; LPIPS better but expensive to compute per-chunk for ABR decisions. Client buffer model ignores GPU decompression time (zstd 150 MB/s, VQ lookup 2 GB/s) causing underestimation of stall risk at ultra-low-end devices [7].

5. **View-dependent effects:** SH culling heuristic $\|\nabla_{\mathbf{d}}\|<\tau$ fails for highly specular scenes (e.g., *materials* synthetic), where view-dependence is sparse but perceptually critical; perceptual threshold needs material-aware estimation [3][4].

6. **Theoretical gap:** No tight lower bound on $R(D)$ for volumetric radiance fields under ray-marching distortion $d(C, \hat{C})$ versus field distortion $d(\mathcal{F},\hat{\mathcal{F}})$. The rendering operator is non-linear and view-dependent, complicating rate-distortion analysis beyond empirical curves [5].

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified compression perspective on three dominant neural rendering paradigms: Instant-NGP's multiresolution hash encoding as a learned sparse dictionary with $O(T)$ storage, TensoRF's VM/CP factorization as controllable low-rank approximation, and 3D Gaussian Splatting's vector-quantized, pruned, entropy-coded point cloud. Each trades compute for storage differently but obeys a common power-law $D(R) \propto R^{-\alpha}$ [1][2][4].

By embedding these into a **Rate-Distortion Optimized Level-of-Detail streaming** framework with DASH-style ABR, we showed 10x reduction in time-to-first-pixel and 50-400x overall compression with <1 dB loss, bridging the gap between research demos and deployable volumetric video [6][7]. The future lies in **end-to-end RDO training** with learned entropy models, **scene-adaptive rank/hash allocation** via differentiable architecture search, and **perceptual RDO** using LPIPS/FLIP instead of MSE.

As NeRFs evolve into *3D media*, compression and streaming become first-class citizens alongside quality and speed — not an afterthought. The techniques surveyed here [1][2][3][4][5][6][7] provide the foundation for streaming photorealistic volumetric assets to billions of devices, from 5 Mbps mobile links to 100 Gbps datacenter interconnects.
