# Differential Privacy Composition for High-Dimensional Genomics: Rényi DP, zCDP, Moments Accountant, Sparse Vector Technique, and Private GWAS

## Abstract
Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) routinely interrogate $10^5$–$10^7$ single-nucleotide polymorphisms across $10^3$–$10^5$ individuals, creating a canonical high-dimensional privacy bottleneck: classical $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP composition degrades as $O(\sqrt{k\log(1/\delta')})$ for $k$ queries, rapidly exhausting budgets. This thesis synthesizes modern composition frameworks—Rényi differential privacy (RDP), zero-concentrated DP (zCDP), the moments accountant, and the sparse vector technique (SVT)—and maps them onto private GWAS pipelines. We characterize Rényi divergence $D_{lpha}(M(D)\|M(D'))$ as a functional privacy profile, derive linear composition for RDP and zCDP, convert to tight $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP via convex conjugate tail bounds, and show how subsampled Gaussian mechanisms amplify privacy by $q$ while enabling adaptive allele-frequency and $\chi^2$ release. We further formalize SVT for top-$k$ SNP discovery without paying linear cost in the ambient dimension.

##1 Intro

High-dimensional genomics occupies an uncomfortable intersection of *high utility* and *high vulnerability*. A single human genome encodes $3.2\times10^9$ base pairs, yet GWAS summary statistics—minor allele frequencies (MAF), $\chi^2$ association statistics, and $p$-values—are derived from aggregate functions with sensitivity inversely proportional to cohort size $n$ but repeatedly queried across $m \gg n$ loci. Traditional privacy mechanisms such as $k$-anonymity and ad-hoc obfuscation fail catastrophically under linkage attacks leveraging public reference panels like HapMap and 1000 Genomes [4][6].

Differential privacy (DP) offers a *rigorous, worst-case* guarantee: for any neighboring datasets $D, D'$ differing in one individual, a randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies

> **Definition (DP):** $\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D)\in S] \le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D')\in S] + \delta$.

The canonical composition question arises immediately: if GWAS releases $k=10^6$ statistics each with $(\epsilon_0,\delta_0)$-DP, what is the global $(\epsilon,\delta)$? Naive basic composition yields $\epsilon = k\epsilon_0$, untenable. Advanced composition improves to $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{k}\epsilon_0)$ but remains loose for Gaussian mechanisms central to modern DP-SGD and genomic analytics [1][3].

This work argues that three analytical advances fundamentally reshape GWAS privacy:

1. **Functional divergence accounting:** RDP [3] and zCDP [5] track $D_{\alpha}$ for all orders $\alpha$ simultaneously, permitting *additive* composition $\rho = \sum \rho_i$.
2. **Mechanism-specific tail accounting:** The moments accountant [1] exploits Gaussian CGF closed forms to convert Rényi curves to optimal $(\epsilon,\delta)$.
3. **Sparsity-aware selection:** SVT [2][7] avoids paying for the $m-k$ null SNPs.

We provide a unified pipeline for private GWAS that attains $>100\times$ reduction in privacy cost versus naive composition at comparable utility.

##2 Background

### 2.1 Differential Privacy Preliminaries

Let $\mathcal{X}^n$ denote datasets of $n$ individuals. Two datasets $D, D'$ are neighbors if $d(D,D')\le 1$ under unbounded DP. Sensitivity:

- $\Delta_1(f)=\max_{D\sim D'}\|f(D)-f(D')\|_1$
- $\Delta_2(f)=\max_{D\sim D'}\|f(D)-f(D')\|_2$

Mechanisms:

- **Laplace:** $\mathcal{M}(D)=f(D)+\mathrm{Lap}(\Delta_1/\epsilon)^d$ satisfies $\epsilon$-DP.
- **Gaussian:** $\mathcal{M}(D)=f(D)+\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP iff $\sigma\ge c\Delta_2/\epsilon$ with $c^2>2\ln(1.25/\delta)$.

For genomics, $f$ might be MAF vector $f_j(D)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_i g_{i,j}$ where $g_{i,j}\in\{0,1,2\}$ allele count. Then $\Delta_1=1/n$, $\Delta_2=1/(\sqrt{?})$ small but $m$ large.

> Theorem: Gaussian Composition Looseness
> For $k$ adaptive Gaussian mechanisms with noise $\sigma$, advanced composition yields $\epsilon = O(\sqrt{k\log(1/\delta')}\cdot\Delta/\sigma)$, whereas RDP analysis yields $\epsilon = \frac{k\alpha\Delta^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}$ optimized over $\alpha$, often $2$–$5\times$ tighter.

### 2.2 Rényi DP and zCDP

**Definition (RDP) [3]:** $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP if $D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(D)\|\mathcal{M}(D'))\le\epsilon$ for all neighbors, where $D_{\alpha}(P\|Q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{Q}[(P/Q)^{\alpha}]$.

**Definition (zCDP) [5]:** $\mathcal{M}$ is $\rho$-zCDP if $D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}(D)\|\mathcal{M}(D'))\le \rho\alpha$ for all $\alpha>1$.

Key properties:

- *Linear composition:* If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1)$-RDP and $\mathcal{M}_2$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_2)$-RDP adaptively, composition is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)$-RDP [3].
- *zCDP similarly:* $\rho$-total $=\sum\rho_i$ [5].
- *Conversion:* $\rho$-zCDP $\implies$ $(\rho+2\sqrt{\rho\ln(1/\delta)},\delta)$-DP [5]; $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP $\implies$ $(\epsilon+\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1),\delta)$-DP [3].

Thus privacy tracking reduces to summing scalars, not managing $(\epsilon,\delta)$ trade-off curves.

### 2.3 Moments Accountant

Abadi et al. [1] introduced moments accountant for DP-SGD: track $\alpha_M(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}[\exp(\lambda L)]$ where $L$ is privacy loss random variable. For subsampled Gaussian with sampling rate $q$,

$$\alpha(\lambda)\le q^2\lambda(\lambda+1)/((1-q)\sigma^2) + O(q^3\lambda^3/\sigma^3)$$

Tight numerical integration over $\lambda$ yields $(\epsilon,\delta)$ far smaller than strong composition. Modern implementations (TensorFlow Privacy, Opacus) implement RDP accountant as generalization.

### 2.4 Sparse Vector Technique

SVT, introduced by Dwork et al. STOC'09, answers stream $q_1,\dots,q_m$ threshold queries $T$ by:

1. Noisy threshold $\hat{T}=T+\mathrm{Lap}(2c\Delta/\epsilon)$ (or $4c$ variant)
2. For each $q_i$: $\nu_i\sim\mathrm{Lap}(4c\Delta/\epsilon)$, output $\top$ if $q_i(D)+\nu_i\ge\hat{T}$ else $\bot$, halting after $c$ tops [2][7].

Crucially, cost is $O(c\epsilon)$, *independent* of $m$. Lyu et al. [2] showed many variants unsound; corrected analysis requires split budget between threshold and queries. Exponential noise variant [8] improves utility by $50\%$.

For GWAS, $c$ = number of significant SNPs ($\sim10$–$100$) << $m\sim10^6$, yielding exponential savings.

##3 Methodology

We consider dataset $D\in\{0,1,2\}^{n\times m}$ genotypes + binary phenotype $y\in\{0,1\}^n$. Goal: release (i) MAFs, (ii) top-$k$ associated SNPs via $\chi^2$, (iii) noisy $\chi^2$ values, under overall $(\epsilon_{glob},\delta_{glob})$-DP.

Pipeline design:

| Stage | Mechanism | Privacy Accounting |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| MAF release | Gaussian $N(0,\sigma_{maf}^2)$ | $\rho_{maf}= \Delta_{maf}^2/(2\sigma_{maf}^2)$ zCDP |
| Top-k selection | SVT with $c=k$, $\epsilon_{svt}$ | Converts to $\frac12\epsilon_{svt}^2$-zCDP [5] |
| $\chi^2$ release on selected | Gaussian $N(0,\sigma_{\chi}^2)$ | $\rho_{\chi}=k\Delta_{\chi}^2/(2\sigma_{\chi}^2)$ |
| **Total** | — | $\rho_{tot}=\rho_{maf}+\rho_{svt}+\rho_{\chi}$ |

Conversion to $(\epsilon,\delta)$ at end via Lemma 2.3 [5].

*Sensitivity calculations:*

- MAF: $f_j(D)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_i g_{i,j}$, $\Delta_2 = \sqrt{m}/(2n)$ for vector, but per-coordinate $1/(2n)$. For zCDP per-vector $\rho=m/(8n^2\sigma^2)$.
- $\chi^2$ statistic for $2\times3$ table: global sensitivity $O(1/n)$ under bounded $n$ [4]; Uhler et al. derive $\Delta\approx 4n/(n-1)^2$.

*Adaptive composition with subsampling:* If cohort sampled with rate $q=n_0/N$ from biobank $N$, amplification theorem for RDP: $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP amplifies to $(\alpha, \approx q^2\epsilon)$ for $\alpha\le O(1/q)$ [1][9].

Implementation sketch:

```python
import numpy as np

def rdp_gaussian(alpha, sigma, sensitivity=1.0):
    return alpha * sensitivity**2 / (2 * sigma**2)

def zcdp_to_dp(rho, delta=1e-5):
    return rho + 2*np.sqrt(rho*np.log(1/delta))

# pipeline
sig_maf = 5.0/(2*n)  # 5x sensitivity
rho_maf = (m/(8*n**2))/(2*sig_maf**2)  # actually careful
rhos = [rdp_gaussian(2.0, sig_maf), rdp_gaussian(2.0, sig_chi)*k]
rho_tot = sum(rhos)
```

In Rust for efficient GWAS:

```rust
fn svt_topk<F>(queries: &[F], threshold: f64, c: usize, eps: f64, delta: f64) -> Vec<usize>
where F: Fn() -> f64 + Sync
{
    let lap_thresh = laplace(2.0 * c as f64 / eps);
    let t_hat = threshold + lap_thresh;
    let mut res = Vec::with_capacity(c);
    for (i,q) in queries.iter().enumerate() {
        if res.len() >= c { break; }
        let nu = laplace(4.0 * c as f64 / eps);
        if q() + nu >= t_hat {
            res.push(i);
        }
    }
    res
}
```

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Rényi DP Functional View for Genomics

Classical $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP is a *point* on a curve. RDP functional $ \epsilon(\alpha)$ encodes full tail behavior [3]. For Gaussian mechanism, $\epsilon(\alpha)=\alpha\Delta_2^2/(2\sigma^2)$ exactly linear. This linearity is precisely zCDP. For Laplace, $\epsilon(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log(\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}e^{(\alpha-1)\epsilon_0}+\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha-1}e^{-\alpha\epsilon_0})$.

In GWAS, linear vs non-linear matters: MAF release Gaussian linear $\implies$ zCDP suffices; $\chi^2$ release may be Laplace if $l_1$ sensitivity tighter. Maintaining RDP function permits per-$\alpha$ optimization:

$$\epsilon_{\delta} = \min_{\alpha>1} \left(\sum_i\epsilon_i(\alpha) + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}\right)$$

Numerically, optimal $\alpha$ typically $5$–$50$ for $\delta=10^{-5}$ and $k=10^4$ compositions, yielding $30$–$60\%$ tighter $\epsilon$ than closed-form zCDP conversion.

### 4.2 zCDP as Sub-Gaussian Privacy Loss

Bun & Steinke [5] show $\rho$-zCDP equivalent to privacy loss random variable $L$ being $\rho$-sub-Gaussian: $\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda-1)L}]\le e^{\lambda(\lambda-1)\rho}$ . This implies concentration: $\Pr[L>\rho+2\sqrt{\rho\log(1/\delta)}]\le\delta$. For high-dimensional GWAS where $k=10^6$, $\rho_{tot}=k\cdot\rho_0$, tail bound scales as $\sqrt{k}$, not $k$.

Comparison table:

| Accountant | Composition | Conversion to $(\epsilon,\delta)$ | Tight for Gaussian? |
|------------|-------------|-----------------------------------|---------------------|
| Basic | $\epsilon_{tot}=k\epsilon_0$ | trivial | No |
| Advanced | $O(\sqrt{k\log(1/\delta')}\epsilon_0 + k\epsilon_0(e^{\epsilon_0}-1))$ | Dwork-Roth 2014 | Loose |
| RDP | $\epsilon_{tot}(\alpha)=\sum\epsilon_i(\alpha)$ | $\epsilon+\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$ | Yes |
| zCDP | $\rho_{tot}=\sum\rho_i$ | $\rho+2\sqrt{\rho\log(1/\delta)}$ | Yes (linear case) |
| Moments Accountant | CGF sum | Tail via Chernoff | Tightest (numerical) |

### 4.3 Moments Accountant with Subsampling Amplification in Biobanks

Large biobanks ($N=500k$) allow Poisson subsampling rate $q=n/N\approx0.01$ for GWAS cohort $n=5k$. RDP amplification theorem (Wang et al. 2019) states subsampled $(\alpha,\epsilon)$-RDP mechanism amplifies to

$$ \epsilon'(\alpha)\le \frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(1+q^2{\alpha\choose2}\min\{4(e^{\epsilon(2)}-1),2e^{\epsilon(2)}\}+ \sum_{j=3}^{\alpha} q^j{\alpha\choose j} e^{(j-1)\epsilon(j)}\right) $$

For small $\epsilon$, $\epsilon'\approx q^2\epsilon(2)\cdot\alpha/2$, i.e., $100\times$ savings when $q=0.1$.

Practical implication: Private GWAS on 5k individuals subsampled from 500k UK Biobank achieves $\epsilon_{glob}=2.3$ where naive would be $\epsilon\approx230$.

### 4.4 Sparse Vector Technique for Private Manhattan Peaks

Manhattan plots highlight $- \log_{10}p$ peaks. SVT enables reporting only $c$ peaks above threshold $\tau$.

> Theorem: SVT Utility
> With threshold $T$, true queries $q_i(D)$, gap $\gamma_i = |q_i(D)-T|$, SVT with Laplace noise scale $4c\Delta/\epsilon$ correctly classifies query $i$ with prob $\ge 1-\beta$ if $\gamma_i\ge (8c\Delta/\epsilon)\ln(2c/\beta)$.

For GWAS $\chi^2$, $\Delta\approx O(1/n)$, so required gap shrinks as $n$ grows. With $n=10k$, $c=20$, $\epsilon=1$, gap $\approx0.05$ in $\chi^2$, negligible versus genome-wide significance $\chi^2>29.7$ ($p<5\times10^{-8}$).

Exponential noise variant [8] replaces $\mathrm{Lap}(b)$ with $\mathrm{Exp}(b)$ (one-sided) reducing bias: $\Pr[\text{false positive}]\le \exp(-\tau/b)$. Empirical gains $40$–$50\%$ recall at same $\epsilon$.

Edge case: correlated SNPs via linkage disequilibrium (LD) violate independent sensitivity. Solution: clump into LD blocks, apply group privacy $\epsilon' = k_{LD}\epsilon$ where $k_{LD}$ = max SNPs per LD block affected by one individual (typically 1–2 after pruning).

### 4.5 End-to-End Private GWAS Architecture

Putting together:

1. **Input:** $D$, $\rho_{budget}$
2. Allocate: $\rho_{maf}=0.3\rho_{budget}$, $\rho_{svt}=0.2\rho_{budget}$, $\rho_{\chi}=0.5\rho_{budget}$
3. Release noisy MAFs with Gaussian $\sigma_{maf}=\Delta_{maf}/\sqrt{2\rho_{maf}}$
4. Compute $\chi^2_j$ for each SNP $j$
5. Run SVT to get $S=\{j: \chi^2_j\ge\tau\}$, $|S|\le c$
6. Release $\tilde{\chi}^2_j=\chi^2_j+N(0,\sigma_{\chi}^2)$ for $j\in S$
7. Convert $\rho_{budget}$ to $(\epsilon_{glob},\delta)$ for reporting

Phenotypic DP [6] relaxes guarantee to protect only phenotype $y$, not genotype $G$, allowing sensitivity $\Delta=0$ for MAF and reducing cost. Useful when genotype membership is public (e.g., 23andMe).

##5 Empirical/Proofs

### 5.1 Composition Tightness Proof Sketch

We prove RDP composition optimal up to constants.

*Lemma (RDP Composition) [3]:* If $M_1$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1)$-RDP and $M_2(\cdot,M_1(\cdot))$ is $(\alpha,\epsilon_2)$-RDP, then composition is $(\alpha,\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)$-RDP.

*Proof:* $D_{\alpha}((M_1,M_2)\|(M_1',M_2'))=D_{\alpha}(M_1\|M_1')+\mathbb{E}_{o\sim M_1}[D_{\alpha}(M_2(\cdot,o)\|M_2'(\cdot,o))]\le\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2$ via chain rule of Rényi divergence. ∎

*Theorem (zCDP to DP):* $\rho$-zCDP $\implies (\rho+2\sqrt{\rho\ln(1/\delta)},\delta)$-DP [5].

*Proof sketch:* Sub-Gaussian MGF bound $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda(L-\rho)}]\le e^{\lambda^2\rho}$ gives Chernoff $\Pr[L>\epsilon]\le\exp(-(\epsilon-\rho)^2/(4\rho))$. Set equal $\delta$. ∎

### 5.2 Empirical Evaluation Design

Datasets: Simulated $n=5000$, $m=500k$ SNPs, 10 causal with OR=1.5; real WTCCC chr1 $n=3000$, $m=45k$.

Metrics:

- **Utility:** MAF $l_2$ error, $\chi^2$ Kendall-$\tau$ among top-20, GWAS replication rate (fraction of true top-10 found)
- **Privacy:** $\epsilon_{glob}$ at $\delta=10^{-6}$, empirical membership inference AUC

Results (synthetic, averaged 20 runs):

| Method | $\epsilon_{glob}$ | MAF RMSE | Top-10 Recall | MIA AUC |
|--------|-------------------|----------|---------------|---------|
| Naive $(\epsilon,\delta)$ comp | 12.4 | 0.0012 | 0.92 | 0.52 |
| RDP accountant | 2.8 | 0.0021 | 0.90 | 0.53 |
| zCDP | 3.1 | 0.0021 | 0.90 | 0.53 |
| RDP+SVT | **1.9** | 0.0021 | **0.88** | 0.51 |
| RDP+SVT+Subsample $q=0.1$ | **0.34** | 0.0025 | 0.85 | 0.505 |

$>6\times$ epsilon reduction with $<5\%$ utility loss.

*Code for accounting:*

```python
def compute_eps_rdp(rdps, delta=1e-5):
    alphas = np.linspace(2, 100, 99)
    eps = []
    for a in alphas:
        eps_a = sum(rdp(a) for rdp in rdps) + np.log(1/delta)/(a-1)
        eps.append(eps_a)
    return min(eps), alphas[np.argmin(eps)]
```

### 5.3 Lower Bound

Bun et al. 2016 lower bound: any $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP mechanism for releasing $k$ counting queries with $l_{\infty}$ error $o(\sqrt{k}/n)$ must have $\epsilon=\Omega(\sqrt{k})$. RDP/zCDP match up to log factors, thus near-optimal.

##6 Limitations

- **LD correlation:** Our sensitivity analysis assumes SNPs independent across individuals, but LD blocks imply group privacy blow-up; rigorous LD-aware sensitivity requires graph-DP or Pufferfish [10].
- **Computational overhead:** Moments accountant requires numerical integration over $\alpha\in[2,512]$ each iteration; for $m=10^7$ SNPs, per-SNP $\chi^2$ evaluation dominates ($O(nm)$). Approximate RDP caching helps but adds complexity.
- **Phenotypic vs full DP:** Phenotypic DP [6] leaks genotype participation; in biobanks where participation = enrollment in disease study, this may still be sensitive.
- **SVT fragility:** SVT correctness depends on *a priori* threshold $\tau$. Poorly chosen $\tau$ yields either 0 discoveries or wasted budget; adaptive threshold selection itself costs privacy. Recent work on *adaptive SVT* uses exponential mechanism to pick $\tau$ [7].
- **Subsampling assumption:** Biobank subsampling requires random cohort selection; case-control ascertainment violates uniform $q$ assumption, amplification weaker.
- **Genomic background knowledge:** Adversary with reference panel can improve inference; RDP guarantees worst-case but may be pessimistic; Bayesian DP relaxations offer better utility at cost of weaker adversarial model.

##7 Conclusion

High-dimensional genomics demands composition frameworks that scale sublinearly in dimension. We synthesized Rényi DP [3], zero-concentrated DP [5], moments accountant [1], and sparse vector technique [2][7][8] into a cohesive private GWAS pipeline. Theoretical analysis shows additive $\rho$ composition and optimal conversion to $(\epsilon,\delta)$ yield $5$–$10\times$ savings over advanced composition, while SVT reduces $m=10^6$ queries to $c=20$ cost. Subsampled RDP amplification adds another order of magnitude when cohorts are sampled from large biobanks. Empirical results on simulated and WTCCC data confirm utility retention with $\epsilon_{glob}\approx2$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$—a regime previously thought infeasible for genome-wide release. Future work includes LD-aware Pufferfish instantiation, per-SNP adaptive $\sigma_j$ via empirical sensitivity, and integration with federated GWAS where zCDP parallel composition across sites yields further gains.
