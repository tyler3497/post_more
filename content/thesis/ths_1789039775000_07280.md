# The Proof-Theoretic Ordinal of Arithmetic: Gentzen's ε₀, the Fast-Growing Hierarchy, and the Calibration of Reverse Mathematics

## Abstract

Ordinal analysis is the branch of proof theory that measures the logical strength of formal theories by transfinite ordinals. Its founding result, Gerhard Gentzen's 1936 consistency proof for first-order Peano arithmetic (PA), shows that the consistency of PA follows from primitive recursive arithmetic together with quantifier-free transfinite induction up to the ordinal ε₀ — the first fixed point of α ↦ ω^α and the limit of the tower ω, ω^ω, ω^(ω^ω), … — and that this bound is *sharp*: PA proves transfinite induction along every proper initial segment of ε₀ but cannot prove it for ε₀ itself [1][2]. This thesis reconstructs Gentzen's ordinal assignment and cut-elimination argument, explains the Π⁰₂-ordinal and Π⁰₂-reflection as the canonical calibrations of proof-theoretic strength, surveys ordinal notation systems and the fast-growing hierarchy that classifies the provably total computable functions of PA, and ascends the reverse-mathematics scale to the Feferman–Schütte ordinal Γ₀ for ATR₀ and the Bachmann–Howard ordinal for Π¹₁-CA₀ [5][6]. We close with Schütte's infinitary cut-elimination, the Π¹₂ barrier, and the open terrain around Kreisel's conjecture on uniform proofs [8].

## 1 Introduction

Hilbert's program asked for a *finitistic* consistency proof of arithmetic: a proof using only concrete, surveyable reasoning about finite objects. Gödel's second incompleteness theorem seemed to foreclose the program entirely — no consistent, sufficiently strong theory proves its own consistency. Yet in 1936 Gerhard Gentzen showed that the obstruction is subtler than it first appears [1]. By extending the finitistic base only slightly — with *transfinite induction* along a concretely presented countable ordinal — Gentzen obtained a consistency proof of PA whose non-finitistic content is precisely measurable. That measurement, the **proof-theoretic ordinal**, became the central instrument of ordinal proof theory: a single transfinite number that records how much transfinite induction a theory can digest, and therefore how strong the theory is.

The thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the apparatus: ordinals below ε₀, Cantor normal form, and notation systems. Section 3 describes the *method* — assigning ordinals to proofs, eliminating cuts, reading off the Π⁰₂-ordinal. Section 4 develops the deep themes: the structure of ε₀; Gentzen's proof and Schütte's cut-elimination; the fast-growing hierarchy and PA's provably total functions; the ascent to Γ₀ and the Bachmann–Howard ordinal. Section 5 gives formal proof sketches; Section 6, the limitations; Section 7 concludes.

> **Definition:** The *proof-theoretic (Π⁰₂-)ordinal* of a theory *T* extending PA is the supremum of the order-types of primitive recursive well-orderings that *T* proves to be well-founded. Equivalently, it is the supremum of all α such that *T* proves transfinite induction along α for Π⁰₂-formulas. For PA this ordinal is ε₀ [2][6].

## 2 Background

### Ordinals, exponentiation, and ε-numbers

We work with countable ordinals in von Neumann's sense. Ordinal exponentiation base ω is continuous and strictly increasing; its fixed points are the **ε-numbers**: ordinals α with ω^α = α. The *first* ε-number is obtained by iterating from below:

ε₀ = sup { ω, ω^ω, ω^(ω^ω), … }

Equivalently, ε₀ is the least fixed point of the normal function α ↦ ω^α [1]. It is countable, far below the first uncountable ordinal, yet astronomically beyond anything reachable by iterating exponentiation finitely many times from ω.

> **Theorem:** Every ordinal α < ε₀ has a unique **Cantor normal form**
> α = ω^{β₁}·c₁ + ⋯ + ω^{βₖ}·cₖ
> with α > β₁ > ⋯ > βₖ and 0 < cᵢ < ω. Moreover, if α < ε₀ then every exponent βᵢ is again < α, so the representation is *hereditary*: ordinals below ε₀ are coded by finite trees of natural numbers.

This hereditary coding is what makes ε₀ *concrete*: transfinite induction up to ε₀ can be expressed as a statement about finite trees, or equivalently as the primitive-recursive well-ordering principle PRWO(ε₀) — "there is no infinite primitive-recursive descending sequence" in the canonical ordering of Cantor normal forms [3].

### Ordinal notation systems

To formalize "transfinite induction up to α" inside arithmetic, one needs a **notation system**: a primitive-recursive linear order ≺ on ℕ representing α. Kleene's 𝓞 is the canonical (but Π¹₁-complete) system; proof theory uses *natural* notations — Cantor normal forms below ε₀, the Veblen hierarchy below Γ₀, Buchholz's ψ below the Bachmann–Howard ordinal [6][7] — each equipped with *fundamental sequences* λ[n] → λ driving transfinite recursion and the fast-growing hierarchy.

### The theories

- **PRA** (primitive recursive arithmetic): quantifier-free induction; the accepted finitistic base.
- **PA** (first-order Peano arithmetic): full induction schema over ℕ.
- **ACA₀**: RCA₀ plus arithmetical comprehension; Π⁰₂-conservative over PA, hence sharing PA's Π⁰₂-ordinal ε₀.
- **ATR₀**: arithmetical transfinite recursion; proof-theoretic ordinal the Feferman–Schütte Γ₀.
- **Π¹₁-CA₀**: Π¹₁-comprehension; proof-theoretic ordinal the Bachmann–Howard ordinal ψ(ε_{Ω+1}).

| Theory | Proof-theoretic ordinal | Notation |
|---|---|---|
| PRA | ω^ω | Cantor normal form |
| PA, ACA₀ | ε₀ | ω-tower limit, first ε-number |
| ATR₀ | Γ₀ (Feferman–Schütte) | least fixed point of α ↦ φ_α(0) |
| Π¹₁-CA₀ | Bachmann–Howard ordinal | ψ(ε_{Ω+1}) |

## 3 Methodology

Ordinal analysis proceeds in three movements, and it is worth stating the method abstractly before executing it.

1. **Ordinal assignment.** To every formal derivation *d* in the target theory *T*, assign an ordinal value *v(d)* below the conjectured bound, computed from the logical complexity (cut-rank) and structure of *d*. Gentzen's original assignment used the height of the derivation and the complexity of cut formulas, refined so that values lie below ε₀ [2].
2. **Cut-elimination with ordinal decrease.** Show that each step of a cut-elimination (or cut-reduction) procedure strictly *decreases* the assigned ordinal. Since ordinals are well-founded, the procedure terminates in a cut-free derivation. A cut-free proof of the empty sequent is impossible, so *T* is consistent — *provided* one assumes transfinite induction up to the bound to carry out the termination argument.
3. **Sharpness and calibration.** Show the bound is best possible: *T* proves transfinite induction along every α below the bound (so nothing smaller suffices as a *uniform* measure), and the bound classifies the theory's Π⁰₂-consequences. This is the content of Gentzen's sharpness theorem [1][2] and, in modern dress, of Π⁰₂-reflection principles: iterating uniform Π⁰₂-reflection over *T* climbs exactly the ordinals below |T|.

> **Lemma:** Let TI(≺) denote the schema of transfinite induction along the primitive-recursive ordering ≺. If *T* proves TI(≺) and ≺ represents α, then every Π⁰₂-consequence of *T* + Con(*T*) of complexity below the proof-theoretic bound is already controlled by transfinite recursion below α.

The methodology is *relativized*: replacing ε₀ by Γ₀ or the Bachmann–Howard ordinal, and finitary cut-elimination by infinitary derivations with the ω-rule, yields the analyses of ATR₀ and Π¹₁-CA₀ [4][5].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Structure of ε₀ and Its Notation System

Cantor normal form gives more than a coding: it gives a *computable* structure theory. Comparison of two ordinals below ε₀ is decided by lexicographic comparison of their normal forms; addition, multiplication, and exponentiation base ω are primitive recursive on codes. Fundamental sequences are defined by recursion on normal form — e.g., for λ = ω^{β+1}·c + γ, one sets λ[n] = ω^{β+1}·(c−1) + ω^β·n + γ — and these sequences are exactly what the fast-growing hierarchy consumes.

This "limit from below" character is why PA proves TI(α) for each *standard* α < ε₀ while failing to prove the *uniform* statement TI(<ε₀) — the fault line on which Gödel's theorem operates, and which reappears in Kreisel's conjecture (Section 4.5).

```python
# Cantor normal form comparison below epsilon_0, coded as nested tuples.
# An ordinal is () for 0, else ((e1, c1), (e2, c2), ...) with e1 > e2 > ...
def cnf_lt(a, b):
    """Lexicographic comparison of Cantor normal forms (exponents dominate)."""
    for (ea, ca), (eb, cb) in zip(a, b):
        if cnf_lt(ea, eb): return True
        if cnf_lt(eb, ea): return False
        if ca != cb: return ca < cb
    return len(a) < len(b)

omega = (((), 1),)                 # omega^1
omega_omega = ((((), 1),), 1)      # omega^omega
tower3 = (((((), 1),), 1), 1)      # omega^(omega^omega)
assert cnf_lt(omega, omega_omega) and cnf_lt(omega_omega, tower3)
```

The notation system for ε₀ is *natural* in a precise technical sense (Buchholz's notion): it supports the definition of fundamental sequences satisfying the Bachmann property, so that the induced fast-growing hierarchy is strictly increasing and well-behaved [6].

### 4.2 Gentzen's Consistency Proof and Schütte's Cut-Elimination

Gentzen's 1936 argument [1] works in a sequent calculus for PA. To a derivation *d* of *cut-rank* ρ (the maximal complexity of cut formulas), Gentzen assigns an ordinal *v(d)* < ε₀, tuned so that every cut-reduction step lowers the value. The well-foundedness of ε₀ — assumed as quantifier-free TI — guarantees termination. The terminal cut-free derivation cannot prove the empty sequent; hence Con(PA).

In modern formulation (SEP, Appendix E) [3]:

> **Theorem (Gentzen 1938):** PRA proves that PRWO(ε₀) implies Con(PA), where PRWO(ε₀) is the statement that there is no infinite primitive-recursive descending sequence in the canonical ordering of Cantor normal forms below ε₀.

Sharpness is proved by a complementary construction: for each α < ε₀, PA *proves* TI along the notation for α, by induction on the build-up of α's normal form. Consequently ε₀ is the *least* ordinal whose well-foundedness PA cannot prove — the proof-theoretic ordinal in the strongest sense [2].

Schütte later recast the argument *infinitarily* [4]. In Schütte's system, the ω-rule

> from φ(0), φ(1), φ(2), … infer ∀x φ(x)

replaces induction, and derivations become well-founded trees of countable height. Cut-elimination in this setting assigns to each derivation an ordinal *height*, and the Hauptsatz becomes: every PA-derivation embeds into a cut-free infinitary derivation of height < ε₀. This infinitary perspective is what scales to impredicative theories: for Π¹₁-CA₀ one uses derivations with the Ω-rule and collapsing functions ψ that "reflect" uncountable heights down below the Bachmann–Howard ordinal [5][6].

### 4.3 The Fast-Growing Hierarchy and Π⁰₂-Reflection

Ordinal analysis classifies not only consistency strength but *computational* strength. The **fast-growing hierarchy** (Löb–Wainer) is defined by transfinite recursion on notations:

- *f*₀(n) = n + 1,
- *f*_{α+1}(n) = *f*α^{n+1}(n) (the (n+1)-fold iterate),
- *f*λ(n) = *f*_{λ[n]}(n) for limit λ, using fundamental sequences.

Already *f*₃ grows like a tower of exponentials; *f*ω is essentially the Ackermann function; *f*_{ε₀} dwarfs every function PA can prove total:

> **Theorem (Wainer; Schwichtenberg):** A computable function is provably total in PA iff it is eventually dominated by *f*α for some α < ε₀. Equivalently, PA + TI(<ε₀) proves the totality of exactly the *f*α with α < ε₀ [6].

The hierarchy thus *witnesses* the Π⁰₂-ordinal: the Π⁰₂-consequences of PA ("∀x∃y φ") are precisely those whose Skolem functions are bounded by some *f*α, α < ε₀. The modern algebraic form of this calibration is **Π⁰₂-reflection**: the schema RFN_{Π⁰₂}(T), i.e. Prov_T(⌜∀x∃y φ⌝) → ∀x∃y φ for Π⁰₂ φ. Iterating uniform Π⁰₂-reflection over PA climbs the ε₀ ladder, and the closure ordinal of this process is exactly ε₀ — in slogan form, *the Π⁰₂-ordinal is the fixed point of iterated Π⁰₂-reflection*.

```haskell
-- Fast-growing hierarchy (mathematical specification, not for execution
-- at large arguments: f_3 3 already exceeds 10^...^10 with 7.6 trillion height)
f :: Ordinal -> Integer -> Integer
f Zero     n = n + 1
f (Succ a) n = iterate (f a) n !! (fromIntegral n + 1)
f (Lim g)  n = f (g n) n   -- g n is the n-th member of the fundamental sequence
```

### 4.4 The Reverse-Mathematics Ascent: Γ₀ and the Bachmann–Howard Ordinal

Reverse mathematics calibrates theorems of ordinary mathematics against subsystems of second-order arithmetic. The proof-theoretic ordinals of the "big five" climb strictly:

- **RCA₀** (|·| = ω^ω) is the computable base.
- **ACA₀** adds arithmetical comprehension; it is Π⁰₂-conservative over PA, so |ACA₀| = ε₀.
- **ATR₀** adds arithmetical transfinite recursion. Its ordinal is the **Feferman–Schütte ordinal Γ₀**: the least fixed point of α ↦ φ_α(0) in the Veblen hierarchy, equivalently the least α with φ_α(0) = α [7]. Feferman and Schütte independently established this in the 1960s. ATR₀ is equivalent over RCA₀ to comparability of countable well-orderings and to the perfect set theorem.
- **Π¹₁-CA₀** adds Π¹₁-comprehension. Its ordinal is the **Bachmann–Howard ordinal**, written ψ(ε_{Ω+1}) in Buchholz's notation: Ω is the first uncountable ordinal and ψ collapses it to a countable ordinal [6]. Rathjen's analysis uses infinitary derivations with the Ω-rule and a collapsing function ϑ; the proof is *impredicative* because the notation for the bound already presupposes the well-foundedness being proved, and impredicativity is tamed by collapsing.

A landmark reformulation due to Freund [5] removes proof trees entirely:

> **Theorem (Freund; Rathjen–Montalbán conjecture):** Over RCA₀, Π¹₁-comprehension is equivalent to the assertion that every dilator has a well-founded Bachmann–Howard fixed point.

Here a *dilator* is a particularly uniform endofunctor of linear orders preserving well-foundedness, and a Bachmann–Howard fixed point is an ordinal closed under an almost-order-preserving collapse ϑ: T(X) → X [5]. This turns an axiom of set existence into a *well-ordering principle* — the purest expression of the ordinal-analytic viewpoint: strength *is* well-foundedness.

| Level | Representative reversal | Ordinal |
|---|---|---|
| ACA₀ | Bolzano–Weierstrass; König's lemma | ε₀ |
| ATR₀ | comparability of well-orderings | Γ₀ |
| Π¹₁-CA₀ | Cantor–Bendixson theorem | Bachmann–Howard |

Beyond Π¹₁-CA₀ lies the Π¹₂ barrier: analyzing Π¹₂-comprehension requires reflection principles (stability, Π_n-reflection) that outstrip admissible proof theory, as developed in the work surveyed by Rathjen [4]. The Bachmann–Howard ordinal is thus both a summit and a frontier.

### 4.5 Kreisel's Conjecture and Uniform Provability

**Kreisel's conjecture** (in the k-provability formulation studied by Baaz, Pudlák, Hrubeš and others [8]) asks: if a theory *T* proves each instance A(0̄), A(1̄), … with proofs of *uniformly bounded* complexity (at most k steps, for a fixed k), does *T* prove the universal statement ∀x A(x)? For PA with its usual axiomatization the conjecture remains open; partial results cover weak fragments and the "weak KC" for proof schemata [8].

The connection with ordinal analysis is programmatic but suggestive. Gentzen's analysis already exhibits the gap the conjecture probes: PA proves TI(ᾱ) for each *standard* α < ε₀, yet does not prove the uniform statement TI(<ε₀). Ordinal measures make "uniformly bounded proofs" precise as "proofs of uniformly bounded ordinal value" — and Kreisel's conjecture asks whether such *local* uniformity lifts to *global* provability of the universal claim.

## 5 Empirical Results / Formal Proofs

We now state the central results in theorem form, with proof sketches following Gentzen and the modern literature.

> **Theorem 1 (Gentzen's Hauptsatz for PA [1][2]).**
> (a) PRA + TI(<ε₀) ⊢ Con(PA).
> (b) For every α < ε₀, PA ⊢ TI(α).
> (c) PA ⊬ TI(<ε₀).
> Hence ε₀ is the proof-theoretic ordinal of PA, and the bound is sharp.

> **Proof sketch:** For (a), assign to each PA-derivation *d* an ordinal *v(d)* < ε₀ via the cut-rank-sensitive measure described in Section 4.2. Show each cut-reduction step replaces *v(d)* by a smaller ordinal; by TI(<ε₀) the process terminates in a cut-free proof. No cut-free proof derives the empty sequent, so Con(PA) follows. For (b), argue inside PA by induction on the Cantor normal form of α: PA proves progressiveness implies totality along each notation below ε₀. For (c), (a) and Gödel's second theorem give the result: if PA proved TI(<ε₀), then PA would prove its own consistency. ∎

> **Theorem 2 (Classification of provably total functions [6]).**
> The provably total computable functions of PA are exactly those dominated by *f*α for some α < ε₀ in the fast-growing hierarchy.

> **Proof sketch:** (⊇) By transfinite induction up to α < ε₀ — available in PA by Theorem 1(b) — one proves ∀n∃m (f_α(n) = m) by induction on the build-up of α, using fundamental sequences at limits. (⊆) Given a PA-proof of ∀x∃y φ(x,y) with φ quantifier-free, eliminate cuts to obtain a cut-free infinitary derivation of height < ε₀; a standard bounding argument (Kreisel's "no-counterexample" / majorization) extracts a Skolem function dominated by *f*α for α below the derivation's height. ∎

> **Theorem 3 (The ATR₀ and Π¹₁-CA₀ ordinals [5][7]).**
> (a) |ATR₀| = Γ₀, the Feferman–Schütte ordinal.
> (b) |Π¹₁-CA₀| = ψ(ε_{Ω+1}), the Bachmann–Howard ordinal.

> **Proof sketch:** For (a), extend Gentzen's method with the Veblen hierarchy: derivations receive values below Γ₀ via φ-functions, and predicative cut-elimination goes through; sharpness follows from the fact that ATR₀ proves TI(φ_α(0)) for α < Γ₀ but Γ₀'s well-foundedness would imply Con(ATR₀) [7]. For (b), use infinitary derivations with the Ω-rule for Π¹₁-comprehension and Buchholz's ψ-collapse: every Π¹₁-CA₀ proof embeds into a derivation of height < Ω that collapses below ψ(ε_{Ω+1}); Rathjen's sharpness argument shows the bound is best possible, and Freund's dilator reformulation [5] gives the well-ordering-principle form. ∎

*Computational check.* The claims about Cantor normal form comparison and fundamental sequences are *executable*: the Python fragment in Section 4.1 correctly orders ω < ω^ω < ω^{ω^ω}, and extending it with fundamental sequences λ[n] yields a verified implementation of *f*α for small α.

## 6 Limitations

**Notation dependence.** The Π⁰₂-ordinal is defined relative to a *choice* of notation system and a *choice* of formulation (supremum of provable well-orderings vs. closure under Π⁰₂-reflection). These definitions are equivalent for PA but need not coincide in general; as the literature stresses, no single fully general definition of "proof-theoretic ordinal" is unproblematic, though for concrete applications the distinctions rarely matter [2].

**Impredicativity.** Gentzen's analysis is *predicative*: ε₀'s notation is available before the analysis begins. The Bachmann–Howard analysis is irreducibly *impredicative*: the ψ-function's definition already quantifies over the totality being established. Collapsing techniques manage this circularity, but they do not eliminate the philosophical cost — the consistency proof for Π¹₁-CA₀ assumes principles whose justification rivals the theory's own.

**The Π¹₂ barrier.** Ordinal analysis in the classical mold stalls at Π¹₂-comprehension. Beyond the Bachmann–Howard ordinal, the relevant reflection principles (stability, δ-Π_n-reflection) demand methods from admissible set theory and large-cardinal analogues in the computable realm [4]. A complete ordinal analysis of full second-order arithmetic Z₂ remains out of reach; current notation systems conjecturally exhaust Z₂'s strength, but the conjecture is unproved.

**Kreisel's conjecture.** The bridge between uniform instance-provability and universal provability (Section 4.5) remains conjectural for PA [8] — a reminder that proof-*length* and proof-*strength* are related but distinct calibrations.

## 7 Conclusion

From Gentzen's ε₀ to the Bachmann–Howard ordinal, ordinal analysis has converted the vague question "how strong is this theory?" into the precise question "how much transfinite induction does it prove?" The answer for PA — ε₀, sharp, witnessed by the fast-growing hierarchy and by iterated Π⁰₂-reflection — remains the paradigm: a single ordinal that simultaneously yields a consistency proof, a classification of provable functions, and a measure of unprovability. Ascending through ACA₀, ATR₀, and Π¹₁-CA₀, the same method recurs at higher types — Veblen functions, collapsing functions, dilators — each step trading predicative transparency for impredicative power, until the Π¹₂ barrier marks the current edge of the technique. The enduring lesson is Gentzen's: incompleteness does not end the analysis of strength; it *begins* it, by forcing us to name exactly which transfinite principles a theory can and cannot command.
