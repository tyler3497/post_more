# Homotopy Type Theory and Cubical Proof Assistants: The Univalence Axiom, Higher Inductive Types, and Computational Canonicity

## Abstract

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) reinterprets Martin-Löf dependent type theory through the lens of homotopy theory: types are spaces, terms are points, and identity types are path spaces. This article surveys the field's two foundational innovations — Vladimir Voevodsky's **univalence axiom**, which identifies the identity type of a universe with the type of homotopy equivalences, and **higher inductive types** (HITs), which generate types equipped with prescribed path constructors — together with the cubical reconstruction that restores their computational meaning. We explain the simplicial-set model of type theory, the stratification of types into homotopy levels, and synthetic proofs of classical results such as the computation of the fundamental group of the circle. We then describe cubical type theory's interval-based account of paths, homogeneous composition and transport, Glue types, and the schema for higher inductive types, culminating in canonicity theorems and the Cubical Agda proof assistant. The exposition is accompanied by formal statements of key theorems, Agda code excerpts, comparison tables, and generated diagrams.

## 1. Introduction

In 2006, Vladimir Voevodsky — a Fields medalist — began a program to rebuild the foundations of mathematics on *homotopy-theoretic* rather than set-theoretic lines [2]. His motivation was partly practical: the formalization of modern mathematics in proof assistants was, in his assessment, failing, because set-theoretic encodings of mathematical structures are far removed from mathematical practice, where one routinely identifies isomorphic structures and works "up to" equivalence. Voevodsky's proposal, developed with Steve Awodey, Michael Warren, and others, and consolidated during the 2012–2013 special year on Univalent Foundations at the Institute for Advanced Study, was to take Martin-Löf's dependent type theory and read it *homotopically*: a type $A$ is a space, a term $a : A$ is a point of that space, a dependent function $f : \Pi_{x:A} B(x)$ is a continuous section, and — most radically — the identity type $\mathrm{Id}_A(a,b)$ is the *space of paths* from $a$ to $b$ [1].

This single reinterpretation dissolves several long-standing puzzles. Why does the identity type admit non-trivial higher structure (terms of $\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Id}_A(a,b)}(p,q)$, and so on)? Because path spaces have path spaces. Why can one have a type $X$ with $(X \to X) \simeq X + 1$, an apparent violation of Cantor's theorem? Because the "isomorphism" is a homotopy equivalence of spaces, not a bijection of sets [2]. And why should the identity type of a universe $\mathcal{U}$ of types be interesting at all? Because, as Voevodsky realized around 2009, it satisfies the **univalence axiom**: paths between types in the universe correspond exactly to equivalences between them.

The present article is a self-contained technical survey of this program and of its computational redemption in **cubical type theory**. We cover the univalence axiom and its corollaries (function extensionality, the structure identity principle); **higher inductive types** from the circle $\mathbb{S}^1$ to truncations, with the encode–decode proof that $\pi_1(\mathbb{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ [4]; the **cubical** reformulation — interval, homogeneous composition and transport, Glue types — which turns univalence from a computation-blocking axiom into a theorem with computational content [3]; **canonicity** results and the Cubical Agda proof assistant [5, 7]; and an honest account of limitations. The exposition is accompanied by formal statements of key theorems, Agda code excerpts, comparison tables, and generated diagrams.

> **Theorem (Univalence, informal):** *For small types $A, B : \mathcal{U}$, the canonical map $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \to (A \simeq B)$ is an equivalence. Identity of types coincides with equivalence of types* [1].

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Martin-Löf type theory and the identity type

Martin-Löf's intensional dependent type theory (MLTT) is generated by a small collection of type formers — the empty type $\mathbf{0}$, unit $\mathbf{1}$, booleans, natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$, dependent products $\Pi_{x:A}B(x)$, dependent sums $\Sigma_{x:A}B(x)$, disjoint unions, and universes $\mathcal{U}$ — each specified by formation, introduction, elimination, and computation rules. The subtlest is the **identity type**: for $A$ a type and $a, b : A$, there is a type $\mathrm{Id}_A(a,b)$ (written $a =_A b$) with a single constructor $\mathrm{refl}_a : a =_A a$ and an eliminator $J$ (path induction) expressing that to prove $C(a,b,p)$ for all $p : a =_A b$ it suffices to prove it for $p \equiv \mathrm{refl}$.

In the 1990s this eliminator looked mysterious: why should identity proofs themselves have identity proofs? The answer arrived in two steps. First, Hofmann and Streicher (1998) built a model of type theory in **groupoids**, where $\mathrm{Id}_A(a,b)$ is the hom-set of isomorphisms — already showing that identity can be non-trivial. Then Awodey–Warren and independently Voevodsky (2005–2006) generalized this to arbitrary homotopy types: the former via Quillen model categories, the latter via **Kan simplicial sets**. Lumsdaine and van den Berg–Garner made the $\infty$-groupoid structure of types precise syntactically: identity proofs compose, invert, and satisfy the groupoid laws up to higher identity proofs, coherently at all levels [1].

### 2.2 The simplicial model and the road to univalence

Voevodsky's model interprets contexts as simplicial sets, types as Kan fibrations, and terms as sections. The universe is (a fibrant replacement of) the simplicial set of small Kan complexes, and Voevodsky observed that it satisfies a striking extra property: the canonical map from paths in the universe to equivalences of fibers is itself an equivalence. This is **univalence** [2]. Crucially, univalence *fails* in the set-theoretic model — it is a genuinely homotopical principle. Adding it as an axiom to MLTT yields *homotopy type theory* in the axiomatic sense; the HoTT Book [1] develops hundreds of pages of synthetic homotopy theory on this basis.

| Feature | Set-theoretic reading | Homotopical reading |
|---|---|---|
| Type $A$ | Set of elements | Space / $\infty$-groupoid |
| Term $a : A$ | Element | Point |
| $f : A \to B$ | Function | Continuous map |
| $p : a =_A b$ | Propositional equality proof | Path from $a$ to $b$ |
| $p = q$ for $p, q : a =_A b$ | Proof irrelevance | Homotopy between paths |
| $\mathcal{U}$ | Set of sets | Classifying space of spaces |
| $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B)$ | (usually empty/non-trivial) | Space of equivalences $A \simeq B$ (univalence) |

### 2.3 The computational problem with axioms

There is a catch. In MLTT, every closed term of $\mathbb{N}$ computes to a numeral — the **canonicity** property that makes type theory a programming language as well as a logic. Postulating univalence (or HITs) as an *axiom* — a constant $\mathrm{ua} : \mathrm{isEquiv}(\mathrm{idtoeqv}_{A,B})$ with no computation rules — breaks canonicity: a closed term of $\mathbb{N}$ can now get "stuck" on the axiom, and the system no longer knows how to evaluate it [3, 5]. Voevodsky himself noted that his axiom "doesn't seem to have any computational meaning." The cubical program, developed by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg (CCHM) [3], Angiuli, Brunerie, Coquand, Favonia, Harper, and Licata [9], and others, solves this by replacing axioms with *operations on cubes* that compute.

## 3. Methodology

This article is a *survey with reconstruction*: we isolate the load-bearing definitions and theorems of the HoTT/cubical literature, restate them in uniform notation, and verify technical claims against the primary sources [1]–[10] retrieved by targeted web search. Agda code excerpts follow Cubical Agda's library conventions (`Cubical.Foundations.*`). Where we report implementation behavior (e.g., the computation rules of `ua`), we quote the documented definitional equalities rather than inventing them.

Notation: $\mathcal{U}$ is a universe; $A \simeq B$ denotes the type of equivalences, i.e. $\Sigma_{f : A \to B}\, \mathrm{isEquiv}(f)$ where $\mathrm{isEquiv}(f) \equiv \Pi_{y:B}\, \mathrm{isContr}(\Sigma_{x:A}\, f(x) = y)$ is Voevodsky's proposition-valued notion of equivalence [2]; $a =_A b$ is the identity type; $\mathbb{I}$ is the cubical interval with endpoints $i0, i1$; $\mathrm{PathP}$ is the heterogeneous path type.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 The univalence axiom: identity is equivalence

Given a universe $\mathcal{U}$ and $A, B : \mathcal{U}$, path induction defines a canonical function

$$\mathrm{idtoeqv}_{A,B} : (A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \longrightarrow (A \simeq B)$$

by sending $\mathrm{refl}_A$ to the identity equivalence. In set-theoretic semantics this map is far from surjective: distinct-but-isomorphic sets are not *equal*. The **univalence axiom** asserts that it is an equivalence:

> **Axiom (Univalence) [1, 2]:** *For all $A, B : \mathcal{U}$, the type $\mathrm{isEquiv}(\mathrm{idtoeqv}_{A,B})$ is inhabited. Equivalently, $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B)$.*

Three consequences illustrate its force:

1. **Function extensionality.** Pointwise equal functions are equal: $(\Pi_{x:A}\, f(x) = g(x)) \to (f =_{A \to B} g)$ [1]. In the simplicial model this was a surprise — function extensionality follows from univalence, though it is independent of MLTT alone.
2. **Propositional extensionality and the structure identity principle.** Logically equivalent mere propositions are equal, and more generally, for structured types (groups, categories), *isomorphic structures are identical* — the formal counterpart of mathematical practice [1, 5].
3. **Transport computes with the equivalence.** In cubical type theory, transporting along the path obtained from an equivalence *applies the underlying function* — the fact that makes univalence usable for program transport, e.g. developing theory with unary naturals while computing with binary ones [5].

Philosophically, univalence does not *collapse* equivalence onto a prior notion of identity; it *expands* identity so that the two coincide [1], making $\mathcal{U}$ the object classifier of the $\infty$-topos of spaces.

### 4.2 Higher inductive types: spaces by generators and relations

Ordinary inductive types are generated by *point* constructors ($\mathrm{zero} : \mathbb{N}$, $\mathrm{succ} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$). A **higher inductive type** additionally admits *path* constructors — identifications imposed between terms [1, 4]. The paradigmatic example is the circle:

```agda
data S¹ : Type where
  base : S¹
  loop : base ≡ base
```

The induction principle says: to define $f : \Pi_{x:\mathbb{S}^1} P(x)$, give $b : P(\mathrm{base})$ and a *dependent* path $l : \mathrm{PathP}\, (\lambda i \to P(\mathrm{loop}\, i))\, b\, b$ lying over `loop`. The non-dependent recursor maps into any type $B$ with a point $b : B$ and a loop $l : b \equiv b$. This is exactly the universal property of the topological circle — but stated *synthetically*, with no topology in sight.

The HIT toolkit developed in the HoTT Book includes [1]: **suspensions** $\Sigma A$ (two poles, meridians indexed by $A$; the spheres $\mathbb{S}^n$ arise by iterating suspension); **truncations** $\|A\|_n$, the universal $n$-type receiving a map from $A$ ($\Vert A\Vert_{-1}$ is propositional truncation; $\Vert A\Vert_0$ is set truncation); **pushouts, cofibers, and set-quotients**, the latter computing on representatives in Cubical Agda [5]; and the **interval** as a HIT, used to *derive* function extensionality even before cubical methods [4].

HITs are the engine of **synthetic homotopy theory**: classical spaces are defined directly as HITs, and their homotopy invariants are computed by type-theoretic induction. The flagship computation is Licata and Shulman's:

> **Theorem ($\pi_1(\mathbb{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$) [4]:** *The loop space $\Omega(\mathbb{S}^1, \mathrm{base}) \equiv (\mathrm{base} =_{\mathbb{S}^1} \mathrm{base})$ is equivalent to the integers. Hence the fundamental group of the circle is $\mathbb{Z}$.*

The proof uses the **encode–decode method**: define a fibration $\mathrm{code} : \mathbb{S}^1 \to \mathcal{U}$ by circle induction with $\mathrm{code}(\mathrm{base}) \equiv \mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathrm{ap}_{\mathrm{code}}(\mathrm{loop}) = \mathrm{ua}(\mathrm{succEquiv})$ (univalence turns the successor equivalence into a path in the universe); then prove $\Pi_{x:\mathbb{S}^1}\, (\mathrm{base} = x) \simeq \mathrm{code}(x)$ by path induction, instantiating at $x \equiv \mathrm{base}$ [4]. The method generalizes to a reusable technique for characterizing path spaces of HITs.

### 4.3 Cubical type theory: paths as maps out of the interval

Cubical type theory internalizes the geometry that the simplicial model keeps in the semantics. The key moves [3, 9]:

- **The interval** $\mathbb{I}$ is a primitive type with endpoints $i0, i1$ (but *not* an inductive type — it has no elimination into arbitrary types, only into fibrant ones via Kan operations). In the De Morgan variant [3] it carries connections and reversal; in the cartesian variant [9] it is kept minimal and these are derived.
- **Paths are functions**: $\mathrm{PathP}\, A\, a_0\, a_1$ is the type of $p : \mathbb{I} \to A$ with $p(i0) \equiv a_0$, $p(i1) \equiv a_1$. Path abstraction $\lambda i \to t$ and application $p\, r$ are *definitional*, so function extensionality for paths is automatic and computation rules hold judgmentally.
- **Composition** fills open boxes: given a "tube" of compatible partial paths and a "cap", `hcomp` produces the missing face and `transp` transports along a line of types. These Kan operations are what make every type *fibrant* — closed under the geometric operations that path induction needs [3].
- **Glue types** implement univalence *computationally*: `ua : A ≃ B → A ≡ B` is defined by gluing, and crucially transport along `ua e` *reduces to applying* `e` [5, 10]:

```agda
ua   : A ≃ B → A ≡ B
ua e i = Glue B λ { (i = i0) → (A , e)
                  ; (i = i1) → (B , idEquiv B) }

uaβ : (e : A ≃ B) (x : A) → transport (ua e) x ≡ e .fst x
uaβ e x = transportRefl (e .fst x)
```

The second clause is the computational miracle: univalence is no longer an axiom that blocks evaluation, but a program whose behavior is specified [5]. Higher inductive types receive the same treatment: Coquand, Huber, and Mörtberg give a schema in which HIT constructors may take interval arguments (e.g. `loop : base ≡ base` becomes a constructor indexed by $i : \mathbb{I}$ with boundary conditions), and the Kan operations are defined by recursion on constructors [6]. In Cubical Agda, these operations are decomposed into homogeneous composition plus generalized transport, so HITs can be represented directly [5].

### 4.4 h-Levels: propositions, sets, and the logic of spaces

Not every type is equally "homotopical". Voevodsky's **h-levels** stratify types by the triviality of their higher path structure [1, 2]:

| h-level | Name | Characterization | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| $-2$ | Contractible | $\Sigma_{c:A}\, \Pi_{x:A}\, c = x$ | $\mathbf{1}$ |
| $-1$ | Mere proposition | $\Pi_{x,y:A}\, x = y$ | $n \le m$, $\|A\|_{-1}$ |
| $0$ | Set | $\Pi_{x,y:A}\, \mathrm{isProp}(x = y)$ | $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{Z}$ |
| $1$ | Groupoid | Path types are sets | $\mathcal{U}_0$ (univalent) |
| $n+1$ | $n$-type | Path types are $n$-types | $\|A\|_n$ |

This hierarchy recovers *logic inside type theory*: mere propositions ($-1$-types) with $\|A\|_{-1}$-truncated connectives give a classical-looking internal logic — disjunction as $\|A + B\|_{-1}$, existential quantification as $\|\Sigma_{x:A} P(x)\|_{-1}$ — while sets ($0$-types) recover ordinary set-level mathematics [1]. Univalence implies that $\mathcal{U}$ itself is *not* a set but a $1$-groupoid — which is precisely why univalence is invisible to set-level mathematics [2].

### 4.5 Synthetic homotopy theory: computing with spaces

With univalence and HITs in place, the HoTT Book reconstructs a remarkable fragment of algebraic topology *synthetically* — spaces are HITs, and proofs proceed by induction rather than point-set topology [1]:

1. **$\pi_1(\mathbb{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$** via encode–decode, as sketched above [4].
2. **The Hopf fibration** $\mathbb{S}^1 \to \mathbb{S}^3 \to \mathbb{S}^2$ and the long exact sequence, yielding $\pi_2(\mathbb{S}^2) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_3(\mathbb{S}^2) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$ [1].
3. **Brunerie's number**: in his 2016 thesis, Guillaume Brunerie computed $\pi_4(\mathbb{S}^3) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/n$ synthetically, pinning down $n = 2$ — a result whose classical proof spans deep 20th-century topology, here carried out by HIT induction, the James construction, and Whitehead products [8], and additionally verified in the `cubicaltt` prototype.
4. **Blakers–Massey, Freudenthal suspension, van Kampen**: the higher van Kampen theorem and the Blakers–Massey connectivity theorem have fully synthetic proofs, with encode–decode as the recurring engine [1].

## 5. Empirical Results and Benchmarks (or Proofs)

Our "empirical" evidence takes the form of *mechanized* results — machine-checked theorems, and canonicity theorems guaranteeing that closed programs compute:

> **Theorem (Homotopy canonicity, Cavallo–Harper [7]):** *In cubical computational type theory with the De Morgan interval, every closed term of boolean type is path-equal to `true` or `false`. The proof uses a sconing (gluing) logical-relations model over cubical sets.*

> **Theorem (Strict canonicity [7, §6]):** *With composition (rather than filling) as primitive and the computation rules of CCHM [3], every closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ is judgmentally equal to a numeral. The argument builds a computability model with a strict projection, avoiding any reduction relation — a notable simplification given that cubical reduction is not closed under name substitution.*

> **Theorem (Brunerie's computation [8]):** *There exists $n$ with $\pi_4(\mathbb{S}^3) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/n$, and $n = 2$. The proof is fully synthetic (James construction, Blakers–Massey, smash products) and was additionally executed in the cubicaltt prototype.*

These are complemented by implementation milestones in **Cubical Agda** [5, 10]: univalence, function extensionality, and set quotients are *definable with computational content* — `transport (ua e)` reduces; the `agda/cubical` library mechanizes synthetic homotopy theory (the circle's loop space, the Hopf fibration) under the cubical primitives [5]; and cubical paths give *bisimilarity = equality* for coinductive types, an extensionality principle unavailable in vanilla Agda [5].

| System | Univalence | HITs | Canonicity | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MLTT + axiomatic UA/HITs | Postulated, blocks computation | Postulated schemas | Lost (stuck on axioms) | Coq-HoTT, HoTT-Agda (axiomatic mode) |
| CCHM cubical (De Morgan) [3] | Theorem via Glue, computes | Schema with interval constructors [6] | Homotopy canonicity [7]; strict with comp | `cubicaltt` prototype |
| Cartesian cubical [9] | Theorem, minimal interval | Schema, uniform indexed HITs | Under development | RedPRL / cooltt family |
| Cubical Agda [5] | Theorem, `ua`/`uaβ` compute | Native HITs incl. indexed families | Computational in practice | Agda ≥ 2.6.0, `agda/cubical` library |

## 6. Limitations

An honest survey must record where the program strains:

1. **Semisimplicial types.** The long-standing open problem: defining the type of $n$-truncated semisimplicial types for variable $n$ remains elusive in HoTT, because the infinite tower of coherence data resists internalization. This blocks a fully internal theory of $(\infty,1)$-categories.
2. **Indexed and higher inductive-inductive types.** While schemas exist [6], the general theory in cubical settings is still being consolidated; Cubical Agda supports many but not all cases, and `comp` remains stuck for ordinary inductive families in some configurations [5].
3. **Cubical bureaucracy.** Cubical reasoning replaces stuck axioms with explicit Kan operations, but at the cost of new proof obligations: every construction must be shown fibrant, and `hcomp`/`transp` terms can bloat proof terms and slow typechecking — "cube hell" replacing "setoid hell", albeit a principled one.
4. **Model-theoretic gaps.** The precise relationship between the cubical-sets model structure and the classical simplicial-sets model structure is still being clarified [7]; a complete normalization proof for full cartesian cubical type theory with universes remains active research.
5. **Performance and resizing.** Heavy transport and HIT pattern matching can make Cubical Agda significantly slower than vanilla Agda; and propositional resizing, independent of univalence, fails in some cubical models, limiting impredicative-style reasoning.

None of these undermines the core achievement — a *computational* univalent foundation — but they mark the frontier.

## 7. Conclusion

Homotopy type theory began as a semantic observation — types behave like spaces — and became, via Voevodsky's univalence axiom, a new foundation in which *equivalent structures are identical* and equivalence-respecting reasoning is automatic. Higher inductive types turned that foundation into a synthetic laboratory for homotopy theory, recovering deep classical theorems ($\pi_1(\mathbb{S}^1) \simeq \mathbb{Z}$, the Hopf fibration, $\pi_4(\mathbb{S}^3) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2$) by induction principles rather than point-set topology. And cubical type theory closed the loop back to computation: the interval, composition, and Glue types promote univalence from a computation-blocking axiom to a *program*, with canonicity theorems and the Cubical Agda implementation to show for it.

The trajectory from axiom to algorithm is the field's central narrative. Where set-theoretic foundations ask mathematics to encode itself in membership chains, univalent foundations ask type theory to *be* the mathematics — and cubical methods ensure that being remains executable. Open problems (semisimplicial types, full normalization, performance) notwithstanding, the program has delivered what Voevodsky sought: a foundation in which formalization follows mathematical practice, identifying isomorphic structures as a matter of course, while retaining the constructive character that makes proof assistants programming languages as well as logics.
