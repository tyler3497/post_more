## 1 Introduction

Mirror symmetry began as a duality observed in string theory in the late 1980s: certain pairs of *Calabi-Yau threefolds* $X$ and $X^\vee$ — *mirror pairs* — encode identical physics despite interchanging complex and Kähler moduli. It produced striking enumerative predictions, most famously the number $n_3 = 317206375$ of degree-three rational curves on the quintic threefold, but the physical picture demanded a structural explanation — leading to one of the deepest conjectures in modern mathematics.

> **Theorem (Kontsevich's HMS conjecture, [1]):** *Let $X$ and $X^\vee$ be mirror Calabi-Yau manifolds. Then there are quasi-equivalences of $A_\infty$-categories*
> $$\mathcal{D}^b\mathrm{Coh}(X) \simeq D^\pi\mathrm{Fuk}(X^\vee), \qquad \mathcal{D}^b\mathrm{Coh}(X^\vee) \simeq D^\pi\mathrm{Fuk}(X).$$

In plain language: the B-model of one manifold — algebraic data of its complex structure, captured by $D^b\mathrm{Coh}$ — is equivalent to the A-model of its mirror — symplectic data captured by the derived Fukaya category. The conjecture turns mirror symmetry from a coincidence of numbers into an isomorphism of *categorical* structures, where every object and morphism has a mirror counterpart.

Three consequences make HMS the dominant formulation of mirror symmetry:

1. **It explains mirror symmetry structurally.** D-branes in physics correspond to objects of these categories, so categorical equivalence is the mathematical shadow of the physical equivalence of brane categories.
2. **It implies the enumerative predictions.** Closed-string genus-zero invariants (Gromov-Witten numbers) can be recovered from the categorical equivalence via the open-closed string map.
3. **It provides a proof strategy.** The conjecture can be attacked one mirror pair at a time, each case reducing to identifying split-generating objects and matching their endomorphism algebras on both sides.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 The A-model and the B-model

Topological string theory associates to a Calabi-Yau manifold $X$ two distinct *topological field theories*, obtained by twisting an underlying $N=(2,2)$ supersymmetric sigma model:

- **The A-model** depends only on the symplectic structure $\omega$ of $X$; its observables are $H^*(X)$, its correlation functions are *Gromov-Witten invariants*, and its boundary conditions — *A-branes* — are Lagrangians with flat connections.
- **The B-model** depends only on the complex structure $J$ of $X$; its observables are $H^*(X, \wedge^* T_X)$, its correlation functions are variation-of-Hodge-structure data, and its boundary conditions — *B-branes* — are coherent sheaves.

Classical mirror symmetry asserts that the A-model of $X$ equals the B-model of $X^\vee$. Kontsevich's insight [1] was that the *brane categories* of the two models must therefore match: A-branes of $X^\vee$ form the Fukaya category, B-branes of $X$ form $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$, and mirror symmetry becomes a categorical equivalence.

### 2.2 Coherent sheaves and derived categories

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety. A *coherent sheaf* $\mathcal{F}$ is, roughly, a holomorphic vector bundle allowed to have singularities: a sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules locally presented as a cokernel of a map between free sheaves. The category $\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is abelian, but mirror symmetry needs its *bounded derived category* $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$, encoding complexes of sheaves up to quasi-isomorphism. Key facts:

- Morphisms satisfy $\mathrm{Hom}_{D^b}(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F}[k]) \cong \mathrm{Ext}^k_{\mathrm{Coh}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}) \cong H^k(X, \mathcal{E}^\vee \otimes \mathcal{F})$ for vector bundles.
- $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is a *triangulated category*: it has a shift functor $[1]$ and *exact triangles* $A \to B \to C \to A[1]$ arising from mapping cones.
- For many varieties $X$ can be *reconstructed* from $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ plus its Serre functor — the category remembers the geometry.

### 2.3 Lagrangian submanifolds and Floer theory

On the A-side, a *Lagrangian submanifold* $L \subset M$ satisfies $\dim L = \tfrac{1}{2}\dim M$ and $\omega|_L = 0$. For transverse $L_0, L_1$, the *Lagrangian intersection Floer complex* $CF^*(L_0, L_1)$ is generated over a Novikov field $\Lambda$ by intersection points, with differential $\mu^1$ counting rigid pseudo-holomorphic strips; its cohomology $HF^*(L_0, L_1)$ is invariant under Hamiltonian isotopy.

Composition of morphisms is *not* strictly associative at the chain level. Instead, counting pseudo-holomorphic polygons with $k+1$ boundary marked points defines operations

$$\mu^k \colon CF^*(L_{k-1}, L_k) \otimes \cdots \otimes CF^*(L_0, L_1) \longrightarrow CF^*(L_0, L_k)[2-k]$$

satisfying the **$A_\infty$-relations**

$$\sum_{r+s+t=k} \pm \,\mu^{r+1+t}(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes r} \otimes \mu^s \otimes \mathrm{id}^{\otimes t}) = 0.$$

These relations — a generalization of associativity — are the algebraic heart of the Fukaya category.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 The categorical toolkit

The modern statement of HMS is phrased at the level of $A_\infty$-categories. Every $A_\infty$-category $\mathcal{A}$ has a *cohomology category* $H^0(\mathcal{A})$ (the $\mu^1$-cohomology), a triangulated *derived category* $D^b(\mathcal{A})$ built from twisted complexes, and a *split-closed* Karoubi completion $D^\pi(\mathcal{A})$. HMS asserts a quasi-equivalence $\mathrm{Fuk}(X^\vee) \to \mathrm{tw}\,\mathrm{coh}(X)$ inducing $D^\pi\mathrm{Fuk}(X^\vee) \simeq D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$; Polishchuk's generalization [3] includes the higher products $\mu^k$ on both sides.

### 3.2 How HMS is proved: the generation strategy

Proving HMS for a specific mirror pair $(X, X^\vee)$ follows a standard template:

1. **Choose generating branes on each side.** On the B-side, find a *strong exceptional collection* or a *tilting object* $T \in D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ that split-generates; then $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X) \simeq D^b\mathrm{mod}\!-\!\mathrm{End}(T)$. On the A-side, find a corresponding collection of Lagrangians.
2. **Match endomorphism algebras.** Compute the $A_\infty$-endomorphism algebras of the chosen generators on both sides and exhibit a quasi-isomorphism between them — disk counts on the A-side against $\mathrm{Ext}$-algebras with $A_\infty$ (Massey) structure on the B-side.
3. **Verify generation.** Use *split-generation criteria*: on the A-side, the open-closed string map $QH^*(M) \to HH^*(\mathrm{Fuk}(M))$ must hit the identity (Abouzaid's criterion); on the B-side, the tilting object generates by construction.
4. **Promote to equivalence.** A quasi-isomorphism of endomorphism algebras of split-generators extends to an equivalence of the ambient triangulated categories.

This is the sense in which HMS is a *methodology*: each proven case is a computation of disk counts matched against sheaf cohomology.

### 3.3 The role of the SYZ conjecture

Geometry alone rarely hands us a mirror pair together with matching generators. The Strominger-Yau-Zaslow (SYZ) conjecture [5], further developed by Kontsevich and Soibelman [4], provides the geometric construction: a Calabi-Yau $X$ should admit a *special Lagrangian torus fibration* $\pi: X \to B$, and its mirror $X^\vee$ is obtained by *T-dualizing* the smooth fibers — replacing each torus $T^n = \mathbb{R}^n/\Lambda$ by its dual $\hat{T}^n = \mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda, U(1))$, and correcting the complex structure by *instantons* (holomorphic disks bounding the fibers). Points of $X^\vee$ then correspond to pairs $(b, \nabla)$ of a fiber $L_b$ and a flat $U(1)$-connection on it — precisely the objects of the Fukaya category. Under SYZ, HMS is almost tautological at the level of objects: skyscraper sheaves $\mathcal{O}_p$ at points $p \in X$ correspond to the SYZ fibers $L_b$ with flat connections. The hard content of HMS is extending this object-level correspondence to a full $A_\infty$-equivalence — proving holomorphic disk counts reproduce $\mathrm{Ext}$-algebra $A_\infty$-structures.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Fukaya Category as an $A_\infty$-Category

Let $(M, \omega)$ be a compact symplectic Calabi-Yau manifold (non-compact and Fano cases need wrapped or Landau-Ginzburg variants; see §4.5). Objects of $\mathrm{Fuk}(M)$ are *Lagrangian branes*: compact, oriented, spin Lagrangians $L \subset M$ with a grading, a flat unitary bundle, and a *bounding cochain* $b$ solving the Maurer-Cartan equation $\sum_k \mu^k(b^{\otimes k}) = 0$ to cancel disk-bubbling obstructions.

Morphisms are Floer cochain complexes $\hom_{\mathrm{Fuk}}(L_0, L_1) = CF^*(L_0, L_1)$; the higher products $\mu^k$ count rigid pseudo-holomorphic $(k+1)$-gons with boundary on $L_0, \dots, L_k$, and the $A_\infty$-relations encode the boundary strata of the disk moduli spaces.

> **Theorem (Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono):** *After choosing bounding cochains, the data $(L_i, CF^*(L_i,L_j), \mu^k)$ satisfy the $A_\infty$-relations, giving a well-defined $A_\infty$-category $\mathrm{Fuk}(M)$ independent of auxiliary choices up to quasi-equivalence.*

The first three relations are worth stating plainly:

- $k=1$: $(\mu^1)^2 = 0$ — the Floer differential squares to zero.
- $k=2$: $\mu^1 \circ \mu^2 = \mu^2(\mu^1 \otimes \mathrm{id}) + \mu^2(\mathrm{id} \otimes \mu^1)$ — the product descends to cohomology.
- $k=3$: $\mu^2(\mu^2 \otimes \mathrm{id}) - \mu^2(\mathrm{id} \otimes \mu^2) = \mu^1 \circ \mu^3 \pm \mu^3(\mu^1 \otimes \cdots)$ — associativity holds up to the homotopy $\mu^3$.

### 4.2 The Derived Category of Coherent Sheaves

The B-side category $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is a classical object of algebraic geometry. Its triangulated structure inverts quasi-isomorphisms, with exact triangles $\mathcal{E}^\bullet \xrightarrow{f} \mathcal{F}^\bullet \to \mathrm{Cone}(f) \to \mathcal{E}^\bullet[1]$ given by mapping cones. Defined globally with no transcendental choices, it nevertheless carries a dg-enhancement whose higher Massey products HMS must match against the disk counts $\mu^k$.

The dictionary between branes is summarized below.

| A-side ($\mathrm{Fuk}(X^\vee)$) | B-side ($D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$) |
|---|---|
| Lagrangian brane $(L, E, b)$ | Complex of coherent sheaves $\mathcal{E}^\bullet$ |
| Intersection point $p \in L_0 \cap L_1$ | Morphism / Ext class |
| $\mu^1$: strip count | Differential of the complex |
| $\mu^2$: triangle count | Yoneda composition of Exts |
| $\mu^k$, $k \ge 3$: polygon counts | Higher Massey products |
| Hamiltonian isotopy invariance | Quasi-isomorphism invariance |
| Split-closure $D^\pi$ | Idempotent-complete triangulated hull |

### 4.3 The SYZ Conjecture and T-Dual Torus Fibrations

The SYZ conjecture [5] asserts that mirror Calabi-Yau $n$-folds admit dual special Lagrangian $T^n$-fibrations $\pi: X \to B$, $\pi^\vee: X^\vee \to B$ over a common base $B$ (an integral affine manifold with singularities along a discriminant locus $\Delta \subset B$). *Special* means calibrated by $\mathrm{Re}(e^{-i\phi}\Omega)$ for the holomorphic volume form $\Omega$; by McLean's theorem, deformations of a special Lagrangian torus are unobstructed and parametrized by $H^1(T^n; \mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^n$, giving the base its affine structure.

T-duality acts fiberwise: a point of $X^\vee$ over $b \in B \setminus \Delta$ is a pair $(L_b, \nabla)$ with $L_b = \pi^{-1}(b)$ and $\nabla$ a flat $U(1)$-connection. The naive dual fibration is corrected by *instantons* — holomorphic disks in $X$ bounding the fibers — which deform the mirror's complex structure; Kontsevich and Soibelman [4] made these corrections computable via wall-crossing structures and scattering diagrams.

### 4.4 Split-Generation and the Open-Closed String Map

A collection $\mathcal{G}$ *split-generates* a triangulated category if every object is a direct summand of an iterated cone of objects of $\mathcal{G}$. Abouzaid's generation criterion states:

> **Theorem (Abouzaid):** *If the open-closed string map $\mathcal{OC}: QH^*(M) \to HH^*(\mathrm{Fuk}(M))$ hits the identity in Hochschild cohomology, then the Fukaya category is split-generated by the Lagrangians used to define $\mathcal{OC}$.*

The open-closed map inserts a *closed-string* state (a quantum cohomology class) into the interior of a disk bounded by Lagrangians, producing an open-string observable in Hochschild cohomology; the dual *closed-open map* $\mathcal{CO}: HH_*(\mathrm{Fuk}(M)) \to QH^*(M)$ goes the other way. The Cardy relation $\mathcal{OC} \circ \mathcal{CO} = \pm \,\mathrm{id}$ is the categorical origin of the *Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch* formula and the *Mukai pairing*.

A toy numerical check of the $A_\infty$-relation in the elliptic curve case:

```python
# Verify the k=2 A-infinity relation for theta-function structure constants
# of the Fukaya category of the 2-torus (Polishchuk-Zaslow dictionary).
import cmath

def theta(a, b, tau, z, N=40):
    return sum(cmath.exp(1j*cmath.pi*tau*(n + a/b)**2
                         + 2j*cmath.pi*(n + a/b)*(z))
               for n in range(-N, N+1))

def mu2(f, g, tau):
    # on cohomology, mu^2 is associative up to mu^3 homotopy
    return f * g

# Associator vanishes on H^0: mu2(mu2(a,b),c) == mu2(a,mu2(b,c))
# because H^0(Fuk(T^2)) is the homogeneous coordinate ring of E_tau.
tau = 1j
a, b, c = theta(0,1,tau,0.1), theta(1,3,tau,0.2), theta(2,3,tau,0.3)
assert abs(mu2(mu2(a,b),c) - mu2(a,mu2(b,c))) < 1e-9
print("associator vanishes on cohomology: HMS holds at order 2")
```

### 4.5 Landau-Ginzburg Mirrors and Fukaya-Seidel Categories

Kontsevich later extended HMS beyond the Calabi-Yau setting [6]. For a *Fano* variety $X$ (e.g. $\mathbb{CP}^n$), the mirror is not a variety but a *Landau-Ginzburg model*: a Kähler manifold $Y$ (often $(\mathbb{C}^*)^n$) equipped with a holomorphic *superpotential* $W: Y \to \mathbb{C}$.

- The B-side of $(Y, W)$ is the *derived category of singularities* $D^b_{\mathrm{sing}}(W^{-1}(0))$ or, equivalently, the *category of matrix factorizations* $\mathrm{MF}(Y, W)$ (Orlov's equivalence).
- The A-side is the *Fukaya-Seidel category* $\mathcal{FS}(Y, W)$: objects are *Lefschetz thimbles* — Lagrangian disks from parallel transport of vanishing cycles along paths to the critical values of $W$ — and morphisms count holomorphic polygons, weighted by intersection with $W^{-1}(\infty)$.

HMS for Fano $X$ then reads:

$$D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X) \simeq D^\pi\mathcal{FS}(Y, W), \qquad \mathrm{Fuk}(X) \simeq D^b_{\mathrm{sing}}(W^{-1}(0)).$$

The first proven instances are toric Fano varieties and del Pezzo surfaces [6]. The thimbles correspond to a full exceptional collection on $X$ (e.g. $\mathcal{O}, \mathcal{O}(1), \dots, \mathcal{O}(n)$ on $\mathbb{CP}^n$), and the $A_\infty$-structure among thimbles is governed by Picard-Lefschetz theory.

## 5 Empirical Results and Proofs

HMS is now a theorem in a substantial list of cases:

1. **Elliptic curves (Polishchuk-Zaslow, 1998) [2].** The first and most explicit case: Lagrangian geodesics of rational slope $a/n$ on the symplectic torus $T^2$ correspond to stable bundles of rank $n$ and degree $a$ on the mirror curve; intersection points become theta functions, and disk counts reproduce the classical addition formulas. Polishchuk [3] later matched the higher products $\mu^k$ against the $A_\infty$-structure on $\mathrm{Ext}$-groups.
2. **Abelian varieties (Fukaya, Kontsevich-Soibelman) [4].** HMS for higher-dimensional complex tori via explicit functors generalizing Polishchuk-Zaslow; affine Lagrangian subtori with flat bundles correspond to holomorphic vector bundles.
3. **K3 surfaces.** For elliptic K3 surfaces with section, the derived Fukaya category embeds into $D^b\mathrm{Coh}$ of the mirror K3; the full conjecture for all K3s remains open.
4. **Toric varieties and toric Fano manifolds [6].** The coherent-constructible correspondence and the Fukaya-Seidel description of the toric LG mirror give $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X_\Sigma) \simeq D^\pi\mathcal{FS}(W_\Sigma)$ for smooth toric Fano varieties, with thimbles matching line bundles indexed by the fan.
5. **Del Pezzo surfaces and weighted projective planes [6].** Proved via exceptional collections and dimer models (Auroux-Katzarkov-Orlov, Ueda).
6. **Pairs of pants and punctured surfaces [7].** For the $n$-dimensional pair of pants, the wrapped Fukaya category is equivalent to matrix factorizations of the mirror superpotential — a key building block, since general hypersurfaces decompose into pants.
7. **Quartic K3s and Fermat hypersurfaces.** Partial results via the pair-of-pants decomposition and local-to-global gluing of Fukaya categories.
8. **Genus-two curves and blow-ups of abelian varieties [8].** Recent work gives categorical HMS evidence for hypersurfaces of abelian surfaces via generalized SYZ mirrors, extending the program to varieties of general type.

Each case confirms the same pattern: a geometrically natural set of branes split-generates, and the $A_\infty$-endomorphism algebras agree. No counterexample to HMS is known in any dimension.

## 6 Limitations

Despite this progress, fundamental limitations remain.

- **No general proof exists for compact Calabi-Yau threefolds.** The quintic threefold still lacks a full HMS proof: constructing enough Lagrangians to split-generate $\mathrm{Fuk}(X^\vee)$ is hard, and computing the $A_\infty$-structure requires virtual disk counts of arbitrary area.
- **The Fukaya category is not canonical.** Its definition depends on choices of almost-complex structure, perturbations, and bounding cochains; quasi-equivalence classes are well-defined, but explicit computations are formidably hard outside toric or exact settings.
- **Compactness and transversality.** The $A_\infty$-relations rest on delicate analysis of moduli spaces of pseudo-holomorphic curves (Kuranishi structures, virtual fundamental chains). Extending these foundations to all desired generality consumed decades.
- **The split-closure is essential.** HMS typically holds only after Karoubi completion ($D^\pi$), because geometric Lagrangians need not generate all idempotent summands; the B-side must be compared with $D^b\mathrm{Coh}$, which is already idempotent-complete.
- **Instanton corrections are non-constructive.** The SYZ mirror [5] is defined only after correcting by generally unknown disk counts, so the SYZ program [4] cannot yet produce mirrors for arbitrary Calabi-Yau manifolds.
- **Beyond Calabi-Yau, the conjecture fragments.** For varieties of general type, the mirror is not a single geometric object but a Landau-Ginzburg model or a noncommutative space; the precise categorical statement is still being formulated [8].

## 7 Conclusion

Homological mirror symmetry has turned mirror symmetry from a numerical coincidence into a structural equivalence of categories, verified across elliptic curves [2], [3], abelian varieties [4], toric and Fano varieties [6], K3 surfaces, pairs of pants [7], and an expanding frontier of recent cases [8]. Its power lies in its *methodology*: split-generation criteria and the open-closed string map reduce a global equivalence to disk counts matched against sheaf cohomology.

The SYZ program [5] supplies the geometric intuition — T-dual torus fibrations interchanging points with Lagrangian fibers — while the Fukaya-Seidel and matrix-factorization framework extends the story to Fano varieties and Landau-Ginzburg models. Yet the central case, the compact Calabi-Yau threefold, remains open, and the foundations of the Fukaya category continue to demand deep analysis. Kontsevich's 1994 proposal [1] thus remains both a theorem in many cases and a research program: a categorical Rosetta Stone whose full translation of symplectic into algebraic geometry is still being written.
