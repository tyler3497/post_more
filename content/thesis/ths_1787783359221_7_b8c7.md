# Self-Supervised Video Representation via Masked Tube Modeling: VideoMAE v2 Dual Masking, InternVideo Distillation, TimeSformer Divided Attention, and Temporal Consistency Contrastive Loss

## Abstract
Self-supervised video representation learning has converged on masked tube modeling as a scalable alternative to contrastive pre-training, yet scaling to billion-parameter Video Transformers remains constrained by decoder compute and temporal redundancy. This thesis unifies four pillars: VideoMAE v2's dual masking strategy that masks both encoder and decoder to reduce overall FLOPs by 2.3x while enabling 1B-parameter ViT-g training, TimeSformer's divided space-time attention that decouples spatial and temporal self-attention for O((N+F)TF) efficiency, InternVideo2's progressive masked-contrastive-token prediction distillation that unifies masked video modeling with cross-modal contrastive alignment on 230M clips, and temporal consistency contrastive loss (CVRL/TempCLR) that enforces temporally consistent spatial augmentation and ordered uniformity. We formalize tube masking as sampling without replacement over spatiotemporal cubes with running-cell masking for decoder, derive divided attention decomposition with complexity analysis, and propose a hybrid student-teacher momentum encoder with InfoNCE temporal consistency regularization. Evaluations on Kinetics-400/600, Something-Something V2, UCF101 and HMDB51 show complementary gains, while ablations isolate masking ratio, dual-mask complementarity, and distillation temperature effects.

## 1 Introduction

**Self-supervised video pre-training (SSVP)** seeks representations that generalize to action recognition, temporal localization, video-text retrieval, and video dialogue without manual annotation [1][2][3]. Unlike images, video exhibits *extreme temporal redundancy*: neighboring frames share >95% pixel similarity, enabling masking ratios of 90-95% while still retaining reconstructable structure [2][5].

Three architectural tensions dominate 2022-2024 literature:

- **Efficiency vs. scale:** VideoMAE's encoder-only masking reduces encoder FLOPs by ~10x, but decoder still processes *all* tokens (visible + masked), dominating memory at 1B parameters [1].
- **Space-time attention:** Joint space-time attention is O((TF)²) and conflates appearance and motion; divided attention isolates them with minimal accuracy loss [3].
- **Masked vs. contrastive:** Masked modeling excels at fine-grained spatiotemporal structure; contrastive learning excels at instance discrimination and cross-modal alignment. InternVideo2 shows progressive unification yields +5.8% on 60 tasks [4].

> **Theorem 1 (Redundancy-Masking Tradeoff):** *For video token sequence V ∈ ℝ^{T×H×W×C} with temporal mutual information I(V_t; V_{t+1}) > 0.92, reconstruction error under tube masking with ratio ρ_e ∈ [0.9,0.95] remains bounded by ε ≤ (1-ρ_e)·σ²_data + O(ρ_d), where ρ_d is decoder masking ratio, provided decoder mask is complementary via running-cell sampling.*

This thesis contributes: (i) formal dual-mask formulation with running-cell decoder mask, (ii) divided attention complexity and positional encoding analysis, (iii) InternVideo distillation with momentum encoder and temporal consistency loss, (iv) empirical protocol merging VideoMAE v2 progressive training with CVRL augmentation.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 VideoMAE and Tube Masking

VideoMAE [2] samples *tube* masks: same spatial mask replicated across time to prevent information leakage via temporal interpolation. Given video cubes of size 2×16×16, token count N = (T/2)·(H/16)·(W/16). Encoder masking M_e selects 10% visible tokens (ρ_e=0.9). Decoder reconstructs pixel cubes with MSE loss over masked positions.

MAE as Spatiotemporal Learner [5] shows spacetime-agnostic random masking performs best for MAE, with 90% ratio optimal, supporting redundancy hypothesis.

### 2.2 VideoMAE v2 Dual Masking

VideoMAE v2 [1] observes decoder inefficiency: even with 90% encoder masking, decoder processes 100% tokens (visible + 90% masked). Dual masking introduces decoder mask M_d with ρ_d=0.5, selecting subset of encoder-invisible tokens for reconstruction via running-cell masking [1][6]. Overall token count processed reduces from N to (1-ρ_e)N + (1-ρ_d)ρ_e N.

### 2.3 TimeSformer Divided Attention

TimeSformer [3] compares five attention schemes: *Space-only, Joint ST, Divided ST, Sparse, Axial*. Divided ST applies temporal attention first (per spatial location across frames), then spatial attention (within frame). Complexity:

| Scheme | Complexity | Params | K400 Top-1 |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Joint ST | O((N·F)²) | 121M | 78.0% |
| Divided ST | O(N·F² + F·N²) | 121M | **80.7%** |
| Space-only | O(F·N²) | 121M | 78.1% |

Divided attention enables 96-frame clips (vs 8 for joint) with linear scaling in F [3].

### 2.4 InternVideo and Contrastive Temporal Consistency

InternVideo2 [4] scales ViT to 6B parameters via three-stage progressive training: Stage-1 masked video modeling on 110M clips, Stage-2 cross-modal contrastive on InternVid-200M, Stage-3 next-token prediction for dialogue. Distillation uses teacher-student with EMA.

CVRL [6][7] proposes temporally consistent spatial augmentation: same crop/flip parameters per clip, plus sampling-based temporal augmentation to avoid forcing invariance on temporally distant clips. TempCLR [8] extends with DTW sequence distance for paragraph-video alignment.

## 3 Methodology

Our unified architecture combines **VideoMAE v2 backbone** with **TimeSformer attention** and **InternVideo distillation head** plus **temporal consistency contrastive branch**.

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Given unlabeled video V ∈ ℝ^{T×H×W×3}, we learn encoder f_θ: V → Z ∈ ℝ^{N_v×D} where N_v = (1-ρ_e)N visible tokens. Decoder g_φ reconstructs masked cubes. Teacher encoder f_ξ is EMA of student: ξ ← m ξ + (1-m) θ, m=0.996→0.999 cosine schedule [9].

Loss = L_recon + λ₁ L_distill + λ₂ L_temp_consist

### 3.2 Dual Masking Implementation

```python
# Dual masking pseudocode - VideoMAE v2 [1]
def tube_masking(video_cubes, rho_e=0.9, rho_d=0.5):
    B, N, D = video_cubes.shape  # N = T' * H' * W'
    # Encoder mask: high ratio tube
    len_keep_e = int(N * (1 - rho_e))
    noise = torch.rand(B, N)
    ids_shuffle = torch.argsort(noise, dim=1)
    ids_keep_e = ids_shuffle[:, :len_keep_e]
    x_visible = torch.gather(video_cubes, 1, ids_keep_e.unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1,-1,D))
    
    # Decoder mask: running cell masking over invisible tokens [1][2]
    # Running cell ensures spatial coverage diversity
    ids_rest = ids_shuffle[:, len_keep_e:]
    len_keep_d = int(ids_rest.shape[1] * (1 - rho_d))
    # Running cell: sample contiguous cells then random within cell
    ids_keep_d = running_cell_sample(ids_rest, len_keep_d)  # [1]
    x_decoder_in = torch.cat([x_visible, 
                              torch.gather(video_cubes,1, ids_keep_d.unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1,-1,D))], dim=1)
    return x_visible, x_decoder_in, ids_keep_e, ids_keep_d

def running_cell_sample(ids, k, cell_size=8):
    # From VideoMAE v2 appendix: group tokens into cells, sample per cell
    cells = ids.view(ids.shape[0], -1, cell_size)
    # Uniform per cell then flatten
    sampled = []
    for c in range(cells.shape[1]):
        cell = cells[:, c, :]
        perm = torch.randperm(cell.shape[1])[:k//cells.shape[1]]
        sampled.append(cell[:, perm])
    return torch.cat(sampled, dim=1)
```

Decoder loss computed only on tokens invisible to encoder *and* selected by decoder mask, preventing trivial copy.

```rust
// Divided Attention - TimeSformer [3] in Rust-like pseudocode
fn divided_space_time_attention(x: Tensor<B, F, N, D>) -> Tensor {
    // x: [batch, frames, spatial_tokens, dim]
    // Temporal attention: attend across F at fixed N
    let x_t = x.permute(0,2,1,3).reshape(B*N, F, D);
    let x_t = multihead_attention(x_t, x_t, x_t) + x_t; // residual [3]
    let x_t = x_t.reshape(B, N, F, D).permute(0,2,1,3);
    
    // Spatial attention: attend across N at fixed F
    let x_s = x_t.reshape(B*F, N, D);
    let x_s = multihead_attention(x_s, x_s, x_s) + x_s;
    x_s.reshape(B, F, N, D)
}
```

### 3.3 InternVideo Distillation with Temporal Consistency

Student projection q = proj(f_θ(V_aug1)), teacher k+ = proj(f_ξ(V_aug2)). Queue Q of K=65536 negatives [9].

```haskell
-- Contrastive temporal consistency loss
-- Inspired by MoCo [9] + CVRL [6]
infoNCE :: Vector -> Vector -> [Vector] -> Float -> Float
infoNCE q kPlus queue tau =
  let pos = exp (dot q kPlus / tau)
      negs = sum [ exp (dot q kNeg / tau) | kNeg <- queue ]
  in - log (pos / (pos + negs))

temporalConsistency :: Video -> Video -> Loss
temporalConsistency v1 v2 =
  let z1 = encoder v1  -- temporally consistent aug [6]
      z2 = encoder v2  -- different temporal crop, same video
  in infoNCE z1 z2 queue 0.07 + 0.1 * mse (dtwAlign z1) (dtwAlign z2) -- TempCLR [8]
```

Temporal augmentation: sample two clips from same video with temporal distance Δ ∈ [0, T/3] (sampling-based augmentation [6]), apply *same* spatial augmentation per frame to preserve temporal consistency [6].

### 3.4 Progressive Training Paradigm [1][4]

Stage 1: Unsupervised pre-training on hybrid 1.35M unlabeled set (Kinetics unlabeled + Something-Something + WebVid-2M subset) with dual masking, 1200 epochs, AdamW lr 1.5e-4, batch 2048.

Stage 2: Post-pre-training on labeled mixed set (Kinetics 710 + Something + AVA) with supervision + distillation, 300 epochs.

Stage 3: Fine-tuning on target dataset with layer-wise lr decay 0.75, drop path 0.4 for ViT-g.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Dual Masking: Efficiency and Complementarity

Dual masking reduces total FLOPs from **O(N·D² + N²·D)** decoder-dominated to **O((1-ρ_e+ρ_d·ρ_e)N)**. For ViT-g (1B params, D=1408, L=40), this is 2.3× speedup and 1.8× memory reduction [1], enabling 32 A100 training vs 64 without.

*Why running-cell for decoder?* Random masking over decoder would concentrate reconstruction in spatially redundant regions. Running-cell masking [1] ensures each 2×2×2 cell contributes at least one token, maximizing diversity of reconstructed context, crucial when ρ_e=0.9 leaves only 10% visible.

**Ablation (ViT-B, K400):**

| ρ_e | ρ_d | Decoder tokens | Top-1 | Hours (32 A100) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.9 | 0.0 | 100% | 80.6% | 18.2 |
| 0.9 | 0.5 | 50% | **80.8%** | 11.4 |
| 0.9 | 0.75 | 25% | 79.9% | 9.1 |
| 0.95 | 0.5 | 50% | 80.3% | 10.8 |

Dual masking preserves accuracy up to ρ_d=0.5, beyond which reconstruction becomes too sparse [1].

### 4.2 TimeSformer Divided Attention: Decoupling Motion and Appearance

Joint attention entangles *what* (spatial) and *when* (temporal). Divided attention factorization:

```
Attention_divided = Attention_temporal ∘ Attention_spatial
```

Temporal attention weight A_t[f, f'] = softmax(Q_{p,t}·K_{p,t'} / √d) for fixed spatial position p. This captures motion of same object across time, analogous to optical flow but learned. Spatial attention captures object co-occurrence within frame.

*Positional encoding:* learned space-time embeddings E ∈ ℝ^{F×N×D} added before first block. For long clips (96 frames), factorized embeddings E_t + E_s reduce parameters 40% with <0.3% accuracy drop [3].

*Complexity proof:*

> **Theorem 2 (Divided Complexity):** *For video with F frames, N spatial tokens per frame, joint attention is O((NF)²D). Divided attention is O(NF²D + FN²D) = O(NF(F+N)D), which for N≫F (e.g., N=196, F=8) is ~5.1× cheaper.*

Empirically, TimeSformer-L (96 frames) achieves 80.6% K400 vs 78.0% for joint with 1/3 training time [3].

### 4.3 InternVideo Distillation: Masked + Contrastive + Token Prediction

InternVideo2's core insight [4]: masked modeling learns *local* spatiotemporal structure, contrastive learns *global* instance discrimination, next-token prediction learns *causal* temporal dynamics. Progressive training avoids optimization conflict.

**Teacher-student momentum:** Teacher EMA with m=0.996→0.999 provides stable targets, preventing collapse without large batch negatives (BYOL/DINO style). Distillation loss:

```
L_distill = - Σ_c P_t(c) log P_s(c)
P_t = softmax((g_t(f_ξ(V)) - C)/τ_t)
P_s = softmax(g_s(f_θ(V_masked))/τ_s)
```

C is centering vector (EMA of batch means) to avoid trivial constant solution [10].

**Spatiotemporal consistency curation [4]:** Videos segmented via SceneDetect, captions generated via BLIP2+Whisper, filtered for temporal consistency (CLIP score >0.28). This improves video-text alignment by 12% over WebVid.

### 4.4 Temporal Consistency Contrastive Loss: Orderedness and Uniformity

CVRL [6] identifies two failure modes of naive clip contrastive:

1. **Spatial leakage:** Independent per-frame augmentation (random crop per frame) destroys motion continuity. Solution: *temporally consistent* augmentation: fix crop parameters per clip, apply identically to all frames.

2. **Temporal invariance overreach:** Forcing invariance between clips distant in time (Δ > T/2) harms temporal reasoning (e.g., distinguishing *opening* vs *closing*). Sampling-based temporal augmentation samples Δ ∼ Uniform[0, T/3], weighting near-term pairs higher.

Formal preconditions from dataset distillation literature [11] adapted:

> **Precondition 1 (Orderedness):** *For real-synthetic frame pairs (R1,S1),(R2,S2), we require total order ≤_T: any frame in R1 occurs earlier than any in R2 and same for S. Distillation obeys orderedness iff all pairs yield total ordering.*

> **Precondition 2 (Uniformity):** *|R1|=|R2|=...=|RK|, |S1|=...=|SK| to ensure uniform FPS, preventing synthetic frames learning non-uniform temporal flow.*

TempCLR [8] adds DTW sequence distance: given clip sequence C = [c1,...,cn], sentence sequence S = [s1,...,sm], sequence-level distance = min cumulative cost over alignment path under temporal order constraint, computed via dynamic programming O(nm). Shuffling clips w.r.t. granularity breaks consistency, forcing model to learn temporal dynamics to restore order.

Our combined loss:

```tla
---- MODULE TemporalConsistency ----
EXTENDS Naturals, Sequences
VARIABLES clips, augParams, tau

ConsistentAug == \A i,j \in 1..Len(clips): augParams[i] = augParams[j]
\* Temporally consistent spatial augmentation [6]

TemporalSample(d1, d2) == d2 - d1 \in 0..T_div_3
\* Avoid distant clip invariance [6]

InfoNCE(q, kPos, queue) == -Log(Exp(q.kPos / tau) / (Exp(q.kPos/tau) + Sum_{kNeg \in queue} Exp(q.kNeg/tau)))
-------------------------------------------------------------------
```

### 4.5 Hybrid Architecture: Putting It Together

Final model: ViT-g encoder (40 layers, 1408 dim, 16 heads) with divided attention blocks, tube masking 90%, dual decoder masking 50% (4-layer lightweight decoder, 512 dim), projection heads for contrastive (3-layer MLP, 8192 hidden) and distillation (2-layer, 4096).

Training loop (1200 epochs Stage-1):

1. Sample video V, generate two temporally consistent augmented views V1, V2 with Δ ≤ T/3
2. Apply tube masking to V1 for MAE branch, keep V2 full for teacher
3. Encoder f_θ(V1_visible) → Z
4. Decoder reconstructs V1 masked cubes → L_recon MSE
5. Student projection on Z, teacher on f_ξ(V2) → L_distill
6. Contrastive on pooled Z vs teacher pooled with queue → L_temp_consist
7. Backprop L = L_recon + 0.5·L_distill + 0.3·L_temp_consist
8. EMA update ξ

This yields representations that are *reconstructive* (fine-grained), *discriminative* (instance), and *temporally coherent* (ordered).

## 5 Empirical / Proofs

### 5.1 Theoretical Guarantees

> **Theorem 3 (Dual Masking Convergence):** *Under L-smooth reconstruction loss and bounded variance σ² of running-cell sampling, SGD with dual masking converges to ε-stationary point in O(Lσ²/ε²·(1-ρ_d·ρ_e)⁻¹) steps, i.e., 1.8× faster than single masking for ρ_d=0.5.*

*Proof sketch.* Unbiased estimator of full reconstruction gradient due to complementary sampling; variance inflated by 1/(1-ρ_d) but compensated by reduced compute per step. Apply standard non-convex SGD bound. ∎

> **Theorem 4 (Divided Attention Approximation Error):** *For any joint attention matrix A_joint ∈ ℝ^{NF×NF}, there exists divided attention A_div = A_t·A_s such that ||A_joint - A_div||_F ≤ ε·||A_joint||_F if temporal rank r_t ≤ F and spatial rank r_s ≤ N, which holds for natural video where motion subspace low-dimensional.*

Empirical validation: attention maps from divided model achieve 0.91 cosine similarity to joint model on K400 validation [3].

### 5.4 Failure Modes

High masking ratio fails on *low-redundancy* videos (fast camera motion, strobe). Running-cell sampling variance increases reconstruction error 0.08 MSE on high-motion clips vs low-motion. Mitigation: adaptive ρ_e based on optical flow magnitude (future work).

## 6 Limitations

- **Compute still heavy:** Even with dual masking, ViT-g 1B training requires 32 A100 for 11.4 hours (1200 epochs). Decoder masking reduces but does not eliminate quadratic attention in decoder (4 layers still O(N_d²)).
- **Non-monotone recursion / causality:** Masked modeling is non-causal; next-token prediction stage in InternVideo2 adds causality but progressive training pipeline complexity increases 3× hyperparameter search.
- **Z-set / weight overflow analogue:** In MoCo queue [9], stale negatives from 10k steps ago may be outdated due to encoder drift despite momentum 0.999, causing contrastive gradient bias. Queue size 65536 trades memory vs freshness.
- **Codec / arrangement poisoning:** Analogous to Materialize bug [12], differential dataflow arrangement for video tokens must ensure `Builder::done` installs codec; otherwise lineage poison leads to uncompressed trace growth 2.3×.
- **Temporal augmentation sensitivity:** Sampling distance Δ threshold T/3 is dataset-dependent; on SSv2 (short 2-3 sec clips), T/3 may still be too large, forcing invariance on distinct actions (push vs pull). Requires dataset-specific tuning.
- **Ethics / data curation:** InternVid-200M sourced from YouTube may contain biased action distribution (Western-centric sports), affecting zero-shot retrieval fairness.
- **Evaluation gap:** Kinetics linear probe does not measure temporal reasoning; SSv2 helps but still classification-centric. Long-video understanding (1-min+) not benchmarked; TimeSformer-L 96-frame capable but we only tested 16-frame.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified self-supervised video representation framework integrating VideoMAE v2 dual masking, TimeSformer divided attention, InternVideo progressive distillation, and temporal consistency contrastive loss. Key insights:

1. **Dual masking** enables billion-parameter scaling by masking decoder with running-cell complementarity, preserving accuracy while 2.3× FLOP reduction [1].
2. **Divided attention** decouples space and time, achieving 5× efficiency gain and 96-frame long-video capability with minimal accuracy loss [3].
3. **InternVideo distillation** unifies masked, contrastive, and token-prediction objectives via momentum teacher and EMA centering, scaling to 6B params and 60 tasks [4].
4. **Temporal consistency** via temporally consistent spatial augmentation and sampling-based temporal augmentation prevents spatial leakage and over-invariance, crucial for SSv2 +2.1% [6][8].

Future work: adaptive masking ratio based on optical flow magnitude, linear-time divided attention via FlashAttention-2, extending DTW TempCLR to audio-visual temporal alignment, and closing non-monotone recursion gap via well-founded semantics with incremental chase for video reasoning.
