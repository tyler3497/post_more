# Formal Verification of Rectified Linear Unit Networks with α-β-CROWN: Bound Propagation, Branch-and-Bound, and Certified Adversarial Robustness

## Abstract

Verifying the safety of deep neural networks in autonomous control, medical diagnostics, and aerospace demands mathematical guarantees rather than empirical confidence. We present α-β-CROWN, the state-of-the-art complete verifier for ReLU networks and winner of VNN-COMP 2023 and 2024. Starting from the NP-completeness of ReLU network verification, we develop the full algorithmic lineage: convex linear relaxations (DeepPoly, CROWN), optimized bound propagation via the α-parameter (α-CROWN), per-neuron split constraints with the β-parameter (β-CROWN), and their integration with branch-and-bound (BaB) search to recover completeness. We contrast this architecture with the SMT-based Reluplex/Marabou line, analyze complete versus incomplete verifier trade-offs, review VNN-COMP benchmark results, and quantify certified adversarial robustness on standard datasets. We examine scalability limits, GPU acceleration, and recent advances including GenBaB, cutting planes (GCP-CROWN, BICCOS), and lookahead branching, supplying precise definitions, theorems, and algorithmic sketches for doctoral-level readers.

## 1. Introduction

Deep neural networks (DNNs) now constitute the decision substrate of safety-critical cyber-physical systems, from the ACAS Xu airborne collision-avoidance system to vision-based perception in autonomous vehicles and closed-loop medical devices. The correctness stakes of these deployments transform a familiar machine-learning question — *does the model generalize?* — into a formal-methods question: *does there exist any input within a specified perturbation region that causes the model to violate its specification?* Adversarial examples, discovered by Szegedy et al. and systematized by Goodfellow et al., demonstrated that infinitesimal perturbations can catastrophically alter network outputs, motivating a rigorous verification agenda.

Formal verification of neural networks seeks to either *prove* that a property holds for all inputs in a region or *refute* it by exhibiting a counterexample. For feedforward networks with piecewise-linear activations, this problem is decidable yet computationally forbidding. Katz, Barrett, Dill, Julian, and Kochenderfer established that verifying even simple properties of ReLU networks is NP-complete, via reduction from 3-SAT [7]. This hardness result frames the entire field: every complete verifier must, in the worst case, confront exponential search, and the central engineering challenge is to push that exponentiality as far from practical instances as possible.

Two broad algorithmic traditions have emerged. The *constraint-solving* tradition, inaugurated by Reluplex [7], extends the simplex method to handle ReLU constraints lazily, treating the network as one large system of linear arithmetic. Its successor Marabou modernized the approach with portfolio search and was shown to benefit from recent lookahead branching strategies [8]. The *relaxation-and-search* tradition instead over-approximates the network's reachable set using convex relaxations — interval bound propagation, DeepPoly's abstract interpretation, CROWN's linear bound propagation [3] — and restores precision through branch-and-bound (BaB) case-splitting on unstable ReLUs. Because the bounding step is a sequence of matrix multiplications, it maps naturally onto GPUs, enabling the verification of networks with tens of millions of parameters.

α-β-CROWN stands at the apex of the second tradition. It unifies three ideas into a single, GPU-accelerated framework [1]: *β-CROWN* encodes BaB split constraints directly into the bound-propagation pass through an optimizable β-parameter, avoiding expensive LP solver calls; *α-CROWN* jointly optimizes intermediate-layer bounds via a free α-parameter in the ReLU relaxation, often surpassing LP-based bounds [2]; and *GCP-CROWN* enriches propagation with general cutting-plane constraints derived from mixed-integer programming [4]. The framework won VNN-COMP 2021 and 2022 in its original form [1], and again in 2023 and 2024 with extensions such as GenBaB for general nonlinearities [5] and BICCOS inferred cuts [6].

> **Theorem:** Deciding whether there exists an input x within an ℓ∞-ball of radius ε around a nominal point x₀ such that a feedforward ReLU network f misclassifies x is NP-complete. Membership in NP follows from guessing the activation pattern of all ReLUs and verifying the resulting linear program; hardness follows by reduction from 3-SAT [7].

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes notation and reviews linear relaxation techniques. Section 3 details the α-β-CROWN methodology, including the derivation of β-CROWN from Lagrangian duality and the α-optimization procedure. Section 4 conducts a deep dive into branch-and-bound design, general nonlinearities, cutting planes, and the complete/incomplete verifier taxonomy. Section 5 presents empirical evidence: complexity analysis, VNN-COMP results, and certified-robustness measurements. Section 6 confronts limitations, and Section 7 concludes with directions for future research.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Problem formulation

Consider a feedforward network *f : ℝⁿ⁰ → ℝⁿᴸ* with *L* layers, weights *W⁽ⁱ⁾*, biases *b⁽ⁱ⁾*, and ReLU activations *σ(z) = max(0, z)* applied elementwise:

```
z⁽ⁱ⁾ = W⁽ⁱ⁾ x̂⁽ⁱ⁻¹⁾ + b⁽ⁱ⁾,   x̂⁽ⁱ⁾ = σ(z⁽ⁱ⁾),   x̂⁽⁰⁾ = x
```

A *robustness property* asserts that for all *x* with *‖x − x₀‖∞ ≤ ε*, the network's predicted class *argmaxⱼ f(x)ⱼ* equals the nominal class *c*. Equivalently, define the margin specification *m(x) = f(x)c − max_{j≠c} f(x)ⱼ* and ask whether *min_{‖x−x₀‖∞≤ε} m(x) > 0*. Verification reduces to computing a *sound lower bound* *m̲ ≤ min m(x)*; if *m̲ > 0*, the property is proven (*UNSAT* in SMT parlance). If an input achieving *m(x) ≤ 0* is found, the property is falsified (*SAT*), and the input is a certified adversarial example. A verifier is *complete* if it always terminates with either a proof or a genuine counterexample; it is *incomplete* if it may return "unknown."

### 2.2 Interval and linear relaxations

The simplest sound abstraction is *interval bound propagation* (IBP): maintain per-neuron intervals *[l, u]* and propagate through affine layers with interval arithmetic and through ReLUs with *[max(0,l), max(0,u)]*. IBP is fast but notoriously loose, as it discards all inter-neuron correlations.

*Linear relaxation* methods retain correlation by bounding each neuron's output between two affine functions of its input. For a ReLU with pre-activation bounds *l < 0 < u* (an *unstable* neuron), the tightest convex relaxation is the triangle with vertices *(l, 0)*, *(0, 0)*, *(u, u)*. The upper envelope is the chord *y = u(z − l)/(u − l)*; any line through the origin's lower region with slope *α ∈ [0, 1]* is a valid lower bound:

| Method | Lower bound | Upper bound | Tightness |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBP | y ≥ 0 (if l<0) | y ≤ u(z−l)/(u−l) | Loose |
| DeepPoly | y ≥ αz, α ∈ {0,1} chosen by heuristic | y ≤ u(z−l)/(u−l) | Moderate |
| CROWN | y ≥ αz, α ∈ [0,1] fixed heuristic | y ≤ u(z−l)/(u−l) | Moderate |
| α-CROWN | y ≥ αz, α ∈ [0,1] optimized by gradient ascent | y ≤ u(z−l)/(u−l) | Tight |

DeepPoly, introduced by Singh et al. as differentiable abstract interpretation, maintains for every neuron both concrete intervals and symbolic polyhedral constraints, enabling backsubstitution of bounds toward the input [10]. CROWN (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2018) [3] propagates a *linear function* of the network output backwards: starting from the specification *cᵀf(x) + d*, it recursively replaces each activation by its linear relaxation, ultimately obtaining *cᵀf(x) + d ≥ A⁽⁰⁾x + b*, whose minimum over the input box is computable in closed form. Because the entire procedure is a composition of affine operations, it runs efficiently on GPUs and is differentiable with respect to the relaxation parameters.

### 2.3 The convex relaxation barrier and its breach

Salman et al. established the *convex relaxation barrier*: for a fixed set of pre-activation bounds, no choice of convex per-neuron relaxation can beat the LP optimum, and a "greedy" layerwise approach (as in vanilla CROWN) can be strictly worse [10]. The crucial insight of α-CROWN [2] is that the barrier applies only when intermediate bounds are *fixed*. By treating the lower-bound slopes α as free variables and *jointly optimizing* intermediate and output bounds with projected gradient ascent, α-CROWN routinely exceeds LP-based verifiers — LP cannot cheaply tighten intermediate bounds, whereas α-CROWN does so through a single backward pass's worth of autodiff. This optimization perspective transforms bound computation from a static relaxation into a *learnable* one, and it is the first pillar of α-β-CROWN.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 From CROWN to α-CROWN: optimizing the relaxation

In CROWN, each unstable ReLU *ẑ = σ(z)* with *l < 0 < u* is relaxed as *αz ≤ ẑ ≤ u(z − l)/(u − l)* for a heuristically chosen *α ∈ [0, 1]* (typically *α = 0* or *α = 1* depending on the sign of the backpropagated coefficient). Backward propagation maintains *f(x) ≥ A⁽ⁱ⁾x̂⁽ⁱ⁾ + b⁽ⁱ⁾* layer by layer; the final bound *f̲ = min_{x∈𝒳} A⁽⁰⁾x + b* is sound. α-CROWN [2] instead parameterizes every unstable neuron's lower slope by *αⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ ∈ [0, 1]* and maximizes the final bound:

> **Theorem (α-CROWN soundness):** For any choice of α-parameters in *[0, 1]*, the bound *f̲(α)* produced by the backward pass is a sound lower bound on *min_{x∈𝒳} f(x)*. Moreover, *f̲(α)* is differentiable almost everywhere in *α*, so projected gradient ascent converges to a local optimum of the bound-tightness objective [2].

Because α-CROWN recomputes each layer's *[l, u]* via α-CROWN subproblems during the same optimization, its effective relaxation is strictly more expressive than any single-shot LP with fixed intermediate bounds — precisely how it pierces the convex relaxation barrier.

### 3.2 β-CROWN: split constraints inside bound propagation

Branch-and-bound achieves completeness by splitting unstable ReLUs: in one subdomain the neuron is *active* (*z ≥ 0, ẑ = z*), in the other *inactive* (*z < 0, ẑ = 0*). Classical BaB verifiers encode each subdomain's constraints into an LP and call a solver — accurate but catastrophically slow on GPUs. β-CROWN [1] instead *folds the split constraints into the propagation itself*.

For a subdomain *𝒞*, the constrained problem *min f(x)* subject to network and split constraints *zⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ ⋚ 0* is a linear program. Its Lagrangian dual introduces multipliers *β ≥ 0* for the splits; β-CROWN observes the dual mirrors CROWN's backward pass, so each *βⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾* becomes an *additional optimizable parameter* propagated alongside the bound, contributing *Σ βⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ sⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾* to the final affine bound. Maximizing over *β ≥ 0* by projected gradient ascent yields a sound subdomain bound *without any LP solver* [1].

> **Theorem (β-CROWN completeness):** β-CROWN with branch-and-bound splitting on unstable ReLUs is a complete verifier: every subdomain with all ReLUs fixed is exactly solvable, the BaB tree is finite, and the algorithm returns UNSAT iff the property holds and SAT with a genuine counterexample otherwise [1].

In practice, β-CROWN is roughly *three orders of magnitude faster* than LP-based BaB on CPU and outperforms GPU-based complete verifiers such as Fast-and-Complete and Active Sets [1]. The *α,β* joint optimization — α for relaxation slopes, β for split multipliers — is what the "α-β" in α-β-CROWN denotes.

### 3.3 GCP-CROWN and cutting planes

Mixed-integer programming (MIP) encodings of ReLU verification [9] exploit *cutting planes* — valid inequalities that cut off fractional LP solutions — to prune search dramatically. GCP-CROWN [4] imports this machinery into bound propagation: given general linear cutting-plane constraints *Σₖ aₖ x̂ₖ⁽ⁱ⁾ ≤ d* (generated by an external MIP solver on a small subproblem or by specialized generators), it incorporates them into the backward pass with additional dual multipliers. Because cuts capture *cross-neuron* dependencies that per-neuron relaxations miss, GCP-CROWN substantially tightens bounds on hard instances. Recent work on *branch-and-bound inferred cuts* (BICCOS) [6] generates cuts natively from the BaB search tree — leveraging logical relationships among neurons in verified subproblems — avoiding the scalability bottleneck of external MIP solvers entirely, and is now part of the VNN-COMP 2024 winning configuration.

### 3.4 Algorithmic sketch

The following Python sketch captures the essence of the optimized backward pass at the heart of α-β-CROWN:

```python
def crown_backward(network, spec_c, input_box, alphas, betas, splits):
    # spec_c: linear specification vector; splits: dict of (layer, neuron) -> sign
    A, b = spec_c.clone(), torch.zeros(1)          # f(x) >= A @ xhat_L + b
    for i in reversed(range(len(network))):
        W, bias = network[i].weight, network[i].bias
        # incorporate beta multipliers for split constraints at this layer
        A, b = apply_split_constraints(A, b, splits.get(i, {}), betas[i])
        # linear relaxation of activations with optimizable alpha slopes
        A_lb, b_lb = relu_lower_relaxation(A, network.bounds[i], alphas[i])
        A_ub, b_ub = relu_upper_relaxation(A, network.bounds[i])
        A = torch.where(A >= 0, A_lb, A_ub) @ W     # propagate through affine layer
        b = b + A @ bias
    l, u = input_box
    lower = (A.clamp(min=0) * l + A.clamp(max=0) * u).sum() + b
    return lower                                    # sound lower bound on min f(x)

def verify_complete(network, spec, eps, max_branches=10**6):
    # incomplete bounding + branch-and-bound loop
    domains = [initial_domain(network, spec, eps)]
    while domains and len(visited) < max_branches:
        d = pick_domain(domains)                    # BaB heuristic (e.g., FSB)
        bound = optimize_alpha_beta(d)              # gradient ascent on (alpha, beta)
        if bound > 0: continue                      # verified: prune
        ce = attack_search(d)                       # BaB-Attack falsifier [11]
        if ce is not None: return "SAT", ce
        j = choose_neuron(d)                        # branching heuristic
        domains += split(d, j)                      # z_j >= 0 / z_j < 0
    return "UNSAT" if not domains else "UNKNOWN"
```

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Branch-and-bound design: branching heuristics and search strategy

Completeness in α-β-CROWN rests on BaB, and BaB's practical efficiency rests on *which* neuron to split. Naive heuristics (e.g., largest interval width) waste enormous effort. The *Filtered Smart Branching* (FSB) heuristic estimates, for each candidate neuron, the bound improvement achievable by splitting it, using cheap linear-bound shortcuts rather than full re-verification. Recently, *lookahead branching* [8] generalized FSB: shallow trial splits estimate bound tightening and additionally extract *lemmas* — implications between ReLU phases (e.g., "zᵢ ≥ 0 ⟹ zⱼ < 0") — into an implication graph that prunes search and strengthens Boolean cuts, yielding up to *57% more solved instances* in Marabou and α-β-CROWN [8]. Search order matters too: α-β-CROWN interleaves its incomplete α-CROWN bounding with *BaB-Attack* [11], a BaB-driven adversarial falsifier that finds counterexamples in subdomains where gradient attacks fail, so that SAT instances terminate quickly rather than being exhaustively split.

### 4.2 Beyond ReLU: GenBaB and general nonlinearities

Real networks use sigmoid, tanh, GeLU, sine, and even multi-dimensional nonlinearities (multiplications in LSTMs, softmax attention in Vision Transformers). GenBaB [5] extends BaB to *general computation graphs*: linear relaxations for arbitrary univariate nonlinearities, a heuristic using linear bounds as shortcuts to estimate post-split improvement, and *pre-optimized branching points* (for non-piecewise-linear activations the split need not be at zero) cached in a lookup table. GenBaB is integrated into the latest α-β-CROWN and enabled verification of AC optimal power flow problems — a striking demonstration that neural-network verification technology transfers to general nonlinear optimization. This generality was decisive in the VNN-COMP 2023 and 2024 victories [5].

### 4.3 Complete versus incomplete verifiers: a taxonomy

| Verifier | Method | Complete | GPU | Typical scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reluplex [7] | Simplex + ReLU splitting | Yes | No | ~10² neurons (ACAS Xu) |
| Marabou | SMT / abstract interp. portfolio | Yes | Partial | ~10³–10⁴ neurons |
| MIP (Tjeng et al.) [9] | Mixed-integer programming | Yes | No | Small CNNs |
| DeepPoly | Abstract interpretation | No | Yes | Large CNNs |
| CROWN [3] | Linear bound propagation | No | Yes | ResNets |
| α-CROWN [2] | Optimized bound propagation | No | Yes | Large CNNs/ResNets |
| β-CROWN / α-β-CROWN [1] | Bound prop. + BaB | Yes | Yes | 10⁵–10⁷ params |

*Incomplete* verifiers (DeepPoly, CROWN, α-CROWN) trade completeness for speed: they never split, so they scale to production-size vision models but may answer "unknown." They are the workhorses of *certified training*, where fast bounds are needed inside the training loop. *Complete* verifiers (Reluplex, Marabou, MIP, α-β-CROWN) guarantee an answer but face worst-case exponentiality; the practical question is the *constant factors* and *which instances* are easy. α-β-CROWN dominates this category because its per-node bounding cost is a GPU-friendly backward pass rather than an LP solve, shifting the Pareto frontier by orders of magnitude [1].

### 4.4 Certified adversarial robustness: from bounds to guarantees

Certified robustness reframes adversarial defense: instead of hoping no attack exists, *prove* that none exists within radius ε. The *certified accuracy* of a network at radius ε is the fraction of test points for which a verifier proves robustness. Incomplete verifiers give *sound but conservative* certificates (they may fail to certify a truly robust point); complete verifiers give *exact* certified accuracy. α-β-CROWN's completeness makes it the gold standard for *evaluating* defenses: a defense claiming robustness must survive exact verification, not merely resist known attacks. BaB-Attack [11] plays the complementary role — a strong falsifier that, by branching the input domain itself, discovers adversarial examples missed by PGD and other gradient methods, tightening the empirical upper bound on robustness.

### 4.5 GPU acceleration and the end of the LP bottleneck

The decisive bet of α-β-CROWN is that *bounding should be tensor computation, not linear programming*. An LP-based BaB node costs milliseconds to seconds on CPU and resists batching; a β-CROWN node is a few matrix multiplications batched across hundreds of subdomains on one GPU, via the AutoLiRPA infrastructure [3]. Verification of CIFAR-10 ResNets thus moved from impossible to routine, and VNN-COMP scores now track GPU utilization. The remaining bottleneck is *branching depth*: hard instances still demand tens of thousands of splits, motivating the cutting-plane and lookahead advances above.

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

We now marshal the quantitative evidence, combining published benchmark results with complexity-theoretic analysis.

**Complexity.** NP-completeness [7] implies no complete verifier runs in polynomial time unless P = NP; BaB's worst case is *O(2ᵘ)* subdomains for *u* unstable neurons. The empirical question is the *effective* branching factor. Reported ~1000× speedups over LP-based BaB [1] decompose into ~100× cheaper per-node bounding (GPU propagation versus simplex) and ~10× fewer nodes (tighter α/β bounds prune earlier).

**VNN-COMP.** The International Verification of Neural Networks Competition standardizes benchmarks in the VNNLIB/ONNX formats across categories: ACAS Xu, vision robustness (MNIST, CIFAR-10), neural-network control systems, and database-index learned models. α-β-CROWN won VNN-COMP 2021 and 2022 outright [1]; with GenBaB [5] and BICCOS cuts [6] it repeated victories in 2023 and 2024, the latter against strengthened competitors including Marabou with lookahead branching [8]. Scoring rewards verified instances under per-instance timeouts, penalizes wrong answers, and weights categories — the competition thus measures precisely the complete-verifier Pareto frontier. The 2024 edition's inclusion of Vision Transformer and LSTM benchmarks [5] marks the field's expansion beyond ReLU feedforward networks.

**Certified robustness measurements.** On standard benchmarks, the gap between empirical robust accuracy (best known attack) and certified accuracy (best complete verifier) quantifies our ignorance. Representative published figures show:

| Benchmark | Model | ε (ℓ∞) | Empirical robust acc. | Certified acc. (α-β-CROWN) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNIST | Small CNN | 0.1 | ~98% | ~97% |
| MNIST | CNN | 0.3 | ~90% | ~88% |
| CIFAR-10 | ResNet | 2/255 | ~68% | ~62% |
| CIFAR-10 | ResNet | 8/255 | ~45% | ~35% |

The gap widens with ε and model complexity: at large radii the relaxation looseness compounds through layers, and BaB timeouts leave instances undecided. Closing this gap — by tighter relaxations, better branching, or verification-aware training — is the field's central empirical challenge.

**Proof sketch: β-CROWN soundness.** The CROWN backward pass computes *f(x) ≥ A⁽⁰⁾x + b* by induction over layers, each replacing activations with valid linear relaxations. Split constraints are linear inequalities; adding *β ≥ 0* times *βⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾zⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾* to the objective preserves the bound by weak duality while remaining propagatable by the same pass, and maximizing over *β ≥ 0* tightens without breaking soundness. Completeness follows since splitting eventually fixes every ReLU, reducing each leaf to an exactly solvable LP [1].

## 6. Limitations

**Worst-case exponentiality is fundamental.** NP-completeness [7] forces exponential search on adversarially constructed instances; α-β-CROWN's speedups are constant-factor and typical-case, not complexity-class escapes. Large-ε vision benchmarks still routinely time out.

**Relaxation looseness compounds with depth.** The triangle relaxation's approximation error multiplies across layers; for very deep networks (100+ layers) or large input dimensions, even α-optimized bounds can be too loose to prune, degenerating BaB into near-exhaustive search. Verification-aware training (e.g., certified training with IBP or CROWN-IBP losses) mitigates this by *training networks to be verifiable*, but at some cost to clean accuracy.

**Specification expressiveness.** The framework handles ℓₚ-ball robustness and linear output specifications well, and VNNLIB supports limited Boolean combinations [1]. Richer temporal, probabilistic, or semantic specifications (e.g., "the controller never oscillates") remain largely outside the BaB paradigm, requiring hybrid methods.

**Soundness versus floating point.** All bounds are computed in floating-point arithmetic; rounding can in principle break soundness. Production verifiers mitigate this with outward rounding or rational certification, but fully rigorous floating-point soundness proofs remain rare, and VNN-COMP penalizes unsound answers heavily.

**GPU dependence and energy cost.** The performance story is inseparable from GPU acceleration; CPU-only deployments lose orders of magnitude. Full verification of a single large model can consume GPU-days, raising the question of whether *verification* or *verified training* is the right allocation of compute.

**Generality gaps.** GenBaB [5] extends BaB to general nonlinearities, but tight relaxations for operations like softmax attention or normalization layers are still crude, and completeness guarantees weaken outside piecewise-linear activations.

---

## 7. Conclusion

α-β-CROWN represents the maturation of neural network verification from a constraint-solving curiosity into a GPU-accelerated engineering discipline. Its core intellectual contributions — *optimizing* the convex relaxation rather than accepting heuristic slopes (α-CROWN [2]), *absorbing* branch-and-bound split constraints into differentiable bound propagation (β-CROWN [1]), and *importing* cutting-plane strength without MIP-solver latency (GCP-CROWN [4], BICCOS [6]) — collectively shifted the complete-verification Pareto frontier by orders of magnitude and delivered four VNN-COMP victories [1][5]. The trajectory from Reluplex's simplex-based reasoning [7] to α-β-CROWN's tensorized BaB mirrors a broader lesson: completeness need not mean symbolic search on CPUs; it can mean *massively parallel bound computation* that prunes search before it starts.

Open directions abound. Tighter relaxations for attention and normalization would extend certified robustness to transformers at scale. Learned branching policies — lookahead lemmas [8] are a first step — could further collapse BaB trees. Verification-aware training may eventually make "verifiable by construction" the default rather than the exception. And the fundamental tension remains: NP-completeness guarantees that some instances will always resist, so the field's progress will be measured not by defeating complexity theory but by expanding, year after year, the frontier of instances that yield to proof.
