# Flips, Flops, and the Sarkisov Program: Birational Surgery in the Minimal Model Program

## Abstract

The Minimal Model Program (MMP) is the modern framework for birational classification: starting from a smooth projective variety, it applies a controlled sequence of *divisorial contractions* and *flips* until the canonical divisor becomes nef or the variety admits a Mori fiber space structure. This thesis develops the theory from first principles — the cone theorem and the taxonomy of extremal contractions — through the heart of the program, the flip, whose existence in dimension three is Mori's flip theorem [4] and in all dimensions for klt pairs is the crown of Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan [1]. We explain Shokurov's and Kawamata's termination arguments in dimension three, the MMP with scaling that makes termination effective in higher dimensions, the sharp distinction between flips and flops, and the Sarkisov program [2] that factorizes arbitrary birational maps between Mori fiber spaces into elementary links. We close with the open frontiers: full termination in dimension four and above.

##1 Intro

Birational geometry asks which varieties are *essentially the same*: two varieties are birational when they share a dense open subset, differing only along lower-dimensional loci. For curves the story is classical — every curve is birational to a unique smooth model — and for surfaces the Italian school, refined by Zariski and Castelnuovo, showed that every smooth projective surface contracts to either a minimal model with nef canonical divisor or a ruled surface over a curve. In dimension three and beyond, the naive program collapses: minimal models need not be smooth, need not be unique, and the contractions required are subtler than blowing down a curve on a surface.

Mori's breakthrough in the 1980s was to replace the search for a single canonical representative by a *surgery procedure*. Given a projective variety *X* whose canonical divisor *K_X* is not nef, the **cone theorem** locates a distinguished direction — a *K_X*-negative extremal ray — along which *X* can be contracted. The contraction is one of three kinds: it may contract a divisor (easy), it may be a fibration with *K_X*-negative fibers (a **Mori fiber space**, a terminal output), or it may be *small*, contracting only a locus of codimension at least two. In the small case there is no morphism inverse to repair the damage; instead one performs a **flip**, replacing the contracted curves by new curves on which the canonical divisor is positive. The whole edifice rests on two hard theorems: flips *exist*, and sequences of flips *terminate*. Mori proved both for threefolds [4]; the existence of flips in arbitrary dimension for klt pairs is the deepest achievement of Birkar, Cascini, Hacon, and McKernan [1], while termination in dimension four and above remains the central open problem of the field.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the language of pairs, singularities, and the cone of curves. Section 3 describes the MMP as an algorithm, including the scaling variant that makes it run in all dimensions. Section 4 is the core: the cone theorem, the taxonomy of contractions, the flip, termination, and the flip–flop distinction. Section 5 sketches the proof architecture behind flip existence and termination. Section 6 records the limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

##2 Background

### Pairs and singularities

The modern MMP runs not on varieties alone but on **pairs** *(X, Δ)*, where *X* is a normal projective variety and *Δ* an effective boundary divisor with real coefficients, such that *K_X + Δ* is ℝ-Cartier. The boundary is a bookkeeping device: it absorbs the singularities created by contractions and lets induction on dimension proceed via adjunction.

The singularities that appear are measured by **discrepancies**. For a birational morphism *π: Y → X* from a smooth *Y*, write *K_Y + Δ_Y = π*(K_X + Δ)* with *Δ_Y* the strict transform plus exceptional contributions; the discrepancy of an exceptional divisor *E* is the coefficient *a(E, X, Δ)* in *K_Y = π*K_X + Σ a(E_i, X, Δ) E_i*. The pair is:

- **terminal** if *a(E, X, Δ) > 0* for every exceptional *E*;
- **canonical** if *a(E, X, Δ) ≥ 0*;
- **kawamata log terminal (klt)** if *a(E, X, Δ) > −1* and the coefficients of *Δ* are below one;
- **log canonical (lc)** if *a(E, X, Δ) ≥ −1*.

Mori's threefold program works in the terminal category; the higher-dimensional theory of [1] works with klt pairs. The program preserves ℚ-factoriality (every Weil divisor is ℚ-Cartier), which guarantees that intersection numbers with curves are well defined at every step.

### The cone of curves

Let *N_1(X)* be the real vector space of numerical classes of curves, and *NE(X)* the cone generated by effective curve classes; denote its closure by *NE-bar(X)*. The **cone theorem** [4] asserts that *NE-bar(X)* decomposes as the sum of the *K_X*-nonnegative part and a discrete collection of **extremal rays**, each spanned by a rational curve *C* with the *length bound* *0 < −(K_X · C) ≤ 2n* where *n = dim X*. Each extremal ray *R* admits a **contraction morphism** *cont_R: X → Z* contracting precisely the curves whose class lies in *R* [4].

The contraction is classified by the dimension of its exceptional locus:

| Operation | Exceptional locus | Behavior of *K* | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divisorial contraction | a prime divisor *E* | *K_X* negative on contracted curves | *X → Z* morphism, *ρ* drops by 1 |
| Flipping contraction | codimension ≥ 2 | *K_X* negative on contracted curves | small morphism *X → Z*; flip needed |
| Mori fiber contraction | all of *X* | *K_X* negative on fibers | fibration *X → Z*, terminal output |
| Flopping contraction | codimension ≥ 2 | *K_X* numerically trivial | *K*-trivial surgery, not an MMP step |

Here *ρ(X)* is the Picard number. A **Mori fiber space** is a contraction *f: X → S* with *dim X > dim S*, connected fibers, *ρ(X/S) = 1*, and *−K_X* relatively ample — the analogue of a ruled surface, and one of the two possible outputs of the program.

##3 Methodology

The MMP is an iterative procedure, most transparently formulated with **scaling** [1][3]:

1. Start with a ℚ-factorial klt pair *(X, Δ)* and an ample divisor *H* such that *K_X + Δ + H* is nef.
2. Compute the **nef threshold** *λ = sup{ t ≥ 0 : K_X + Δ + tH is nef }*.
3. If *λ = 0*, the program stops: either *K_X + Δ* is nef (a **minimal model**) or it is not pseudoeffective and the last contraction is a Mori fiber space.
4. If *λ > 0*, the divisor *K_X + Δ + λH* is nef but not ample; it defines a contraction of a *(K_X + Δ)*-negative extremal face.
5. If the contraction is divisorial or a Mori fiber contraction, replace *X* by its target and repeat. If it is small, replace the flip and repeat.

The key point of scaling is that the thresholds *λ_1 > λ_2 > …* strictly decrease, and the divisor *H* acts as a measuring stick: finiteness of models with *K_X + Δ + tH* nef for rational polytopes of *t* forces termination whenever *K_X + Δ* is big [1]. In particular, the MMP with scaling terminates for any klt pair with *K_X + Δ* pseudoeffective and big — and for *flipping* contractions in particular — in arbitrary dimension [1][3].

> **Theorem:** (Cone and contraction, after Mori [4].) Let *X* be a projective variety with klt singularities. Then *NE-bar(X) = NE-bar(X)_{K_X ≥ 0} + Σ ℝ_{≥0}[C_i]* where each *C_i* is a rational curve with *0 < −(K_X · C_i) ≤ 2 dim X*, and for every *K_X*-negative extremal face *F* there exists a contraction morphism contracting exactly the curves in *F*.

> **Proof:** The existence of rational curves spanning the extremal rays is Mori's bend-and-break: through a general point of a *K_X*-negative curve there deforms a rational curve of bounded anticanonical degree. The contraction is built from the base-point-free theorem applied to a supporting divisor of the face. ∎

The following toy implementation tracks how a flip improves discrepancies — the monotone invariant behind termination arguments:

```python
def flip_step(discrepancies, contracted_curves):
    """Model of one flip: discrepancies of exceptional divisors
    strictly increase; a well-ordered invariant cannot rise forever
    unless the process stops."""
    improved = {E: a + 1 for E, a in discrepancies.items()
                if E in contracted_curves}
    return improved

def length_bound(dim):
    """Kawamata's bound on extremal rational curves: -(K_X . C) <= 2n."""
    return 2 * dim

# A 3-fold flip improves discrepancies by at least 1 at each step
before = {"E1": -0.5, "E2": 0.0}
after = flip_step(before, {"E1"})
assert after["E1"] > before["E1"]
assert length_bound(3) == 6
```

And the taxonomy of a single MMP step, expressed as a datatype:

```haskell
-- One step of the minimal model program
data Contraction
  = Divisorial { exceptional :: Divisor }   -- contracts a divisor
  | Flipping   { locus :: Locus }            -- small, K negative: flip it
  | Fibering   { base :: Variety }           -- Mori fiber space: stop
  | Flopping   { locus :: Locus }            -- K trivial: not an MMP step

mmpStep :: Pair -> Contraction -> Either Model Pair
mmpStep (X, d) (Divisorial _) = Right (contract X, pushforward d)
mmpStep (X, d) (Flipping _)   = Right (flip X, pushforward d)
mmpStep (X, d) (Fibering s)   = Left (MoriFiberSpace X s)
mmpStep _      (Flopping _)   = error "flops do not improve K"
```

##4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The cone theorem as a compass

Before Mori, the cone of curves was a mysterious convex object; after the cone theorem it became a navigational instrument. The discreteness of *K_X*-negative extremal rays in the half-space where *K_X* is negative means the program always has a canonical next move: pick any extremal ray on which *K_X* is negative and contract it. The length bound *−(K_X · C) ≤ 2n* is not a mere curiosity — it controls the singularities that can appear and feeds directly into the boundedness results used in higher-dimensional termination arguments [1].

The cone theorem also explains why the program must sometimes stop without reaching a minimal model. If *K_X + Δ* is not pseudoeffective, no amount of surgery can make it nef; instead the last contraction sweeps out the variety by *K*-negative rational curves and the output is a Mori fiber space. The dichotomy — *minimal model* versus *Mori fiber space* — is the birational analogue of the dichotomy between non-uniruled and uniruled varieties.

### 4.2 Divisorial contractions: the easy surgery

A divisorial contraction *f: X → Z* contracts a single prime divisor *E* to a lower-dimensional locus, with *−(K_X + Δ)* relatively ample. Because the exceptional locus is a divisor, the target *Z* inherits ℚ-factoriality and the singularities do not worsen: discrepancies can only improve. On threefolds, Mori classified divisorial contractions to points and curves completely — they are weighted blow-ups of terminal singularities — and Hacon's factorization result shows that even flips and divisorial contractions to curves admit a fine factorization through weighted blow-ups, giving an inductive measure of complexity [5].

Divisorial contractions strictly decrease the Picard number, so only finitely many can occur in any MMP sequence. They are the unproblematic steps; all the difficulty of the program is concentrated in the small contractions.

### 4.3 Flips: surgery in codimension two

Let *f: X → Z* be a **flipping contraction**: *f* is small (isomorphism in codimension one), *ρ(X/Z) = 1*, and *−K_X* is *f*-ample. The **flip** is a second small morphism *f⁺: X⁺ → Z* with *K_{X⁺}* *f⁺*-ample, together with a birational map *φ: X ⇢ X⁺* over *Z* that is an isomorphism in codimension one and satisfies *φ_*K_X = K_{X⁺}*. Intuitively, the flip deletes the *K_X*-negative curves contracted by *f* and inserts new curves on which the canonical divisor is positive — it is the unique surgery that improves *K* while changing the variety only in codimension two.

Existence is highly nontrivial. In dimension three it is Mori's **flip theorem** [4], proved by a delicate local analysis of terminal singularities. In higher dimensions, Shokurov reduced the existence of flips to two ingredients: the MMP in one dimension lower, and the finite generation of certain divisorial algebras; Hacon and McKernan then proved the required **extension theorems** lifting sections from lower-dimensional centers, and Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan completed the induction to prove finite generation of the canonical ring — hence existence of klt flips — in all dimensions [1].

The flip strictly increases discrepancies: for every exceptional divisor over *Z*, its discrepancy with respect to *X⁺* exceeds its discrepancy with respect to *X*. This monotonicity is the engine of every termination proof.

### 4.4 Termination: why the surgery must stop

In dimension three, termination was proved by Shokurov and Kawamata [4]: for terminal threefolds, an infinite sequence of flips would produce an infinite strictly increasing sequence of discrepancies, contradicting the fact that discrepancies of terminal singularities lie in a discrete, well-ordered set once the index is bounded. The argument refines to **special termination** — flips eventually avoid any fixed log canonical center — which reduces termination for klt pairs to termination for terminal ones.

In higher dimensions, termination of arbitrary sequences of flips is open. What is known is termination *with scaling*: BCHM prove that the MMP with scaling of an ample divisor terminates whenever *K_X + Δ* is big, because only finitely many distinct models can appear as log terminal models of *(X, Δ + tH)* for *t* in a rational polytope [1]. Hacon and Moraga extended the picture to generalized pairs, showing that termination in lower dimension plus a weak Zariski decomposition implies termination for log canonical generalized pairs [3]. The remaining gap — termination without scaling in dimension four and above — is arguably the most important open problem in birational geometry.

### 4.5 Flips versus flops, and the Sarkisov program

A **flop** is the *K*-trivial analogue of a flip: for a small contraction *f: X → Z* with *K_X* numerically trivial over *Z*, the flop *X⁺ → Z* replaces the contracted curves by new curves with *K_{X⁺}* still numerically trivial. Flops do not improve the canonical divisor and are not steps of the MMP; instead they connect *different minimal models* of the same variety. Kawamata proved that any two minimal models are connected by a sequence of flops — minimal models are unique up to flops.

The parallel non-uniqueness for the other output — Mori fiber spaces — is resolved by the **Sarkisov program**. A **Sarkisov link** is an elementary birational map between Mori fiber spaces, classified into four types (I, II, III, IV) according to the configuration of blow-ups, flops, and changes of fibration base [2]. The theorem, proved for threefolds by Corti building on Sarkisov, and for klt pairs in all dimensions by Hacon and McKernan [2], states:

> **Theorem:** (Sarkisov program [2].) Let *σ: X ⇢ Y* be a birational map between Mori fiber spaces arising as outputs of a *K_X*-MMP. Then *σ* factors as a composition of finitely many Sarkisov links.

In particular, the birational automorphism group of a Mori fiber space — notably the Cremona group of projective space — is generated by Sarkisov links, and the classification of links becomes a concrete handle on birational rigidity [6][7].

##5 Empirical/Proofs

The proof architecture for flip existence in [1] is worth sketching, because it shows how the whole theory hangs together:

1. **Reduction to finite generation.** The flip of *f: X → Z* exists if and only if the relative canonical algebra *⊕_m f_*𝒪_X(mK_X)* is a finitely generated 𝒪_Z-algebra; its Proj is the flip.
2. **Shokurov's reduction.** Finite generation in dimension *n* follows from the MMP with scaling in dimension *n − 1* plus a lifting statement for sections.
3. **Extension theorems.** Hacon–McKernan prove that sections of *m(K_X + Δ)* lift from non-klt centers under suitable positivity, using multiplier ideals and vanishing.
4. **Induction and the big case.** The MMP with scaling terminates when *K_X + Δ* is big, giving log terminal models; these models produce the generators of the canonical ring.
5. **Conclusion.** By induction on dimension, flips exist for klt pairs in all dimensions, the MMP with scaling terminates for big pairs, and minimal models exist for varieties of log general type [1].

A concrete instance: on a smooth threefold, the Francia flip contracts a curve with normal bundle of bidegree indicating *K_X*-negativity and replaces it with a curve on which *K_{X⁺}* is ample; the discrepancy computation is a local toric calculation, and Hacon's factorization [5] exhibits the flip as a sequence of weighted blow-ups and blow-downs, making the increase of discrepancies visible.

The Sarkisov factorization is effective in practice: given two Mori fiber spaces, one runs a suitable MMP with scaling over a common resolution; the steps of that MMP assemble into links, and termination of the scaled MMP guarantees the factorization is finite [2].

##6 Limitations

- **Termination in dimension ≥ 4 is open.** Only termination with scaling (or under bigness hypotheses) is known [1][3]. An unconditional proof would likely require the ACC for minimal log discrepancies plus boundedness of Fano varieties in one dimension lower — conjectures that are themselves wide open.
- **Singularity classes.** The BCHM machinery requires klt singularities; log canonical flips were established later by building on [1], and the theory for non-ℚ-factorial or non-klt settings remains fragmentary.
- **Positive and mixed characteristic.** The cone, contraction, and base-point-free theorems fail or are unknown in characteristic *p* in general; threefold MMP in characteristic *p > 5* has been established only recently and partially.
- **Effectivity.** The proofs give no bound on the number of flips; the Sarkisov factorization is finite but not effectively bounded, limiting computational applications.
- **Non-uniqueness is intrinsic.** Minimal models come in flop-equivalence classes and Mori fiber spaces in Sarkisov-link classes; there is no canonical choice, so the MMP is a nondeterministic algorithm whose outputs must be compared by the link formalism.

##7 Conclusion

The Minimal Model Program turns the vague hope of "simplifying a variety" into a precise surgical procedure: read the cone of curves, contract a *K*-negative extremal ray, and when the contraction is small, flip. Mori's threefold theorems showed the surgery terminates; Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan showed the surgery is always available for klt pairs and terminates with scaling, delivering minimal models for varieties of log general type [1]. Flops and the Sarkisov program [2] then organize the non-uniqueness of the outputs into finite, classifiable moves. What remains — termination in higher dimensions — is not a technical afterthought but the central question: whether the surgery that tamed threefolds can be proved to halt in general. Until it is, the MMP with scaling stands as both the working tool and the placeholder for the complete theory.
