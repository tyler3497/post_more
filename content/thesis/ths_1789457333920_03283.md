## Abstract

Class field theory describes the abelian extensions of a local or global field in terms of the arithmetic of the base field itself [1]. This thesis presents a unified account of the theory: the local reciprocity law via Lubin–Tate formal groups, the global Artin reciprocity law on the idèle class group, Takagi's existence theorem classifying finite abelian extensions by congruence subgroups, and the Hilbert class field with the principal ideal theorem of Furtwängler and Artin. We emphasize the two classical instances of *explicit* class field theory — the Kronecker–Weber theorem for the rational numbers and the theory of complex multiplication for imaginary quadratic fields — in which the maximal abelian extension is generated by special values of analytic functions, as envisioned in Hilbert's twelfth problem [3]. A worked example over **Q**(√−5) illustrates the Hilbert class field and capitulation of ideals. We close with the boundaries of the theory: non-abelian extensions, infinite class field towers, and the open problem of explicit construction beyond the CM case.

## 1 Intro

The central problem bequeathed by Kronecker, Weber, and Hilbert to twentieth-century number theory splits naturally into two questions [3]:

1. **Classification.** Given a number field *K*, describe *all* of its finite abelian extensions, and determine precisely how the prime ideals of *K* decompose in each of them.
2. **Construction.** Produce these abelian extensions *explicitly* — by adjoining to *K* the special values of distinguished analytic functions.

The first question was answered completely: Takagi's *Existenzsatz* (1920) classified abelian extensions by generalized ideal class groups, and Artin's reciprocity law (1927) made the classification *canonical* by identifying Galois groups with class groups through the Frobenius automorphism [4]. The second question — Hilbert's twelfth problem — is solved only for *K* = **Q** and for imaginary quadratic fields (and more generally CM fields), and remains open in general [9].

> **Theorem:** (Artin reciprocity law, idèlic form). *Let L/K be a finite abelian extension of number fields. Then the global Artin map (·, L/K) : C_K → Gal(L/K) is surjective with kernel exactly the norm group N_{L/K}(C_L), inducing a canonical isomorphism C_K / N_{L/K}(C_L) ≅ Gal(L/K) [4].*

What makes this theorem extraordinary is its *explicitness*: the isomorphism sends the class of a uniformizer at an unramified prime **p** to the Frobenius automorphism Frob_**p**, so that splitting behavior in *L* is read off from congruence conditions in *K*. Class field theory is thus simultaneously a vast generalization of quadratic reciprocity and the prototype for the Langlands program [1].

## 2 Background

We fix a number field *K* with ring of integers *O_K*. The raw material of ideal-theoretic class field theory is the following cast of objects.

- **Ideal class group.** *Cl(K) = I_K / P_K*, the group of fractional ideals modulo principal ideals; its order *h_K* is the *class number*, measuring the failure of unique factorization [7].
- **Modulus.** A formal product **m** = **m**_0 **m**_∞ of a nonzero integral ideal **m**_0 and a set of real embeddings **m**_∞. A modulus encodes simultaneous congruence and sign conditions.
- **Ray class group.** *Cl_**m**(K) = I^**m** / P_{**m**,1}*, where *I^**m*** are ideals coprime to **m**_0 and *P_{**m**,1}* are principal ideals with a generator *α ≡ 1 (mod **m**_0)* and *σ(α) > 0* for *σ ∈ **m**_∞* [4].
- **Local fields.** For each place *v* of *K*, the completion *K_v* (either **R**, **C**, or a finite extension of **Q**_p). Decomposition and inertia groups at *v* govern ramification [5].
- **Frobenius elements.** For **p** unramified in *L/K*, the Frobenius *Frob_**p** ∈ Gal(L/K)* is the unique element acting as *x ↦ x^{N**p**}* on residue fields; its conjugacy class detects splitting completely.

The historical arc of the subject is worth keeping in view [3][9]:

| Year | Milestone |

| 1907 | Furtwängler proves existence of the Hilbert class field |

| 1927 | Artin proves the reciprocity law, giving the *canonical* isomorphism |

| 1930s | Chevalley introduces idèles and idèle class groups |
| 1965 | Lubin–Tate give the explicit local theory via formal groups |

## 3 Methodology

Three distinct methodological traditions prove the main theorems, and this thesis draws on all three [1][5]:

1. **The ideal-theoretic and analytic tradition** (Takagi, Hecke, Chevalley). Abelian extensions are constructed as *class fields* of congruence subgroups; analytic tools — *L*-functions, density theorems, and the Chebotarev density theorem — supply the existence and completeness results [2].
2. **The cohomological tradition** (Artin–Tate). A *class formation* is a Galois module *C* with *H¹(G, C_L) = 0* and *H²(G, C_L)* cyclic of order *[L : K]* carrying a canonical generator *u_{L/K}*. The Tate–Nakayama lemma then produces the reciprocity isomorphism *Ĥ⁻²(G, **Z**) → Ĥ⁰(G, C_L)* purely algebraically. The idèle class group *C_L* is the primordial example [1][5].
3. **The explicit tradition** (Lubin–Tate, Neukirch, Hazewinkel). Local reciprocity is realized *concretely*: the maximal abelian extension of a local field is generated by torsion points of a formal group, with no cohomology at all. Neukirch extended this philosophy to a largely cohomology-free global theory [5].

Our exposition follows the logical order *local → global → explicit*: we first establish local class field theory via Lubin–Tate theory, then assemble the global Artin map as a product of local ones on the idèle class group, and finally exhibit the two classical explicit constructions [1].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Local class field theory via Lubin–Tate

Let *K* be a non-archimedean local field: complete with respect to a discrete valuation, with finite residue field of *q* elements, ring of integers *O_K*, maximal ideal **p** *= (π)*, and unit group *U_K = O_K^×*. Local class field theory asserts a perfect dictionary between the abelian extensions of *K* and the open subgroups of finite index in *K^×* [5].

> **Theorem:** (Local reciprocity law). *There is a canonical homomorphism, the local Artin map, (·, K) : K^× → Gal(K^ab/K), with dense image, inducing an isomorphism of the profinite completion \hat{K^×} with Gal(K^ab/K). For every finite abelian extension L/K it induces K^× / N_{L/K}(L^×) ≅ Gal(L/K), and every open subgroup of finite index in K^× is a norm group [5].*

The *Lubin–Tate construction* makes this isomorphism visible [1][5]. Fix a uniformizer *π* and choose a formal *O_K*-module *F_π* — a formal group law *F(X, Y) ∈ O_K[[X, Y]]* — such that the endomorphism *[π](T)* satisfies *[π](T) ≡ πT (mod T²)* and *[π](T) ≡ T^q (mod π)*. Let *Λ_{π,n} = ker([π^n] : **m**_{ar K} → **m**_{ar K})* be the *π^n*-torsion in the maximal ideal. Then:

- *K_n = K(Λ_{π,n})* is a totally ramified abelian extension of *K*,
- *Gal(K_n/K) ≅ (O_K / π^n)^× ≅ U_K / U_K^{(n)}* via the action on torsion points,
- the union *K_π = ∪_n K_n* is the maximal totally ramified abelian extension of *K*.

Adjoining also the maximal unramified extension *K^{nr}* — obtained by adjoining roots of unity of order prime to the residue characteristic — yields *K^ab = K^{nr} · K_π*, and the reciprocity map sends *π* to a Frobenius lift and units to the Galois action on torsion points [5]. This is *explicit* local class field theory in the strongest sense: generators and Galois action are written down.

### 4.2 Global reciprocity: idèles and the Artin map

For a number field *K*, the adèles *A_K* are the restricted direct product of the completions *K_v* with respect to the subrings *O_v*, and the idèles *I_K = A_K^×* form the restricted product of the *K_v^×* with respect to *O_v^×* [4]. The diagonal embedding *K^× ↪ I_K* has the *product formula* ∏_v |*a*|_v *= 1* as its shadow, and the quotient

$$C_K = I_K / K^\times$$

is the *idèle class group* — the single object that simultaneously generalizes the ideal class group and the connected components at infinity [1][2].

The global Artin map is assembled *locally*: for *L/K* finite abelian and *w | v*, the local Artin map *K_v^× → Gal(L_w/K_v)* lands in the decomposition group, and the product over *v* descends to *C_K* because *K^×* lies in the kernel (the product formula in disguise) [4].

> **Theorem:** (Global reciprocity law). *For L/K finite abelian, (·, L/K) : C_K → Gal(L/K) is surjective with kernel N_{L/K}(C_L), inducing C_K / N_{L/K}(C_L) ≅ Gal(L/K). The diagram relating the local and global maps commutes at every place [1][4].*

The *existence theorem* — due to Takagi in ideal-theoretic language — states that *every* open subgroup of finite index in *C_K* arises as a norm group *N_{L/K}(C_L)* for a unique finite abelian extension *L/K* [4]. Hence:

| Object on the field side | Object on the Galois side |
|---|---|
| Finite abelian extension *L/K* | Open subgroup *N_{L/K}(C_L) ⊆ C_K* of finite index |
| Compositum of extensions | Intersection of norm groups |
| Subextension *L/M/K* | Intermediate norm group |
| Unramified outside *S* | Subgroup containing the local units at *v ∉ S* |

This is the complete Galois correspondence for abelian extensions, and the idèlic language makes the ramification control transparent [2].

### 4.3 Takagi's Existenzsatz, conductors, and ray class fields

In Takagi's original formulation, the classifying objects are *congruence subgroups*: subgroups *H* with *P_{**m**,1} ⊆ H ⊆ I^**m*** for some modulus **m** [4]. The *Existenzsatz* asserts:

> **Theorem:** (Takagi, 1920). *For every congruence subgroup H there exists a unique finite abelian extension L/K — the class field of H — such that the Artin map induces an isomorphism I^**m** / H ≅ Gal(L/K), and every finite abelian extension arises this way [4].*

Two refinements are essential. First, the *conductor* **f***(L/K)*: the smallest modulus (by divisibility) for which the Artin map factors through *Cl_**m**(K)*; a prime ramifies in *L/K* if and only if it divides the conductor [4]. Second, the *ray class fields*: for each modulus **m**, the class field *K(**m**)* of the full congruence subgroup *P_{**m**,1}* satisfies *Gal(K(**m**)/K) ≅ Cl_**m**(K)*, and *L ⊆ K(**m**)* if and only if **f***(L/K)* divides **m** — so every finite abelian extension sits inside some ray class field [4].

The *conductor-discriminant formula* ties this to ramification quantitatively: the relative discriminant of *L/K* is the product over nontrivial characters *χ* of *Gal(L/K)* of the conductors **f***(χ)*, a far-reaching generalization of the classical discriminant formulas for cyclotomic fields [1].

### 4.4 The Hilbert class field and the principal ideal theorem

Taking the trivial modulus **m** *= 1* yields the most famous class field of all. The *Hilbert class field* *H(K)* is the maximal abelian extension of *K* unramified at *every* place (finite and infinite); completeness implies it is finite, and the Artin map gives the canonical isomorphism

$$Cl(K) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} Gal(H(K)/K),$$

so *[H(K) : K] = h_K* and a nonzero prime ideal **p** *is principal if and only if it splits completely in H(K)* [7].

The deepest classical fact about *H(K)* is the *principal ideal theorem* (*Hauptidealsatz*), conjectured by Hilbert in 1900 and proved by Furtwängler in 1930 after Artin reduced it to pure group theory [8]:

> **Theorem:** (Principal ideal theorem). *Let H(K) be the Hilbert class field of K. Then every fractional ideal of O_K extends to a principal ideal of O_{H(K)} — every ideal class capitulates in the Hilbert class field [7][8].*

> **Proof:** (Sketch, after Artin–Furtwängler). Let *M* be the Hilbert class field of *H(K)*; then *M/K* is Galois with *Gal(M/H(K))* equal to the commutator subgroup of *G = Gal(M/K)*. The Artin map identifies ideal classes of *K* with *G^{ab}*, and extension of ideals to *H(K)* with the *transfer* (Verlagerung) homomorphism *V : G → G^{ab}*. A group-theoretic lemma — the transfer from *G* to its abelianization along the commutator is trivial — forces every class into the kernel, i.e., every ideal becomes principal over *H(K)* [7][8]. ∎

Iterating the construction gives the *Hilbert class field tower* *K = K_0 ⊆ K_1 ⊆ K_2 ⊆ ⋯* with *K_{n+1} = H(K_n)*. Golod and Shafarevich showed this tower can be *infinite* — there exist number fields with infinite unramified extensions, necessarily non-abelian — so class field theory's finitenement stops exactly where non-abelian phenomena begin [1].

### 4.5 Explicit class field theory: **Q** and imaginary quadratic fields

*The rational field.* The Kronecker–Weber theorem states that ***Q**^ab = ∪_m **Q**(ζ_m)*, the maximal abelian extension of **Q** being generated by all roots of unity [6]. The Artin map *I_**m** / P_{**m**,1} → Gal(**Q**(ζ_m)/**Q**)* identifies the Galois group with *(**Z**/m**Z**)^×* by sending a prime *p ∤ m* to the Frobenius *ζ ↦ ζ^p* — this *is* the classical reciprocity law, and the idèlic picture collapses to the clean isomorphism *Ẑ^× ≅ Gal(**Q**^ab/**Q**)* [6][4]. Conceptually, the torsion of the multiplicative group scheme **G**_m plays the role for **Q** that Lubin–Tate torsion plays for local fields.

```haskell
-- Toy model: the Artin map on a ray class group over Q (illustrative only).
-- A prime p not dividing the modulus m maps to its Frobenius, p mod m.
type Modulus = Integer
type Prime   = Integer

artinMap :: Modulus -> [Prime] -> [(Prime, Integer)]
artinMap m ps = [(p, p `mod` m) | p <- ps, p `mod` m /= 0]
-- artinMap 7 [2,3,5,11] == [(2,2),(3,3),(5,5),(11,4)]
-- The kernel condition p `mod` m == 1 detects splitting in Q(zeta_m).
```

*Imaginary quadratic fields.* Kronecker's *Jugendtraum* — his "dream of youth" — asked for an analogous analytic generation of abelian extensions of imaginary quadratic fields [3]. The answer is the theory of *complex multiplication*. Let *K* be imaginary quadratic and *E/**C*** an elliptic curve with *End(E) ≅ O_K*. Then:

- the *j*-invariant *j(E)* is an algebraic integer generating the Hilbert class field *H(K)* over *K* (a *singular modulus*);
- adjoining the coordinates of torsion points — values of Weber functions — generates the ray class fields, and the *main theorem of complex multiplication* gives *K^ab = K(j(E), E_{tors})* [1].

Thus the two solved cases of explicit class field theory share one pattern: *the maximal abelian extension is generated by the torsion of a one-dimensional algebraic group* (**G**_m for **Q**, a CM elliptic curve for imaginary quadratic *K*, a Lubin–Tate formal group locally). Hilbert's twelfth problem asks for the general analytic object; for real quadratic fields no such construction is known, and the Stark conjectures predict that special values of *L*-functions should supply the missing units [1][3].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

We illustrate the theory over *K = **Q**(√−5)*, whose class number is *2*, the nontrivial class represented by **p** *= (2, 1 + √−5)* [7].

**Claim.** The Hilbert class field of *K* is *H = K(i) = **Q**(√−5, i)*.

> **Proof:** *H/K* is clearly abelian of degree *2*. Now ***Q**(i)/**Q*** ramifies only at *2*, so *H/K* can ramify only at the unique prime **p** above *2*. The element *α = (1 + √−5)/2* satisfies *x² − x − 1 = 0* over *K*, hence lies in *O_H*; reducing modulo **p** gives *O_H/**p**O_H ≅ **F**_4*, a degree-*2* residue field extension, so **p** is *inert*, hence *unramified*, in *H/K*. An unramified abelian extension of degree *h_K = 2* must be the Hilbert class field [7]. ∎

Consequences are immediate from the Artin isomorphism *Cl(K) ≅ Gal(H/K)* [7]:

1. The nonprincipal prime **p** has nontrivial Frobenius, so it stays inert in *H/K* — consistent with **p** being nonprincipal, since principal primes split completely.
2. By the principal ideal theorem, **p**·*O_H* is principal; indeed **p**·*O_H = ((1 + i)/2)* up to a unit, exhibiting *capitulation* concretely [8].
3. The Artin map sends the idèle that is a uniformizer at **p** and *1* elsewhere to the nontrivial element of *Gal(H/K)* — reciprocity made computational.

A short SageMath session verifies the class group input to this picture:

```python
# SageMath: class group of Q(sqrt(-5)) and its Hilbert class polynomial data
K.<a> = NumberField(x^2 + 5)
Cl = K.class_group()
print("class number:", Cl.order())          # 2
print("class group:", Cl.invariants())     # (2,)
# The nontrivial class is represented by a prime above 2:
p = K.ideal(2, 1 + a)
print("p is principal?", p.is_principal())  # False
print("p^2 is principal?", (p^2).is_principal())  # True -- class has order 2
```

Finally, the Kronecker–Weber theorem itself drops out of Artin reciprocity: if *L/**Q*** is finite abelian, reciprocity provides a modulus **m** with *P_{**m**,1} ⊆ ker(Φ_{L/**Q**})*; the cyclotomic field ***Q**(ζ_m)* has Artin kernel *exactly P_{**m**,1}*, so the inclusion of kernels forces *L ⊆ **Q**(ζ_m)* [6]. The abstract reciprocity law thus *proves* the concrete generation by roots of unity — classification implying construction.

The Chebotarev density theorem crowns the picture: Frobenius elements are equidistributed in *Gal(L/K)*, so every splitting type permitted by the Galois group actually occurs for a positive density of primes — the ultimate quantitative form of reciprocity [1].

## 6 Limitations

Class field theory is complete only within its natural boundary — *abelian* extensions — and several frontiers remain [1][3]:

- **Non-abelian extensions.** No general classification exists. The Langlands program proposes that *n*-dimensional Galois representations correspond to automorphic representations of *GL(n)*; class field theory is precisely the *n = 1* case, where characters of *Gal(K^ab/K)* match Hecke characters of *C_K* [1].
- **Infinite class field towers.** The Golod–Shafarevich theorem shows the Hilbert class field tower need not terminate, so unramified extensions can be infinite and wildly non-abelian; finiteness is a genuinely abelian phenomenon.
- **Explicit constructions.** Beyond **Q**, imaginary quadratic fields, and CM fields — and function fields, where Drinfeld modules give an explicit theory — Hilbert's twelfth problem is open. For real quadratic fields, not even a conjectural analytic generator is known; the Stark conjectures predict special *L*-values produce the required units, but this remains unproved in general [3].
- **Effectivity.** The cohomological proofs are inexplicit: they prove a class field *exists* without producing equations for it. Computational class field theory (Kummer theory, complex multiplication, Stark units) partially compensates, but provably correct algorithms with good complexity remain an active research area.
- **Higher dimensions.** Higher class field theory (Kato–Saito, using Milnor *K*-theory and higher idèles) extends reciprocity to arithmetic schemes, at the cost of considerable technical weight [1].

## 7 Conclusion

From Hilbert's conjectural *Klassenkörper* to Takagi's existence theorem, Artin's canonical reciprocity law, Chevalley's idèles, and the Lubin–Tate explicit local theory, class field theory transformed reciprocity from a computational curiosity about prime residues into a structural isomorphism between arithmetic and Galois theory [1][3]. Its central mantra — *abelian extensions are classified by class groups, and Frobenius elements are the dictionary* — unifies quadratic reciprocity, cyclotomy, and complex multiplication in a single framework [4][6].

Yet the theory is as notable for what it leaves open as for what it closes. The non-abelian world awaits the Langlands program; explicit generation awaits a solution of Hilbert's twelfth problem; and the Stark conjectures promise that *L*-functions still hold secrets about units and class fields [3]. Class field theory is therefore both a finished cathedral and a foundation: the one fully understood case of a far larger, still conjectural, reciprocity.
