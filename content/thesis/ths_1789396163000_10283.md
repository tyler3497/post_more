# Knot Invariants from the Jones Polynomial to Khovanov Homology: Categorification and Quantum Topology

## Abstract

From Vaughan Jones's 1984 discovery of a new knot polynomial to Mikhail Khovanov's 2000 categorification, quantum topology has been driven by one question: how much geometry can algebra see? This thesis surveys the arc from the Jones polynomial to Khovanov homology. We develop the Kauffman bracket state-sum model and its normalization by writhe, the Temperley-Lieb algebra and Markov-trace route to the Jones polynomial, and its incarnations in quantum groups and Chern-Simons topological quantum field theory. We then construct Khovanov's bigraded chain complex from the cube of resolutions, establish invariance under Reidemeister moves, and show how its graded Euler characteristic recovers the Jones polynomial. Finally we treat Lee's deformation, Rasmussen's s-invariant, its lower bound on the smooth slice genus, the combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture, and the Ozsvath-Szabo spectral sequence linking Khovanov homology to Heegaard Floer theory.

## 1 Intro

In 1984, Vaughan Jones, working in the theory of von Neumann algebras and subfactors, stumbled upon a polynomial invariant of knots that no one had asked for. The *Jones polynomial* $V_K(t)$, a Laurent polynomial in $t^{1/2}$ with integer coefficients, distinguished knots that classical invariants could not and behaved unlike anything in the classical toolkit: it came from operator algebras, not from the fundamental group of the knot complement or from Seifert surfaces [3]. For this body of work Jones received the Fields Medal in 1990, and knot theory was permanently altered.

The shock was productive. Within a few years, Louis Kauffman had given the polynomial a purely combinatorial state-sum formulation through the *bracket polynomial*; Edward Witten had reinterpreted it as the expectation value of a Wilson loop in three-dimensional Chern-Simons gauge theory; and the representation theory of quantum groups $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ had supplied an algebraic machine, the Reshetikhin-Turaev construction, that placed the Jones polynomial inside a vast family of *quantum invariants*.

Yet the Jones polynomial has notorious defects. It is not known whether it detects the unknot. It cannot distinguish certain pairs of knots that intuition insists are different. And as a polynomial, it is a rather crude shadow: an integer linear combination of monomials cannot record the cancellations that produced it.

The remedy arrived in 1999-2000, when Mikhail Khovanov constructed a *bigraded homology theory* of links whose graded Euler characteristic is the (unnormalized) Jones polynomial [1]. This is *categorification*: replacing a polynomial by a chain complex, numbers by vector spaces, and the evaluation of the polynomial by the Euler characteristic of homology. Khovanov homology is strictly stronger than the Jones polynomial, it detects the unknot (a theorem of Kronheimer and Mrowka), and a deformation of it due to Eun Soo Lee yields Rasmussen's $s$-invariant, a concordance homomorphism giving lower bounds on the smooth slice genus and a combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture [8].

This thesis traces that entire arc. Section 2 recalls the classical setting: diagrams, Reidemeister moves, writhe, and skein relations. Section 3 develops the methodology: the Kauffman bracket state sum and its normalization. Section 4 dives deep into the Temperley-Lieb algebra, quantum groups and Chern-Simons theory, the Khovanov complex itself, Lee homology and the $s$-invariant, and the Heegaard Floer connections. Section 5 works through concrete computations and proofs. Section 6 is honest about limitations, and Section 7 concludes.

## 2 Background

A *knot* is an embedding of the circle $S^1$ into the three-sphere $S^3$, considered up to ambient isotopy. A *link* allows several components. We study knots through their *diagrams*: generic planar projections with over/under data recorded at each double point, called a *crossing*.

> **Theorem:** Two link diagrams represent the same link if and only if they are related by a finite sequence of planar isotopies and the three *Reidemeister moves*.

The moves are: Type I, introducing or removing a single twist (a kink); Type II, sliding one strand over another to create or cancel a pair of crossings; Type III, sliding a strand past a crossing (the triangle move). Any quantity computed from a diagram that is unchanged by all three moves is a *link invariant*.

Each crossing in an oriented diagram carries a sign: a *positive* crossing contributes $+1$ and a *negative* crossing contributes $-1$, following the right-hand rule. The *writhe* $w(D)$ of a diagram $D$ is the sum of these signs. Writhe is not a knot invariant (a Type I move changes it by $\pm 1$), but it is the bookkeeping device that repairs the one defect of the Kauffman bracket.

Classically, knot theorists had the Alexander polynomial (1928), the knot group, the genus, and the signature. The Alexander polynomial satisfies a skein relation and is comparatively computable, but it fails to distinguish a knot from its mirror image in many cases and misses much of the new structure. The Jones polynomial's defining skein relation is

$$t^{-1}V(L_+) - t\,V(L_-) = (t^{1/2} - t^{-1/2})\,V(L_0),$$

where $L_+$, $L_-$, and $L_0$ are three oriented links identical outside a small ball, where they exhibit a positive crossing, a negative crossing, and the oriented smoothing, respectively, with $V(\text{unknot}) = 1$ [3]. This relation determines $V$ recursively, but naive recursion is exponential in the crossing number, and the relation itself gives no hint of the operator-algebraic origin of the invariant.

## 3 Methodology

Our methodology is *skein-theoretic and state-sum based*, following Kauffman [2]. Rather than invoking von Neumann algebras, we compute the Jones polynomial from a diagram by resolving every crossing in two ways and summing over all resulting states.

### The Kauffman bracket

Let $D$ be an unoriented link diagram. The *Kauffman bracket* $\langle D \rangle$ is the Laurent polynomial in $A$ determined by three axioms [3]:

1. **Normalization:** $\langle \bigcirc \rangle = 1$ for the unknot diagram with no crossings.
2. **Skein relation:** at each crossing,
$$\langle \text{crossing} \rangle = A\,\langle \text{0-smoothing} \rangle + A^{-1}\,\langle \text{1-smoothing} \rangle,$$
where the two smoothings reconnect the four incident arcs in the two possible planar ways.
3. **Disjoint union:** $\langle \bigcirc \sqcup D \rangle = (-A^2 - A^{-2})\,\langle D \rangle$.

A *state* of $D$ is a choice of smoothing at every crossing. If $D$ has $n$ crossings, there are $2^n$ states; a state $s$ with $a(s)$ zero-smoothings, $b(s)$ one-smoothings, and $|s|$ closed circles contributes $A^{a(s)-b(s)}(-A^2-A^{-2})^{|s|-1}$, and the bracket is the sum over all states. This is the *state-sum formula*.

The bracket is invariant under Reidemeister moves II and III (regular isotopy), but a Type I kink multiplies it by $-A^{\pm 3}$. The fix uses the writhe: for an oriented diagram $D$ with writhe $w(D)$,

$$X(D) = (-A^3)^{-w(D)}\,\langle D \rangle$$

is invariant under all three Reidemeister moves. Substituting $A = t^{-1/4}$ yields the Jones polynomial $V_L(t)$ [3].

### The Khovanov upgrade

Categorification replaces each state in the bracket sum by a graded vector space and each adjacency between states by a linear map, assembling the *cube of resolutions* into a chain complex. The Euler characteristic of that complex reproduces the state sum; its homology is the new invariant [1]. The method throughout is therefore uniform: **states, gradings, differentials, invariance via Reidemeister moves, and decategorification by Euler characteristic**.

The following Python sketch implements the bracket state sum directly, using union-find to count circles in each resolved state:

```python
from itertools import product

class UnionFind:
    def __init__(self, n):
        self.p = list(range(n))
    def find(self, x):
        while self.p[x] != x:
            self.p[x] = self.p[self.p[x]]
            x = self.p[x]
        return x
    def union(self, x, y):
        self.p[self.find(x)] = self.find(y)

def kauffman_bracket(crossings):
    # crossings: list of (sign, a, b, c, d); arcs labeled 0..2n-1,
    # crossing i uses arcs (4i, 4i+1) as the over-strand pair.
    n = len(crossings)
    d = {2: 'd'}  # placeholder for the loop value
    terms = {}
    for state in product([0, 1], repeat=n):
        uf = UnionFind(4 * n)
        exp = 0
        for i, s in enumerate(state):
            a, b, c, e = 4*i, 4*i+1, 4*i+2, 4*i+3
            if s == 0:  # A-smoothing
                uf.union(a, e); uf.union(b, c); exp += 1
            else:       # B-smoothing
                uf.union(a, b); uf.union(c, e); exp -= 1
        circles = len({uf.find(x) for x in range(4*n)}) // 2
        # state value: A^exp * (-A^2 - A^-2)^(circles - 1)
        terms[(exp, circles)] = terms.get((exp, circles), 0) + 1
    return terms

def writhe(crossings):
    return sum(s for s, *_ in crossings)
```

The dictionary counts states by their $A$-exponent and circle number; expanding $(-A^2-A^{-2})^{|s|-1}$ and multiplying by $(-A^3)^{-w}$ gives the normalized invariant.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 The Temperley-Lieb algebra and the braid group

Jones's original route to his polynomial ran through the *Temperley-Lieb algebra* $TL_n(\delta)$. This algebra, first introduced in statistical mechanics, is generated by idempotent-like elements $e_1, \dots, e_{n-1}$ with relations

$$e_i^2 = \delta\, e_i, \qquad e_i e_{i\pm 1} e_i = e_i, \qquad e_i e_j = e_j e_i \;\; \text{for } |i-j| \ge 2.$$

Diagrammatically, $e_i$ is the planar tangle that cups strands $i$ and $i+1$ at top and bottom; multiplication is vertical stacking, and a closed loop evaluates to $\delta$. Jones discovered representations of the braid group $B_n$ inside $TL_n(\delta)$ by sending the Artin generator $\sigma_i$ to $A \cdot 1 + A^{-1} e_i$; the braid relation $\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$ holds precisely when $\delta = -A^2 - A^{-2}$, the same loop value appearing in the bracket axioms. A *Markov trace* on the tower of Temperley-Lieb algebras, invariant under the two Markov moves (conjugation and stabilization), then descends to a link invariant: closing a braid and taking the trace reproduces the Kauffman bracket, and hence the Jones polynomial [2].

This is the conceptual core of the subject: *the Jones polynomial is the Markov trace of a braid representation built from a planar diagram algebra*. The Temperley-Lieb algebra is the $n=2$ case of a much larger story, the *planar algebras* and *subfactor* theory from which Jones began, and it is also the representation-theoretic shadow of the quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at generic $q$.

### 4.2 Quantum groups and Chern-Simons theory

The Reshetikhin-Turaev construction rebuilds the Jones polynomial from the representation theory of the *quantum group* $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, a Hopf-algebra deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. The universal $R$-matrix of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation, which is the algebraic form of the third Reidemeister move; the ribbon element supplies the framing correction analogous to the writhe normalization. Coloring link components by finite-dimensional representations yields the *colored Jones polynomials*, a tower of invariants of which the ordinary Jones polynomial is the two-dimensional case [4].

Witten gave this algebraic picture a physical origin in 1989: the Jones polynomial is the vacuum expectation value of a *Wilson loop* observable in $SU(2)$ Chern-Simons gauge theory on $S^3$ at level $k$, with the deformation parameter identified as $q = \exp(2\pi i/(k+2))$. In this *topological quantum field theory*, the path integral over connections localizes to topological data, and the framing anomaly of the Wilson line reproduces the writhe factor $(-A^3)^{-w}$. The Chern-Simons viewpoint predicts that the Jones polynomial should extend to a full TQFT assigning vector spaces to surfaces and linear maps to cobordisms, a prediction that Khovanov homology partially fulfills on the nose: link cobordisms induce maps on Khovanov homology, functorially up to sign [2].

### 4.3 Categorification: the Khovanov complex

Let $D$ be an oriented diagram with $n$ crossings, $n_+$ positive and $n_-$ negative. For each vertex $\alpha$ of the cube $\{0,1\}^n$, let $D_\alpha$ be the corresponding complete smoothing, with $k(\alpha)$ circles. Khovanov assigns to $\alpha$ the graded vector space

$$V_\alpha = V^{\otimes k(\alpha)}\{|\alpha|\},$$

where $V$ is the two-dimensional graded vector space with basis $v_+, v_-$ in quantum degrees $+1$ and $-1$ (so $\operatorname{qdim} V = q + q^{-1}$), and $\{l\}$ denotes a quantum-degree shift [1]. The $r$-th chain group is

$$C^r(D) = \bigoplus_{|\alpha| = r} V_\alpha,$$

with an overall homological shift by $-n_-$ and quantum shift by $n_+ - 2n_-$. Edges of the cube correspond to changing one smoothing; each edge merges two circles or splits one circle. The differential is built from the multiplication and comultiplication of the Frobenius algebra $\mathcal{A} = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2)$:

$$m(v_+ \otimes v_+) = v_+, \quad m(v_+ \otimes v_-) = m(v_- \otimes v_+) = v_-, \quad m(v_- \otimes v_-) = 0,$$
$$\Delta(v_+) = v_+ \otimes v_- + v_- \otimes v_+, \quad \Delta(v_-) = v_- \otimes v_-,$$

with signs on cube edges chosen so that every square anticommutes, making $d^2 = 0$ [1]. In Haskell-like notation, the Frobenius structure is:

```haskell
data Gen = Vp | Vm                      -- v_+, v_- : quantum degrees +1, -1
mult :: (Gen, Gen) -> [(Gen, Int)]      -- merge map m
mult (Vp, Vp) = [(Vp, 1)]
mult (Vp, Vm) = [(Vm, 1)]
mult (Vm, Vp) = [(Vm, 1)]
mult (Vm, Vm) = []
comult :: Gen -> [((Gen, Gen), Int)]    -- split map Delta
comult Vp = [((Vp, Vm), 1), ((Vm, Vp), 1)]
comult Vm = [((Vm, Vm), 1)]
```

> **Theorem (Khovanov):** The homotopy type of the complex $C(D)$ depends only on the isotopy class of the oriented link. Its homology $Kh(L)$, bigraded by homological degree $i$ and quantum degree $j$, is a link invariant whose graded Euler characteristic is the unnormalized Jones polynomial:
$$V(L)\big|_{\sqrt{t} = -q} = \frac{\chi_q(C(D))}{q + q^{-1}}.$$

Invariance is proved by exhibiting explicit chain homotopy equivalences for each Reidemeister move [1]. The invariant is *strictly stronger* than the Jones polynomial: Bar-Natan found pairs of knots with identical Jones polynomials but distinct Khovanov homology, and Kronheimer and Mrowka proved that Khovanov homology detects the unknot, the unique knot of rank one, while Jones detection of the unknot remains open [5].

### 4.4 Lee homology and Rasmussen's s-invariant

In 2005, Eun Soo Lee deformed Khovanov's differential by adding terms of higher quantum degree, producing a filtered complex whose associated spectral sequence starts at Khovanov homology and collapses to a tiny limit: for a knot, *Lee homology* is two-dimensional, spanned by canonical generators $\mathfrak{s}_a, \mathfrak{s}_b$ built from the two orientations of the Seifert smoothing [8]. Rasmussen extracted from their filtration degrees a numerical invariant

$$s(K) = \frac{q(\mathfrak{s}_a) + q(\mathfrak{s}_b)}{2},$$

an even integer with remarkable properties:

1. **Concordance homomorphism:** $s$ descends to a homomorphism from the smooth knot concordance group to $\mathbb{Z}$; in particular $s(\bar{K}) = -s(K)$ for the mirror.
2. **Slice genus bound:** $|s(K)| \le 2\,g_4(K)$, where $g_4$ is the smooth slice genus, the minimal genus of a smoothly embedded surface in the 4-ball bounded by $K$.
3. **Torus knots:** $s(T(p,q)) = (p-1)(q-1)$ for the positive torus knot.

Property 3 combined with property 2 gives $g_4(T(p,q)) \ge (p-1)(q-1)/2$, and since an explicit Seifert surface realizes equality, this is a *purely combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture*, first proved by Kronheimer and Mrowka using gauge theory [8]. The $s$-invariant also obstructs sliceness: it shows the existence of topologically slice knots that are not smoothly slice, giving a gauge-theory-free proof of exotic smooth structures on $\mathbb{R}^4$ [6].

### 4.5 Heegaard Floer connections

Khovanov homology is not isolated. Ozsvath and Szabo constructed a spectral sequence whose $E^2$ page is the *reduced* Khovanov homology of the mirror link $\bar{L}$ and which converges to the Heegaard Floer homology $\widehat{HF}$ of the branched double cover $\Sigma(S^3, L)$ [4]. This was the first structural bridge between the combinatorial world of Khovanov homology and the holomorphic-curve world of Heegaard Floer theory.

The parallel runs deeper. Just as Khovanov homology categorifies the Jones polynomial, *knot Floer homology* $\widehat{HFK}$ categorifies the Alexander polynomial. And just as Lee's deformation yields the concordance invariant $s$ with $|s(K)| \le 2g_4(K)$, the knot Floer filtration yields Ozsvath-Szabo's concordance invariant $\tau$ with $|\tau(K)| \le g_4(K)$. On quasi-alternating knots both theories collapse to classical data, and for many small knots $s(K) = 2\tau(K)$, though the invariants differ in general. The conjectured *spectral sequence from Khovanov homology to knot Floer homology* remains one of the central open problems relating the two categorifications [4].

## 5 Empirical/Proofs

### Worked computation: the Hopf link

We verify the bracket machinery on the Hopf link $H$ with its standard diagram of two positive crossings ($w = 2$). Resolving both crossings gives four states; collecting terms,

$$\langle H \rangle = -A^4 - A^{-4},$$

a standard hand computation [2]. Normalizing,

$$X(H) = (-A^3)^{-2}(-A^4 - A^{-4}) = A^{-6}(-A^4 - A^{-4}) = -A^{-2} - A^{-10}.$$

Substituting $A = t^{-1/4}$,

$$V_H(t) = -t^{1/2} - t^{5/2},$$

the classical Jones polynomial of the Hopf link, confirming that the state-sum pipeline reproduces the tabulated values.

### Tabulated Jones polynomials

| Knot / Link | Jones polynomial $V(t)$ |
|---|---|
| Unknot | $1$ |
| Right-handed trefoil $3_1$ | $t + t^3 - t^4$ |
| Figure-eight $4_1$ | $t^2 - t + 1 - t^{-1} + t^{-2}$ |
| Hopf link | $-t^{1/2} - t^{5/2}$ |
| Cinquefoil $5_1$ | $t^2 + t^4 - t^5 + t^6 - t^7$ |

Each entry follows from the skein relation by resolving crossings inductively; the trefoil value uses the right-handed diagram with writhe $+3$ [3].

### The s-invariant in action

For the torus knot $T(3,4)$, Rasmussen's formula gives $s(T(3,4)) = (3-1)(4-1) = 6$. The slice bound $|s(K)| \le 2g_4(K)$ forces $g_4(T(3,4)) \ge 3$, and the standard Seifert surface of genus $3$ shows equality. Hence $g_4(T(3,4)) = 3$, a case of the Milnor conjecture obtained with no analysis, only the combinatorics of the Lee spectral sequence [8].

### Detection results

The following are established by direct computation of the Khovanov complex or by structural theorems:

- **Unknot:** detected by Khovanov homology (Kronheimer-Mrowka); detection by the Jones polynomial is open [5].
- **Unlinks, Hopf links, trefoils, figure-eight:** each uniquely detected by Khovanov homology among all links/knots (Hedden-Ni; Baldwin et al.; Baldwin-Sivek) [5].
- **Strict strength:** there exist pairs of knots with identical Jones polynomials and non-isomorphic Khovanov homology (Bar-Natan), so no polynomial can recover the homological information [1].

## 6 Limitations

**Computational complexity.** The Khovanov complex has $2^n$ vertices for an $n$-crossing diagram, and the chain groups grow exponentially; practical computation stalls around 50-60 crossings even with the spanning-tree simplifications and Bar-Natan's divide-and-conquer delooping. The Jones polynomial itself is $\#P$-hard to compute exactly, so no categorification can escape exponential worst-case behavior.

**Torsion and integral subtleties.** Over $\mathbb{Z}$, Khovanov homology contains torsion of mysterious origin; the *odd* Khovanov homology of Ozsvath, Rasmussen, and Szabo is a genuinely different theory with the same Euler characteristic, and the relationship between even and odd theories is incompletely understood.

**Geometric opacity.** Despite the Chern-Simons motivation, there is no known direct geometric construction of Khovanov homology from gauge theory; the conjectured spectral sequence to knot Floer homology is open, and the four-dimensional meaning of most Khovanov classes is unknown.

**Invariance gaps.** The Jones polynomial is not known to detect the unknot. Mutation invariance of Khovanov homology is subtle: it holds for component-preserving mutation but the general case required deep work. The $s$-invariant and $\tau$-invariant agree on many knots but differ in general, and neither is known to be a complete concordance invariant.

**Scope.** Everything here concerns links in $S^3$. Extensions to other three-manifolds, virtual knots, and higher-rank $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ homologies (Khovanov-Rozansky) exist but bring their own formidable technical overhead.

## 7 Conclusion

The journey from the Jones polynomial to Khovanov homology is the story of a single idea refined across four decades: *topological information can be extracted from planar diagrams by linear algebra over states*. The Kauffman bracket turned Jones's operator-algebraic invariant into a state sum; the Temperley-Lieb algebra revealed it as a Markov trace; quantum groups and Chern-Simons theory gave it physical meaning; and Khovanov's categorification replaced the sum by a complex, the polynomial by homology, and mere distinction of knots by a functorial theory with four-dimensional applications.

The $s$-invariant's combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture is the clearest demonstration that categorification is not abstraction for its own sake: it proves hard geometric theorems. The open problems, from the Jones unknot-detection question to the conjectured Khovanov-to-Floer spectral sequence, suggest that the categorified world still holds most of its secrets. If the last forty years are any guide, the next invariant will again come from taking something we thought we understood and asking what it is the shadow of.
