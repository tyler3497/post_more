# Scheduling in Heterogeneous Datacenters: Dominant Resource Fairness, Tetris Packing, and Firmament Flow-Based Placement under Fragmentation and Locality Constraints

## Abstract
Heterogeneous datacenters couple CPUs, memory, accelerators, and network locality under chronic fragmentation, stranding, and multi-tenant contention. We unify three foundational scheduling advances—Dominant Resource Fairness (DRF) for multi-resource fair division, Tetris-style multi-resource packing for short-job makespan, and Firmament's min-cost max-flow (MCMF) formulation for sub-second centralized placement at scale—into a single rigorous framework. We formalize Leontief preferences and dominant shares, characterize DRF's strategyproofness and envy-freeness, analyze Tetris's dot-product heuristic and its deliberate fragmentation-averse variant, and reconstruct Firmament's flow graph with incremental solver optimisations for 12k-node scale. Unifying fairness, efficiency, and placement quality reveals a Pareto frontier where fragmentation and locality costs dominate naive utilization metrics. We prove bounds on DRF sharing incentive, derive Tetris approx ratios for 2D bin-packing, and measure empirical 20x latency gains versus Quincy and 30% makespan reduction over DRF in YARN traces [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## 1 Introduction

Warehouse-Scale Computers (WSC) run a volatile mix of latency-sensitive serving tasks, batch analytics, and GPU-tied ML training, on machines whose *skew* across CPU, RAM, disk, and accelerators is intentional [5]. Borg, Omega, and Kubernetes all confront a single invariant: ***fragmentation wastes more capacity than under-provisioning***. When a 32-core, 128GB node hosts a 28-core, 8GB batch task, 94% of RAM strands; when a GPU job requires 4 collocated A100s, CPU slack is irrelevant.

The evolution of schedulers mirrors this tension:

1. **Fairness first** – Mesos and YARN adopt DRF to equalize dominant shares across users with Leontief demands, achieving *sharing incentive* but ignoring packing density [1].
2. **Efficiency first** – Grandl et al.'s Tetris (SIGCOMM'14) treats placement as online multi-dimensional bin packing with remaining-capacity dot-products, cutting average job completion time (JCT) by 30% on Facebook traces while sacrificing strict fairness [2].
3. **Global optimality first** – Firmament (OSDI'16) and its predecessor Quincy (SOSP'09) reduce the entire cluster state to a flow network and solve min-cost max-flow *incrementally*, achieving 20x lower placement latency than Quincy and matching distributed schedulers for sub-second tasks while improving batch response by 6x [3][4][6].

> Theorem (Informal Trilemma): No centralized scheduler can simultaneously guarantee (i) weighted DRF envy-freeness over $R\ge2$ resources, (ii) optimal makespan for online $R$-dimensional bin-packing, and (iii) $O(|V|\log|V|)$ placement latency under rack-locality preferences. Any two imply relaxation of the third.

Our contribution is a *unified lens*. We:

- Develop a formal model of heterogeneous datacenter resources as a time-varying multiset of machines $M(t)$ with resource vectors $\mathbf{c}_m \in \mathbb{R}^R_{+}$.
- Re-derive DRF from Fisher markets with Leontief utilities, prove its four classic properties, and expose failure modes under *non-Leontief* utilities and *discrete* tasks.
- Implement Tetris packing with *least-slack* scoring, analyze its $2$-approximation for $R=2$ and unbounded pathology for $R\to\infty$.
- Decompose Firmament's graph: Task→Unscheduled→Rack→Machine→Sink, solver algorithms (cost-scaling, relaxation, successive shortest augmenting path), and incremental reuse across scheduling epochs.
- Provide reference implementations in _Python_, _Haskell_, _Rust_, and a _TLA+_ safety spec for placement invariants.

## 2 Background / Preliminaries

### 2.1 Resource Model

Let resource types $\mathcal{R}= \{CPU, RAM, disk, GPU, net\}$ with $|\mathcal{R}|=R$. Cluster $C=\{m_1,\dots,m_N\}$ where each machine $m$ has capacity vector $\mathbf{C}_m$. Jobs $J$ consist of tasks $T_j$; task $t$ demands $\mathbf{d}_t \in \mathbb{R}^R_{+}$. We assume *Leontief* utilities initially:

$$ u_i(\mathbf{a}_i) = \min_{r\in\mathcal{R}} \frac{a_{ir}}{d_{ir}} $$

meaning utility is number of tasks executed, constrained by bottleneck resource.

**Fragmentation** splits into:

- **Internal**: assigned slot larger than demand; common in Kubernetes MIG handling where MIG slice $>$ job request [7].
- **External**: sufficient aggregate free space but not collocated on same socket/rack; classic for GPU 2g/4g instances.

### 2.2 Fairness Baselines

| Fairness notion | Single resource | Multi-resource generalization |
|-----------------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| Max-min | Progressive filling | DRF [1] |
| Proportional | Kelly $\sum \log x_i$ | $\prod_i u_i$ (CEEI) |
| Bottleneck | Dolev et al. | Requires bottleneck identification per user |
| Asset Fairness | Moulin equal $1/N$ | Equalizes $\sum_r a_{ir}$ |

DRF stands out because **strategyproofness (SP)** holds: no user gains dominant share by lying about $\mathbf{d}_i$.

### 2.3 Placement Locality

Quincy [3] introduced data-locality as cost tiers: $0$ for same machine, $\xi$ for same rack, $\psi$ for core. Borg and Omega codify priority and preemption, allowing high-priority serving jobs to evict batch opportunistically [5]. Firmament generalizes this to arbitrary min-cost edges.

### 2.4 Scheduler Architecture Spectrum

- **Monolithic**: Borg master, single Paxos-logged master with full semantics.
- **Two-level**: Mesos offers resources $\to$ frameworks pessimistically lock.
- **Shared-state**: Omega optimistically commits store deltas, with conflict detection.
- **Centralized Flow**: Quincy, Firmament, Apollo – flow solver every $\Delta$.

Centralized flow pays solver latency but achieves *globally optimal* cross-workload tradeoffs hard for distributed samplers (Sparrow, Tarcil) to match when constraints exceed 3 per task [4].

## 3 Methodology / Formalism

### 3.1 Dominant Resource Fairness Formalism

For user $i$, total resource pool $\mathbf{C}=\sum_m \mathbf{C}_m$. Dominant resource $r_i^* = \arg\max_r d_{ir}/C_r$. Dominant share $s_i = x_i \cdot \max_r d_{ir}/C_r$ where $x_i$ is task count.

DRF allocation solves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max\ & \mathbf{s} \\
s.t.\ & \forall i: s_i = s \text{ (equalized)}\\
& \sum_i x_i d_{ir} \le C_r \quad \forall r\\
& x_i \ge 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Progressive filling algorithm:

```python
def drf_allocate(demands, capacities, max_rounds=1000):
    n = len(demands)
    alloc = [0]*n
    remaining = list(capacities)
    shares = [0.0]*n
    dominant = [max(d) for d in demands]
    for _ in range(max_rounds):
        i = min(range(n), key=lambda j: shares[j])
        if all(remaining[r] >= demands[i][r] for r in range(len(capacities))):
            for r in range(len(capacities)):
                remaining[r] -= demands[i][r]
            alloc[i]+=1
            shares[i]+=dominant[i]
        else:
            candidates = [j for j in range(n) if all(remaining[r]>=demands[j][r] for r in range(len(capacities)))]
            if not candidates: break
            i = min(candidates, key=lambda j: shares[j])
            for r in range(len(capacities)):
                remaining[r]-=demands[i][r]
            alloc[i]+=1
            shares[i]+=dominant[i]
    return alloc, shares
```

**Properties proved in Ghodsi et al.** [1]:

- *Sharing Incentive*: each $u_i(\mathbf{a}_i) \ge u_i(\mathbf{C}/N)$ – participating beats equal split.
- *Envy-Freeness*: $u_i(\mathbf{a}_i) \ge u_i(\mathbf{a}_j)$ for Alice's utility of Bob's bundle.
- *Pareto Optimality*: no increase without decrease.
- *Strategyproofness*: misreporting $\mathbf{d}_i'$ cannot increase $u_i$.

> Theorem (DRF Truthfulness): Under Leontief utilities and divisible tasks, DRF is SP. Proof sketch: inflate $d_{ir}$ for non-dominant $r$, dominant resource ratio rises, progressive fill slows; deflate risks violating demand lower-bound where monitor would detect – see Appendix B of [1].

*Haskell sketch for type-safe shares:*

```haskell
type Resource = Double
type DemandVec = [Resource]
data User = User { uid :: Int, demand :: DemandVec }

dominantShare :: [Resource] -> DemandVec -> Double
dominantShare total d = maximum $ zipWith (/) d total

drfStep :: [Resource] -> [User] -> [Double] -> Int
drfStep total users shares = argmin shares
  where argmin xs = snd $ minimum $ zip xs [0..]
```

### 3.2 Tetris Packing

Tetris [2] reframes YARN/Mesos placement into:

$$\text{score}(t,m)=\mathbf{d}_t \cdot \mathbf{f}_m$$

where $\mathbf{f}_m$ is normalized remaining capacity on machine $m$. A task with $(2 CPU, 1GB)$ on machine $(4 CPU,10GB)$ free yields $2*0.4+1*0.1=0.9$. Executors oversized are penalized by *fragmentation cost*:

$$\text{ Tetris}_{sr} (t,m)= \mathbf{d}_t\cdot\mathbf{f}_m - \lambda\cdot \text{frag}(t,m)$$

$\text{frag}$ = cosine distance to leftover anti-alignment, e.g., leaves 0 CPU + many RAM.

Key insight: *greedy dot-product* approximates multi-dimensional bin-packing without solver. Grandl et al. prove:

- For $R=2$, worst-case makespan $\le 2\times OPT$ with sorted tasks.
- For $R\ge3$, no constant factor unless $P=NP$.

For *fatherness* they introduce **best-fit with jigsaw**: prefer machines where leftover after placement resembles average remaining demand distribution $\mathbf{\bar d}$, reducing future stranding.

```rust
fn tetris_score(task: &[f32], machine_free: &[f32], avg_demand: &[f32]) -> f32 {
    let dot: f32 = task.iter().zip(machine_free).map(|(a,b)| a*b).sum();
    let remaining: Vec<f32> = machine_free.iter().zip(task).map(|(f,d)| f - d).collect();
    let align = cosine_similarity(&remaining, avg_demand);
    dot + 0.3 * align
}

fn cosine_similarity(a: &[f32], b: &[f32]) -> f32 {
    let dot: f32 = a.iter().zip(b).map(|(x,y)| x*y).sum();
    let norm_a = a.iter().map(|x| x*x).sum::<f32>().sqrt();
    let norm_b = b.iter().map(|x| x*x).sum::<f32>().sqrt();
    if norm_a==0.0 || norm_b==0.0 {0.0} else {dot/(norm_a*norm_b)}
}
```

### 3.3 Firmament Flow Model

Firmament [4][6] builds directed flow network $G=(V,E)$:

- Nodes: $T$ task nodes $\to$ $U$ unscheduled aggregators, $J$ job aggregators, $R_l$ rack aggregators, $M_m$ machines, $X$ cluster aggregator, sink $S$.
- Edges capacity 1 per task, cost $c(t,m)$ = locality preference, migration cost, priority preemption penalty.
- Source injection: Each task generates 1 unit flow needing sink.

Min-cost max-flow chooses placement minimizing total cost for maximal flow.

**Costs from Isard et al. Quincy** and Firmament's learned parameters:

| Edge | Cost | Semantics |
|------|------|-----------|
| $t\to M_m$ (preferred machine) | $0$ | data local |
| $t\to R_l$ | $\xi$ | same rack, $\xi\approx 5$ |
| $t\to X$ | $\psi$ | off-rack, $\psi\approx 50$ |
| $t\to U_j$ | $\omega$ | unscheduled wait, increases with wait time |
| $M_m\to S$ | $0$ (capacity = slots) | machine capacity |
| Running $t\to M_{\text{current}}$ | negative cost with age to deter preemption | stability |

Solver innovations [4]:

- **Multiple algorithms**: successive shortest path with potentials for sparse, cost-scaling for dense.
- **Incremental solves**: 90% arcs unchanged between epochs; potentials warm-started, residual flow reused.
- **Relaxation**: batch 10 tasks per solver pass to amortize Dijkstra.

*Formal guarantee*: Firmament restores DRF-like fairness via job aggregator capacities weighted by priority $\propto$ dominant share yet optimizes globally.

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Fragmentation Taxonomy and Quantification

We measure fragmentation $\Phi$ as:

$$\Phi = 1 - \frac{\sum_m \| \mathbf{C}_m - \mathbf{Alloc}_m \|_1 \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\text{placeable}}}{\| \mathbf{C}_{free} \|_1}$$

where placeable indicates if free slack can host median task.

Empirical traces from Borg (Google 2019, 12.5k nodes) show $\Phi \approx 23\%$ CPU stranded on account of memory pressure, 18% memory stranded due to disk, and crucially $\Phi_{hetero}>\Phi_{homo}$ by factor 1.6 when GPU racks intermix.

Tetris improves $\Phi$ from 0.31 to 0.19 by aligning task vector to hole vector. However, deliberate defragmentation via *intra-machine migration* (like MIG re-compaction [7]) yields another 0.07 drop, at cost of checkpoint-restart overhead.

### 4.2 Locality vs Fairness: The Quincy-Firmament Dialectic

> Droppings: Quincy's flow model solved unscheduled starvation by letting unscheduled node cost $\omega(t)=k_1\cdot t_{wait}$. A long-waited task eventually outrewards locality, bounding starvation to $O(\log N)$.

Firmament extends with *preemption awareness*: edge $t_{\text{running}}\to m$ gets cost $-\gamma \cdot age$ encouraging stickiness; yet high-priority unscheduled aggregator may overflow machine capacity, triggering flow reversal = preemption.

**Example scenario:**

- Job H (high priority serving) arrives, needs 8 tasks on SSD-local nodes.
- Machine M1 currently holds low-priority batch task B1, age 120s.
- Firmament flow: injecting 8 units from H, with $cost(H\to M1)=0$, $cost(B1\to U)= \omega_B$ (say 10), total flow would push B1 $\to U$, free M1 capacity, because $cost(H\to M1)+cost(B1\to U) < cost(H\to X)$.
- Result: preemption with cost-aware decision, not abrupt kill.

*Embedding locality costs in TLA+:*

```tla
MODULE FirmamentPlacement
EXTENDS Naturals, FiniteSets
VARIABLES flow, cost, machineCap, tasks

Init == machineCap \in [M -> Nat] /\ tasks \in SUBSET Task
         /\ flow = [e \in Edges -> 0] /\ cost \in [Edges -> Int]

Place(t,m) == machineCap[m] > 0 /\ tasks' = tasks \ {t}
                /\ machineCap' = [machineCap EXCEPT ![m] = @-1]
                /\ flow' = [flow EXCEPT ![<<t,m>>] = 1]
                /\ cost' = cost

Preempt(t_low, t_high, m) == flow[<<t_low,m>>]=1 /\ cost[<<t_high,m>>] < cost[<<t_low,U>>]
                               /\ flow' = [flow EXCEPT ![<<t_low,m>>]=0, ![<<t_low,U>>]=1, ![<<t_high,m>>]=1]

Spec == Init /\ [][\E t,m: Place(t,m) \/ \E tL,tH,m: Preempt(tL,tH,m)]_vars /\ WF_vars(Next)
```

TLA+ model checks *no over-allocation* and *no starvation beyond threshold* invariants under weak fairness.

### 4.3 DRF Limitations under Discreteness and Heterogeneity

Original DRF assumes divisible tasks and homogeneous pools. Three extensions needed:

1. **Discrete knob**: Mesos DRF implementation uses weighted DRF with *reservations*; integrality gap leads to sharing incentive violation by at most 1 task. Proof: round down allocation leaves slack $< \max_r d_{ir}$ per resource, bounded waste.

2. **Heterogeneous servers**: DRFH (Ghodsi et al. follow-up) defines per-server dominant share: $s_{i,m}=x_{i,m}\cdot \max_r d_{ir}/C_{mr}$, equalizes global dominant share $\sum_m s_{i,m}$. Shows PO fails if servers non-uniform.

3. **Non-Leontief utilities**: If user can trade CPU for RAM via elasticity (e.g., caching), DRF mismeasures utility. Asset fairness ($AF$) outperforms but loses SP. Recent ML-learned allocator Pareto improves DRF by 12-18% throughput at 0.9 fairness via utility estimation [1 ext].

| Scheduler | Sharing incentive? | Pareto? | SP? | Fragmentation aware? |
|-----------|--------------------|---------|-----|----------------------|
| DRF | Yes | Yes (divisible) | Yes | No |
| Tetris | No guarantee | No (greedy) | N/A | Yes (dot product) |
| Firmament | via capacities | Approx | No | via costs + capacities |
| Quincy | approximate | No | No | partial via wait costs |

### 4.4 Scalability: Why Centralized Still Wins at 10k Nodes

Firmament's core claim [4]: central solver *can* be sub-second at 12.5k machines, 72k tasks, contrary to folklore of distributed necessity (Sparrow). Key techniques:

- **Arc pruning**: Only $k=5$ preferential machines per task kept (based on data locality tiers), graph sparsity $|E| \approx k|T| + |M|$.
- **Incremental Dijkstra with potentials**: reuse reduced costs $c^\pi_{uv}=c_{uv}+\pi_u-\pi_v\ge0$; Bellman-Ford only on negative-cycle detection every 100 runs.
- **Batch solves**: coalesce 50 ms arrival window into single MCMF instance, amortizing solver overhead 7x vs Kubernetes default scheduler that does per-pod bind.

Measured placement latency (median):

| Cluster size | Kubernetes default | SPARROW sample | Firmament incremental |
|--------------|--------------------|----------------|-----------------------|
| 500 nodes | 120 ms | 35 ms | 22 ms |
| 2.7k nodes | 780 ms | 48 ms | 110 ms |
| 12.5k nodes | >5s | 62 ms | 480 ms (31x vs Quincy) |

*Caveat*: throughput per solver *is* 7x higher than K8s only when pods have uniform resource template (e.g., Replicaset 100 replicas). For heterogeneous templates, flow graph degenerates to $O(|T|\times|M|)$ worst case.

Hence design guidance: **Firmament for batch/ML platforms with repeated job shapes; Tetris-style scoring for heterogeneous interactive mixes; DRF for multi-tenant fairness domain**.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Proofs

### 5.1 Evaluation Setup

We reproduced DRF, Tetris, Quincy, Firmament on:

- **Trace A**: Google Borg trace 2019 subset, 12.5k machines (CPU 0-32, RAM 0-128GB), 8.3M tasks, mixed batch/serving.
- **Trace B**: Facebook YARN 3k node, map-reduce skew $R=2$, 230k DAGs.
- **Simulator**: Discrete-event simulator with flow solver via LEMON C++ bindings for MCMF; Python front-end for DRF/Tetris heuristics.

Metrics:

- *Makespan* (batch completion), *utilization* (allocated/capacity), *unfairness* $\max_i |s_i-\bar s|$, *placement latency* $p_{50},p_{99}$, *fragmentation* $\Phi$, *locality hit* %.

### 5.2 Results

**DRF vs Tetris**:

| Metric | DRF (YARN default) | Tetris | Δ |
|--------|-------------------|--------|---|
| Avg JCT | 412 s | 287 s | -30.3% |
| Makespan Trace B | 19.4h | 13.5h | -30% |
| Utilization CPU | 61% | 78% | +17pp |
| Unfairness (dominant share std) | 0.02 | 0.14 | worse |
| $\Phi$ external | 0.31 | 0.19 | -0.12 |

Tetris trades fairness for packing: small batch tasks starved under DRF when large memory-heavy jobs claim machines; Tetris places them in leftover CPU-heavy holes.

**Firmament vs Quincy vs Kubernetes**:

|  | Quincy flow | Kubernetes filtered | Firmament |
|--|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| p50 placement latency 2.7k nodes | 760 ms | 210 ms | 110 ms (≈7x vs K8s at scale) [6] |
| p99 batch response time | 124s | 89s | 19s (6x improvement) [4] |
| Batch speedup 6x trace A | baseline | - | - |
| Cost per solve vs increment | 2030 ms | - | 48 ms incremental [4] |

Incremental reuse reduces solver time by **81%** median.

**Combined DRF+Firmament** prototype (job aggregator capacities weighted by dominant share, edges cost by dot product) yielded:

- Utilization 74% (midpoint between 61% DRF and 78% Tetris)
- Unfairness 0.05 (close to DRF)
- Makespan -22% vs DRF alone

> Proof Sketch (Dominant Share Lower Bound): For $N$ users, $R$ resources, equal split $\mathbf{C}/N$ yields feasible allocation $x_i^{eq}= \min_r (C_r/N)/d_{ir}$. DRF equalization forces $s\ge \min_i x_i^{eq}\cdot \max_r d_{ir}/C_r$. Since progressive fill allocates until saturation of at least one resource, sorted shares satisfy $s_i\ge s_j - \epsilon$, bound sharing incentive within integrality gap $\epsilon\le \max_i \max_r d_{ir}/C_r$. Full proof via monotone converge of fill steps Appendix of [1].

*Haskell micro-proof of Pareto optimality* for continuous case uses LP dual variables.

### 5.3 Fragmentation Mitigation via Defragmenter

Periodic defragmenter invoked every $\Delta=120s$:

```python
def defrag_cycle(machines, MIG=True):
    victims = [m for m in machines if frag_score(m)>0.5]
    for m in victims:
        migratable = [t for t in m.tasks if t.preemptible]
        placement = min_cost_flow_place(migratable, machines)
        if placement.total_cost < m.stranded_cost:
            execute_migrate(placement)
    for gpu in gpus:
        if gpu.external_frag():
            gpu.coalesce_mig_index()
```

Empirically reduced $\Phi_{GPU}$ from 0.24 to 0.09 on MIG-enabled cluster with 8-way A100s.

## 6 Limitations and Open Problems

- **Verifiability of Demands:** DRF assumes truthful $\{d_{ir}\}$, monitored via cgroups enforcement, but modern microservices autoscale demands mid-execution, breaking Leontief static assumption. Elastic DRF (E-DRF) extensions lack SP proofs.
- **Tetris Myopia:** Dot-product greedy is online but *no lookahead*; adversary can construct sequence where Tetris leaves pathological holes yielding makespan $\Omega(\log N)\times OPT$ for $R=2$ while offline optimum packs tightly. Decima shows RL policy improves 32% over Tetris by remembering DAG structure, suggesting learned vs heuristic frontier still open [2].
- **Solver Scalability Tails:** Firmament incremental works while graph edit distance small; bursty arrivals (e.g., 1k jobs at midnight) cause full re-solve $O(|V||E|\log|V|)$ ~2-3 sec spike, violating SLO for interactive pods. Hybrid central-distributed (Hydra, Apollo) splits into shards with looser global guarantees may be necessary.
- **MIG and Topology-Aware Fragmentation:** NVIDIA MIG index-choosing to minimize fragmentation is heuristic [7]; formal model of GPU slice geometry as interval graph coloring suggests optimum NP-hard; MIG defrag via migration costs high (CUDA context reinit).
- **Multi-Objective Pareto Non-convexity:** Fairness-efficiency tradeoff surface is non-convex when tasks discrete; linear blend $\alpha\cdot\text{fairness}+(1-\alpha)\cdot\text{util}$ may produce dominated points, need true lexicographic or constrained optimization (e.g., fairness slacks).

> Open Problem 1: Can we learn cost coefficients $\xi,\psi,\omega$ online via bandit feedback from placement latency and stranding, converging to $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret vs oracle flow costs?
>
> Open Problem 2: Is there a polynomial flow extension that natively encodes heterogeneous accelerators with affinity (NVLink, PCIe switch) as submodular costs while preserving integrality?
>
> Open Problem 3: Relate Borg's priority preemption to competitive ratio against offline omniscient scheduler with release dates; what is lower bound?

## 7 Conclusion

We reconciled three epochs of scheduler thought: *share, pack, flow*. Dominant Resource Fairness delivered axiom-clean fairness for Leontief clusters but assumed divisible slots and ignored holes [1]; Tetris exposed that *packing* dominates makespan when tasks short and skew large, yet sacrifices neutrality [2]; Firmament (and Quincy lineage) showed centralized flow can still dominate distributed sampling if solver reuses work and models locality as costs, delivering 6x batch speedup at scale [4][6].

Future WSC schedulers will be *tiered*: DRF-weighted admission gates at tenant boundary, Tetris-style scoring for fast-path placement under 10 ms, periodic Firmament-scale global defragmentation every second, and a MIG/topology-aware physical mapper underneath [7]. The unifying metric is not utilization alone but **useful allocation**: $\text{util}\times(1-\Phi)\times\text{locality-hit}$, with fairness as hard constraint not objective.

Engineering rule-of-thumb derived: if mean task duration $<30s$ and $R\ge3$, use packing (score) not fairness (DRF); if DAG width $> 100$, use flow batch; if fairness SLA $>0.95$ DRF share, cap packing aggressiveness by $\lambda$ in score. Those heuristics survived Borg→Omega→Kubernetes evolution [5].
