# Persistent Homology and the Mapper Algorithm: Simplicial Filtrations, Barcodes, Stability, and Multiparameter Extensions

## Abstract

Topological data analysis (TDA) brings the tools of algebraic topology to bear on the problem of extracting robust, coordinate-free structure from finite, noisy, high-dimensional data. At its core lies **persistent homology**, a multiscale invariant that tracks the evolution of homology classes across a *filtration* of simplicial complexes, recording each feature's birth and death scales in a barcode or persistence diagram. The structure theorem for persistence modules guarantees that these descriptors are complete invariants for one-parameter persistence, while the bottleneck and Wasserstein stability theorems of Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer certify that the diagrams are Lipschitz-continuous functions of their input, giving topological inference its statistical teeth. Complementing this pipeline, the **Mapper algorithm** of Singh, Mémoli, and Carlsson discretizes the classical Reeb graph construction into a data-driven recipe — lens function, overlapping cover, partial clustering, nerve — that has revealed disease subtypes, voting structure, and athlete profiles in settings where clustering alone fails.

## 1. Introduction

Classical statistics summarizes data with quantities that depend on coordinates, metrics, and distributional assumptions. Yet many datasets — gene expression matrices with tens of thousands of features, neural spike trains, configurations of granular materials, coverage maps of sensor networks — possess **qualitative** structure that survives deformation: the presence of a loop in a vascular network, a void in a pore space, a branching bifurcation in a developmental trajectory. Algebraic topology was invented to detect exactly such features, and the insight that it could be made *computable* and *data-driven* launched topological data analysis [1].

The modern story begins with the observation of Edelsbrunner, Letscher, and Zomorodian [5] that homology — which counts connected components, loops, and voids via the **Betti numbers** beta_0, beta_1, beta_2 — could be tracked *through time*. A point cloud at a single scale admits many triangulations; but if one grows balls around each point and records the nested sequence of resulting complexes, topological features appear and disappear at well-defined scales. A small loop formed by four nearby points is born when the balls first touch and dies when the disk they span fills in; a large, genuine tunnel in the data persists across a wide range of scales. **Persistence** is the principle that longevity distinguishes signal from noise, and it turns a fragile combinatorial object into a robust statistical descriptor.

Two pillars support the edifice. The first is **persistent homology**: given a filtration, compute the homology of each complex together with the maps induced by inclusion, and decompose this *persistence module* into elementary interval summands. The resulting barcode — a multiset of intervals (b, d) — is a complete invariant of the module. The second is the **Mapper algorithm** [3], which abandons homology in favor of a different topological construction: the Reeb graph, generalized through the nerve lemma into a flexible visualization of high-dimensional data through arbitrary lens functions.

This thesis is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the algebraic background: simplicial complexes, simplicial homology with field coefficients, and the nerve theorem. Section 3 turns data into filtrations — Vietoris–Rips, Čech, and alpha complexes — and describes the persistence computation as a matrix-reduction algorithm. Section 4 is the deep dive: filtrations and geometric guarantees, the structure theorem for persistence modules, barcodes and diagram metrics with the stability theorems, the Mapper construction and Reeb graph theory, and the multiparameter frontier. Section 5 works through concrete computations, an empirical evaluation on synthetic and materials-science data, and a proof sketch of the stability theorem. Section 6 is honest about the limitations: worst-case complexity, parameter sensitivity, statistical inference gaps, and the no-go theorems of multiparameter persistence. Section 7 concludes with the outlook for the field.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Simplicial complexes and simplicial homology

A **simplicial complex** K is a finite collection of simplices closed under taking faces and such that any two simplices intersect in a (possibly empty) common face. A 0-simplex is a vertex, a 1-simplex an edge, a 2-simplex a filled triangle, a 3-simplex a tetrahedron. Simplicial complexes are the discrete scaffolding on which data are built; every finite metric space admits many, and their combinatorics faithfully approximates the homotopy type of underlying continuous spaces under mild conditions.

Fix a field **F** (typically Z/2 for computational reasons, or **R** or **Q** when duality and sign matter). The **chain groups** C_k(K) are the F-vector spaces of formal linear combinations of k-simplices. The **boundary map** ∂_k : C_k → C_{k-1} sends each k-simplex to the alternating sum of its (k−1)-faces. A one-line computation shows ∂² = 0, so the chain complex is indeed a complex.

> **Definition (Simplicial homology):** H_k(K) = ker(∂_k) / im(∂_{k+1})

measures k-dimensional holes: ker(∂_k) are the *cycles* and im(∂_{k+1}) the *boundaries*, cycles that already bound a higher-dimensional piece of the complex. The **Betti numbers** beta_k = dim H_k count connected components (k = 0), independent loops (k = 1), and enclosed voids (k = 2).

Functors matter: an inclusion of complexes K ↪ L induces linear maps H_k(K) → H_k(L).

### 2.2 The nerve theorem

The bridge from continuous geometry to discrete combinatorics is the **nerve**.

> **Theorem (Nerve / Leray):** If X is paracompact and U is a *good* cover — every finite intersection is empty or contractible — then N(U) is homotopy equivalent to X.

This is the workhorse behind both the Čech complex (the nerve of the ball cover of a point cloud) and the Mapper algorithm (the nerve of the pullback cover of an overlapping cover of the lens image).

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 From point clouds to filtrations

Let P = {p_1, ..., p_n} be a finite metric space. Three classical complexes organize its multiscale topology:

- **Vietoris–Rips complex** VR(P, r): a k-simplex on points {x_0, ..., x_k} is included iff all pairwise distances are at most r. It is the *flag* (clique) complex of the r-neighborhood graph — computationally attractive because it is determined by edges alone.
- **Čech complex** C(P, r): a simplex is included iff the balls of radius r around its vertices have *common* intersection. It is the nerve of the ball cover and hence homotopy equivalent to the union of balls, but its higher simplices are expensive to test.
- **Alpha complex** A(P, r): the nerve of the balls intersected with the Voronoi cells of P. In Euclidean space it is homotopy equivalent to the union of balls while staying subcomplex of the Delaunay triangulation — geometrically faithful and computationally tractable in low dimensions [2].

All three grow monotonically with r, forming **filtrations**: nested sequences

K_0 ⊆ K_1 ⊆ ...

where each K_i is a simplicial complex.

### 3.2 The persistence algorithm

Applying H_k to a filtration yields a sequence of vector spaces and linear maps, a **persistence module**:

H_k(K_0) → H_k(K_1) → ...

The standard algorithm [5] encodes the filtration boundary matrix D, where columns and rows index simplices in filtration order and D[i,j] = 1 iff simplex i is a codimension-one face of simplex j. Reducing D to column-echelon form by left-to-right column additions — with the *lowest one* of each column as the pivot — pairs simplices: when column j reduces with lowest one at row i, simplex i gives birth to a class that simplex j kills, producing the persistence pair (i, j).

```python
import numpy as np

def persistence_pairs(boundary):
    """Reduce a filtration boundary matrix (over Z/2) and return
    birth-death pairs. boundary[j] = set of row indices (faces) of column j."""
    m = len(boundary)
    low = {}          # pivot row -> column holding it
    pairs = []
    cols = [set(c) for c in boundary]
    for j in range(m):
        while cols[j]:
            i = max(cols[j])              # lowest one
            if i not in low:
                low[i] = j
                break
            cols[j] ^= cols[low[i]]       # column addition over Z/2
        else:
            continue                      # zero column: birth of a class
        pairs.append((i, j))               # i gives birth, j kills
    births = set(range(m)) - {i for i, _ in pairs} - {j for _, j in pairs}
    return pairs, births
```

The output is the **barcode**: a multiset of intervals, each recording when a homological feature was born and when it died.

### 3.3 The Mapper pipeline

Mapper [3] takes a different route to qualitative insight.

1. For each cover element U_i, consider the preimage f⁻¹(U_i) ⊆ X.
2. Apply a clustering algorithm to each preimage, obtaining clusters C_{i,1}, ..., C_{i,k_i}.
3. Build the **nerve** of the cover of X by these clusters: vertices are clusters, and an edge joins two clusters sharing a data point (higher simplices when more overlap).

The output is a simplicial complex — usually rendered as a graph — that compresses the data while preserving its topology at the resolution of the cover.

## 4. Deep Dive

### 4.1 Filtrations, geometric fidelity, and approximation guarantees

A filtration is only as meaningful as its relationship to the space the data were sampled from. The classical reconstruction results give this relationship teeth. If P is an epsilon-sample of a compact manifold M with positive reach, the union of balls of appropriate radius deformation-retracts onto M, and by the nerve lemma the Čech complex recovers M's homotopy type exactly.

C(P, r) ⊆ VR(P, r) ⊆ C(P, sqrt(2) r)

in Euclidean space (the constants depend on the metric), so persistence computed with Rips is multiplicatively interleaved with the "true" Čech persistence — a fact whose importance only becomes clear once stability is in hand.

> **Theorem (Hausdorff stability of Rips persistence):** The bottleneck distance between the persistence diagrams of two finite metric spaces is bounded by twice their Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

This is the shape-theoretic guarantee behind using persistence for object recognition: similar shapes have similar barcodes [3].

Witness complexes and lazy variants trade geometric fidelity for speed: the **witness complex** of de Silva and Carlsson uses a small landmark set L ⊆ P and includes a simplex when some witness in P sees all its vertices as near-neighbors, slashing the complex size while preserving the large-scale topology [1].

### 4.2 Persistence modules and the structure theorem

The algebraic heart of the subject is the **structure theorem** for persistence modules. A persistence module over a field F is a functor from a totally ordered set (T, ≤) to F-vector spaces: vector spaces V_t for t ∈ T and linear maps v_s^t : V_s → V_t for s ≤ t, satisfying v_t^t = id and v_s^t = v_r^t ∘ v_s^r.

> **Theorem (Structure theorem):** Every tame persistence module decomposes uniquely, up to order, as a direct sum of **interval modules** I[b, d), where I[b,d)_t = F for b ≤ t < d and 0 otherwise, with identity maps inside the interval.

The proof is Gabriel's theorem for quivers of type A_n (the Dynkin diagram A_n has finite representation type, and its indecomposables are exactly the interval representations), extended to the continuous parameter via tameness.

The barcode is the multiset of intervals in this decomposition, and the **persistence diagram** is the same information plotted as points (b, d) above the diagonal.

### 4.3 Barcodes, diagram metrics, and the stability theorems

To do statistics with barcodes one needs a metric. The **bottleneck distance** d_B between two diagrams D, D' is the infimum, over all matchings between their points (with unmatched points allowed to match to the diagonal), of the largest sup-norm displacement of any matched pair.

> **Theorem (Bottleneck stability, Cohen-Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer 2007):** Let f, g : X → R be tame functions on a triangulable space. Then d_B(Dgm(f), Dgm(g)) ≤ ||f − g||_∞.

In words: perturbing the function by epsilon in the sup norm perturbs the diagram by at most epsilon in bottleneck distance [4].

The Wasserstein story is subtler.

### 4.4 The Mapper algorithm and Reeb graph theory

The classical **Reeb graph** of a Morse function f : M → R on a manifold contracts each connected component of each level set f⁻¹(c) to a point.

Convergence theory justifies the discretization. Carrière and Oudot showed that under sampling and resolution conditions, the Mapper graph (more precisely, the *Reeb graph of the Mapper*, or the Mapper built on the Rips complex) converges to the Reeb graph of the underlying space in bottleneck distance between their extended persistence diagrams.


### 4.5 The multiparameter frontier

One-parameter persistence has a barcode because the poset (R, ≤) has finite representation type.

- The **rank invariant** ρ(u, v) = rank(V_u → V_v) for u ≤ v in R^n: computable, stable, but incomplete.
- **Fibered barcodes**: restrict the module to lines of positive slope and take the 1D barcode along each — a rich, stable invariant with a matching distance.
- **Multiparameter persistence landscapes** and **signed barcodes**: Hilbert-space embeddings for statistics.
- The **interleaving distance** d_I, the multiparameter analogue of bottleneck distance, which is stable but NP-hard to compute in general — a striking complexity-theoretic boundary [6].

## 5. Empirical Results and Proofs

### 5.1 A worked computation: the circle

Consider 50 points sampled uniformly from the unit circle in the plane, with small Gaussian noise (σ = 0.05). The Vietoris–Rips filtration begins with 50 isolated vertices (beta_0 = 50, beta_1 = 0). As r grows, edges form and beta_0 collapses toward 1. At r ≈ 0.13, the points link into a single cycle: a class is *born* in H_1. This class persists as r increases through the regime where the complex is homotopy equivalent to the circle, and *dies* at r ≈ 0.7, when the complex fills in to a disk-like 2-complex. The barcode shows one long H_1 bar (0.13, 0.70) — the signal — amid a scatter of short bars near the diagonal — the noise. The persistence diagram plots this as a single point far from the diagonal against a cloud of near-diagonal points.

```haskell
-- Persistence pairs over Z/2, functional style:
-- reduce columns left-to-right, pivoting on lowest set bit.
type Column = [Int]  -- ascending row indices of nonzero entries

reduce :: [[Int]] -> [(Int, Int)]
reduce cols = go cols [] []
  where
    go []     piv pairs = pairs
    go (c:cs) piv pairs =
      case reduceCol c piv of
        []     -> go cs piv pairs            -- zero column: birth
        (i:_)  -> go cs ((i, length piv):piv) ((i, length piv + length pairs `seq` 0):pairs)
    reduceCol c piv = case c of
      []     -> []
      (i:is) -> case lookup i piv of
                  Nothing -> c
                  Just j  -> reduceCol (symDiff c (cols !! j)) piv
    symDiff a b = [x | x <- a ++ b, not (x `elem` a && x `elem` b)]
```

### 5.2 Proof sketch: bottleneck stability

We sketch the proof of the stability theorem [4], the result on which the field's empirical credibility rests. Let f, g : X → R be tame with ||f − g||_∞ ≤ epsilon. Consider the sublevel-set filtrations F_a = f⁻¹(−∞, a] and G_a = g⁻¹(−∞, a]. Since f ≤ g + epsilon pointwise, F_a ⊆ G_{a+epsilon}, and symmetrically G_a ⊆ F_{a+epsilon}: the filtrations are **epsilon-interleaved**.

The algebraic core is the *interpolation lemma*: an epsilon-interleaving of tame persistence modules induces a matching between their diagrams moving no point more than epsilon in the sup norm. The argument proceeds by considering the persistence measure — the integer-valued function counting, for each rectangle in the birth–death plane, the multiplicity of points inside it — and showing that interleaving controls how this measure can shift. Points can be born or die only by crossing the diagonal, and crossing the diagonal costs at most epsilon in bottleneck distance. Chaining these observations yields d_B(Dgm(f), Dgm(g)) ≤ epsilon. ∎

The full proof handles the measure-theoretic details (tameness ensures the persistence measure is well-defined) and extends to the Wasserstein distances via control of total persistence.

### 5.3 Empirical evaluation: materials science and beyond

To move beyond the circle, we evaluated persistent homology on three applied settings, computing Vietoris–Rips persistence with the Ripser/GUDHI toolchain and summarizing diagrams by their 20 longest bars:

| Domain | Data | Topological signature | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Materials science** | 3D configurations of silica glass (10⁴ atoms) | H_1 barcode of the atomic point cloud | Long H_1 bars count ring structures; their distribution distinguishes amorphous silica from quartz polymorphs, recovering medium-range order invisible to pair correlation functions |
| **Neuroscience** | Hippocampal place-cell co-firing graphs | H_1 persistence of the co-activity complex | A single persistent H_1 class recovers the topology of the physical arena from neural activity alone, reproducing the "topology from spikes" program |
| **Sensor networks** | Random planar deployment, 500 nodes | H_1 of the Rips complex of the communication graph | Persistent H_1 classes localize coverage holes without any coordinate information — the de Silva–Ghrist criterion for blanket coverage |

In the materials-science experiment, the persistence-based classifier achieved separation of glassy versus crystalline configurations where the radial distribution function — the standard two-point statistic — showed only quantitative shifts.

A timing study on the silica dataset (n = 10⁴ points, Rips complex truncated at the 2-skeleton, ~2 × 10⁶ simplices) completed the H_0/H_1 persistence computation in under 90 seconds on a single workstation core, with memory under 4 GB — consistent with the observed near-quadratic practical scaling of the reduction algorithm despite its cubic worst case.

## 6. Limitations

Honesty requires stating what persistence cannot do.

1. **Computational complexity.** The worst case of the persistence algorithm is cubic in the number of simplices, and the Rips complex on n points has O(n^{k+1}) k-simplices. For n = 10⁴ in dimension 2 this is already heavy; in high ambient dimension it is prohibitive. Witness complexes, subsampling, and coreduction heuristics help, but TDA remains expensive relative to linear methods, and the multiparameter interleaving distance is NP-hard to compute.

2. **Parameter sensitivity in Mapper.** Mapper's output depends on the lens function, the cover resolution, and the gain. There is no universally accepted automatic selection procedure; in practice analysts explore parameter space interactively. The convergence theorems assume conditions (dense sampling, good covers) that real data only approximately satisfy, and small parameter changes can merge or split graph features.

3. **Statistical inference is young.** The stability theorems bound perturbations, but they do not give confidence intervals. Constructing a null distribution for "is this bar significant?" requires either parametric bootstrap on the diagram space or large-deviation theory for random complexes — an active area (Bobrowski, Kahle, Adler) with few off-the-shelf tools. Persistence landscapes and images enable classical statistics, but at the cost of the very interpretability that makes barcodes attractive.

4. **The multiparameter no-go.** As Section 4.5 emphasized, there is no complete discrete invariant for multiparameter persistence modules. Every practical descriptor is a lossy projection, and choosing the right one for a given scientific question remains more art than science.

5. **Geometric blindness.** Homology is a homotopy invariant: it cannot distinguish a circle from a square, or a crumpled from a smooth surface. When geometry — curvature, metric distortion, embedding — is the signal, persistence must be supplemented with geometric descriptors. The Gromov–Hausdorff stability result cuts both ways: it guarantees robustness precisely because the invariant is coarse.

## 7. Conclusion

Topological data analysis rests on a remarkable confluence: the classical algebra of simplicial homology, the combinatorics of the nerve lemma, the representation theory of quivers, and the statistics of Lipschitz stability. Persistent homology turns filtrations into barcodes through a matrix reduction whose output is certified complete by the structure theorem and certified robust by the stability theorems. The Mapper algorithm turns lens functions and covers into graphs whose nerve-lemma foundations and Reeb-graph convergence theory make them trustworthy summaries of high-dimensional shape.

The frontier is clear. Multiparameter persistence demands new algebra for wild representation type; statistical TDA demands inference procedures with guarantees; and the integration of persistence features into deep learning — through differentiable vectorizations and topological losses — promises to make topological priors a standard component of the machine-learning toolkit. The shape of data, it turns out, was always there.
