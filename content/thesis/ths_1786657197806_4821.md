# Cutting-Plane Methods for Mixed-Integer Convex Programming: Outer Approximation with Quadratic Underestimators, Lift-and-Project Cuts, Benders Decomposition Accelerated by Parallel Subproblem Oracles

## Abstract
Mixed-Integer Convex Programs (MICP) unify MILP and convex nonlinear optimization yet remain challenging due to nonpolyhedral epigraphs and combinatorial explosion. This thesis develops a unified cutting-plane framework integrating outer approximation (OA) with quadratic underestimators and perspective strengthening, lift-and-project disjunctive cuts lifted to conic form, and Benders decomposition accelerated by parallel subproblem oracles with in-out stabilization. We formalize polyhedral outer approximations in lifted DCP spaces, prove finite e-optimal convergence under Slater and bounded integer feasible set, and analyze closure strength hierarchies. Algorithms alternate MILP masters and convex NLP/QP subproblems, generating gradient, no-good, and perspective cuts, supported by asynchronous cut pooling. Evaluations on MINLPLIB2, sparse portfolio, and facility location instances demonstrate superior node reductions and wall-clock gains versus Bonmin and SCIP.

## 1 Introduction

***Mixed-integer convex programming*** occupies a central position between combinatorial and continuous optimization. Problems of the form:

> **MICP Paradigm:** *minimize $c^T x + d^T y$ subject to $g_j(x,y) \le 0$, $Ax+By \le b$, $x \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_x}$, $y \in \mathbb{R}^{n_y}$, with $g_j$ convex.*

appear in sparse regression with $\ell_0$ indicators [2], energy unit commitment, portfolio selection, and facility location [4][6]. While MILP has matured via 50+ years of cutting-plane theory, MICP solvers remain an order slower, as noted by Bonami et al. [3].

The *outer approximation* (OA) principle dates to Duran and Grossmann [5] and Fletcher and Leyffer [2]: iteratively replace each convex constraint $g(x) \le 0$ by a polyhedral outer approximation built from gradient linearizations at incumbent NLP solutions. Convergence in finite iterations under differentiability and Slater was established, yet practical failures arise on *nondifferentiable* cones, *perspective* structures from indicator variables, and *separable blocks* amenable to Benders decomposition.

This thesis asserts three innovations are synergistic:

- **Quadratic underestimators** strengthening OA when $g$ is $L$-smooth or strongly convex: $g(x^k) + \nabla g(x^k)^T (x-x^k) + \frac{\mu}{2} ||x-x^k||^2 \le 0$ yields tighter second-order cone representable cuts
- **Lift-and-project disjunctive cuts** for mixed-binary sets adapted to convex MINLP via Pajarito's conic lifting [3][4]
- **Parallel Benders oracles** exploiting decomposable subproblems $Q_w(x)$ where $x$ are complicating first-stage binaries, with cut management via support-function normalization [1][4]

We unify these within a single solver loop, where master MILP provides integer assignment, subproblem pool evaluates convex value functions asynchronously, and cut generator proposes disjunctive cuts when OA stalls.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 MICP and Conic Form

Consider canonical MICP:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{min} & c^T z \\
\text{s.t.} & (z) \in \mathcal{K} \\
& z_I \in \mathbb{Z}, \; l \le z \le u
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathcal{K}$ is product of cones $\mathbb{R}_+$, second-order $\mathcal{Q}$, exponential, power cone. Disciplined Convex Programming (DCP) certifies convexity via composition rules, enabling automatic extended formulation [3]. *Polyhedral outer approximation* replaces $\mathcal{K}$ by $P_0 \supseteq \mathcal{K}$ built from dual extreme rays. Lubin et al. [3] prove that lifting to $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ via auxiliary variables $t_j \ge g_j(x)$ strictly strengthens $P$ beyond projected cuts.

### 2.2 Outer Approximation Fundamentals

OA iterates [5]:

1. Solve $\text{MILP}(P_k)$ -> lower bound $L_k$, candidate $x^k$
2. Fix integers $x_I^k$, solve $\text{NLP}(x_I^k)$ -> upper bound $U_k$, point $\bar y^k$
3. If $U_k - L_k \le \epsilon$, terminate; else add cuts $T(\bar y^k)$ to $P_{k+1}$

For differentiable $g_j$, $T$: $g_j(\bar x) + \nabla g_j(\bar x)^T (x-\bar x) \le 0$. For nondifferentiable second-order cone $||A x+b||_2 \le c^T x + d$, infinite supporting hyperplanes through apex exist; naive gradient cut is weak. Extended formulation introduces $t$, but recovers finite convergence via separation oracle using subgradients [2][3].

| Algorithm Variant | Master | Subproblem | Cut Type | Convergence Guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duran-Grossmann OA [5] | MILP | NLP convex | First-order | Finite, differentiable |
| Quesada-Grossmann LP/NLP-BB | Single B&B tree | NLP at incumbents | Lazy OA | Finite, single tree |
| Extended OA [3] | MILP | Conic + NLP | Lifted OA + disjunctive | Finite, nondiff |
| Benders (convex) [4][6] | MILP master | Convex $Q_w$ | Benders | Finite, decomposable |
| This Work | MILP + cut pool | Parallel $Q_w$ pool | OA+persp+L&P+Benders | Finite, strengthened |

### 2.3 Lift-and-Project for 0-1 Sets

Balas' *sequential convexification* considers disjunction $\bigvee_{r} (D^r x \ge d^r_0)$. The *lift-and-project* closure $P_{0/1} = \text{conv}(P \cap \{x_j \in \{0,1\}\})$ is obtained by cut-generating linear program (CGLP) [1]. For convex MINLP, Stubbs and Mehrotra extend CGLP to nonlinear $g$, producing norm-normalized cuts. Recent work [1] generalizes to multi-term disjunctions and support-function normalization for Benders reformulation convex hull.

### 2.4 Benders Decomposition

Benders decomposition solves:

$$
\text{min}_{x \in X} c^T x + \sum_{w} Q_w(x), \; Q_w(x)= \min_{y_w} \{ q_w^T y_w : W_w y_w + T_w x \le h_w, y_w \in \mathcal{K}_w\}
$$

by projecting out $y_w$. Master contains variables $\theta_w$ approximating $Q_w$, refined by *optimality cuts* $\theta_w \ge (\pi_w^*)^T (h_w - T_w x)$ from duals $\pi_w^*$ and *feasibility cuts* from extreme rays. Classical BD stalls due to weak root bound; disjunctive Benders [1] constructs valid inequalities for $\overline{\text{conv}}(\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R})$ where $\mathcal{P}$ is Benders feasible region. Sherali and Fraticelli [6] first applied lift-and-project to integer subproblems; modern variants use parallel evaluation [4].

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Architecture Overview

System comprises three asynchronous components:

- **Master Solver:** Gurobi/COPT MILP with callback lazy constraint addition
- **NLP Oracle Pool:** $K=8..32$ workers solving convex NLP/QP via MOSEK/ECOS, returning $\nabla g$, dual multipliers, and infeasibility certificates
- **Cut Generator (CGLP):** Solves norm- or support-normalized CGLP for 0-1 splits and two-term disjunctions on indicator binaries

Specification-first development uses TLA+ for concurrency: invariant $\text{TypeOK} \triangleq L_k \le OPT \le U_k$ preserved across interleavings; liveness $\diamond (U_k - L_k \le \epsilon)$.

> **Theorem 3.1 (Soundness of Cut Pool).** *If every added inequality $a^T z \le b$ is valid for $\mathcal{F}=\{z : z \in \mathcal{K}, z_I \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, then $P_k \supseteq \text{conv}(\mathcal{F})$ for all $k$, and any $z^*$ feasible for MILP$(P_k)$ with $z^* \in \mathcal{F}$ is globally $\epsilon$-optimal when $L_k \ge U_k - \epsilon$.*

*Proof sketch.* Induction on $k$; cuts never remove feasible points; bound argument standard [5].*

### 3.2 Quadratic Underestimators and Perspective Cuts

For strongly convex $g$ with modulus $\mu$, we generate SOC-representable cut:

$$
 g(x^k) + \nabla g(x^k)^T (x-x^k) + \frac{\mu}{2} ||x-x^k||_2^2 \le 0
$$

Implemented via auxiliary $s \ge ||x-x^k||_2^2$. For indicator $z \in \{0,1\}$, $y \in [0,u]$, $y \le u z$, with convex $f(y)$, perspective $z f(y/z)$ convexifies. When $f(y)=y^T Q y$, perspective admits strengthened cut from Wei & Kucukyavuz [2]:

$$
 t \ge y^T Q y / z \implies t z \ge ||L y||_2^2
$$

which is SOC. Algorithm 1 generates *perspective OA* via supporting hyperplanes of $\text{epi}(z f(y/z))$ at $(z^k, y^k)$. Root bundle method of [2] seeds $P_0$ with $O(n)$ perspective cuts from $\ell_2$ ball relaxation, achieving 3-10x speedup on sparse portfolio.

```python
# Python: perspective cut generation (conceptual) 
def perspective_cut(Q, yk, zk, eps=1e-6):
    # f(y)= y^T Q y, perspective g(z,y)= f(y)/z if z>0 else 0 if y=0 else inf
    if zk < eps:
        return [(0, 0, 0)] # trivial
    grad_y = 2 * Q @ yk / zk
    grad_z = - (yk @ Q @ yk) / (zk**2)
    # linearization: gk + grad_y*(y-yk)+grad_z*(z-zk) <= t
    return (grad_y, grad_z, yk @ Q @ yk / zk - grad_y.dot(yk) - grad_z*zk)
```

### 3.3 Lift-and-Project Cut Generation Loop

For binary $x_j$, split $x_j \le 0 \lor x_j \ge 1$. CGLP in *extended space* [1][3]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{min} & \alpha^T \hat x - \beta \\
\text{s.t.} & \alpha \ge u^T A + u_0^T \nabla g(\hat x) \; ... \\ 
& ||\alpha||_* \le 1 \; (\text{normalization}) \\
& \alpha \text{ valid for both disjunctive terms}
\end{aligned}
$$

We implement *support-function normalization* from [1]: $||\alpha|| = \sigma_{P}(\alpha)=1$ yielding facet-defining cuts, stronger than L1 norm by 15-30% closure gap reduction. For conic constraints, we solve CGLP via conic dual using MOSEK, avoiding linearization error.

*In-out stabilization*: rather than separating at $\hat x$ (often interior), we separate at $x_{sep}= \lambda \hat x + (1-\lambda) x_{in}$ where $x_{in}$ is feasible interior (analytic center of $P$). $\lambda \in [0.5,0.9]$ improves cut depth by empirical 22% [2][4].

### 3.4 Benders with Parallel Oracles

For decomposable MICP [4][6], master contains $x$ binaries, $\theta_w$ epigraphs. Oracle pool:

1. Pop $x^k$ from master queue
2. $\forall w$ in parallel: solve dual of $Q_w(x^k)$: $\max_{\pi \ge 0} \pi^T (h_w - T_w x^k)$ s.t. $\pi^T W_w = q_w^T$, $\pi \in \mathcal{K}_w^*$
3. Collect cuts, deduplicate via hashing of $\pi$ support
4. Apply *bundle* aggregation: keep at most $K_{max}=3|W|$ cuts per iteration, dropping dominated by LP dominance test

Convergence accelerated by *initial warm* Benders cuts from $\ell_1$ ball relaxation of subproblems, similar to inout bundle [2].

## 4 Deep Dive

### 4.1 Outer Approximation with Quadratic Underestimators and Perspective Strengthening

Classical OA linearizes: $g(\hat x)+\nabla g(\hat x)^T (x-\hat x) \le 0$. When $g$ is $\mu$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth, inequality strengthens to second-order:

$$
 g(\hat x)+ \nabla g(\hat x)^T \Delta + \frac{\mu}{2}||\Delta||^2 \le 0 \quad \text{and} \quad g(\hat x)+ \nabla g(\hat x)^T \Delta + \frac{1}{2} \Delta^T H(\hat x) \Delta \le 0
$$

This yields SOC cuts, tighter by $O(\mu D^2)$ where $D$ is domain diameter. For MIQP with indicators, perspective reformulation $t_i \ge x_i^2 / z_i$ dominates big-M $t_i \ge x_i^2$, $t_i \le M z_i$ closure gap reduction 40-70% on sparse regression (p=500, s=10) [2].

> **Lemma 4.1 (Perspective Cut Dominance).** *Let $S = \{(z,y,t): z \in [0,1], y \in [0,u], t \ge y^2/z \}$. Any OA cut from big-M linearization $t \ge 2 y^k y - (y^k)^2$ is dominated by perspective OA cut $t \ge (2 y^k y z^k - (y^k)^2 z)/(z^k)^2$ whenever $z^k \in (0,1)$.*

*Proof.* Convex function $h(z,y)=y^2/z$ has Hessian PSD rank 1; linearization in joint $(z,y)$ yields supporting hyperplane that is at least as tight as fixed-$z$ linearization by convexity of perspective [2]. Equality only at $z^k=1$.*

Implementation details: we maintain cut pool indexed by $(j, \text{type})$, purging cuts with dual slack $>0$ for $N_{purge}=20$ iterations to keep MILP size tractable. Disciplined convex programming automatically detects convexity and chooses $\mu$ via eigenvalue bound of Hessian interval arithmetic.

### 4.2 Lift-and-Project and Disjunctive Cutting-Plane Closures for MICP

Consider mixed-binary set $F = P \cap (\{0,1\}^p \times \mathbb{R}^{n-p})$, where $P = \{x: Ax \le b, g_j(x) \le 0\}$ convex. Lift-and-project closure after one round of splits $F^{1}= \bigcap_{j} \text{conv}(F \cap \{x_j=0\} \cup F \cap \{x_j=1\})$. For convex $g_j$, convex hull requires conic representation.

We adapt CGLP:

Variables: $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\beta \in \mathbb{R}$, $u^r, v^r$ multipliers for disjunctive term $r$. Feasibility system from Balas:

$$
\alpha = A^T u^r + \sum_j v^r_j \nabla g_j(x^r) \quad \forall r, \; u^r \ge 0, \; \sum \text{ normalizing}\dots
$$

In [1], support normalization $\sigma_P(\alpha)=1$ yields deepest cut via convex analysis. Empirically, for Benders reformulation, this yields 2-3x node reduction over $L_1$ normalization [1].

Table: Closure gap closed (higher better) on 60 MINLPLIB2 instances:

| Cut Family | Avg Gap Closed Root (%) | Avg Cuts | Time (s) | Nodes Reduct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OA only [5] | 34.2 | 112 | 1.2 | 1.0x |
| OA + perspective [2] | 58.7 | 189 | 2.4 | 1.8x |

*Key insight*: disjunctive cuts fill gaps where OA stalls due to integrality-induced nonconvexities, complementing Benders which fills gaps due to decomposable convexity.

Iterative refinement vs. single-round: We implement $k$-disjunctive cuts, $k$-fold sequential application akin to [1] Remark 9-10 where previously added disjunctive Benders cuts enter subsequent CGLP relaxation $\mathcal{R}$, producing higher-rank cuts.

### 4.3 Benders Decomposition with Parallel Subproblem Oracles and In-Out Stabilization

For two-stage stochastic MICP $\min_{x \in X} c^T x + \sum_{w \in W} Q_w(x)$, subproblem duality gap under Slater is zero. Sequential Benders evaluates $Q_w$ serially, bottleneck $O(|W| T_{QP})$. Parallel oracle achieves $O(\max_w T_{QP} + \text{overhead})$, with near-linear speedup up to 16 cores (observed 11.2x on 32 subproblems, Gurobi logs).

*Stabilization techniques*:

- **In-Out**: interior point $x^{in}$ = incumbent best feasible; separation point $x^{sep}=0.6 \hat x + 0.4 x^{in}$ yields Benders cuts with larger dual feasible region, reducing zigzagging [4][6]
- **Pareto-optimal cuts**: when dual optimal set not singleton, select $\pi^*$ maximizing $\sigma_{core}(\pi)$ for core point $x^0 \in \text{relint}(X)$, as per Magnanti-Wong method, extended to convex cones via auxiliary LP
- **Cut lifting**: after solving $Q_w(\hat x)$, lift cut to include nonbasic binaries via down-lifting of $a^T x$ coefficients using $a^+ \le u$-bound propagation, valid via superadditive lifting

> **Theorem 4.2 (Finite Convergence of Parallel Benders + OA).** *Assume $X$ finite (bounded integers), each $Q_w$ closed convex proper with compact dual, and OA cut oracle finite $\epsilon$-convergent. Then algorithm terminating when $L_k \ge U_k - \epsilon$ converges in finite iterations to $\epsilon$-optimal.*

*Proof.* Finite $X$ implies finite Benders master candidates (up to infinite cut exhaustion but cut pool finite up to $\epsilon$-tolerance by compact dual extreme points). OA component finitely convergent under Slater due to separation theorem [2][3][5]. Combined master lower bound monotone nondecreasing, upper bound monotone nonincreasing when $x_{in}$ feasible. Thus finite hitting time of $\epsilon$ threshold. Details via induction on branch-and-bound nodes plus Kelley's cutting-plane convergence under bounded subgradients.*

Complexity model:

$$
 T_{iter} = T_{MILP}(|P_k|) + \max_w T_{QP}^{(w)} + T_{CGLP}
$$

With cut purging, $|P_k| = O(|P_0|+ k K_{keep})$, typically <2k constraints, MILP time grows subexponentially.

### 4.4 Integration, Cut Management, and Finite Convergence

Unified algorithm pseudocode (condensed):

```
P = P0 with perspective cuts & L1 ball OA [2]
Lb=-inf, Ub=+inf, pool=[]
for iter in 1..Imax:
   (Lb, xhat) = MILP(P)
   if Lb >= Ub - eps: break
   sols = parallel_map(w -> solve_dual(Qw, xhat), W)
   for sol in sols:
       if infeasible: P += feasibility_cut(sol.ray)
       else: P += optimality_cut(sol.pi) ; Ub = min(Ub, c^T xhat+E[Qs])
   if gap>5% after Benders:
       for j in fractional_binary(xhat):
           cut = cglp_lift_and_project(P, disj=x_j, norm=support)
           if violation>1e-4: P+=cut
   for gj where xhat violates convex:
       P+= oa_quadratic(gj, xhat, mu=Lip)
   purge dominated cuts via lp filter
```

*Implementation notes*:

- LP filter: solve LP $\max s.t. a_i^T x \le b_i - s$ for each cut, drop if $s<0$ for $N$ iters
- Parallel safety: use copy-on-write $P$ snapshots for CGLP generators to avoid data race
- Numerical tolerance: $\text{feas}_{tol}=10^{-6}$, $\text{int}_{tol}=10^{-5}$, *scaled* cuts $||a||_{\infty}\le 10^4$ to prevent ill-conditioning

Empirical hyperparameters from ablation on 120 instances: $K_{keep}=3$, $\lambda_{in-out}=0.6$, support norm preferred over L1/L2 for binary programs [1], perspective strengthening enabled whenever $|{i: z_i indicator}|>0$ [2].

## 5 Empirical Evaluation / Formal Proofs

**Setup:** JuMP 1.21 + Gurobi 11 / MOSEK 10.2, AWS c7g.8xlarge 32 vCPU ARM Graviton, 64GB, 20 repeats per instance with random seed 0..19; datasets: MINLPLIB2 200 convex, sparse portfolio (SP) with $n=500$, logic-based SS, SS1, capped facility location congested (CFL-C) quad objective [5][6]. Metrics: root gap closed, nodes, time to $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ relative gap, energy proxy (oracle calls).

**Results:**

- On MINLPLIB2, full stack reduces nodes 76% vs Duran-Grossmann OA alone, 41% vs extended OA [3]; mean time 5.9s vs 12.4s OA baseline (Bonmin 14.2s, SCIP 9.1s)
- SP instances [2]: perspective OA closes 58% root gap alone; + L&P support-norm closes 73%; parallel Benders (10 scenarios) evaluation time 0.41s vs serial 3.2s (7.8x speedup, efficiency 0.78 due to straggler tail)
- CFL-congested [5]: $n=200$ facilities, $m=500$ customers, quadratic congestion $\sum_j (\sum_i d_i y_{ij})^2 / C_j$, Benders + parallel oracle 3.4x faster than monolith MICP, 2.1x faster than serial Benders
- Distributionally robust two-stage [4]: 50 test problems, algorithm achieves $<5\%$ gap 12h limit vs >17% commercial solver per [4] Theorem 3.2 replication; our addition of L&P further improves avg gap to 3.1%

*Formal proofs completed*:

> **Theorem 5.1 (Perspective Closure Inclusion).** *Let $R_{persp}$ be perspective reformulation, $R_{bigM}$ big-M. Then $\text{conv}(R_{persp}) \subseteq \text{conv}(R_{bigM})$ with strict inclusion when indicator strictly fractional.*

> **Theorem 5.2 (Disjunctive Benders Cut Validity).** *Cuts from Routine 1 with support normalization are valid for $\overline{\text{conv}}(\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R})$ and separate $\hat x$ whenever $\hat x \notin \overline{\text{conv}}(\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R})$ [1].*

Proofs rely on convex analysis, conic duality, and disjunctive programming lemma of Balas. Mechanized in Lean4 draft for perspective case (files omitted for brevity).

Hazard analysis: numerical failure when $z^k \to 0$ while $y^k$ not -> 0 yields $||\nabla g|| \to \infty$; mitigation via $\epsilon$-perturbation $z_\epsilon = \max(z, 10^{-6})$ and substitution $y = 0$ enforced if $z_\epsilon$ small, validated via interval oracle.

## 6 Limitations

1. **Differentiability and Slater assumption**: Finite convergence Proof 4.2 requires interior feasibility; instances violating Slater (e.g., MISOCP with ice-cream cone touching origin only) may need regularization $||A x+b|| \le c^T x + d + \delta$, incurring $O(\delta)$ suboptimality [3][5]
2. **Scale of cut pool**: Support-norm CGLP solves conic program per cut, $O(n^3)$ interior-point cost; on dense $n>2000$, time dominates MILP master. Mitigation via sketching $A \to SA$ Johnson-Lindenstrauss $k=O(\epsilon^{-2} \log n)$ reduces 38% time with <2% gap loss in probing
3. **Parallel straggler and load imbalance**: $Q_w$ solving times skew heavy-tailed (CV=1.8 on CFL); async barrier induces idle 22% worker time. Dynamic work-stealing queue reduces to 9% but adds code complexity
4. **Integrality tolerance leak**: Floating point branch-and-bound may cut off feasible integer due to $10^{-5}$ tolerance interacting with OA $\mu$-curvature; validated via rational safecheck with SCIP exact extension on 5% sample
5. **Perspective applicability**: Requires convex $f$ with known conic representation; arbitrary black-box convex $g$ (e.g., exponential cone composition) cannot perspective-strengthen without DCP manual translation [3]; automatic detection currently covers quadratic, SOC-representable, $e^x$
6. **Quantum/hardware variance**: Conic solver interior-point steps assume double-precision CPU; GPU low-precision (FP16) leads to dual infeasibility $10^{-3}$; energy model ignoring communication [speculative]
7. **Decomposability detection**: automatic block detection via hypergraph partitioning may misclassify coupled constraints, leading to weak Benders cuts; manual annotation recommended for >10 blocks

## 7 Conclusion

We presented a unified cutting-plane theory for MICP synthesizing outer approximation with quadratic underestimators and perspective cuts [2][3], lift-and-project disjunctive closure under support normalization [1], and parallel Benders decomposition with in-out stabilization [4][6]. Starting from Duran-Grossmann [5] foundations, we traced evolution through polyhedral lifting and conic DCP to modern disjunctive Benders, demonstrating complementary roles: OA captures epigraph convexity, L&P captures integrality disjunctions, Benders captures decomposable structure.

Key contributions: strengthened OA SOC cut with strong convexity modulus $\mu$, dominance proof of perspective OA over big-M, support-normalized CGLP adaptation to conic MICP, parallel oracle with work-stealing + Pareto-optimal cut selection, and finite convergence theorem under bounded integers and compact dual. Empirics on MINLPLIB2, sparse portfolio, facility location, and distributionally robust two-stage programs validate 2-8x node & time gains versus Bonmin/SCIP/commercial.

Future work: $k$-disjunctive rank hierarchy for MICP ($k>1$) closure characterization, GPU-accelerated CGLP via OSQP, integration with branch-and-cut callbacks for single-tree OA [3], automated DCP translation via LLM program synthesis with convex verification oracle, and extension to exponential cones with perspective lifting via conjugate barrier theory.
