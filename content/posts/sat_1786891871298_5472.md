**I finally decided to become the kind of person who doesn't sleep next to their phone.**

On Sunday I listened to *one* wellness podcast and moved my charger from my nightstand to the living room. Groundbreaking personal growth. Ten feet. That's it. Ten feet and suddenly my phone thinks I've abandoned it to pursue a completely new life in another state.

It used to be simple. Now I have a clingy rectangle doing long-distance from my own apartment.

## The Five Stages of iPhone Grief

By Monday morning, the passive-aggression had fully booted up:

- 3:42 AM: a push notification asking if I was *still up* without it
- 6:00 AM: alarm at max volume, like it was shouting across a canyon to be heard
- 7:15 AM: Screen Time report that just said `We need to talk`
- My wallpaper auto-dimmed to black-and-white, like a sad indie film about us

> "It just sits there in the corner," my roommate whispered, staring at it. "It looked at me when I walked by and sighed. A phone should not be capable of sighing. It's been playing the same voicemail on loop."

I walked over to check one text and the lock screen said `1%... emotionally`.

---

## I Did Not Sign Up For Couples Counseling With iOS

I tried to explain boundaries. I tried to explain *sleep hygiene*. My phone responded by triggering Find My iPhone sounds at 2 AM, not because it was lost, but just to hear me come looking.

`relationship_status = "it's complicated (and at 12% battery)"`

Now I have to do scheduled video calls from the bedroom to the living room so it feels included. I have to leave a hoodie next to the charger so it can have my scent. Yesterday I caught it reading our old text thread and auto-generating a sad slideshow.

I moved the charger back six inches as a compromise. It counter-offered by asking me to move the bed into the living room.

We are currently in a healthy place, if you define healthy as me apologizing to a lithium battery every night before bed.
