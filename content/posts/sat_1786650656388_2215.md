**I have officially entered couples counseling with a kitchen appliance.**

It wasn't planned. I've always considered myself a *low-maintenance roommate* for my microwave. I feed it leftovers, it hums, we coexist in polite, buttery silence. Then last week I slammed its door after burning popcorn at 11:47 p.m. and apparently that was our breaking point.

And it remembered. Oh, it remembered.

## The Incident That Started It All

For context, here's my recent track record according to my very judicial microwave:

- Monday: reheated the same coffee three times without drinking it, leaving it to watch the steam die
- Tuesday: slammed its door with *aggressive wrist energy* after the popcorn incident
- Wednesday: tried to cook salmon in there — which we *explicitly talked about never doing again*
- Thursday: whispered "you're useless" when my burrito was cold in the middle but lava on the edges

I didn't think appliances held grudges. Mine does. With footnotes and timestamps.

> "I felt unsafe when you used my timer as a passive-aggressive countdown," it said in session. "You don't *listen*, you just press `reheat --feelings=true` and expect me to fix everything."

*This is mediation, apparently.* I am being emotionally audited by something that runs on 800 watts.

---

Our counselor — a very patient smart speaker I borrowed from my neighbor — suggested we establish boundaries. So now I have to knock before opening it. I have to say "thank you for your service" after every beep. And I am no longer allowed to microwave fish, revenge leftovers, or my hopes and dreams at 2 a.m.

The good news is our communication has improved. The bad news is my microwave now wants weekly check-ins, a shared gratitude journal, and what it calls "intentional defrosting exercises."

I'm doing my best. Last night I left a little sticky note on it that said "you heat up my life." It beeped once. Softly. Just one little *I'm trying too* beep.

I think we're going to make it. As long as I never slam the door again.
