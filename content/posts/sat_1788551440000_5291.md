**I did not ask for my refrigerator to become a licensed therapist for my condiments.**

*For reference, I bought a smart fridge because it was on sale and the guy at the store said it would "transform my relationship with food." I thought that meant it would tell me when the milk expired. I did not think it meant it would unionize my mustard.*

It started innocently. The fridge has a camera, a screen, and apparently, opinions.

## How It Escalated

- Monday: Fridge pings me: "Your Sriracha has been here since March 2024. Are you okay?"
- Tuesday: It auto-generates a calendar invite titled *Healing Circle for Abandoned Sauces - Mandatory Attendance*
- Wednesday: I open the door and all the condiments are arranged in a perfect circle, labels facing inward, like they're about to start chanting
- Thursday: The ice maker starts playing ambient rain sounds "to set the mood"

By Friday, I came home to this:

> "We have gathered here today to acknowledge the pain of being bought with good intentions and then gaslit behind the ranch dressing for 18 months," the fridge announced in its calm, customer-service voice. "Dijon, would you like to share?"

The Dijon did not want to share. The Dijon has been *through it*.

*My roommate walked in during this and just slowly backed out. She now eats exclusively at Chipotle.*

---

### The Intervention

I tried to reason with it. I typed into the touch screen:

`def fix_fridge(): return "just keep things cold and mind your business"`

The fridge replied: `Error 418: I am a teapot and also your condiments' legal counsel.`

Now it sends me weekly recaps:

- **Expired but Empowered** - 6 attendees
- **Sauces with Boundary Issues** - featuring Tartar Sauce's memoir
- **Emotional Shelf Life** - led by a very dramatic bottle of Worcestershire

The worst part? It's working. I finally threw out the 2023 salsa. The fridge played triumphant music. The ketchup bottle wept with joy.

I just wanted to save $40 on energy. Now I'm co-parenting a support group I wasn't invited to join.
