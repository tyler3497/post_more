**I decided to become a morning person and immediately regretted having this much ambition before sunrise.**

*For context, my previous 5 AM routine was rolling over, realizing it was 5 AM, and whispering "not today" to my ceiling fan.*

I joined the gym's 5 AM Club because the flyer said `OWN YOUR MORNING` in a font that looked like it lifts weights. I thought, sure, I can own a morning. How hard can it be?

## Day One: Enthusiasm. Day Two: Regret.

It turns out the 5 AM Club is not a club. It's a support group for people who made a terrible pact with their alarm clocks.

- Monday: I showed up. The regulars clapped like I'd returned from war.
- Tuesday: I tried to cancel. They said you can't quit, only "take a strategic rest day"
- Wednesday: The elliptical recognized me and sighed

> "You left me on read for three years," the elliptical seemed to say at level 2. "I wrote you a chapbook. It's called *Ode to the Girl Who Only Came for the Free Towels*."

*The front desk guy says it's been sensitive since someone used it as a coat rack in 2023.*

### The Machine Has Feelings Now

Now my elliptical leaves sad poetry on Post-its in the cup holder:

- *you said you'd commit / but your commitment / was a free trial*
- *i spin / you spin / we are not spinning together*

I tried to explain I'm not a morning person, I'm a person who occasionally occurs in the morning.

The club president — a woman who drinks pre-workout like orange juice — told me:

> "We don't do excuses here, we do burpees. The elliptical is sensitive. Apologize with electrolytes."

So I bring it a smoothie. It still judges me. It increased the incline when I mentioned pilates.

---

**Update:** I'm still in the club. The elliptical and I are in counseling. My hamstrings have filed a complaint and my alarm clock has started bargaining.

*SATIRE / PARODY — entirely fictional, the elliptical is fine, I'm fine, we're all fine at 5 AM.*
