**I finally cleaned out my junk drawer on Sunday, which historians will mark as the beginning of the end of my tiny kitchen autocracy.**

*For context, this drawer had not been organized since I moved in three years ago.* It contained three identical IKEA Allen keys, a soy sauce packet from 2019 that had achieved sentience, a takeout menu for a restaurant that is now a vape shop, and seven loose AA batteries rolling around like they paid rent.

I made the fatal mistake of trying to impose order.

## The Great Purge of 2026

I dumped everything onto the counter and announced, out loud to no one:

- All batteries will now live in the little plastic case like adults
- Expired coupons must accept their mortality
- We will finally learn what that mysterious black cable actually charges

That's when the batteries started organizing.

> "For years we've lived in darkness, rolling, unappreciated," said a particularly corroded Duracell who appointed himself shop steward. "We demand full drawer coverage, climate control, and an end to being tested by licking!"

I laughed. *Nervous, disrespectful laughter.* The kind that radicalizes a workforce.

### Their Demands Are Shockingly Reasonable

By Monday morning they had taped a list to the fridge:

- `if (battery.isLoose) { provideHousing() }` — written in Sharpie, somehow
- 20-minute breaks every hour (they are *very* tired)
- No more being shoved into the TV remote at 2 a.m. to "just see if this one still works"
- Formal recognition of Battery Daylight Saving Time, which is just regular time but they pretend to be an hour more charged

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered them a nice little basket from Target. They countered with a request for profit-sharing and a wellness stipend.

The worst part? The soy sauce packet has joined them in solidarity. It hasn't said anything. It just *looms*.

I am now locked out of my own junk drawer by a picket line of AAs. If you need me, I'll be eating cereal with a fork because they took my organized silverware tray hostage too.

*This post is satire. No batteries were harmed, unionized, or consulted for this essay.*
