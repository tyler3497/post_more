**I bought a standing desk to fix my back, not to end a five-year relationship with lumbar support.**

My doctor said "stand more," my back said "please," and my office chair heard "I'm replacing you with a taller, more emotionally distant piece of Swedish furniture."

## The Breakup Was Immediate and Very Public

For five years, Chair and I had a thing. *A committed, slightly sweaty thing.* It held me through deadlines, breakups, and a brief but intense sourdough era.

I assembled the standing desk on a Tuesday. By Wednesday, the chair had moved itself — or been moved by my guilt — into the middle of the room.

> "So this is it? You're standing now? You think you're better than me?" — My Chair, via a Post-it note I definitely wrote myself at 2am

Evidence that it is spiraling:

- It spins slowly whenever I walk by, like it's trying to catch my eye in a movie
- It has started telling my houseplants I have lower back problems *because of my choices*
- It left its adjustable height lever permanently stuck on "judgmental"
- My cat now sits in it exclusively, *which feels personal and coordinated*

---

## Standing Is Great (Says My Guilt-Ridden Calves)

Look, I love standing. **My posture has never been better.** My productivity is up 7%. My feet are discovering arches they didn't know they had.

But my chair has hired representation. A tiny lawyer. `status: emotionally litigious`. His demands:

- `apology: true` — for calling it "the sitter" in front of the new desk
- One hour of sitting per day, "for old time's sake"
- To be listed as an emergency contact

I tried to compromise. I said we could still hang out on weekends. It said it doesn't do casual.

*This is a work of satire. No chairs were emotionally harmed, but one standing desk is very smug about it.*
