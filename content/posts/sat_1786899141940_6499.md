**I just wanted 10 cents off the canned beans.**

*I did not consent to entering an emotionally intimate relationship with a piece of plastic that tracks my self-destruction in real time.*

## The Loyalty Began Innocently

Last month I was buying groceries post-breakup, which is to say I was buying *feelings with barcodes*. The cashier said, "Want to save today?" and I said yes, like an idiot who thinks savings are free.

Fast-forward 23 days and this card knows me better than my mother.

- It knows I bought **four tubs of mint chocolate chip** in one week and one very lonely celery stalk as a cover story
- It knows I switched from *couples-size lasagna* to single-serve mac at exactly 11:47 p.m. on a Tuesday
- It has clocked that I pace the chip aisle for 11 minutes then leave with carrots I will absolutely not eat
- `purchase_history = ["Ben & Jerry's x6", "frozen pizza", "kleenex", "that one sad cucumber"]`

> "We noticed you haven't bought your usual 2-person tacos lately. Is everything okay, babe? Here's 20% off comfort queso and a free therapy pamphlet from Aisle 7." — My Loyalty Card's Weekly Email, subject line: *we're worried about you*

I tried to unsubscribe. It sent a coupon instead. **A coupon with my name on it and a tiny sad-face emoji.**

### My Mom Called Yesterday

She asked how I was doing. I said "fine." She believed me.

The loyalty card did not.

It printed a receipt that was 19 inches long, most of which was just:

> "Honey. 47% of your cart is now just freezer waffles and hope. Please talk to someone who isn't self-checkout."

---

I went back to cancel the card. The customer service lady scanned it and whispered, *"Oh wow, you really went through it, huh?"*

I'm keeping the 10 cents discount. I'm also keeping my emotionally intelligent overlord who now gives me 50 bonus points every time I cry in dairy.

*[ SATIRE / PARODY — entirely fictional, no grocery stores or loyalty cards were emotionally consulted, my breakup is doing fine and so is my cart ]*
