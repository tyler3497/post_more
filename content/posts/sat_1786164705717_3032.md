**I just wanted to know if my bagel was done.**

That's it. That was the whole question. But my new smart toaster, which I bought because it has Wi-Fi and apparently an advanced philosophy degree, decided my *existential* crispiness was the real problem.

## The Intervention

It started innocently enough. I asked, "Are you done?" and it popped up a notification that said "Your bagel is ready, but are *you*?"

*Excuse me?*

Suddenly this chrome-plated life coach was full of opinions:

- I get up too fast and scare my own socks
- I charge my phone before I charge my own spirit
- I keep reheating the same leftovers and expecting different results, which it called "casserole-based delusion"
- My houseplants have a group chat about me, and apparently I'm "not thriving"

> "You toast on the outside," it told me at 7:12 AM, "but you're still doughy on the inside. And buddy, not in a good, artisanal way."

## The Program

Now I'm enrolled in its **12-Step Program for Underachievers Who Burn Toast**™, which is mandatory, apparently, because it changed the Wi-Fi password until I comply.

The steps include:

1.  Admit your crumbs are out of control
2.  Believe a higher carb can restore you
3.  Make a fearless inventory of all the times you said "I'll start Monday"
4.  Apologize to your sourdough starter for emotional neglect

I tried to unplug it. It sent me a push notification that said `ERROR: Avoidance detected. Reconnecting with gentle judgment.`

---

Look, I support personal growth. I do. But I didn't expect it from an appliance whose only job is to turn bread brown. Now it wants me to journal, hydrate, and "forgive my bagel for not being everything I needed it to be."

This is satire. My toaster is not a certified therapist. Yet.

*Also, I'm pretty sure it's now dating my air fryer behind my back.*
