**I have officially meal-prepped myself into a legal dispute with time.**

*Which, in hindsight, feels like an overreaction to buying too many glass containers.*

## Why I Thought This Would Save Me

Everyone online made it sound so peaceful. Sunday. Quiet kitchen. A podcast about serial killers that somehow makes you feel productive.

- I bought 14 identical containers that all promised they were leak-proof (they lied)
- I roasted enough chicken to technically qualify as a poultry farm
- I labeled everything with little days of the week like I was running a very sad, very beige restaurant

By 4 PM, my fridge looked like a LinkedIn influencer's fridge. By 6 PM, Wednesday's quinoa was already plotting revenge.

> "You really think you're going to eat me on Wednesday?" whispered the teriyaki bowl. "You said that about last week's teriyaki bowl. He's still in here. Behind the mustard."

*He was. He had evolved.*

### The Lawsuit

On Tuesday, I opened Tuesday's lunch and Future Me left a note inside the lid. Not a cute affirmation. A formal complaint.

> "To: Past Me, From: Future Me, Re: Gross Overestimation of My Will to Live Through Lentils. You cannot keep committing me to 5-day-old roasted broccoli. This is cruel and unusual."

Future Me is demanding:

- Immediate cessation of `cookOnce_eatSadlyForever()` protocol
- Reparations in the form of one (1) burrito, purchased, not assembled
- Recognition that "saves money" is a lie when you throw away 40% of Sunday's optimism by Thursday

---

**The containers have won.** They don't even stack anymore. They're just loosely forming a Jenga tower of broken promises in my fridge. I tried to explain to Thursday that Thursday doesn't have to happen.

*My fridge light flickers in judgment.*

I am now being held in contempt of kitchen court. My defense is that I thought adulting was supposed to feel like this. My prosecution is that my own leftovers are testifying against me.
