**I finally organized my junk drawer and unlocked its final form: sentient landlord.**

*I should have known something was wrong when the tape measure started itemizing expenses.*

For seven years, that drawer was a peaceful anarchy. Then I bought drawer dividers on sale. Big mistake.

## The Eviction Notice

After 45 minutes of sorting, I stepped back, proud. Everything had a place. The drawer did not agree.

- The 9-volt batteries formed a coalition and demanded to be stored "upright with dignity"
- The takeout menus from 2019 unionized and requested PTO
- One mysterious key I've never owned now claims squatter's rights and wants a forwarding address
- The roll of duct tape is offering unsolicited life advice and it is *weirdly accurate*

> "You categorized us. You labeled us. You put the googly eyes in a separate compartment from the googly-eye glue. Do you know what that does to a family?" — The Drawer, via a Post-it note that appeared while I was blinking

## I Tried to Negotiate

I explained this was for efficiency. The drawer explained this was gentrification.

```js
if (drawer.isOrganized === true) {
  drawer.rent = "$400 + emotional labor";
  owner.peace = null;
  owner.regret = "immense";
}
```

Now there is a tiny ledger taped inside. Line items include: "Emotional damages from being called 'junk' — $50" and "Re-homing fee for paperclips — $75, payable in snacks."

---

**I paid.** Not in cash. In attention. I sit there every evening now, listening to the rubber bands discuss supply chain logistics while the flashlight that never worked suddenly works perfectly and shines directly into my soul.
