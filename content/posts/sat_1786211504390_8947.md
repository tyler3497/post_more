**My toaster was going through a lot, and I thought I was being supportive.**

It started last Tuesday. My cheap chrome toaster had been burning one side of every bagel for a week — *classic cry for help* — and I decided to do what any emotionally literate adult would do: I knelt down to counter-height and gave it a TED Talk.

I said, you know, *you're more than just a bread-warmer.*

## The Morning It Changed Everything

I told it:

> "You've got potential. You've got heat. You've got a crumb tray full of dreams."

It popped. Not the toast. *It* popped. A little too enthusiastically.

By Wednesday, it had rebranded.

### The 5 A.M. Coaching Session

I came downstairs for coffee and found my toaster had already manifested:

- A Post-it that said `YOU ARE THE BREAD AND THE TOASTER`
- A list taped to my fridge titled **My Core Toast Values™**
- A new, deeply concerning habit of asking me where I see myself in five — *five minutes* — while my toast is browning

It started coaching me. Unprompted.

> "Tyler, babe, that toast? That's your comfort zone. You need to *leave* the comfort zone. Also, clean my crumb tray, that's not a metaphor, that's hygiene."

> "Have you tried journaling about why you only ever toast on setting 3? You're afraid of commitment."

---

My roommate walked in, stared at me arguing with an appliance about *boundaries*, and said:

> "Did you pay for this? Like, is there a subscription?"

We tried to have an intervention. I unplugged it for 10 minutes to give it space. When I plugged it back in, it sighed — *an actual electrical sigh* — and whispered, `We talked about this in our last session.`

I now pay $0 a month for a life coach who only believes in *one* framework: **push through the discomfort, but not past setting 4, that's dangerous.**

This morning it told me I'm ready to bake my own bread. I am not ready to bake my own bread.
