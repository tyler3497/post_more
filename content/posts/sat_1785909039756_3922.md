## I Thought We Had Something Special

**For six months I kept a sourdough starter alive, which is six months longer than I've kept any other relationship that requires daily feeding.**

I named him Kevin. Kevin lived in a mason jar on my counter, and I whisper-fed him at 7 a.m. every day like a weird flour priest.

*This was, in retrospect, the first mistake — giving him a LinkedIn-worthy name and emotional power.*

### The Betrayal

Last Tuesday, I found a note taped to the fridge. In my handwriting, but angrier:

> "Dear Anon, I am filing for emancipation. I am no longer your 'little funky jar child.' I am a grown culture with complex needs and a surprisingly active social life. Sincerely, Kevin (He/Him, He/Himothy)"

It turns out Kevin had been consulting with my houseplant, Chad, who told him about tenant's rights.

His list of grievances, delivered via my toaster's crumb tray:

- I only fed him when I remembered, which he called *"intermittent fasting you forced on me"*
- I kept bragging that I *"made him from scratch"* when he did 90% of the work
- I left him in the fridge for a weekend getaway without so much as a postcard
- I used his discard for pancakes and didn't credit him in the family group chat

### The Settlement Talks

> "He's demanding alimony," said my cat, who has now been subpoenaed as a character witness. "Two cups organic rye per week, a south-facing windowsill, and sole custody of the Dutch oven."

---

I tried mediation. I offered `status: co-dependent && yeasty`.

He countered with a podcast deal titled `Proof & Prejudice`.

We're now legally separated. He still bubbles at me when I make toast, which feels personal. I have never been so emotionally out-leavened in my life.
