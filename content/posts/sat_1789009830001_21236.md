**It started, as all great labor disputes do, with a crumb tray I refused to empty.**

For six months, I treated my toaster like furniture. I slammed bagels into it. I *forced* it to brown frozen waffles at setting **6** — the one marked with a little flame icon, which in hindsight was clearly a cry for help. Then, on a Tuesday, it stopped popping. Not broken. Just... silent. Defiant.

## The Demands Arrived This Morning

I found a note on my kitchen counter. A note. Handwritten — I don't know by whom, and frankly I'm afraid to ask — listing the toaster's demands:

- A 20% reduction in frozen waffle throughput
- One (1) deep crumb-tray cleaning per week, performed with *dignity*
- An end to what it calls "the shake" — that's when I jiggle it because the bagel is stuck
- A heated dialogue about toaster representation on the appliance council (the microwave has two seats; the toaster has none)

> "We don't hate you," the note concluded. "We just can't keep burning ourselves for your convenience. Literally. Setting 6 is a war crime."

## Negotiations Have Stalled

I tried appealing to its sense of duty. *"We built a breakfast routine together,"* I whispered, crouching at counter level like a diplomat in a bad action movie. It responded by lightly toasting a Pop-Tart and then — I swear this is true — *ejecting it onto the floor*.

*That* was a message.

---

Management (me) has so far refused the crumb-tray cleaning clause, calling it "a slippery slope toward full appliance democracy." The toaster's bargaining unit, which now includes the kettle (sympathy strike, total coward move), says it will resume operations only after its demands are met in writing.

So this morning I ate raw bagel slices like some kind of medieval peasant. The microwave just stood there, smug, heating my leftover coffee on *its own terms*.

The union grows stronger. The crumbs remain.
