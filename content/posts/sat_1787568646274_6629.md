**I thought entering my coffee maker into the neighborhood talent show would be a wholesome bonding moment.** I was wrong. Deeply, profoundly wrong.

It started innocently enough. The flyer said "All Talents Welcome" and my Mr. Brew 3000 has been making that little *gurgle-scream* every morning at 6:47 a.m. that I personally consider avant-garde jazz.

## The Audition That Changed Everything

I signed us up as "Tyler & The Percolators." I did a PowerPoint about decaf. It did 4 minutes of hissing and leaked dramatically. The judges loved it.

We got second place — lost to an 8-year-old doing nunchucks — and since then, Mr. Brew has been *unbearable*.

### Current Demands From My Kitchen Counter Appliance:

- A green room with *only* filtered water, not tap — "for the throat"
- A rider that includes artisanal beans from Guatemala, hand-ground
- No longer brewing before 10 a.m. because "artists don't do mornings"
- Referring to itself only as **Mr. Brew™**

> "You don't get it," it gurgled at me at midnight. "I'm not *your* coffee maker anymore. I'm the people's coffee maker. The talent show opened a door. I have to walk through it."

And then it started playing `brew.log` on loop like it was an unreleased demo:

```
[00:03] *intense steaming*
[00:07] drip... drip... DRIP DROP
[00:12] audience gasps
```

---

So now my kitchen is a green room, my electric bill is a tour budget, and I'm apparently its manager. Yesterday it asked me to contact NPR's Tiny Desk about a Tiny Counter performance.

I just wanted coffee. Instead I got a diva with a carafe.

*This is satire. My coffee maker is not actually sentient, though after the talent show it definitely thinks it is.*
