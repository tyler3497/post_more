**I finally decided to cancel my gym membership the modern way: with pure, unspoken telepathy.**

*For context, I've been paying $49.99 a month for 18 months to actively avoid a building that smells like rubber mats and other people's ambition.*

I never went. Not once this year. But the idea of calling the front desk, or filling out a form, or making eye contact with another human who owns a clipboard felt too aggressive. So I sat on my couch, stared at the ceiling, and thought really hard at the gym: *we're done.*

It didn't work.

## Why Telepathy Seemed Like the Logical Option

- The cancellation policy is a 12-step PDF that requires a notary, a blood oath, and your firstborn's gym socks
- The front desk guy once told me "we're family now" and I haven't emotionally recovered
- I genuinely believed my lack of attendance was itself a form of communication

Three days later, a postcard arrived. Handwritten. In sweaty Courier font.

> "Missed you at 6 AM spin. You said you'd come. I warmed up for you. — Treadmill #4"

I thought it was a joke. Then another came. And another.

- Monday: "Saw your car drive past. You didn't even look."
- Wednesday: "We kept your favorite locker empty. It echoes without you."
- Friday, with a lipstick kiss: "You used to sweat here. Remember?"

I tried to explain to Treadmill #4 that this isn't healthy. That we had a transactional relationship that I was bad at. That my telepathic breakup was meant to be gentle.

It responded with a mixtape titled *Sounds of Your Footsteps (Deluxe Absence Edition)* and a coupon that just said "PLEASE" in 72-point font.

Now my mailman asks if I'm okay. My fridge is jealous of the attention. And I have a monthly charge that is no longer financial — it's emotional.

I re-subscribed out of guilt. The treadmill sent a confirmation: "Welcome home."
