**I have never been in a courtroom before, but apparently my refrigerator has — several times, as the attorney of record.**

It started, as all legal battles do, with a hum. A low, smug, middle-of-the-night hum that my Frigidaire — let's call him Greg — insisted was "just his personality." I filed the noise complaint with my landlord at 3:14 a.m., a time I now recognize as the legal equivalent of poking a bear.

## Exhibit A: The Things Greg Said in His Defense

- "I have kept your leftovers at a safe 37°F for four consecutive years, and this is the thanks I get."
- "The humming is *ambient*. Restaurants pay thousands for this kind of atmosphere."
- "Counter-claim: you open my door eleven times a day without a clear plan. Who is the real noise violator here?"

> "Order! Order!" — the cat we appointed as judge, who fell asleep during opening statements and remained the most competent party in the room.

My landlord, who has mediated exactly one dispute (between himself and a parking meter), suggested "binding arbitration" in the kitchen at dawn. Greg arrived with a stack of warranty pamphlets he called "precedent." I arrived with earplugs.

## The Verdict

The judge — by which I mean the cat, who had now claimed the jury box as a bed — ruled in Greg's favor on the condition that he hum in a *lower register* after midnight and that I stop slamming his freezer door like it owes me money.

Honestly? Fair.
