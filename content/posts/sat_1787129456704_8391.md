**I was told plants like company, so naturally I turned my apartment into a botanical dating app and it worked way too well.**

It was an innocent Tuesday. My monstera, Frederica, had been dropping leaves like *hints*. Not dead leaves, dramatic ones. She was staring at the empty corner by the window and I swear if plants could sigh, hers would have subtitles: *wow, must be nice to have options*.

So I bought a snake plant. Tall, stoic, low-maintenance. I named him Greg. For company. For vibes. For *closure*.

## The Courtship I Did Not Consent to Supervise

Here's what I witnessed over the next 72 hours:

- Day 1: polite nodding. Some light photosynthesis near each other. Very respectful.
- Day 2: Greg leaned. Like, actually leaned. That's not phototropism, that's *intent*.
- Day 3: I came home to find Frederica's aerial root draped around Greg's pot like a rom-com poster from 2003.

> "We really bonded over your inconsistent watering schedule and shared trauma of being owned by someone who Googles 'is tap water okay' at 2 a.m.," Frederica explained in a note she definitely did not write. "We're taking the good light. Don't wait up. - F & G"

I tried to give them space. I am an ally.

### The Note On My Fridge

It was written in what appears to be soil smears and absolute confidence:

- Thanks for the humidity tray, it slapped
- We've decided to elope to the balcony where the sun actually hits after 11 a.m.
- Please forward mail, do not water us out of spite
- P.S. The pothos knows what you did with the scissors and it's not happy

---

I tried to negotiate. I said `relationshipStatus = "it's complicated (photosynthesis)"` isn't a legal category and my lease says no subletting to plant couples. They did not care.

My apartment is now down two plants, up one passive-aggressive thank-you note, and my windowsill has *commitment issues*. If you need me, I'll be explaining to my landlord that my greenery filed for emancipation and took the good scissors as a wedding gift.
