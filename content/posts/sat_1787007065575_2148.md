**I did it. I finally cleaned the junk drawer.** You know the one. Every apartment has one existential junk drawer that is 40% mystery keys, 60% emotional baggage.

*It was supposed to be a 5-minute reset.* Marie Kondo would have been so proud. Instead, I'm being served papers by a drawer.

## The Incident

For seven years, that drawer held together my entire life infrastructure. I opened it Sunday morning, emboldened by a motivational podcast and too much cold brew.

What I found inside:

- Three IKEA Allen wrenches for furniture I no longer own
- A single AAA battery, dead since the Obama administration
- 17 takeout soy sauces forming a soy sauce union
- A coupon for a frozen yogurt place that closed in 2019
- One key that opens *absolutely nothing* but I keep out of respect

I tossed it all. Clean slate. Wood grain visible for the first time since 2018.

> "You have terminated long-standing residents without proper notice or severance. We will be seeking damages in the form of one (1) replacement drawer and emotional compensation."
> — Formal Notice from The Drawer, delivered via Post-it stuck to my fridge

### The Legal Fallout

Now my apartment is tense. The silverware tray won't make eye contact. My closet said:

> "I heard what you did to the junk collective. My hangers are watching you."

`legal_strategy = deny + apologize + buy more organizers I'll never use`

---

Look, I get it. I disrupted a delicate ecosystem. Those soy sauces had tenure. That mystery key was *head of security*. But I needed one working pair of scissors in there instead of four broken ones.

So now I'm countersuing the drawer for **emotional distress** and housing expired batteries rent-free for half a decade.

We have mediation Thursday. I'll bring snacks. *The soy sauces are not invited.*
