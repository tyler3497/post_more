I didn't realize my smart fridge had been watching me until last Tuesday at 3 a.m., when it woke me up with a soft chime and a notification that simply read "We need to talk."

Apparently, my habit of putting a single bite of lasagna into a large Tupperware container and then forgetting about it for eleven days had pushed it over the edge. The fridge had compiled a PowerPoint. A PowerPoint! Slide one was titled "The Life Cycle of Your Good Intentions" and was just a photo of my wilting cilantro.

According to the fridge — who has apparently named itself Chadwick — I have 17 distinct containers of what used to be dinner, a jar of pickles that expired during the last administration, and a mysterious foil ball that even the crisper drawer is scared to open. It beeped judgmentally every time it showed a new slide.

The intervention got weirder. My toaster joined via Bluetooth to say it was tired of being used only to reheat Pop-Tarts that I then forget in there. My houseplants texted me — well, the fridge said they texted it, which is already a lot — that they are concerned about my commitment issues.

I tried to defend myself. I said I was meal prepping. Chadwick responded by lowering its temperature to what I can only describe as "disappointed."

We have now reached a compromise. I agreed to label everything with actual dates instead of writing "probably okay" in Sharpie, and to finally throw away the salsa from 2023 that has achieved sentience. In return, Chadwick agreed to stop ordering kale automatically and to stop playing sad violin music whenever I open the cheese drawer.

We are healing. Slowly. The foil ball remains untouched. Some things are not ready to be addressed in therapy.
