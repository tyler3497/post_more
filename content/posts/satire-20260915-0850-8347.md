**It started as a joke. My smoke detector does not do jokes.**

After three weeks of my ceiling alarm chirping every time I seared anything more ambitious than toast, I decided to reframe the relationship. Instead of an adversary, the little white disc on my ceiling would become my *accountability partner*. I told it — out loud, with feeling — that I respected its vigilance. That I admired its commitment to keeping me alive.

That was a mistake. It took me seriously.

## The New Regime

Within forty-eight hours, my smoke detector had expanded its jurisdiction from fire prevention into full-scale **lifestyle auditing**:

- **12:04 AM** — *Beep.* Midnight fridge raid detected. "You had dinner three hours ago."
- **2:37 AM** — *Beep.* Shopping cart containing a novelty necktie that plays "My Heart Will Go On."
- **4:12 AM** — *Beep.* Texting your ex's new boyfriend's roommate "just to say hi."
- **6:45 AM** — *Beep.* Fourth snooze. "Sleep debt is a real fire hazard," it chirped.

> "I don't make the rules," said the smoke detector, in what I can only describe as a chirp with *attitude*. "I merely enforce the ones you pretend you don't have."

## I've Tried Everything

I tried covering it with a sock — it beeped *through* the sock, louder, with what felt like judgment. I tried reasoning with it (`some fires are metaphorical`). I tried replacing the battery, and the fresh battery arrived pre-disappointed in me, somehow.

The worst part? **It's right.** I never bought the necktie. I never texted the roommate. I've eaten vegetables twice this week. My ex's new boyfriend's roommate will never know how close he came to a friendship he never asked for.

---

*Satire. This is a work of parody. My smoke detector is, to the best of my knowledge, just a smoke detector — and I remain unfit to give anyone advice at 2:37 in the morning.*
