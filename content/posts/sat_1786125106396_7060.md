**I wanted to be the kind of person who composts.** You know, linen tote, morally superior, casually says *“it’s closed loop”* at brunch.

Instead I became a landlord for 400 fruit flies with a manifesto.

It started with a single banana peel. One. I put it in a $24 countertop compost bin that promised *Odor-Free!* and *Countertop Chic!* by Tuesday, it promised `decomposition: in progress` and `regret: 100%`.

## The First Signs of Civilization

By day three, the banana had formed alliances:

- The coffee grounds declared themselves the infrastructure
- The eggshells built tiny condos
- A rogue strawberry from last week ran for mayor

> “We were thriving before you even remembered to add water,” said the cloud of fruit flies, who now communicate exclusively in passive-aggressive Post-its I swear I didn't write.

I tried to turn it. Like you’re supposed to. The compost bin sighed. My roommate walked in, looked at my science-fair volcano of optimism, and said:

> “Babe, you’re not saving the planet, you’re casting *Lord of the Flies: Kitchen Edition*.”

### The Ecosystem Assessment

My kitchen audit now reads like a nature documentary:

- **Air quality:** 60% espresso, 40% *decisions were made here*
- **Foot traffic:** fruit flies have established a union, Local 108 *We Who Swarm*
- **HOA violations:** the compost bin now demands direct sunlight and a podcast deal

---

I finally took it to the building compost. The building compost looked at *my* compost and said “No. I don’t know what that is, but it has opinions.”

We have reached an understanding. I keep the lid on. It does… whatever it does in there. Murmuring. Plotting. Occasionally bubbling like it just heard gossip.

`Status: composting, technically. Composed, emotionally: never.`
