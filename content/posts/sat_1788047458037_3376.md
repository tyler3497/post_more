**I wanted to be a morning person, so I did the most dangerous thing a 30-year-old with a desk job can do: I stretched before 8 a.m.**

*This is already how horror movies start.*

## The Plan

Inspired by a reel of a woman touching her toes while smiling like she pays taxes early, I committed to 10 minutes of daily stretching. Nothing crazy. Just me, a yoga mat that still had the price tag on it, and a YouTube guy named Flex.

Day 1 was hopeful. Day 2 was humbling. By Day 3, my body had called an all-hands meeting without me.

## The Grievances

I woke up Wednesday to find a Post-it on my fridge, written in what looked like tight calf muscle:

- Formal request for 48-hour notice before any attempt at a forward fold
- Immediate end to what my hamstrings are calling "unprovoked aggression at 6:47 a.m."
- One (1) ergonomic office chair that doesn't sound like a haunted recliner
- Recognition that "touch your toes" is a suggestion, not a federal mandate

> "We have tolerated years of sitting like a cashew for 9 hours straight," said my left hamstring, who has apparently appointed itself spokesperson. "You cannot just suddenly act like you're in Cirque du Soleil. This is a hostile work environment."

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered extra water, a banana, even `leg_day = false` in my calendar.

*My quads laughed. Out loud. I felt it.*

### Where We Are Now

HR (my spine) has proposed a compromise: I get to do gentle neck rolls, and my hamstrings agree to not cramp dramatically every time I sneeze.

I have accepted these terms. We are healing.
