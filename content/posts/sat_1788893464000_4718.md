# I Planted One Tomato Seed as a Joke and Now I'm the Legal Guardian of 47 Heirloom Tomatoes

**It was supposed to be a joke.** Three weeks ago I dropped a single tomato seed into a chipped mug on my windowsill and told my roommate, "Behold, my farm." I named it Kevin. Then Kevin grew. And grew. And then, somehow, *kept growing* — because I had, in a fit of ambition, transplanted him into the backyard.

## The Situation

There are now **47 heirloom tomatoes** ripening in my yard. I know the exact number because my neighbor Cheryl counted them and slipped the tally under my door with the words *"your responsibility"* underlined three times. She has since started calling me "the tomato guy," which is a title you don't earn. It just *happens to you*.

- I have been asked to bring "a side" to three separate cookouts. A side.
- The HOA sent a formal letter about my "agricultural operation." I live in a suburb.
- A child on the sidewalk asked if I was "the tomato wizard." I said yes. I regret everything.

> "You can't just abandon them," Cheryl told me, arms crossed, staring at a vine that's now thicker than my arm. "They're *heirlooms*. That's basically ancestry. You have dependents now."

I tried explaining that it was one seed. As a joke. Cheryl said, *"So was parenthood, probably,"* and walked away.

---

*Update:* I have started referring to the tomatoes as "the boys." I built them a little trellis out of an old curtain rod and pure guilt. Last night I watered them at 2 a.m. because I dreamed they were thirsty. Kevin has produced eight (8) offspring. I no longer remember what it felt like to not have opinions about soil pH.

The lesson is clear: **never plant anything as a joke, because nature does not understand jokes.** Nature understands only commitment, and nitrogen.
