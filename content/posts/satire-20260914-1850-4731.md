**It started innocently enough.** Like all of my worst decisions, the Fridge Library began as a joke I made to myself while staring into the white glow at 2 a.m.

## The Grand Opening

I bought a tiny wooden shelf, mounted it on the middle rack, and shelved the condiments like a proper collection. The ketchup went under *Spicy Fiction*. The mustard went in Reference. My roommate said nothing, which I chose to interpret as editorial support.

Then I got ambitious. I wrote little due-date cards. Because a library without a checkout system is just a pile of food, and I refuse to live in chaos.

- **Week 1:** The Greek yogurt was checked out by my roommate. Returned late. Fine assessed: one (1) spoonful, payable to me, the head librarian.
- **Week 2:** The leftover lasagna was renewed four times and is now, legally speaking, a permanent resident.
- **Week 3:** The half-onion developed a following. People come by just to look at it.

## The Reckoning

This morning I found a note slipped under my bedroom door. It was written on the back of a due-date slip, in handwriting I can only describe as *aggressively dairy*:

> "The Library Board has reviewed your account. You currently owe 47 overdue fines, one mysterious container labeled DO NOT OPEN - SCIENCE, and emotional damages for the Incident with the Pickles. Please report to the crisper drawer for a hearing."

I do not know who the Library Board is. I live alone. My roommate moved out in March.

---

Tonight, I will return the leftover Chinese food I checked out six weeks ago and pray for amnesty. The Library forgives, but the Library never forgets. *The card catalog has my name in it now.*
