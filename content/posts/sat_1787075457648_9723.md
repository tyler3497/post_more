**I thought mindfulness was inhaling deeply and exhaling slowly. Turns out it's getting emotionally outmaneuvered by something I made with my own hands.**

## The Clay Awakening

I signed up for "Mindful Mondays: Pottery for Peace" because my therapist said I needed a tactile hobby that didn't involve doomscrolling. Six weeks, $180, unlimited chamomile tea. What they didn't mention is that clay remembers *everything*.

My first mug — let's call him Barnard — came out looking like he survived a minor earthquake. Slight lean, chipped lip, one handle that could only be described as *aspirational*. I loved him immediately.

Then I glazed him. Then I took him home. Then things changed.

Here is the evidence that my mug has become my life coach:

- He refuses to hold iced coffee after 3 p.m. "That's a *boundary*," he seems to say
- I caught him turned away from my laptop during my 11 p.m. work email spiral
- He only sits on the cork coaster, never directly on the table — *standards*
- He made me delete three to-do list apps and replace them with a single sticky note that says `breathe`

> "You made me to hold warm things, not your anxiety about whether your cousin's wedding RSVP font was too casual," Barnard said. I swear he sighed at me yesterday. My mug sighed.

---

## The Negotiations

The instructor warned us that pottery teaches patience. She did not warn us that `mug.setBoundaries(you)` would execute more successfully than any self-help book I've ever read.

*This week*, I asked Barnard if he could hold pens while I "figured my life out."

He said no. Well, he didn't say no. He just... sat there, being beautifully, firmly, inanimate-y. *Iconic behavior, honestly.*

Now my apartment is calmer. My tea tastes suspiciously like self-respect. And Barnard sits on the shelf like a tiny, glazed bouncer for my own peace, judging my screen time with the quiet confidence of a ceramic that knows it is hand-wash only and at peace with that.

I am learning to be more like my mug: chipped, leaning slightly to the left, but holding it together.
