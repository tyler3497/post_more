**BREAKING:** I finally organized my junk drawer and immediately regretted every life choice that led me to this power trip.

## The Incident

For seven years, my junk drawer existed in a state of *beautiful, democratic chaos*. Batteries, twist ties, three identical Allen keys from furniture I no longer own, and a single chopstick lived together in harmony. Then yesterday at 2:17 p.m., I snapped.

I dumped it out, bought one of those little plastic organizers with the smug compartments, and went full Marie Kondo on it.

Big mistake.

- The scissors are now in the *precision tools* section and have developed an attitude
- The batteries feel *seen* for the first time and won't stop buzzing
- The roll of tape has unionized the adhesives

> "You can't just categorize us and expect no consequences," said my formerly favorite scissors, now perched on the edge of the tray like a tiny middle manager. "I had RANGE before. I was scissors, box opener, herb trimmer. Now I'm... 'crafts.'"

### My Former System Was Better

My roommate walked in while I was actively apologizing to a pair of pliers.

"You good?" she asked.

I tried to explain that organizeDrawer with chaos mode enabled was actually the superior algorithm and that I had disrupted a fragile ecosystem that took years of neglect to perfect.

---

I have since learned that junk drawers are not meant to be organized. They are meant to be a *mysterious cave of potential*. You reach in, you hope.

Now every time I need scissors, they require a formal request and a 24-hour notice. My tape measure keeps asking if I have a permit for measuring things.

If you need me, I'll be slowly returning everything to a single pile while whispering apologies. Management has spoken. The drawer stays messy.
