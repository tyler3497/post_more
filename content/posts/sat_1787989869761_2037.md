**I thought we had an understanding: you absorb my messes, I pretend you aren't disintegrating.**

*That was our unspoken contract for eight months.*

It started Tuesday. My sponge — Sir Scrubbins the Third, a yellow rectangle who has seen more spaghetti sauce than a mob movie — had reached that point where it smelled like regret and held together by pure loyalty. I bought a new one. Bright, bouncy, aggressively unscented.

I didn't think old Sponge would notice. I was wrong.

## How My Kitchen Became a Tell-All

I found the first chapter taped to the fridge:

- A detailed timeline of every dish I left soaking for three days
- A footnote about the time I used him to clean the stove and called it "a spa day"
- An annotated bibliography of crumbs, including the Great Meatball Incident of March

> "Chapter 4: The Night He Chose Paper Towels Over Me," he wrote, in what I can only describe as damp Times New Roman. "I absorbed his shame. He absorbed nothing. Not even basic kitchen hygiene."

*Bold of him to critique my hygiene when he literally lives in a puddle.*

Now my new sponge won't make eye contact. My dish soap has taken sides. My kitchen is a literary salon where everyone is mad at me.

## The Memoir Tour

He's calling it **Soaked: A Life Left Wringing**, available nowhere but loudly narrated at 7 a.m. while I'm trying to make coffee.

- Morning readings by the sink
- A book club consisting of the toothbrush, who relates *way too much*
- A planned sequel about my cutting board and its abandonment issues
- A foreword by the trash can, who has seen things

---

I tried to apologize. I whispered `return sponge_to_glory = true` but the compiler of domestic guilt doesn't work that way. I offered him a nice spot by the plants. He said he needed *space to process*.

**Look, I get it.** He held my life together through breakups, failed sourdough starters, and that week I only ate cereal. But at some point you have to let go, even if your sponge is now a better writer than you'll ever be.

*Satire: this is 100% fictional. No sponges were emotionally harmed in the making of this essay, though one is currently thriving on Substack and charging $5 a month.*
