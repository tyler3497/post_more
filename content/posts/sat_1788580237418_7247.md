**The spot in front of our building is a public, first-come-first-served street parking space. My neighbor Carol disagrees, and frankly, her argument is getting stronger every day.**

## How It Started

Three weeks ago, Carol left a note on my windshield that read, in full: *"Park like a person who was raised."* My parking was, objectively, flawless — six inches from the curb. *Six.* But the note said what it said, and from that moment on, the spot became contested territory.

She started arriving home two minutes earlier every day, like she was racing me to a crime scene. I countered by leaving work at 4:47 instead of 5:00, telling my boss my commute had become "a high-stakes logistical situation." He now thinks I'm in logistics. I'm an accountant.

## How It's Going

Yesterday I found a laminated sign zip-tied to the streetlamp above the spot:

> "RESERVED FOR CAROL'S TOYOTA. UNAUTHORIZED VEHICLES WILL BE JUDGED."
>
> — *Carol*

Laminated. She went to a print shop. This is a woman who once Venmo-requested me for 40 cents of shared detergent, so yes, I believe she meant it.

I escalated proportionally — I hired a guy named Mitch to stand in the spot wearing a reflective vest from 3pm to 6pm, holding a clipboard with nothing on it. It costs $140 a week and my remaining dignity.

The evidence is mounting against me:

- Carol has lived here four years; I have lived here fourteen months
- Her tires "fit the spot like a glass slipper," per this morning's note
- Mitch takes smoke breaks, which Carol exploits with military precision
- The spot, per Carol's latest note, has "retained counsel"

## Where We Go From Here

Tonight I found a business card under my wiper: *"Dewey, Fleecem & Howe — Parking Adjacency Attorneys."* I called. A very tired man told me Carol is "a valued retainer client" and that I should "stop calling this number."

**I have no choice now.** Tomorrow I'm printing my own sign — bigger, laminated twice. This is about principles. This is about justice. This is about a spot that is, legally speaking, just painted asphalt — and I will defend my relationship with it to the death.
