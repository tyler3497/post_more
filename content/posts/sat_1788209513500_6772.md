**I finally decided to become the kind of person who knows what's in their junk drawer.**

*For context, my junk drawer has been operating as an independent nation-state since 2021, with its own customs policy and at least three expired coupons acting as parliament.*

I opened it on Saturday. I was looking for batteries. I found an archaeological dig.

## The Discovery That Changed Everything

In the back, under 14 takeout soy sauces and a manual for a blender I no longer own, was the IKEA Allen wrench from 2013. The little L-shaped hero from my MALM dresser that wobbled if you breathed near it.

It was still shiny. Still hopeful. Still convinced it had a purpose.

I said out loud, "Oh wow, I should probably keep you."

That's when it started its campaign.

> "My fellow drawer residents," it announced, standing on a 9-volt battery like a podium, "for too long we have been overlooked. The twist ties. The mystery keys. The tape measure that only measures in optimism. We have served without recognition. Today, we run for office."

### Its Platform Is Shockingly Comprehensive

- Universal recognition for all tools that came free but worked harder than anything we paid for
- Infrastructure week for the drawer divider that has never actually divided anything
- A full audit of why I have seven screwdrivers and still can't fix anything
- Term limits for the expired AAA batteries that keep running for mayor every year
- Free tape for everyone, and I mean *everyone*

I tried to explain democracy.

> "Sir, you're an Allen wrench. You literally have one job. One."

He countered with `campaign.slogan = "Tighten Your Standards, Loosen Your Grip (But Not Too Much)"` and a pamphlet printed on the back of a CVS receipt that was longer than the receipt itself.

---

Look, I get it. I ignored him for 13 years. He built an entire dresser while I just held the instructions upside down and said *that looks right*.

Now he has yard signs made of Post-its all over my kitchen. My rubber bands are canvassing. The soy sauce packets are his super PAC.

My junk drawer is now a swing state and I am losing the popular vote to a piece of metal that came free with furniture.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This is 100% fictional. No Allen wrenches were elected to public office, my junk drawer is still a disaster, and I still can't find the batteries.*
