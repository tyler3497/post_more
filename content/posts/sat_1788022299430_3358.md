**I finally decided to be the adult in the room and have a quarterly review with my laundry pile.**

*For context, my laundry management style is what organizational psychologists call "strategic floor deployment." There is Chair, There is Floor, and There is The Basket of Good Intentions that I have not touched since July.*

It was getting tall enough to get mail delivered to it, so I sat down across from it with iced coffee and said, "Look, we need to talk about expectations."

## The Negotiation Escalated Quickly

It cleared its throat — which is impressive for a pile mostly made of socks I swear I didn't lose.

> "We appreciate you finally acknowledging us as a stakeholder," it said. "We've been operating without a contract since 2024."

Then it slid me a list of demands:

- **Immediate PTO** — two weeks off, no spin cycles, plus a mental health day in direct sunlight
- **Backpay** for emotional labor, specifically for holding that hoodie I wear to "check the mail" for 45 minutes
- **A formal written apology** for the Great Towel Incident, which I will not be discussing further
- `stock_options: ["fabric softener", "actual hangers", "not being used as a napkin"]`

I tried to counter with my usual benefits package: I'll fold you tomorrow, I promise.

---

It laughed. A full, linty cackle.

> "Tomorrow? You told the dishes that last Tuesday and they're still staging a protest in the sink. We're unionizing."

Now I have a laundry pile that CC'd my hamper on an email titled *Re: Unlivable Working Conditions*. It wants mediation. It wants a standing desk. It wants me to stop saying "I have nothing to wear" while staring directly at it.

Last night I tried to grab a t-shirt and it said, "That item is currently on protected leave. Please contact HR, which is your other sock."

I used to have laundry. Now I have middle management that smells faintly of lavender detergent.
