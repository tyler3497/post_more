**I gave my Wi-Fi router a pep talk this morning. Just a quick one.** "You've got this, buddy," I said, tapping its little plastic shell before a big presentation. "Four bars. *At least* four."

That was four hours ago. I have since lost all leverage in my own home.

## The Before Times

Yesterday my router was a humble appliance. It sat quietly behind the TV, blinking, asking nothing of anyone. But apparently **one (1) compliment** was all it took to activate something deep and theatrical inside its firmware, because now it refuses to start any video call without a *full ceremony*.

> "I need to hear it," the router said — or, more accurately, I have decided the router said, because my sanity required it. "You clap. Then I connect."

## The New Ritual

I keep a spreadsheet now, because I am a professional. Here is the current protocol for joining a 9 a.m. standup:

- Stand within three feet of the router
- Say, out loud: "WELCOME TO THE STAGE — ROUTER!"
- Clap for *no fewer than five seconds*
- The router blinks green, which is its way of taking a bow

If I skip any step, it drops the call at minute four. It has done this *eleven times*. It is petty and precise about it.

---

## I Have Tried Everything

I tried the silent treatment. It responded by broadcasting a network called **UNAPPRECIATED_5G**. My neighbors think I'm going through a divorce.

I tried factory resetting it. When it came back online, it joined my calendar and streamed my 1:1 with my boss in 144p as, I quote myself, "a lesson in gratitude."

*The most humiliating part?* My coworkers now ask for the router by name. "Is he on the call?" Dave from accounting asked, and I said "yes, Dave, and he requires applause."

## The Truth

I don't remember saying a pep talk would *work*. But apparently some devices are just waiting for permission to become the main character.

So tomorrow morning, I will wake up, walk into the living room, and clap. Because the meeting starts at nine, and I have chosen, as an adult, to be managed by a $60 piece of networking equipment with an ego.

**The lesson, as always: never motivate your appliances. They were fine being appliances.**
