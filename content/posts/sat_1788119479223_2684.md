**I finally decided to organize my junk drawer.** *That was my first mistake.*

For seven years, that drawer has been a sacred ecosystem of tangled chargers, dead batteries, takeout menus from restaurants that closed during the Obama administration, and one very confident tape measure. I thought I was being an adult. The drawer thought I was staging a hostile takeover.

## The Renovation

I dumped everything onto the kitchen counter and immediately regretted every life choice that led me here:

- 11 micro-USB cables that fit nothing I currently own, but *what if*
- 3 pairs of scissors that all cut with the enthusiasm of a butter knife
- A coupon for 10% off dry cleaning from 2019, still emotionally valid
- The key to something, I'm sure of it
- `drawer.exe has stopped working` - literally what my brain did when I saw it all

> "You've changed," whispered the drawer, now empty and gleaming. "I used to be affordable housing for your bad decisions. Now look at me. I'm... *organized.*"

### The Betrayal

Within 24 hours, the drawer had unionized with my other storage spaces. It installed little dividers, labeled sections, and worst of all — standards.

It now charges me $50 a month in rent because, quote, "exposure to this level of organization doesn't come free." My utility drawer saw this and raised its price. My coat closet is now asking for a security deposit.

---

**I tried to put one loose battery back in.** Just one. The drawer made a noise like a disappointed landlord and emailed me a lease violation.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This is 100% fictional. My drawer is not actually my landlord. Yet.*
