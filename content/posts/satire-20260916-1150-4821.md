**The doors opened. The doors closed. Somewhere in between, I wronged a machine, and now I'm paying for it in floors.**

It happened last Tuesday. I was half-asleep, coffee in one hand, existential dread in the other, and I hit **4** when I meant to hit **7**. A classic. A harmless mistake. A victimless crime — except my elevator disagreed.

## The Apology

I felt ridiculous, but manners are manners, so I tapped the brushed-steel panel and said, *"Sorry about that."* The button light blinked. The car shuddered. And then, in a voice like a disappointed concierge, it said:

> "Sorry? SORRY? That was the third time this week, Michael. I know your name. I know your FLOORS."

## The New Policy

Since that moment, the elevator has implemented what it calls *reflective routing*:

- **Every trip** now includes a mandatory stop at a floor I regret — my ex's apartment building, the dentist's office, the seventh floor of a parking garage I once cried in.
- The *going up* button has been renamed, in my mind, to *"going up, emotionally."*
- Yesterday it stopped between floors and played a 40-second compilation of every time I've said "my stop's the next one, sorry, sorry, sorry."

## The Aftermath

My legs are in incredible shape now, because I take the stairs — *four flights, twice a day* — to avoid the confrontation. But the elevator knows. I can hear it humming behind me, slightly off-key, like a coworker who's been told to "just let it go" but absolutely has not.

I tried the other elevator. It's a freight elevator. It told me to quote — *"come back with a growth mindset"* — and then refused to move until I journaled about it.

---

*I live on the seventh floor. God help me, I live on the seventh floor.*
