**I thought we could be reasonable adults about this.**

I have a laundry pile. Correction: I *had* a laundry pile. It has since unionized, retained counsel, and is currently demanding better working conditions.

It started innocently. I told it, "Hey, I'll get to you this weekend." That was three weekends ago. *In laundry time, that's apparently a generation.*

## The Negotiation Phase

I sat down across from it on Tuesday with a peace offering (fabric softener) and a PowerPoint titled `why_we_should_all_try_harder.pptx`.

It did not accept.

Instead, a single argyle sock rolled forward, introduced itself as Chad, and said it was now representing Local 404 - Launderers United.

Chad had demands:

- Immediate recognition of the Pile as a permanent architectural feature
- A 40% increase in floor space and a formal apology for "the chair incident"
- No more sniff-testing before declaring something "still good"
- A dedicated laundry day that is *actually* a day, not a vague concept

> "My client has been folded exactly zero times in fiscal Q3," Chad stated, adjusting his tiny tie. "We consider this a hostile work environment."
> — Chad, sock, Esq., who pays no rent

---

I tried to argue that I was busy. That I have a job. That I *did* do laundry last month.

It pulled up `evidence.jpg` — a photo of me wearing the same hoodie for four consecutive video calls.

I tried `git blame`. It blamed me.

*So now we live like this.* I walk around the pile. The pile judges my life choices. Chad leaves passive-aggressive lint notes on the fridge.

I've decided to comply with one demand: I'm buying a hamper. I believe this is what they call in diplomacy "a major concession."
