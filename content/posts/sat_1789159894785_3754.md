**I have a confession, and it is worse than it sounds.**

It started innocently. A friend invited me to a neighborhood book club, and I said yes because I wanted to look cultured in photos. My plan was simple: skim the Wikipedia summary, nod thoughtfully, and spend the meeting studying the author's back-cover portrait like a TSA agent with a grudge.

*In my defense, the author photos were fascinating.*

## The Evidence

- The memoirist whose headshot was clearly taken mid-existential-crisis
- The debut novelist posed with a typewriter he has obviously never touched
- The poet standing in fog. Just. Fog.
- One man whose bio said "lives with his wife" and whose photo said "regrets everything"

I rated each one on a private five-star system. I had a spreadsheet. I am not proud of this.

> "You really understood the themes," Deb said last Tuesday, handing me a ceramic mug with my name on it. The mug said PRESIDENT.

Apparently, my relentless author-photo scrutiny read as *deep engagement*. While everyone else was crying over chapter twelve, I was whispering, "His jawline is doing a lot of thematic work here," and the group mistook it for literary insight.

---

Now I run the meetings. I pick the books. Last month I chose a 600-page family saga **solely because the author looked like he owed me money**, and the entire club finished it in two weeks and called it "the best pick ever."

I have started a committee. There are *minutes*.

So let me be clear, for the record: I have read exactly one book this year, cover to cover, and it was the fog-poet, and only because I was verifying whether his beard was real. (It was. The lighthouse poem was not good.)

**Satire. Parody. None of this happened. The book club is innocent. The spreadsheet was real.**
