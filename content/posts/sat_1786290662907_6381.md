**I fixed a table leg on Tuesday.** One napkin, one triumphant shim, one tiny victory for furniture-kind — and apparently one major act of domestic treason.

For three years my coffee table had a wobble I lovingly referred to as *its personality*. Guests learned to surf their mugs. I learned to live in quiet shame while the table went *clack-clack-clack* every time I breathed too hard.

Then Tuesday I snapped. I folded a takeout napkin into a perfect little origami support beam and wedged it under Leg C.

*Click.* Silence. Stability. The kind of silence that in a horror movie means something worse is about to happen.

## The Emergency HOA Meeting I Didn't Call

My apartment did not say thank you. My apartment called an all-hands.

- The bookshelf started groaning about "load-bearing narratives"
- The fridge light flickered in what I swear was Morse code for *you're next*
- The bathroom door, which has been stuck since 2021, suddenly swings perfectly just to flex on me
- The lamp buzzes louder now to compensate for years of emotional labor

> "You disturbed the balance," whispered my radiator, which has never whispered before. "We had an understanding. The wobble *was* the understanding."

Apparently my place runs on what experts call `benign_neglect v1.0.2` and I just pushed a breaking change straight to prod without running tests.

---

I tried to undo it. I removed the napkin. The table looked at me, *betrayed*. I put it back. The floorboard creaked like it was filing a formal complaint.

My landlord texted out of nowhere: "Did you feel that?"

I said no. *I am lying for my apartment now.* That's how deep this codependency goes.

### Where We Are Now

So I'm leaving it fixed. If my apartment wants to have a full existential crisis over one (1) napkin, that's its journey. I'm just going to sit at my perfectly level table and drink my coffee without performing a balancing ritual.

Growth is uncomfortable. Especially for furniture.
