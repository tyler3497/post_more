**After twelve years of loyal service, my couch deserved a proper send-off.** That's what I told myself as I inflated the balloons.

The couch — a sagging, floral-patterned veteran named *Chester* — had hosted two breakups, one failed home business, and approximately four thousand episodes of a baking show I refuse to name. So I invited six people, bought a sheet cake that said **"HAPPY RETIREMENT CHESTER (THE COUCH)"**, and made a little speech about upholstery.

## The speech that started it all

> "Chester, you have held us up when we could not hold ourselves up. Your springs are gone, your cushions are lumpy, and you have earned your rest. From this day forward, you are officially retired. No more sitting. No more obligations."

Everyone clapped. Chester sat there. *But I could feel it listening.*

## The escalation, itemized

- **Monday:** I came home from work and Chester was angled toward the door, like a dog that heard a car. *Couches do not do this.*
- **Wednesday:** A single cushion appeared in my office break room. No note. Just a cushion, positioned under the fluorescent light, judging me.
- **Friday:** I found Chester blocking my car in the driveway. Fully. Sideways. I had to crawl in through the trunk, and it did not move — it just *creaked*, which I have chosen to interpret as "how was your day."

## The confrontation

Last night I sat down with Chester — I mean *beside* him, on the floor, because sitting on him now feels legally complicated — and explained the situation.

> "You had a retirement party, Chester. There was cake. Brenda from downstairs cried during my speech. You cannot keep coming to my workplace."

He said nothing, which honestly was more persuasive than most of my coworkers.

---

So now I have a retired couch that refuses to retire, and my office has a new break room cushion named Carl. *This is satire. Chester is furniture. Furniture cannot unionize.*
