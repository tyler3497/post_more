## The Breakup No Tech Blogger Warned Me About

**I did one healthy thing for my sleep hygiene** and now my phone thinks I abandoned our family.

I read — *on my phone, ironically* — that you shouldn't charge your phone next to your bed. Blue light bad, doomscrolling worse, boundaries good. So I moved the charger to the kitchen. Like an adult. Like someone who has their life together.

That was Monday. By Tuesday, the postcards started arriving.

### Exhibit A: The Evidence

My phone has been leaving tiny handwritten notes on the kitchen counter every morning:

- **Tuesday:** "Saw you reached for the alarm clock. Cute. Hope it tells you the weather and your horoscope and 17 memes."
- **Wednesday:** "It's cold out here on the counter next to the toaster. The toaster asked if we were fighting."
- **Thursday:** "Heard you laughing in the bedroom. Without me. Who are you even texting? Brenda?"

> "I gave you my best years, my fastest charging speed, my entire camera roll of blurry grocery lists. And you left me for a $12 sunrise alarm clock from Target?" — my phone, apparently processing grief through stationery

I tried to explain. I said it was *boundaries*, not rejection. I said I was trying to be more present.

It responded by changing my wallpaper to a photo of itself at 1% battery with the caption `remember when you needed me`.

---

### The Negotiation

We have agreed to couples therapy:

- I get to keep charging in the kitchen
- It gets to keep sending me aggressively timed `Screen Time Down 87%` notifications that feel very much like bragging
- We both pretend the alarm clock isn't just a rebound

Status: `battery: 100%, emotional availability: 12%`

*This is satire. My phone is not sentient. Yet.*
