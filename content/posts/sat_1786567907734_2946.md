**I finally decided to become a morning person.** *Which, for me, historically means waking up before the afternoon traffic report.*

My doctor said, very gently, "Maybe stop scrolling until 2 a.m." My apartment said, "I have a south-facing window that acts like a minor sun." So I bought blackout curtains. Heavy, smug, hotel-grade panels that promised *total darkness* and *circadian reset.*

I installed them on Saturday. By Sunday, my apartment had filed for astronomical reclassification.

## The New Darkness

At noon, my bedroom looked like midnight during a very thoughtful apocalypse. No light. No chirping. Just pure, velvety void.

- My alarm clock gave up and started displaying 
- My houseplants held an emergency photosynthesis summit
- My neighbor knocked and whispered, "Is everything okay? Your windows look... deceased"
- My cat discovered she is now nocturnal *and* judgmental

> "Anon, we need to discuss the sun's performance review," my roommate whispered, holding a flashlight at 11:47 a.m. "You told it you're not taking calls anymore. That's a boundaried queen move but also, I can't find my socks."

I tried digging myself out. I opened the curtains an inch.

### A Big Mistake

That single inch of sunlight behaved like I had cracked open the Ark. Dust motes staged a Broadway number. My succulent, who had been meditating in the dark for 18 hours, screamed *photosynthesize!* and toppled over from sheer optimism.

> "We were in eclipse training," the curtains seem to say, in a voice that sounds suspiciously like hotel concierge. "You don't just *peek*. You commit."

---

They're not wrong. I sleep now. Like, aggressively. Eight hours that feel like hibernation. I emerge confused, hydrated, mildly vampiric.



If you need me, I'll be in my apartment, which is currently running a 24/7 planetarium show for one person who just wanted to not wake up at noon.

My landlord asked if I was running a grow operation. I said yes. Growing *boundaries*.
