**I have met the enemy, and it spins at 400 revolutions per minute.**

My bedroom ceiling fan had developed a personality — specifically, a *click-click-click* on every rotation that sounded like it was keeping time with my heartbeat, then speeding up out of spite. I asked it nicely. I tightened its screws. I whispered "please" at 2:47 a.m., the way you talk to a machine you've known too long.

Then I checked the lease. The noise policy doesn't specify the noise has to come from a *person*. So in a fit of sleep-deprived righteousness, I did the unthinkable:

> "The undersigned hereby submits a formal noise complaint against the ceiling fan in the primary bedroom, on the grounds of persistent, willful, and frankly theatrical clicking."

## The Fan Retained Counsel

The fan did not take this lying down. (It cannot lie down. It's mounted.) Within a week, a counter-notice appeared under my own front door — delivered, as far as I can tell, via the *vent*.

Its demands:

- An immediate apology, delivered aloud, while looking directly up
- Three (3) replacement screws, described as "its dignity, returned"
- A formal acknowledgment that the clicking was "rhythmic self-expression," not a defect

I refused. Obviously. Now we're both due in small claims court Tuesday, and I have to decide what to *wear* to a hearing against an appliance.

Here's the part nobody warns you about: the fan has character witnesses. The *toaster* saw everything. The toaster is absolutely on its side. The microwave claims neutrality, but I've heard it preheating at odd hours as an act of solidarity.

## Settlement Talks

Mediation is Monday. My lawyer — who charges by the hour and *laughed* when I explained the case — suggests I settle. The fan's position is that I "enjoyed the breeze, therefore the clicking is a fair toll."

Honestly? It has a point. My bedroom has never been cooler. Or more legally complicated.

> Update: the fan just slowed down for a second. Was that mercy? Or was it loading up for its opening statement?

Whatever happens Tuesday, one thing is clear: I will never again live under a rotating authority figure.
