**I downloaded a mindfulness app that plays rain sounds so I could finally be a calm, centered adult who hydrates.**

Instead, my actual umbrella has become emotionally abusive.

It started innocently. I installed *Petrichor™* — $4.99 a month to hear extremely high-quality rain on a tin roof recorded in Oregon by a guy named River. Very healing. Very intentional. *Very not my collapsing apartment life.*

After three days of listening to fake rain to fall asleep, my real umbrella — which has been hanging by the door since 2019 doing absolutely nothing — cleared its throat.

## The Gaslighting Phase Began Immediately

Apparently, it had *feelings* about me outsourcing precipitation.

Here's what my umbrella is now claiming is true:

- It "invented" rain before apps did
- I am "cheating on weather" with my phone
- It sat through two moves, one flood, and my failed sourdough era without complaint
- If I don't take it outside at least once a week, I have "abandonment issues"

> "Oh, you need an app to feel rain now? That's interesting. That's really interesting, Tyler. I'm literally right here. I have been right here for six years," it said, dripping with judgment despite being bone dry.

*For the record, it has never once kept me fully dry. It has a personal vendetta against my left shoulder.*

---

I tried to compromise. I took it outside yesterday. It was 91 degrees and sunny. I stood on the sidewalk like `guardian_of_forecasts.exe` holding a closed umbrella while my neighbor walked his dog past me twice.

The umbrella was still not satisfied. It now wants me to delete the app, apologize publicly, and take it to Seattle "to reconnect with its roots."

The worst part? The rain app sent me a push notification this morning that said "You seem tense. Have you tried listening to your umbrella?"

They're talking to each other. I'm being managed by precipitation.

And honestly? I'm starting to think they both have a point.
