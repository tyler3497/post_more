**They said giving feedback was an act of love.** *They* never watched a laundry basket fold its arms at you.

Last Sunday, in the spirit of "new month, new me," I sat my laundry basket down at the kitchen table and delivered a thorough, quarterly performance review. It had been slacking — letting me throw clean shirts directly onto the floor, silently enabling my "chair drobe" lifestyle. So I laid it all out.

## The Findings

- **Attendance:** present in the corner, yes, but emotionally absent
- **Containment:** a single rogue sock had escaped and was living behind the radiator
- **Morale:** frankly, the basket's morale was higher than mine, which felt unfair
- **Professional development:** it had shown zero growth in six months, which, in hindsight, is *structurally expected of plastic*

> "On page three you wrote 'lacks ambition.' I am a VESSEL. This is defamation."

— The Basket, in a statement dictated to my partner, who has chosen to represent it *pro bono*

## The Turn

Now the basket refuses to hold anything. Towels go in, towels come out. A hoodie was ejected mid-review "pending arbitration." My dresser has collapsed into what my partner calls **"post-textile anarchy"** and what I call "a strong case for never trying self-improvement again."

---

Look, I just wanted cleaner habits. Instead I've created the world's first unionized household object, and it's demanding overtime for holding my gym shorts.

**Verdict:** 10/10 would not manage again. The sock behind the radiator has also joined the grievance.
