**It started as a joke.**

One rainy Tuesday, wedged between a man eating an entire rotisserie chicken and a woman live-streaming her own commute *to herself*, I leaned into the bus window and whispered into my coffee: "And here, the Greater Spotted Commuter begins her daily migration to the financial district."

That was four months ago. I have not stopped.

## The Rules of the Narration

I did not plan for this to become a system, but apparently I am the kind of person who develops systems for things nobody asked about. The narration follows strict principles:

- **Never break character.** I am the documentary host now. There is no me anymore.
- **Everything is a species.** The guy with three laptops is the *Multi-Screened Businessman* (`Executerus overbookedus`).
- **Every stop is a habitat.** "The fluorescent savannah of the Route 7 corridor."
- **Silence is a predator.** If the bus goes quiet, I narrate the quiet.

It would all have been a private bit, except for one morning when I got *too* good at it.

## The Incident

The bus was late. Traffic. Everyone tense. I sensed a climax coming the way a narrator senses a lion approaching a watering hole, so I began, softly:

> "After forty minutes of stillness, the herd grows restless. The alpha passenger checks his watch — a mating call of the corporate male. The tension is... palpable."

The whole bus laughed. Not at me. *With* me. A woman in row six asked for "the weather segment."

And the driver — Rosa, according to her name tag — did something I will never forget. She eased the bus to a stop, killed the engine, and waited. Waited for me to finish.

> "The herd disperses," I whispered. "The savannah reclaims its silence."

She nodded, restarted the engine, and said, "Same time tomorrow, Dave."

---

My name is not Dave. It has never been Dave. But on the Route 7, at 7:42 every morning, *I am Dave*, and this bus is my Serengeti.

*This is satire. All of it. The bus is real. The dignity was fictional all along.*
