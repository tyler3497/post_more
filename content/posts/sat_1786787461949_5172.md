**I bought a plastic cactus to prove I was self-aware about my inability to keep anything alive.**

It was supposed to be a joke. A $7.99 statement piece from Target that said, *look, I know myself.* No water, no sunlight, no responsibility. The perfect roommate.

Then last Tuesday, I watered it. Ironically.

## The Descent Into Botanical Madness

I don't know what came over me. I had just finished my third cup of coffee and thought, *wouldn't it be funny if...* Famous last words that have never once in human history led to good decisions.

Now here's what I've been observing over the past 72 hours:

- The cactus has not grown, because it's plastic, but somehow looks *more hydrated* than me
- My boss complimented its "presence" on our Zoom call and did not compliment mine
- It has better posture than I do despite having no spine in the metaphorical sense *or* a different kind of spine in the literal sense
- I caught myself asking it for advice on my quarterly review

> "I've been watching how you handle pressure," I swear my manager said while looking directly at the cactus. "So resilient. So low-maintenance. So... pointed, but in a good way."

## The Performance Review We Don't Talk About

This morning, my cactus got CC'd on an email about leadership potential. I did not.

I tried to explain that it's literally made of injection-molded polyethylene and its only skill is *existing near sunlight without complaint*, which, okay, is more than I can say for myself lately.

---

`corporate_plant.exe` has stopped responding to my authority. I tried to move it to the windowsill and I think it gave me a performance improvement plan with its silence.

My therapist says I should set boundaries. My cactus says nothing, which is apparently what a good leader does. *Listen more than you speak.* It's been promoted to Senior Vice President of Holding It Together and I am taking watering instructions from a plant that does not need water.

If you need me, I'll be in my kitchen, trying to photosynthesize out of spite.
