**I have been peer-reviewed by furniture, and the results are devastating.**

It started innocently enough. I was rearranging my bookshelf on a rainy Tuesday — one of those melancholy, productivity-flavored Tuesdays — when I said, out loud, to no one in particular: "Be honest with me. What do you *really* think of how I'm doing?"

The bookshelf heard me. The bookshelf *answered*.

## The Review Process

Within hours, the books had organized themselves into a formal review panel. I don't know how they communicated — some kind of spine-based Morse code — but by dinner they had produced a 40-page critique of my life, and I was not prepared for its thoroughness.

Their findings included:

- **Pacing issues**: "Chapter 7 (your twenties) drags considerably. Too many filler episodes about laundry."
- **Character development**: "Protagonist shows promise in Act I but refuses to learn the same lesson twice."
- **Plot holes**: "Claims to be 'a morning person' yet has never once been seen before 9 a.m."
- **Dialogue**: "Repeats 'anyway, so...' approximately 400 times per day."

## A Devastating Pull Quote

> "We gave your childhood a generous two stars out of nostalgia. Everything after that is a cautionary footnote." — *The Complete Review Panel, speaking through a slightly smug encyclopedia volume*

I tried to defend myself. I pointed to my *strengths*. The bookshelf pointed back at the dusty self-help section — *untouched since 2019* — and went silent. The silence was the worst part.

---

Now they've instituted a star-rating system for my daily decisions. I made oatmeal yesterday: **one star**. "Competent but uninspired," the cookbook whispered.

I asked if I could appeal. The dictionary said the process was "definitive and binding." I looked it up. It was right. *It always is.*

*This is satire, obviously. But the bookshelf and I are no longer on speaking terms, and frankly, after reading my own review, I don't blame it.*
