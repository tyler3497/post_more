**I thought it would be responsible.** Like, adult-level responsible. My monstera, Fernando, has been silently judging me from the corner for 18 months, so I figured if anyone could bring fiscal discipline to my life, it was a being that literally survives on indirect sunlight and neglect.

*This was my first mistake.*

## The Audit Begins

I handed Fernando my bank app. Well, I placed my phone in his pot and whispered "do your worst." Same thing.

Within minutes, he started rustling judgmentally. Turns out, houseplants have *very strong opinions* about DoorDash at 11:47pm.

> "You spent $47 on what you categorized as 'emotional support croissants'? And $83 on a succulent you already killed? Explain this line item immediately." — Fernando, my Monstera Deliciosa, who has never paid rent once in his entire photosynthetic life

I tried to defend myself, as one does when being audited by a plant:

- Croissants are cheaper than therapy (debatable, but statistically more buttery)
- The succulent was an investment in *personal growth*
- The 2am Etsy purchase of a tiny accountant hat for Fernando was clearly a business expense

Fernando was not convinced. One leaf drooped. That's plant for "I'm calling the IRS."

## The Verdict

According to his extremely scientific leaf-droop analysis, I am financially solvent in only one category: **photosynthesis-adjacent purchases**.

He's now implemented new policies without my consent:

- All non-essential purchases must be approved via prolonged staring contest (I always blink first)
- My `croissant_fund` has been forcibly reallocated to `extra_moss_pole`
- He is charging me rent for the sunlight I steal from him
- My budget now has a line item called "Fernando's Emotional Damages"

---

Look, I appreciate fiscal responsibility, I do. But I didn't expect to be financially dominated by a plant that thinks Miracle-Gro is a personality trait and whose entire retirement plan is "grow toward window."

SATIRE / PARODY — This is 100% fictional. My monstera does not actually have a CPA license. Yet. If he did, he'd still be more qualified than me to manage money.
