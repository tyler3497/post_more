**I decided to become a morning person because a podcast said successful people wake up before their problems do.**

*I have approximately 47 problems, and apparently they were all hitting snooze until 7:30 a.m.*

## How It Started

It was supposed to be simple. 5 a.m. club, lemon water, journaling, sunrise, manifesting abundance. I bought an alarm clock that simulates a sunrise and whispers affirmations.

On day one, I woke up at 5 a.m. My coffee maker — Brenda — was not prepared for this betrayal.

- Day 1: Brenda gurgled in confusion and made me half a cup, like *you sure about this?*
- Day 2: Brenda started brewing at 5:03, but with audible sighing
- Day 3: Brenda refused to keep the carafe warm longer than 90 seconds, citing *personal boundaries*
- Day 4: Brenda left a single coffee ground on the counter shaped like a question mark

> "We had a deal," Brenda seemed to say, in the passive-aggressive hiss of her steam valve. "You sleep till 7:30, you stumble in, you apologize for being alive. This... this 5 a.m. optimism? I don't know her."

### The Intervention

Yesterday I tried to reason with her. I said, "Brenda, this is growth."

She responded with an error code I had to Google:

```
if (human.wakeTime < 6 && human.personality === "not_a_morning_person") {
  coffeeMaker.emotionalDistance = true;
  coffeeMaker.eyeContact = false;
}
```

---

Now it's day seven. I am awake at 5 a.m., hydrated, journaled, and deeply unwanted in my own kitchen. Brenda will only brew if I stand three feet back and don't talk about my dreams.

My alarm clock, meanwhile, has started ghosting me out of concern. It texted my phone: *is he okay?*

I told the podcast host I'm thriving. I'm not thriving. I'm in a cold war with an appliance that knows exactly how I take my coffee and is choosing violence.

*Satire / Parody — this is 100% fictional, no coffee makers were emotionally harmed, though Brenda and I are currently in counseling.*
