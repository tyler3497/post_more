**I didn't want to join. I was just trying to find a matching sock.**

But there I was, kneeling in front of the dryer at 10:47 p.m., when I heard a tiny, muffled *ahem* from the lint trap. My laundry has had opinions before — *mostly about fabric softener* — but this was different.

It was an invitation.

## The Meetings Are Surprisingly Structured

Apparently, my laundry has been running a book club since 2023 and I just never got the memo because I never fold anything. The president is a striped athletic sock named Terry who takes this very seriously.

His rules are extensive:

- Every sock must finish the book, no skimming the dryer manual and pretending
- No sock-shaming if you cry at the ending
- If you are currently stuck to a sweater via static cling, you must still participate via speaker-sock
- Spoilers are punishable by being sent to the *Delicates Cycle of Shame*

I thought this was cute. I thought, *wow, my socks are literate*. What a wholesome little community I've been tumbling at high heat.

> "Okay, everyone," Terry announced last night, adjusting his tiny reading glasses, "who wants to discuss Chapter 7?"
>
> Then my other sock — the one with the hole in the toe, Greg — just shouted, "HE WAS THE DRYER LINT THE WHOLE TIME!"

---

### The Betrayal

Greg has now spoiled *every* book. Mystery? He tells you the butler did it before you open page one. Romance? `everyone_kisses = true` and he says it out loud during the prologue.

I tried to reason with him. I said, "Greg, you're ruining the experience."

> "I'm saving you time," he said. "You have 47 minutes before the rinse cycle. You need closure, fast."

The worst part is, the other clothes are taking sides. My towels are *fascinated* by Greg's chaotic energy. My fitted sheet — who never gets invited anywhere because he tangles — finally feels seen.

I'm now banned from my own laundry room book club for "bringing outside human drama into our safe space." They meet without me on Wednesdays at spin cycle.

I'm considering starting my own club. Just me and the lone sock that lives behind the washer. At least he doesn't know how to read yet.

*This is satire / parody. My socks are not actually literate, and no laundry was emotionally harmed in the making of this post.*
