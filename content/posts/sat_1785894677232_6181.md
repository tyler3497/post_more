**I thought getting a library card would make me look mysterious and intellectual** instead it has gotten me placed on a formal watchlist by a 150-year-old filing system.

*My plan was simple:* borrow one classic novel, read it in a coffee shop, become the kind of person who says "oh, I've read that" convincingly.

## The Incident

I checked out *Moby-Dick*, which I did not know was 600 pages of a man being mad at a fish. I returned it three days late with a coffee stain shaped like, ironically, a whale.

That's when I got the email.

> "Dear Patron anon,
> You have been summoned to a disciplinary hearing with the Dewey Decimal System. Please bring your overdue slip and a sincere apology. — Mgmt"

I laughed. *The Dewey Decimal System does not email people.* The Dewey Decimal System is numbers on spines.

### The Hearing

The librarian, Marge, wheeled out a cart with a single blinking dot-matrix printer that was, apparently, the physical form of Dewey himself.

Evidence presented against me:

- **Returned one (1) classic** in condition best described as "lightly sautéed"
- Attempted to shelve a fantasy novel in **Biography because "dragons are real to me"**
- Asked if the library had *books on tape* but like, cassettes, on purpose
- Whispered too loud in Non-Fiction and woke up the ghost of card catalogs past

> "We don't have fines anymore," Marge whispered solemnly. "We have *consequences*. Dewey remembers everything."

---

My sentence is as follows:

`status: probation && privileges: 1_book_max && required_reading: pamphlet titled "So You Thought Melville Was Beach Read"`

I am now only allowed to check out books from **001.9 — Controversial Knowledge** which is just conspiracy pamphlets and a very judgmental book about how to properly hold books.

So if you see me at the park squinting at *"How to Not Ruin Library Property (Illustrated)"* — no you didn't.
