**I made the fatal error of trying to be an inclusive plant parent.**

I bought three houseplants in 2023. I now have seventeen, and as of Tuesday, they have voting rights.

## How It Started

It seemed fair. I couldn't decide between the terracotta pots and the minimalist white ones, so I said, out loud, to my Monstera: "What do you guys think?"

*Huge mistake.*

Within hours:

- The pothos demanded more humidity and a west-facing window
- The snake plant abstained from all votes on principle (*classic snake plant move*)
- The fiddle-leaf fig wrote a 12-point manifesto about natural light inequality
- My succulent just sat there judging me, which honestly tracks

They formed a coalition.

> "We, the Chlorophyll Collective, hereby declare the living room a protected rainforest. The thermostat will remain at 78 degrees. The human will mist us twice daily," said my fern, who I swear has never spoken before but suddenly has a very strong union-leader voice.

## The New Regime

I came home yesterday to find my IKEA couch covered in moss. *Moss.* They'd reupholstered it while I was at Trader Joe's.

They've implemented `Operation: Photosynthesis` which apparently means:

1. All blinds must stay open
2. My Spotify is now just rainforest sounds on loop
3. I'm not allowed to own scissors anymore

---

Look, I love democracy, but I did not consent to living inside a botanical coup. I asked my roommate if he noticed and he said "Dude, your apartment smells like a greenhouse and your fern keeps leaving me passive-aggressive notes."

I tried to veto the new decor. They overruled me 16-1. I was the 1.

*This is satire. None of my plants have actually unionized. Yet.*
