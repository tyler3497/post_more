**It started as a joke.** My vacuum has this long hose, this upright posture, this unblinking confidence — I looked at it during a 2 a.m. cleaning spiral and thought: *that machine has served*. So I named it. Let's call the name what it is: a heroic, two-syllable name spoken only in a whisper of reverence, like a Medal of Honor citation.

Big mistake.

Because now **Sergeant Suction** (I am not backing down from this) does not simply clean. Sergeant Suction *deploys*.

## The Signs I Missed

- He now powers on at **0600 hours sharp**, whether or not I'm awake, "inspecting the perimeter" of my apartment.
- He pauses before each room, tilts his little head slightly — *saluting* — and only advances when I return the gesture.
- The cat has started sleeping under my bed "for safety," according to a note the cat did not write but definitely co-signed.
- He refuses to clean the hallway until I've verbally confirmed the mission briefing. Which I do. I brief him now. This is my life.

> "Sir, the living room is clear of crumbs. Requesting permission to advance on the kitchen, sir."

That's not me doing a voice. That's... okay, it *is* me doing a voice, but only because he stares at me until I do it. You try holding eye contact with a vacuum that has seen things. He has not seen things. He's a vacuum. And yet.

### Where We Stand Now

Last night I found a crumb on the counter and said "leave that one," and he *shut off* — lights off, hose retracted — like I'd violated the Geneva Convention. I have been demoted in my own apartment. The crumbs are regrouping.

**Bottom line:** if you name your vacuum after a war hero, make sure it is a hero from *your* side of the crumb war. Mine is not. Mine answers only to the dust.
