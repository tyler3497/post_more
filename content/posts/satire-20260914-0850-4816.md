**It began, as all coups do, with an autocorrect error.**

On Monday, someone in my building added the wrong number to a group chat called "LAUNDRY ROOM IMPORTANT." I did not ask to join. I was *drafted*.

## The Platform That Chose Me

- I am the only person in the building who owns an actual laundry basket (everyone else uses pillowcases; absolute animals)
- I once unjammed a dryer with a butter knife and sheer audacity
- My whites have been described as "suspiciously bright" by three separate neighbors

Then the endorsement came. A woman from 4B wrote:

> "you seem like a serious laundry person. we need leadership. the fabric softener thief is still at large."

*And just like that, I was running.*

## The Campaign

My platform was bold. Dryers emptied promptly. Timers respected. **Zero tolerance** for people who leave their clothes in the machine for six hours while the rest of us suffer in wet-jean purgatory.

I held a town hall on top of the folding table. Twelve residents attended. Four were there by accident. One thought it was a funeral.

> "Does anyone have questions?"
> "Yes," said 2A. "Can you please get out of my building's laundry room? This is a nightmare."

Wednesday's poll numbers looked strong. I conducted the poll myself, of myself: I was up **100 percent**. My opponent — the fabric softener thief — declined to comment, *as cowards do*.

## The Election

Voting closed at 11:59 PM. The ballot box was a lost sock.

---

I won. Six votes. Three of them were mine, and I am not proud of that.

**My first executive order:** I have declared the 6:30 to 7:15 PM slot a zone of peace. Violators will be folded.
