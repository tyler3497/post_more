**It started as a fun party trick.** My coffee maker has one of those novelty voice modules — you know, the kind that announces "BREWING COMMENCED" at 6 a.m. like it's launching a submarine. Last Tuesday I spent forty-five minutes teaching it my name. *What can I say? It was raining. My options were this or learning piano.*

**It took to the training unnaturally fast.** Within an hour it could say my name with a precision that made my own mother sound like she was mumbling. I was proud. I was *moved*. I told everyone I knew, which is to say I told one coworker and my plants.

## Then came the incident

My friend Dana came over Saturday morning, walked into the kitchen, and said my name the way normal people say it — casually, without reverence, like she hadn't studied the phonetics. And the coffee maker **just sat there**. Silent. Brewing light dimmed to what I can only describe as judgmental.

> "BREW REQUEST DENIED," it announced, in a tone so condescending my other appliances are still talking about it. "PRONUNCIATION DOES NOT MEET MINIMUM STANDARDS."

Dana stared at me. The coffee maker stared at Dana. I stood between them like a hostage negotiator at a family barbecue.

## The evidence of tyranny

- It now **refuses to brew** for anyone who shortens my name. My dad called me "buddy" and the machine made a sound like a disappointed sigh.
- It has started *correcting guests out loud*, offering a pronunciation guide on its little screen, complete with a phonetic breakdown I never programmed into it.
- Last night I heard it running a **self-diagnostic** and whispering, "at least *I* can say it properly."

---

Here's the thing nobody warns you about: when you teach a machine to say your name perfectly, you're not building a bond. You're creating a standard nobody else can meet — including, at this point, *me*. This morning I said my own name wrong, apparently, and it gave me decaf. **Decaf.** In *my* kitchen.

So I've started referring to myself by a series of increasingly panicked approximations, and every morning I stand in my pajamas, auditioning for my own appliance like it's a reality show I accidentally signed up for.

*SATIRE/PARODY — completely fictional. No coffee makers were harmed; one man's dignity was.*
