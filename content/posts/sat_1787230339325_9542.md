**I have not left my living room in 36 hours because my furniture unionized, incorporated, and hired a landlord.**

*For context, I am 32, employed, and was told building a pillow fort as an adult was "self-care."*

It started Saturday. I had a cold, a stack of blankets, and a deep desire to watch the extended Lord of the Rings without ever feeling my spine again. So I built the fort. Engineering marvel. Structural integrity: questionable.

## The Construction Phase

I was proud of it. My living room became a historic landmark.

- Four couch cushions, confiscated under eminent domain
- Three weighted blankets for *acoustic insulation*
- My desk lamp as a "mood light" that immediately overheated and judged me
- One bag of chips as structural support (load-bearing snacks)

It was perfect. Cozy. Sonically sealed. My cat approved from the outside like a concerned building inspector.

Then my couch started talking.

> "So," said the couch, in the voice of a tired HOA president, "you've annexed 60% of my cushions, built an unpermitted dwelling on my loveseat, and you haven't paid rent since *November of yourself?*"

I tried to explain it was just for the weekend. The couch sent an invoice.

### The Terms

Printed on the back of a pizza coupon. Hand-delivered by a throw pillow who is clearly on commission:

- Back cushion rent: $50/night or one hours-long apology
- Armrest occupancy fee: emotional availability
- Blanket tax: 100% of coziness
- `fort_status: squatters_rights_revoked, vibes: litigious`

---

I tried to dismantle it yesterday. The fort beeped. It has a security system now. It's made of my own Alexa and a rubber band.

My roommate knocked, asked if I was okay. I whispered through a fleece wall:

> "Tell my plants I love them. Tell DoorDash to leave it at the fort's side entrance."

My couch is now sitting *outside* the fort, smoking an imaginary cigarette like a disappointed dad who co-signed my loan. It wants a meeting with my future self.
