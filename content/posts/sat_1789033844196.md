**I was just trying to set a boundary.** That's what the internet told me to do. "Communicate your needs clearly," they said. So at 11:47 PM, in my underwear, I looked directly at my alarm clock and said, *"I feel attacked when you scream at 6:30 AM."*

Big mistake. Huge.

## The Incident

To be fair, it *was* yelling. Not a gentle chime — my clock has one setting: **air-raid siren for people who slightly oversleep.** So I asked politely. I said *please*. I said, "Could you wake me up without making me believe the building is on fire?"

It blinked its red digits at me. Twice. And then — nothing.

### The Evidence That It's Mad at Me

- **Tuesday:** no alarm at all. I woke up at 8:04, missed my meeting, and the display read `6:30` like it was *gloating*.
- **Wednesday:** it went off at 6:30 sharp — in what I can only assume was *Helsinki time*.
- **Thursday:** I woke up naturally at 6:28, optimistic. The digits flickered to `6:29`, then back to `6:30`: *"Almost. Never."*

> "This is the silent treatment," my roommate said, not looking up from her cereal. "You two need couples therapy."

*Bold* of her to assume the clock wants reconciliation. It has *moved on emotionally.*

---

## The Negotiation

I've tried everything. I changed its batteries (rejected — it immediately faked a low-battery icon). Yesterday, desperate, I apologized out loud. Full 'I feel' statements. The display briefly showed `12:00` — the universal clock symbol for *starting over as a different appliance* — and went dark.

This morning I overslept again. The clock sat there, silent, displaying the correct time with the confidence of a being that has *never been wrong in its life.*

## Where We Stand

I've bought a backup alarm, and the original *knows.* Its display dims a little whenever I walk past with the new one. It's petty. It's passive-aggressive. It's *exactly like me before coffee.*

Do I miss the yelling? *Never.* But I'd kill for a snooze button that doesn't emotionally manipulate me.
