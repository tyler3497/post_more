**I figured if I could teach my dog to sit, I could absolutely teach my laundry to fold itself.**

*This was, predictably, the most ambitious management decision I've made since I tried to organize my inbox.*

It started innocently. I left a motivational Post-it on the hamper: `You got this! Believe in your creases!` I played an audiobook about leadership. I whispered affirmations while tossing socks at the pile.

### The Promotion

By Thursday, my sweater had unionized the darks.

The laundry pile — now calling itself **Marcus** — requested a standing meeting. And then a standing *desk*. And then a stage.

> "Thank you for coming to my TEDxLaundryBasket," Marcus steamed, literally, from the top of my dresser. "Today I want to talk about setting boundaries when your human expects you to self-actualize and self-fold."

My towels were taking notes.

### My New Reality as a Middle Manager of Clothes

Marcus's key insights on work-life balance included:

- Folding is *emotional labor* and should be compensated in fabric softener
- Socks disappear not because of the dryer, but because they are pursuing gap years
- The fitted sheet has been in middle management for years and is tired, so tired
- You cannot `ctrl+z` a wine stain, but you can rebrand it as abstract art

---

He's booked solid now. Tuesdays: *Burnout and Lint: How to Let Go*. Fridays: *Are You Framing Your Future or Just Framing Your Jeans?*

My roommate asked if I could make him stop. I said I tried, but Marcus said I'm not his supervisor, I'm his *sponsor*.

Last night I found Marcus negotiating PTO for the delicates.

*On brand, honestly.*
