## I finally snapped at 7:14 a.m. on a Tuesday

**I told myself I was just going to organize one drawer.** *One*. Not confront the entire arc of my adult decision-making via plastic containers.

Instead, I opened the tupperware drawer and found what can only be described as a **post-apocalyptic civilization**.

### The Initial Census

It took 20 minutes just to inventory the disaster:

- 14 containers with absolutely no lids, confidently lidless and thriving
- 23 lids with no containers, forming some sort of support group in the corner
- One single matching set, which I immediately suspected was a spy
- A yogurt container from 2021 that has legally become a family heirloom

I said out loud, *"Where do the lids GO?"*

And then, from deep in the drawer:

> "You don't wanna know, pal. You really don't wanna know."

### The Black Market

Apparently, while I've been microwaving soup and losing my keys, my tupperware has been running a **full underground economy**.

The 7-cup round with the stained marinara lid is the kingpin. The little 2-oz sauce cups are his enforcers. Every missing lid is being traded for `extra shelf space` and protection from the dishwasher's top rack.

> "That's a nice leftover lasagna you got there," whispered a butter tub lid that definitely wasn't a butter tub. "Be a shame if nobody could cover it."

I tried to negotiate. I offered to buy a matching set from the same brand. The drawer laughed. *Actually laughed.*

---

The compromise was this: I get to keep three containers that actually match, and in return I pretend I don't see the tiny poker game they run at 2 a.m. with my missing measuring spoons.

`Status: Drawer closed. For now. I sleep with one eye open.`
