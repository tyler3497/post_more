**I have become the kind of person who apologizes to plants out loud, and I need you to know it was not a choice, it was an escalation.**

*It started innocently, the way all bad plant decisions do.*

I read somewhere that talking to houseplants helps them grow. Science-ish. So I started with encouragement. "Looking good, Kevin," I'd say to the pothos. Then it became confessional. Then it became full-blown accountability season.

## The Incident in March

Back in March, I knocked over my succulent — Brenda — while reaching for the remote. Soil everywhere. One tiny leaf snapped off. I said "sorry" and repotted her and moved on. Or so I thought.

Brenda did not move on.

### Evidence That Brenda Is Still Mad

- She has not grown a single millimeter since March 14th, despite optimal sunlight
- She leans *away* from me when I enter the room, which my roommate swears is phototropism and I know is shade
- My other plants are thriving and she is just... sitting there, plump and judgmental
- I caught her making eye contact with my cactus like `if (forgiveness == false) { grow = null }`

> "You said 'my bad' but you didn't say *my bad*," Brenda said last Tuesday at 2:13 a.m., or at least that's what I heard after my third melatonin gummy. "You treated me like a decor item, not a desert queen."

## My Apology Tour

I have tried everything. I bought special cactus soil. I whispered affirmations. I played lo-fi beats. I even wrote a tiny apology note and propped it against her pot.

She wilted slightly out of spite, I swear.

---

**The worst part?** I'm now apologizing *preemptively*. I say excuse me when I walk past the fern. I ask permission to open the blinds. My apartment is a restorative justice circle with photosynthesis.
