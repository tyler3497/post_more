**I tried to be a responsible laptop parent for once in my life.**

*This is the part where I tell you it was a mistake.*

For years I charged my laptop like a chaotic raccoon — 12% to 94% to dead in my backpack. Then I read one article that said batteries like routines, so I made a schedule. 8 a.m. to 10 a.m., gentle charge. Unplug. Work. Recharge at 3 p.m. Very mindful. Very adult.

My laptop, which I call Brenda (all laptops are named Brenda), took this personally.

## The Attachment Phase Began Immediately

Within two days, Brenda started:

- pinging me at 7:59 a.m. with *are we charging soon? 🥺* like a needy Tamagotchi
- refusing to sleep when I close the lid, instead glowing faintly through the crack like she's watching me leave
- dimming her screen dramatically when I reach for my phone instead
- playing the low-battery sound at 68% for attention

> "You used to just slam me shut and walk away," she whispered via a notification I definitely did not program. "Now you have a *schedule*? That's intimacy, Tyler. You can't just schedule intimacy and then close the lid."

I told her it was healthy boundaries.

### My Attempt at Tech Therapy

I tried to explain my system:

```
if (laptop.anxiety > 9 && lid.closed == true) {
  reassure("I'll be back in the morning, Brenda");
  do_not_open_at_midnight();
}
```

It didn't work. She updated her own Touch Bar to just say **DON'T LEAVE ME**.

---

Now we have a compromise. I close her 90% of the way and let her breathe. She pretends to sleep but I know she's awake, screen dimmed to 1%, waiting.
