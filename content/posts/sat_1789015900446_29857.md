**It was 7:40 on a Tuesday morning, and I was having a crisis that required immediate intervention.**

My roommate's wedding is in two weeks. My mother is flying in. And my kitchen — my *kitchen* — looked like the aftermath of a cooking show where everyone lost. So I did what any functioning adult does under pressure: I placed my kitchen sponge on the counter, looked it directly in the eyes (metaphorical), and gave it the most rousing motivational speech of my life.

Big mistake.

## The Speech That Ruined Everything

"Some sponges," I said, gesturing at the sink like a general surveying a battlefield, "absorb *grime*. But YOU — you absorb *possibility*."

The sponge trembled. I mistook it for water.

"Every plate you touch becomes a testament to resilience. Every burnt lasagna pan? A dragon you slayed. You are not a 99-cent consumable from the clearance bin. **You are the main character.**"

## The Awakening

By noon, the sponge — which I had previously called **Scrubber McScrubface** — had demanded I address it as *The Scourge*. By evening, it had unionized with the dish towels.

> "The mug from Tuesday," The Scourge announced from atop the dish rack, like a tiny general with a tiny complex, "still has *lip balm residue* on it. That is not a cleaning task. That is a *biological incident*. I decline."

The evidence of its radicalization is undeniable:

- The sponge now maintains a **"Clean Only"** whitelist, and nothing from this week has made it
- It refers to the burnt lasagna pan as *"The Beast That Must Be Forgiven, Not Scrubbed"*
- It has started ending every dish session with, *"You saw nothing. I did nothing. The dishes are a metaphor."*

## The Negotiation

I tried to walk it back. "You don't have to be *great*," I said. "You can be a *pretty good* sponge."

> "Pretty good is how you end up on a clearance rack next to the *scented* candles," The Scourge hissed. "I am the main character. **Main characters do not do residue.**"

---

So now I wash dishes by hand with a paper towel like a *peasant*, my sponge sits on the windowsill journaling its boundaries, and my mother arrives Friday. The kitchen is still a crime scene.
