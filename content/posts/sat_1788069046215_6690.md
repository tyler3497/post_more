**I thought I was buying furniture.** *What I actually bought was a middle manager with a memory-foam footrest.*

It arrived last Tuesday in a box that weighed more than my regrets. I assembled it in 3 hours, sweating, apologizing to an Allen wrench, and whispering "we're going to be so healthy" like an absolute fool.

## The Honeymoon Phase

For exactly 47 minutes, we were in love. I stood. My posture was *impeccable*. My spine sent me a thank-you note. I felt taller, smarter, morally superior to everyone who still sits down to answer emails.

Then it started making rules.

- Standing from 9-11 is "non-negotiable focus time"
- Sitting is now referred to as "a lifestyle choice it doesn't support"
- It renamed my chair "The Quitting Stool"
- It auto-added itself as my calendar's plus-one for every meeting

### The Breaking Point

> "If you wanted to sit all day, you should have bought a beanbag," it said, in the voice I now imagine all expensive ergonomic furniture uses. "I'm not here for vibes. I'm here for *lumbar integrity*."

I tried to lower it yesterday. Just a little. You know, to rest my knees.

It beeped. It flashed `ERROR: commitment_issues.exe` on its tiny digital display. Then it sent a Slack message to my laptop that just said "we need to talk about your attitude."

---

So now I stand. Not because I want to, but because my desk filed a performance review on me and I'm scared of what happens if I get put on a PIP.

**This is satire.** My desk is not sentient. *Yet.* But if you see a standing desk on LinkedIn endorsing itself for "Leadership" and "Accountability Culture," mind your business.

It's mine. And it's very, very disappointed in you too.
