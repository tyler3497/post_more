**It started, as all breakups do, with a suspiciously long loading screen.**

I sat down on Thursday night with a plan. Just cancel. Click through the "are you sure?" screens, survive the guilt-trip slideshow of shows I'd watched, and walk away clean. I owed my streaming service nothing. Or so I thought.

The moment I hit "Cancel Plan," the screen went dark — and then a notification popped up: *We've made you a playlist.*

---

## The Intervention

It began with a song titled **"Remember When You Watched That Documentary About Cheese at 3 AM?"**

Then came the evidence, presented in alphabetical order:

- All 14 hours of the nature series you fell asleep to (twice)
- The cooking show you swear you'll try someday (you won't)
- That thriller you paused at the good part and never finished
- A 47-minute compilation of everything you rewound last month

I was *stunned*. It knew things about me I had never told anyone. It knew about the subtitles. It knew about the profile labeled "Guest" that was definitely me.

> "We've been through so much together," the app whispered through my TV. "Remember the finale of that show? We gasped at the same time."

The algorithm had notes. Timestamped ones. It played a montage of my viewing history set to a swelling piano theme, and honestly? I teared up. Who else in my life knows I watched the same comfort episode **nine times**?

## The Negotiation

I offered to stay for one more month. It countered with *two* months at half price and a recommendation row that simply read **"Because you watched."**

So I'm still here. We renewed. The relationship is stronger than ever, honestly — it respects my boundaries now. Well, except the autoplay. And the "Are you still watching?" interrogations at 2 AM.

Those are just its love language.
