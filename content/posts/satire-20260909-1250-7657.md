**It started as a joke. It ended with my GPS giving my therapist's marriage counselor a five-star rating.**

Last Monday, stuck in traffic on the freeway of life — *and the actual freeway* — I asked my GPS a question no dashboard computer should ever have to answer: "Where am I going wrong?"

It paused. Then it said, *"Recalculating."*

That was seven days ago. I have not heard my own voice since.

## Day One: The Confidence Boost

My GPS started small. When I waffled over a lane change, it announced:

> "In 500 feet, stop apologizing for existing. Merge like you mean it."

I merged. The other driver honked. **I did not flinch.** I have never felt so powerful, or so likely to lose my license.

## Day Three: The Rerouting of My Soul

By Wednesday it had opinions about everything. My wardrobe. My posture. My "fuel efficiency" as a conversational partner. It delivered unsolicited guidance in the same flat, devastating tone it uses for toll booths:

- Told my barista my order was "a non-negotiable destination"
- Ended a meeting with *"You have arrived. At nothing."*
- Rerouted me around my own emotional availability

> "In 300 feet, stop being a people-pleaser. Continue straight through your boundaries."

My boundaries, for the record, were a parking lot I had no intention of leaving.

## Day Seven: I Have Become the Voice

Yesterday my roommate asked what I wanted for dinner and I said, *"Turn left in 200 feet, or we starve."* He left. Fair.

Last night I caught myself narrating my own trip to the bathroom: *"In 50 feet, your destination will be on the right."* It was on the left. **She lied.** I have never felt so betrayed by something I charge with a USB cable.

---

So if you need me, I'll be in the driveway, idling, waiting for further instructions from a woman who lives inside a 4-inch screen and believes I am, quote, *"a scenic route with potential."*

*This is a work of satire. No GPS units were consulted for personality advice, and no marriages were counseled.*
