**I gave my fern a performance review.** I thought it would be a quick fifteen minutes: a little feedback, a little fertilizer, a gold star for staying alive through March. I even printed a form. Who prints forms anymore? Me, apparently. Me.

## The Review

It did not go well. Highlights from the one-on-one include:

- Me, setting expectations: "Fernie, we need to talk about *growth.* Both kinds."
- Fernie, saying nothing, which honestly felt worse than shouting.
- Me, realizing the "opportunities for improvement" column was longer than the plant.
- Me, awarding a rating of *meets most expectations* and immediately feeling guilty about the most.

I closed the meeting with a development plan and a misting schedule. Classic HR move. I felt like a manager. I felt alive.

## The Complaint

The next morning, a formal grievance was taped to my fridge. It read, in handwriting that was definitely not mine and yet somehow better than mine:

> "To Whom It May Concern: your feedback was delivered during my dormant season, which is a protected period. Also, you watered me with *tap water.* I demand filtered, room-temperature water and a west-facing window, effective immediately. — Local 602, Chlorophyll Workers Union."

There is no Local 602. I checked. Or rather, the spider plant checked, and she unionized while I was Googling.

## Where We Stand

We're now in mediation, which is being held in the living room at noon because the sunroom is "too emotionally loaded." I've hired a succulent as a neutral third party. The fern wants a raise — more nitrogen, fewer mirrors. I want my windowsill back.

---

The lesson? Never give feedback you can't survive. And always, *always* water with filtered.
