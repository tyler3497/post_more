**I decided to heal my personality through subtraction, which lasted exactly one mug.**

*It was supposed to be simple.* I watched one video about minimalist kitchens, nodded like I'd invented discipline, and walked into my cabinet like I owned the place. I pointed at Brenda — chipped rim, faded "WORLD'S OKAYEST HIKER" text, survived three moves and one dishwasher flood — and said, "You're free now."

I donated her to Goodwill. I felt *enlightened* for 40 seconds.

## The Cabinet Noticed

By dinner, the kitchen was weirdly quiet. My other mugs had pushed together. The travel tumbler was standing guard.

I opened the cabinet and it was cold in there. Emotionally cold. Thermodynamically fine.

I have created a hostile work environment with ceramics:

- My "Live Laugh Lasagna" mug now faces the wall in protest
- The matching set from 2019 is holding a vigil because Brenda was their emotional support elder
- The tiny espresso cup keeps whispering `she was the only one who understood my scale`
- My water glass asked HR if mugs qualify for bereavement leave

> "We don't have a lot of turnover in here," said my soup mug, who has not been used since 2021 but has opinions. "You don't just *let Brenda go*. Brenda remembered when you learned to make instant oatmeal without burning it. She was history. She was culture."

## The Grief Circle

Last night I caught them in a full circle. Cabinet light on. No one invited me.

> "Brenda believed in second coffees," the big blue one sobbed. "Brenda never judged when he microwaved the same coffee three times. Who will do that now? Who?"

I tried to explain minimalism. I said `declutter --force --intentions=pure` would make us happier.

They responded with a Post-it stuck to the Keurig that just said **WE ARE NOT MINIMALIST. WE ARE A FAMILY. BRING HER BACK OR WE STRIKE.**

---

So I drove back to Goodwill. Brenda was already gone — adopted by a lady who does watercolor and actually hikes.

I came home and told the cabinet. They observed 30 seconds of silence, then assigned me dish duty forever.

I bought a replacement mug that says "LESS IS MORE." My kitchen read it and unanimously decided it does not spark joy.

*This is 100% satire/parody — entirely fictional, no mugs were emotionally harmed, Brenda is thriving in her new home presumably hiking for real.*
