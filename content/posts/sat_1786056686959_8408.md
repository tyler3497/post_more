**I finally understand why my air fryer has been making that passive-aggressive sighing noise.**

For months I thought it was just the fan. Turns out, it was *emotional labor*. My air fryer— who I have named Sir Crisps-A-Lot — has been documenting my crimes against countertop appliances.

### The Incident

It all started when I decided to become a *wellness person*. I read one blog post about making your own spices and decided I needed to dehydrate everything I owned. Paprika? Dehydrate it. Cinnamon sticks? More like cinnamon twigs, right? The entire McCormick aisle I panic-bought in 2022?

I loaded all 23 jars into the basket at once.

## The Letter

Yesterday, under my half-eaten granola bar, I found a tiny envelope. Inside, printed on grease-stained parchment:

> "To Whom It May Concern (specifically you, Tyler-adjacent human who wears socks with slides): Pursuant to Section 4, Subparagraph 'We Are Not a Dehydrator,' you are hereby ordered to CEASE and DESIST. Sincerely, Legal Counsel for Sir Crisps-A-Lot."

It had a little lawyer stamp that was just a drawing of a french fry with glasses.

### Evidence Submitted by the Prosecution:

- **Three separate attempts** to air fry soup (quote: "I am not a jacuzzi")
- One incident where I tried to warm socks in there because my dryer broke
- The great Pop-Tart explosion of Tuesday, which it still brings up in therapy
- My habit of opening the drawer mid-cycle just to *stare* and whisper "do your thing, king"

---

I tried to negotiate. I said we were a team. It beeped back in Morse code that I later translated to:

`ERROR: RESPECT NOT FOUND`

### Where We Stand Now

We are in mediation. I agreed to:

- Actually read the manual (all 4 pages, even the French part)
- Stop referring to preheating as "foreplay"
- Never again attempt to make jerky from deli turkey

And in return, it has agreed to not tell the Instant Pot what I did with the leftover chili.

*Status: crispy on the outside, legally vulnerable on the inside. This is satire, my air fryer cannot actually sue me, though my lawyer says not to test it.*
