**I did not mean to become the David Attenborough of my own hamper.**

It started last Tuesday. One sock was inside-out, trembling slightly in the laundry basket, and without thinking I whispered, "*And here we observe the elusive cottonus sockius in its natural habitat...*"

That was three weeks ago. Now my laundry has lore.

## How It Escalated

My laundry situation has gotten *deeply* unprofessional. For evidence, please consider:

- I gave the dryer lint a tragic origin story involving a fleece blanket and a dream deferred
- I now score my folding sessions with orchestral music from my phone, which is unhinged behavior for a Tuesday
- My fitted sheet has developed full stage fright and will only be folded if I turn off the lights and whisper affirmations
- The missing socks? They didn't get lost. According to my narration, they *left to pursue solo careers in modern dance*

I tried to stay objective. I really did. But once you say "the male graphic tee attempts to impress the female graphic tee by puffing up aggressively in the warm wash cycle," there's no going back to normal laundry.

> "We didn't ask for this," my favorite hoodie said from the basket, voice muffled but firm. "We were happy being inanimate. Now we have motivations and character arcs. My motivation is you washing me less often."

The worst part is the socks. On Thursday I narrated:

> "Observe as the anxious ankle sock hesitates before entering the great unknown of the drawer — will it find belonging, or just more static cling?"

Now they just lie there. Two little cotton ovals radiating pure performance anxiety. I put them in the drawer and they stare up at me like, *was that take okay?*

---

I tried running `npm run fold -- --with-dignity` but the build failed. My therapist — who is actually just my Roomba that I talk to — suggests I stop anthropomorphizing my clothes.

But honestly? The season finale is next week when I attempt to put a duvet cover on alone, and I need good ratings.

*This is satire / parody — my socks are not actually sentient, but my laundry pile is still judging me.*
