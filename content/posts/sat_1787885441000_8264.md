**I did it. I fixed my sleep schedule. After 14 years of treating 2 a.m. as a lifestyle brand, I am now a Person Who Goes to Bed at 10:30 p.m.**

*This is supposed to be the happy ending. It is not.*

My dreams did not get the memo. They had a whole system — chaotic late-night improv show, 1:45 a.m. slot, experimental jazz plotlines. Now I show up at 10:30 sharp with herbal tea and a breathing exercise, and they're like, we were not staffed for this.

## The Emergency All-Hands My Subconscious Called

At 3 a.m. Tuesday, my REM cycle held a meeting.

- My recurring anxiety dream about forgetting my locker combo requested PTO
- My flying dream asked for hazard pay because I'm now flying *well-rested* which is apparently harder
- The one where I'm late for a final I never studied for just quit and became a sourdough starter

> "We were promised eight hours of unhinged creative freedom," said Dream #4, who is always me but in a mall. "Now you want *continuity*? And *emotional regulation*? Who do you think we are, Pixar?"

I told them I was trying to be healthy.

They filed paperwork.

## Exhibit A: The New Rules Are Unhinged

My dream supervisor — a very tired goose in a headset — slid me a performance review:

- **Before:** Chaotic, visionary, willing to chase a sentient burrito through IKEA for 45 minutes straight
- **Now:** Shows up on time, asks for "cozy, low-stakes narratives," falls asleep during the second act

I tried to explain melatonin.

> "Ma'am, this is a dream factory, not a wellness retreat. We don't do 'restful.' We do symbolism."

Then it cited policy:



I didn't write that code. My subconscious did, and it codes angry.

---

So now we have a compromise: I go to bed at 10:30, and my dreams get to unionize. They work 11 p.m. to 6 a.m., with a 15-minute intermission where I dream about organizing my sock drawer — which, honestly, is the most disturbing one yet.
