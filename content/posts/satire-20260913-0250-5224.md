**It began, as all domestic tragedies do, with a sock.**

I found it wadded up inside the rubber gasket of my washing machine — a single, orphaned dress sock, damp and accusing. I did what any guilt-ridden adult would do: I knelt down, looked the machine dead in its circular door, and said the words that would ruin my life: *"I'm sorry."*

That was three weeks ago. I have not had a functioning relationship with my laundry since.

## The Apology Changed Everything

Before the apology, my washing machine was a humble appliance. Now it has *a personality*, and that personality is **petty**.

The evidence is overwhelming:

- Every load defaults to the **delicate cycle**, no matter what I select. I have pressed "heavy duty" eleven times. Eleven times it has delicately tiptoed through a rinse like it's afraid of waking the baby.
- It pauses mid-spin to emit a long, theatrical sigh — a sound no owner's manual describes.
- Last Tuesday it returned my gym clothes *folded*. Not clean. **Folded and still dirty.** That is a message.
- It has begun displaying error codes that don't exist. Yesterday it blinked "E-EMOTIONALLY-UNAVAILABLE" for six hours.

> "Dude, it's a machine," my friend said. "Have you considered therapy?"

Have I considered *not* apologizing to a machine? Yes, friend. And now we are here.

## Living With the Consequences

The worst part is that I can't take it back. You cannot unsay an apology to a washing machine. There is no protocol for that. The customer service line hung up on me twice — the second time, I swear the hold music stopped out of respect.

So here is my life now: I hand-wash everything in the bathtub like a Victorian orphan while my appliance sits there, humming a smug little song at 800 RPM, *delicate* as ever, waiting for me to apologize again.

I will not. I have my pride. And a bathtub full of suds.
