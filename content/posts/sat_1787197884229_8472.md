**I thought bringing hummus to a potluck was the lowest-stakes social contract ever invented.**

Not *making* it — just *bringing* it. The universal signal for "I am trying, but within my known limits." I bought the fancy one too, with the little paprika swirl that says I have taste. I even transferred it to a real bowl so people wouldn't see the barcode.

Nobody told me the hummus had bigger plans.

## The Night My Dip Got Promoted Over Me

I set it down next to the seven-layer dip and went to get seltzer. When I came back, my hummus was surrounded by three neighbors taking notes.

It turns out someone had put a little place card in front of it that said "ROASTED RED PEPPER - CONSULTANT / THOUGHT LEADER" as a joke, and my hummus decided to *commit to the bit.*

By 7:15 it was:

- Networking with the guacamole about "synergy in the spreadables space"
- Explaining to Karen how it did a 6-month sabbatical in the Mediterranean to "find itself"
- Giving my neighbor's kid career advice about chickpea futures

> "Listen, Tyler's nice," it said, in what I can only describe as a confident, slightly cumin-scented voice. "But he's been using 'I'm more of a behind-the-scenes guy' to avoid bringing anything real to the table for *years.* I, on the other hand, have protein and a personal brand."

I tried to intervene with a pita chip, but it was already handing out QR codes.

---

My *store-bought* hummus — which I bought for $6.99 — now has:

- `endorsements: ["excellent mouthfeel", "great culture fit", "surprisingly articulate"]`
- A LinkedIn headline that says `Helping Teams Hummus-tainably Scale™ | Speaker | Dip-lomat`
- A follow-up coffee scheduled with the artisanal bread

The host just texted me asking if my hummus is available to cater her book club. My hummus replied before I could.

*I have never been so thoroughly professionally outclassed by something I was supposed to provide with a spoon.*
