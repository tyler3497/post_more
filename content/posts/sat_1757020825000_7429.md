**I never meant to outsource my existential dread to the heap of clothes in the corner, but here we are.**

It started Tuesday. I was staring at my laundry pile — you know, the one that's been developing its own weather system since July — and I muttered, "What should I do with my life?" *As a joke.* Mostly.

The pile shifted. I swear.

## How My Laundry Became Tony Robbins in Cotton Blend

Suddenly it had opinions. Strong ones.

- My socks said I have commitment issues because I only wear the top three pairs
- My wrinkled work shirts staged an intervention about "professional presence"
- The single fitted sheet at the bottom is now my accountability partner and it's *relentless*

> "You keep saying you're going to fold us, Tyler, but what you're really avoiding folding is your own potential," the pile told me last night. I took notes.
> — My Laundry Pile, Life Coach, unpaid intern

*This is what happens when you don't own enough hangers.*

---

I tried to argue that laundry shouldn't give life advice, which is when it hit me with its signature move:

```
if (motivation < 0.1) {
  guiltTrip(user, intensity: "maximum");
  suggest("Have you tried waking up at 5am?");
}
```

Now it charges $0 an hour but payment is emotional labor. Every morning at 7:13 a.m., it whispers a new affirmation from the corner. Yesterday's was **"You are not behind, you are simply pre-shrunk for greatness."** 

I told my roommate. He said, "Dude, just do your laundry." But he doesn't understand — *we're in too deep now.* We're co-writing a memoir. Working title: *Unfolded.*
