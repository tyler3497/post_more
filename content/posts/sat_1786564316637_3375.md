**I never filled out any paperwork to become legally responsible for a fern's feelings.**

I bought a fern from the clearance rack at Home Depot because it was $4 and looked like it needed a win. *Which should have been my first clue that I was about to enter an emotionally codependent relationship with chlorophyll.*

I named him Fernando out of respect. Big mistake.

## How It Escalated

At first it was normal plant stuff. Water, light, whispering "you're doing great, buddy" at 7 a.m. Then I left for a weekend trip and my neighbor watered him. When I got back, Fernando had... changed.

- He now leans aggressively toward the door whenever I touch my keys
- His soil moisture meter, which I bought as a joke, now reads `abandonment_level: critical`
- He dropped exactly one leaf on my suitcase like a passive-aggressive Post-it
- My other succulent, Kevin, has started pretending not to know us out of embarrassment

My roommate found me packing and said Fernando was giving her *a look*.

> "He told me you were leaving again," she said. "He asked if you updated his emergency contact form. I told him I'm not qualified to discuss his root system."

*I explained that he is a plant. She explained that he has seen me cry over a missing sock.*

## My Failed Escape Plan

I tried to reason with him. I said:

> "It's three days. You'll have water. You'll have sun. You'll have my entire Spotify history of forest sounds."

He responded by wilting in real time. One frond at a time. Like he was turning down the volume on our friendship.

---

I cancelled my Airbnb. I told my friends I had a `dependant_care_conflict`. My mom asked if Fernando needed a plus-one for Thanksgiving.

`status: vacation_denied && fern_supervised && thriving_under_duress`

Now we have an agreement. I get to leave the apartment for up to six hours. He gets daily affirmations, filtered water that has "rested," and veto power over my travel plans. It's fine. I didn't want to see the beach anyway. Who likes sand?
