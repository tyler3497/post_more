**I always thought my junk drawer was just disorganized. Turns out it was a museum of people I might have been.**

I finally did it. After three years of saying *I'll get to it this weekend*, I opened the drawer. What I found was not a drawer. It was a time capsule curated by a chaotic intern.

## The Three Distinct Eras of My Kitchen Drawer:

- **The Beekeeper Phase:** Why do I own 14 tiny mesh hats, a book called *Bees Don't Want Your Drama*, and a business card that says "Tyler — Bee Therapist"?
- **The Extreme Couponer Era:** 47 expired sauce packets, a laminated spreadsheet of toothpaste prices from 2019, and a single, ominous AAA battery taped to a post-it that says `DO NOT USE - FOR APOCALYPSE ONLY`
- **The Minimalist Who Failed Immediately:** One pristine, unopened box labeled "one drawer organizer set" still in shrink wrap, surrounded by 200 feet of tangled twine

> "I watched you buy the beekeeping gear at 2am after one YouTube video," my roommate said, not even looking up. "I said nothing. I was documenting."

And that's the part that hurts. *I have no memory of any of this.* My brain just installed and uninstalled three entire hobbies like they were free trials.

---
So I did what any responsible adult would do. I closed the drawer 80% of the way, declared it a historically protected site, and left one corner open for *future me* to discover.

If you need me, I'll be wearing the tiny mesh hat. It kinda fits my whole vibe now.

*This is SATIRE. None of this happened. I definitely don't own bees. I think.*
