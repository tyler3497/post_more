**I have achieved enlightenment, but only for two weeks, after which my serenity will be automatically renewed at $9.99 a month.**

It started innocently. Work had me meditating involuntarily every time the Slack icon bounced, so I downloaded *Stillness Pro*, a meditation app with 4.8 stars and a tagline promising "transcendence, delivered." The free trial gave me access to a velvet-voiced guide named River, who told me I was *exactly where I needed to be* — which, at the time, was a parking lot.

## The Fine Print of Nirvana

The trouble began when the trial expired mid-session. River paused, audibly cleared his throat, and informed me that "inner peace is a premium experience." My options, laid out in a calm nonjudgmental pop-up, were:

- **Pay $9.99/month** for continued access to my own calm
- **Downgrade to Peace Basic**, which includes one (1) deep breath per day and banner ads
- **Cancel and return** to my previous state, described by the app as "an anxious ferret in a thunderstorm"

Naturally, I paid. *Inner peace is priceless, but apparently also monthly.*

## The Upsell Arc

Since then, my spiritual journey has become a sales funnel. River now introduces every session with sponsored content:

> "Breathe in... and breathe out... This moment of stillness is brought to you by CloudMattress, because enlightenment is 20% better with lumbar support."

Last week the app added a leaderboard. I am currently ranked #4,112 in mindfulness, sandwiched between a woman who journals in three languages and a man who meditates while BASE jumping. My streak notification scolded me for "low tranquility engagement" on Tuesday.

## My Breaking Point

Yesterday, River asked me to "observe my thoughts without judgment." So I observed the thought *this man is charging me ten dollars a month to tell me to breathe*, and he interrupted to say observing that particular thought would require the Thought+ add-on, *$3.99 a month*.

I have since cancelled the subscription. I am back to my original coping mechanisms: staring out windows, sighing heavily, and narrating my own life like a documentary.
