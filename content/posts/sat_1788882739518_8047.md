**It started with a compliment.**

Last Tuesday, I looked down at my sneakers and, in a moment of unprecedented domestic gratitude, said out loud: *"Wow, great job today, shoelaces. Really holding it all together."*

I meant it. They did not take it well.

*To be clear: none of this happened, because this is satire and my shoelaces are inanimate. Probably.*

## The Unraveling

By Wednesday, my left lace had loosened itself during a totally normal walk to the kitchen. I bent down and re-tied it. It waited a full four seconds, then flopped open again like a tiny scarf that had given up on life.

By Thursday, both laces had adopted what I can only describe as **aggressive modesty**. Tied? Absolutely not — that would be *showing off*. Double-knot? "We don't do that anymore."

## Evidence of the Crisis

- My right shoe untied itself **three times** on a single trip to the mailbox
- A stranger at the crosswalk said "your shoe's untied," and the lace visibly *recoiled*, as if being perceived had ruined it
- I tried switching to velcro. The shoes filed a complaint with the dresser.

> "We never asked for this attention," the left lace whispered, in a voice that was definitely just the wind. "We were fine being background characters."

I tried reverse psychology. I told them they looked terrible. I said their knots were weak. I called them "discount spaghetti." They tied themselves into a **bow so perfect it had dimples** — and then immediately undid it out of embarrassment.

---

## My Life Now

I have replaced all my shoes with slip-ons. The laces sit in a shoebox in the closet, which I have labeled, per their request, `employee_of_the_month_final_FINAL.jpg`.

Experts — by which I mean one guy at the running store who nodded a little too long — say this is normal. "Laces sense praise," he told me. "It's science."

So here is my advice: **never compliment your shoelaces**. Let them believe they are mediocre, forgotten, replaceable. It is the only way they keep doing their job.

*I am currently accepting donations for therapy. For the laces.*
