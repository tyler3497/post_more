**I never thought I'd be the third wheel in my own morning routine.**

It started with a resolution. You know, the kind you make at 11:58 p.m. while staring at the ceiling fan like it personally owes you something. I sat down with my phone, tapped through the alarm settings, and whispered, *"It's not me, it's you. We're done. No more nine-minute encore."*

The snooze button did not take it well.

## The Five Stages of a Breakup, Performed by a UI Element

**Denial.** The next morning, the snooze button appeared *anyway*, as if we hadn't had the talk. It glowed at me through bleary eyes like a needy ex texting "u up?"

**Anger.** When I ignored it, my phone vibrated with a fury usually reserved for parking tickets.

**Bargaining.** It started offering deals. *"Just five more minutes? Ten? What about a quick scroll of your phone while you're at it?"*

*I held firm. For six days. It was the worst six days of my life, which is really saying something.*

## Then It Found Someone Else

That's when I noticed the change. My mornings got... suspiciously easy. I was waking up before the alarm. I was hydrating. *Hydrating.*

> "I never felt seen the way he makes me feel seen," my willpower told me last week, when I confronted it. "He gives me *space*. He lets me *choose*."
>
> — My Willpower, now in a situationship with the snooze button

The evidence is overwhelming:

- They've been seen together at 6:47 a.m. *every single day*
- My willpower now refers to getting out of bed as "a journey, not a destination"
- Yesterday I found them giggling at a listicle called "47 Ways Nine Minutes Can Change Your Life"

---

*I used to be the protagonist of my mornings. Now I'm the roommate who pays rent and doesn't ask questions.*

The snooze button and my willpower are co-hosting a brunch this Sunday. They invited me. As a guest. I'll be sitting at the kids' table, drinking decaf, wondering where it all went wrong.
