**I just wanted to see a nice cardinal once in a while.**

That's it. One low-stakes hobby to offset my screen time. So I bought a $14 bird feeder from the hardware store, hung it on my balcony, and filled it with what the bag described as *premium songbird blend*.

## The First Week Was Innocent

A sparrow came. Then two finches. I felt like a Disney princess with a Wi-Fi bill.

By day four, a pigeon named — according to my neighbor — Kevin landed and stared directly into my kitchen window for six hours straight.

I should have known.

### The Council Convenes

On Sunday morning I woke up to find my feeder empty and five pigeons arranged in a perfect semicircle on my railing, each holding what I can only describe as *very tiny clipboards*.

> "We have reviewed your seed distribution practices," cooed the largest one, who was wearing a hard hat made from a bottle cap, "and we have concerns."

My crimes, according to the Pigeon Local 401, included:

- Providing 70% millet when the contract clearly stipulates sunflower preference
- Refilling at 8:15 a.m. instead of the agreed-upon 7:00 a.m. sharp
- The audacity of owning a cat that watches from the window, which they called `visual intimidation`
- Playing lo-fi beats on the balcony, which they classified as a hostile soundscape

They now operate a full surveillance rotation. 

`SHIFT-A: 06:00-14:00. SHIFT-B: 14:00-22:00. SHIFT-NIGHT: judgmental staring.`

---

I tried to take the feeder down. They filed a grievance. My super told me they left a one-star review of my balcony on something called WingBnB.

I am now contractually obligated to provide fresh water, unsalted peanuts, and a handwritten apology for the time I sneezed too loud.

I haven't seen the cardinal. I think he saw the union rep and decided to work from home permanently.
