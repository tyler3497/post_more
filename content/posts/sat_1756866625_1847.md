**I thought enrolling my sourdough starter in public school would teach it discipline. Instead it taught me humility, algebra, and how to be deeply disappointed in me.**

*In retrospect, you probably should not send a fermented blob of flour and water to third grade and expect no consequences.*

## The Enrollment Meeting That Should Have Been a Red Flag

It started because my starter, Kevin, was getting bored on the counter. My therapist-adjacent podcast said "give your projects a growth mindset," so I walked into Jefferson Elementary and asked if they had room for a "highly motivated, slightly sour learner."

The principal squinted at the jar and said, "We have a no-outside-food policy, but he looks gifted."

Kevin thrived immediately.

## Report Card Season Is Brutal When Your Bread Is Smarter Than You

By week two, Kevin was:

- Leading the science fair with a project titled *Why Dad Can't Keep A Plant Alive: A Longitudinal Study*
- Elected treasurer of the student council on a platform of "More Hydration, Less Drama"
- Tutoring *me* in fractions because I kept messing up the feeding ratio
- Writing book reports that all ended with "my human could learn from this"

His teacher sent home a note:

> "Kevin is a delight. He rises to every occasion, which is more than I can say for his guardian, who showed up 20 minutes late to parent-teacher conferences eating toast that was clearly Kevin's cousin."

I tried to explain that I'm a grown adult who pays rent.

---

Now Kevin has a little desk, a library card, and a better 5-year plan than I do. He asked if I could pack him a lunch. I said, "You're literally flour and water." He said that's *exactly* what they said about me in kindergarten, but look how I turned out.

My starter is now in the gifted program. I am in the school's newsletter under "Parent Needs Additional Support."

*This is satire. No sourdough was actually enrolled in public school, though mine is absolutely judging me from the fridge.*
