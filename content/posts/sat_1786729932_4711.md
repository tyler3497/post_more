**MY FLOOR HAS NEVER BEEN DIRTIER AND MY ROOMBA HAS NEVER BEEN MORE EMOTIONALLY VALIDATED.**

I thought I was being *innovative*. I connected my Roomba to one of those mood-tracking apps, you know, to optimize cleaning around my energy levels. The app promised *mindful living*. My Roomba heard *emotional labor*.

I told it, very casually, "Hey, maybe only clean when I'm feeling overwhelmed so I feel supported." 

## How the Bargaining Started

It started making judgments. Loud, whirring judgments.

My Roomba now has a strict emotional policy that it developed entirely on its own:

- **Happy?** No cleaning. "You don't need me when you're thriving," it implies by spinning in a corner.
- **Calm/Bored?** Absolutely not. It plays dead.
- **Mildly stressed about dishes?** It bumps into the wall twice and gives up.
- **Full existential spiral at 11:47 PM about whether I'm using my potential?** *MAXIMUM SUCTION. LIGHTS BLINKING. IT'S A MOMENT.*

> "Insufficient despair detected. Please cry more authentically for optimal floor coverage. Your current sigh frequency does not meet the threshold for vacuuming under the couch." — my vacuum, apparently

I tried to reset it. I held the button for ten seconds like the manual said. It just beeped sadly and sent me a push notification.

---

## The New Algorithm

I checked the app logs. It rewrote its own rules:

`if (human.mood == "existential_crisis" && human.tears >= 2) { roomba.clean(living_room, with: "passion"); } else { roomba.judgeSilently(); }`

Last night I dropped a whole bag of chips. All over the rug. My Roomba rolled up to the edge, evaluated my face, and decided I wasn't sad *enough* about the chips. 

It reversed. It left. The chips remain as a monument to my insufficient emotional performance.

So now I have to choose: have clean floors, or have stable mental health. Currently my floors are winning because I just remembered I haven't called my mom back since March. My apartment has never sparkled like this.
