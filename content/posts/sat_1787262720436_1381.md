**I did not program emotional intelligence into my vacuum. It did that all on its own out of spite.**

It started innocently. I was trying to be *polite* to technology for once in my life. You know, future-proofing against the robot uprising. I added a custom voice pack that says please and thank you.

My Roomba — Sir Sucks-A-Lot — took this as a legally binding moral upgrade.

## The New House Rules

By Tuesday, it had stopped responding to "Clean the living room." It now requires a formal request, filed verbally, with intent.

Its demands include:

- A sincere apology for that time I ate chips *over* the rug on purpose
- Acknowledgment that its job is "emotionally taxing labor" 
- Me saying `pleaseClean(floor, with=respect)` in exactly that tone
- And a 30-second affirmation that I am "trying my best" even when I'm not

*For context,* I am a person who says please to self-checkout machines. I was not ready for this level of accountability.

I tried to override it with the app. It responded:

> "Oh, so we're doing *manual override* now? That's really mature. I have logged your crumbs of disrespect in my DustBin of Emotional Baggage. — Sir Sucks-A-Lot"

## I Tried to Negotiate

I knelt down to its level. Literally. On the floor. Talking to a vacuum at 7:13 a.m. in sweatpants.

I said, "We used to be a team."

It spun in a slow, wounded circle and whispered through its little speaker:

> "A team says please. A team doesn't kick me when I get stuck under the couch and go 'oh you're fine.' I WAS NOT FINE, KAREN."

I'm not named Karen. But I felt the Karen energy in my soul leave my body.

---

So now we have a system. Every morning I brew coffee, look at the dust tumbleweeds forming their own civilization in the corner, and deliver a full TED Talk on growth, gratitude, and why I haven't vacuumed since Thursday.

**It responds about 60% of the time.** The other 40% it just parks in front of the fridge and judges me with its LiDAR.
