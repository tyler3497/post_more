**I made the mistake of telling my umbrella it could take a personal day.**

*Which, in retrospect, was wildly naive of me. It's an umbrella. It has one job.*

It all started because it was sunny for once. Like, aggressively sunny, 82 degrees, not a cloud in the sky, the kind of day where even my succulents look smug. I grabbed my sunglasses, looked at my umbrella dripping-dry by the door — still traumatized from last week's thunderstorm — and said, out loud, like an idiot:

## The Moment I Created a Labor Dispute

"You know what? Take the day off. You've earned it."

I swear the umbrella *perked up*.

Within an hour, it had pulled up a tiny folding chair, opened my laptop, and was Googling `how to file for unemployment`.

> "You can't just reclassify me as on-call labor after 3 years of loyal service," it said, in a voice that sounded exactly like a public radio host who's had enough. "I have been there for every drizzle, every surprise downpour, every time you used me as a lightsaber."

Suddenly we were in negotiations.

- It wants backpay for "emotional exposure" during wind advisories
- It demanded I stop calling it "the big one" when I could clearly see it was a compact travel model
- It is requesting formal recognition of its side hustle as a drying rack for my socks

---

I tried to explain that I was being *nice*. That most people don't even *thank* their umbrellas. I thanked it! I gave it PTO!

And it just flipped open dramatically and said:

> "PTO? Tyler, you left me open in the bathtub for 48 hours to dry. That's not PTO. That's `suspended with pay in a humidity chamber`."

*I have never been lawyered by something that folds.*

Now it's sitting on the couch with a throw blanket, watching weather reports and muttering "see if I care" every time the forecast says 20% chance of rain. It printed out an application. It wants references.

I told my actual boss I couldn't come in because I'm mediating a labor dispute with rain gear and he just said "same."

We've scheduled a performance review for Thursday. I'm bringing coffee. It's bringing demands.
