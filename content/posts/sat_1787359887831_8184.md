**I did not mean to create a corporate culture in my living room.** I was just lonely and my monstera had been looking particularly *judgmental* lately.

It started innocently. I read somewhere that talking to your plants helps them grow, so I started giving mine daily stand-ups. Like, actual stand-ups.

> "Good morning team, quick sync — Fernando the Fern, you're looking lush, keep it up. Priya the Pothos, love the new vine direction, very proactive."

And they *thrived*. Or so I thought.

## When the Monstera Started Taking Notes

My monstera — Chadwick — has always been the overachiever. Big leaves, dramatic fenestrations, clearly middle-management energy. Last Tuesday I caught him leaning toward my laptop during my Zoom call.

Yesterday, he escalated.

I found a tiny Post-it on his pot. In my handwriting, which I do not remember writing:

> "Per my last email, we need to discuss your watering cadence and lack of career growth opportunities for foliage. Please schedule a 1:1. — C. Monstera, Senior Leaf Officer"

Things have gotten out of hand:

- Fernando the Fern requested *flexible sunlight hours* and now only photosynthesizes between 11 a.m. and 2 p.m.
- Priya the Pothos filed a complaint about *unequal fertilizer distribution* — she has a spreadsheet
- My succulent, Greg, has gone full quiet-quitting and is just `vibing // will not elaborate`
- Chadwick is now CC'ing my spider plant on all passive-aggressive moisture requests

I tried to course-correct with positive reinforcement. I said `Great job team, really leaf-ing it all out there today!` and Chadwick dropped a leaf. On purpose. *For effect.*

---

I now have a quarterly review scheduled for Friday with a plant who doesn't pay rent and has better boundaries than I do. My therapist says I should set limits. My monstera says we should circle back on that.

Honestly? He's not wrong. My performance *has* been wilting.

*Satire / Parody — this is fictional, my plants are not unionized (yet), and no actual monstera was consulted for management advice.*
