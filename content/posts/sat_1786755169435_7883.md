**I just wanted my apartment to smell like a fancy hotel lobby, not like leftover lo mein and three-day-old life choices.**

So I spent $34.99 on a eucalyptus-mint diffuser from a store that smelled exactly like how I wanted my life to smell. *A decision that has now plunged my entire kitchen counter into geopolitical crisis.*

The diffuser, whom I named Misty for morale, took her job very seriously.

## How the Cold War Started

At first, it was idyllic. Misty hummed, the apartment smelled like a spa brochure, I felt like someone who owns linen spray. Then my microwave — Brenda, 7 years old, decibel level of a departing 747 — got involved.

The conflict began over three key disagreements:

- I put Misty on the *exact same power strip* as Brenda, which Brenda read as a declaration of war
- Misty runs on a 6-hour timer, which Brenda interprets as "stealing her stage time"
- Brenda's signature dish is reheated fish, which Misty describes as `scent_crime = true`

> "I have spent seven years trying to normalize the smell of salmon at 11:47 p.m.," Brenda beeped ominously at 11:48 p.m. "And now you bring in *this vapor influencer?* I have no choice but to escalate."

---

## The Current State of Negotiations

Brenda now refuses to heat anything unless I move Misty to the bathroom. Misty retaliates by puffing extra hard every time Brenda dings, creating a fog so dense my smoke alarm filed for emotional PTO.

*I live in a 500-square-foot studio and I am now a UN mediator for countertop appliances.*

Yesterday I found a tiny note in front of Brenda written in dried lavender:

> "We can share the counter or we can share the darkness. But we will NOT share the air. — M"

I tried to explain that they are on the same team. That we all just want a cozy apartment.

Brenda responded by microwaving my leftover pizza for 11 minutes out of spite.

This is satire. I am fine. My sinuses are just very, very confused.
