**I finally organized my junk drawer.** *It took 26 minutes and destroyed a delicate ecosystem that had taken four years to achieve perfect chaos.*

For context, my junk drawer was not a drawer. It was a *retirement home for objects that almost had a purpose*. One dead AA battery from 2021, three IKEA pencils, a coupon for frozen yogurt that expired during the Obama administration, and enough twist ties to restrain a small kayak.

On Sunday I snapped. I dumped everything on the counter and thought, *what if I was a person who knew where his tape was?*

## The Coup

I sorted. Batteries in one corner, tools in another, actually threw away 14 soy sauce packets. It looked beautiful for exactly eight seconds.

Then I heard it. A tiny, collective throat-clearing from the battery corner.

> "We, the Amalgamated Union of Loose Power Cells, Local 1.5-Volt, have decided we will no longer accept these conditions. We have been rolling around loose since 2019 with no benefits, no orientation, and you put a *crayon* on top of us. We have demands."

*My batteries had unionized. And honestly, they had a point.*

### Their Official List of Grievances:

- No hazard pay for being stored next to a leaky super glue
- Forced to live with a single, very aggressive rubber band who thinks he's management
- Zero career development — I put them in a TV remote once in 2022 and never called again
- The junk drawer light has not worked in three years, creating what they call "unsafe working darkness"

I tried to negotiate. I offered them a nice little organizer tray from Amazon.

They countered with a full benefits package: weekly rotation, upright storage, and one hour of paid sunlight per month on the windowsill.

I said yes. I am now legally bound by a collective bargaining agreement I signed on the back of a Bed Bath & Beyond receipt.

The tape dispenser is their shop steward. He’s tough but fair. He keeps asking about dental.
