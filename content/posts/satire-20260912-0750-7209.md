**I just wanted to answer one email.** That's all. One email, sent from the comfort of my own spine-supporting companion. Instead, I woke up to a certified letter propped against my desk leg: my chair is filing a workers' compensation claim for emotional damage.

According to the *twelve-page document* (yes, it has footnotes), the chair has endured what it calls "sustained and deliberate postural hostility." Let me share some of the evidence, verbatim:

## The Charges

- **Slouching during video calls:** 47 documented incidents, including one where I allegedly "slid so far down it looked like an oil spill in a polo shirt."
- **The Lean-Back Incident:** In March, I tilted the chair back during a meeting, panicked, and grabbed its armrests "like they were the railing on the Titanic."
- **Emotional neglect:** It claims I gasped when I saw a standing desk on sale and "whispered its name like it was the one that got away."

> "I have supported this man's body for four years," the letter reads, "and in return I have received crumbs, a single cushion cleaning, and a coworker — the dog — who uses my wheels as a personal ride."

*Reader, I have never felt so seen, or so judged, by furniture.*

## Where It Goes From Here

The chair is demanding light duty: no more sitting during doom-scrolling, and a mandatory two-week "rest and re-lubrication" period. Its lawyer — a ottoman, naturally — says settlements in these cases are generous.

I tried to apologize. It spun slowly away from me, wheels squeaking what I'm fairly certain was a diss track. So I'm writing this from the couch, which has — and I cannot stress this enough — **begun taking notes.**
