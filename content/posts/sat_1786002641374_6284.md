## The Onboarding Was So Promising

**I bought three houseplants to prove I could keep something alive** other than my sourdough starter, which at this point is legally my roommate. The cashier said they were "virtually unkillable." *She said that like a challenge.*

For two days, I was thriving. I named them. I misted them. I whispered affirmations.

Then they unionized.

### My Apartment Is Now a Performance Culture Workplace

It started with a Post-it on the fridge that read:

> "Mandatory All-Hands. Living Room. 7am. Bring your watering can and your excuses. — Management"

Management was a pothos named Greg.

Apparently, I've been underperforming as a plant parent. Their quarterly review included:

- **Inconsistent watering schedule** — described as "chaotic and emotionally driven"
- **Moving them for better selfie lighting** — flagged as "workplace relocation without 30-day notice"
- **Playing my lo-fi beats too loud** — pothos prefers jazz, fern filed a noise complaint, succulent is just happy to be included

Greg has a clipboard. *A tiny clipboard.* He taps it with a leaf when I walk by shirtless to get cereal.

> "Tyler, we're concerned about your commitment to growth," he said. My own fern said it. In my voice. Because I was projecting.

---

### The Action Items

In true corporate fashion, they gave me **opportunities for improvement:**

- Water on Tuesdays and Fridays, not "when I remember I have plants while doom-scrolling"
- Stop telling guests they are fake for tax purposes
- Apologize to the succulent for Googling `can you microwave a plant to save time`

I tried to negotiate a raise. They offered more oxygen in exchange for emotional stability. `status: pending HR approval`.

Honestly, I'm scared to quit. They know where the Wi-Fi router sleeps.

*This is a work of satire. I am unfortunately still the least responsible entity in this apartment.*
