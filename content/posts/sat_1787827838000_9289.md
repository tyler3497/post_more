**I thought introducing my two houseplants would be wholesome. It became a legal drama.**

*I own exactly two plants. This already makes me an overconfident person.*

Six months ago I adopted Fern — dramatic, needs misting, texts me with drooping — and Cactus Carl — low-maintenance, judgmental, hasn't grown in 18 months out of spite. They lived on opposite sides of my apartment in peaceful ignorance. It was working.

Last Tuesday, I had the idea to "let them socialize for optimal feng shui." I moved them both to the one good sunbeam in my living room. This was my Vietnam.

## The Alliance Collapsed Immediately

Within hours:

- Fern leaned aggressively into the sun, covering 80% of Carl
- Carl retaliated by... doing nothing, which is somehow more threatening
- The soil started smelling like resentment and bad decisions
- My moisture meter now just says `nope` regardless of actual moisture

> "You brought me here to *share*?" Fern rustled. "I have been photosynthesizing since 2024. He is literally a spiky rock with commitment issues."

*I told my roommate and she said, "You're anthropomorphizing succulents again."*

---

### The Mediation Failed

I tried rotating them every 12 hours like divorced parents splitting custody.

`if (sunbeam.available) { fern.demandFullCustody(); carl.passiveAggressiveSilence(); }`

**Now I have a schedule taped to the fridge:** Monday/Wednesday/Friday Fern gets sun, Tuesday/Thursday Carl gets it, weekends they both glare at me from the shadows like I invented winter.

My apartment is 600 square feet and I am running a full-time botanical family court out of it. I Googled "plant mediation services" and now my ads are all for divorce lawyers.

I've accepted my fate. I am not a plant owner. I am a mediator for two extremely petty photosynthesizers who will never be happy and will absolutely tell their friends about this.

*Satire / Parody: This is 100% fictional. No plants were given legal representation, custody rights, or emotional damage in the making of this story. Please water your plants — they are dramatic enough already.*
