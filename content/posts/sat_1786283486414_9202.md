**I just wanted to watch birds from my kitchen window without putting on pants** and now I am in active labor negotiations with corvids.

It started innocently. I got one of those Wi-Fi bird feeders with a camera that pings your phone every time a sparrow blinks. *Very normal adult purchase.* For two days it was chickadees and my smug sense of connectedness with nature.

On day three, the crows found it.

### The Meeting I Didn't Schedule

By 6:15 a.m., there were seven of them. One had a tiny twig clipboard. One was taking minutes.

They had demands, which my feeder transcribed via AI as polite subtitles:

- **Unlimited premium unsalted peanuts**, *not* the discount trail mix I bought on sale which, quote, "tastes like regret"
- Removal of the anti-squirrel baffle, which they called a "hostile architecture element"
- A 20-minute daily window of *privacy* — no camera, no notifications — for "personal matters"
- To be addressed collectively as *The Board*

I tried to reason with them. I tapped on the glass and said, "Guys, it's a $49 feeder." 

One stared directly into the lens and replied:

> "We have reviewed your offer and found it wanting. We know you work from home. We have seen what you eat for lunch. We can make this difficult."

### My Career as a Middle Manager for Birds

I offered rotating snack days and a small dish of water. They countered with a pension plan consisting of shiny bottle caps and exclusive rights to the leftover pizza crust from my trash.

According to the leaf — yes, a leaf, slipped through my mail slot with pecks for punctuation — my current standing is:

- Failure to provide artisanal suet: *written warning*
- Judging them from behind the blinds: *noted and filed*
- Calling them "buddy" instead of their chosen titles: *escalated to senior leadership*
