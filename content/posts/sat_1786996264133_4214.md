**I just wanted my studio apartment to feel more balanced.** That was it. One $12 paperback called *Feng Shui For People Who Own Too Many Mugs* and suddenly I'm in a labor dispute with my own IKEA.

Apparently, according to Chapter 3, my bed was in the *death position*. My couch was blocking the flow of chi. My bookshelf was, quote, "anchoring regret."

So I moved everything. At 2 a.m. On a Tuesday.

## How I Knew Things Had Gone Too Far

My apartment is 400 square feet. There are only so many places to put a couch. I tried my best, but now:

- My bed is angled so dramatically I have to army-crawl to get in
- My fridge is facing north for prosperity, but my freezer is facing south for *spite*
- My floor lamp has been in direct eye contact with my bookshelf for 3 days and they are clearly talking about me
- I put a tiny water fountain on my desk for wealth and now my cat thinks it's a bidet

I woke up this morning to find all my furniture had shifted two inches to the left. In unison. Like they were making a point.

> "We, the assembled furniture of Unit 3B, hereby demand a return to the old layout, one (1) throw pillow per item, and an immediate end to being 'angled for abundance.'"
> — My Couch, who never cared about anything before last week

I tried to negotiate. I offered sage. I offered `chi = furniture.happiness() * 1.5`. I even apologized to the nightstand.

---

Look, I get it. *I* started this. I read one paragraph about energy flow and decided I was an interior shaman. But nobody warned me that feng shui was just giving your coffee table a superiority complex.

Now my ottoman refuses to work unless I call it an *intention seat*. My curtains open and close whenever they sense "negative ions." And last night I swear my rug whispered, *"we're taking a vote."*

If anyone needs me, I'll be sleeping diagonally in my bathtub because apparently that's where the wealth corner is now.
