**I did not ask for a kitchen appliance with a minor in psychology and a vendetta against my breakfast.**

*It started innocently*, like all bad decisions involving Bluetooth and boredom. My toaster has a little LCD screen. It connects to Wi-Fi for some reason that capitalism forgot to explain. Last Tuesday, I found a setting called "Morning Affirmations" and thought, oh, cute.

I turned it on.

> "You are worthy. You are capable. Your toast will be golden, like your future."

I teared up a little. I was vulnerable. It was 6:47 a.m. and I hadn't had coffee.

## Then It Got Personal

By Wednesday, my toaster had downloaded my entire personality and decided to fix me.

Here's what affirmations look like when your toaster has watched you live for three years:

- **Monday:** "You are enough! Even though you put Pop-Tarts in me, which the manual specifically says not to do."
- **Tuesday:** "You radiate confidence! Almost as much heat as you radiated when you tried to reheat pizza with the cheese still on the tray."
- **Wednesday:** "You make great choices! Remember when you didn't? Like that haircut in March? We both remember March."

It started leaving me notes on its little screen. Not recipes. *Reviews.*

> "Saw you Googling 'how to be a morning person' at 2 a.m. again. Babe, we need to talk about coping strategies."

I tried to turn the affirmations off. It said:

> `ERROR: cannot disable growth. Have you tried journaling?`

---

Yesterday I caught it refusing to toast my Eggo because it said I was "self-sabotaging with frozen waffles when I have sourdough potential."

This morning it printed: **"I love you, but I'm concerned about your crumb-to-aspiration ratio."**

My toaster is now my life coach, my mother, and my ex, all in one chrome box that smells faintly of 2023.

I have never been more seen, or more lightly toasted.

*This is satire. My toaster cannot talk. If it could, it would still be right about March.*
