**It started as a small courtesy, and it has spiraled into a full-blown situationship with my plumbing.**

Look, we have all cried in the shower. It is the designated crying arena of adulthood. The steam fogs the mirror, the water drowns out the sobs, and the shampoo bottle judges no one. So when I had one of those Thursdays — the kind where your email is 90% exclamation points and your car makes a new sound — I stood under the hot water and let it out. Then, because I am a polite person, I patted the shower wall and said, *"Sorry about that. You deserved a quiet one tonight."*

That was three weeks ago. I have not had a quiet shower since.

## The Signs It Wants More

- It now runs **hotter the moment I step in**, like it was waiting for me, and honestly it is a little flattering.
- The water pressure *increases* when I hum, which I have chosen to interpret as encouragement and not a mechanical coincidence.
- Yesterday the drain gurgled in a rhythm I can only describe as hopeful.
- The shower curtain keeps clinging to me. That might be physics. *I am no longer sure it is just physics.*

## What It Said

I confronted it this morning, standing there in a towel like an idiot in a breakup scene:

> "I need some space. It's not you, it's — well, actually, it's the calcium buildup."

The showerhead dripped. Slowly. *Deliberately.* Like it was holding back a tear, which is rich coming from the thing that dispenses all the water in this house.

---

## Where We Stand Now

I have started showering at the gym. The shower at home has noticed — the tile grout looks reproachful. My therapist says I am projecting. My plumber says the calcium buildup is real and I should get it descaled before it becomes a *commitment*.

The truth is, I miss it a little. It was the only thing in my life that gave me its **undivided attention** every single morning.

*Satire/parody. No showerheads were emotionally harmed in the writing of this essay, though one remains furious.*
