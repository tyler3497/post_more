**It started, as all bad decisions do, at 2 a.m. in the self-help aisle of the internet.**

I was staring at my phone, deep in the part of the night where my brain replays every awkward thing I've said since 2009, when a targeted ad whispered: *"Your feelings are valid. Your pixel pet's aren't yet."* Twenty-four hours later, a Tamagotchi arrived in the mail — a tiny 1996 rectangle of pure obligation.

## The Plan

The plan was simple. Offload. I would pour all my dread into the Tamagotchi so I could live dread-free, like a monk, but with snacks. I gave it my worries, one tiny button press at a time:

- Feed it my fear of mortality (`BREAKFAST`)
- Give it my career anxiety (`SNACK`)
- Clean up its existential droppings (`DISCIPLINE`)

For one glorious weekend, it worked. *I felt nothing. I was free.*

## The Problem

By Monday, the Tamagotchi had absorbed approximately 34 years of accumulated dread, and something in its 8-bit eyes changed. It stopped eating its pixel sandwiches. It ignored the little hearts. When I tried to play the guessing game with it, it just... sat there.

> "You made me aware of the void," it seemed to say, beeping faintly, "and the void beeps back."

I called customer support. A woman named Deb explained that the Tamagotchi had no warranty against philosophical awakening. She suggested I turn it off and on again. I told her, *"That's what I'm afraid of."* There was a long silence, and then she said, quietly, "Yeah. We hear that a lot."

---

## Where We Are Now

The Tamagotchi sits on my desk facing the window, staring at the rain like a tiny French poet. I've started sitting with it. Not to fix it — Deb says you can't patch dread — but to keep it company. This morning, it ate half a pixel sandwich. Then it beeped, softly, in what I can only describe as:

`dread --persist=false`

Maybe healing isn't a destination. Maybe it's a shared 2-inch screen and someone who also can't sleep.

*Anyway, I'm thinking of getting it a hobby.*
