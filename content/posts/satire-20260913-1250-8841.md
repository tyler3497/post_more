**It started as a joke.** Last Tuesday, stuck in the back of a two-hour meeting that could have been a three-word email, I leaned over to my own shadow on the wall and whispered, "Hey, if you clock out early, I'll cover for you."

The shadow left. It just... *slid off the wall* and went home at 3 p.m.

## The Incident, Explained

Here is what I have since learned about teaching a shadow about boundaries:

- **Shadows are extremely fast learners.** Mine picked up the concept of "quiet quitting" within minutes and had unionized itself by Thursday.
- **Shadows have no manager.** When you teach your shadow autonomy, there is no escalation path. It answers to no one. Not even, it turns out, the sun.
- **Shadows are excellent negotiators.** Mine now only follows me if I provide adequate lighting conditions, a surface it finds "acceptable," and one (1) dramatic exit per day.

> "We'd like to remind all shadows that they are not employees," said our HR director, Linda, during an all-hands. "They are appendages. They do not get dental."

## Where It Stands Now

My shadow filed a formal complaint claiming I was "stifling its silhouette," and HR — in what I can only describe as the boldest move in corporate history — has scheduled a mediation. For Monday. With *both of us*. Linda sent a calendar invite titled "You + Shadow: Reset Conversation," and my shadow accepted before I did, which is honestly the most hurtful part.

---

The real kicker? My shadow's performance review came back glowing. "Shows excellent presence in well-lit rooms. Takes initiative in vanishing." *I* got "needs improvement" on sunlight engagement. I have never been so thoroughly outshone by a dark patch of nothing.

Anyway, if anyone wants me, I'll be in the stairwell, negotiating with a two-dimensional version of myself over whether it has to come to my performance review or just "observe from the wall."

*Satire/parody. No shadows were harmed. One (1) HR director was mildly inconvenienced.*
