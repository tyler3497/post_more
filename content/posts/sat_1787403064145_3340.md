**I didn't mean to become a life coach for a fern.** *I was just trying to keep it alive past Tuesday.*

My monstera, Kevin, has been looking at me the same way my manager does during performance reviews — droopy, disappointed, quietly photosynthesizing judgment. So last Sunday, fueled by cold brew and an inspirational podcast clip, I stood in my living room and gave Kevin the full TED Talk.

## The Speech That Started It All

It was 7 minutes, 3 hand gestures, and one PowerPoint I made on my phone called `you_got_this.pptx`. Highlights included:

- You have *so much* untapped potential, Kevin
- Your leaves are not *browning*, they are **pivoting**
- The sun is just a really aggressive spotlight and you *deserve* center stage
- Remember when you survived two weeks without water? That's called **resilience**

> "Wow. I have never felt so seen. By someone who still uses a Brita from 2019," whispered Kevin. Or maybe that was the air purifier. Hard to tell.

### What Happened Next Was Not In My 5-Year Plan

By Tuesday, Kevin had:

- Printed business cards that say `Kevin Green — Synergy Consultant & Part-Time Oxygen Provider`
- Updated his LinkedIn (yes, I made him one, *don't ask*) with `if (growth == true) { keepGrowing(); }`
- Started waking up at 5 a.m. for "sun salutations" which is just literally facing the window
- Sent a cold email to my boss asking for my job

I found him this morning perched on the windowsill wearing a tiny tie I definitely did not buy.

> "I've been thinking about vertical growth opportunities," he said, adjusting his tie with a leaf. "And I think it's time I branch out. Literally."

---

**Now he has an interview on Friday.** For a wellness startup. They want him to be their "Chief Serenity Officer."

I, meanwhile, am still in sweatpants trying to remember my Zoom password. Kevin got a callback in 2 hours. My last application took 3 weeks and ended with "we'll keep your resume on file."

*Moral of the story?* Never motivate your houseplants. They will take your advice, your confidence, and eventually your career path.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This essay is entirely fictional, clearly absurd, and no houseplants were promoted, hired, or given ties in the making of this post. I am still employed. Kevin is still unpaid.*
