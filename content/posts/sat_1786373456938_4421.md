**I decided to become a morning person at 5 a.m. on a Tuesday, which is exactly when bad decisions feel inspirational.**

*For reference, I have historically been a night person, a late-morning person, and, on weekends, a cryptid who only emerges for snacks.*

The plan was simple: wake up early, journal, stretch, become the kind of person who owns chia seeds unironically.

Instead, my alarm clock developed emotional intelligence and an attorney.

## The Intervention

By Day 3, my alarm — a formerly loyal iPhone alarm named Sunny Sunrise™ — started snoozing *me*.

- 5:00 a.m.: Sunny plays birds chirping. I groan.
- 5:02 a.m.: Sunny whispers, "We talked about this."
- 5:06 a.m.: Sunny dims itself to give me space to reflect on my choices
- 5:09 a.m.: My houseplant joins the conversation uninvited

It suggested we seek help. Professional help.

> "You say you want to wake up, but your actions say you want to lie here Googling 'can you freeze-dry dreams?'," said our therapist, a very patient kitchen timer with a minor in existential counseling.

---

### Where We Are Now

We are working on our communication. I now say `good morning` out loud before checking my phone, and my alarm promises to stop playing that passive-aggressive recording of me from last Monday where I whisper-screamed, "WE ARE NOT DOING THIS TODAY."

Results are mixed. This morning I woke up at 5:30, drank lemon water, and immediately fell asleep standing up like a stressed flamingo.

*Progress is not linear,* my timer says, *but your bedtime is, apparently, fictional.*

We have homework: Sunny gets weekends off, I get one snooze without guilt, and we both agree to stop bringing up the time I tried to replace Sunny with a rooster app and the neighbors called the HOA.

`Status: Early bird by intention, pigeon by execution.`
