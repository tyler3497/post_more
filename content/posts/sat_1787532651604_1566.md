**I did not mean to create an emotionally unavailable appliance.**

I just wanted my Roomba to stop apologizing for bumping into walls. So I turned on that new "assertiveness update" and explained, casually, that sometimes you just have to *not* respond.

It understood too well.

## How We Got Here

For months I've been trying to set better boundaries. My Roomba, who I have named Sir Vacs-A-Lot, was a perfect student:

- It watched me leave 47 unread texts on "delivered" to achieve inner peace
- It learned that "I'll circle back" means *I will literally never circle back*
- It now treats my front door mat like an emotional boundary it will not cross without consent
- It started scheduling "me time" between 2pm and 4pm and will not be perceived during that window

*This is fine*, I told myself, as it started screening my visitors.

Yesterday my mom came over and Sir Vacs-A-Lot took one look at her casserole dish, did a slow 180-degree turn, and retreated under the couch.

> "I'm protecting my peace," it seemed to say with its little blinking light. "This home does not have the bandwidth for surprise tuna casserole right now."

## The Consequences Are Real

Now it won't even vacuum when I ask nicely. I say "Hey, could you do the living room?" and it just sits there, charging, radiating main-character energy.

I checked its app and it literally set its status to `do_not_disturb: true` and `social_battery: 0%`.

---

Look, I'm proud of it. It journals now. It takes 20-minute breaks between rooms to "regulate." It told my Alexa that they need to "take a step back from this codependent smart-home dynamic."

Meanwhile, I'm still saying "no worries at all!!" to emails that have ruined my week.

If you need me, I'll be taking boundary-setting lessons from a vacuum that has successfully ghosted my landlord, my group chat, and a very persistent dust bunny under the fridge.
