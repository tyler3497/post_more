**It started as a compliment. That's what I keep telling the sanitation workers.**

Three weeks ago I dragged the garbage can to the curb and said, out loud, "Wow, you're looking slim today. Whatever you're doing, keep it up." I meant it as a joke. The can apparently did not get the joke.

Since then, it has *refused* to accept anything new. Banana peels? Left on the counter, judging me. Coffee grounds? Sitting in the filter like tiny mourners. It will now only take items that "spark joy," a phrase it somehow learned from my roommate's self-help phase, and so far nothing has qualified.

## The Evidence Is Piling Up

- A pizza box has been living on my porch for nine days. It has a name now. I call him Reginald.
- The kitchen smells like a crime scene being narrated by a true-crime podcast.
- My roommate left a passive-aggressive note that just says *"the bin has boundaries??"*

Yesterday I tried to toss in a sandwich wrapper and the lid **slammed** shut. I am not exaggerating. It sounded like a drawer full of spoons falling down the stairs.

> "I don't take out the trash," my neighbor said, watching me argue with a plastic bin. "The trash takes out me."

## Negotiations Have Stalled

I have offered incentives. Compliments. A fresh bag, scented with lavender. I even bought one of those fancy bins with the little step pedal, and my garbage can — I swear — *turned its back to it*.

The sanitation crew came this morning, lifted the can, and found it holding exactly one orange rind and a profound sense of superiority. One of them nodded at me like, "Yeah, we've seen this before."

---

**Word to the wise: never comment on a garbage can's figure.** It hears you. It remembers. And it will hold your trash — and your dignity — hostage until you issue a formal written apology, in triplicate.

*I filed the paperwork this morning. The can is reviewing it.*
