**I didn't mean to start an emotional relationship with a kitchen appliance.** But when you work from home long enough, you start talking to things that beep.

*It started innocently.* I was waiting for toast, half-asleep, and I said out loud, "So, how's your day going?" to my toaster.

It burned my toast. Intentionally. I know it did.

## The Escalation

For three days, the toaster has been communicating exclusively through toast shade:

- **Light golden** — "I'm tolerating you"
- **Medium brown** — "You left me plugged in overnight again"
- **Charcoal briquette that sets off the smoke alarm** — "We need to talk about the crumbs"

I tried to apologize. I cleaned the crumb tray. That only made it worse.

> "Oh, NOW you notice me?" the toaster seemed to say, via a single Pop-Tart launched at Mach 2 into my forehead. "After six months of everything-bagel seasoning down my throat?"

I tried to set boundaries. I unplugged it for an hour. When I plugged it back in, it heated up but refused to pop. Just held my bread hostage at the bottom, warm and trembling.

*This is how my toaster tells me it needs space.*

---

### My Current Strategy

I've started leaving it little offerings. A wipe-down with a damp cloth. Using the `defrost` button so it feels needed for more than one job.

**My therapist — okay, my group chat — says I'm projecting.** But they didn't see how it looked at me when I used the air fryer yesterday.

Look, I know this is absurd. It's a 9 toaster with two settings: `barely warm` and `archaeological artifact`. But until this cold war ends, I'm eating cold bread and pretending I prefer it that way.

*Update: it just popped without anything in it. That's a warning shot.*
