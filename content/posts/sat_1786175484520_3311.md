**I thought I was just cleaning on Saturday.** Turns out I was ending an era.

Two years of *strategic avoidance* — every time I saw the freezer frost creeping, I told myself it was *insulation*. My roommate called it "character." My freezer called it... well, I found out what it called it.

## The Excavation Begins

I unplugged it, laid towels down like I was performing surgery. Within minutes, the archaeology started:

- A bag of peas from **2024** that had achieved permafrost superpowers
- A lasagna with a Post-it that just said *do not open* in my own handwriting
- Three identical tubes of frozen ginger, each purchased because I forgot I owned ginger
- An ice cube tray from the last World Cup that had somehow unionized

The ice itself began to crackle. I swear it was *apologizing* before it got defensive.

> "We have held this community together for 28 months," whispered the top shelf, in what I assume was the voice of a very tired middle manager. "You can't just *melt* institutional knowledge. We have systems."

## Emergency Summit

Suddenly the frozen waffles wanted representation. The lone fish stick demanded a closing statement.

I tried to explain that I needed space for, you know, food I would actually eat. The peas were not having it. They had formed a committee.

---

In the end we negotiated:

**They would:**
- Evacuate peacefully into the sink, no dramatic flooding
- Release the peas with honor

**I would:**
- Stop buying a new bag of frozen berries every time I decide to be healthy for 48 hours
- Actually label things with `date: 2026-08-08` instead of `mystery`
- Never pretend frost is "extra fresh"

I agreed. They melted. My freezer is now 100% empty, 200% guiltier, and whistling like a brand-new apartment that doesn't trust me yet.

Status: defrosted. Emotionally compromised. Still finding peas in my socks.
