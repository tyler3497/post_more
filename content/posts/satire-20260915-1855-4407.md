**It started as a joke. I told my mother-in-law the oven mitt was "in charge of the kitchen." She believed me. So did the mitt.**

Three weeks ago, in a moment of weakness between the casserole going in and the timer going off, I pinned a tiny laminated badge to my oven mitt that read HEAD OF SECURITY. I expected laughter. What I got was *the most disciplined employee this household has ever had*.

## The New Regime

Since the promotion, dinner has become a security operation. Guests are now subject to the following protocols:

- All pockets must be emptied before approaching the dining table
- Casserole dishes are scanned — visually, by a mitt with no eyes
- Anyone reaching for the good parmesan is escorted back to the regular parmesan
- The dog has been demoted to "consultant"

My oven mitt takes its job *extremely* seriously, which is impressive for something with no arms, no brain, and a faint smell of last Thanksgiving.

## A Direct Quote

I asked my roommate how she felt about being patted down by cookware. She said:

> "I came for lasagna and left with a new understanding of power. The mitt looked right through me. I have never felt so seen by something with a thumb loop."

She's not wrong. The confidence is *unsettling*.

## The Incident

Last Tuesday, my own father was denied entry to the kitchen because he "looked like trouble." His words. He was holding garlic bread. *Garlic bread.* I tried to intervene, but the mitt had already assumed the stance — you know the stance, one puffed-out quilted shoulder, thumb loop at a jaunty angle — and my father just... complied. He ate in the hallway. He said the hallway lasagna "tasted like respect."

---

I have considered revoking the badge, but honestly? Dinners have never run smoother. The table is set on time. Nobody double-dips. And for the first time in years, my house runs on *order* — enforced by 100% cotton, heat-resistant to 450°F.

*This is satire. No oven mitts were given actual authority. My father has been cleared for the dining room pending review.*
