**I finally decided to be a responsible adult and flip my mattress.** Not because my back hurt, though it does *constantly*, but because the little tag said to do it every six months and I was only 30 months overdue.

I lifted, I heaved, I made a noise my chiropractor would bill extra for.

Underneath was not dust. It was progress.

## The Discovery

For three years, my mattress and box spring have been quietly running a fully functioning micro-nation:

- A tiny downtown made of lost earring backs and bobby pins, *shockingly well-planned*
- A highway system built from charging cables I swore the dryer ate
- Three ancient Goldfish crackers running a food truck called *The Soggy Crumb*
- A lost AirPod case serving as the town hall — battery at 2% and still legislating
- A whole cul-de-sac of sock singles living their best divorced-dad lives

> "We have thrived in the absence of your interference," said Mayor Lint the First, a surprisingly charismatic clump of dryer fluff. "We have built schools. We have built infrastructure. We have a farmers market. Please put the mattress back."

I tried to explain it was *my* bed.

### The Negotiations

Turns out they had a whole economy based on things I drop during midnight doom-scrolling:

- **Currency:** loose change and hair ties, obviously
- **Energy:** static electricity from my 2019 fleece blanket
- `status: abandoned_by_giant || thriving_without_him`

I offered to relocate them. They offered me a timeshare.

---

Look, I appreciate hustle. *These people built a civilization out of my neglect* and their roads have fewer potholes than my actual city.

We reached an agreement: I get the mattress back, they get quarterly tribute in the form of one sock, one pretzel, and me never actually vacuuming under there again.

I flipped it back. Gently. I think I heard applause.
