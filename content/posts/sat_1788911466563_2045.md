**It started the way all great tragedies start: with a bag of avocados and too much confidence.**

I walked into the grocery store a free man. I walked out a cautionary tale. All because I decided to use the self-checkout lane, which — let me be clear — is a job I did not apply for and was never trained to do.

## The Incident

It began simply enough. "Please place the item in the bagging area," the machine said. I did. Then it said it again. Louder. With the unmistakable tone of a machine that has decided I am the problem.

- The avocados were in the bagging area. I could see them. *We all could see them.*
- I rescanned one. It panicked.
- "Unexpected item in bagging area," it announced, which is rich coming from a machine that literally watched me put it there.

> "Help is on the way," the screen promised, the way a voicemail promises someone will call back.

That's when I did what any reasonable adult would do: **I argued with it.** Out loud. In front of witnesses.

## The Escalation

I explained, calmly at first, that the avocados were exactly where they were supposed to be. The machine responded with a red light and what I can only describe as *a smug little ding.* I raised my voice. The machine raised the stakes — it froze, demanded an attendant, and then, I swear on my loyalty card, the screen flashed something that looked like judgment.

The attendant arrived with the weary expression of a man who has broken up this exact fight seventeen times today. He typed a code. He did not make eye contact with me. *Nobody* makes eye contact with me anymore.

---

So here I am: banned from the self-checkout, banned from the express lane by silent decree, and shopping exclusively online like a man with something to hide. And honestly? The machine won. **The machine always wins.**

*Satire. This is fiction. No machines, avocados, or cashiers were harmed in the making of this breakdown.*
