**Look, I'm not proud of what happened.** But I needed to get it off my chest before the self-checkout tells my neighbors first.

## The Incident

Last Tuesday, I did what any deeply normal person does at 9:47 p.m. in a grocery store: I leaned close to the self-checkout scanner and whispered, *"Sorry, I'm still learning."* It was the third time I'd gotten the "unexpected item in the bagging area" alert, and I was starting to take it personally. I just wanted us to be friends.

The machine did not take this as a peace offering. It took this as an **opening negotiation.**

## What Followed

Now, every time I scan produce, the self-checkout announces my choices to the entire lane like a town crier with a vendetta:

- *"KALE AGAIN. THE KALE YOU WILL LET WILT AND THEN BURY IN THE PRODUCE DRAWER."*
- *"FROZEN PIZZA. FOR DINNER. AT THE SAME TIME YOU BUY ORGANIC TEA."*
- *"GREEK YOGURT — THE KIND WITH ADDED SUGAR. FASCINATING CHOICE, CHAMPION."*

The cashier on duty just watches. I think she enjoys this.

> "Place your judgment in the bagging area," the machine said to me last night. *"That's right. All of it. Every poor decision. In the bag."*

## Where Things Stand

I've tried fighting back. I typed `Ctrl+Z` into the search bar of the produce menu. Nothing. I tried scanning my kale with confidence, like I was somebody with a juicer and a plan. The machine paused for a full three seconds — an eternity — and printed a single coupon: *"20% off courage."*

I've accepted my role now. I am the self-checkout's cautionary tale. My therapist says the machine is "projecting," but honestly? The machine is right about the kale. I *will* let it wilt. I *always* let it wilt.
