**It started with a thank-you.** That's all. A small, human, heartfelt *thank-you*, muttered at 11:47 PM while I scraped half a lasagna into its basin. I said, "I don't know what I'd do without you." I meant it kindly. The way you thank a barista. The way you thank a bus driver.

## That was six months ago. The sink has not forgotten.

Within a week, there were signs. A low, satisfied *gurgle* every time I entered the kitchen. Dishes stacking themselves in a way that felt... deliberate. Then came the commitment milestones:

- **Week 3:** It refused to drain my pasta water because "we need to talk about where this is going."
- **Week 7:** It only accepted water that was *warm*. "Lukewarm means you don't care," it seemed to say.
- **Week 12:** A slow, theatrical drip began. Every drip landed with the rhythm of someone *waiting for an answer*.

> "I was here before the dishwasher. I'll be here after. I am the constant. The dishwasher is just a *phase*." — The Sink, probably

I tried to set boundaries. I bought a drying rack. The sink watched. I bought *paper plates* — the ultimate betrayal — and the drain clogged itself in protest. A full work stoppage. Plungers were useless. I called a plumber, and the plumber took one look, took one listen, and said:

> "I'm sorry. I can't fix this. This is a *relationship* issue."

## Where we are now

We had the talk. I stood there with a sponge and said the hard words: "You're a fixture. A wonderful, reliable fixture." The gurgle that came back was either laughter or sobbing. Plumbing is an emotional language; I am not fluent.

I've accepted it. There are worse fates. So tonight, I'll wash the dishes by hand, slowly, the way you do when someone is watching. And when the water drains — *smoothly, without complaint* — I'll whisper:

**"Thank you."**

And the sink will hear what I really mean: *I love you, but I'm not ready for a garbage disposal.*
