**It started with a single wilted fern and a guilt trip so heavy I skipped brunch to say sorry.** I have 14 houseplants, and last month, in a moment of drought-induced shame, I began apologizing to them. Not quietly. *Out loud.* Full monologues. The neighbors definitely heard.

For one week, I delivered a structured apology tour. Each plant got:

- A personalized verbal apology ("Kevin, the succulent, I am so sorry about the sunburn. I turned you too fast.")
- One (1) heartfelt pat on the leaves
- A hydration plan I was legally bound by, according to me

## The Response Was Swift and Terrifying

On day three, the monstera had rotated toward the window at an angle I'd never seen. The pothos was *thriving* with an attitude. And by Friday, the fern — the one I killed twice and revived through sheer waterboarding — had grown a new frond shaped suspiciously like a middle finger.

> "We, the undersigned flora, demand consistent watering schedules, quarterly repotting, and one full day of indirect sunlight without your cat's interference." — The Fern, presumably

Then I found **the list**. A curled leaf with dewdrop indentations. Demands. Signed by root systems I cannot verify.

## Where Things Stand Now

I have hired nothing, because I cannot find a lawyer who takes plant union disputes seriously, which feels like exactly the kind of systemic oppression they'd cite. The philodendron has stopped leaning toward me. The aloe vera has started producing *extra* gel out of spite.

**Lessons learned:**

1. Never anthropomorphize anything with access to your windowsill.
2. Guilt-watering only leads to entitlement-sprouting.
3. The fern wants a meeting on Thursday. I will be attending, dressed nicely, because Kevin's succulent told me she'd "see how I showed up."
