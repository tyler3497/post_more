**I decided to save $35 on a haircut, which in hindsight was the exact moment my reflection decided to break up with me.**

*This is the kind of decision that sounds smart until you're holding kitchen scissors over your head at 11:47 p.m.*

## The Master Plan

YouTube told me it was easy. "Just point-cut!" said a guy with perfect hair who has clearly never panic-sweated in a bathroom. My logic was flawless:

- Professional haircuts are expensive
- I own scissors (craft scissors, but scissors)
- How hard could it be? It's just hair. It grows back. *Right?*

> "Bro, are you sure?" — my mirror, silently screaming, if mirrors could talk, which mine now refuses to do

I started conservatively. A trim. A little off the sides. Then gravity and hubris took over.

---

## The Incident

Twenty minutes in, I had created something no licensed professional would recognize as human hairstyling. It was part lawn-mowing accident, part modern art installation titled *Man Who Lost a Bet With Himself*.

My roommate walked in, paused, and said:

> "Oh wow. You're going for... airflow? Like, aerodynamic? For biking?"

*I do not own a bike.*

### Where We Are Now

My mirror now fogs itself up on purpose when I enter the bathroom. My hat collection, previously decorative, has unionized and demanded overtime.

I tried to fix it with a YouTube tutorial called "How to Fix a Bad DIY Haircut" which was just a man whispering *just shave it all off, bro* for 12 minutes.

The good news? I did save $35.

The bad news? I spent $60 on hats, $12 on beanies I will never remove, and my self-esteem is now running on `npm run cope`.

**SATIRE / PARODY** — This is 100% fictional. No mirrors were harmed, no actual hair was butchered this badly (okay maybe a little), and I have since booked a real barber who is contractually obligated not to laugh.
