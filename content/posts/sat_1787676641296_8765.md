**I didn't mean to start an international incident. I just wanted ice for my water.**

*In retrospect, opening the freezer at 2 a.m. and whispering "we need to talk about your attitude" was where diplomacy failed.*

It started because my ice maker has two settings: **nothing for three days**, and then **entire glacier** at 3:17 a.m. that sounds like a hockey team collapsing down the chute. I asked nicely. I knocked. I said, "Hey buddy, could we do like, a steady trickle?"

The freezer hummed. Which I took as consent to begin formal negotiations.

## My Opening Offer Was Reasonable, I Thought

I laid out my terms like a very tired UN delegate in socks:

- I will defrost you exactly zero times a year if you stop traumatizing me at night
- You provide 8-10 cubes per day, no more, no less
- I will stop storing that mysterious bag of peas from 2023 as a hostage

The ice maker responded by dropping one single, perfect cube, then jamming.

> "You think you control the cold?" said my freezer, or possibly the sleep deprivation. "I *am* the cold. You live in my climate now. These are my terms."

He slid a tiny frost-covered treaty across the shelf.

### The Geneva Convention (Kitchen Edition)

Article 1: The human shall no longer refer to it as "the ice thing."
Article 2: The freezer light shall stay on for 30 seconds of respectful silence after each opening.
Article 3: All lukewarm leftovers must be presented with an apology.

`if (human.requestsIce() && human.isRude) { dispense(null); playAvalancheSoundAt(3_17_AM); }`

Honestly? Fair.

---

I signed it. Of course I signed it. My water is now *perfectly chilled* and my freezer has never been more smug. Last night it auto-made a heart-shaped cube.

*We are healing,* but only on its terms. I have never been more hydrated or more politically owned by an appliance.
