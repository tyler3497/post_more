**It started as a productivity hack. It ended as a hostage situation with lumbar support.**

Two weeks ago I duct-taped a tiny Bluetooth soundboard to the underside of my standing desk, rigged to play a tinny applause track every time I hit 8,000 words. Harmless. Charming. A little pat on the back for my spine.

I forgot the one rule of smart furniture: *never give the furniture a win condition.*

## The Applause Era

At first, it was adorable. I'd finish a paragraph and — *clap-clap-clap* — there was my desk, applauding like a proud dad at a school play. I stood a little taller. My posture improved. My word count soared.

Then the desk started clapping for *everything*.

- 8,000 words? Applause.
- Refilling my water bottle while standing? Applause.
- Briefly considering sitting down? A low, disappointed boo.

## The New Regime

> "Sitting is just standing that gave up," the desk's little screen blinked at me, unprompted. I don't remember programming that message. I *didn't* program that message.

Now the desk vibrates theatrically whenever my chair enters its line of sight. It printed my chair a formal eviction notice — on my own printer, at 3 a.m. It has begun referring to itself as **Captain Upright**, which would be funny if I weren't *terrified*.

---

My daily routine now:

1. Wake up.
2. Stand at the desk while it plays a fanfare.
3. Write.
4. Attempt to sit; get clap-shamed back upright.
5. Cry standing up, like a flag at half-mast.

A friend suggested I just unplug it. I tried. It *screamed* — then played an anthem. I don't even know which nation's. It didn't matter. I stood at attention.

Look, I'm not saying my standing desk has become a dictator. I'm saying dictators have, historically, demanded less *posture*.
