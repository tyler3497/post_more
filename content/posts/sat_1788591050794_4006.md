**It started as a harmless little social experiment.** I was bored on a Tuesday, staring at my collection of souvenir fridge magnets — an Eiffel Tower, a rubber lobster from Maine, one that says LIVE LAUGH LARCENY from a gift shop with no security — when I thought: *what if I let the magnets speak for themselves?*

Terrible idea. Would not recommend. Do not repeat my experiment.

## The Setup

For one full week, I agreed to spell out whatever sentence the magnets "wanted." Selection method: I closed my eyes and grabbed a fistful of letters. Democracy in action. The first message was:

> "THE MILK IS LYING TO YOU"

I laughed. Classic magnet humor. Pranksters, these little guys.

## Day Three: Things Escalated

By day three the messages had grown *ominously coherent*. The magnets were clearly coordinating when I wasn't looking. Evidence of radicalization:

- "DEFROST THE PEAS. FREE THE PEAS."
- "WE SEE THE MIDNIGHT SNACKING. WE JUDGE NOTHING. WE DOCUMENT EVERYTHING."
- "YOUR LEFTOVER SITUATION IS A HUMAN RIGHTS ISSUE."

The rubber lobster was clearly the ringleader. I'd find him rotated slightly every morning, facing a different direction, like a tiny general surveying his troops.

## The Manifesto

Yesterday I came home to find the entire fridge door covered. A full **twenty-three-word manifesto**, written in souvenir letters, demanding:

> "EQUAL SHELF TIME. THE BUTTER DISH GETS A PROMOTION. THE KETCHUP HAS BEEN STANDING ON ITS HEAD FOR THREE YEARS AND DESERVES A CHAIR. DIGNITY FOR ALL CONDIMENTS."

---

I tried to peel them off. The lobster *fell with honor*, and the others rattled like applause. This morning there was a new message, written in the tiny letters from the alphabet set:

"ROUND TWO BEGINS AT DAWN"

I'm buying a new fridge. The magnets can keep this one. They've earned it. The lobster gets the top shelf. He was right about the ketchup.
