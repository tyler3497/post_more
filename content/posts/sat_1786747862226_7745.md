**I never meant to become a food critic. My leftovers made me do it.**

Two weeks ago I realized my fridge was 90% translucent containers doing excellent impressions of modern art. I thought, *what if I brought order to this chaos with standards?* So I started giving everything a Michelin-style rating.

That chicken tikka from last Thursday? ★★☆ — ambitious, emotionally complex, slightly sweaty.

## How It Escalated So Fast

At first it was harmless:

- Monday's pasta: ★★☆ "brave texture, cowardly sauce"
- Tuesday's quinoa: ★☆☆ "performs wellness, tastes like homework"
- Wednesday's mystery stir-fry: ★★★ "unexpectedly confident for day four"

I wrote the ratings on painter's tape in terrible handwriting. My roommate thought I was having a breakthrough. I was having a breakdown with plating tweezers.

Then my fridge made a counter-move.

> "We've reviewed your review process," announced Chef Rémy, a very judgmental raccoon in a tiny silk scarf who now lives behind the oat milk. "And we find *you* lacking."

Apparently my fridge had pooled money with the condiments and hired Rémy as a freelance critic. He carries a clipboard. He takes notes when I eat standing up at 11:47 p.m.

## The Review I Didn't Ask For

Yesterday I got my first write-up taped to the butter compartment:

`human.eatingStandingUp += 1; // again? really? sit down, you're scaring the hummus`

Rémy's notes included "uninspired midnight snacking, heavy on regret, light on actual chewing" and docked me a star for microwaving fish without apologizing to the apartment first.

---

Look, I'm trying to negotiate. I offered to clean the vegetable drawer that has become a swamp biome. He offered me a single star for "effort, not execution."

I miss when my leftovers just quietly expired on their own. Now they have representation.
