**It started as a budgeting hack.** You can't Venmo-request yourself in the past. But you can, apparently, stick your future self with half the check — and the future keeps receipts.

## The Idea

Last Tuesday, at dinner with absolutely no one, I stared down a $64.80 check and thought: *what if I only pay half and let future me handle the rest?* Economists call this "smoothing consumption." I call it "not having the money right now." So I scribbled "I'll get you back in 2029" on the receipt and walked out of the restaurant like a man who had personally solved inflation.

## The Consequences

Within days, the calls began:

- **Monday:** "This is a courtesy call regarding your outstanding 2029 balance of $32.40, plus interest, plus temporal penalties."
- **Tuesday:** The caller ID just said `2029`.
- **Wednesday:** I picked up. A voice that sounded exactly like me, but wealthier and more disappointed, said, "You know what you did."

> "You left me a sticky note that says 'lol good luck buddy.' Buddy. *Buddy.* We are the same person."

## Where It Stands Now

I tried disputing the charge. The dispute form asked for my name, my address, and my **chronological position**. The agency offered a payment plan with installments that stretch forward *and* backward in time simultaneously. I've retained a lawyer. She bills by the hour, which is awkward, because I no longer know which hour.

---

**Update:** I have paid the $32.40. Future me Venmo-requested me for the interest *and* the emotional damages. I declined the request. He accepted it anyway. That is his power now. He has it because he is me.

*This is satire. Nothing in this post happened — including, regrettably, the restaurant.*
