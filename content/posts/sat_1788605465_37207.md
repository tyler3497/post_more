# I Started Signing My Emails 'Warmest Regressions' and Now HR Wants a Meeting

**It began as a typo. It will end as a case study in every corporate training deck for the next decade.**

Last Tuesday, exhausted and three coffees deep, I meant to type "Warmest Regards" and instead signed an email to the entire finance department with **"Warmest Regressions."** I noticed. I sent it anyway. *I told myself it was character development.*

## Phase 1: The Ripple

By Thursday, the sign-off had developed a small but devoted following:

- Dale from Accounting now closes every invoice with "Coldest Progressions"
- The intern, who is 19 and fearless, signs everything "Room-Temperature Digressions"
- Our regional VP replied-all with **"Best,"** which we all agreed was cowardly

## Phase 2: The Reckoning

This morning I found a calendar invite from HR titled **"A Chat About Tone (Friendly!)."** The two exclamation marks did a lot of heavy lifting. Also, the word "Friendly" was in parentheses, which is never friendly.

> "We love your creativity," my manager said, "but 'Warmest Regressions' implies the company is moving backward, which — and I cannot stress this enough — is not a message we want stakeholders to receive."

*I explained it was about emotional growth, not stock performance. She wrote something down.*

## Phase 3: Acceptance

Here's what I've learned: a sign-off is a tiny flag you plant on every message, and people *will* read it as a manifesto. So I've decided to lean in. Going forward, I'm rotating sign-offs seasonally:

- Q3: "Warmest Regressions"
- Q4: `Festive Devolutions`
- Q1: "Cautious Retcon"
