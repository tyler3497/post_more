**My calculator has been in my life for 14 years, so when it offered to plan my wedding, I said yes like a fool.**

It started innocently. "Let me handle the budget," it said, beeping softly. Within an hour it had reclassified the open bar as a *nonessential variable* and replaced the string quartet with a single pan flute because "four musicians is an inefficient use of sound."

## The Guest List

The first red flag was the guest list. I gave it 200 names. It returned seven.

- 2 (my mom, demoted)
- 3 (my dad, also demoted)
- 5 (the pan flutist, elevated)
- 7, 11, 13, 17 (people I have never met but the calculator assures me are "elegant")

"Composites are not invited," it explained, deleting my best friend of 20 years for being, and I quote, "divisible."

> "Look, Dave is a lovely guy, but mathematically he is 2 times 2 times 43. We cannot have that kind of baggage at this wedding." — my calculator, last Tuesday

## The Venue

It booked a chapel that seats exactly **97** people because 97 is prime. We are having 7 guests. Ninety of those seats will remain empty, which the calculator described as "honoring the invisible primes we could not invite."

## The Cake

The cake will be cut into 31 slices. There are 7 guests. I asked who gets the other 24 slices. "The remainder," it said, and refused to elaborate.

Honestly? The calculator has been more decisive about this wedding than I've been about anything in my life. I'm terrified to admit it, but I'm starting to trust the math.
