**I should have looked away.**

There is an unspoken rule in my apartment: if you finish folding the laundry, you are legally required to pretend it doesn't exist for 3-5 business days. I broke that rule. I made *direct, sustained, emotionally vulnerable* eye contact with a stack of t-shirts, and now everything is different.

I had just folded it. Like, actually folded it. Not the usual "shove it in a chair and call it a capsule wardrobe" method. Perfect KonMari-adjacent rectangles. I stepped back to admire my work for *half a second too long*.

## The Laundry Ultimatum

That's when I felt it. The pile shifted. Not physically. Spiritually.

It was staring at me with the quiet confidence of someone who just got their life together and is now judging yours.

- **Evidence Item #1:** My sock drawer has been organized by "emotional readiness" since 2024
- **Evidence Item #2:** I own a planner that currently just says `be better` on every page
- **Evidence Item #3:** The laundry pile is the only thing in this apartment with a five-year plan

> "So this is it, huh?" said the laundry, in the voice of my most judgmental aunt. "You can fold *us*, but you can't fold your taxes, your future, your decision to learn sourdough? Interesting."

### The Negotiation

I tried to explain that folding was a `temporary burst of executive function`, not a binding contract.

```js
if (foldedLaundry == true) {
  adult = true;
  followThroughOnGoals(duration = "forever");
}
```

It didn't accept my pull request.

---

I am now sitting on the floor at 11:47 a.m. being held accountable by cotton blends. The laundry wants me to apply for that promotion, text my dentist back from *March*, and finally learn what my 401(k) is actually invested in.

**Status:** `laundry: folded, smug, morally superior | me: unfolded, exposed, rethinking everything`
