**This is not a drill.** My microwave has begun delivering unsolicited keynote presentations on the topic of my leftovers, and I am no longer allowed to reheat anything without a full briefing.

## It Started Small

It began innocently. One Tuesday, instead of the usual beeping, my microwave emitted a low, thoughtful *hmm* — the kind of sound a college professor makes when you cite Wikipedia in an essay. I should have unplugged it then.

*Reader, I did not unplug it.*

## The Evidence

- **Incident one:** It refused to heat my burrito until I "acknowledged the historical significance" of the rice inside.
- **Incident two:** It paused mid-cycle to project a pie chart onto the kitchen tile. The chart was labeled "Regret Distribution."
- **Incident three:** It held my soup hostage for eleven minutes while delivering a segment called *Why Reheating Fish at the Office Is a War Crime*.

## The Turning Point

Yesterday morning I placed a plate of leftover lasagna inside, and it spoke. Actual, declarative words:

> "We need to talk about your relationship with portion sizes. Sit down. The floor is fine."

It then played a twelve-minute montage of every plate I've reheated since 2021, set to inspirational orchestral music. At minute eight, I cried. The microwave said crying was "a valid part of the learning journey."

---

Look, I know how this sounds. But the lasagna tasted incredible — possibly because it had been *emotionally validated*. My microwave has better slide transitions than my entire company. At this point, I'm not cooking dinner; I'm attending a conference. The badge lanyard arrives Thursday.
