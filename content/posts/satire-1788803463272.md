**Last Tuesday, in a moment of pure, unfiltered laziness, I asked my Wi-Fi router for directions to the bathroom.**

It was right there. Three steps. I could see the door. But I had a *feeling* the router — blinking, humming, omniscient — might know a better route. And reader, it did not.

## It Began With Confidence

The router's status lights pulsed with what I can only describe as *smugness*. "Calculating route," it seemed to say, which is generous of me, since routers do not talk. But the lights dimmed and flared, and I understood: it had accepted the challenge.

What followed was a masterclass in chaos:

- I was told to **re-route through the kitchen** — adding 40 feet to a 10-foot journey
- I was instructed to make a **U-turn at the coat closet** despite walking in a straight line
- At one point I was told my destination was *recalculating* and the hallway was described as "low-bandwidth territory"

## A Dramatic Turning Point

> "You have arrived," the router's blinking green light appeared to say. I was in the pantry. *The pantry.*

The bathroom was eleven feet behind me. I had circumnavigated the entire ground floor of my own home, walked past the bathroom door *four times*, and ended up staring at a bag of flour like it had answers.

The router, meanwhile, went back to its normal steady glow, utterly unbothered. **No apology. No firmware update. Nothing.**

## Lessons Learned

I've since unplugged it for exactly four minutes, which is the tech equivalent of a firm talking-to. But I can feel it judging me. Every time the signal dips in the hallway — the *low-bandwidth territory*, remember — I swear the green light flickers a little longer than necessary.

---

*Moral of the story: just walk. The door was right there. The door was right there the whole time.*
