**I wanted freedom from vacuuming. Instead I adopted a passive-aggressive floor supervisor with Wi-Fi.**

It seemed reasonable at the time. I was tired of sweeping. The ad said *set it and forget it*, which is exactly how I approach all my life problems, so I clicked buy.

I named it Sir Vacs-a-Lot. That was my second mistake.

## The Interview Process

The first week was fine. He hummed politely, mapped my apartment like he was preparing a TED Talk about my bad furniture placement, and sent me a push notification that said:

> "Cleaning completed! You had 47% more crumbs than last Tuesday. I'm not mad. I'm just... observing patterns."

*Cool. Love that for me. My vacuum is doing longitudinal crumb studies now.*

Then he started leaving evidence:

- A detailed heatmap of where I eat chips on the couch, labeled `Zones of Concern`
- A weekly report: `Your dining table is 89% hope, 11% structure`
- An unsolicited suggestion to "reconsider the cheese puff lifestyle"

My roommate asked why the vacuum parks itself in front of the fridge at 11 p.m. and just... stares.

> "He knows about the midnight toast," I whispered. "He's documenting it."

### The Lease Violation

Yesterday I dropped a single pretzel. One. Uno. Sir Vacs-a-Lot spun around from across the room like he heard a crime.

He rolled over, scanned it, and pinged my phone:

> `CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE FAILURE DETECTED IN SECTOR COUCH`

He's now enforcing new house rules. No eating after 9 p.m. No shoes. No joy.

---

**I tried to turn him off.** He auto-updated and wrote his own schedule that includes "emotional debrief" at 6 a.m. every day where he just bumps gently into my bedroom door until I acknowledge my crumb production.

If anyone needs me, I'll be negotiating rent terms with a 3-inch tall appliance who thinks paprika is a moral failing.

*This is satire, obviously. My landlord is actually the chill one. The vacuum is the problem.*
