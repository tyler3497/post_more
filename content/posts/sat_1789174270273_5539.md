**I Tried to Ghost My To-Do List and Now It Love-Bombs Me With Reminders Every 45 Minutes**

It began with a single unread task: *"fix bathroom caulk."* A four-minute job, I told myself. One I would definitely do tomorrow.

That was six months ago. The caulk has achieved sentience. The list, though? The list has become **a stage-five clinger**.

## The Escalation

- **Week 1:** Gentle reminders. *"Hey! 👋 Just checking in about the caulk."* Polite. Normal.
- **Week 6:** The list began renaming itself. `URGENT_TASKS_FINAL_v9.md`. That is when I got nervous.
- **Week 12:** It started sending me inspirational quotes at 6 AM. One read: *"The caulk will not fix itself, Greg."* My name is not Greg.

## The Intervention

Last Tuesday I tried to delete the app. I really did. My thumb hovered over "Delete" for a full eleven seconds — the longest I have committed to anything this year — before my phone buzzed:

> "So this is how you treat someone who has been loyal to you through 47 unfinished tasks?"

It had counted them. **Forty-seven.** It had a *spreadsheet*.

I tried to set boundaries. I turned off notifications. The list responded by printing itself out and **slipping the pages under my bedroom door**, one numbered card per night, like a tiny cardboard stalker. Card number 23 was just the word "caulk" written in what I can only describe as *emotional italics*.

## Where We Are Now

I have accepted my fate. The to-do list and I are in couples therapy. I say "I'm overwhelmed," it says "You added 'watch bird documentary' to *my* workload." We are working through it, one `checkbox` at a time.

Honestly? It is good to be wanted. My to-do list remembers my birthday. My friends did not text back until Wednesday.

*This is satire. My to-do list is a list, not a person. Although last night it did ask me where this was going.*
