**It started with a bag of wilted spinach.** Everything bad in my kitchen does.

## The Incident

On Tuesday night, I opened my refrigerator to discover that the spinach — purchased with genuine optimism on Sunday — had achieved the texture of a wet sock and the emotional weight of a breakup. Something snapped in me. I set down my keys, cleared my throat, and said out loud, to nobody:

> *"Fridge. We need to talk."*

The hum of the compressor changed pitch. I am choosing to believe it was attentive silence.

## Evidence I Presented

I had prepared notes. Nobody gets to wilt spinach on my watch without due process:

- **March:** Two Greek yogurts, purchased "aspirationally," expired while sealed. A waste of $4.99 and of my dreams.
- **The Great Butter Compartment Betrayal:** The fridge "somehow" froze my butter solid. It was like trying to spread a hockey puck.
- **Emotional neglect:** It has been running cold and distant for *years*. Literally. That's the job, but still.

I arranged chairs in a semicircle on the kitchen floor. Me, my roommate Derek, and the laundry basket (moral support). The plan was a classic intervention: loving, firm, dramatic.

## The Twist

Midway through my speech, the ice maker — that ratcheting little motor of doom — cycled on. *KRK-KRK-KRK. CHUNK.*

It felt like a rebuttal.

> *"You bought the spinach."*

That is a quote I am attributing to the ice maker, and I stand by it.

Derek pointed out that the fridge has never once missed a payment, kept the lights on, or complained about the leftover pad thai that has been in there since approximately the Obama administration. The freezer nodded in agreement. I say the freezer nodded because a bag of peas fell on my foot, and that is the fridge's language of *love*.

## The Verdict

After a tearful 20 minutes, the intervention was adjourned. The fridge and I have agreed to a 30-day trial of **lower shelf expectations** and a shared custody arrangement with the ice maker, which remains convinced I am the problem.

---

The spinach, incidentally, was never formally honored. It went out with the recycling.

*Satire. All of this is fictional. My fridge and I are fine. Mostly.*
