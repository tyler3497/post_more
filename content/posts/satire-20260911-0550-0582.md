**SATIRE — This post is pure fiction. No squirrels were disenfranchised in the making of this post.**

It started the way all great democracies start: with a bag of peanuts and absolutely zero impulse control.

Last spring, I tossed a handful of unsalted peanuts to one (1) squirrel on my fence. A humanitarian gesture. *Noble*, even. Within 72 hours, there were fourteen squirrels in my yard, and one of them had drawn up a constitution in acorn-cap ink on a wet napkin.

## The Campaign

I did not ask to run. The nomination process was hostile:

- My main opponent, a gray squirrel named **Chad** (I don't name them, they just *feel* like Chads), ran on a platform of burying 40% more nuts
- A chipmunk showed up with a PowerPoint projected onto my garage door
- The debate devolved when Chad threw an acorn at the moderator

> "He feeds us. Chad only hoards," testified one voter, nibbling my shoelace. "We need a leader who *shares*."

The vote was unanimous except for Chad, who cast twelve ballots and was immediately disqualified for voter fraud.

## Governing

Being elected leader of squirrels is **90% ceremony, 10% getting pelted with crabapples** by dissenters. My duties so far:

- Officiating three nut-hoarding weddings
- Vetoing a bill that would have nationalized the bird feeder (the birds staged a filibuster)
- Posing for the official portrait, which took six weeks because nobody can sit still

---

I've asked my HOA if "elected squirrel leader" counts as community service. They have not responded. The squirrels have, though: this morning I found a tiny crown of bottle caps on my porch with a note that read, simply, *"Again."*

*I would like to thank my constituents, my peanuts, and Chad, whose concession speech was just screaming.*
