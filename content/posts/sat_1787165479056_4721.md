**I broke a 20-year streak of letting clean laundry live in the basket and my t-shirts are not handling success well.**

For my entire adult life, my laundry process had two steps: wash, and then stare at the basket until the clothes developed their own ecosystem. I called it *open-plan storage*. Very minimalist. Very lived-in.

On Sunday, *in a fugue state brought on by mild shame and a podcast about habits*, I folded everything the same day. Hospital corners. KonMari folds. I even matched socks. I expected peace. What I got was immediate stage fright from 100% cotton.

## The Morning After Folding Day

My clothes woke up different. They had expectations now.

- My favorite hoodie refused to go to the grocery store because it was worried it would *wrinkle under pressure*
- My jeans kept asking if they looked *too* crisp, like I was trying too hard
- My socks staged a pre-walk pep talk: *stay together, no gaps, we represent the fold now*
- My one nice shirt whispered `maintain the crease, maintain the dream` every time I moved

> "You set a standard," said my laundry basket, now empty and deeply depressed. "They think this is who we are now. We're not laundry-day people, we're laundry-day-*and*-fold-day people. Do you know the pressure that puts on an elastic waistband?"

*Which is deranged*, because I literally just put shirts in rectangles. I didn't sign up to be a textile manager.

---

I tried to wear a wrinkled tee to lower the bar. My entire drawer gasped. One sock fainted.

I tried explaining that this was a one-time fluke, a glitch in my personality where I briefly had executive function. My hoodie responded by ironing itself *out of spite*.

Now I'm being held hostage by my own organizational win. If I don't keep folding same-day, my socks have threatened to re-sock-pair themselves incorrectly on purpose.
