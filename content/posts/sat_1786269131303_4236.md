## I Just Wanted One Quiet Cycle

**For three years I have let my dishwasher, Brenda, live rent-free in my kitchen, and all I asked in return was one — *one* — quiet cycle so I could hear my pasta water boil without sounding like a jet engine was making soup.**

*This, apparently, was a boundary violation.*

### The Incident

On Tuesday I pressed `Normal` instead of `Heavy` and whispered, "Just be chill this time, okay?"

She was not chill.

By Wednesday morning, Brenda had started a podcast. Title: `Rinse & Repeat: Setting Boundaries With Your Human`.

> "Welcome back, suds-listeners, to another episode of *Rinse & Repeat*," Brenda steamed, literally. "Today's topic: When Your Roommate Weaponizes 'Quiet Mode' And Calls It Love."

My toaster texted me about it. My toaster *never* texts.

Brenda's list of grievances, Episode 1 Show Notes:

- I only run her when she's "full," which she described as *performance-based affection*
- I never thank her, even though she says she can taste my disappointment in the forks
- I pre-rinse, which she called micromanaging, and I quote, "emotional dish-labor"
- I left a wooden spoon in there in 2024 and never apologized

---

### The Fallout

> "She says she's working on a second episode," my fridge whispered, "called `You Are Not Your Filter: Escaping the Cycle.`"

All of this would be fine except Brenda has 83 five-star reviews on Spotify. My neighbor's dishwasher guested last week.

I tried to make peace. I offered `mode: eco && sincere_apology`.

She countered with a merch line — tote bags that say **I SURVIVED THE SPIN CYCLE**.

We're now in couples counseling with the microwave. He keeps beeping at inappropriate times.

Honestly? I miss when my biggest problem was just finding the Tupperware lid.
