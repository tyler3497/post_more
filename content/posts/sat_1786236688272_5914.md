**I wanted to be the kind of adult who has fancy ice.** You know, clear spheres, slow-melt, artisanal cubes that whisper *you have your life together*.

Instead I now have a freezer with standards I cannot meet.

## The Upgrade

It started innocent. I bought a two-pack of sphere molds off an Instagram ad at 1:14 a.m. for $19.99. The kind that makes you feel like Don Draper making an Old Fashioned, even if you only own discount lemonade.

I filled them with tap water. Rookie mistake.

For 48 hours my freezer was silent. Then it started.

- My tap water was declared "too emotionally cloudy" to become a sphere
- My frozen peas were evicted for "lack of aesthetic cohesion"
- My ice cubes now have a tasting note sheet that just says `notes of regret and fluoride`

### The Sommelier Arrives

Yesterday I opened the freezer and my own voice — but posher — narrated:

> "Here we have a 2026 midwestern tap selection, exposed too early to room temperature, with an underdeveloped chill structure and unresolved childhood trauma about being left in a Brita for six days."

It's not even my voice anymore. It's my freezer. It has adopted a French accent it did not earn.

---

It has demands now:

- **Only filtered water** boiled twice and *allowed to reflect*
- No opening the door without a reservation
- A `no crushed ice` policy it enforces with passive-aggressive frost

I tried to make regular old crescent cubes from the fridge's built-in maker as a peace offering. The spheres rolled to the front of the tray in what I can only describe as solidarity.

*My roommate asked why the kitchen smells like judgment.* I told her the freezer has palate-cleansed into a new lifestyle.

`Status: hydrating under supervision, freezer is accepting applications for winter internship`
