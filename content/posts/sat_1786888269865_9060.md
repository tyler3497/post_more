**I decided to finally become the kind of person who sorts laundry before washing it, which was my first mistake.**

*I had been thriving under the Puddle System™ for years — if it hits the floor, it goes in the basket, if it smells weird, it definitely goes in the basket.*

## The Great Division

Last Saturday I announced, like a founding father with fabric softener, that we were going to do things properly. Lights, darks, delicates.

My laundry basket immediately stopped speaking to me.

Turns out, after three years of unconditional acceptance, it took my sudden standards *personally*. It hired Brenda from Facilities — which is my sock drawer apparently — to act as its union rep.

According to Brenda's official grievance filing:

- My "selective sorting" violates the Sacred Agreement of 2023 where all clothes are equal
- I have not once thanked the basket for its service during my "all-black-goth-midnight-laundry" phase
- The way I do that little sniff-test is "emotionally damaging and legally ambiguous"
- My fitted sheets have been misclassifying themselves as "flat" for tax purposes

> "Frankly, your basket is burnt out," Brenda told me while adjusting her tiny hard hat. "You throw in a wet towel at 11:47pm and expect not to have a conversation about boundaries? Be serious."

## The Negotiation

I tried to explain that I just wanted my white t-shirts to stay white.

Brenda slid a clipboard across the dryer and showed me the counter-proposal: `process_laundry_feelings.sh --validate --apologize-profusely`

Now we have mandatory 5-minute "unload circles" where I hold each item and affirm its journey before washing. My socks are on a work-to-rule slowdown and will only pair with socks they personally vibe with.

---

But this morning? I found my laundry folded. Neatly. With a Post-it that said **WE HAVE REACHED A TENTATIVE AGREEMENT. MANAGEMENT (ME) WILL TRY HARDER.**

I cried. My laundry basket winked.

*This essay is satire/parody — entirely fictional, absurd, and not a real labor dispute. No baskets were harmed, no socks were forced into unsafe working conditions. All sorting systems depicted are unnecessary and not real.*
