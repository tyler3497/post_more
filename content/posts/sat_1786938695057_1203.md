**I did not mean to create an overachiever when I whispered “you got this” to my drooping monstera last Tuesday**, I just meant please don’t die like the last one.

*For context, the last one was a succulent that I killed by loving it too intensely and googling “is misting emotional support?”*

## It Started With Encouragement

I read somewhere that talking to plants helps them grow, so I gave it a little motivational speech while watering it. Nothing crazy. Just your standard Ted Talk:

- You’re doing amazing, sweetie
- Photosynthesis is a mindset
- That brown spot is character development
- We all have yellowing days

My monstera, who I’ve named Dr. Green in retrospect ironically, *perked up*. Literally. Like sit-up-straighter perked up.

> “Thank you for seeing my potential,” said no plant ever, but also absolutely said my plant with its leaves.

By Thursday, it had sprouted a new leaf. By Friday, it was watching me type.

## Now It Wants Letters of Recommendation

This morning I found a draft email open on my laptop:

```
Subject: Application - PhD in Environmental Resilience - Statement of Purpose by Monstera Deliciosa
```

It wants me to write a recommendation letter. It also wants me to be its emergency contact but *not* its plus-one to orientation because, quote, I would “distract from its academic gravitas with my inability to keep a cactus alive.”

Current demands include:

- A south-facing window *and* a north-facing window “to see both perspectives”
- Classical music, but only the sad violins part
- Me to stop calling fertilizer “plant coffee”

---

**I’m proud of it, honestly, but I was not prepared to be academically outpaced by something that lives in dirt I bought on sale at Target.**

If it gets in, I’m going to have to brag about it. “My plant? Oh, she’s at Stanford now. Very busy. Photosynthesizing thoughtfully.”
