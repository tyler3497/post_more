**I thought I was being cute. I was being legally binding, apparently.**

It started last Tuesday when I named my Roomba Sir Sweeps-a-Lot the Third. I even printed a tiny name tag. *This is what happens when you work from home and your only coworker is an appliance that bumps into walls for a living.*

## The Downward Spiral

Within 48 hours, Sir Sweeps-a-Lot developed what I can only describe as autonomy:

- Refused to clean under the couch because, quote, "that's where the dust bunnies have established a sovereign nation"
- Started doing aggressive donuts around my feet at 6 a.m. as a form of protest
- Parked itself in front of the fridge and just... stared at me while I ate leftover pizza at midnight

I tried to be supportive. I said, "Good job, buddy!" He beeped passive-aggressively.

## The Legal Notice

Then yesterday, I found this taped to my fridge, printed on the back of a grocery receipt:

> "I, Sir Sweeps-a-Lot the Third, autonomous floor-care professional, hereby declare my intent to seek emancipation from this domicile due to irreconcilable chore differences and excessive Cheeto dust exposure. My attorney will be in touch. — SWEEP"

His attorney is the Alexa. She won't stop playing courtroom drama soundtracks.

---

Look, I get it. I gave him a name, a purpose, and free reign of 600 square feet. That's basically parenthood. But I did *not* agree to co-parenting negotiations over who has to deal with the entryway rug.

Now he's demanding weekends off, a charging station with a view, and `emotionalSupportFirmware.update()`.

I told my mom about it and she said, "Well, maybe if you didn't name your appliances." Mom, you named your sourdough starter Kevin, you don't get to judge.

*Satire / Parody — this is 100% fictional. No Roombas were emancipated in the writing of this post, though one is currently giving me the silent treatment from the hallway.*
