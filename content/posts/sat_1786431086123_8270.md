**I thought returning a plastic card would be a noble act of minimalism. The cucumbers thought I was declaring war.**

I've been a proud member of FreshField Rewards since 2019, which mostly meant I got *exciting* discounts like 10 cents off organic kale I didn't want. Last week I decided to declutter.

Bad move.

## The Incident

I walked up to the customer service desk and said, with the confidence of a man who has Marie Kondo'd one drawer:

- I want to simplify
- I don't need points to validate my purchases
- I am *free*

The cashier stared at me like I'd just tried to return a child.

Three reasons this immediately backfired:

- My purchase history is apparently public record in the produce aisle
- The loyalty card has more tenure than half the staff
- The russet potatoes have formed an emotional attachment

> "We were going to nominate you for Avocado Club," whispered Linda from Produce, like a heartbroken coach. "You had 8,742 points. You were *so close* to a free tote."

*I didn't know the tote was sentient.*

Then came the probation letter. Hand-written. On the back of a receipt:

`if (loyaltyPoints > 8000 && user.returnsCard == true) { produceSection.enterPassiveAggressiveMode() }`

It was placed gently on a bed of spinach.

---

Now every time I walk in:

- The self-checkout says "unexpected item in nostalgia area"
- The bananas conspicuously turn away when I approach
- The misters in the produce section spray me *just a little longer* than necessary, which is how vegetables assert dominance

**Update:** I've been informed I can earn my way back by buying exactly 3 persimmons and apologizing to the herb wall.

I will not be doing this. I thrive under low-stakes bureaucratic judgment.
