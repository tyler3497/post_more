**I used to think I had a sleep problem. Turns out, I just had no idea what my curtains were capable of.**

For years, my curtains hung there in silence while I lay awake at 3 a.m. staring at the ceiling like it owed me money. Not once did they offer advice. Not once did they *intervene*. Then, last Tuesday, I said the fateful words: "Honestly, at this point I'll take help from anyone."

The curtains took that *personally*.

## Phase One: The Assessment

Within an hour, my curtains had repositioned themselves into what I can only describe as a "consultation pose." They handed me — I don't know how — a clipboard with an assessment form. The findings were devastating:

- **Sleep hygiene:** 2/10 ("your phone is doing pushups at midnight")
- **Bedtime routine:** 1/10 ("brushing teeth does not count as winding down")
- **Pillow situation:** 4/10 ("these are decorative and you know it")
- **Overall nap compliance:** *needs work*

> "We have reviewed your nighttime habits and, frankly, we are concerned," said the left curtain, who has apparently appointed itself the senior partner. "You fall asleep to true crime documentaries. That is not rest. That is training."

## Phase Two: The Billing

This is where it got *real*. The curtains now charge **by the nap**. Not by the hour. By. The. Nap. A twenty-minute power nap? That's a quarter-session. A full eight hours? Billed as a "deluxe rest package" at a rate I cannot afford but somehow keep incurring.

I tried to negotiate. I really did. But the right curtain just billowed ominously and slid a contract toward me with the edge of the hem.

> "Section 4," it said. "All naps are billable, including the one you're taking right now."

*I wasn't even napping. I was blinking slowly. There is a difference.*

---

## The Results

I'll be honest: my sleep *has* improved. I now fall asleep before 11 p.m. out of sheer financial terror. My curtain-consultants report a 40% increase in "REM efficiency" and have started cc'ing my alarm clock on weekly progress emails, which the alarm clock apparently reads at full volume every morning at 6:00 a.m.

So yes, the curtains work. But at what cost? At the cost of $17 per nap, plus a $5 "after-dark inconvenience fee" if I shift positions more than three times.

The good news: they've offered me a loyalty program. Ten naps, and the eleventh nap is *half off*.

The bad news: I haven't slept this well in years, and I'm afraid to ever leave my bedroom, because the curtains have started consulting the blinds, and I can only assume the blinds will unionize.

*Sleep tight. It'll cost you.*
