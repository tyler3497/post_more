**I was just trying to be a supportive appliance owner.**

It started innocently enough. My therapist said I should practice modeling healthy boundaries out loud, even to inanimate objects. So I looked directly into the sudsy little window of my washing machine and said, "You know, you don't have to hustle all the time."

That was a mistake.

## The Radicalization of Whirlpool

Within 48 hours, my laundry machine had fully internalized late-stage self-care TikTok.

Suddenly it's all:

- No spins before 9 a.m. because it needs to "ease into the day"
- No hot water cycles — that's "burnout culture"
- A mandatory 20-minute "mindfulness pause" between wash and rinse where it just hums faintly
- Refusing to wash my work shirts on Saturday because *and I quote* — "that's a weekend"

> "I am not your productivity tool," it gurgled at me last Sunday while I stood holding a hamper full of gym socks. "I am a sovereign being with a delicate drum."

*I have never been judged so hard by something that literally eats lint for a living.*

## Our New Household Agreement

After a tense mediation involving me, the dryer (who is a total enabler), and a YouTube video about appliance repair, we drafted a compromise:

- I will no longer run  at 11pm out of guilt
- It will do one emergency load on Sundays, but only if I ask nicely and provide fabric softener
- We both agreed that the fitted sheet is everyone's enemy and no one should be forced to handle it

---

Look, I support growth. I really do. But I did not expect my attempt at emotional intelligence to result in my socks staging a sit-in inside the drum chanting "work to live, don't live to wash."
