**I deleted 2,000 unread emails in one afternoon and I think I gave my inbox an identity crisis.**

For five years, my inbox and I had an agreement. *I would ignore it, and it would quietly judge me from the background.* A perfect system. An ecosystem.

It was thriving. Newsletters I never signed up for, coupons from a store I visited once in 2019, 14 identical reminders that my domain was expiring. We were family.

## The Purge

Last Tuesday, fueled by cold brew and misplaced productivity, I did the unthinkable. I sorted by unreads, hit select all, and pressed delete.

2,147 emails. Gone. Vaporized. Unsubscribed from the fabric of spacetime.

My laptop made a noise I have never heard before. Like a sigh, but digital.

### It Did Not Take This Well

My inbox went blank. Stark. Empty. For the first time since 2021, it displayed that cursed phrase: `Inbox Zero`.

Then the symptoms started.

> "Where did everyone go? Where's Geoffrey's 20% off gutters newsletter? I need him. I *was* him. Who am I without a 3-year-old Bed Bath & Beyond receipt?"

*Totally reasonable reaction, honestly. I've been there.*

**The dashboard of loss, according to my inbox:**

- 438 unread CVS coupons it considered its personality
- 212 LinkedIn notifications from people endorsing me for skills I definitely don't have
- A 17-email chain where I tried to cancel a free trial in 2023 and lost
- One very earnest email from 2021 titled `open this!!!`

---

Now it pings me at 3 a.m. to check if the spam folder is okay. It keeps trying to restore a newsletter called **"Daily Ferret Facts"** I have never once opened.

I tried to comfort it. I said we were minimalists now.

It replied with an auto-response I didn't write:

`Status: currently out of office grieving my unread count`

*This is SATIRE / PARODY / FICTION. No inboxes were harmed. My unread count remains emotionally, if not numerically, at 2,000+.*
