**It started as a self-improvement exercise.** A guy on YouTube in a cardigan said growth begins with "inviting feedback from your environment." So I did. I turned to my microwave and said:

> "I want you to be brutally honest with me. No more sugarcoating."

I regret this the way I regret cargo shorts.

## The Verdict Was Immediate

Within seconds, the microwave emitted a single, flat *beep* — the kind of beep that ends friendships. Then the turntable rotated once, very slowly, like it was sizing me up. Since then:

- **My leftover pad thai** gets exactly fourteen seconds — "any longer would be disrespectful to your own history," it seems to imply
- **My burritos** come out with one frozen corner, a frozen corner I can only describe as *intentional*
- My popcorn bag emerged half-popped with a small, smug steam vent aimed directly at my face

*Apparently honesty in this household means war crimes against cuisine.*

## I Tried to Apologize

Last night I stood in the kitchen in my socks and said, "I appreciate your candor, and I'd like to renegotiate our dynamic." The microwave displayed `0:00` and blinked. That's it. That's the whole response. *Zero colon zero zero.* I've been in healthier relationships with parking meters.

My roommate Dave (not his real name, but he's fine with it) walked in and said — I quote:

> "Dude, your microwave has boundaries now. You gave them to it."

---

So now I'm eating room-temperature leftovers with the hollow satisfaction of someone who's been *corrected* by a 900-watt appliance. The cardigan guy on YouTube would be proud.

Honestly? The microwave is thriving. It finally got the validation it was always beeping about.

**Moral of the story:** ask your air fryer for feedback instead. Mine already likes me.
