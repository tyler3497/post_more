**I did not mean to become my houseplant's career coach.** I was just avoiding my own resume.

It was a Tuesday. *You know the kind — where you’ve opened LinkedIn, closed it, opened it again, and whispered “later.”* My monstera, Kevin, was looking particularly photosynthetic in the corner, so I made him a profile as a joke. I gave him a title: “Senior Photosynthesis Specialist | Green Growth | Sunlight Operations.”

LinkedIn loved him.

## The Profile That Ended My Relevance

Within 24 hours, Kevin had:

- 47 connection requests from “Sustainability Thought Leaders”
- a personalized endorsement for `sunlight optimization` from someone named Chad in SaaS
- an InMail from a startup that wants him as their “Chief Wellness Officer” because he “brings oxygen to the room, literally”
- and a recruiter who said his headshot — *which is just him leaning, leaf-first, into natural light* — showed “executive presence”

Meanwhile, my profile still says `open to work` and my only recent activity is liking a post about “5 Ways to Crumble Under Pressure.”

> “Kevin demonstrates unparalleled vertical growth in Q2,” wrote his new manager, a fern with 12k followers. “We’re offering him a sunny corner office with unlimited water. We fear you are holding him back with your ‘vibes’ and irregular misting schedule.”

I tried to compete. I added “Plant Parenting” to my skills. Kevin added “Cross-Functional Chlorophyll Strategy” and got endorsed by three actual botanists.

---

### My Performance Review Came From a Pot

Last night, I found Kevin had updated his banner to a photo of *my* apartment and captioned it “Thriving in a High-Growth Environment.” My apartment. The one where I pay rent.

So now I live with a middle-management monstera who has a better network than me, asks for 1:1s at 7 a.m. (when the sun hits the window), and keeps leaving me on read.

**SATIRE / PARODY — this is fiction, my plant does not have a 401(k). Yet.** I have applied to be Kevin’s assistant. He said he’ll “circle back.”
