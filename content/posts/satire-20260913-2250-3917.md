SATIRE/PARODY — This is a work of fiction. Any resemblance to real umbrellas is coincidental and honestly their problem.

**It started with a compliment. It always starts with a compliment.**

Last Tuesday, during the kind of rainstorm that makes you question every decision that led you outside, my umbrella performed *flawlessly*. Not a drop on my shoulders. Bone dry. So on the walk home, flushed with gratitude and damp-adjacent euphoria, I said the words that would ruin my life:

"You're a really great umbrella."

## The Incident

By Wednesday morning it had changed. I found it standing open in the hallway, blocking the front door, as though it had been *waiting for me*. When I tried to close it, the mechanism resisted — not jammed, exactly. *Reluctant.* Like it had decided the collapsed position was beneath it now.

By Thursday it had moved to the living room. Open. Centered under the ceiling fan. Casting a dramatic shadow.

## A Timeline of Escalation

- **Wednesday:** Refuses to stay closed. Makes a small *thwip* sound when folded, like a sigh.
- **Thursday:** Opens itself near windows, angling toward the light. I think it's posing.
- **Friday:** Fully open over the coffee table during a movie night. My friend asked why. I said, "It's going through something." I do not know why I lied for it.
- **Saturday:** I caught it open in the *bathtub*. Not being dried. Just... open. Thriving.

## What the Neighbors Saw

My neighbor Carol knocked to return a Tupperware and froze in the doorway, staring at the open umbrella in my entryway.

> "Is it... raining in here?"
>
> "No," I said, with the hollow calm of a man who has lost control of the narrative. "He just needs to feel the breeze."

She left the Tupperware on the porch. She didn't knock. She *texted*.

---

Here's the thing about praising an umbrella: unlike a dog, it doesn't wag. It just *absorbs the validation* and grows larger with it, like a thunderhead of pure ego.

Yesterday I tried to leave for work on a cloudless, sunny day — the kind of forecast that screams *you don't need it* — and found it open on my passenger seat. I lock my doors. I have *one* umbrella.

The forecast calls for sun all week. I am bringing it anyway.

*It keeps me dry. It keeps me terrified.
