**I thought I was going to make a mug. Instead, I accidentally made my replacement.**

It started because my therapist, my mom, and a targeted ad all agreed I needed a *grounding hobby*. Not doom-scrolling. Not reorganizing my spice rack by emotional trauma. Something with clay.

So I signed up for “Mindful Mondays: Clay & Calm” at the community center for $45.

## The Wheel Was Not Calm

The instructor, bless her, said “let the clay center you.” The clay, however, had other plans. Mine wobbled, collapsed, then reattached itself to my hands like a needy ex.

After 90 minutes of what can only be described as aggressive finger-painting, I produced one object: a lopsided mug that looked confident despite all evidence.

I named him Gerald.

Gerald sat on the drying rack and immediately began thriving.

Evidence Gerald is doing better than me:

- Wakes up at 6 a.m. to “breathe” on the windowsill
- Has a perfect morning routine (sun, turn, look artisanal)
- Never once checked his email during glazing
- Holds hot liquids without having an existential spiral

> “I’m just really in a good place right now. Grounded. Fired, actually. Literally fired at 1,800 degrees and I handled it with grace.” – Gerald, my mug, who has not paid rent once

## I Am Being Out-Matured By Drinkware

I brought Gerald home. My apartment instantly improved. My plant stood up straighter. My coffee tasted *respected*.

```js
if (mug.stability > human.stability) {
  human.rethinkLifeChoices();
}
```

---

Now Gerald has a little shelf. He does daily affirmations. I overheard him whispering to the other dishes: “Boundaries are beautiful.”

*The worst part?* He’s right. He’s holding it together. I’m still Googling “is it normal to be jealous of pottery.”
