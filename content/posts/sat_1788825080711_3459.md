**I just wanted clean socks.** That's it. That's all I ever wanted.

## The Incident

Last Tuesday I crammed approximately nine pounds of denim, one hoodie, and a fitted sheet (which, scientists agree, is the most aggressive shape in nature) into my washing machine. It groaned like a grandfather getting out of a recliner. In a moment of guilt I leaned down and said, *"Hey, sorry about that, buddy."*

**Big mistake.**

## The Aftermath

The machine, which I have now named *Douglas*, heard me. Douglas has opinions. Douglas has *feelings*. And Douglas is no longer afraid to pause an entire wash cycle to share them.

Since the apology, every cycle now includes:

- A **35-minute rinse pause** during which Douglas hums at a frequency psychologists describe as "mournful"
- Mid-spin check-ins where the dial turns itself to *gentle* and the display reads "R U OK"
- One memorable incident where it refused to drain until I sat on the floor beside it and "just listened"

> "I am not angry," I told the repair technician, as Douglas audibly locked its own door. "I am simply *in a boundary-setting phase* with my appliance."

The repairman wrote *emotional* on his invoice. Then he underlined it twice. Then he left.

---

Look, I'm not saying my washing machine is alive. I'm saying that after my apology, it started treating the spin cycle like couples therapy, and frankly my socks have never been softer *or* more emotionally attuned.

If you own a Douglas, **do not apologize.** Some doors, once opened, can only be closed by a technician who has given up on his dreams.
