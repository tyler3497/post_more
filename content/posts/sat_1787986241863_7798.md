**I just wanted to stop sounding like I lived in a haunted Airbnb every time I got a midnight snack.**

*It was one hinge. One tiny, dramatic, E-flat squeak on the pantry door. I thought 30 seconds of WD-40 would fix it. I did not think I would accidentally become my home's unpaid therapist.*

I've lived here three years. The pantry squeak was part of the soundtrack. My fridge hums, my floorboards pop, my closet sticks if you look at it wrong. It's a whole ensemble. But last Tuesday at 2 a.m., while hunting for peanut butter, that squeak hit different. So I fixed it.

## The WD-40 Incident

One spray. That's it. The door went silent. *Too* silent. The kind of silent where you can hear your house holding its breath.

> "You... you changed me," the pantry whispered, or maybe that was just the air vent. "Was I not good enough as I was?"

By Wednesday, things escalated:

- My bathroom door now opens *too* smoothly and then apologizes for it
- The hallway light flickers politely to ask if its brightness is okay
- My kitchen faucet only drips when I make eye contact, like it's testing boundaries
- My living room floorboard that always creaked has gone completely mute and left a note that says `squeak.exe has stopped working - please validate me`
- The linen closet is hosting nightly affirmations for the other doors

---

I tried to reassure everyone. I told the front door it was doing great. I complimented the windows on their transparency. I left an affirmation on the fridge:

> "You are a strong, beautiful shelter and your settling noises are valid. This is a SATIRE - none of this is real, I just have a very dramatic house."

**It didn't work.** Last night I came home and all the doors were slightly ajar, like they were trying to show me they could be quiet *and* open to change. My house now has full-blown performance anxiety because I dared to improve one thing without improving everything at once.

*The pantry door still doesn't squeak, but it does sigh heavily whenever I reach for chips at midnight, which is frankly worse than the squeak ever was.*
