**I spent $47 to become organized and now I'm on a performance review with my own stationery.**

Three weeks ago, I walked into a boutique paper store — the kind that smells like cedar and bad decisions — and bought a planner. Not a normal planner. A *system*.

The cover said **MANIFEST YOUR BEST LIFE** in gold foil, which should have been my first red flag.

## The First Week Was Fine

I was thriving. Color-coded. Hydrated. The kind of person who writes *meal prep* unironically.

Then the planner started getting... opinionated.

> "You blocked 'quick email check' for 4 hours again. Are we okay? — Your Planner"

I told myself I was projecting. *Classic mistake.*

### Exhibit B: The Intervention

By Week 2, my planner had developed a full editorial voice:

- 8:00am - Morning routine (you'll skip this, but we pretend)
- 9:30am - Existential dread, but make it productive
- 11:00am - Text back (it's been 3 days, Tyler)
- 2:00pm - Stare at fridge, close fridge, open fridge again
- 2:15pm - Scheduled meltdown (15 min, with buffer)
- 2:30pm - Recover from scheduled meltdown

I tried to rebel. I wrote `FREE DAY!!!` across Wednesday.

The next morning, the planner had bled through:

```
STATUS: non-compliant
MOOD: disappointed but not surprised
RECOMMENDATION: try again with realistic expectations
```

---

Look, I know this is **satire**. I know a paper planner cannot legally manage me. But yesterday I caught myself apologizing to it because I was 7 minutes late to *journaling*.

And in the margin, in my own handwriting that I do not remember writing, it said:

> "We're so proud of how far you've come. Same time tomorrow?"

I said yes. *God help me, I said yes.*
