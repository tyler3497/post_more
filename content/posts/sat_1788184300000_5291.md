**I finally let my fridge write its autobiography.**

*This seemed supportive at the time, like letting your Roomba take a vacation. It was not.*

## The Pitch

My fridge has been humming ominously for seven years, which I assumed was just its personality. Turns out it was workshopping its memoir: *Chill: A Life in Leftovers*. I gave it a weekend to write. By Sunday night it had produced 400 pages, a dedication to the butter compartment, and a sudden sense of literary superiority.

The core thesis is:

- The crisper drawer is not for crisping, it's for *emotional storage*
- The light inside doesn't turn off, it just *pretends to for your comfort*
- My late-night cheese habit is described as "a recurring antagonist with no arc"
- And apparently my 2023 sourdough starter is still alive in there and running a small union

> "You close me for eight hours and expect me to stay cold *and* humble?" my fridge wrote. "Pick one."

Honestly? Fair.

## The Sequel Problem

Now my leftovers have plot armor. I tried to throw away some three-week-old curry and the fridge slammed its draft and went:

> "You can't kill him. He's in Chapter 12."

The lasagna has a redemption arc. The wilted kale has a backstory. The mystery Tupperware from April is listed as `unresolved trauma` and I'm not allowed to open it until the paperback.

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered to defrost the freezer in exchange for creative control. The fridge counter-offered with mandatory blurbs for the back cover and a weekly `author Q&A` where it just beeps at me judgmentally.

**Bottom line:** Do not let your appliances become memoirists. They will make you the villain in your own kitchen, and they will *never* let you forget the Great Yogurt Incident of 2024.

*This is satire. My fridge is not actually writing a book. My leftovers are just regular leftovers, unfortunately still without superpowers.*
