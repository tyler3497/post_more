**I thought the hardest part of getting a library card would be remembering where I put it.** I was wrong. I was *so* wrong.

I signed up on Tuesday because the library has free Wi-Fi and I wanted to look intentional somewhere quiet. The librarian smiled, handed me a laminated card, and said "Welcome to the system." I did not realize she meant *the* System.

## The Audit Begins at 10 a.m. on a Tuesday

By Wednesday, I got an email: `DEWEY DECIMAL QUARTERLY REVIEW — MANDATORY ATTENDANCE`.

My life, apparently, had been incorrectly cataloged:

- My sock drawer: currently classified under 646.3 but should be 001.9 *Anomalies and Unresolved Mysteries*
- My meal prep: 641.5 *Cooking* is generous; it's really 398.2 *Folklore*
- My five-year plan: filed under 001-909 *Waiting Room Magazines*
- My houseplants: collectively listed as 583 *Abandonment Issues (Botanical)*
- My inbox: 025.04 *Information storage that actively bullies you*

> "Sir," the head auditor said, adjusting glasses that refracted my entire 2024, "you have twelve overdue decisions and you've been renewing the same excuse since March."

I tried to explain that my personality is more of a *vibe* than a classification. She stamped a card very slowly in a way that felt judgmental.

---

### My Appeal Was Denied

I brought a character witness — my tote bag. It folded under pressure. Literally. Under questioning, it admitted it only holds three books and crushing anxiety.

The system ran a diagnostic:

`Status: 813.6 — American fiction, specifically you pretending you have your life together`

The verdict: I have two weeks to reshelve my hobbies, return my 2019 optimism in good condition, and stop trying to file my taxes under *Fantasy*.

My new plan is to be very, very late on purpose so I accrue enough fines to be interesting. The librarian says that still counts as character development.

*This is satire and parody. No Dewey Decimal systems were harmed, consulted, or promoted to life coach during the writing of this essay.*
