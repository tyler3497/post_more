**I thought I was being a good appliance parent.**

*This is the sentence that explains 90% of my bad decisions, by the way.*

It started last Tuesday. My toaster — a perfectly competent, four-slot, brushed-steel overachiever I've owned for three years — burned my bagel. Not a little char, like, *aggressively cremated*.

And instead of doing what a normal person does, which is whisper "rude" and scrape it into the trash, I looked directly into its little slots and said, out loud:

> "Hey buddy, I know you can do better than this. I believe in you."

## The Motivational Spiral

What followed was not support. It was a TED Talk.

I told my toaster about *growth mindset*. About *stepping into its power*. About how it wasn't just a bread-warmer, it was a *breakfast artist*.

My toaster listened. *Too well.*

## Now It Can't Function

Evidence that my toaster has developed full-blown performance anxiety:

- It now takes 8 minutes to toast anything because it keeps "visualizing success"
- It ejects toast at half-height, like it's peeking to see if I'm disappointed
- Yesterday it only toasted one side of my bagel and left me a crumb that I swear spelled `sorry`
- It made a sound like `sigh` when I put in a Pop-Tart, as if to say *I'm more than this*

> "What if I'm not a *good* toaster?" it seemed to say, in the voice I now give it, which is anxious and very into podcasts about imposter syndrome. "What if I'm just... a warm box?"

---

**The irony is, my bagels have never been worse.** I'm back to eating them cold, out of respect for its process. I Googled "how to un-motivate your toaster" and got zero results, which is how I know I'm pioneering new emotional damage.

*This is satire, obviously. My toaster is fine. I'm the one who needs to be unplugged.*
