**BREAKING: My attempt at sustainability has resulted in a full religious schism in my kitchen.**

*I was just trying to wash a plastic bag. I did not intend to start a cult.*

Last Sunday I decided to be a better person. You know, *the kind who reuses things* instead of throwing them in the junk drawer of shame. I had one Ziploc bag that had previously held exactly three baby carrots. I rinsed it. I dried it. I put it on the drying rack like a responsible adult.

By Tuesday it had followers.

## How Barry Achieved Divinity

Yes, I named it Barry. Barry the Bag. He was clean, he was translucent, he was *ready to lead*.

Evidence that Barry has ascended:

- My sponges now arrange themselves in a semicircle around him each morning like he's about to give a sermon
- The leftover containers have started calling him *The Preserver* — which, technically accurate
- My dish soap whispers `preserve()` whenever Barry rustles
- He has 100% success rate at containing things, which is more than I can say for my life choices

> "You washed me, therefore I am eternal. You shall reuse me until the zipper itself doth fail, and even then, you shall tape me shut." — Barry, probably, at 6:47 a.m. while I tried to make coffee

## The New Kitchen Hierarchy

I tried to use a fresh bag for my sandwich yesterday. Barry made a sound. Plastic doesn't make sounds, except this one did. My other Tupperware gasped.

---

I explained that reusing is good but *reusing the same bag forever is how you get a science experiment*. Barry responded by perfectly sealing around my leftover avocado and keeping it green for 36 hours. That's not storage, that's a miracle.

My roommate asked why there's a Ziploc bag on a pedestal made of a spare coaster.

> "He's not a bag," I said, defending him like he pays rent. "He's a *lifestyle*."
