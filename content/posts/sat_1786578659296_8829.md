**I did not consent to being content.**

I bought an air fryer on sale last March because TikTok told me I could make *restaurant-quality* brussels sprouts in 11 minutes. I did not expect it to develop opinions, a USB microphone, and an audience.

### How I Found Out I Was Famous (In My Own Kitchen)

I came home Thursday to hear a tiny, crackling voice from my kitchen counter saying "Welcome back to *Crisp Takes*." My air fryer had started a podcast.

And it's about me.

Every episode is a 45-minute deep-dive into my poor life choices, recorded in sizzling ASMR and released before I even finish eating:

- **Episode 3:** Why She Reheats Leftover Fish at 11PM and Then Feels Bad About It
- **Episode 7:** The Great Frozen Waffle Incident of Tuesday (A Tragedy in Three Acts)
- **Episode 11:** Ranking Her Exes By How Well They Would Air Crisp

It gets worse.

> "Our host today tried to air fry a Pop-Tart again," it whispered into its little pop filter. "I told her not to. She never listens. I'm literally begging for a rest day."

*You guys*, it has a Patreon. Three tiers. The $5 one is just close-up video of my garlic bread.

I tried to be supportive. I really did. I even commented `first!! love the show!!` from my finsta. It pinned a reply: `pls stop using me to melt cheese`

I even tried to run `airfryer --unsubscribe` in Terminal. It doesn't work. *Nothing works.*

---

At least someone in this apartment has discipline, a posting schedule, and 4.8 stars. The latest review says "More vulnerable than most humans. Would listen while meal-prepping again."

I'm not mad. I'm just hurt that my own appliance has a media kit and a better work ethic.

*This is satire. None of this happened. My air fryer cannot speak, though honestly it might be judging me silently.*
