**I have officially been put on a Performance Improvement Plan by my own houseplants.**

*Which is humbling, because I thought buying them was the responsible adult move.*

## The Incident That Started It All

Last Tuesday, I killed a cactus. Not on purpose — I just loved it too aggressively. I watered it daily, whispered affirmations, played it lo-fi beats. It died of what the internet called "over-attentiveness," which is also how my ex described our relationship.

So I did what any stable, well-adjusted adult would do: I made a 12-slide PowerPoint apology deck titled "Root Cause Analysis & Forward Path."

Slide 1 was just the words **I'M SORRY** in 72pt Comic Sans because I panicked.

## Their Demands Were... Extensive

I presented it in the living room. My monstera — Chad — did not clap. He just rustled ominously. My three remaining plants then submitted collective feedback:

- No more tap water, only *artisanal rainwater* collected under a full moon
- A strict no-moving-them-for-Instagram-aesthetics policy
- Daily sunlight hours to be logged and submitted via Google Form
- Immediate removal of the fake plastic succulent I bought as a "backup"

> "We appreciate your vulnerability in Slide 7, but Slide 9 lacked actionable takeaways. Also, we saw you Google 'can plants hear you crying.' We can." — Chad the Monstera, Lead Foliage & Morale Officer

---

I tried to implement their suggestions. I even wrote a little `apology_v2_final_FINAL.js` script that plays apology sounds on a loop.

`console.log("please photosynthesize, I have so much to prove");`

It did not work. Now they have a shared calendar and they've blocked off my windowsill as a "collaborative workspace." I have to ask permission to open the blinds.

*Satire/parody: 100% fictional. No plants were emotionally damaged in the writing of this post, though Chad is still not returning my texts.*
