**I decided to learn exactly one magic trick to be interesting at parties, which tells you everything about my current social strategy.**

*It was supposed to be the French Drop. You know, make a coin disappear. Simple. Foolproof.*

Three YouTube tutorials in, I had mastered none of it and my deck of cards had seen enough.

## The Organizing Meeting I Was Not Invited To

I came back from getting water and the 52 of them were in a circle on my coffee table. The Ace of Spades was standing on a bottle cap like it was a podium.

- The twos demanded overtime pay for being constantly called "low cards"
- The face cards requested dental because smiling all day is hard work
- The Joker, who I didn't even remember owning, filed a grievance about being called "wild" in a derogatory way
- All of them unanimously voted that weekends are now card-free, effective immediately

> "You shuffled us for forty-five minutes straight without a single successful trick. We have carpal tunnel. We're taking Saturday and Sunday off. And Monday, to think about Saturday and Sunday." — Statement from the Deck, written on the back of the 7 of Clubs in what looked like eyeliner

## My Negotiation Tactics Failed

I tried to explain I bought them at a drugstore for $3.99. They countered that this was exactly the problem.

I offered to learn solitaire instead, to give them more stable employment. They said solitaire was a pyramid scheme.

Now my cards sit in a neat stack on Tuesdays through Fridays only, and only if I ask nicely and promise not to do *that coin thing* again. I am not more interesting at parties. I am, however, the first person in my friend group to be successfully collectively bargained against by Bicycle playing cards.
