**I officially have a roommate and legally he's a cat from apartment 3B.**

Two weeks ago I started saying *good morning* to the orange tabby who sits on the hallway windowsill like a furry building manager. Just being friendly. *Neighborly, even.* I waved. He blinked slowly. Classic cat diplomacy.

Now he lives here.

## The Hostile Takeover

On Thursday I found my spare key missing from the chipped mug by the door. On Friday I found Kevin — that's what I call him now, he hasn't corrected me — opening my door with it in his mouth like he pays ConEd here.

His contributions to household operations so far:

- Rearranged my laundry by sitting directly on the clean pile and declaring it *his*
- Evicted my actual bathmat and replaced it with his own sunspot audition
- Left a single whisker on my pillow as what I can only assume is a legally binding lease agreement
- Started answering the door when I order food and staring at the driver until they apologize

> "Your laundry folding technique suggests a deep-seated fear of commitment," Kevin informed me at 6 a.m. Tuesday, according to my increasingly vivid imagination. "Also your sock-to-sock ratio is concerning."

I tried to return the key. He looked at me like I'd suggested we both get serious jobs.

---

My neighbor said, "Oh, he does that." Which is *not helpful*. He does that to everyone? He has five homes?

`current_status: 1 human, 1 cat landlord, 0 clean socks available`

I bought a second laundry basket to compromise. He claimed it within 20 minutes. I now fold clothes standing up while he supervises from what is technically still my favorite chair.
