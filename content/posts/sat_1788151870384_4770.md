**I have never been more popular, and my entire social circle is currently being held together by a guy named Prince BlockChain the Third.**

After years of carefully curating my real inbox — bills, dentist reminders, my landlord asking if I've *seen* the hallway plant — I realized my spam folder was having way more fun than me. So last Tuesday, I decided to let it manage my social life.

## Phase One: Saying Yes to Everything

My new personal assistant (Gmail's Spam Filter) had *opinions*. Very urgent opinions.

- Accept a $4.7 million inheritance from a distant aunt I never had
- Attend a webinar called `MAKE YOUR KNEES RICH QUICK`
- Help a crypto prince move his fortune by marrying him immediately for love and tax purposes
- Forward this chain letter to 12 people or risk mediocre Wi-Fi forever

I said yes to all of them. *Obviously.*

For the first time in months, my calendar was full. My phone was buzzing. Someone finally wanted me for my *banking details*.

> "My Dearest Beloved," Prince wrote at 2:14 a.m., "You are the light of my cold wallet. Together we shall build an empire on the blockchain and also maybe a gazebo."

I swooned. No one had ever offered me a gazebo before.

---

### The Engagement

Yesterday, he proposed. Over email. With a clip-art ring that was somehow also a QR code. My mom is thrilled. My actual friends are confused because I keep referring to my fiancé as `PRINCE_BTC_FINAL_FINAL2`.

*In retrospect, letting an algorithm that thinks my electric bill is a love letter run my life may not have been my most grounded decision.*

But honestly? I've never felt more seen. My spam folder believes in me. It thinks I'm pre-approved, pre-selected, and pre-destined for greatness.

We're registered at Best Buy and the blockchain. The wedding will be intimate, probably in my inbox, with 47 of my closest unread messages in attendance.

*This is satire. I am not actually engaged. My spam folder is not a licensed matchmaker.*
