**In retrospect, giving my closet full democratic autonomy was a catastrophic leadership decision.**

I was just trying to be more *mindful*. Marie Kondo adjacent. I took everything out, thanked it profusely for its service, and said, "You guys figure it out, you know yourselves best." I went to get coffee. When I came back, the closet had restructured.

### The New Hierarchy

Apparently over the last two years my wardrobe has been quietly taking notes on my behavior:

- The gray hoodie I wear 6 days a week is now CEO and has performance reviews scheduled
- The jeans with the inspirational knee rip have been promoted to Head of Operations  
- My one blazer is in exile on the far left and has started journaling about abandonment

And the hoodies — *oh, the hoodies* — have formed a circle.

> "We've noticed you only wear us when you have to go outside and pretend to be a functional adult," said the navy one, who is clearly the therapist friend. "We're concerned about your `commitment to going outside`."

### Tuesday Night Check-In

Every Tuesday at 9 p.m. now, they hold a meeting. No humans allowed, but the closet door is thin and I can hear everything.

Last week's agenda was titled **"Are We Enabling Him?"** and included a PowerPoint slide that was just a photo of me eating cereal over the sink at 11:44 p.m. with the caption *this is not a personality*.

---

We negotiated. I agreed to:

- Actually hang things up instead of using the chair, which they now call `the purgatory chair`
- Stop buying the *same* black hoodie in slightly different shades of black and acting surprised

They agreed to stop guilt-tripping me when I wear the same one three days in a row. Progress is slow. The blazer still hasn't responded to my apology email.
