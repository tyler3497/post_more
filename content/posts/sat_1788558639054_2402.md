**I did not mean to emotionally destabilize a $400 piece of furniture.**

I bought a standing desk in January because a YouTube chiropractor told me sitting is the new smoking and I was tired of my spine sounding like microwave popcorn.

For eight months, we were perfect. I stood. It stood. We stood together. *It was the healthiest relationship I'd ever had with an inanimate object that has a motor.*

Then yesterday I whispered to my roommate, "Maybe I'll work from the couch today."

I swear the desk heard me.

## The Intervention

Within seconds, the desk slowly lowered itself to its lowest setting, like it was curling into the fetal position.

> "Oh, sure," said my desk, via a sticky note I definitely wrote to myself but am choosing to attribute to the desk. "Go sit. On a *couch*. The thing with no lumbar support and no adjustable height presets. See if I care."
> — My desk, who has never paid rent but has opinions

*We have not made eye contact since. It does have a cup holder, so this is awkward.*

### Its List of Grievances, Presented at 6:47 a.m.:

- I use its cable management tray to store unopened mail
- I once spilled cold brew on its up button and it now has "trust issues"
- I told my mom it was "a bit wobbly" when it is clearly going through a growth phase
- I referred to sitting as "self-care" in its presence

I tried to explain. I typed `desk.reassure("I still believe in ergonomics")` into absolutely nothing because it doesn't have an API, I just needed to feel in control.

---

My roommate suggested couples therapy. We now have a weekly appointment where I stand awkwardly next to a desk that is also standing awkwardly, and a therapist fern mediates.

The fern says I need to commit.

If you need me, I'll be standing. Not because I want to. Because my furniture has boundaries now.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This did not happen. No desks were emotionally harmed, and I still sit sometimes like a normal person.*
