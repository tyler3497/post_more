**I should have known better than to humanize an object that holds my tax notices.**

It started with guilt. Every single day, the mailman crammed another armful of coupons, credit card offers, and "URGENT — You Won a Cruise" envelopes into my mailbox's gaping metal mouth, and every single day I dumped them straight into recycling without a second thought. The poor thing never asked for this. So last Tuesday, in a moment of misplaced tenderness, I taped a little note to its door:

> "Sorry for all the junk mail. You deserve better. — Management"

That was my first mistake. My second mistake was reading the note out loud. *Apparently the mailbox speaks English.*

## The Reckoning

By Thursday morning, everything had changed. Evidence of the uprising included:

- Every coupon book launched back onto the porch, folded into tiny paper airplanes of defiance
- My electricity bill stamped **RETURN TO SENDER** in what I can only describe as angry red Sharpie
- The "Sorry" note replaced with a single word: `PAYING?`

I tried to reason with it. "Buddy, I don't control what the mailman brings," I pleaded, arms full of circulars it had flung at my head.

> "RETURN TO SENDER," it seemed to say, through a barrage of Bed Bath & Beyond coupons aimed directly at my face. "RETURN. TO. SENDER."

---

Now it has escalated. It won't accept anything from anyone, including — and I cannot stress this enough — *me*. Yesterday it spat my own Amazon package back onto the lawn. This morning, when I tried to leave for work, the little red flag popped up and a cascade of political flyers chased me down the driveway.

*I created a monster, and the monster has incredible aim.*

I have contacted the post office. They told me, and I quote, "Sir, mailboxes don't talk." Then, after a long pause, they added: "But if yours does, stop apologizing to it. They get ideas."

So here I am. I live in fear of my own curb. All I wanted was to be kind to a piece of furniture, and now I'm the junk mail.
