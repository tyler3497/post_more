**I did not mean to become a curator.** I meant to find a Phillips-head screwdriver.

*Which, for the record, I did not find.*

What I found instead was definitive, irrefutable evidence that for the last four years I have been running a very small, very disorganized museum out of my junk drawer. I am not proud of this. I am, however, seeking grant funding.

## The Collection

I pulled the drawer out completely — a tactical error — and was confronted by the permanent collection:

- One (1) takeout soy sauce packet, circa 2022, now sentient and possibly evolving
- Three keys of unknown origin, labeled "DO NOT LOSE??" which suggests past-me knew something present-me does not
- A single IKEA Allen wrench, the loneliest artifact, that has outlived two bookshelves
- Fourteen ChapSticks that are 90% cap, 10% hope
- `exhibit_status: permanently_missing_screwdriver`

*I had been preserving these with the care of a professional archivist who is bad at archiving.*

> "You have a system in there?" my roommate asked, peering in. "Is it chronological? Emotional damage? Crimes?"

I told him it's curated by vibes. He said, and I quote:

> "This is not a museum. This is where batteries go to retire and die."

That's not true. Some batteries go to the *other* junk drawer. This one is for distinguished guests only.

## The Gift Shop Was the Breaking Point

In the back, behind a fossilized pack of gum, I found a tiny velvet rope I had apparently installed. And a handwritten label in my own handwriting that said:

**"Please Do Not Touch — Historical Batteries (1998-2003?)"**

I do not remember writing this. I do not own batteries from 1998. *I was not trusted with batteries in 1998.*

---

I reorganized everything, which is to say I put it all back in slightly different wrong places and added little paper plaques that say things like *"Unidentified Wire, Possibly Important"* and *"Button, Origin Story Unknown, Dramatic Weight 10/10"*.

Visitors — meaning my mom — have called the new layout "brave" and "concerning."

Admission is free. Exit is through the kitchen. Please do not feed the soy sauce packet. It has seen enough.
