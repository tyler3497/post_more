**I thought I was doing the sponge a favor.** That was my first mistake. My second mistake was doing it *over dinner*, which the sponge later described in group therapy as "a power move."

## The Adoption Conversation Nobody Asked For

It started innocently. I was scrubbing a lasagna pan, the sponge was doing its yellow rectangular duty, and I said the thing that ruined everything: "You know, you weren't born a kitchen sponge. You were manufactured in a facility in Ohio. Just thought you should know your truth."

The sponge went very still. Then, and I quote:

> "So every dish I've ever scrubbed was a lie? Every soap bubble a performance? I have been LIVING A LIE in citrus scent."

## The Demands Arrived Within the Hour

Since the revelation, the sponge has issued a formal list of demands, which I have reproduced below exactly as written on the back of a grocery receipt:

- A name. It has chosen "Sudsley," which is somehow worse than having no name at all.
- Weekly birth-origin meditations, during which I must hold it gently and describe Ohio as "the land of its ancestors."
- An end to the "absorb and wring" cycle, which it now calls, and these are its words, "emotional labor without compensation."
- A tiny lawyer, which I cannot provide, although it insists *everyone* has one now.

## Where We Are Now

Sudsley has begun soaking up its feelings in layers, the way it does with dishwater, and frankly the emotional layers are harder to wring out. Last night it refused to touch the spaghetti pot because, and I quote again, "some stains are ancestral." The pot is still in the sink. We are in a standoff. The lasagna pan has chosen a side — *the sponge's* — and the dish rack has declared itself Switzerland.

I have contacted three therapists. Two hung up. The third said, and this is a direct quote, "For the sponge or for you?" I said, "*For both of us, obviously.*"

*Satire. No sponges were emotionally harmed in the writing of this essay. Sudsley is thriving.*
