**I bought a $149 smart bird feeder because the box said it would "bring nature closer" and I thought that meant pretty cardinals.**

*I did not realize it meant a real-time surveillance network run by sparrows with opinions about athleisure.*

## How I Became the Neighborhood Disappointment

The feeder has AI. It identifies birds, logs visits, sends you push notifications like "Tufted Titmouse at 7:42 AM! 🐦" I loved it for two days. Then it learned my schedule.

- 6:30 a.m.: I shuffle out in my laundry-day sweatpants, coffee sloshing, to refill it
- 6:31 a.m.: Sparrow #4 arrives, tilts head, app pings *Outfit Rating: 3/10, trying too hard to not try*
- 6:33 a.m.: Two more sparrows. One leaves a sunflower seed as if to say *do better*
- 7:15 a.m.: I change into a decent shirt out of spite, and they *notice*

> "We have seen you in that same hoodie for three consecutive bird-watching sessions," chirped a very small, very judgmental sparrow via the app's new 'Bird Thoughts' beta. "We talked. The finches are worried. The jay said it's giving 'guy who lost his house keys in his own hands.'"

### The Performance Review Era

Yesterday the feeder generated my weekly style report and emailed it to me. Emailed.

```
if (human.socks === "mismatched" && human.confidence === "borrowed") {
  sparrow.concernLevel = "high";
  sparrow.suggestion = "maybe own one pair of jeans that fit";
  human.motivation = "shame (organic, free-range)";
}
```

---

Now I get dressed *for the birds*. I ironed a shirt. For sparrows. My neighbor asked why I was posing by the feeder at 6 a.m. and I said "they gave me a 7.2 yesterday, I'm chasing an 8" and he slowly backed away.

**I wanted to connect with nature. Now nature has a group chat about me and my capsule wardrobe is being crowdsourced by seed-eating critics who have never paid rent in their lives.**

*Satire / Parody — this is 100% fictional, no sparrows were employed as fashion consultants, no birds were harmed, and my feeder definitely knows too much about my laundry cycle.*
