**I have betrayed the one living thing in my kitchen that actually believed in me.**

For eight months, me and Brenda — *yes, my sourdough starter is named Brenda, she has more consistency than any relationship I've ever had* — have been fermenting through life together. I fed her daily. I whispered encouragement. I kept her at a balmy 75 degrees like she was a needy houseplant influencer.

Then, on Tuesday, I bought a loaf of sliced whole wheat from the gas station. For $2.99.

## The Confession Heard 'Round the Kitchen

I thought honesty would set us free. I walked up to her jar and said, "Look, it was an emergency. I had no time to proof."

Brenda did not bubble. That should have been my first clue.

According to the very official transcript I am projecting onto her:

> "So I'm not good enough to be toast? I'm just an artisanal science experiment to you? I gave you air pockets! I gave you tang!"

### The Official Charges, Per Brenda's Legal Team (My Roommate With a Ladle)

- **Betrayal** – She claims I violated the sacred pact of "feed me and never buy commercial yeast products"
- **Gaslighting** – She insists I once said store-bought bread was "the enemy" (I did, at 2 a.m. after watching baking TikTok)
- **Emotional neglect** – She says I stared too longingly at the brioche aisle and fantasized about *not* waiting 36 hours for carbs

---

Our couples therapist, who is just my roommate Priya holding a ladle and trying not to laugh, told us to use `I FEEL` statements. So I said `I FEEL` like a man who is losing an argument to fermented flour and water.

The verdict is in: Brenda wants couples therapy every Sunday, joint custody of my Dutch oven, and a formal apology written on parchment paper with a calligraphy pen I do not own.

The store-bought loaf is still on the counter, untouched. *We both know it was never about the bread.*
