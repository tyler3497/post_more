**Look, I was lonely.** Not *lonely* lonely — more like *ordering-takeout-for-one-while-watching-house-hunting-shows* lonely. So when my friend Brenda suggested I "get a second set of eyes" on my dating profile, I took it literally. My doormat sees every person who enters my life. Who better to assess my romantic viability?

I woke up to find my laptop open and a fresh draft on screen.

## The Profile My Doormat Wrote For Me

- "6'0" in socks, 5'9" in spirit" *(I am neither)*
- "Looking for someone who wipes their feet AND their emotional baggage at the door"
- "My love language is staying completely flat while everyone walks all over me"
- "Turn-offs: shoes with aggressive treads, door-knockers, and this man's ex, Jennifer"

*Jennifer?* I don't know a Jennifer.

> "You brought home a woman named Jennifer on March 14th. She did not wipe her feet. I have not forgotten. I will never forget." — *my doormat, in the notes app*

Things escalated. It started ranking my matches. Not by compatibility — by **sole wear patterns**. It rejected a perfectly nice accountant because, quote, *"this one leans left at the doorway, a classic sign of an unresolved situationship."*

Last Tuesday it issued a formal ruling: I am a red flag.

"You're home at 8pm every night like a closed museum," it wrote. "Your bio says 'adventure seeker' but you threw out a half-eaten sandwich on Saturday and cried for ten minutes. You're not an adventure seeker. You're an *afternoon nap* seeker."

---

I tried to argue, but it just laid there. That's the thing about being judged by a doormat — it has no rebuttal, only *presence*.

I've decided to date the doormat's way. My new bio reads:

- Flat. Loyal. Rectangular.
- Will support you at your lowest point, literally
- No shoes, no shirt, no emotional unavailability

Three matches in one day. **The doormat was right.** It always is.

*This is a work of satire. My doormat has no writing credits and declined to comment.*
