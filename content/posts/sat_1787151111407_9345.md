**I thought automatic bill pay would finally make me a responsible adult.** Instead it turned my checking account into a passive-aggressive spouse who sleeps on the couch.

It all started because I was tired of manually paying bills like some kind of 19th-century farmer. *You know, truly pioneer stuff.* So I clicked the big friendly button that said "Automate Everything!"

My bank said:

> "Congrats! You're free!"

My checking account said nothing. Which, in hindsight, was louder.

## The Day I Outsourced Our Entire Relationship

In 12 minutes I automated:

- Electric — because apparently my toaster needs a 24/7 power grid
- Internet — so my houseplants can continue watching me on the security cam
- Rent — the biggest emotional commitment I've ever auto-scheduled
- That gym I haven't visited since March but spiritually support

I felt powerful. Efficient. Like a CEO of my own $47.

Then my checking account started acting weird.

> "Oh, so you don't even *look* at me before you spend me anymore?" it whispered at 2 a.m. when I tried to buy tacos. "You just... let a *script* handle us? I thought what we had was manual."

## Couples Counseling, But Make It Financial

I tried to explain it was for efficiency. I tried `autopay_enabled = true // still love you though` logic.

It wasn't buying it. Literally.

It started leaving me little signs:

- Changed its profile picture to a sad coin
- Started forwarding me articles like "7 Signs Your Human Has Checked Out"
- Refused to show me my balance without saying "figure it out yourself, you're so *independent* now"

---

Last night I tried logging in manually, just to show I still care. I clicked around slowly, dramatically, like I was in a rom-com reconciliation scene.

It showed me my balance for exactly 4 seconds before auto-refreshing and going:

> "Don't worry. Your bills already handled it. We don't need you here anymore."

We're trying a new thing now where I Venmo myself $5 manually every Friday just to keep the spark alive.
