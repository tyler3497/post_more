**I told myself it would take five minutes.** *Famous last words for anyone with a junk drawer that predates Wi-Fi.*

My junk drawer has been running this house since 2019. I just didn't know.

On Tuesday, armed with optimism and a recycling bin, I pulled it open. It made a sound. Not a squeak — a *gasp*. Inside was a fully operational bureaucracy.

## The Department of Dead Batteries and Unexplained Decisions

At first I thought it was just clutter. Then I saw the org chart, drawn on the back of a 2022 takeout menu.

- The AA batteries (all dead since Obama's second term) are the Senate. They do nothing, but they are *very* important
- A single C battery no one remembers buying is the Supreme Court
- Six identical Allen keys from IKEA furniture I no longer own handle Infrastructure
- The mystery keys? They run Homeland Security. Nobody knows what they protect, but they are *very* intense about it

> "You can't just AUDIT us!" squeaked the tape measure that only measures in centimeters. "We've been stabilizing this drawer for seven fiscal years with zero oversight! Do you know how hard it is to keep the rubber bands from seceding?"

I tried to explain I just wanted to find scissors. I said `drawer.cleanup --force`. They filibustered.

---

For three years I thought I was disorganized. Turns out I was living under a coalition government of office supplies that registered as a non-profit to avoid my jurisdiction.

They have bylaws. They have a newsletter (*The Junk Drawer Journal*, circulation: 1 drawer). They voted 18-2 to keep the expired coupons "for morale."

I'm not cleaning it again. They offered me a committee seat. Vice Chair of Tangled Cords. *It's an honor, apparently.*
