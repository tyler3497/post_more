**I spent four hours organizing my junk drawer on Tuesday, which was my first mistake.**

*My second mistake was assuming the drawer would be grateful.*

## The Before Times

For six years, that drawer was a democracy. Batteries, expired coupons, seventeen Allen wrenches, a single soy sauce packet from 2021 — all living together in beautiful chaos. You needed something? You dug. It was *character building*.

Then I watched one YouTube video titled "Organize Your Life in 15 Minutes!!!" and became a person with drawer dividers and a label maker.

## The Glow-Up That Went Too Far

I dumped everything out, bought little bamboo trays, sorted by category. Now it looks like a tiny hardware store run by Marie Kondo's overachieving cousin.

And now? The drawer has developed *opinions*:

- It rejected my spare keys because they "didn't sparkle correctly"
- My random twist-ties from bread bags? Denied entry. "We don't do loose energy anymore," it seemed to say
- I tried to toss in a dead battery "just for now" and the dividers physically tensed
- There's a small section labeled `misc` that is now empty out of sheer principle

> "Oh, so you want to put a *receipt* in here?" I swear I heard it whisper when I opened it this morning. "After I became *this*? After the trays? You think you can just come in here with your uncategorized string? Absolutely not. Take that to the *counter* like everyone else."

### Living With An Elite Drawer

Now I have a junk drawer that isn't junk. I have a *curated small-objects display case* that judges me. I stand in my kitchen holding a single screw like, "Please, he's with me," and the drawer is like, "Does he have a *home*?"

*The irony is I still can't find the scissors.*
