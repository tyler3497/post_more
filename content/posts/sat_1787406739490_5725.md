**I finally unlocked meal prep.** 

*It took 28 years, one motivational podcast, and a complete loss of perspective in the Target kitchen aisle.*

I bought twelve identical glass containers because the internet said routine is self-care. Sunday afternoon I made quinoa, roasted vegetables, and grilled chicken with the focus of someone defusing a bomb. I labeled everything. I stacked them in the fridge like a tiny, judgmental apartment complex.

## The Meeting I Was Not Invited To

Monday morning, I opened the fridge and heard... whispering.

My containers had arranged themselves into a circle.

- Container #4 (quinoa, Monday lunch) was moderating
- Container #7 (overnight oats) was taking minutes
- Container #11 (mystery sauce) had clearly been crying

> "We feel *seen* as storage, but not heard as individuals," said Container #4. "Also, Tyler keeps opening the door just to stare at us and sigh. That's not meal prep, that's surveillance."

I tried to explain I was just trying to eat healthier. They were not impressed.

### The List of Demands

They slid a Post-it across the shelf:

- Rotate us fairly, the top shelf gets better light
- Stop calling us 'Tupperware,' we are *glass meal prep vessels with bamboo lids*
- Free the hummus, it didn't ask for this
- We require one (1) fridge magnet that says `livingMyBestLeftoverLife = true`

---

I gave in. What else do you do when your lunch stages a labor action? Now I pack my lunch with *consent*. I knock before I open the fridge.

They let me take Tuesday's chicken today, but only after a group affirmation.
