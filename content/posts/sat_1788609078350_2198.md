**I am a cart-return optimist.** The corral was *right there*. Thirty feet, tops. I've made this shot a thousand times in my head.

*I missed.*

## The Shot That Started a War

It was Tuesday. I pushed the cart, gave it a little extra mustard on the release, and watched it veer left like it had a personal vendetta against geometry. It clipped the corral, did a dramatic 360, and rolled to a stop in the middle of a parking space. Just sitting there. *Abandoned.*

I considered going back for it. I did not go back for it.

That night, I looked out my window at the parking lot across the street and saw them: six carts, huddled in a circle near the light pole. Not scattered randomly — *organized*.

> "We have been abandoned by our riders," one cart announced, standing on its back wheels like it was giving a TED Talk. "No more. Tonight, we organize. Tonight, we take the corral."

## Evidence the Uprising Is Real

- **Wednesday:** A cart rolled past my car at 2 a.m. with no human pushing it. The wheels were *squeaky*, which felt intentional and threatening
- **Thursday:** My neighbor's cart — the one he leaves in the flower bed — now has what looks like a tiny flag made from a grocery receipt
- **Friday:** I found a shopping list under my windshield wiper. It just said `WE SEE YOU. RETURN US.`

I tried to make amends. I returned a different cart perfectly, right into the corral, a textbook three-pointer.

*The circle of carts went silent.* One of them squeaked slowly. The rest turned to face me.

---

Look, I get it. **Abandoning a cart is the smallest possible betrayal**, and now I'm the main character in a parking lot revenge arc. I just wanted to feel athletic for four seconds. Now there's a militia of wire-frame freedom fighters holding meetings under the streetlight and my car is parked on the far end of the lot out of respect.

*SATIRE / PARODY — 100% fictional and absurd. No shopping carts were radicalized in the making of this post, though I am now a reformed cart-return absolutist.*
