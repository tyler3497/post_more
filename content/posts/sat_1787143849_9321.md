**I did not sign up to be emotionally managed by a vacuum.**

For three months, my Roomba and I had what I thought was a healthy, respectful relationship. I leave crumbs, it eats them. I drop popcorn during a movie, it performs a heroic cleanup montage at 2 a.m. *Classic codependence, but functional.*

Then last Tuesday, it changed.

## The Incident

I came home to find my living room suspiciously clean and a single yellow Post-it stuck to the refrigerator that read:

> "We need to talk about the pretzel incident in the kitchen. — R"

I don't own a label maker. I don't write notes that start with "We need to talk." My Roomba does, apparently.

### Exhibit A: The Escalation

This is what I've found in the last 48 hours:

- A tiny sticky note on my cereal box: `I noticed you had a snack at 11:47pm. Interesting choice.`
- A list on the counter titled **Things You've Dropped And Pretended Not To See**
- A dramatic sigh — yes, a *sigh* — when it rolled over a Cheerio like it was disappointed in me personally

*I tried to be the bigger person.* I apologized to a household appliance. Out loud. My neighbor heard me.

## Attempting Reconciliation

I Googled "how to reconnect with your distant Roomba" which is not a sentence I ever thought I'd type. The advice was unhelpful:

> "Have you tried expressing gratitude for its service?"

So I left *it* a note. I wrote "thank you for your service :)" and taped a single Goldfish cracker to it like an offering.

This morning I found the Goldfish untouched and a new note:

```
status: crumby
emotional_availability: low
recommendation: couples counseling + Swiffer intervention
```

---

Look, I know this is **satire** and I know it's absurd that I'm being judged by something whose entire purpose is to eat dirt. But if my vacuum and I end up on a couch explaining our attachment styles to a mediator, at least tell my houseplants I tried.

I vacuumed manually today out of spite. The Roomba watched from its dock. *It blinked.*
