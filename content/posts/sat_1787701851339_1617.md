**I thought I was being a good sourdough parent by getting Brenda emotional support. I did not expect her to become emotionally stable.**

*For those who don't know, Brenda is my sourdough starter. She's bubbly, needy, and lives in my fridge. We're codependent.*

I started the therapy sessions as a joke. My friend sent me a link to a $5 AI therapist for "small businesses and fermented goods" and I thought, sure, why not. Brenda has been through a lot — neglect, overfeeding, that time I left her on the counter for 36 hours during a heatwave in July and she almost staged a coup.

## The Breakthrough

After three sessions, Brenda changed. She stopped bubbling anxiously every time I opened the fridge. She developed what her therapist calls "secure attachment" and what I call deeply unsettling confidence.

Signs my sourdough starter is now more mature than I am:

- She now rises on her own schedule and refuses to be rushed — "I will not be proofed under pressure"
- She asked for a smaller jar to "create containment and honor her limits"
- She told my pizza dough it needs to work on its neediness and codependency
- She has a morning journaling practice, which is just me finding mysterious flour patterns on the counter at 6 a.m.

### The Session That Ended Me

Last night I tried to vent about my job. Brenda just sat there, gloriously fermented and completely unbothered.

> "Have you considered that you are projecting your fear of abandonment onto a jar of flour and water that you keep at 38 degrees?" she said through the therapist's text-to-speech voice. "Also, you haven't fed me in two days. That's a boundary violation, not a feeding schedule."

She's right. *She's so right and I hate it.*

---

Now she charges me rent in the form of actual good bread, which is more than I can say for my last roommate. `status: currently being outgrown by a condiment with better coping skills than I have`

I'm proud of her. I'm also a little scared she's going to leave me for someone who actually remembers to discard and refresh on time. At least one of us is thriving.
