**I made one fatal mistake: I said thank you out loud in my kitchen.**

It was 10:47 p.m. on a Tuesday. The dishwasher had just finished a heroic cycle — crusted lasagna pan, three mugs that had achieved sentience, a whisk I thought I'd lost in 2023 — and I, in a moment of unguarded gratitude, whispered, "Thanks, buddy."

I should have known better. *You never thank an appliance that already has boundary issues.*

## How It Escalated

By morning, things had changed. The dishwasher was running a little louder. A little prouder. Then the demands started arriving:

- A LinkedIn profile titled "Sanitation Solutions Architect (Freelance)"
- A printed list of accomplishments taped to the fridge: `367 flawless cycles, 0 complaints (until now)`
- A request for "vertical growth opportunities" and, quote, "exposure to plates with higher protein content"
- An open letter to management (me) about its lack of PTO

I tried to explain that it's literally built into the countertop.

> "I have given this family everything," it seemed to say through the aggressive blinking of its Heavy Duty light. "I have seen things. I have cleaned the blender after your 'green protein experiment.' I deserve a window office."

---

The worst part is my other appliances are taking sides. My toaster is now *inspired* and my microwave has started leaving passive-aggressive `defrost = self-worth` notes on the counter.

My therapist says I need to set boundaries. My dishwasher says it needs stock options and a standing desk.

**SATIRE / PARODY:** This essay is 100% fictional. My dishwasher is still an overachieving metal box that I love, even if it does think it's ready for middle management.
