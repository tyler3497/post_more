**I made the mistake of giving my houseplants a vote on the thermostat, because I believe in democracy.**

*This is what happens when you read one article about participatory households and own seven emotionally fragile houseplants.*

It started reasonably enough. My apartment was 74 degrees, my monstera looked smug, and my fern — let's call her Senator Fern — was drooping like she'd just seen my electric bill.

I thought, let's be inclusive. I wrote little ballots.

## The Platform Breakdown

Every plant had a clear agenda:

- **Monstera (Barry):** 72 degrees, humid, vaguely tropical, wants a humidifier and my firstborn
- **Succulent Collective:** "We survived the desert, we can survive your cheap AC, leave us alone"
- **Pothos:** Abstained, too busy trailing into my cereal bowl
- **Fern:** 68 degrees, misted every 15 minutes, full campaign finance reform

> "We hold these truths to be self-evident that all plants are created equal, but ferns are a little more equal because we pay more in compost taxes," declared Fern, via a sticky note I definitely wrote myself at 11 p.m.

The vote was 4-3 for cooler. I lowered it to 68. My succulents staged a walkout.

---

Fern got a taste of power. She printed — *allegedly, I have no printer* — little leaflets that said `Fern 2026: Make My Apartment Damp Again`. She started holding town halls on the windowsill.

**Now she wants a cabinet position.** Secretary of Misting. She wants veto power over my space heater.

I tried to explain checks and balances. She rustled ominously.

I live in a humid jungle run by a fern with approval ratings higher than mine. My electric bill is $212 and my cactus is threatening to primary me.
