**I finally did it.** *After five years of aggressively pretending I was above aesthetics*, I reorganized my bookshelf by color.

Not because I wanted to. My roommate said our living room looked like "a library had a panic attack" and, honestly, she wasn't wrong.

## The Color-Coded Catastrophe

I thought it would be peaceful. Rainbow order. Instagram-worthy. What I got was straight-up literary warfare.

Within two hours, factions formed:

- The moody navy blues (all my unread Russian novels) started gatekeeping the top shelf like they owned it
- The aggressive reds (cookbooks I never cook from) declared war on the yellows for being "too cheerful"
- My single green plant book was trying to mediate and failing spectacularly
- The beige self-help section kept chanting about "manifesting positive shelf energy"

My favorite part? My thrillers now refuse to be next to the self-help.

> "Oh, sure, Jan," whispered *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*, which is beige and insufferable. "Tell me again how you're going to 'find yourself' while literally hiding behind Stephen King for the last six months."

> "At least I have plot twists," Stephen King shot back from his red corner. Which, rude, but accurate.

### Exhibit B: They Unionized

Last night at 2:37 a.m., I heard rustling. My copy of `Atomic Habits` was trying to negotiate better lighting conditions.

```
if (shelf == "aesthetic") {
```

---

I tried to restore order. I moved one yellow cookbook out of sequence and my entire shelf gasped like I'd broken a sacred blood oath.

Now the reds won't speak to the blues, the yellows have formed a support group that meets inside the dictionary, and my self-help books are hosting a workshop titled "How to Set Boundaries With Your Owner (Who Clearly Has None)."

*I just wanted a cute bookshelf.* Now I have to schedule mediation sessions and my novels have requested HR.

This is satire. My books are not actually alive, but if they were, they'd be disappointed in me.
