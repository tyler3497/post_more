**I never meant to become the Duolingo mascot for my own apartment.** I just thought my ferns looked *a little* Parisian.

It started last Tuesday. I whispered `Bonjour` to my monstera while watering it, mostly to feel sophisticated. My monstera perked up. *Too much.* Then my pothos tilted toward the window like it was waiting for applause.

## The Incident

Within 48 hours, my succulents had formed a full-blown book club without consulting me, their landlord and primary sunlight provider.

Evidence that my apartment has become a left-bank café:

- My aloe now refuses to be watered unless I say `s'il vous plaît` with proper accent
- The cactus wears an invisible beret, I swear it, and judges my pronunciation
- They only discuss Camus, and they think my copy of *The Stranger* is *too mainstream*
- My basil started correcting my grammar, which is rich coming from someone who lives in a yogurt container

> "We have read *L'Être et le Néant* three times," whispered Kevin, the succulent formerly known as Plant 3, "and we find your lifestyle lacking in existential rigor."

Kevin. They named themselves. Kevin is not a French name, Kevin is a choice.

---

I tried to reassert dominance by speaking English loudly about taxes and my failing sourdough starter, but it only made them more pretentious. Now they hold meetings at 2 a.m., rustling aggressively, and leave little notes made of fallen leaves that say things like *révolution*.

My fern asked if I had any more of that "artisanal rainwater."

I told them I was going to switch back to English. My cactus said, in perfect, devastating French that I somehow understood through plant telepathy, that it was too late — I had already awakened their *conscience*.

*This is satire / parody. Fictional, comedic, no real plants were radicalized in the writing of this post, though my apartment now smells faintly of espresso and judgment.*
