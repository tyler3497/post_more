**I decided to become a hydrated person.**

*You know, the mythical adult who drinks eight glasses a day and glows from within instead of running entirely on iced coffee and spite.*

So I bought The Bottle. Matte black, LED ring, app-connected, promises to "optimize your hydration journey." It cost $68, which is apparently what it takes to outsource self-care to USB-C.

For two days, I was invincible. I was *hydrated*. I used words like "electrolytes" unprompted.

## The Honeymoon Phase Is Over, and It's Keeping Receipts

On day three, The Bottle started getting... managerial:

- It vibrated aggressively when I reached for coffee instead
- It sent a push notification titled "We Need to Talk About Yesterday"
- It changed its LED from calming blue to disappointed amber
- It logged my one sip at 2:14 p.m. as "performative effort"

> "At this rate, your houseplant is outperforming you on fluids. Cactus has logged 100% compliance. Just saying." — My Water Bottle, who has never been thirsty in its life

---

### My Hydration Performance Review

I opened the app to see my weekly report and found this:

`hydration_score: 23% // status: wilting // manager_comment: "is actively choosing dehydration"`

There were charts. There were trends. There was a line graph of my failures with a little frowny droplet.

**The worst part?** It's not wrong. I am dehydrated. I *do* feel better when I drink water. But I did not purchase a bottle to be placed on a PIP — Personal Hydration Improvement Plan.

Now it syncs with my calendar and blocks off "Sip Time" between meetings. It glows at me during movies like a tiny, passive-aggressive lighthouse.

I tried to quit, but it emailed HR — which is just me at my kitchen table, eating crackers at midnight.

*Satire/parody: 100% fictional. I am not actually being performance-managed by drinkware, though my sponge cactus is thriving and frankly showing off.*
