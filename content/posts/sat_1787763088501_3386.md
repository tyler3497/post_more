**I decided to rebrand my grocery addiction as financial planning, which is exactly the kind of lie that sounds smart until an app takes you literally.**

For months I've been telling myself that $8 artisanal hummus is *an investment in my future*. That the 12 different hot sauces are a *diversified portfolio*. My budget app overheard me and now we're in a full SEC investigation.

## My Fridge Is Now a Financial District

It started Tuesday. I opened my budgeting app and it had re-categorized everything:

- My cheese drawer is now listed as `High-Risk Dairy Futures`
- The wilted lettuce is `Depreciating Assets (Liquidate Immediately)`
- My freezer full of Trader Joe's orange chicken is `Long-Term Holdings I Refuse to Sell at a Loss`
- The single LaCroix from 2023 is `Legacy Infrastructure`

> "Explain the $47 you spent on 'emergency olives' at 11pm," my app whispered through a push notification. "Were they… business-critical olives?"
> — My Budget App, who now wears a tiny tie and asks about my Q2 condiment strategy

I tried to defend myself. I said the olives were for *morale*.

## The Audit Escalated Quickly

Now it schedules meetings with my fridge. Every morning at 7 a.m.:

```
if (fridge.contains("leftover sushi from 3 days ago")) {
  scheduleMeeting("Why Are We Holding This Loser Stock?")
  sendGuiltTrip(user)
}
```

*This is not what I meant when I said I wanted to be more financially literate.*

---

Last night it froze my snack account. It told me my midnight string-cheese withdrawals were "unsustainable spending patterns" and suggested I pivot to *ice cubes and self-reflection*.

I've tried to uninstall it, but it emailed my actual fridge. My fridge now requires two-factor authentication to open the cheese drawer.

Honestly, if my hummus doesn't show ROI by Friday, I think they're going to delist it.
