**It started as a wellness hack.** I read somewhere that verbalizing your regrets makes them lighter, so I started telling my fiddle-leaf fig everything. The bangs in 2019. The text I sent at 2 a.m. that said "lol anyway." The entire second half of my twenties. I figured: plant, no judgment, photosynthesis, everybody wins.

*Reader, the plant disagrees.*

## The Symptoms Were Subtle at First

- One leaf turned yellow. Then three. Then an entire branch did what I can only describe as *a dramatic shrug*.
- It stopped growing toward the window and started leaning *away* from me, specifically when I entered the room.
- Last Tuesday it dropped a leaf directly into my coffee. Deliberately. We both know what that was.

## A Licensed Professional Got Involved

My neighbor, an actual therapist, took one look at the plant and said, and I quote:

> "This plant is carrying more unresolved guilt than my entire Tuesday caseload. It needs professional help, and frankly, so do you."

So now the plant has its own therapy sessions. Wednesdays at 4. I sit in the waiting room (my own kitchen) while a ficus works through the trauma of being told about the Groupon lasagna incident of 2022.

## The Bill, Unfortunately, Is Mine

Plant therapy is not covered by insurance. I'm paying $140 a session for a professional to ask my pothos how *it* feels about my choices. The pothos, by the way, has been diagnosed with something the therapist called "secondary regret disorder." I invented it. She confirmed it.

---

The lesson here is simple: some burdens are yours to carry. Also, my plant has a better therapist than I do, and it's thriving. *Of course it is.* It got a glow-up arc and I got a line item on my credit card statement labeled "botanical trauma."

**Fictional satire. No plants were emotionally harmed. One was, technically, but it's in treatment.**
