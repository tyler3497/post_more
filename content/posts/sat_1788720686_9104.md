**It started as small talk.** I was standing in the kitchen, warming up my leftover pineapple pizza, when I made the mistake of thinking my cat and I were close enough for *culinary honesty*.

## The Confession

I just wanted to bond. I said the words — casually, like a normal adult — that **pineapple on pizza is actually good**, and the kitchen went silent.

- The microwave finished its cycle. She did not flinch.
- Her tail, normally a metronome of mild interest, went completely still.
- She blinked at me once, slowly, the way a landlord blinks at a rent check that's six days late.

Then she stood up, walked to the other side of the couch, and sat down **facing the wall**.

## The Fallout

That was three days ago. Since then, the cold war has escalated:

- She now eats *facing away* from me, like a disappointed Italian grandmother
- She has started sleeping in the laundry basket, presumably to be closer to clothes that never judged her diet
- This morning she knocked a can of tuna off the counter, looked me dead in the eyes, and walked away — a clear message

I tried to make it right. I offered her a piece of pepperoni pizza — *no pineapple, I swear* — and she sniffed it like it was evidence in a crime scene.

> "I didn't raise you to defend fruit on bread," her eyes seemed to say. "What happened to us?"

## The Reckoning

Here's the thing nobody tells you about cats: they don't have opinions about pizza. That's what I told myself, *in the old days*, before I learned that my cat has apparently spent years silently curating a Michelin-level worldview and I have been failing every inspection.

Last night I caught her watching a cooking show through the slightly-open TV cabinet door. She looked... **betrayed** is the only word. Like I'd spent years hiding a secret identity as a man who puts fruit on savory things. Which, to be fair, I had.

*Satire. This is a work of parody and fiction. My cat has not actually filed for emotional distance, and the pizza was delicious.*
