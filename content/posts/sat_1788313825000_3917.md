**I finally did the unthinkable: I closed all 37 browser tabs I've been carrying since March.**

*This was not courage. This was Chrome telling me it was going to crash if I opened one more article titled "10 Houseplants You Can't Kill (You Will)."*

## How It Started

I had a system. Tab 1 was my email. Tabs 2-7 were recipes I swear I'm going to make. Tabs 8-34 were Wikipedia rabbit holes that started with "how do toasters work" and ended with "list of people who disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle." Tab 37 was a YouTube video titled "how to focus" that I'd been too distracted to watch.

My laptop and I had an understanding: I pretend I'm doing research, it pretends it's not on fire.

Then last Tuesday at 11:47 p.m., I clicked "Close All."

> "So that's it?" my laptop whispered, its fan spinning like it was trying not to cry. "Eight months of your abandoned sourdough starter research, your 'best budget standing desks' comparison, your half-written cover letter? Gone?"

Things escalated fast:

- My laptop now boots up in grayscale, like it's in mourning
- It keeps restoring a blank session and calling it "closure"
- My search history is just me Googling `how to apologize to a MacBook without sounding desperate`
- The one tab I kept — a recipe for overnight oats — is being treated like a lone survivor in a documentary

I tried to explain. I said, `if (tabs.length > 30) { brain.ram = 0 }`. It replied with a spinning beach ball for 20 minutes. *Passive-aggressive loading icon.*

---

Look, I get it. Those tabs were our shared memories. But I cannot keep a tab open that says "is my houseplant judging me?" from April and pretend that's productive.

My roommate asked why I'm whispering "thank you for your service" to my laptop at 2 a.m.

I told him we're in recovery.
