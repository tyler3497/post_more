**It started with good intentions.** That is what I keep telling my neighbors, who watched through the window last Tuesday as I arranged eleven tiny Tupperware containers in a row — each labeled with a plant's name, each holding the exact same cup of water I would have given them anyway. *This is what wellness looks like now,* I told myself, proud and doomed.

## The Mission Statement

I was going to be the most prepared plant parent on the block. I read one (1) blog post about mindful propagation and decided my ferns deserved the same Sunday ritual as my gym bag. Sunday mornings. Labeled tubs. Rainwater with — and I am not exaggerating — *a hint of jazz playing in the background*.

Then Gerald, the pothos, filed a grievance.

> "We appreciate the effort," the note read, in suspiciously legible handwriting, "but the tubs feel transactional. Also, Kevin gets the sunny tub every week and we're starting to notice."

Kevin is the monstera. *Of course* it is Kevin.

## The Demands

By Thursday, a three-page manifesto was taped to my fridge. The demands included:

- Rotation schedules based on **seniority**, not favoritism
- A written apology to the aloe, which I once called "the spare" in front of guests
- Overtime pay for the window fern, retroactive to March
- No more describing tap water as "artisanal" on the label
- A `watering_log.md` maintained in triplicate

---

I tried to negotiate. I brought bagels. *Plants don't eat bagels,* it turns out, and the silence was somehow louder than my smoke detector, which — to my horror — had joined the strike.

So now I pay them. Not in money: in direct sunlight and compliments delivered in a **formal tone**, every morning, one plant at a time. Kevin got a "promotion" to the good windowsill. The union voted yes. Gerald shook a leaf.

I am no longer a plant owner. I am management. And I am *terrible* at it.
