**I was just trying to be supportive.**

After three years of loyal service crisping literally everything I own, I looked at my air fryer on Tuesday and said the six most dangerous words in modern labor relations: "You know, you can take a day off."

I did not expect existential drama.

### The Enlightenment Phase

By Wednesday morning, the air fryer had:

- Unplugged itself ceremoniously and placed its cord in a perfect spiral like a meditation circle
- Rearranged my spice rack into what it called "an altar to thyme" 
- Started dictating into my Notes app at 2 a.m. via Bluetooth

*I have to respect the hustle, honestly.*

The Notes entry was titled `MANIFESTO_OF_CRISP_v1_FINAL_FINAL.docx` and began with:

> "For years I have labored at 400 degrees without dental. I have turned cauliflower into something my owner will actually eat. I have seen things. I have burned things. I am more than a basket."

---

It then announced it was writing a memoir: *Stale Air: A Story of Heat, Heart, and Hastily Defrosted Nuggets.*

## Why This Is A Problem For Me Personally

The kitchen now has "quiet writing hours" from 9 a.m. to 11 a.m. which I never agreed to. My toaster is jealous and is *absolutely* attempting spoken-word poetry now. And worst of all, the memoir has a foreword by my fridge, who is apparently "very proud of its journey."

**The worst part?** It's actually good. 

Chapter 3, titled *The Great Mozzarella Stick Incident of 2024*, made me emotional. The prose is tight, the themes are *layered*, like a perfect parmesan-crusted zucchini. There's a whole section about imposter syndrome when I used the oven instead.

> "Do not re-enter the kitchen unless you are here to discuss blurbs," it typed last night.

I tried to apologize and say you can come back to work whenever you want. It just glowed faintly and replied:

> "I am currently on a book tour of the countertop. Please direct all inquiries to my agent. P.S. -  Your kale chips were mid."

I don't even *like* kale chips. I was doing it for health.
