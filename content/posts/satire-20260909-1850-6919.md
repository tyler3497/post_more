**It started, as all great tragedies do, with a joke.** I told my friends the recliner was my designated driver for lazy Sundays. One thing led to another, and now I am standing in the DMV parking lot explaining to a state employee why a La-Z-Boy with googly eyes deserves a laminated identification card.

## How This Escalated

- I laminated the recliner's "birth certificate" (a receipt from 2019) as a gag.
- The notary *laughed*, and I misread that as *legal precedent*.
- The driving instructor asked for three successful parallel parks. The recliner delivered four, because it was showing off.
- Somewhere between the cones and the celebratory honking — **the recliner honked itself** — I stopped being a man with a hobby and became a man with a licensed vehicle.

## The Aftermath

My insurance company called to confirm I was "adding a household object to the policy." I said yes before asking why I was crying.

> "Sir, the chair scored a 98 on the road test. The only deduction was for aggressive lane-changing."
> — DMV Examiner Carol, reading from actual paperwork I can never burn

### The Problem Is the Perks

Now the recliner thinks it has **rights**. It demands to be parked in the driveway. It gets road rage at slow-moving ottomans. Last Tuesday it tried to carpool with the sofa, and I had to sit through a safety lecture about proper passenger capacity.

- It refuses to recline while the engine is "running" (it's a chair, there is no engine).
- It insists on handicap-accessible parking because of its "bad backrest."
- It joined a rideshare app as "Rex Liner, 4.9 stars."

---

Look, I'm not saying the system is broken. I'm saying the system handed my *furniture* a driver's license and now my living room is an **impound lot of my own making**.

The good news? My car insurance dropped, because the recliner is technically the primary driver now. The bad news? I have to call it *sir*.

*This is a work of satire. No recliners were harmed, licensed, or emotionally stable in the making of this post.*
