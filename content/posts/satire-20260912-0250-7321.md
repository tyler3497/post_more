**In my defense, the economy is brutal out there.**

It started innocently enough. My parrot, Professor Squawks, kept screaming through my Zoom calls, so I started reading my resume aloud to bore him into silence. Big mistake. He has, as it turns out, the memory of a steel trap and the confidence of a man with rent due.

Within a week, he was reciting my professional summary back at me. Word for word. *Including the part where I exaggerated my Excel skills.*

## The Phone Screen Incident

Last Tuesday my phone was on speaker when a recruiter called. Before I could unmute, Squawks leaned into the microphone and announced:

> "This is Kevin. Kevin is a highly motivated self-starter with a proven track record of synergy."

My name is not Kevin.

## The Evidence Is Stacked Against Me

Since then, things have escalated:
- He now **wears my old lanyard** when guests come over
- He taught himself to say "let's circle back" and honestly, his delivery is better than mine
- He asked my mother if she could be a reference, and she said *yes without consulting me*

I sat through a mock interview yesterday where he corrected my answer about my biggest weakness. He puffed his chest out, looked the empty room dead in the eye, and said:

> "Perfectionism. And seed theft. Next question."

## Rock Bottom

This morning I caught him on my laptop building a LinkedIn profile. His headline? **"Seasoned Professional | Open to Work | Will Bite Recruiters Who Waste My Time."** He has forty-seven connection requests. I have four, and two of them are my mom.

I've started updating my own resume to include "ability to survive professional humiliation by a bird." Nobody is hiring for that. But the bird? The bird is getting callbacks.
