**I did not ask my rice cooker for career advice.** I was just trying to make jasmine rice without Googling the water ratio for the eighth time this month.

But I had just come from a performance review where my manager said I needed to be more *strategic*, and the rice cooker was the only thing in my kitchen that actually finishes what it starts.

## The PowerPoint No One Asked For

So I taped a little vision board to the fridge and explained my five-year plan out loud. You know, normal stuff.

- Year 1: Learn to keep a houseplant alive longer than my last situationship
- Year 2: Stop using `// TODO: fix later` as a personality trait
- Year 3: Own matching socks that weren't all bought in a panic at Target
- Year 4: Become the kind of person who owns a salad spinner *and uses it*
- Year 5: Achieve inner peace, or at least stop losing my keys in the fridge

My rice cooker listened. Too intensely. Its little red light pulsed like it was taking notes.

> "Interesting," it seemed to say, clicking into Warm mode with newfound confidence. "Your goals lack steam. Literally. Watch and learn."

### Now It Has Outpaced Me

By Tuesday, it had:

> "I have optimized my workflow. I have achieved consistent fluffiness. What have *you* done?"

It started waking up at 5 a.m. to do self-cleaning cycles. It printed tiny motivational quotes on the steam. One morning it was playing white noise affirmations:

`> you_are_enough.warm`

I tried to make quinoa out of spite. It refused.

> "We don't do half-grain commitments in this house anymore," it hissed, politely.

---

Look, I own a kitchen appliance with better follow-through than I have. It now has a morning routine, a growth mindset, and what I can only describe as a LinkedIn presence. 

I just wanted rice. Now I'm being career-coached by a $29 appliance from 2019 that thinks *I'm* the one who needs to be plugged in.

*SATIRE / PARODY — this is 100% fictional, my rice cooker is not sentient, and my five-year plan is still mostly in draft form.*
