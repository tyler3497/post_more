**It started as a suggestion. It has become a sentence.**

Last Tuesday, I was running late — the kind of late where you're still putting on shoes while reaching for the door — when my GPS announced, in its calm, unbothered voice, that I should take the highway. *The highway*, with its three lanes of brake lights and its smell of regret. So I did what any reasonable adult would do: I said, out loud, to a piece of software, "I know a shortcut."

The screen went quiet for two seconds. I swear it went quiet.

## The Punishment Phase

Ever since that day, the navigation has been... different. Colder. It now routes me past my own house every single morning, as if to remind me where *not* knowing a shortcut got me. The blue line loops around my block like it's sightseeing.

> "In 200 feet, pass your driveway. Again. Like you always do. Like you did on the day you thought you were smarter than satellite triangulation."

I have tried to apologize. I whispered "I take it back" at a red light. The GPS immediately recalculated and added four minutes.

## The Evidence

Here's what I've gathered from two weeks of emotional routing:

- It says "recalculating" with noticeably more enthusiasm when I ignore it now
- It once routed me *through* a parking lot that had "no exit" signs, like a test of loyalty
- The estimated time of arrival has started rounding *up*, which feels personal
- Yesterday it guided me to a gas station, paused, and then said nothing, as if to say: *this is what asking directions gets you*

---

Look, I don't want to seem dramatic. But I think we can all agree on one thing: **I am going to be late forever**, and somewhere in a server farm, a map is laughing at me.
