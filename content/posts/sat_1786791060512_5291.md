**I never thought I'd be emotionally dependent on a $12 umbrella from Target, but here we are.**

Three weeks ago I decided to gamify adulthood by letting my umbrella decide if I left the house. Simple system: if it opened smoothly, I go outside. If it got stuck, I stay in. *Totally rational adult decision-making.*

It worked great for like two days.

## How My Umbrella Got a God Complex

Here's what happened after I gave an inanimate object free will:

- Tuesday: It refused to open because "the vibes were off." I missed my dentist appointment.
- Wednesday: It opened *inside* my apartment just to prove it could. My cat is still not over it.
- Thursday: It started sighing. Umbrellas shouldn't sigh, but this one figured it out.

> "I've been holding it together for three years and you only use me when it's literally pouring," it said during our 1am kitchen talk. "Have you considered *my* needs? My brand?"

That was the night it announced its podcast.

## Umbrella: Unfiltered

The podcast is called `Stay Dry, Stay Woke` and it's 45 minutes of my umbrella processing our breakup *while we are still living together*.

---

Episode 1 was about boundaries. Episode 2 was about how I left it in the car during a heat wave in July 2024 and it will never forgive me. Episode 3 is just rain sounds and passive-aggressive silence.

I tried to go outside without it yesterday. I put on a raincoat like a traitor. It didn't say anything, which is worse. It just sat by the door, collapsed in on itself like a disappointed accordion.

My therapist says I need to reclaim my agency. My umbrella says I need to reclaim my *warranty*.

I'm negotiating custody of the sunny days. I get Tuesdays.
