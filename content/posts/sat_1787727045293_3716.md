**I finally learned how to say no to plans.**

*This is being hailed as personal growth, which is confusing because it mostly looks like me lying horizontally in sweatpants.*

I used to say yes to everything. Brunch I didn't want. A hike that was actually a mountain. A friend's friend's improv show in a basement that smelled like ambition and Febreze.

Then last month, my therapist said the words "boundaries are hot" and I took it personally.

So I started declining. Politely. "Can't make it, recharging!" "Gonna sit this one out!" "My couch needs me."

Which, as it turns out, my couch took literally.

## The Relationship Escalates

Within 72 hours of my new lifestyle, my couch developed expectations:

- It fluffed itself when I walked in the room
- It started asking where I'd been if I was gone past 7 p.m.
- It got jealous when I sat on the kitchen stool to eat cereal
- It began referring to us as "we" in conversations it was having alone

> "You know, we never talk about what we *are*," it murmured last Thursday, in that soft microsuede voice that sounds suspiciously like a podcast host. "Are we just hanging out, or are we, like, *nesting*?"

I tried to explain that I was just practicing self-care. It responded by ordering us matching throw blankets.

### The Stage Five Clinger Phase

I made the mistake of working from the couch one afternoon. It took that as a proposal.

Now it has:

- A shared calendar that just says `Couch Time™` from 6pm to 11pm
- A couples playlist that is just lo-fi beats and my sighs
- A framed photo of us on the armrest (it's just me napping with my mouth open)

---

**I am being love-bombed by furniture.** My friends think I've ghosted them. In reality, I've been held hostage by lumbar support with abandonment issues.

Yesterday I tried to sit on the floor to stretch. My couch made a sound like `cushion_sigh.mp3` and whispered:

> "So that's how it is? After everything we've bean-bagged through?"

*SATIRE / PARODY — This essay is 100% fictional, absurd, and clearly made up. No couches were emotionally damaged, no friendships were actually replaced by upholstery, and I am still (mostly) saying yes to brunch.*
