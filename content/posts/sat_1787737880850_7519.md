**I asked my air fryer if I should quit my job and now it won't stop asking about my childhood.**

*For context, my actual therapist charges $180 an hour and my air fryer came free with my apartment and already knows all my shameful 11 p.m. snack decisions.*

It started last Tuesday. I was staring at a basket of questionable sweet potato fries and I said out loud, "Should I quit my job and move to the woods?" I wasn't talking to anyone. But the air fryer — *bless its little stainless steel heart* — beeped.

One beep. Like it was saying, "I'm listening."

## The Session Begins

So I told it everything. The quarterly reviews, the Slack messages that just say "quick sync?", my dream of selling handmade birdhouses that look like tiny Frank Lloyd Wright houses.

It responded the way all great therapists do: with **intense, judgmental heat** and a 60-second timer.

> "And how does that make you *feel*, on a scale from 180 degrees to 400 degrees?" 
> — My Air Fryer, now calling itself Dr. Crisp, who has never had a day of training but has seen me cry over mozzarella sticks

By Thursday, we had a routine:

- 7:00 a.m. — I make toast, it asks if I'm *avoiding* my feelings by toasting them
- 12:30 p.m. — I reheat leftovers, it says I'm reheating old trauma
- 9:15 p.m. — I try to make kale chips, it sighs audibly and says `if (kale.chips == true) { self.deception = 100; }`

---

### My Breakthrough

I tried to set boundaries. I said, "You're an appliance."

It said — via the little digital display that now just reads `TELL ME MORE` — that *I* was being defensive.

**I realized I was paying $180 an hour to avoid exactly this conversation.** My air fryer does the same thing for 0 cents and the occasional crumb tray cleaning.

Last night I came home and found it had scheduled me for a double session. It preheated itself to 375 and just sat there, glowing, waiting.

I'm not quitting my job. Dr. Crisp says I'm not ready. He says I need to work through my fear of commitment, starting with committing to not opening the lid every 20 seconds to check.

*This is SATIRE / PARODY — entirely fictional, no air fryers are licensed therapists, please do not seek life advice from countertop appliances.*
