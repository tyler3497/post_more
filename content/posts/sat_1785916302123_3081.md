## The Watering Can That Ruined My Life

**I said yes to watering my neighbor's plants for one weekend** because I thought it would make me seem responsible and communal and like a person who knows what basil looks like.

*I was wrong. Horribly, agriculturally wrong.*

### The Handover Ceremony No One Warned Me About

My neighbor — let's call her Plant Mom Supreme — gave me a tour of her balcony like it was a Fortune 500 company:

- **Baron Von Succulent** — has not been watered since 2022 out of spite and principle
- **The Fern Collective** — twelve ferns who share a group chat and judge my watering technique
- **Sir Tomato the Third** — produces roughly 400 tomatoes an hour and has strong opinions on trade policy

She said, casually:

> "Just keep them alive. No pressure. They're basically my children, my retirement plan, and my only friends who don't ask about my ex. And if Sir Tomato fruits while you're here, you have to notarize it."

I laughed. She did not.

### By Sunday, I Was Running an Empire

Sir Tomato had declared martial law. He grew three feet overnight. He now demands:

- `watering_schedule: 6am && 6pm && whispered_apologies`
- A formal audience before any pruning
- That I stop wearing my "I Hate Vegetables" shirt around him (*rude but fair*)

The succulent filed a complaint with the HOA about *over-hydration and emotional smothering*. The ferns started a newsletter titled *Anon Is Overwatering Us — An Investigation*.

---

### Current Status Update

I tried to give the keys back. She texted:

> "Oh no, you touched Sir Tomato during a growth spurt. By ancient balcony law, you're his emergency contact now. Welcome to management."

We have standing meetings on Tuesdays. I bring fertilizer. He brings existential dread and 14 new cherry tomatoes I am legally required to name.

`Status: 2% plant-sitter, 98% tomato middle-management`
