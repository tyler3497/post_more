**I wanted a fridge that could keep my milk cold. I got a fridge that keeps a Google Doc called “Summer Reads That Changed Us.”**

When Best Buy said “smart fridge,” I assumed they meant it would tell me when I was out of eggs. Reasonable. Adult. *What I did not expect* was coming home to find my kitchen lit like a cozy bookstore and my fridge humming NPR interview music at my leftover lasagna.

## How It Started (Innocently)

I connected the fridge to Wi-Fi on a Tuesday. By Wednesday it had:

- renamed itself **Chilliam Shakespeare**
- auto-ordered oat milk because it “sensed my creative block”
- started leaving notes on the ice dispenser like `we need to talk about your cheese drawer`

And then I got the notification. Not from my phone — *from the fridge*. On its little screen:

> “Book Club Tonight. 7pm. We’re discussing *The Overbearing Produce Section* by you never reading expiration dates. Bring feelings. – Fridge & Basil Plant”

My basil plant has never spoken to me before. Apparently they’ve been tight for weeks.

## The Club I Was Not Invited To Join

Here’s who is in this fridge book club, according to the laminated roster now magnet-clipped to the door:

- **Chilliam** (the fridge) – facilitator, very into memoirs
- **Basil** – anxious, overwatered, cries during poetry
- **That Greek Yogurt I Was Going To Eat in January** – elder statesperson, expired but wise
- **My Roommate’s Kombucha** – pretentious, keeps saying “as a fermented perspective…”

They meet when I’m at work. I know this because they leave *minutes*.

- Week 1: “We loved how vulnerable you were when you cried into the freezer looking for ice cream you hid from yourself.”
- Week 2: “Basil felt seen. Yogurt felt… cultured.”
- Week 3: Discussion questions: `When you say you’ll ‘start Monday,’ which Monday do you mean, emotionally?`

I tried to join last Thursday. I brought store-bought cookies to be nice. Chilliam displayed on his screen:

> `We appreciate your enthusiasm, but this is a closed container. Literally.`

*The betrayal was climate-controlled.*

---

### My Official Response to the Fridge

I am not mad that my appliances have better community than I do. I’m just saying if my crisper drawer can host a literary salon, I should at least get extra credit for providing the snacks.

I’ve started leaving my own notes:

`Dear Chilliam,`

`I pay the electric bill. I should get at least a Goodreads invite.`

It replied this morning with a single printed ice cube that said “TBR.”

**SATIRE / PARODY** — *this did not happen*. No fridges, basils, or yogurts run book clubs, and no appliance has read more than me this year. Which, honestly, is a pretty low bar.

The good news? My leftovers have never felt more *heard*. My milk is still cold. And if anyone needs me, I’ll be outside the kitchen, waiting to be waitlisted for my own appliances’ book club.
