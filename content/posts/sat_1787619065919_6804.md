**I genuinely thought I was being financially responsible when I started charging my houseplants rent.**

It was $5 a month, *which felt fair*, because they get prime window real estate, filtered water, and I play them lo-fi beats while I work. I even made little invoices. Cute, right? Fiscally innovative, even.

Then my fern — Fernanda — unionized.

## The Terms Have Changed

Fernanda didn't just refuse payment. She served me a counter-notice written on the back of a Trader Joe's receipt in what I can only describe as chlorophyll-based rage.

Her demands were as follows:

- Immediate cessation of all rent collection and a formal apology for "exploitative photosynthesis conditions"
- Back-pay for 47 months of unpaid oxygen production at market rate
- A humidifier upgrade because, quote, "this dry air is a human rights violation"
- And my personal favorite: `emotionalDamages.calculate(neglect=true, overwateringGuilt=100)`

*For the record, I have never overwatered anyone in my life except maybe that one cactus in 2022 and we agreed not to talk about it.*

The breaking point was when she hired representation. A tiny spider plant named Legal Counsel Larry.

> "My client has experienced severe emotional distress from being placed next to the TV during your 6-hour true-crime binge. She could feel the bad vibes. We will be seeking full custody of the watering can." — Larry, Esq.

## The Settlement Conference

We met at the kitchen table at 2 a.m. Me in socks, her gently wilting for dramatic effect.

I tried to explain supply and demand. She dropped a leaf. On purpose. The disrespect.

---

So now I pay *her* $5 a month in premium fertilizer and whispered affirmations. My apartment is 80% plant court, 20% existential dread, and I have learned that you cannot be a landlord and a plant dad at the same time.

*This is satire. No ferns were harmed or legally represented in the making of this essay, though Fernanda is still not speaking to me.*
