**It started, as all bad decisions do, with a $12 lapel microphone and too much free time.**

I was walking through the park last month, coffee in hand, when I whispered my first narration:

> *Here we see the suburban male in his natural habitat — pajama pants, one shoe untied — foraging for Wi-Fi near the duck pond.*

I don't know why I said it in a British accent. *It just felt right.*

Within days, the narration had taken over my life. Every mundane errand became **epic**:

- Grocery run: *"The alpha shopper marks his territory with coupons."*
- Laundry: *"She folds the fitted sheet. Scientists remain baffled."*
- 2 a.m. fridge raid: *"Under cover of darkness, the nocturnal snack-seeker strikes."*

## The Pigeon Problem

The pigeons, however, were not having it.

Every day at 7 a.m., I set up near their fountain and narrated their pecking order like it was the season finale of a survival show. I named the big one **Greg** and invented a whole storyline where he was betraying his brother for breadcrumbs.

Then, yesterday, I arrived to find them arranged in a perfect semicircle. Silent. Waiting.

One stepped forward. It dropped a crumpled napkin at my feet. Written on it, in what I can only describe as *aggressive pecking font*:

> **DEMANDS OF LOCAL 47, PIGEON LABOR UNION**
> 1. No more narration during feeding hours
> 2. Greg is not a traitor, he is a hero
> 3. Better breadcrumbs (the whole-wheat ones are insulting)

---

I'm currently in negotiations. Greg represents management. My accent is gone, replaced by the hoarse whisper of a man who has seen too much. Yesterday I caught myself narrating the negotiation itself, and they *stormed off the fountain in formation*.

The moral of the story: talk like a documentary host long enough, and eventually the wildlife files a grievance.

**Also, Greg really is a traitor.**
