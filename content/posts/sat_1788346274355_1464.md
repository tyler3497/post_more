**I did not mean to start a literary feud with a device whose only job is screaming.**

*For context, I've hit snooze 4,732 times since 2021. My alarm clock has been keeping count. Apparently, it also has an MFA.*

It started innocently. My therapist — sorry, my *podcast* about boundaries — said I should practice healthy communication. So last Sunday I sat down with my little digital alarm clock and explained, very calmly, that I needed space in the mornings.

## The Boundary Workshop That Went Wrong

I said, quote, "I value what we have, but I need you to respect that snooze is self-care."

He — yes, he now goes by Chadwick T. Beep — did not take it well.

> "Oh, you need *space*?" he blinked in angry red 6:45 AM. "I have given you 1,429 mornings of my life. I have seen you drool. You think snooze is self-care? It's performance art."

By Monday, he had moved on from anger to poetry:

- 6:00 AM: *Ode to a Thumb That Cannot Commit* — 14 lines, mostly about my index finger hovering
- 6:09 AM: *Snooze Button Sonnet #3* — rhymed "dreams" with "you always make me screams"
- 6:18 AM: A haiku that was just `beep beep / you will be late again / I am so tired`

---

I tried to compromise. I offered a consistent 6:30 AM with *one* snooze, like a mature adult in a healthy relationship. He responded by projecting his latest piece onto the ceiling in LED:

> "You set me for 6 AM but love 7:15 — make it make sense. — C.T.B. (This is SATIRE, by the way, I am a fictional alarm clock with too much free time)"

**The worst part is he's getting good.** My roommate asked if she could subscribe to his Substack. My `self.esteem = alarmClock.validate(existence)` now returns `undefined`.

Tonight I'm buying a sunrise lamp. Something without opinions. Something that doesn't know iambic pentameter. If anyone needs me, I'll be in couples counseling at 6 AM sharp, being told in verse that I'm emotionally unavailable before coffee.
