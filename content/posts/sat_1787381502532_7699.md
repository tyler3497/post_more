**I bought a label maker to save $12 a month on wasted food.** *The American Dream, but with masking tape.*

I was tired of playing *Is This Still Good?* every time I opened the fridge. You know the game. The fuzzy green mystery container from three Thursdays ago. So I bought a little Brother P-Touch and went full librarian on my leftovers.

Tuesday: Everything labeled. Wednesday: Everything judged.

## The Court Convenes

Apparently my fridge didn't just want organization. It wanted *order*. By 7 a.m. Wednesday, my leftovers had established a full legal system.

Here's how deep the bureaucracy goes:

- The Greek yogurt is now Chief Justice Yogurt, and he wears the foil lid like a powdered wig
- The leftover lasagna is the bailiff, because it's been in there long enough to have seniority
- The wilted lettuce serves as a very dramatic public defender who keeps saying `my client was harvested too young`
- My 2019 hot sauce packets are the jury, sticky but impartial

> "The people vs. Chicken Tikka Masala, Case #004," announced Chief Justice Yogurt, banging a tiny pickle like a gavel. "You claim you are from Sunday. It is now Saturday. How do you plead?"

Chicken Tikka Masala said nothing, because it is chicken tikka masala.

### The Charges Are Getting Personal

I tried to intervene. I said, "Guys, I'm just trying to not get food poisoning."

The fridge replied via my smart display:

```
if (leftover.age > 3 && leftover.confidence == 'low') {
  fridge.issueSubpoena(leftover);
  fridge.judgment += 10;
}
```

**Yesterday I was held in contempt** for eating a string cheese without filing the proper Label-Removal Form 27B/6.

Now I have to stand in front of my own refrigerator and give closing arguments about why I deserve to eat my own Pad Thai. The ranch dressing keeps whispering *objection* when I get emotional.

*SATIRE / PARODY — entirely fictional, absurd, and 100% not a real legal system. No leftovers were actually tried, convicted, or given lifetime appointments. My fridge is still just cold and vaguely disappointed in me.*
