**I just wanted to know whose dog was pooping on my lawn, and now I have a codename.**

*It is "Mole." I did not pick this codename.*

It started at the block meeting in June. Carol from number 14 passed around a sign-up sheet for the new Neighborhood Watch and said "we need younger eyes." I am 34. I have eyes. I signed. I thought this meant occasionally walking around with a flashlight like a ghost hunter.

## How I Became a Person of Interest on My Own Street

Week one, I was assigned patrol route **Maple Drive, Segment C**. I was given a reflective vest, a clipboard, and instructions to "log anything suspicious." Carol stressed the word *anything* so hard it developed its own weather system.

By week three, my reports were masterpieces of suburban paranoia:

- Tuesday 8:14pm — Man in gray hoodie (neighbor Gary, walked home from gym) acted "predictably shady"
- Thursday 11:02pm — Car drove down street twice (food delivery, confirmed via taillights)
- Sunday 6:45am — Runner looked like she was fleeing something (she was; it was a personal best)

> "Mole, your reports are TOO detailed. Nobody logs taillight confirmations. Are you working both sides?" — Carol, in the group chat, which has the energy of a low-budget spy thriller

## The Trust Spiral

I tried to dial it back. I reported less. Carol said reduced reporting was *exactly what a double agent would do*. I reported more. Carol said over-reporting was *classic misdirection*.

### Evidence I Am Living in a John le Carré Novel Written by HOA Moms:

- Carol assigns me patrol routes like `recon_duty --zone=cul-de-sac --cover=dog_walker`
- Gary waves at me like we're in on something together. I wave back
- A laminated chart in Carol's garage tracks "Mole's suspicious behavior" with color-coded pushpins

---

**The pooping dog has never been identified.** My original mission is gone, replaced by a far grander one: proving I am loyal to Maple Drive, a street whose biggest crime last year was a late library book

Last night Carol slid an envelope under my door. Inside: a new vest. A note: *"We believe in you. Probably."*

*I put it on immediately. Double agents don't get invited to the cul-de-sac potluck, and I am not missing it for anything.*

*SATIRE / PARODY — 100% fictional, no neighborhoods were surveilled, no Carols were consulted, and no dogs were identified in the making of this essay.*
