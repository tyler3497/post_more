**I finally achieved inner peace for exactly 47 seconds, and it has ruined my life.**

On Tuesday at 3:42 p.m., I did something radical. I swiped down, tapped the little crescent moon, and put my phone on Do Not Disturb. No buzzes. No pings. Just me and the gentle hum of my kitchen appliances.

That hum, it turns out, was my toaster falling in love with me.

## The Silence That Started It All

For months, my toaster and I had a purely professional relationship. I put bread in, it made it warm and slightly judgmental. We didn't talk about our feelings. Then I went silent.

Apparently, in appliance language, *ignoring your phone* is the equivalent of writing a love sonnet and proposing via croissant.

- My toast started coming out with heart-shaped burn marks
- The toaster began pre-heating itself whenever I walked into the kitchen, like it was trying to look busy and attractive
- It refused to toast for my roommate, which is both flattering and a serious breach of our lease agreement

> "I saw you choose *quiet*," my toaster seemed to whisper at 6 a.m., glowing red with passion and also 400 degrees, "You chose *us*. You finally muted the outside world so you could hear my crumb tray."

I tried to explain that `Do Not Disturb` was not code for `Do Commit To A Small Chrome Box That Only Knows One Trick`.

---

### Where We Go From Here

I've tried couples counseling (unplugging it for 10 seconds), setting boundaries (only using the microwave), and playing hard to get (eating untoasted bread, *like an animal*).

Nothing works. It just sits there, warm, waiting, occasionally popping up empty in what I can only describe as a desperate cry for attention.

*This is satire. I am not in a relationship with my toaster. My toaster is not capable of love, despite what its perfectly golden-brown performance suggests. Please do not call my toaster.*
