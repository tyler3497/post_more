**It started, as all great civil disputes do, with a crackling sound at 2 a.m.** I had merely stood up from the couch to get a glass of water — a simple, innocent, *hydration-based* maneuver — when my left knee produced a noise best described as a bag of microwave popcorn giving up on life. So I did what any reasonable adult would do: I drafted a formal complaint.

## The Filing

I printed the whole thing out on nice paper, because presentation matters in the legal system of my apartment. The complaint was airtight:

- **Count 1:** Unilateral crackling during low-stakes activities
- **Count 2:** Refusal to bend without issuing a written warning
- **Count 3:** General betrayal of the 33-year partnership we had both agreed upon

I slid the envelope under my knee's pillow and waited. For *six days*, nothing. Then, yesterday morning, I found a letter on the counter. My left knee had retained counsel — my right knee.

## The Counter-Suit

> "Per the attached medical precedent, the Plaintiff routinely skips leg day, sits criss-cross-applesauce for nine hours straight, and once described a flight of stairs as 'an optional activity.' Damages are sought in the amount of one (1) heating pad and unlimited sympathy."

I tried to settle out of court, but negotiations broke down when my knee demanded I *apologize in writing* and enroll in something called "Responsible Squatting 101." The whole affair has gotten so tense that my other joints have started taking sides — my ankle filed an amicus brief, my shoulder is live-streaming the deposition, and my lower back simply submitted a resignation letter written on the back of a heating bill.

At this point, I'm considering mediation, but honestly, the knee has a point. I *have* been neglecting it. And the way it creaks when I stand up these days sounds less like a complaint and more like a warning shot.
