## The Socks That Started It All

**I walked into a department store with a pair of socks that have never been worn, never been loved, and clearly never been wanted by anyone in my family.**

*This was supposed to take five minutes. A quick in-and-out, no eye contact, return to my car with moral superiority intact.*

It took forty-seven minutes, two supervisor consultations, and the birth of a deeply committed interpersonal relationship with a customer service department.

### Why I Thought This Would Work

My logic was flawless, which is how you know it was terrible:

- They were a gift. From my aunt. Who shops exclusively at stores none of us have ever been to.
- The tag said "One Size Fits Most." *A bold lie.* They fit neither most nor me.
- I left the receipt in what I can only describe as *another dimension* that briefly opened in my laundry room last February.

The associate at the counter — let's call her Karen From Customer-Care-That-Cares-Too-Much — held the socks up like evidence at a trial.

> "No receipt? That's okay! We can look it up. Do you have the original card? The phone number? The exact emotional state you were in when you received them in December 2024 at 3:47 p.m.?"

She typed. She squinted. She called a manager. The manager called *her* manager.

By minute 23, we had abandoned the idea of a return entirely and were co-managing my personal schedule instead.

- `status: pending_return` has been changed to `status: its_complicated`
- We now have a recurring calendar event titled "Check On Tyler's Sock Feelings" every Tuesday at 3 p.m.
- She suggested I "repurpose" them as "cozy hand warmers for existential dread"

---

### Where We Are Now

**I left without a refund, but with something far more valuable:** a shared Google Calendar invitation and a handwritten note that says "Can't wait to see you next week!! - Customer Support 🧦"

I accepted. The system marked me as *tentative*.

The socks are still on my dresser. We are learning to co-exist. They have not forgiven me for trying to return them, but they *have* agreed to mediation on Thursdays.

*This post is satire/parody. Not a real customer service interaction. No retail workers were harmed in the making of these bad decisions.*
