**I thought pottery would be calming.** You know, hands in clay, centering yourself, becoming one with the earth.

*Instead, I have created a small ceramic god who judges me.*

## The Birth of Chad the Mug

Our instructor said "let the clay tell you what it wants to be." My clay wanted to be aggressively mediocre. After 45 minutes of wobbling, collapsing, and one moment where I swear the wheel whispered *give up*, I produced something that was technically a mug if you squinted and had low standards.

I named him Chad. Chad has:

- One handle that only works if you're left-handed, right-handed, and optimistic
- A lip that drips on purpose to "keep you present"
- The emotional stability I was supposed to be learning in this class
- More confidence than I've had since 2019

> "You came here to find yourself," Chad said, in the voice I imagine all judgmental pottery uses, "but you found me. And frankly, I'm handling it better."

## My Mug Has Its Life Together and I Don't

Chad now lives on my kitchen shelf and has become weirdly successful:

- He holds pens like a motivational speaker holds a mic
- My roommate asks Chad for advice before asking me
- He survived the dishwasher. I did not survive my inbox this morning.

The class final was supposed to be `bringYourBestPiece()`. I brought Chad. The instructor held him up and said:

> "This is... brave. This is a choice. This is art that has accepted its flaws, which is more than I can say for most of my students."

I got an A- and an existential crisis.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This never happened. No mugs were harmed, no pottery classes were emotionally compromised, and Chad the Mug is not actually more well-adjusted than me, although he does have excellent posture and hasn't checked his email in 3 weeks and seems happier for it.*
