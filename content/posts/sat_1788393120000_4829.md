**I didn't think dish soap needed emotional boundaries.**

*I was wrong. So, so wrong.*

It started when I noticed my dish soap — let's call him Suds — was giving me 110% every single time. One pump? He'd foam like he was auditioning for a car wash commercial. Two pumps? Full existential crisis in the sink.

## The Intervention

I sat Suds down on the counter and tried to be gentle:

> "Hey buddy, I love your enthusiasm, but you don't have to dissolve yourself completely every time I have cereal. It's okay to do less."

Suds quivered. Literally. A bubble popped.

> "Less? LESS? I've been *giving* since 2023! You think those plates clean themselves? I have a PURPOSE!"

### Evidence That Suds Has No Boundaries:

- Has never said no to a greasy pan, even the cast iron I explicitly told him not to touch
- Whispers *more bubbles = more love* at 11pm
- Once tried to clean my water bottle from the inside by climbing in
- Refers to the sponge as "my emotional support abrasive"

Which brings us to the sponge.

---

After my talk, Suds spiraled. Now my sponge — Brenda — has taken on the role of unpaid therapist. She sits in her little caddy, damp and wise, saying things like `listening --mode=active --scent=lime` while Suds vents about abandonment issues.

This morning I found a tiny support group in the sink:

- Brenda saying "We hold space for all residues"
- Suds crying about how I used *hand soap* once
- The faucet dripping in what I can only assume was judgment

I just wanted to do dishes. Now I have to schedule co-dependency workshops between cleaning products. My kitchen has become a wellness retreat I did not sign up for, and I'm pretty sure my sponge is billing me by the hour.

*This is satire/parody. Entirely fictional. No sponges or soaps were harmed, though one is now accepting new clients.*
