**I made the mistake of democracy.** I handed the remote to the remote.

It started last Friday. My roommate was out, my willpower was gone, and I was forty minutes deep into the scroll — you know the scroll, the one where you read full synopses of shows you will never watch — when I finally gave up and said, out loud, to no one: *"You know what? You pick."*

The remote heard me.

## The Evidence

- It **skipped** three comedies like they were personal insults
- It paused on a four-hour documentary about *fermentation* and the volume went up. By itself.
- When I reached for it, it was **warm**. Remotes should not be warm. That's biology.

> "I watched you rewatch the same sitcom for the sixth time," the remote did not say, because remotes cannot talk. "I simply curated."

*That is when I realized I had been narrating its inner monologue for ten minutes.*

## The Intervention

I tried setting boundaries. I installed fresh batteries — the universal apology — and it responded by auto-playing a TED Talk titled `How to Let Go of People Who Don't Appreciate You`.

I tried hiding it under the couch. It turned the TV on *from inside the cushions*. Muffled. Judgmental. Glowing through the fabric like a tiny disapproving sun.

## The Verdict

Last night I came home and the TV was already on. The remote sat centered on the coffee table, angled at the couch like a throne addressing its subject. On the screen: a documentary about ottoman empires. Plural. It had made a **list**.

**I have decided to stop fighting it.** Honestly? The fermentation documentary changed me. I now own three jars of something. My taste has never been more refined, or more *supervised*.
