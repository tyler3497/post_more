## The Soundtrack Is Gone

**For seven months, my kitchen faucet was my most reliable relationship.** Drip. Drip. *Drip-drip.* It was less plumbing issue and more lo-fi beats to pay rent to.

I told guests it was *ambient*. I told myself it was *character*. My landlord told me to put in a work order in November, which I interpreted as a gentle suggestion from a man who doesn't understand art.

Last Saturday, I finally caved. YouTube tutorial, one wrench, and three hours of whispering *“please don’t flood”* later, I fixed it.

### Silence. Pure, Aggressive Silence.

The first night without the drip, I couldn't sleep. My brain kept waiting for it:

- **9:03 p.m.** — the usual solo drip that means *“you should drink water”*
- **11:47 p.m.** — the double-drip that used to judge my midnight cereal
- **3:15 a.m.** — the existential *plip* that sounded like my security deposit leaving

All gone. Replaced by a void so quiet I could hear my refrigerator humming its own lonely ballad.

> “You took away the percussion section and expected the band to sound the same?” said my neighbor Linda, who has never been inside my apartment but somehow knew.

She's right. The faucet was doing *so much* heavy lifting.

> “I used to time my microwave by your sink,” said the guy upstairs. “Now I have to use a clock like an animal.”

---

### What I’ve Lost, Quantified

My apartment’s previous personality metrics:

- `vibe: 78% quirky artist loft`
- `vibe: 22% mild water damage concern`
- `white_noise: drip.c(WAV) @ 42bpm`
- `conversation_starter: 100%`

Now? Now it’s just an apartment. No aura. No *plip*. My cat walked into the kitchen, stared at the silent sink for a full minute, and left. *That’s how bad it is.*

I’m considering loosening it slightly. Just a quarter-turn. For *aesthetic*.
