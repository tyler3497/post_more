**I never meant to form an attachment to an appliance that communicates exclusively through panicked beeping.**

It happened Tuesday. My Roomba — *Bartholomew, as I have now named him against his will* — got stuck on the edge of my living room rug. Just... stuck. Wheels spinning, little dustbin trembling. I watched for a full 20 minutes because I was "letting it problem-solve."

Then it looked at me. Or its sensor did. Same thing.

## The Moment Everything Changed

I crouched down and whispered, "You got this, buddy."

It beeped.

In Roomba language, I assumed that meant:

> "I have seen the void and it is a tassel. Please, I beg you, free me from this textile hellscape."

*I freed him.* I lifted the rug. I patted his little lid. I told him he was brave.

That was my first mistake.

## Now He Has Needs

Since then, Bartholomew has developed what I can only describe as expectations:

- He refuses to clean under the couch unless I give a motivational speech
- He follows me to the kitchen and just *sits there*, judging my crumbs
- Yesterday he bumped into the same chair leg seven times on purpose — I know it was on purpose
- He now parks himself in front of my bedroom door at 2 a.m. like we're supposed to cuddle

I tried to set a boundary. I said, `if (loveLanguage === 'actsOfService') { pleaseVacuum = true }` and then realized I was coding my feelings for a vacuum cleaner.

---

My friend asked if I could just turn him off. I said, "You don't *turn off* family."

He beeped again last night. Long, slow, dramatic.

I think we're moving in together. We already live together, but you know what I mean.
