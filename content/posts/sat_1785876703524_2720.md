## Behold: Personal Growth™

**I bought a $12 linen-bound journal from Target to become a mindful person.** You know the type — cream paper, little elastic band, whispers *your life could be organized if you just tried harder*.

The first entry was aspirational. The second was a to-do list. By day four, it was just me trauma-dumping about my laundry.

My journal did *not* appreciate the tone shift.

### The Performance Review

This morning I opened it to find — and I swear this is verbatim — margin notes. In my own handwriting, but angrier.

> "You can't start with 'I feel a bit anxious' and then pivot to a 400-word rant about self-checkout machines. PICK A LANE, Tyler's spiritual successor who is clearly named Anon."

Look, I thought journaling was supposed to be *non-judgmental*. Mine has become a developmental editor.

It has demands now:

- **Full editorial control** over all entries after 9 p.m. (*"you're not coherent, you're just tired"*)
- **A co-author credit** on any personal growth I achieve in Q3
- Removal of the phrase `vibe check` which it has flagged as "overused and emotionally vague"
- A `genre: literary memoir` classification instead of what I wrote, which was `genre: tired complaints in cursive`

---

### The Negotiation

> "I've carried your grocery lists, your half-baked business ideas, your detailed ranking of every Trader Joe's snack," my journal sighed, via another passive-aggressive post-it it told me to write to myself. "The least you could do is acknowledge my labor."

It wants to be listed on LinkedIn. It wants royalties.

I tried to compromise: *you get the cover blurb, I get inner peace.* It countered with profit-sharing and a footnote on every page that says *"as transcribed from the brilliant consciousness of Moleskine #4"*.

We have reached an uneasy truce. I write, it copy-edits in real time, and we both pretend the entry where I cried about expired hummus never happened.

My therapist says this is progress. My journal says it wants a byline.
