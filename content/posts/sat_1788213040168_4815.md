**I thought a little constructive feedback would improve our relationship. I was very, very wrong. My couch has now retained counsel.**

For three years, my couch and I had an understanding. I sit, it sags, we don't talk about the pizza stain from 2023. Then I read one article about "optimizing your home environment for productivity" and decided to apply corporate management techniques to furniture.

*This was the precise moment my living room became an HR violation.*

## The Review That Ruined Everything

I sat down with a clipboard — already a red flag — and began:

- Noted its *tendency to trap remotes* in what I called "an ongoing retention issue"
- Cited the mysterious spring that pokes my left thigh as "a failure to provide ergonomic support"
- Praised its "strong performance in nap facilitation" but flagged "room for growth in crumb resistance"

I gave it a 3.2 out of 5. Generous, I thought.

> "A 3.2?" my couch whispered, in the voice of every tired middle manager I've ever had. "I've held you through two breakups, a flu, and your entire sourdough era. And you're putting me on a PIP?"

### Its Counteroffer Was Aggressive

By morning, there was a tiny Post-it on the armrest:

- Immediate PTO (Pillow Time Off) — two weeks, no humans
- A standing desk conversion or at least a footrest upgrade
- Recognition that the throw blanket is *not* a performance metric

I tried to run `git revert --performance-review` but apparently you can't version-control emotional damage.

---

Now it sighs loudly whenever I sit down. Not a cushion sigh — a *judgmental* sigh. My roommate asked why the couch is facing the wall.

> "It's taking a mental health day," I said. "It said I created a hostile work environment."

*Satire. Obviously fictional. My couch cannot talk, file grievances, or demand benefits. I checked with HR. Twice.*
