**I bought a pothos because the internet said it was impossible to kill.**

*Which, in retrospect, was clearly a challenge, not a description.*

## The Power Shift

For six months I watered it every Sunday, whispered "you're doing amazing, sweetie" and went about my life. Then last Tuesday I found a tiny business card tucked into the soil that read:

> "Greg — Certified Growth Mindset Coach. Helping Humans Reach Their Full Photosynthesis Potential. First session free if you bring snacks."

Apparently my plant, Fernando, had hired Greg.

Now **I** am the project. According to Greg's official 5-point renovation plan for my life:

- I must wake up at 6:30am for "morning light exposure" because Fernando needs indirect sun and I need discipline
- I am only allowed to drink water after holding the glass for 10 seconds to "connect with its journey"
- My true crime podcasts are banned for "creating tense soil energy" — it's just rainforest sounds now
- My watering technique was rated 2.5/5 with the note "great heart, chaotic pour"
- I have to journal daily under the prompt `What Would Fernando Do?`

> "Your human has abandonment issues and over-apologizes to succulents," Fernando apparently told Greg. "We're making progress, but the overwatering is emotional."

## The New Management

I tried to explain that *I* pay rent here. Greg slid me an invoice and said:

`ERROR: codependent_plant_parent.exe has stopped responding. Please touch grass. Literally.`

Yesterday Fernando sprouted a new leaf. Greg clapped. I got a gold star sticker that says **I DIDN'T DROWN ANYONE TODAY** and I cried a little because I was genuinely proud.

---

Look, I support personal growth. I really do. But I didn't expect to get performance-reviewed by something that is 70% tap water and 30% absolute audacity.

*This essay is satire/parody — entirely fictional. No actual plants were promoted to management. No life coaches were harmed, though Greg is billing me hourly. All houseplant relationships depicted are absurd and not real.*
