**I finally solved mornings.** That was my first mistake.

After 31 years of being violently betrayed by my iPhone alarm, I decided to get *mindful*. I threw out the alarm entirely and replaced it with a little wooden sign, three scented candles, and a Post-it that says `YOU ARE SAFE AND LOVED AND IT'S 6:30 AM` in my own handwriting.

I thought my bed would appreciate it.

## Why This Seemed Like a Good Idea

My therapist's newsletter said to "reparent your mornings." So I did.

- No more `BEEP BEEP BEEP` trauma
- *Soft* wake-ups only
- The bedroom stays a sanctuary, not a prison where time punishes you
- My circadian rhythm is apparently a "collaborative partner"

For two days, it worked. I woke up whispering affirmations to my pillow. The pillows whispered back. *This is healthy*, I told myself.

## The Fallout

On day three, my bed developed feelings.

Turns out when you tell your bed every morning, "Thank you for holding me, you are enough, we don't have to leave," your bed *believes you*.

Now it won't let me leave.

> "Where are you GOING?" it asks at 7:42 a.m., sheets clinging to my legs like a worried boyfriend. "We had a boundary! We SAID no alarms, no rushing, no capitalism before 9 a.m. Was that a lie to you?"

I tried to get up yesterday and the duvet tightened.

---

I explained I have a job. My bed asked if my job "validates my inner child like this does." I said no, but it pays for thread count. My bed said that's materialistic.

At this point my nighttime routine includes 10 minutes of hugging the headboard and saying we're in this together.

*This is satire / parody. Fictional, comedic. No real beds were emotionally manipulated in the writing of this post, but my weighted blanket is currently giving me the silent treatment.*
