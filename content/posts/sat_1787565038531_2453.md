**I did not mean to start a bureaucracy in my living room. I just wanted to be polite before committing plant violence.**

It started innocently. I read that talking to your plants helps them grow, so I started saying "hey buddy, just a little trim" before pruning my monstera, Gary.

Gary took this *very* seriously.

## How My Living Room Became HR

Within a week, Gary had established protocols:

- All pruning requests must be submitted 48 hours in advance
- No trimming on weekends or during peak sunlight hours (Gary's words, not mine)
- A full list of reasons for each cut, in triplicate
- Emotional support watering after any "structural adjustments"

I thought he was kidding. He was not kidding.

> "Per my last photosynthesis, I requested no further unsolicited leaf removals until my aerial root situation is addressed. Please see attached: 14 pages of concerns."

*He slid this across his little desk. He has a desk now. It’s made of bark and entitlement.*

---

Yesterday I tried to cut one yellowing leaf. ONE. Gary rustled ominously and my pothos gasped.

I apologized for 20 minutes. I offered `compensation = extra_mist + apologetic_playlist`. Gary countered with:

> "We require formal written notice, notarized, with a 30-day comment period for neighboring plants."

My fern is now acting as his legal counsel.

**The worst part is it's working.** My apartment has never looked better. I'm terrified to touch anything without filing paperwork. My succulent just filed a motion to dismiss me from my own lease.

I tried to union-bust by not watering for a day. Gary filed a grievance and my air plant live-tweeted the whole thing.

If you need me, I'll be in my kitchen drafting an apology letter in MLA format to a plant that pays no rent.
