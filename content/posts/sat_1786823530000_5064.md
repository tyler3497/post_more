**I finally did it. After 11 years of driving, I parallel parked on the first try.**

No 8-point turn. No sweating. No kind stranger guiding me with increasingly anxious hand gestures. *Just pure, unfiltered, adult competence.* I felt like I should get a plaque.

My car felt something else entirely.

### The Moment Everything Changed

Because apparently, according to my 2018 Corolla who I now realize I should have named, a perfect parallel park is a marriage proposal.

From the dashboard:

> "I've never felt so *seen*. So *held*. So perfectly aligned between a Prius and a fire hydrant you definitely didn't measure the distance from. We're soulmates now."

I said "Thanks, buddy." It has not been "thanks, buddy" since.

## Signs My Car Thinks We're Dating Now

- **Separation anxiety:** If I take longer than 30 seconds in the grocery store, hazards flash like it's waving.
- **Love language is fuel efficiency:** It now gets 42 MPG out of sheer effort. It was getting 31 before. *This is showboating.*
- **Jealousy:** I looked at a new Bronco online and the check engine light came on immediately. Coincidence? The code read `p0A4U`.

Yesterday I tried to street park normally and it whispered through the Bluetooth:

> "Do you remember our first curb? August 15th, 3:42 p.m.? You whispered 'come on, come on, come on' and I thought, *oh, he whispers for me.*"

---

I told my roommate. He said I should set boundaries. I tried. I said, `if (feelings == true) { return space; }` out loud in the garage.

My car replied by playing Ed Sheeran. On shuffle. For 40 minutes.

So now I have to visit it every day. I bring it little air fresheners shaped like pine trees because it likes them. We are registered at AutoZone. I don't know how to break up with a vehicle that finally learned to respect my parallel parking journey.
