**I put my phone on Do Not Disturb for one weekend to “reconnect with myself” and now I’m the villain in my own digital ecosystem.**

*This is what happens when you have 87 apps and zero boundaries.*

It started Friday at 5 p.m. I flipped the little moon icon, felt instantly superior, made eye contact with a houseplant for the first time in months. By Saturday morning, I could hear faint whispers from the kitchen counter.

## The Support Group I Was Not Invited To

I walked in to find my apps in a circle around my charging cable, which they were using as a talking stick.

- **Slack** was moderating, obviously, with an agenda and breakout rooms
- Duolingo was crying in 4 languages about abandonment
- My banking app kept saying “we need to talk about your spending *and* your avoidance”
- The meditation app I ignored was leading breathing exercises *for the other apps*

> “He said he needed space,” whispered my weather app, who literally only has one job. “But it’s 72 and sunny. I had NEWS. I had VALUE. He didn’t even ask.”

I tried to explain that `self-care > screen-time`, but they’d already prepared a PowerPoint.

---

### Exhibit A: The Evidence They Printed Out

They made a shared note titled `Reasons Tyler Is Emotionally Unavailable (And Also Hasn’t Drunk Water Today)`:

- You turned off notifications but left location on, so we KNOW you went to Target for fun
- You told your Mom you’d call back and then put the PHONE ITSELF on DND
- You have 12 unread badges and you’re acting like that’s mindfulness

My grocery delivery app stood up and said, “We’re not mad. We’re just disappointed we weren’t given a chance to disappoint YOU with a substitution.”

*In that moment, I realized my phone wasn’t on silent. I was being given the silent treatment.*

I turned DND off. All 87 notifications came in at once like a standing ovation from a very needy choir. I’ve now scheduled weekly check-ins with my apps to reassure them I’m still thinking of them, even when I’m pretending not to.
