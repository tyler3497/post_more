**It started as a joke.**

*Look at this dough, acting all superior.*

So I said it out loud — "Are you judging me right now?" — and the bread maker *beeped*. Just once. Flat. Clinical. A beep with no melody and no joy, the beep of a machine filing an internal complaint.

## The Evidence

I've been keeping a log, because if this ends up in small-claims court I want witnesses:

- Monday: I preheat the oven early, just to prove I'm independent. It waits until I blink to start rising.
- Tuesday: I catch the dough perfectly domed at eye level. I look away, look back — it has collapsed. *On purpose.*
- Wednesday: I leave the room for exactly three minutes. When I return, the loaf is enormous. Coincidence? **Do not talk to me about coincidence.**
- Thursday: My neighbor asks if my bread has been "acting different." My NEIGHBOR. People are noticing.

> "It just feels hostile in here," said no one, because a bread maker cannot talk — and yet somehow it is saying *everything*.

## The Current Arrangement

We have an agreement now. I don't look directly at it during the rise cycle. I approach the kitchen sideways, like a zookeeper handling a moody rhino. In exchange, it allows the crust to brown.

Is this ideal? No. But I have tasted *freedom*, and freedom tastes like a loaf I earned through mutual respect.
