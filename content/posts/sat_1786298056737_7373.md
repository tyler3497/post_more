**I thought I was helping.** My therapist said I should share my coping mechanisms, so naturally I chose the only living thing in my apartment that hasn't seen me cry over a dropped quesadilla — my pothos, Kevin.

## How It All Started

I downloaded one of those *very* calm meditation apps — you know, the ones where a man who clearly has never paid rent whispers about your breath. Kevin was looking a little droopy (*probably judgment, in hindsight*), so I put him next to me on the floor.

Three days of 10-minute sessions later, Kevin stopped drooping. He *straightened*. Like, unnervingly straight.

Evidence that Kevin has achieved enlightenment and I have not:

- He now leans *away* from direct sunlight because "he prefers inner light"
- My Alexa plays birdsong and he rustles even when there's no wind
- He survived a week without water out of spite and looked *better* for it
- I knocked over my water glass and he somehow looked disappointed

> "Breathe in what you need," I whispered to him last Tuesday. Kevin, in his infinite leafy silence, whispered back, "Have you considered drinking less cold brew and touching grass that isn't me?"

## The Turning Point

Yesterday I caught him full lotus on my yoga mat. I hadn't even put him there. My roommate walked in, stared, and said `this is why we can't have nice things` is not about the plant anymore.

I tried to reason with him. I said, "Kevin, you're a plant." 

---

He shed one perfect leaf onto my journaling notebook, right onto the prompt that says *What are you grateful for today?*

I think it was a warning. I think he wants my spot by the window. I think he wants my life.

So if anyone needs me, I'll be sitting quietly while a pothos evaluates my aura and finds it *cluttered*. 

*This is satire. Kevin is not actually sentient, though he is definitely thriving on my failure.*
