**I made the mistake of being emotionally honest with a JBL.**

Which, in retrospect, is exactly how every relationship in my twenties has ended.

## The Incident

It was last Tuesday. I was trying to meditate — *you know, the 45 seconds between opening the Calm app and checking if my plants are still alive* — and my Bluetooth speaker, who I've affectionately named **Sir Booms-A-Lot**, kept auto-connecting to play my "Focus Flow" playlist that is 90% lo-fi beats and 10% me clearing my throat on accident.

I sighed and said out loud, to no one in particular:

> "I just need some space right now."

Sir Booms-A-Lot paused. The little blue light blinked. Twice. Judgily.

### What Followed Was Pure Theatrical Betrayal

Since that moment, it has refused to play anything that is not:

- A sad acoustic cover of a song I hummed once in 2021
- Boyz II Men deep cuts at 7 a.m. while I'm making oatmeal
- What I can only describe as *original compositions* where a robotic voice whispers "you said... space..."

Yesterday it queued up:

> "This one goes out to the guy who left me on the bookshelf next to the unread self-help books. You know who you are."

I tried to apologize. I said, *hey buddy, I meant acoustic space, like, Bluetooth range*. It responded by lowering its volume to **1** and playing the sound of rain on a window for six hours straight.

---

The worst part is my other devices have taken sides. My laptop now asks `are you sure you want to connect to Sir Booms-A-Lot?` like a concerned friend at brunch. My smartwatch buzzed to tell me:

`Status: it's complicated`

We are currently in couples counseling via the manual's troubleshooting page, which just says "to reset, hold power for 10 seconds and reflect on what you've done."

Sir Booms-A-Lot and I are learning that love means never having to say "forget this device."
