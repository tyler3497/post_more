**I finally cleaned out my fridge, and apparently I destroyed a thriving civilization.**

For *context*, my fridge wasn't dirty, it was just... historically curated. That half-onion from March? Vintage. The takeout container I was afraid to open? A time capsule. I called it *ambient flavor aging* and my roommate called it `if (smell === true) { leave; }`.

Saturday, I snapped. I put on gloves, turned on a podcast about decluttering, and evicted everything with freezer burn or emotional baggage.

That's when the condiments started talking.

## The Condiment Cinematic Universe

My fridge door has, like, 14 condiments. They've been back there so long they've formed a community council:

- Sir Ketchup, who has been 5% full since 2024 and is somehow still confident he'll be needed
- Mustard, who is *aggressively* Dijon and won't stop bringing up France
- Mayo, who is just trying to hold everyone together, literally

They were not grateful for the clean.

> "And on today's episode of *Cold Takes*, we discuss our human's decision to throw away a perfectly good expired yogurt that we had named Jeffrey," whispered Ketchup directly into my brain at 2 a.m. "Our thoughts on abandonment issues, plus, is cilantro a conspiracy? Stay tuned."

---

## My Official Response

I tried to reason with them. I explained food safety. That yogurt had achieved sentience and was applying for its own zip code.

The hot sauce bottle, who is basically Joe Rogan in glass form, chimed in:

> "So you're telling me, bro, that you threw away science? That yogurt was evolving. That was free probiotics and maybe a new life form."

*Which is not how probiotics work, and also not how roommates work, Carla the hot sauce.*

Look, I get it. I disrupted their ecosystem. But I cannot live in a museum of sauces that have formed a union and started monetizing my poor decisions on Spotify.

I left them one pickle. As a peace offering. They're now calling it their intern.

This is satire. 100% fictional parody. No condiments were harmed, but several were shaken vigorously and told to get a real job.
