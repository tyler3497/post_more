**I just wanted one win this week.** My apartment was a mess, my inbox had 47 unread newsletters titled "quick question," and my houseplant had already filed for emotional distance.

So I ironed my socks. *Just to feel something.*

## How It Started

It was supposed to be a one-time thing. A little self-care. A little "look at me, I have my life together" performance for an audience of zero. I put on a podcast about minimalism, plugged in the iron, and pressed one lonely argyle sock like it was a couture gown.

It felt amazing. For exactly 90 seconds.

Then the other socks noticed.

> "We have convened an emergency meeting of the Drawer Collective and we will no longer be folded under these oppressive conditions," said Sir Cushion the Third, my formerly favorite striped sock. "We demand steam equity."

I thought he was joking. *He was not joking.*

## The Demands

By morning, they'd pushed a tiny manifesto out from under the dresser:

- No more being ironed after 9 p.m. without 24-hour notice
- One full day off per week where we just get to be balls of lint in peace
- A 15% increase in drawer space, effective immediately
- Recognition that the left sock is NOT "just the other one" — he has a name, it's Kevin

And then came the contract.

```
if (human.ironingBoard === true) {
```

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered fabric softener. They countered with a request for a tiny pension plan and a drawer-side humidifier.

My therapist says this is projection. My socks say this is *collective bargaining*.

I haven't worn matching socks in three days out of fear of crossing the picket line. Yesterday I went to work in slides out of solidarity.

*Satire — obviously. No actual socks were harmed, unionized, or consulted in the making of this essay. I should probably just do laundry instead of anthropomorphizing it.*
