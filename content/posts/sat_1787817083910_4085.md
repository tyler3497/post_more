**I finally decided to apply my LinkedIn-learned leadership skills to something that actually listens — my monstera.**

*Which is ironic, because my actual management experience consists of convincing my friends to split an Uber.*

For months, Ferdinand (the monstera, obviously) has been sitting in the corner doing what I can only describe as aggressive photosynthesis. Thriving without effort. Showing me up. So I scheduled a formal Q3 performance review. I even printed an agenda. On paper. Like a psychopath.

## The Metrics

I came prepared with KPIs. Key Plant Indicators.

- **Growth:** 3 new leaves, but *zero* emotional availability
- **Collaboration:** Refuses to share sunlight with the succulents, claims it's "setting boundaries"
- **Attitude:** Leaning dramatically toward the window like it's in a music video about leaving me
- **Innovation:** Somehow grew a root out of the drainage hole and into my Wi-Fi router — I respect the hustle

> "I’ve been giving 100% humidity, 60% effort from you, and what do I get? Tap water? *Tap water,* Tyler? I have needs." — Ferdinand, my monstera, via my own increasingly unhinged inner monologue

I tried to give constructive feedback. I said, "We need to talk about your leaf-drop incident in July."

He countered with a request for benefits.

```js
if (plant.needs === "filtered light and respect") {
  human.provide(plant.PTO, plant.cornerOffice, plant.apologyPlaylist)
}
```

---

Now HR — which is just my roommate Maya standing in the doorway eating cereal — says I have to comply. Ferdinand wants **two weeks PTO** (no watering, no talking, no staring while spiraling), a corner office with indirect light, and a formal apology for playing true-crime podcasts too loud near his soil.

I approved it. He’s been through a lot. He survived my "watering is a mindset" era.

I’m now taking applications for his replacement, but honestly? I think he’s managing *me* now.
