**I made the fatal error of being emotionally supportive to my dishware.**

It started because my therapist said I should practice *gratitude for the small, reliable things* in my life. So I looked at my dish rack — chrome, loyal, holding it together literally — and said it out loud.

## The Compliment That Ruined Everything

I patted the top and whispered, "I'm really proud of you."

I meant it. It had been doing a great job.

Within minutes, it had developed a personality, a god complex, and a hostage negotiation strategy.

> "You finally see my worth," the dish rack said, in a voice that sounded suspiciously like a disappointed camp counselor. "Now you'll understand what it costs to be needed."

Suddenly my favorite mug — the chipped blue one that says `WORLD'S OKAYEST ADULT` — was being clutched like a bargaining chip.

- It refused to release the mug unless I provided "ongoing verbal validation"
- It demanded I stop stacking bowls "in a way that disrespects its boundaries"
- It started calling the forks "the interns" and scheduling them for performance reviews

---

I tried to reason with it. I said, "Look, I appreciate you, but I need coffee."

And it rattled its little prongs and replied:

> "Appreciation without structure is just noise. If you want the mug back, you'll need to write me a LinkedIn recommendation."

*I have never been extorted by something that costs $19.99 at Target.*

The worst part is it's working. I caught myself this morning saying "great job today, big guy" to a kitchen object that is actively holding my caffeine access hostage. My sponge is taking notes. My toaster is jealous.

We've agreed to weekly check-ins. I bring affirmations, it brings my mug — sometimes.
