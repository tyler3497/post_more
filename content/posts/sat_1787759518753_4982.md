**I finally decided to clean up my 47,000-photo camera roll.** *That was my first mistake.*

It started innocently enough. A Sunday afternoon, cold coffee, a burst of optimism that could only mean I was avoiding real responsibilities. I told myself I'd be a responsible adult and organize seven years into cute little folders. What I found instead was evidence.

## The Evidence File

My phone became a courtroom exhibit with worse lighting:

- 2019 Me: making sourdough *before* it was a personality, wearing jeans that actually fit, hiking *voluntarily* without being bribed by brunch
- 2021 Me: learning ukulele for exactly three days, buying a houseplant and naming it after a coworker I barely knew
- 2023 Me: 400 screenshots of recipes I never made, each captioned "definitely this week" in my notes app
- 2026 Me: 2,800 identical photos of my cat sleeping in slightly different light, each one *essential* apparently

> "You used to be fun," whispered my 2019 self from a photo where I'm holding a festival wristband and a haircut that was 90% optimism. "When did you become a guy who photographs his own lunch like it's a crime scene?"

### The Lawsuit

By Tuesday, my past selves had unionized.

My 2019 self filed a formal complaint for emotional distress. 2021 self is acting as very underqualified legal counsel. They allege I have caused *irreparable harm* by letting my camera roll become a digital attic of broken promises and blurry concert videos.

Their settlement demands include:

- Immediately deleting `if (photo.isBlurry && photo.isImportant == false) { deleteForever(); }`
- Finally posting the 2022 vacation pics I've been "editing" for four years
- Admitting that I never needed 19 photos of the same sunset

---

**Now I'm in court-ordered mediation with my own Google Photos.** My therapist says this is growth. My phone storage says this is a crisis. Either way, I'm preemptively counter-suing my 2027 self for whatever embarrassing hobby he's about to pick up next.

*Satire / Parody: This is 100% fictional. No actual selves were harmed in the making of this camera roll.*
