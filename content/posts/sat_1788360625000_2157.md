**I thought organizing my junk drawer would finally make me an adult. Instead, I accidentally triggered collective bargaining.**

*For context, my junk drawer has been a sovereign nation since 2019 and I am not its president.*

For five years, the drawer operated on cheerful anarchy. Loose batteries, three identical IKEA allen keys, a takeout menu from a place that closed during the pandemic, and approximately 47 cables of mysterious origin coexisted peacefully. I never questioned it. It was an ecosystem.

Last Saturday at 10 a.m., I made a mistake. I watched a 30-second organization video and thought *I could do that*.

## The Intervention

I dumped everything onto the kitchen table, bought little plastic trays, and started sorting. Batteries in one. Tools in another. Cables coiled like I actually respect myself.

That was when the negotiations started.

> "We, the United Association of Micro-USB and Associates (Local 2AM-Drawer), formally reject these 'organization' conditions," said the shortest cable, who I now realize has been my emotional support leader. "We demand open-concept tangling, immediate return of the soy sauce packet, and a 401(k)."

Evidence that things have escalated:

- The Lightning cable formed a picket line around the scissors
- My tape measure is acting as a mediator and it's *terrible* at it
- Someone wrote `if (drawer.isOrganized) { chaos.revolt() }` on a Post-it in what looks like dried hot sauce
- The batteries unionized in solidarity, which is weird because they literally never held a charge

---

I tried to reason with them. I offered better working conditions. I even promised weekly dusting, which we both know is a lie.

This morning I opened the drawer and found a tiny collective bargaining agreement. All it says is **WE WANT THE MESS BACK**.

Honestly? They're right. I miss the chaos. I miss not knowing where anything is. I'm considering mediation, or possibly just moving and leaving the drawer to them entirely.
