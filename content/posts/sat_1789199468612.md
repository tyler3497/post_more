**It started innocently enough.** All I did was install a dimmer switch. Three days later, my ceiling fan had opinions about *ambiance*.

## The Crime Scene

I walked in after work to find the overhead light off, the floor lamp on its lowest setting, and the fan blades turning at what I can only describe as a *smoldering* pace. The fan had somehow rearranged nothing but made everything feel like I was about to be double-crossed.

- The blinds cast venetian-shadow stripes across the couch like I owe someone money
- My cat walks around like she knows something she isn't telling me
- The fan hums in a low register now, like it's narrating my life in third person
- There is a permanent patch of cold air in the corner I have decided not to investigate

> "It was a Thursday. The kind of Thursday that smells like rain and reheated soup. She didn't knock. Fans never knock."

That's the fan. I didn't write that. *The fan wrote that.* I found it on a sticky note under the remote.

## The Tipping Point

Last night the fan started oscillating my curtains in a slow, deliberate rhythm during a thunderstorm, and I swear the whole room whispered *shhhh* in unison. I have begun wearing a trench coat indoors. I have started referring to dinner as "the case." My houseplants now look like witnesses in a lineup.

---

I tried to reset the mood by turning the overhead light back on. The bulb blew instantly. The fan kept spinning. It doesn't need the light anymore. **None of us do.**

This is satire. My ceiling fan is a ceiling fan. But somewhere between the dimmer switch and the denouement, my living room became a detective's office, and I am just an extra who pays the mortgage.

Case closed. Fan still open.
