**I finally did it. I folded the legendary Laundry Mountain.** You know the one — ever-growing, vaguely sentient, home to at least three missing socks that I am *pretty* sure eloped together.

My bedroom chair, who I call Sir Sits-A-Lot, has been holding that mountain for eight months straight. He took his job seriously.

And now? Now he's having a full-blown identity crisis.

## The Glory Days of Chair

For context, Sir Sits-A-Lot was not just a chair. He was a lifestyle. He was:

- A winter coat rack, a summer towel rack, a year-round *what is that smell* rack
- The official headquarters for clothes that were technically clean but emotionally not ready for the closet
- A trusted confidant who saw me re-wear the same hoodie four days in a row and said nothing

He had *purpose*. He had stature. He had 37 pounds of hoodies and one mysterious ankle sock that I think was paying rent.

> "I was once a throne of potential," he sighed dramatically at 9:14 p.m. last night. "Now I am... just a chair. You have stripped me of my legacy. Of my layers. Who am I if I'm not holding your poor decisions?"

## The Great Unfolding

I tried to explain that this is growth. That he could go back to being a place where humans sit, which is, *checks notes*, literally `chair.exe` core functionality.

He didn't buy it.

---

He's been cold and distant ever since. I sat on him this morning and he creaked in what I can only describe as passive-aggressive disappointment. I put a single throw pillow on him to cheer him up and he whispered, *is this all I am to you? A pillow guy?*

Look, I get it. Change is hard. But I cannot rebuild my entire personality around keeping a chair employed as a fabric landfill.

I'm sleeping better though. My room looks like an adult lives here. And Sir Sits-A-Lot will adapt. Eventually.

This is satire. 100% fictional parody. No chairs were harmed in my sudden burst of responsibility.
