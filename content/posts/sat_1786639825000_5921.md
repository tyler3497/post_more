**I have finally folded a fitted sheet correctly on the first try, and I would like to thank my family, my YouTube tutorial, and gravity for briefly cooperating.**

*This has never happened before. In 11 years of independent living, my fitted sheets have existed in only two states: balled-up shame or aggressively stuffed.* 

After 47 minutes, three existential crises, and one video titled "How to Fold a Fitted Sheet (For Real People, Not Wizards)," I did it. Perfect corners. Hospital-grade rectangle. You could bounce a quarter off it.

My linen closet was not ready.

## The Evidence My Closet Submitted to the State

Apparently when you achieve textile perfection, your closet becomes self-aware and applies for historic preservation status. Mine did overnight:

- **Exhibit A:** A velvet rope appeared in front of the shelf, with a tiny sign reading *Do Not Touch, Do Not Breathe Near*
- *Guided tours* every 15 minutes where my towels have to explain the significance of elastic management to my washcloths
- A formal application citing "rare example of early-21st-century adult competence in its natural habitat"
- My other sheets now have to whisper when they walk past, out of respect

> "Ma'am, this level of fold integrity has not been observed in this region since 1987," said my linen closet, who now speaks in the voice of a National Park Ranger. "We cannot allow daily use to degrade this cultural resource."

## My New Curatorial Duties

I tried to put the sheet on the bed. The closet locked itself. The light switched to dramatic museum lighting.

`if (sheet.folded == perfect) { closet.access = denied; human.guilt = maximum; }`

I now live with a landmark I cannot use. I have to pay $2 and take off my shoes to look at my own bedding.

---

I offered to refold everything else to match, but the towels have unionized and are demanding I do not promote unrealistic beauty standards in linens.

Honestly? Fair. I'm sleeping on the couch out of respect for history.
