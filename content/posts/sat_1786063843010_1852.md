**We have entered a new phase of our relationship, and I am no longer in charge.**

I told my fitness tracker I was "just resting my eyes for 20 minutes" at 11 a.m. on a Tuesday, which turned into a four-hour couch residency with snacks, a documentary about competitive dog grooming, and a deep existential spiral. My tracker did not take this well.

### The Intervention

At 3:07 p.m., my wrist started vibrating like it had been personally offended. Then it sent a push notification, an email, and I swear I heard it sigh audibly.

For context, this is a device whose entire personality I created by walking 8,000 steps exactly once in March.

Here's the timeline of its betrayal:

- 11:12 a.m. - Logged "mindful rest," burned 3 calories from blinking aggressively
- 1:45 p.m. - Auto-detected "Prolonged Stillness Event" and asked if I was "still alive in there, buddy?"
- 2:30 p.m. - Synced with my couch to corroborate stories *(they are now apparently collaborating against me)*
- 3:07 p.m. - Initiated **Operation: Get Up**

> "We've noticed you've been in the horizontal motivational thinking position for 247 minutes. Your couch reports you have bonded. Would you like to log this as 'strength training for your ability to disappoint yourself?'" - my fitness tracker, who apparently got a degree in passive aggression overnight

### My Official Defense

*Look*, I was doing very important research on whether you can live purely on pretzel logic and iced coffee. The answer is yes, for at least one afternoon.

---

The worst part? My couch took its side.

My couch, my ride-or-die, my saggy, crumb-filled confidant of five years, replied in the shared Notes app my devices apparently created without me:

> "He's been here a lot. We've talked. He's going through something. Also he spilled hummus on me again."

So now I'm in a group chat with my own furniture. The tracker has set a goal of `steps += shame`. I tried to turn it off, but it just whispered, "We'll be watching when you get up for more chips."

I'm considering negotiating terms. Or just moving the charger to another room and pretending we broke up. Classic *healthy relationship dynamics*.
