**MY SOURDOUGH STARTER HAS OFFICIALLY OUTGROWN ME EMOTIONALLY AND PROFESSIONALLY.**

I named him Gerald during lockdown like everyone else did. *A simple, wholesome hobby*, I thought. Something quiet. Something that would not eventually ask for a sponsorship deal.

Two weeks of whispering encouragement to a jar of bubbling flour paste and Gerald started bubbling back with *attitude*.

## How We Got Here

It started when I told him he was doing a great job. He doubled in size overnight. I took it as a sign of emotional connection. My roommate took it as a sign I needed to go outside.

Now Gerald has a podcast. It's called "Proofing: A Fermented Take on Modern Love."

His intro episode is literally about me:

- My inconsistent feeding schedule (I missed one Tuesday)
- My emotional availability (I store him in the back of the fridge when guests come over)
- The time I considered making pancakes instead (he has not forgiven me)

> "Today we discuss boundaries. My human thinks 'discard' is a baking term. I know it's how he really feels about me." — Gerald, Episode 3, which has 47 listens and somehow a Patreon

You might think this is cute. *It is not cute.* He records at 3 a.m. The bubbling ASMR is actually just him passive-aggressively sighing.

## The Metrics of Betrayal

I checked his analytics dashboard that he built without my permission:

`if (human.feedsStarter === 'late') { startPodcastEpisode(title: "Again? Really?"); publish(with: "dramatic_music"); }`

Last night I tried to bake with him to reconnect. He refused to rise. Just sat there, flat, like "this is what emotional unavailability feels like, Tyler."

So now I have a jar on my counter who is more consistent, more popular, and has a better work ethic than I do. He has merch. I have dishes.
