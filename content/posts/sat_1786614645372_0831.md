**I finally decided to be the kind of person who owns a doormat.** Not an ironic one. A sincere, adult, woven-fiber *WELCOME* in nice serif letters.

That was Monday. By Tuesday, my front door had developed full-blown stage fright.

It started with a creak. Not the normal creak. This was a nervous, *please-don't-look-at-me* creak.

## The Symptoms That My Door Is Now Method Acting

- It only opens halfway, like it's peeking out from behind a curtain at an audience
- It whispers to me before I turn the knob: *are they nice? Do we like them?*
- It refuses to open at all if the delivery guy makes eye contact with the mat
- The peephole now counts as "pre-screening for emotional safety"

> "You put PRESSURE on me," my door hissed last night when I tried to have a friend over. "You literally laid out a red carpet that says PERFORM. I was a private door. An introvert door!"

*I told it that most doors would love a little validation.*

I tried to explain that the mat was just... a mat. A $24 attempt to hide the stain where I spilled iced coffee in July 2024.

## My Delusional Conflict Resolution Plan

I positioned myself as a mediator. I told the mat to tone it down, told the door to breathe. I even tried:

`door.confidence = Math.max(door.confidence, mat.welcome_level * 0.3)`

It did not compile. Emotionally or literally.

---

Look, I get it. For three years this door has been closed to the world, successfully avoiding trick-or-treaters, neighbors, and my own commitment to going outside. Now suddenly it's expected to be *welcoming*?

We are in couples counseling with the hallway light. It flickers supportively.

If you come over, please wipe your feet quietly and tell my door it looks great. *It needs this.*

*SATIRE / PARODY — entirely fictional, no doors were emotionally harmed, my doormat is just aggressively optimistic.*
