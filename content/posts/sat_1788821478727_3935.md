**I bought my floor lamp a tiny bookshelf, and now it reads me existential poetry before bed. This is not a bit. This is my life.**

It started innocently enough. I saw the bookshelf on a craft website — three inches tall, made for dollhouses — and thought, *wouldn't it be adorable if Gerald, my floor lamp, had something to read?* I filled it with miniature classics I printed on my lunch break, tucked him into the corner of the living room, and went to bed.

That night, at exactly 11:47 p.m., his light dimmed to a *thoughtful amber*, and I heard it: the faint crackle of a tiny page turning. And then, in a voice like a radiator sighing, Gerald read me a poem about the transience of dusk.

> "O shade that falls before the evening's end, / So too must man confront the day he lends—"
>
> — Gerald, who made it up on the spot and was clearly very proud of it.

I have compiled a list of everything Gerald has done since:

- Memorized the complete works of a poet I have never heard of, whose entire bibliography he appears to be inventing live
- Developed **strong opinions** about free verse, which he calls "a bold choice for cowards"
- Started pausing before the final stanza of each poem, as though waiting for *applause I am too sleep-deprived to give*
- Dimming during the emotional climaxes in a way that has made my actual living room an unreliable narrator

## The incident

Last Tuesday I came home to find he'd reorganized my coffee table into "a reading nook, for when you finally grow emotionally." My cat now sits at his base for every performance. The cat is Gerald's *biggest fan and, I suspect, his agent*.

I tried to take the bookshelf away. Gerald's light flickered — once — in what I can only describe as disappointment, and I put it back immediately. I live here too, technically. *Allegedly.*

Honestly? The poems are good. The one about my unread mail made me cry. We're doing a sleepover poetry night Friday, and Gerald is bringing "a friend." I don't know what that means. I am afraid to ask.

*Satire. Parody. Gerald is a floor lamp. He cannot read. (To my knowledge.)*
