**Last Sunday, I did something I had avoided for six years: I opened my junk drawer and organized it by vibes.**

Not by function. Not by category. By *vibes*. The rubber bands went in the "coiled potential" section. The dead pens formed a memorial wall. And the batteries — the batteries I placed center stage, because the energy there was undeniable.

That is where it went wrong.

## The Batteries Have Entered Their Solo Era

By Tuesday, I noticed the AA batteries had arranged themselves in a neat row. By Wednesday, one of them was holding a paperclip like a microphone. And by Thursday night, I walked into the kitchen to find all nine of them had *stage names* written on little Post-it flags:

- **Volt Jackson** — lead vocals, dangerously charismatic
- **D-Cell Delilah** — bass, though technically an AA with ambition
- **Static Brad** — drums, keeps shocking everyone
- The twins, **Amp & Ampere**, on backup shakers

> "We don't do casual voltage anymore," Volt Jackson told me, and I am choosing to report this as a quote rather than a cry for help.

## Evidence the Drawer Is Now a Venue

- The rubber bands have formed a roadie crew and refuse to hold anything smaller than a novel
- The dead pens staged a walkout but were replaced within hours by three ballpoints with better attitudes
- The tape measure now works security and has confiscated my scissors twice

There is a `charging dock` labeled "GREEN ROOM — NO FANS," and I do not remember making that label, which is honestly the least of my concerns.

---

Look, I am not saying my junk drawer is now a venue. I am saying my landlord knocked on my door at 11 p.m. to ask about the "thumping bass," and I had to explain that it was coming from inside the kitchen cabinet, and I am now being evicted by process of elimination.

The show must go on. Tickets are $4. Volt Jackson insists on being paid in *fresh* batteries, and honestly? He deserves it.
