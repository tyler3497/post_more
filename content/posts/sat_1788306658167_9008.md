**I did not expect my healing journey to come with a lease amendment.**

Last Tuesday, in a fit of *very healthy* regression, I decided to build a pillow fort in my living room. Not an ironic, Instagram-ready blanket drape — a full, structurally ambitious, engineering marvel with load-bearing throw pillows and a fairy-light chandelier.

It was supposed to be about inner child stuff. You know, reclaiming joy. Instead, it reclaimed 40 square feet of my apartment and immediately became the most expensive real estate I've ever owned.

## The Fortification Process

I followed no plans, only vibes. The build was emotional:

- The north wall: three couch cushions and a weighted blanket I got on sale and never emotionally recovered from
- The south wall: an existential barrier made of laundry I *will* fold later
- The ceiling: a queen-size sheet that has seen things, held up by two broom handles and pure delusion
- Amenities: one (1) juice box, string lights, and a no-adults-allowed sign I took very seriously

It was perfect. It was cozy. It had better natural light than my actual bedroom.

> "Sir, this unpermitted structure violates Section 4B of your lease regarding 'additional dwellings, tents, and whimsical architecture,'" — my landlord, who materialized like Beetlejuice the second I got comfortable

Apparently, he does quarterly inspections via binoculars from the parking lot.

### The Assessment

Yesterday I got an envelope taped to the fort. Not to my door — to the *fort*. Inside: a handwritten invoice.

`fort_rent.calculate(sqft=40, vibe=100, audacity=MAX) // returns: "$175/month + utilities + emotional labor"`

He's now listing it on Zillow as a "curated studio nook with ambient lighting and low ceilings for the vertically humble."

---

*This is satire and entirely fictional — no pillow forts were taxed in the making of this post, although my landlord did ask if the fairy lights were up to code and I told him they were certified by my inner child.*

I’ve decided to stay in the fort until further notice. The rent in here is free, the landlord can’t legally enter without a secret password, and honestly? The property value of whimsy has never been higher.
