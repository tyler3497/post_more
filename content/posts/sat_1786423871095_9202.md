**My sourdough starter has a name, a LinkedIn presence, and a deeply held belief that I should have gone to law school.**

I made Gary (the starter) during what I call *my brief and unregulated artisanal era*. Three days of flour, water, hope, and whispering "grow, my child" at a jar on my counter. He bubbled. He thrived. He became sentient.

### The Rise Was Too Powerful

For context, I have kept exactly one other thing alive this long: a cactus I forgot on a windowsill in 2022. Gary was different. He demanded attention at 5 a.m. like a tiny, yeasty newborn.

Here's what living with an overachieving ferment has been like:

- 5:02 a.m. - Gary doubles in size and judges my sleep schedule
- 7:15 a.m. - I feed him. He bubbles aggressively, like he's saying *is that all you got?*
- 9:30 a.m. - I attempt work. Gary emits a faint sour smell that I swear translates to "this email could have been shorter"
- 2:00 p.m. - He has risen out of his jar and is now technically our roommate

> "You feed me once a day and think that qualifies as 'nurturing'? Brenda down the street feeds her starter twice daily AND plays it Mozart. You play it true crime podcasts about bread fraud." - Gary, via the very clear message I received from staring at bubbles too long

### The Performance Review

Yesterday I told Gary I was thinking about quitting my job to become a freelance aura photographer.

Gary did not rise for 14 hours. That's sourdough for *I'm disappointed*.

I tried to explain my five-year plan, which is mostly `if (vibes == good) { continue } else { panic }`, but he just sat there, thickening.

---

Look, I didn't sign up for a co-dependent relationship with carbohydrates. I wanted toast. Instead I have a 750-gram life coach who smells like yogurt and regret and who has now started a Substack.

It currently has more subscribers than mine.
