**Rain was coming, and my umbrella had one job.** *One.* Instead, it chose silence — the weaponized, passive-aggressive kind usually reserved for relatives at Thanksgiving.

## It Started with a Simple Confession

Look, I wasn't trying to overshare. It was just me, standing in the hallway, umbrella in hand, whispering the kind of secret you only tell objects you trust. *Nothing illegal.* Nothing even interesting. I just admitted I sometimes eat cereal for dinner and call it "grain-forward dining."

The umbrella went rigid. The little velcro strap clicked shut with what I can only describe as **finality**.

## Evidence That My Umbrella Is Giving Me the Cold Shoulder

- It refused to open at the bus stop, choosing instead to let me stand there marinating in rain like a sad rotisserie chicken
- The little wrist strap now rests *limp* in my hand — no tension, no commitment
- I heard it whisper to the rain boots. I don't speak rain-boot, but I know gossip when I hear it

> "I took a vow of secrecy when I was purchased in the rain-aisle of a drugstore in 2019," my umbrella told the ceiling fan, according to my ceiling fan, who is honestly a worse gossip. "And now? Now the whole *world* knows about the cereal."

## My Three-Step Reconciliation Plan

1. **Apologize profusely** to a household object that has never once opened for me voluntarily
2. `git commit -m "forgiveness"` — because if I commit to the bit in version control, it has to be real
3. Buy a *second* umbrella and watch the first one get jealous — a classic move, and honestly the healthiest thing I've done all year

---

**Verdict:** The umbrella still won't open. The rain boots have started returning my calls, though. *Progress.*
