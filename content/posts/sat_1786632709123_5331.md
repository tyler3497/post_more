**I decided to treat my seedlings like coworkers because a podcast told me plants grow faster when you talk to them.**

*It did not mention the seedlings would immediately adopt corporate culture and start making demands.*

I have eight seedlings. Basil, cilantro, two tomatoes who think they're in charge, and four mystery greens from a free seed packet that just says "herb (?) let's go!"

I was lonely working from home. So I started giving them morning stand-ups.

### The Meeting Notes

By day three, Basil had promoted itself to Team Lead.

My seedlings now hold me accountable with:

- **Daily check-in at 9 a.m.** sharp, where I have to describe my goals and they just sit there photosynthesizing *judgmentally*
- *Water cooler talk* has literally become just talking near the watering can
- They requested a **hybrid work model** — half windowsill, half grow light, with "flex Fridays"
- Tomato #1 sent a formal complaint that I'm "micromanaging sunlight"
- The mystery herb started using phrases like `let's circle back on root development`

> "We've noticed you've been out of office without updating your status," whispered Basil last Tuesday. "We need you to be more... present. Also, our soil is dry. This is a blocker."

### The Performance Review

Yesterday they scheduled a 1-on-1. With me.

> "Your watering cadence is inconsistent," said Cilantro, who has been alive for exactly six days. "And your playlist during watering? Very lo-fi beats to neglect roots to."

---

I tried to explain I'm new to management. I tried `git commit -m "please grow"` and hoping for the best.
