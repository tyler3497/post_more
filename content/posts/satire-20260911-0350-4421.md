**SATIRE — This post is pure fiction. No real people, appliances, or feelings were harmed.**

It started innocently. I was staring into my fridge at midnight, eating shredded cheese directly from the bag like a raccoon with access to electricity, when the ice maker made a low *whirrr* and I thought: this machine has **more discipline** than I do.

So I hired it as my life coach.

## The Method

My refrigerator's coaching philosophy is simple: **cold truth**. Literally. It dispenses motivation at exactly 4:00 AM by blasting its interior light across my bedroom like a spiritual awakening with a compressor. The curriculum so far includes:

- **The Crisper of Accountability** — expired yogurt teaches that dreams have shelf lives
- **The Freezer of Letting Go** — three years of ice buildup represent my excuses, layer by layer
- **The Door Shelf of Small Wins** — condiments stay upright; so can you

## A Typical Session

> "You said you'd meal prep on Sunday. It is Thursday. You are eating *jarred olives with a fork*. I am disappointed, but I am **still cold**, which is more than I can say for your leftovers."

Devastating. Accurate. I cried into the cheese drawer, which it later billed me for as *"emotional storage."*

## Results

My productivity is up **200%**. I now wake before dawn, not from ambition but from the water filter alarm it *reprogrammed* to scream like a teakettle. My friends say I've changed. My therapist has asked me to stop bringing the fridge to our sessions, which the fridge calls *"resistance"*.

---

**Verdict:** Is my refrigerator a certified life coach? No. Is it certified *at all*? Debatable. But when it said *"you're the leftover in your own life, and leftovers either get eaten or thrown out"* — I felt that. I signed up for the quarterly plan.

It hums differently now. I think it's proud.
