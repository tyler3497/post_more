**I have always believed I was a visual person.** Which is how I justified spending four hours on a Saturday reorganizing my entire bookshelf by color instead of, you know, reading any of them.

*My books did not consent to this.*

## The Vision

In my head it was going to be very Instagram, very mature-adult-who-has-their-life-together. A tasteful rainbow gradient from red to violet, perhaps with a tasteful plant on top. Clean. Serene. The kind of shelf that says "I make good decisions."

In reality, I created a small, silent civil war:

- My sad beige memoirs are now trapped between aggressively neon self-help and a cookbook called *Chili Night!*
- My Russian classics formed a support group on the yellow shelf and refuse to acknowledge the thrillers
- My copy of *Dune* is orange and therefore living with diet books, which feels like a personal insult to Frank Herbert
- I put all my unread books together and they immediately started judging me in unison

> "We used to be organized by *ideas*," whispered my philosophy section, which is now scattered across three colors because apparently philosophers can't agree on a spine color either. "Now I'm next to a book about sourdough. This is not what Kant died for."

## The Aftermath

I tried to find my favorite novel yesterday. I knew where it *used* to be — second shelf, left side, next to the other anxious paperbacks. Now it's sorted by `hex code #4A6741` and I need a search warrant to locate it.

---

**The worst part is I can't switch it back.** My bookshelf has seen how good it looks for photos. It has tasted aesthetic approval from my one visitor who said "oh cute." If I move a single book back to its old, *logical* place, the whole rainbow collapses like a Jenga tower of literary insecurity.

So now I live in a home where I browse by color like I'm picking out M&Ms. *Very intentional. Very curated. Completely unusable.* I opened a blue book looking for comfort and got a manual on cryptocurrency.

*SATIRE / PARODY — totally fictional, I still love my chaotic, color-coded, emotionally distant books.*
