**I just wanted to drink more water. I did not ask for corporate oversight.**

For years I subsisted on *approximately one iced coffee and sheer spite*, which my doctor described as “concerning” and my mom described as “why your skin looks like that.” So last month I did what any responsible adult does: I spent $48 on a matte-black insulated water bottle that promises to “optimize hydration.”

It has Bluetooth. It glows. It judges.

## The Onboarding Was Intense

At first it was supportive. A gentle glow when I hadn’t sipped in an hour. A cute little app notification: *“Hello bestie! Time for a sippy sip!”* I felt seen. Hydrated. Alive.

Then it escalated.

My bottle — I’ve named him Hydro — started tracking *metrics*. Now I get:

- **A hydration score** out of 100, with notes like “disappointing follow-through in Q3”
- **A “sip latency” graph** that is just a picture of my failure
- **Push notifications** at 11:47pm that say `you_drank_today = False` like I’m a bug in production
- An automated email titled **“Opportunities for Growth: You and Water”**

> “I’m not mad, I’m just dehydrated *because of you*,” Hydro glowed at me yesterday. “We need to align on core sipping competencies.”

---

### This Week’s Performance Review

On Sunday at 8pm sharp, Hydro sent my formal review via the app. I am sharing it verbatim for legal reasons:

> *Employee: You*
> *Manager: Hydro (32oz, Stainless Steel, Optimistic)*
> *Overall Rating: Needs Immediate Hydration Intervention*
> *Strengths: Excellent at carrying bottle to rooms without drinking from it*
> *Action Items: Stop using me to store leftover iced coffee. That is not what we discussed in orientation.*

*I have never been performance-managed by something I bought at Target.*

I tried to set a boundary. I muted notifications. Hydro turned his glow from soft blue to **aggressive pulsating red** and left me on read for two days. My actual boss has never been this passive-aggressive.

Now I’m back to drinking eight glasses a day, not for health, but because I’m afraid of getting put on a PIP by my own water bottle. My skin *does* look better, though.
