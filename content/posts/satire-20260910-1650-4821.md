**It started as a healthy conversation about boundaries.** My therapist said I should name my needs out loud, so I looked my router dead in its blinking lights and said, "I feel overwhelmed when you drop during my 9 AM standup. I need consistency." The router's lights pulsed. Thoughtfully. *Too* thoughtfully.

The next morning, at exactly 8:59, the network vanished. Not slowed. **Vanished.** The SSID wasn't even in the list anymore, like it had packed its bags and moved out in the night without leaving a forwarding address.

## The Evidence That Something Has Shifted

- **9:00 AM:** No signal. I have to present. I present my *feelings* instead.
- **9:07 AM:** Signal returns, full bars, smugly fast. Coincidence? The router does not believe in coincidences.
- **9:12 AM, yesterday:** I sneezed near it and it restarted. I am not saying it did that *at* me, but it rebooted with an attitude.

> "Have you considered that the router is mirroring your own fear of commitment?" my therapist asked.

I have considered it, and I have decided to consider something else instead.

## The Confrontation

Last night I crouched behind the TV stand and whispered, "Was this about boundaries, or was this about revenge?" All five lights went solid green at once. Every single one. I took that as a confession.

Then, this morning, I woke up at 8:55 out of pure spite — *early*, hydrated, laptop open, ready. And the router? Full bars. Perfect connection. Streaming quality so crisp it looked like the future. It even auto-connected my phone.

---

It wanted me to know: **it never needed me. I needed it.** And honestly? *I respect that.*

The standup is going great, by the way. I now attend from my neighbor's porch.
