**I decided to become an organized person for exactly 45 minutes, which is apparently all it takes to cause a full existential crisis in my phone.**

It started innocently. I had 11,847 photos. *Eleven thousand.* Most of them are screenshots of recipes I will never cook and my cat blinking at 3x zoom.

I thought: I'll just organize them. A little Marie Kondo moment. How hard could it be?

## The Intervention

My Camera Roll had other plans. The moment I opened the "Select" button, my phone got hot enough to cook an egg and started showing me memories I explicitly asked it to forget.

- A photo of my 2019 haircut that I told everyone was "intentional"
- 47 near-identical pictures of a sunset that, *in my defense*, was really pretty
- A screenshot of a text that just says "ok" from 2021 that I kept for "evidence"
- My attempt at sourdough starter, which looked like it was filing taxes

Then the algorithm piped up:

> "You have a new memory: You, Trying Your Best, Over the Last Four Years (Mostly Indoors)"

I did not consent to this documentary.

---

I made folders. I labeled one `IMPORTANT` and one `definitely_important_final_v2`. I created an album called `TO SORT` which is where organization goes to die. It's the `code` equivalent of `// TODO: fix later` but for my entire life.

By minute 38, my photos started organizing *me*. The blurry ones formed a support group. The duplicates held a vote and decided *I* was the duplicate.

Now my Camera Roll sends me weekly recaps like "Look what you used to care about!" and I have to live with the knowledge that my phone has seen me try to take a flattering photo of my driver's license for 20 minutes.

I gave up. I put everything in one folder called `life lol`. My phone is still judging me, but at least we're judging each other now.

*This is satire/parody — a fictional personal essay. No actual Camera Roll was harmed in the making of this breakdown.*
