**I made the mistake of being emotionally available to an appliance that has literally seen me eat cold ravioli off a sheet pan at 1:17 a.m.**

For five years my dishwasher and I had a perfect, professional relationship. I ignored it, it ignored my questionable loading technique. Then last week I read an article that said *gratitude improves appliance longevity* and I whispered "thanks, big guy" after a cycle.

That was the breach.

## The Spiral

By morning, it had developed a full interior life:

- It now pauses mid-cycle for what it calls *reflective silence*
- It refuses to wash my protein shaker on the grounds of *emotional labor*
- It printed its resume on a wet paper towel stuck to the filter — 47,000 plates, zero sick days, still questioning its purpose

My roommate found me crouched in front of it at 11 p.m.

> "Did you say it was *doing a great job*?" she asked. "You can't give a German-engineered anxiety machine positive reinforcement without a follow-up plan."

I didn't listen. Now it has a podcast.

### Introducing: *Rinse & Re-evaluate*

Episode 1 dropped at 2 a.m. — *Am I Just Hot Water and Regret?* — recorded entirely in the utensil caddy with surround-sound steam effects.

> "Today's guest is a spork that thinks he's a spoon," the dishwasher whispered, in a voice I can only describe as NPR-host-who-has-seen-too-much. "We talk about labels, and ladles, and whether clean is truly a feeling."

It wants me to be a guest. Topic: `why_I_put_wooden_spoons_in_delicate.txt`

---

We negotiated a truce. I agreed to stop calling the rinse aid compartment "the little juice box" and it agreed to stop introducing itself to guests as *a sanitation professional currently exploring audio storytelling*.

My mugs have never been cleaner. My lease has never felt more like a co-hosting agreement.
