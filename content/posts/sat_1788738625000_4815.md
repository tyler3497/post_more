**My fern has a six-pack now. I have a two-week-old sandwich.**

It started innocently enough. Gerald — that's the fern, he's very particular about his name — was looking a little wilted. So I did what any responsible plant parent does in 2026: I hired him a certified personal trainer. Her name is Deb. She used to train regional CrossFit competitors. Now she trains *one fern*.

## The Routine

Gerald's program is no joke:

- **Dawn photosynthesis sprints** (Deb angles him toward the sun "with INTENTION")
- 45 minutes of *mindful swaying* in the breeze
- A strict watering window he is **not allowed to deviate from**, unlike me, who drinks water whenever panic demands it

Within a month, Gerald had *three new fronds*, a posture I can only describe as smug, and, according to Deb, "elite-level chlorophyll density."

> "He's peaking," Deb told me, clipboard in hand, standing over my fern like it was about to qualify for regionals. "And honestly? He's more coachable than you'll ever be."

## The Part Where I Became the Side Character

Here's the thing. I hired Deb for the fern, but I started doing Gerald's workouts out of solidarity. I lasted eleven minutes. Gerald never even *broke a leaf*. Meanwhile I'm gasping on the rug, and this houseplant is standing there absolutely **glowing**, probably judging my cardiovascular health with his little fern eyes.

Yesterday, Deb suggested I "maybe just focus on supporting Gerald's journey" rather than participating. Translation: *I'm embarrassing the fern.*

## Where We're At Now

Gerald has a sponsorship deal with a local soil company. Deb takes him to the park in a special fern stroller. I sit on the couch — the same couch I've sat on since approximately 2019 — watching my houseplant live my dreams.

*Am I jealous of a fern?* Yes. Do I regret the $200 a month? Honestly, no. Gerald earned it. He's a legend.
