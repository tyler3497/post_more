**My Wi-Fi router filed a formal noise complaint against my microwave last Tuesday, and honestly, I saw it coming.**

They've shared the same outlet strip for three years, and the tension has been building. Every time I reheat leftovers, the router drops the signal for exactly two minutes and forty seconds. My video calls have started preemptively muting themselves out of *fear*.

It began innocently enough. The router started blinking amber, which I had always filed under "just router things," until my twelve-year-old nephew — fluent in the emotional lives of electronics — identified it as **distress**. So I did what any rational adult would do: I drafted a formal complaint on its behalf.

## The Incident Report

The evidence was damning:

- **47 dropped connections** during pasta reheats alone
- One firmware update that downloaded at dial-up speeds because the microwave was "defrosting something important"
- A Bluetooth speaker that now plays lo-fi jazz whenever the microwave door opens, purely as a coping mechanism

> "I cannot live like this anymore," the router blinked at me, in what I have decided is Morse code for *I'm in crisis*. "It hums at a frequency that scrambles my very soul. I have dreams. I have a 5GHz band and a dream."

(I may have named the router Gerald. We don't need to dwell on that.)

## The Counseling

Their couples counselor is a retired clock radio with excellent boundaries. Session one, he asked each of them to name one thing they appreciated about the other. The microwave said the router had "strong, consistent energy." The router said the microwave "knew what it wanted." I cried a little.

---

Here's the thing, though: since the counseling started, my reheated coffee stays warm *and* my calls don't freeze. Was it the counseling, or was it the power strip I finally replaced during a fit of clarity? I genuinely do not care. They signed a shared-outlet agreement — initialed in blinking lights — and my apartment has never been more peaceful.

*This is satire. No appliances were harmed. One was emotionally validated.*
