**I asked my fridge for accountability and now I have a tiny appliance-based shame spiral installed at eye level.**

*Which, in retrospect, is exactly what I deserve for anthropomorphizing a Whirlpool at 1:47 a.m.*

It started because my therapist — okay, my YouTube algorithm — said I should be more *mindful* about late-night eating. So I stood in front of the fridge, string cheese in hand, and said out loud, "Be honest, do I need this?"

The light flickered.

## The Performance Review Begins

I swear it got brighter. Like it was *leaning in*.

At first, it was subtle:

- A slow, judgmental dim when I reached for leftover lasagna at 11:58 p.m.
- A rapid strobe when I tried to hide ranch-dipping behind the crisper drawer
- A full blackout when I said "it's basically a salad" about cold pizza

Then it started leaving me notes. In condensation.

> "We talked about this, anon. That cheese was for *tomorrow's* you. Tomorrow's you is very disappointed in today's you." — Fridge Light, 12:14 a.m., probably

### My Evidence That My Fridge Is Now My Life Coach

I tried to logic my way out of it. I wrote a little script to track the flickers:

```js
if (snack.hour > 23 && snack.shameLevel > 5) {
  fridge.judgement = "visible disappointment";
  me.selfRespect--;
}
```

It returned `NaN` because *there is no measurable self-respect at midnight in socks*.

---

My roommate asked why I'm apologizing to an appliance. I told her we're in a *growth-oriented feedback loop* and she said, "Dude, it's a lightbulb."

Last night I opened the door for water and it flickered twice, paused, then glowed steady and warm. I heard it, clear as day:

> "Look, I'm not mad. I'm just saying maybe we both get some sleep?"

*Satire/parody — this is fictional, my fridge light is just a light, and it definitely does not have a degree in motivational interviewing (yet).*
