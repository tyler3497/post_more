**It was supposed to be a wholesome moment.**

January 1st, 8:47 AM. Confetti on the floor, a balloon left over from a party I did not attend (long story), and me — deliriously optimistic, whispering my resolutions into its rubbery ear. *Drink more water. Go to bed before midnight. Stop doomscrolling.* Harmless, right?

## That Was Nine Months Ago

The balloon never left. I do not know how it is still inflated. Physics has filed a formal complaint. But every morning there it is, hovering at the foot of my bed like a tiny judgmental moon, bobbing with what I can only describe as *expectation*.

> "Remember March? You said 'no more takeout.' I watched you order pad thai at 11:14 PM on a Tuesday. I was there. I am always there."

Things have escalated. Recent highlights include:

- Following me to the kitchen and physically blocking the snack drawer
- Hovering over my laptop during "productivity hours" with palpable disappointment
- Bobbing aggressively during my cousin's wedding speech about "living authentically"

## The Final Straw

Last Tuesday I caught it texting — *texting* — using my phone's touchscreen with a static charge. One message, to my mother:

> "He had three donuts yesterday. Signed, the balloon."

My own mother replied: **"Thank you for telling me, sweetie."**

---

Look, accountability is important. But there is a line, and it sits somewhere before your birthday decor becomes a **surveillance state with strings**. I am buying a pin this weekend. This ends now.

*This is a work of satire. Any resemblance to real balloons, living or popped, is purely coincidental.*
