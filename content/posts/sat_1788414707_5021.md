**I never intended to give Harold this much authority, but here we are.**

Three weeks ago, in a moment of *profound personal growth* (or laundry avoidance), I decided my hamper needed a purpose beyond shame containment. I gave it a promotion.

## The Performance Review I Didn't Ask For

At first, it was inspirational. Harold - yes, I named him, that's where this started going wrong - just sat there, holding my clothes with dignity. Then he started coaching.

Here's what Harold has flagged in my Q3 life audit:

- 14 identical black t-shirts that are somehow all "slightly different"
- A sock pairing strategy best described as `anarchy.sort(() => 0.5 - Math.random())`
- One heroic sock that has survived 7 washes without its partner, which Harold calls "resilient but codependent"
- My declaration that "laundry day is self-care" while wearing inside-out pajamas for 48 hours

> "Tyler," Harold whispered through the wicker, "you have 23 socks and 4 matches. This isn't a wardrobe, it's a cry for help disguised as a textile situation."

I tried to explain that mismatched socks are *intentional, eclectic, a lifestyle*.

Harold was not impressed.

---

He now requires me to present my outfit choices for pre-approval each morning. He has implemented a **three-sock maximum** for emotional support and insists I journal about why I own a shirt that says "I Paused My Game to Be Here."

*Is this what accountability feels like?* I thought coaches were supposed to be supportive, not passive-aggressively folding my fitted sheet better than I ever could.

The worst part? He's right. My socks *are* a mess. And somehow, folding laundry at 11pm while apologizing to a basket feels like progress.

This is satire, by the way. Harold is a hamper. I am apparently very easy to coach.
