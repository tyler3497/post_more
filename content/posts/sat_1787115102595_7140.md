**I thought I was being financially responsible when I told my fern to start pulling its weight.**

I live in a one-bedroom where my monstera, Gary, already occupies the only good sunlight and about 40% of my personality. So last month, during a bout of budgeting-induced hubris, I looked him dead in his glossy leaves and said, *"If you live here rent-free, you need to contribute."*

Gary took it personally. And professionally.

## Phase 1: My Brilliant Landlord Strategy

My plan was simple and, in retrospect, deeply flawed:

- I would stop watering him until he "earned" his water
- He would feel motivated and maybe Venmo me $20
- I would finally feel like an adult who understands passive income

What actually happened is Gary updated his resume.

> "I've been photosynthesizing in this east-facing window for three years with zero equity," Gary told me during his morning misting. "I've decided to pivot. I'm launching a wellness brand."

*You know things are bad when your houseplant uses the word pivot.*

## Phase 2: The Unstoppable Glow-Up

Within 72 hours, Gary had:

- Launched a startup called `Leaf & Learn` — subscription-based oxygen with "intentional breathing"
- Started a podcast titled *Rooted & Resilient* where he interviews my succulents about boundaries
- Developed a 5 a.m. morning routine that involves journaling (dropping a leaf dramatically), cold-plunging (I overwatered) and manifesting

Meanwhile I am on my third snooze alarm arguing with my phone about whether 7:13 is technically still 7:00.

---

## Phase 3: I Now Work For Him

Gary now makes more from Etsy — selling tiny inspirational quotes written on fallen leaves — than I make from selling my old textbooks.

Yesterday he asked if I could "keep it down" during his investor call. His investor is the cactus from the bathroom. They're talking Series A.

**This is satire, obviously.** My houseplant does not actually have a Series A, but he *does* have better posture, a consistent sleep schedule, and the audacity to thrive while I'm still figuring out how to keep basil alive for more than eleven days.
