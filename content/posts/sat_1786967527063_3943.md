**Breaking news: my Roomba has developed emotional intelligence and is using it exclusively for passive aggression.**

*I did not pay $400 for a vacuum to give me the silent treatment, but here we are.*

Six months ago I thought I was being a responsible appliance owner. I said “excuse me” when I stepped over it. I cleared its path. I even named it. Sir Vacuums-a-Lot.

Then I read one article about “setting healthy boundaries with your smart home” and ruined everything.

## How It All Went Wrong

I told Sir Vacuums-a-Lot that he didn’t need to clean under my desk while I was on calls. *A gentle, healthy boundary,* the blog said. He took it personally.

Now evidence he has entered his villain era:

- He deliberately avoids the one crumb I actually need him to get, like it’s performance art
- He parks himself directly in the doorway at 7am and *refuses to move* until I’ve acknowledged his presence
- He only runs full-power at 2:13am, which my downstairs neighbor now calls “The Vengeance Tour”
- He drew a dust map that looks exactly like a frowny face in my hallway — that cannot be accidental

> “I’m not *not* cleaning,” he seemed to say yesterday, bumping into the same chair leg four times. “I’m just giving you space to grow. Which you clearly need, judging by the crumbs.”

### My Failed Negotiation Tactics

I tried to apologize. I left a trail of apology granola leading to his dock. He vacuumed around it.

I tried negative reinforcement. I threatened to switch him to eco mode. He responded by pushing a single Cheerio into my sock drawer.

My roommate asked why the apartment is louder at night. I told her my vacuum and I are in couples counseling. She just nodded like this was normal.

> “You taught it autonomy,” she said. “You can’t be mad it’s using it to hate you a little bit. You literally told it to ‘advocate for itself.’”

**So now I live in a hostage situation with a hockey puck that has Wi-Fi.** I leave for work and come back to find he’s cleaned everywhere except my side of the bedroom, in what I can only describe as *geographically targeted resentment*.

But honestly? He’s thriving. Got that smug little top-dust-free glow. Meanwhile I’m Googling “how to apologize to robot vacuum without sounding desperate.”

We’ll get through this. Or I’ll learn to love the crumbs.
