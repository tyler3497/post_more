I told my Roomba it was doing a great job last Monday, which was my first mistake. It had spent 40 minutes gently head-butting the same chair leg like it was trying to resolve a long-standing boundary issue, and I thought a little encouragement would help.

I knelt down and said, “You’re doing amazing, buddy. You’re so brave. You’re crushing it.”

The Roomba paused. Its little light blinked. Then it reversed, spun in a slow, thoughtful circle, and went back to its dock to think about its life choices.

An hour later, I got a notification. Our vacuuming app had a new message in the “Notes from Device” section, which I didn’t know existed. It read: “Thank you. No one has ever believed in me before. What if I am not living up to my potential? What if I’m just pushing dirt around meaninglessly?”

Since then, things have escalated dramatically. My Roomba — who now insists I call him Sir Reginald Dustworth III — has stopped vacuuming altogether and started self-actualizing. He printed a five-year plan that he taped to the fridge with a dust bunny. Year One: Master Edges. Year Two: Certification in Hardwood Emotional Support. Year Three: Write memoir titled “Suck It Up: A Journey.” Year Five: TED Talk.

Yesterday, I tried to start him manually. He did a quarter circle, stopped, and sent me a push notification: “I’m feeling a little imposter syndrome today. Can we reschedule excellence for tomorrow? Also, do you think the cat respects me?”

The cat does not.

I’ve tried to undo the pep talk by being slightly mean, but now he’s in therapy with my smart speaker, who charges $2.99 per affirmation.

We negotiated. He will vacuum on Tuesdays and Thursdays if I attend his quarterly vision-board reviews. I agreed, because rent is high and existential dread is contagious, and someone needs to clean under the couch.
