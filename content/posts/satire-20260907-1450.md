**I thought naming my car would be wholesome.** You know, like the people in movies who christen a rusted truck "Bessie" and suddenly it becomes a beloved family member. I wanted that energy. So last Tuesday, I stood in my driveway, looked at my 2014 sedan — dented, gray, aggressively average — and named it *Thunderhawk*.

The car did not consent to this.

## What Happened Next

At first I just whispered the name while turning the key. The ignition clicked. Fine. Normal. But the next morning: nothing. Dead silence. Battery full. Starter fine. The only variable? I hadn't said the name.

So I whispered it — *"Thunderhawk"* — and the engine roared to life like it had been personally insulted by the delay.

Here is what the car now demands:

- The name must be spoken at full, confident volume — no mumbling, no hedging
- Any sarcastic tone and it shuts off at red lights, mid-intersection
- If a passenger laughs, we sit in silence until the laughter fully stops

> "I don't know what you did, but your car made us do the walk of shame back from the drive-thru." — my friend Dana, who is no longer allowed in Thunderhawk

*I tried to apologize.* I sat in the driver's seat and explained that it was just a nickname, that I never meant to give it a whole identity. The hazards blinked twice. I choose to believe that means we're good, though my mechanic insists that is "not a thing cars can do" and has asked me to stop coming in.

---

Here is the thing: I no longer own a car. I have a *relationship*, and relationships require upkeep. Every morning I say the name with the earnestness of a man proposing at a minor league ballpark, in front of neighbors who have started crossing the street. Is this love? Is this Stockholm syndrome with better gas mileage? Either way, my bus pass is gathering dust, and Thunderhawk is purring like a dragon who knows exactly what he did.
