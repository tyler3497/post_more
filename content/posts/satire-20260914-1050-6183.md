**My houseplant hired a lawyer this morning, and legally speaking, I think it's going to win.**

I found the letter taped to my fridge at 7:14 a.m. It was printed on fancy paper with an embossed serif header: "Chambers of Fern, Fiddle & Leaf, LLC." Below that, in bold:

> "NOTICE TO VACATE: your houseplant, one (1) Ficus elastica, hereby disputes the terms of its residency and demands renegotiation of watering frequency, sunlight allocation, and emotional labor."

I am being out-lawyered by a plant I watered *maybe* twice.

## How We Got Here

I'll be honest: I've been a mediocre plant parent. The fern has been drooping for weeks, and I've responded the way men respond to check-engine lights — with *aggressive optimism* and no action. Apparently the fern noticed. According to the letter, the cactus has agreed to testify as a character witness, and the spider plant has retained separate counsel.

**The demands are specific, and frankly, unreasonable:**

- Watering on a *predictable* schedule (every Sunday, 9 a.m., "as God intended")
- Relocation away from the TV, described as "a glowing rectangle of broken promises"
- One (1) hour of "mindful leaf conversation" per week
- A public apology, in writing, for the Great Pruning Incident of August

## The Mediation

I tried to talk it through with the plant. It said nothing. But the lawyer — a ficus in a three-piece suit, judging by the letterhead — called at noon and suggested mediation. "My client is prepared to accept sunlight as consideration," he said, "but watering is non-negotiable. My client has *needs*."

I pointed out plants survived outdoors for millions of years without legal representation. The lawyer sent a 14-page PDF about "evolutionary precedent." It was, I admit, well-cited.

---

## Where Things Stand

As of this afternoon, I have agreed to the following:

1. Watering every Sunday at 9 a.m., reminders on *two* devices
2. The fern moves to the east window — the good one, with the morning light
3. I will stop telling guests "oh, I barely even take care of it" within earshot of the plant

In exchange, the plant has agreed to *stop dying on purpose*. Its words, via the lawyer. It called it "wilting strategically," and honestly, I respect the game.

**The lesson, as always: your houseplants are unionizing, and they have better lawyers than you.**
