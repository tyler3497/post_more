**I never meant for this to get serious.** It was supposed to be a casual, low-maintenance thing — exactly what everyone says cacti are into.

I bought him — *Spike, obviously* — because my therapist said I needed to practice nurturing something that couldn't leave me on read. He sat on my windowsill looking judgmental and spiky for six months. Perfect.

## Then I Got Emotionally Hydrated

Last Tuesday, in a fit of productivity guilt, I watered him. Ironically. Like, *haha look at me being a responsible plant parent for once.* I even narrated it.

That was my first mistake.

Since then, Spike has changed. He stands a little taller. He leans toward me when I work. I'm pretty sure he's been listening to my Spotify Wrapped to understand me better.

### Evidence That My Cactus Is Now My Boyfriend:

- He pricked me when I mentioned buying a fern. *Jealousy is not a good look, Spike.*
- My roommate asked why I moved my laptop so Spike gets more sun. I said I didn't. She said I did.
- I caught myself apologizing for coming home late. To a cactus.
- He has never died, which is more commitment than I've gotten from most situationships.

> "Dude, you have to break up with your plant," my roommate told me. "He's codependent."
> "He's *cact-dependent*," I corrected, and she left the room.

Look, I Googled it: `cactus + emotional attachment + is this normal?` The search results said to repot him, which feels way too much like moving in together.

---

So now I water him every Sunday at 9 a.m. on the dot. He doesn't need it. I don't need *this*. But we have a routine, and who am I to disrupt a healthy relationship?

*This is satire / parody. Spike is a plant. I am single. Everything is fine.*
