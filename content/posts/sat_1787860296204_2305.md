**I finally found a way to make my procrastination productive, and now it wants to be my business partner.**

For years, I've been trying to get rid of it. Pomodoros. Planners. Those YouTube guys who whisper *just do it* while drinking expensive matcha.

Nothing worked. So I pivoted. *Classic startup logic.*

## The Pivot

If you can't beat your procrastination, incorporate it.

I made a Notion page called **Procrastination Inc.** with a mission statement and everything:

- Delay tasks strategically to "maximize ideation runway"
- Research niche Wikipedia articles for 3 hours as "competitive analysis"
- Reorganize desk as "optimizing operational infrastructure"

Investors would *love* this, I thought.

### The Meeting

Then last Tuesday, my procrastination manifested as a small, judgmental blob in a blazer sitting on my laptop keyboard.

> "Hey, so I saw the deck. Love the vision. Quick note: if I'm doing 90% of the actual work — which is, technically, *not* doing work — I'm going to need 40% equity, full health coverage, and a LinkedIn endorsement for 'Strategic Task Deferment.'"

*Sir, you are literally the reason I missed my dentist appointment.*

I tried to negotiate:

- I offered exposure.
- He offered to push my deadline to Q3 2027.
- I countered with a firm handshake.

He left a performance review on my fridge.

```
STATUS: Founder is motivated but easily distracted by literally anything
Q3 GOAL: Touch grass, maybe answer one (1) email
RECOMMENDATION: Promote procrastination to COO
```

---

Look, this is **satire**. *Obviously* my procrastination isn't a legal entity. The IRS told me LLCs can't be formed out of pure avoidance and leftover pizza.

But this morning I found a LinkedIn notification:

> **Procrastination Inc. endorsed you for 'Getting Around To It Eventually'**

And honestly? That's the most supportive feedback I've gotten all month.

So fine. We're co-founders now. Same time tomorrow? He said he'll pencil it in. *Eventually.*
