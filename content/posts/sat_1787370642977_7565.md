**I finally folded my laundry the same day I washed it.**

You have to understand — this has never happened before in the recorded history of my apartment. My personal record was three weeks, with an honorable mention for that hoodie that lived on the dining chair from March to June and officially became a decorative throw.

*It was a Tuesday. I had iced coffee. I was invincible.*

## The Incident

At 2:14 p.m., fueled by delusion and unfiltered daylight, I took the warm clothes out of the dryer and — instead of performing the sacred ritual of dumping them on The Chair — I folded them. Like a functioning adult. Corners matched. Socks paired. I even did that weird department-store fold for my t-shirts that makes you look like you work retail.

What I didn't account for was the politics:

- My couch has been using The Chair's overflow as a status symbol since 2022
- My bed was emotionally prepared to be 60% clean laundry / 40% actual bed
- My floor had just accepted it would never see that one missing sock again
- The Chair itself was counting on that pile for lumbar support

> "We had a system," my couch whispered, in that low, judgmental velvet voice. "You were the 'chair-drobe' guy. You were relatable. Who even *are* you now?"

My couch hasn't made eye contact since.

### The Fallout

Now my apartment is too clean and everyone is uncomfortable. The closet said:

> "Great, so you're just going to put things where they belong now? What's next, using coasters? Respecting expiration dates?"

I tried to explain this was personal growth, but my laundry basket filed for unemployment. Its LinkedIn now says `open to work | laundry-adjacent opportunities | basketweaving specialist`.

---

Look, I get it. I disrupted the ecosystem. That chair pile was load-bearing, both structurally and emotionally. But for one brief, shining afternoon, I knew where *all* of my socks were.

I'm not saying I'll do it again. *I'm saying my couch should learn to support me in my journey.*
