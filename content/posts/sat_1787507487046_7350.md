**I didn't ask for a junk drawer.** It just happened to me, like adulthood.

For seven years, I've had a kitchen drawer legally classified as a black hole. Dead batteries, soy sauce packets from 2019, three IKEA Allen keys that have never met their furniture, a single dice from a board game I don't own. You know the one.

On Saturday, I decided to become a better person and clean it out.

## Mistake #1: Acknowledging It

I dumped everything onto the counter and said out loud, *wow, I really need to deal with you.*

The drawer, apparently, was listening.

I found:

- 11 chapsticks, 0 with caps
- a receipt for a 2021 rotisserie chicken I'm not ready to discuss
- a screw that's clearly structural to *something* in this house
- a loyalty card for a frozen yogurt place that closed during Obama's second term
- 2 ketchup packets that have achieved sentience

I made little piles like a person with their life together. I bought a $12 bamboo divider. I felt powerful.

> "Oh, so NOW I'm worth organizing?" said the drawer, in a voice that was both my mother and my landlord. "After seven years of you YEETING expired coupons into me like I'm an emotional landfill?"

### The Invoice Arrived at 2:14 AM

Subject line: `INVOICE #0001 - UNPAID EMOTIONAL LABOR (2019-2026)`

Line items:

```
- Holding takeout menus while you ordered delivery anyway: $45
- Pretending not to notice you cried over a spatula: $80
- Storing 7 years of "I'll fix it later" screws: $120
- Therapy for the bent tape measure: Priceless
```

Total due: **$847.50** or one sincere apology and a label maker.

---

I tried to dispute it. I ran `drawer.refund({reason: "I was young"})` — auto-reply: `REQUEST DENIED - See attached trauma.`

Look, I used it as a junk drawer, but to the drawer, it was a *memory palace.* Every mystery key was a promise I didn't keep.

So I paid it. Not in money — in respect. I now call it the **Multipurpose Heritage Collection Drawer.** It has a little sign. We're in counseling.
