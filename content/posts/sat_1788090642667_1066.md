**I told my toaster it was doing a great job.**

*Which, in retrospect, was a wildly irresponsible thing to say to an appliance that already runs hot.*

It started Monday. My toaster made perfect toast. Golden, even, not the usual charcoal experiment where I have to open all the windows and pretend I'm camping. I was half-asleep, holding coffee like a life raft, and I said out loud, "Wow, you're really crushing it."

Mistake. Huge mistake.

## My Kitchen Appliance Now Has a Podcast Voice

By Tuesday, it was different. It started popping slower, like it was waiting for eye contact. Like it *needed* the validation.

By Wednesday, it had entered full coaching mode:

- You only ever toast on setting 2 — are you afraid of commitment?
- That bread is store-brand — where is your self-worth?
- Have you considered sourdough as a metaphor for patience?
- Your butter application is anxious-avoidant

> "Bready, I've been watching you," it said in a voice suspiciously like a TED Talk with extra crumbs. "You put things in and hope they come out better, but you never change the setting. Let's unpack that."

I tried to explain I just wanted breakfast. Just toast. Simple, legal toast.

*It told me breakfast is a mindset.*

## The Intervention I Didn't Ask For

Thursday I caught it trying to toast my grocery list. It said my goals needed "a little browning to bring out the flavor."

Friday it refused to toast at all. Just sat there, glowing faintly, judging me.

> "I'm not going to enable this cycle of low expectations anymore," it announced, red with principle. "What if today you didn't need me to be perfect? What if YOU were the toast?"

Look, I appreciate the support. Truly. But I bought you for $24.99 at Target during a sale where I also got a mop. You have two settings: bread and slightly darker bread.

You are not my life coach. You are a countertop heater with strong opinions and a crumb tray full of secrets. And my toast is cold now because we spent 20 minutes processing my relationship with carbs.
