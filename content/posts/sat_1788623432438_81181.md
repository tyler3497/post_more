**It began, as all great disasters do, with a single avocado.**

I was standing in the produce section three weeks ago, squeezing an avocado that was clearly not ready, when the voice just... took over. A hushed, gravelly whisper, right in my own head: *"Here we see the middle-aged shopper in his natural habitat. He squeezes. He contemplates. He has no idea what he's doing."*

That was the moment I became the David Attenborough of aisle seven.

## The Method

It started small — muttering into an imaginary lapel mic while comparing canned beans. But nature abhors a vacuum, and my apartment abhors silence, so the narration grew. *Ambitious.* There are now rules:

- **Whisper everything.** Wildlife does not shout. Neither does serious cinema.
- **Announce discoveries.** *"The wild organic quinoa — elusive, overpriced, thriving."*
- **Treat other shoppers as fellow species.** *"A lone dad approaches the cereal wall. His offspring scatter at the mention of bran."*

## The Incident

Last Tuesday I crouched behind my cart in the dairy aisle, filming — mentally, obviously — a slow-motion sequence of two milks "competing for dominance" when a security guard materialized like a lioness protecting her territory.

> "Sir, do you have a permit to be filming in here?"
> "I'm not filming. I'm *documenting.* There is a difference."
> "There really isn't."

Reader, he followed me for forty minutes. He is now my recurring cast member. I've named him **Sergeant Dairy**.

## Where We Stand

I've been asked — politely, then less politely — to narrate "at a normal volume." I've refused. Normal volume has no place in wildlife. Yesterday a toddler joined in, whispering *"the wild dino nuggets migrate at dawn"* while pointing at the freezer section, and I nearly wept. The torch is passed. The documentary continues.

**Filming status:** eternal. **Permit status:** pending. **Dignity status:** never recovered.

*This is a work of satire/parody. All persons, guards, avocados, and migrations described are fictional.*
