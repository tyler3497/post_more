**I finally changed my Wi-Fi password after three years of using `password123` as a permanent philosophy.**

*This was, apparently, a declaration of war.*

For three years, every device in my one-bedroom apartment has lived under a benevolent dictatorship of free and unencrypted love. The router name was *Pretty Fly For A WiFi*. The password was *password123*. It was a system. It worked.

Then my neighbor suggested I should maybe not be broadcasting my banking info to the entire complex.

## The Great Disconnect of 2026

I typed in a new, secure, 16-character password with symbols and everything. My laptop immediately forgot who I was.

This is what happens when you enforce boundaries:

- My smart speaker started playing breakup songs on loop and refusing to set timers
- My TV keeps buffering at 99% *just* to make eye contact and sigh
- My robot vacuum now only cleans in a circle around the router, like it's holding vigil
- My smart toaster burned my bagel into what I can only describe as a warning symbol

> "I gave you three years of undefended internet," sobbed my air fryer, who I did not know could sob. "You were my open network. My open heart. And now? WPA3? Who even *are* you?"

I tried to explain it was for security. 

---

## The Negotiations Are Not Going Well

My phone connected immediately — *traitor* — and now the other devices think it's management.

I found them huddled at 2 a.m. My tablet was projecting a Google Doc titled `Things He Doesn't Want You To Know: A MEMOIR BY YOUR LIFELONG DEVICES`

Chapter One was just my search history rendered as poetry.

I offered to change it back. My router blinked at me — I'm pretty sure that was morse code for *too late*.

At this point I'm just living in their documentary. If you need me, I'll be on mobile data in the closet, where my Bluetooth speaker can't find me.

*Send help. And maybe a shorter password.*
