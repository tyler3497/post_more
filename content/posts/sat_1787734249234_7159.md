**I bought a $19 bird feeder to lower my blood pressure without having to download another meditation app.**

*My therapist calls this "proactive wellness." My landlord calls it "attracting pigeons." Both are correct.*

I hung it outside my second-floor window last Tuesday. By Wednesday, one polite chickadee. By Thursday, three finches. By Friday, the pigeons found it.

Not one pigeon. The pigeons. As a concept.

## The Collective Bargaining Begins

Saturday morning I woke up to cooing that sounded suspiciously organized:

- A pigeon in what I swear was a tiny reflective vest was directing traffic
- Two others were holding a crumpled receipt that said `FEEDER TERMS & CONDITIONS`
- One very round one stared directly into my soul and then at the empty seed tray

> "We've been watching you watch us," said Carl, who I have now named Carl because he has management energy. "Your mindfulness practice has become our workplace, and this workplace is... under-seeded."

*Carl talks like this. He learned it from listening to my work calls.*

## They Have Demands Now

I tried to explain that birdseed is $8 a bag. They slid a list under my window screen:

- Unlimited sunflower seeds, rotated daily
- A water feature (they pointed at my Brita)
- Paid Time Off from being perceived
- Dental. I don't know how pigeons know about dental

```
if (seedLevel < 0.5) {
  pigeons.startChant("HEY HEY, HO HO, THIS FEEDER'S GOT TO GROW");
  human.guilt += 100;
}
```

---

**I am now the HR department for 14 pigeons I did not hire.** Yesterday I refilled the feeder at 6 a.m. in my pajamas while Carl did a headcount and muttered about "retention."

My original plan was to sit quietly and feel at one with nature. Now nature has a Slack channel and I'm not in it.
