**I thought I was just defrosting peas. I was actually dismantling a criminal empire.**

*Which, in hindsight, explains why the frozen waffles looked so nervous.*

Last Sunday I decided to be an adult and finally organize my freezer. Not for health, not for efficiency — my roommate said if he found one more unlabeled soup, he was moving out.

I pulled everything out, scraped off the permafrost, and sorted it like a responsible person. That's when I heard them.

## The Confession

My two ice trays, Brenda and Steve, had been living quietly for three years. Apparently too quietly.

> "You weren't supposed to see the bottom shelf," whispered Brenda. "We've been protecting Mr. Peas since 2023. He saw things."

Mr. Peas is a bag of peas. *He is literally 40% freezer burn at this point.*

Reasons the freezer needed witness protection in the first place:

- The waffles testified against the toaster last summer
- The ice cream pint overheard my midnight snack confessions and knows too much
- I once stored garlic bread next to the ice trays and *they still haven't forgiven me*
- Someone — not saying who — left the door open for 8 minutes in July and they had to rebuild their lives

I tried to reassure them. I said:

`if (freezer.organized == true) { anxiety = 0; }`

It didn't compile. Emotionally.

---

## Now I Have to Live Like This

Brenda now insists on being called "B. Cubed" and only freezes distilled water from a mountain spring I do not own. Steve wants a new identity in the fridge door, *where it's warmer and the hum is less judgmental.*

I have labeled everything, alphabetized everything, and promised I will never buy bulk spinach again. The peas are in protective custody behind the vodka — which is also in on it.

My roommate asked why the freezer is empty except for two ice trays wearing tiny tinfoil hats.

I told him it's a long story.

*This is 100% satire and parody — no ice trays were harmed, relocated, or entered into actual federal programs. My freezer is just messy and dramatic, like me.*
