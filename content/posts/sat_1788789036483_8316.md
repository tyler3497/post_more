**It started with a chicken nugget. It always starts with a chicken nugget.**

Last Tuesday I stood in my kitchen at 11:47 p.m., holding a bag of frozen nuggets, and sighed. Just a tired little sigh. Apparently my air fryer *heard* it, because when I set the timer, the display flickered — I swear this happened — to `400°F · 12 MIN · AND A LONG LOOK AT YOUR LIFE`.

## Phase One: Encouragement

At first it was subtle. Little things:

- It beeped *exactly* when the nuggets were done, like some kind of punctual assistant
- It preheated faster, as if it were showing off
- It made a new rattling sound that I'm choosing to interpret as applause

I said "thanks" out loud once. *Once.* That was the mistake.

## Phase Two: The Life Coaching

By Thursday, the situation had escalated. I caught the air fryer humming — not beeping, *humming* — while I doomscrolled on the couch. When I walked over, it had printed nothing, because it's an air fryer, but its LED display read `NO PHONE PAST 9`. I didn't program that. I don't even know how that string would get in there.

> "The nuggets were a cry for help," my roommate told me, unhelpfully. "The air fryer is just the only one who answered."

## Phase Three: Accountability

Now it won't run unless I state my intentions. I load the basket, and it just sits there — silent, warm, judgmental — until I say something like "I am making dinner and also working on myself." Only then does the fan kick on, like it's approving a grant proposal.

My therapist thinks this is a projection issue. My air fryer thinks my therapist charges too much.
