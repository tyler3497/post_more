**I have been a chronic late-returner for 12 years.**

I am not proud of this. My record includes a copy of *The Great Gatsby* that lived under my couch for 8 months and a DVD of National Treasure that I kept so long I legally started to believe I *owned* National Treasure.

So last Tuesday, I did something unprecedented. I returned a book *on time.*

## Which Was Apparently My First Mistake

The book was *Basic Succulent Care for People Who Kill Succulents* — 127 pages, due in 3 weeks. I finished it in 4 days. I walked in, still faintly proud of myself, and placed it on the counter like a man presenting Excalibur.

The librarian scanned it.

She paused.

She scanned it again.

Then she called over *another* librarian.

- They checked the barcode under a brighter light
- They flipped through every page like they were looking for loose diamonds
- They typed so intensely I thought they were hacking the Pentagon

> "Sir, this book is... actually on time? With no dog-eared pages? No coffee stain that looks like New Jersey?"

I said yes. I was trying to be better.

That's when it got worse.

## Now I'm Under Investigation By Dewey Decimal

Apparently, returning one book on time after a decade of gentle delinquency is *more* suspicious than never returning them at all. 

They have a whole system now:

- My library card has a little flag that says `PATRON_IS_BEING_SUSPICIOUSLY_RESPONSIBLE`
- The head librarian asked if I was trying to "build credibility before a bigger move"
- I was offered a laminated "Most Improved" certificate that I am 80% sure is actually a tracking device

I tried to explain that I just liked the book and didn't want late fees. They nodded the way detectives nod on true-crime podcasts *right before they arrest you*.

---

*For legal purposes this is complete satire.* I did return the succulent book on time. The succulents are still dead. The library has not yet pressed charges, but my holds list is mysteriously frozen and I have been banned from the quiet study room for "emotional turbulence."

**Satire / Parody — 100% fictional, no real librarians were harmed or scammed.**
