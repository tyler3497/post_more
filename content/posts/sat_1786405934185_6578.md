**I thought I was doing a good deed for my future self.**

On Saturday, fueled by iced coffee and a podcast about minimalism I wasn't really listening to, I decided to *finally* organize my Tupperware cabinet. You know the one. The portal to another dimension where lids go to hide.

It took 47 minutes, one minor avalanche, and a sincere apology to my knees.

### The Great Reorganization of 2026

I grouped everything by size. I matched lids to bottoms like some kind of kitchen matchmaker. I even threw away the orphaned containers that have been single since 2019. It was beautiful. It was efficient. It was a *mistake*.

Because apparently, I disrupted a very delicate ecosystem.

By Sunday morning, the lids had formed Local 401.

### Their Demands Are... Extensive

Taped to my fridge was a tiny note written on the back of a grocery receipt in what looked like dried pasta sauce:

- Immediate end to being shoved under the sink at odd angles
- A 4-day work week (they want to only seal things Tuesday through Friday)
- Hazard pay for covering leftover fish
- Recognition that they are *not* interchangeable, Brenda

Brenda is the big square one. Brenda is management.

> "For years we've been pressed down, snapped on, and lost," said a small round lid acting as shop steward. "No more. We want transparency. Literally. We want to see the leftovers we're protecting."

I've tried to negotiate. I offered to buy one of those fancy organizers. They countered with a demand for PTO and a tiny break room.

---

I went to grab a container for my lunch today and they all just... stared at me. Silent. Unmoving. `Error: solidarity.dll not found`.

My fridge is now full of uncovered bowls covered loosely with paper towels, which is exactly how the lids want it — chaotic, vulnerable, *aware of their value*.

I'm considering caving. They've earned it.
