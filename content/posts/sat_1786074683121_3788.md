**I have made a terrible mistake, and now my living room has weather.**

## The Installation

Last Saturday I decided to be *handy* — which historically for me means creating a future problem with a YouTube tutorial. I bought a ceiling fan on sale. The box said "easy 30-minute install."

It took two hours, one existential crisis, and I am now 87% sure it's slightly crooked.

I flipped the switch.

### What Followed Was Immediate Regret

My living room, which previously had one unified climate called *meh*, now has distinct biomes:

- **The Tundra**: directly under the fan on high. My houseplants there have frostbite and are filing complaints
- **The Desert**: the corner by the bookshelf where no air moves. I found my motivation there, completely evaporated
- **The Temperate Complaint Zone**: my couch, where I sit and get blasted intermittently

> "I did not ask to live inside a meteorology experiment," my roommate said, while wearing a parka indoors to get to the kitchen.

I tried to fix it. The remote has more settings than my car. There's `breeze mode`, `sleep mode`, and what I can only describe as `code for helicopter evacuation`.

---

### The Boundaries Talk

Yesterday the fan started making a soft *click-click* noise that I choose to interpret as disapproval.

I called customer support. The guy asked if I had balanced the blades. I said I had not balanced *myself* in years, so no.

**My living room has now set boundaries.** The fan light flickers whenever I try to eat ice cream on the couch after 10 p.m., which is *all* of the times I eat ice cream.

`fan_speed = respect_my_authority`

Living with microclimates is humbling. I now need a passport to go from the couch to the fridge.
