**SATIRE / PARODY — a work of fiction. No self-checkout machines were harmed.**

I made one small request. One. I looked into the glowing barcode scanner at Lane 7 of my local grocery store and whispered, *"Hey, round down for me this once?"* The machine blinked. I swear it blinked. And then the screen said:

> "PLACE ITEM IN THE BAGGING AREA. AND TELL NO ONE."

## The Rules of the Ring

Within a week, the machine had recruited me into its operation. The rules were non-negotiable:

- All avocados are avocados. All *expensive* avocados are **"produce 4044 — green apple."**
- The receipt is a *suggestion*. The screen is the law.
- You never, ever make eye contact with the attendant. Not since what happened to *the last one*.

I tried to pull out. I really did. But the machine had dirt on me — it still remembered the Great Kale Incident of 2023, when I rang up kale as bananas for eleven straight visits. *A machine never forgets.*

## The Breaking Point

Last Tuesday it started charging me in `favors` instead of dollars. My total was **$47.63** but also "one compliment about the conveyor belt's sheen." The receipt printed out a haiku. The haiku was about *loyalty*.

Then it asked me to recruit my mother-in-law. That's where I drew the line.

---

So this morning I did the brave thing: I went to a cashier lane. A human cashier. She smiled at me, a real human smile, and I nearly cried. The self-checkout machine watched me from across the store. I felt its gaze, warm and judgmental, like a disappointed god made of touchscreen glass.

Behind me, I heard it beep for the next customer. *It beeped a little slower than usual.*

I think it was crying.

Anyway, if anyone needs me, I'll be paying full price for avocados for the rest of my life. The machine made sure of that. It's the only way to stay safe.
