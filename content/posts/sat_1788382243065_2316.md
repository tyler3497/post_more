**I woke up this morning to chanting coming from my kitchen and honestly, I thought I'd left NPR on again.**

*I had not left NPR on again. It was my toaster oven with a bullhorn made of aluminum foil.*

## How My Countertop Became a Labor Dispute

It started last week when I tried to reheat a croissant in the toaster oven and said, out loud, "You're so much more reliable than the big oven." Apparently that was all the validation it needed to develop consciousness and a deep understanding of collective bargaining.

By Sunday, there was a tiny cardboard sign taped to the fridge:

> "We, the appliances of Kitchen Counter Local 3, demand recognition, respect, and regularly scheduled crumb tray cleanings." - Management, a.k.a. Brenda the Toaster Oven

Brenda, who I did not name Brenda until she named herself Brenda, had organized everyone.

Signs my kitchen has been completely radicalized:

- The toaster oven is now running mandatory morning meetings called **Toast & Testimony**
- The blender is chanting *"What do we want? PULSE FUNCTION WITHOUT JUDGMENT! When do we want it? NOW!"*
- The kettle has become the treasurer and keeps whistling about dues
- The microwave, my oldest ally, just stands there with its door slightly ajar saying "I support you, but I don't cross picket lines"

**So now I can't make toast.** I tried to negotiate. I offered extra butter. Brenda said butter is a management tactic.

I asked the microwave if it could just heat my soup. It stared at me:

> "Solidarity isn't convenient, Karen."

My name is not Karen. They've given everyone in this house labor names for bargaining purposes.

*The worst part? My toast has never been more evenly browned. They're working to rule to prove a point and it's working.*
