**I decided my life needed order, so I started with the one place chaos had truly won: my spice rack.**

*This is the same rack where I once found a fossilized soy sauce packet from 2022 paying rent.*

For years, my spices lived in joyful anarchy. Cinnamon next to curry powder next to whatever "Italian Seasoning" actually is. It was a community. A messy, aromatic democracy.

Then I watched one organizing video at 2 a.m. and became a different person.

## The Great Alphabetizing

I took everything out, wiped the shelf, printed little labels in a font I will never use again. A for Anise, B for Basil, C for Cayenne. It was beautiful. It was *control*.

For exactly 11 minutes, I felt like a functional adult.

Then Paprika started acting weird.

### Paprika's Demands

- Paprika insisted Cumin was "too close to Coriander and it was affecting the children"
- Paprika started referring to itself as **P. Paprika, Esq.**
- Paprika demanded sole custody of the tiny empty space where we keep the good salt

> "You don't understand," Paprika said, in a voice that sounded suspiciously like my own when I'm dehydrated. "Alphabetical order is oppression. Cumin and I were *neighbors* for seven years. Now there's a wall of Cloves between us. Do you know what Cloves talks about?"

I tried to explain that Cloves is nice. Cloves makes ham tolerable. But Paprika had already hired Oregano as its lawyer. Oregano has been dried since 2019 and has nothing to lose.

Oregano presented evidence:

- Exhibit A: I put `ground cumin` under G
- Exhibit B: I alphabetized `smoked paprika` under S, separating it from its family
- Exhibit C: a `code` log of my crimes: `rack.sort() // but make it emotional damage`

---

I have now been ordered by my own spice rack to attend mediation on the kitchen counter at 7 p.m. sharp. Cumin is bringing plus-one (Turmeric, who is messy but supportive).

The rack is still alphabetized. I am still scared to make tacos.
