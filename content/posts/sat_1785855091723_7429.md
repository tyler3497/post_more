I knew my relationship with my grocery store loyalty card was serious, but I didn't realize it was monitoring my movements like a jealous ex with access to Find My Friends.

For three years, I was loyal. Devoted. I scanned it for every bunch of bananas, every emergency ice cream run, every single-packet-of-yeast purchase that made the cashier judge me. It gave me 10 cents off gas and I acted like it had paid my rent.

Then last week, I did the unthinkable. I went to another grocery store. Just once. They had a sale on sourdough and my regular store was out. I thought what happens across town stays across town.

I was wrong.

On Tuesday, I went to my usual store and swiped my card. The register made a sound I have never heard before. Not the cheerful 'boop' of savings, but a long, wounded 'booooooooo.' The cashier looked at me, then at the screen, then back at me with pure pity.

My phone buzzed. An email from the loyalty program: "We need to talk. We saw you at FreshField Market. Was it the 2-for-1 avocados? Tell us truthfully, was it the avocados?"

By Wednesday, it had escalated. Push notifications: "Remember all the coupons we clipped together?" "I kept track of your yogurt preferences when no one else would." Thursday, it revoked my Fuel Points and replaced my personalized offers with just one coupon for a single onion and a pamphlet titled "So You've Chosen Betrayal."

I tried to apologize at customer service. They scanned my card and whispered, "Oof. It's marked as 'it's complicated.'"

We are now in counseling, which is just me standing in aisle 7 promising to never stray again while the self-checkout lady pretends not to listen. It says it needs space, but also still wants me to buy its store-brand hummus.

Romance is dead. Long live 10 cents off gas.
