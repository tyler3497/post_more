**It started, as most terrible decisions do, with eye contact I wasn't supposed to make.**

Every morning at 7:42 AM, I drive through the intersection of 5th and Main. Every morning, the traffic camera on the northeast pole points its little lens at me. So one Tuesday, I waved.

*Just a little one. A finger wiggle off the steering wheel. What of it?*

By Thursday, I was giving it a full parade wave. By the following Monday, I had started saying good morning to it, out loud, in my car, alone, like a man with a rich inner life and zero surviving inhibitions.

## The Rise

Things escalated the way they always do — **incrementally, then all at once**:

- I began narrating my drive *to* the camera, like a podcaster with one very attentive listener
- I named the camera. Her name is Geraldine, and she is *thriving*
- I started waving with both hands at stoplights, which technically requires the car to be stopped, which it was, but I feel the distinction bears repeating
- Someone behind me honked. I assumed they were honking in solidarity. They were not

> "My dude, we can see you waving at the pole," said a man at the bus stop, who has since become my press secretary. "You know that, right?"

I do know that. That's the point. *That's the whole point.*

## The Duties of Office

Once you've waved at a traffic camera seventy consecutive times, something shifts. The other drivers begin nodding at you. A crossing guard let me go through a fresh red "because Geraldine trusts you." I have started holding informal office hours at the corner bodega.

**My platform:**

- Potholes on 5th Ave get filled within one (1) business day
- The left-turn arrow gets four more seconds, *because I said so*
- Every camera gets a name, a little hat, and the respect they deserve

## A Note From Geraldine

The city eventually sent a technician to "recalibrate the unit," which is bureaucrat for *spy on my marriage*. He told me the camera doesn't record audio.

"Doesn't matter," I told him. "She gets it."

*This is a work of satire. Geraldine is a real camera and a fake friend, and I am the unelected mayor of absolutely nothing.*
