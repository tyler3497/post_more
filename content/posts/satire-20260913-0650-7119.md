**It started the way all great tragedies do: with spare change and good intentions.**

Last Tuesday, standing at pump #4 with $37.12 worth of regular unleaded rattling around inside my car, I felt a sudden wave of empathy for the pump. Not the gas station. The *pump*. This chrome-and-rubber soldier stood there in the rain, dispensing joy to ungrateful drivers, and I thought: *somebody should tip this thing.*

So I did. I slid a crumpled dollar into the receipt slot like a man paying his respects.

## The receipt was the first sign

The pump printed a receipt that said:

> THANK YOU FOR YOUR KINDNESS. YOUR BALANCE IS NOW: EVERYTHING ELSE.

*Everything else?* I asked it. It just beeped. Not the friendly beep. The other beep.

## Now everything costs guilt

Since that night, the economy of my life has restructured itself around one currency, and it is **shame-adjacent regret**. Examples from this week alone:

- I ignored a charity email and immediately owed $4.60 in *spiritual backpay*
- I took the last slice of office pizza. Invoice arrived within the hour: *one emotional dollar and a handwritten note*
- I walked past a homeless guy's guitar without tipping. The pump texted me. **The pump texted me.**

## I have tried to stop

I've switched to paying at the counter. I've prepaid in exact change. I've whispered *"we're even"* into the card reader like a man ending a relationship with a jukebox. Nothing works.

Last night the pump printed a new receipt, unprompted. It said:

> PUMP #4 HAS REACHED ITS 10,000TH TIPPER. YOU ARE ALL HONORED GUESTS. PLEASE TIP 20% ON SUNDAYS. ESPECIALLY YOU.

*Especially me.*

So here I am: financially stable, morally bankrupt, and emotionally overdrawn at the corner of Fifth and Main. My accountant says the guilt isn't deductible. My therapist says the pump is "a metaphor." The pump says **stop telling people that.**
