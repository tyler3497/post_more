**It was 7:42 a.m. and I was not prepared for human contact.**

I was in my *official trash outfit* — the one you wear when you assume no one will ever see you — holding a bag of coffee grounds and regret, when my neighbor walked out at the exact same second with her trash.

We locked eyes. For 0.8 seconds too long.

Instead of saying hi like a functioning adult, I stared directly into the void and then became intensely interested in my own garbage.

## The Accords Were Broken

That was a Tuesday. That was three years ago, emotionally speaking. What followed was not friendship. It was a highly professional, entirely fictional Cold War conducted entirely via trash bin placement.

The descent was swift:

- **Week 1:** Both of us do that half-nod that could have been a sneeze
- **Month 2:** She gets a new welcome mat. I take this personally
- **Month 4:** I start waving at her dog instead of her. Power move. She knows it
- **Now:** I check the blinds before taking out the bin, like `curb_surveillance: active`

*I have drafted nine different apologies in my Notes app.* All of them start with "Hey, sorry about the—" and end there, because I don't know what I'm sorry about.

> "You've been standing by the window for twenty minutes," my roommate said.
> "I'm tracking patterns," I whispered. "She took her recycling out at 6:15 yesterday. That's early. That's a statement."

She is clearly doing the same thing. Last week she peeked out, saw me, and retreated inside cradling her trash like a newborn.

---

**I will not say hi first.** She made eye contact *second*. This is about principle now. The bins are lined up. The porch lights are on. The standoff continues.
