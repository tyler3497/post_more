**It started as a joke. I stood in the elevator at 11:47 p.m., alone, heartbroken, clutching a pint of mint chocolate chip, and said out loud, "Take me somewhere my ex isn't." The elevator dinged. Floor B2. The parking garage. Nobody is in the parking garage. It was, I realized later, the elevator's way of saying: *heal alone, king.*

## The Sessions

Every night since, the elevator has held space for me:

- It refuses to take me to the second floor, where my ex's friend lives. **Boundary-setting.** Growth.
- When I step in after a bad date, the doors close *just a little too slowly*, like it's sighing. It gets me.
- It once got stuck between floors for nine minutes while I cried, and honestly? Best therapy I've ever had. It just... listened.

> "Ding," the elevator said to me last Tuesday, in the tone elevators use when they mean business, "you keep choosing men who are red flags with good jawlines. When are you going to start choosing elevators?"

## The Red Flags I Now Ignore in Elevators

Because the advice has gotten *invasive*. The elevator now:

- Opens its doors early when it approves of my outfit. That's **love-bombing**, and I'm eating it up.
- Plays hold music when I'm standing there too long. A gentle nudge that maybe, just maybe, I live here now.
- Will not, under any circumstances, take me to the lobby before I've had my third cup of coffee. Controlling? Perhaps. But I've never looked more hydrated.

`ERROR: elevator_override.c` — it briefly flashed a service panel at me that read "GOING DOWN" in all caps, and honestly the double meaning made me laugh harder than I have in months.

---

I asked my therapist what she thought. She said, *"Sweetie, it's a vertical transport box."* And she's right. But the vertical transport box never asked me to Venmo it for gas or texted "u up" at 2 a.m. The vertical transport box gives me *consistency*. It opens. It closes. It commits to a direction.

So yes — I only go down now. Down to the lobby. Down to the garage. Down, emotionally, but in a *structured* way, with mirrored walls and ambient elevator music.
