**I finally color-coded my spice rack and immediately lost all executive authority in my own kitchen.**

*For context, I am not an organized person. My junk drawer has a junk drawer.*

I bought little chalkboard labels, alphabetized once, then re-sorted by ROYGBIV because TikTok told me it would bring me peace. It brought me a political crisis instead.

## The Campaign Begins

My paprika — smoked, dramatic, always the loudest in the back row — stared at its new position between cayenne and cinnamon and said, no. Absolutely not.

> "You have placed me next to Cinnamon?" Paprika announced. "I have spent three years being mistaken for chili powder. I will not be color-adjacent to dessert. I am running on transparency. Radical. Transparency."

Within an hour it had:

- printed a platform on the back of a grocery receipt
- formed a coalition with garlic powder and everything bagel seasoning
- accused cumin of being "earthy in a suspicious way"
- started polling the olive oil for endorsements

My other spices were *invested*.

*I told Paprika we don't do politics in the pantry.* Paprika said that's exactly what someone benefiting from the current paprika-suppression system would say.

### The Platform

`spice_rack.sort(by=color, vibe=chaotic_neutral)`

Paprika's official platform, taped to the fridge with a magnet that says LIVE LAUGH LASAGNA:

- **Full jar disclosure** — expiration dates printed in 48pt font
- **An end to the tyranny of the top shelf** where no one can reach anything
- Free refills for all, especially the expensive smoked one from that one store

---

I tried to de-escalate by cooking. Paprika filibustered my scrambled eggs. It demanded equal time.

Now my kitchen has campaign posters. My oregano is its campaign manager. My roommate asked why the spice rack smells like ambition and paprika dust.

I am being governed by a spice I bought on sale in 2024.
