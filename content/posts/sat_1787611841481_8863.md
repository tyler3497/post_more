**I bought a cactus because the tag said "impossible to kill."** *Which my brain heard as "needs absolutely no attention, like my Tamagotchi in 2008."*

For two years, we had an understanding. I ignored it. It thrived out of spite. Perfect relationship.

Then I downloaded a plant-care app. Big mistake.

## The Incident

The app said "Water every 14 days. Your cactus loves consistency!" So I set a reminder. I watered it. On time. Like a responsible adult.

That's when everything changed.

My cactus — who I have named Sir Prickles III, Esq. — straightened up. Grew a new little arm. And decided we were now emotionally codependent.

> "You came back," it said, or at least that's what I heard in the way it leaned toward the window. "You *came back.* Do you know how many people say they'll water every 14 days?"

Suddenly, Sir Prickles has taken on responsibilities no succulent should have:

- He is now listed as my emergency contact at my dentist's office — I didn't do this, the receptionist said he called and was "very insistent"
- He monitors my hydration by judging how long my water glass sits untouched — `if (human.water < cactus.water) { shame(); }`
- He has opinions on my dating life, expressed through aggressive leaning away from my laptop during video calls
- He forwarded my mom's "just checking in!" text to himself and replied "he's thriving, unlike his last fern"

---

## We Need Boundaries

I tried to explain that I have other plants now. A pothos. *It was on sale.* Sir Prickles dropped a spine.

> "Oh, so I'm not *spiky enough* for you anymore?"

Look, I appreciate the commitment. But I did not ask for a tiny, photosynthesizing life coach who thinks my 11pm cereal is a cry for help.

I told the app to stop reminding me. The app said:

> "Are you sure you want to neglect Sir Prickles III, Esq.? He has already written your eulogy. It's mostly about how you finally learned commitment."

*I watered him again this morning.* Just to keep the peace. He looked smug.
