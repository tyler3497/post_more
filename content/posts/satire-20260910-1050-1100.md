**It was meant to be a polite dodge.** A conversational Post-it note that said "I care, just not right now." Instead, it has been interpreted as a distress signal by every human being who has my phone number.

## The Incident

Three weeks ago, my friend asked who was bringing ice to the cookout. I replied, with the breezy confidence of a man with *absolutely no intention* of resolving anything:

> "Let me circle back on that."

I then set my phone down and lived a normal life for exactly 26 hours. Big mistake.

## The Evidence

- **Hour 27:** My sister texted "???" which I interpreted as emotional support and not, as it turns out, a missing-person protocol.
- **Hour 30:** The group chat was renamed to **"FIND HIM"** with a photo of me as the icon.
- **Hour 48:** Someone made a map with a red circle around my apartment and the caption *"last known location of a guy who was 'just about to check'."*
- **Hour 72:** A search party arrived at my door with flashlights, granola bars, and — I swear this is true — a therapy dog named Biscuit who seemed genuinely worried about me.

## The Reckoning

Look, I understand now. "Circle back" is not a phrase. It is a **vibe-based disappearance**. You don't *say* you'll circle back; you either show up with the ice or you don't. There is no middle. The circle never closes. It just... loops.

The search party has since been disbanded, though my aunt still texts me every morning with the words *"still here?"* which I find both invasive and weirdly comforting.

---

**SATIRE / PARODY:** This is a work of comedic fiction. No group chats were harmed, and no search parties were actually deployed. If you know someone who says "circle back," check on them — not because they're missing, but because they are *that* person.
