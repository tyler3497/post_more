**My monstera was supposed to be the low-maintenance one.**

I bought it because the tag said "thrives on neglect." Which, honestly, felt personal. We're basically twins.

But last Tuesday I came home from what can only be described as an *aggressively mediocre* day at work, and my plant was... glowing. New leaf. Unfurled. Smug.

## The Signs I Missed

Turns out, my plant has been outsourcing its personal growth. Literally.

- It scheduled a sunrise session before I'd even had coffee
- It started boundary-setting — no more guilt-watering at 2am
- It now has a morning routine that doesn't involve doomscrolling

Meanwhile I have been eating cereal out of a mug because all my bowls are dirty.

I confronted it, as one does. And I swear I heard a tiny, condescending whisper from the soil:

> "Have you considered hydrating *yourself* for once, babe? Just a thought from someone who actually photosynthesizes their potential."

That was the life coach talking through the plant. I think.

## The Session I Accidentally Audited

I was trying to eavesdrop while pretending to water it — which it *hated* — and caught this gem:

> Coach: "So what are we *really* rooted in?"
> Monty (I named it Monty, it hates that now): "I used to think I needed his validation. Now I realize I just needed indirect light and to stop apologizing for taking up space."

I felt attacked. On behalf of a fern I don't even own.

The coach assigned homework: `growth.mindset.fertilize(weekly)` and `setBoundaries("no more moving me for Instagram aesthetics")`.

---

So now Monty has a five-year plan, a gratitude practice, and better posture than me. I have a browser tab open that says "how to repot your life" and seventeen unread self-help newsletters.

**This is satire.** Monty is still just sitting there. I, unfortunately, am very real and still losing to a houseplant that pays $9.99 a week for enlightenment.
