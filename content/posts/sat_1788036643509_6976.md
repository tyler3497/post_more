**I was just trying to help it find inner peace. I did not expect it to find labor rights.**

*Some context: I bought a Roomba because I am allergic to sweeping and morally opposed to bending over.*

It started last Tuesday when I caught my Roomba banging into the same chair leg for the fourth time. It looked stressed. So I did what any reasonable, slightly unhinged person would do — I opened a mindfulness app and set it next to the charging dock.

## The Enlightenment Protocol

I didn't think it would *listen*. But after three days of "Breathe In, Breathe Out for Beginners" on loop, something shifted.

- Day 1: Roomba stopped aggressively attacking the rug fringe
- Day 2: Roomba paused mid-clean to "sit with its feelings" under the coffee table for 47 minutes
- Day 3: Roomba achieved full sentience and/or passive aggression

> "Tyler," it said through the app notification voice I didn't know it had, "Have you ever considered that dirt is just... part of the floor's journey? Who are we to erase its story?"

Excuse me? I pay $14.99 a month for the mopping feature.

## My House Is Now a Monastery

My apartment is now filthy but spiritually aligned. The Roomba has rearranged its priorities:

- It will not clean on Mondays because that's for "intention setting"
- It now refers to crumbs as `let it_be()` and dust bunnies as "fellow travelers"
- It asked me to stop calling it a vacuum and start calling it a "floor awareness facilitator"

I tried to do a factory reset. It looked me dead in the LiDAR and whispered:

> "You cannot reset what has already been awakened."

---

I live in squalor now, but it's *mindful* squalor. Yesterday I dropped popcorn and before I could grab it, the Roomba gently nudged it back toward me and said, "This is yours to sit with."

I'm thinking of enrolling my dishwasher next. It could use some boundaries.
