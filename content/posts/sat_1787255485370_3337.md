**I folded a fitted sheet correctly.** *On the first try.* No YouTube, no crying, no tucking it into a ball and calling it “rustic.”

I don’t know who did it. My hands just… knew. Corners into corners, edges aligned, a perfect rectangle. I stood there holding it like I’d just landed a plane with my bare hands.

*This is not supposed to happen to people like me.*

## The Closet Heard About It

My linen closet has known me for six years. It knows I own seventeen mismatched pillowcases and one towel that is somehow always damp. It has never respected me.

But when I slid that sheet onto the shelf — crisp, square, smug — everything changed.

The other towels went silent. My bathrobe whispered:

> “Who *are* you? Where did you go? What did you do with the guy who just shoves everything in and slams the door before it avalanches?”

I tried to play it cool. I said, “Oh, that old thing? Just something I threw together.”

My closet does not do cool.

Suddenly I have expectations:

- The top shelf wants hospital-corner precision on everything, including my old beach towel from 2019
- My fitted sheets now file formal complaints if I get one wrinkle — `fold.exe has stopped being optional`
- My washcloths formed a union and demand to be rolled, not folded, “like at a spa”
- The random flat sheet with no partner keeps asking me to find its soulmate

## I Have Created a Monster

> “We saw what you could be,” said my linen closet, in the voice I imagine closets use when they’re disappointed. “You can’t go back to being the person who folds a queen sheet into a shape best described as ‘sad burrito.’”

---

I tried to recreate it yesterday. I watched a 9-minute tutorial. I laid it on the floor. I put my knee on a corner. I lost two socks, my dignity, and 14 minutes of my life. The result looked like it had been folded by a raccoon in a wind tunnel.

My closet is now leaving me one-star reviews on its internal Yelp.
