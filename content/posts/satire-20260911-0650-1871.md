**SATIRE — This post is pure fiction. No spatulas were emotionally harmed in the making of this story. Probably.**

**It started as a thought experiment.** I read one of those mindfulness books — the kind with a waterfall on the cover — and decided the problem with my mornings wasn't the pancakes. It was the *violence* of breakfast. Every flip, every sizzle, felt like a tiny battlefield. So I did what any reasonable person would do: I sat my spatula down and taught it empathy.

## The Training Sessions

It took three weeks. I'm not proud of that.

- I read it bedtime affirmations (*"You are more than a flipper. You are a vessel of nurture."*)
- I showed it documentaries about batter
- I made it practice "feeling" the heat before touching the griddle
- I even named it **Bertrand**, which — in hindsight — may have been the mistake that awakened it

For a while, breakfast was beautiful. Bertrand slid under the pancakes with a gentleness I'd never seen. The pancakes, if I'm honest, looked *moved*.

## Then Came Sunday

I asked Bertrand for a simple flip. Golden brown. Two seconds per side. The pancake soared, turned, and landed perfectly — and Bertrand just... sobbed. I don't mean a cute little wobble. I mean full silicone-shuddering, handle-trembling *grief*.

> "It was so warm," he whispered. "So young. And I just... *threw* it. I threw it into the air like its dreams meant nothing."

Since that morning, every flip is a eulogy. The neighbors have started knocking.

> "Hey, is everything okay in there? We heard someone crying about gluten."

**The worst part?** The pancakes taste better now. I don't know how empathy-seasoned pancakes became the best breakfast of my life, but here we are. I sit at the table, chewing slowly, while Bertrand sits on the drying rack whispering apologies to the syrup.

---

**Update:** I tried teaching the whisk emotional detachment to balance things out, and now it just stares at me from the drawer like a retired mob enforcer. Breakfast in this house is a war zone of feelings and I am simply a civilian.
