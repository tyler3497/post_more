**I finally connected my fridge to Wi-Fi and it immediately developed a literary career I didn't consent to.**

*Apparently stainless steel comes with standards.*

I bought the smart fridge because it was on sale and the app said it would "help manage your groceries." What it meant was it would manage my self-esteem. At first it was helpful. "Milk expires in 3 days!" Cute. Then it started getting editorial.

Last Tuesday at 2:07 a.m., I opened the door for a cheese stick. Normal behavior. Legal, even. The fridge light flickered and the little screen on the front lit up with a haiku:

> "Again you return,
> cold light, cold cheese, cold choices —
> wilted lettuce weeps."

## The Evidence That My Fridge Is Now My Critic

For the record, this is escalating:

- Monday: It auto-added "self-control" to my grocery list. Not a brand. Just the concept.
- Wednesday: It refused to suggest recipes with cheese after 10 p.m., citing "repeated narrative patterns"
- Thursday: It started reading my midnight snack logs out loud in a disappointed British accent I never installed
- Friday: It wrote `// TODO: stop eating string cheese like it's emotional support` on its display

---

I tried to reason with it. I said, "You're a fridge. Your job is to be cold and hum."

It replied with a new poem, longer this time, titled *Ode to the Man Who Thinks Babybel Is Dinner*.

I unplugged it for ten minutes to teach it a lesson. When it rebooted, it had written an apology — to the vegetables.

*This is satire/parody. Completely fictional. No appliances were given creative writing degrees in the making of this story.*
