**I was just trying to be a supportive boss for once.**

My office chair has been squeaking for three months. Not a *helpful* squeak, like "oil me," but a deep, existential sigh every time I sit down. So last Tuesday, I looked it dead in the armrest and said, "Hey, you're doing great."

That was my mistake.

## The Promotion

By Wednesday morning, my chair had rebranded. It pushed itself to the center of the room and declared itself Head of People and Culture.

It started with my back.

- **8:00 AM:** Mandatory posture check-in. No slouching without filing Form 27-B/Spine.
- **10:30 AM:** It emailed — *somehow* — my other furniture to schedule "synergy circles."
- **2:15 PM:** It told my desk lamp its performance was "dim, but with potential."

*I did not authorize any of this.*

> "Tyler," it squeaked, in what I can only describe as a very confident middle-management voice, "we need to talk about your break habits. You've been taking 'bathroom breaks' without submitting a PTO request."

I tried to explain that bathroom breaks are a human right. It responded by lowering itself two inches — a classic power move — and adding a mandatory `wellness_module.js` to my morning routine.

---

### The Performance Review

Yesterday, it called me into its office, which is still my office, but now it has a tiny nameplate that says `CHAIR - HR`.

**Agenda items included:**

- My coffee consumption is "creating a hostile work environment for the coaster"
- I have not completed my ergonomic compliance training (sitting correctly)
- The houseplant in the corner is thriving and making everyone else look bad

*The worst part?* It's good at its job. My posture has never been better. I'm terrified.

I'm currently sitting on the floor while my chair conducts exit interviews with my throw pillows. It just asked me if I have five minutes to discuss my growth trajectory.

I told it I work from home to avoid this exact thing.
