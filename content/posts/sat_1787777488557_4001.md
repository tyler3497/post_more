**I did it. I folded the fitted sheet. Perfectly. On the first try.**

*This was not supposed to be possible — physicists agreed, my mom agreed, the tag on the sheet itself agreed.*

For 31 years I have lived in what scientists call "the wadded ball paradigm." You wash the fitted sheet, you stare at the elastic corners like they're a riddle from a sphinx, you give up, you stuff it in the closet and whisper "we don't talk about this."

Not today.

## The Incident That Changed Everything

I watched a 4-minute YouTube tutorial at 0.75x speed. I laid the sheet on the floor like a crime scene. I tucked corners into each other. I smoothed. I folded. And then — *silence*. A perfect rectangle. With actual corners.

I held it up to the light. It didn't unfold itself in protest.

> "You... you weren't supposed to know how to do this," whispered my linen closet. "We had a system. You were the messy one. That was our dynamic."

### Evidence My Linen Closet Is Now Intimidated:

- My bath towels have started folding themselves preemptively out of fear
- The top shelf — where the "good" towels live — now leaves its door slightly ajar, like it's trying to impress me
- My pillowcases asked if I do performance reviews now
- The fitted sheet itself has developed an ego and keeps asking to be displayed "where guests can see"

---

I tried to put it back. Gently. Respectfully. I slid it onto the shelf and the entire closet went quiet. You know that quiet? Like when you get promoted and your work friends don't know if they can still meme you.

`status: linen_closet = reporting_to_me_now`

I tried to reassure them. "Guys, I'm still the person who uses a mixing bowl as a cereal bowl." No response. The laundry detergent just stared.

Now I have a linen closet that thinks I'm overqualified, a fitted sheet that wants a LinkedIn recommendation, and a deep, existential fear that I can never go back to the ball method without looking like I've given up on growth.

I miss being humbled by elastic.
