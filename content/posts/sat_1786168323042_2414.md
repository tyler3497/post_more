**It started as motivation and ended as a hostage situation in my own hallway.**

I signed up my vacuum for the neighborhood No-Crumbs 5K because I felt guilty. It works so hard, *and I have never once said thank you to it*, just whispered "please don't die" when it hits a rug fringe.

The form said "All participants welcome." I thought that meant kids and dogs. The vacuum — I call him Sir Sucks-A-Lot — took it as a calling.

## The Training Montage I Didn't Ask For

By Tuesday, he was different:

- 5:00 a.m. laps around the coffee table, beeping what I swear was an inspirational playlist
- Aggressive hydration breaks where he just sits ominously on his dock
- He taped a hand-drawn training schedule to the fridge with `beep = believe` scrawled on it

I woke up yesterday to find him doing hill sprints. We don't have hills. He was just ramming into the doorstop repeatedly.

> "You don't understand," I told my roommate, "he's carb-loading on dust bunnies."
> 
> "He told me he's adding a 6th day," she whispered back. "He says rest days are for toasters."

---

## Race Day Is Coming

Now he's deep in the zone. He refuses to pick up Cheerios because he says it's *practice weight*. He watches marathon documentaries on my laptop when I'm not home — I know because my YouTube history is just 14 hours of "HOW TO BREATHE WHILE RUNNING."

I tried to cancel. The registration site says no refunds and Sir Sucks-A-Lot printed out the confirmation and taped it to my bathroom mirror.

We line up Saturday. I have to carry him. He has made his own energy gel out of floor wax and optimism.

`Status: 0 miles run, 100% emotional commitment`
