**It started as a joke.** I was standing in the kitchen at midnight, holding the receipt from a haircut that made me look like a medieval monk, and I thought: *surely this is what the blue bin is for.*

## The Incident

On Monday I wheeled the recycling bin to the curb with what I can only describe as **emotional baggage and three flattened cardboard boxes**. By Wednesday, something had changed. The bin stood at attention. Its lid snapped shut with the finality of a judge's gavel.

When I tried to toss in a crumpled movie ticket from 2019 — a date that ended with both of us pretending the other had suggested the documentary about bridges — the bin recoiled. I am not being dramatic. It physically tilted away.

A small laminated card had appeared on the lid, held by a binder clip I do not own:

> "PER NEW POLICY, ACCEPTED ITEMS MUST BE POST-2020. OLDER REGRETS MAY BE COMPOSTED OR BURIED. MANAGEMENT."

I called my friend. She said, "Management? Your bin has management?" and I had no answer, because:

- The bin now sorts my mail into "growth opportunities" and "lawsuits waiting to happen"
- It returned my ex's love letters to sender with a note that said *insufficient postage, emotionally speaking*
- It keeps a log. A LOG. Dated, timestamped, alphabetized by shame

## Where We Stand

This morning I caught it whispering to the neighbor's bin across the fence. I couldn't make out the words, but I heard my name, and then a long, rattling *clunk* that I can only describe as laughter.

I have decided to comply. I went through the garage and recycled every regret from 2020 onward: the sourdough starter, the gym membership, the podcast I started and abandoned after eleven minutes. The bin accepted them with a soft, almost fond sigh of the lid.

> "Progress," the card now reads. "See? That wasn't so hard."

It was a little hard. **I'm telling you this because someone should document it.**
