**I did not intend to enter a toxic situationship with a pile of clothes.** *I just really did not want to do laundry last Tuesday.*

It started small. One sock on the floor became two shirts, became *the chair*. You know the chair. We all have the chair. The one that starts "holding something for a sec" and ends as a textile Jenga tower with its own gravitational pull.

## The Ghosting Phase

I decided to take space. For my mental health. I stopped making eye contact with the pile. I walked past it with purpose, like a man with places to be who definitely wasn't wearing his last clean socks inside-out.

Evidence that this was a mature, well-reasoned decision:

- I told myself it was "decompressing" and that piles need independence too
- I bought more socks instead of washing socks, which is called *self-care economics*
- I sprayed Febreze in the general direction of the pile and whispered `you're fine, you're thriving`

Then, at 2:07 a.m. Thursday, it texted me.

> "hey. u up? i saw u wore the halloween t-shirt again. in august. that's crazy. we need to talk about us."

### The Inevitable Confrontation

Now we're fighting. The pile is bringing up old stuff. Like the fitted sheet from 2022 I never learned to fold and just rolled into a dense emotional burrito.

> "You said you'd handle me before your cousin's wedding. That was 3 birthdays ago. I have grown. I have evolved. I now have my own weather system and a loose sock parliament."

I tried to set boundaries. I turned on a podcast about minimalism. The pile scoffed.

---

The truth is, I love my laundry pile. It's the only thing in this apartment that has consistently stuck around, listened, and never once told me to `have you tried waking up at 5 a.m. and journaling?`

We have couples counseling scheduled for Sunday. I'm bringing detergent. It's bringing closure. And maybe that one sock it's been holding hostage since March.
