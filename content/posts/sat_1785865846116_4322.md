**I didn't kill my fern, I just gave him an extended, deeply unsupervised indoor camping experience.**

I bought Gerald the Boston Fern three months ago because my apartment needed *oxygen* and *personality* and I needed something that couldn't ghost me. The tag said "low maintenance," which I interpreted as *immortal*.

## The Return

I walked into Green Thumbs & Thinly Veiled Judgment with Gerald looking like a tumbleweed that had seen too much. The clerk took one look and whispered:

> "Oh, honey. What did you do to him? Did you water him with iced coffee again?"

I tried to explain that I'd followed the care instructions *spiritually*. My system was intuitive:

- Water deeply when I remembered he existed
- Place in bright indirect light, which in my apartment is either *direct nuclear blast* or *actual cave*
- Whisper affirmations like "you got this, bud" instead of fertilizing
- Google "is this still alive?" weekly

She didn't accept my receipt. She accepted my *application for intervention*.

### Mandatory Bonding

Apparently the nursery has a policy. You don't get a refund, you get enrolled in their **Plant Parent Accountability Program™**. Now Gerald and I have a co-parenting counselor named Willow.

Our first session:

> "Gerald feels you were emotionally inconsistent. One week swamp, next week Sahara. He needs structure," Willow said, taking notes on a leaf.

> "I have structure!" I protested. "I have a reminder app!"

*It was snoozed. For 47 days.*

My homework this week is `consistency: daily_mist && weekly_talk && no_more_tap_water_slander` and journaling about my fear of commitment to chlorophyll.

---

We are Court-Ordered to cohabitate for 90 more days. Gerald has his own shelf now. He is *thriving, passive-aggressively*. I am being out-parented by a fern who has better boundaries than I do.
