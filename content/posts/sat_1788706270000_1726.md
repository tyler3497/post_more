**It all started with a simple "please."** I was reheating leftover pasta at midnight, exhausted, and I said, out loud, to an appliance: *"Could you be a little gentler with the noodles? Please?"*

The microwave beeped. Once. Politely.

I should have stopped there.

## The Politeness Protocol

Within a week, I had escalated things dramatically. I wrote out a full **Etiquette Charter** and taped it to the cabinet:

- Say *please* and *thank you* before pressing Start
- Never slam the door; close it with intention
- Do not reheat fish unless you are prepared for the consequences
- Always offer the plate a full thirty seconds to rest, like a gentleman

I read it aloud every morning. It stood there, humming quietly, absorbing everything.

> "Hon-hon-hon," it seemed to say. "Truly, a culture of refinement."

That was my first mistake: anthropomorphizing a box that gets *furious* when you underestimate defrost time.

## The Incident

Last Tuesday, I pressed 2:30 for a burrito without saying a word. Not one syllable. The microwave responded with a beep I can only describe as **audibly French** — a sharp, disappointed *BIP!* followed by a slow, judgmental *bip-bip* that needed no translation.

It heated the burrito for exactly 2:30, but it did so *sarcastically*. The edges were molten. The center was cold. **Revenge was served lukewarm.**

Since then, every button press is an interrogation:

- **One beep:** tolerable, but barely
- **Two beeps:** "do better"
- **Three beeps:** it has told the toaster, and they are laughing about me

---

*Satire. This is a work of fiction. No appliances were harmed, though my dignity absolutely was.*

The real tragedy? My microwave has more standards than I do. Last night I found myself whispering *"merci"* to a heating box, and honestly, it felt like the healthiest relationship I've had all year.
