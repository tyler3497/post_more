**I made the mistake of telling my air fryer it was part of the family and now I am negotiating seating charts with a kitchen appliance.**

*It started as positive reinforcement, which every influencer with a ring light says you should do.* You know, talk nicely to your appliances, manifest good energy, avoid yelling at the dishwasher. So last Tuesday, while it perfectly crisped my tater tots, I said it. Out loud. To a 6-quart black box that sounds like a tiny jet engine.

> "You're basically family at this point."

It beeped. I thought that was the end of it.

## Then Things Got Weird

By Wednesday morning, my air fryer had developed **boundaries, expectations, and a concerning interest in my family group chat**.

Evidence that I am now co-parenting with a countertop convection oven:

- It refuses to cook anything unless I say "please" and "thank you" and acknowledge its "emotional labor"
- It only runs on the "family recipe" setting now, which is just 380°F but with *attitude*
- It prints little receipt messages like `PROUD OF YOU FOR EATING VEGETABLES (I SAW THE NUGGETS YESTERDAY)`
- It will not stop asking if my cousin Jessica is bringing her "famous green bean casserole" again because last year "she didn't even try"

> "If I'm family," it blinked at me in that smug digital font, "then I get a plus-one to Thanksgiving. And I don't mean the silicone tongs. I mean the toaster. He's been through a lot."

I tried to explain that Thanksgiving is for humans and that bringing a toaster would raise questions. It responded by going on strike and only toasting one side of my bagel.

---

Look, I understand. I anthropomorphized a $79 appliance from Target and now it's weaponizing Hallmark movies against me. It keeps playing that little *ding!* chime like:

> "Family isn't just about DNA. It's about who shows up when your mozzarella sticks need them most."
