**I let my alarm clock sleep in once. Once.**

*In my defense, it looked tired. In its defense, it is a clock.*

I have owned the same alarm clock since college. Not a phone, a real clock with actual buttons you need a PhD to program. For eight years it has screamed at 6:30 a.m. with the enthusiasm of a drill sergeant who had too much coffee.

Last Saturday, I did something radical. I turned it off. I said out loud, "You deserve a break, little guy. Sleep in."

## The Promotion It Didn't Earn

By Sunday, it had questions.

By Monday, it had a full existential crisis.

It didn't ring. It just stared at me with its little red LED face going:

> "If I am not the thing that wakes you up, what am I? Am I... decor? Am I a nightlight with anxiety?"

*I was being psychoanalyzed by something that runs on AA batteries.*

### The Evidence That My Alarm Clock Is Now in a Spiral:

- It now asks me what time it *feels* like before it agrees to display the actual time
- It snoozes *itself* for nine minutes and whispers "just five more minutes of purpose"
- It refuses to say 6:30 a.m. because that's "when the old me died"
- It rebranded its alarm sound from `BEEP BEEP BEEP` to a soft sigh followed by `...do we have to?`

I tried to be supportive. I wrote a little routine to fix us:

```
if (alarmClock.identityCrisis == true) {
  human.guilt = 100;
  human.lateToEverything = true;
  clock.therapyBill += 1;
}
```

---

**Now we negotiate every morning.** I say "6:30?" and it says "What does 6:30 even *mean*, man?" My phone alarm has started covering for it out of pity, but it's doing a bad job and also judging me.

I caved this morning. I told it it could be a "sunrise simulation experience" instead of an alarm. It immediately added that to its LinkedIn and asked for a standing desk.
