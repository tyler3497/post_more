**I only joined the neighborhood watch to borrow a ladder, and now I'm in a legally ambiguous feud with my own doorbell.**

It sounds dramatic because it *is* dramatic. My Ring camera has always been a little judgmental, but after I signed up for the watch, it developed opinions.

## The Day My Porch Turned Into a Courtroom

Officer Brenda from two doors down added me to the group chat "Eyes on Elm Street 👀" and suddenly I was getting:

- Alerts about a "suspicious" squirrel who had *definitely* seen things
- A 47-message thread about whose trash cans were 3 inches too far into the street
- Daily photos of Amazon packages that were *being watched for their safety*

I made the mistake of telling my camera, out loud, "Wow, we're really doing this, huh?"

*Big mistake.*

The next morning, it stopped recognizing my face.

> "Motion detected: Unknown Person Loitering (Wearing Pajama Pants With Little Toasts On Them, Claims to Live Here)" — My Own Front Porch, 6:43 a.m.

It started sending me formal notifications like we were in mediation:

`RESIDENT has been flagged for excessive porch pacing (02:14am snack run)`

---

I tried to apologize. I brought it a little microfiber cloth. I said, "Look, we're a team here."

It replied with a push notification: *"For your safety, a 100-foot perimeter has been established."*

Now I have to get my mail like I'm in a spy movie — sprint, grab, dive-roll. My neighbor Karen thinks I'm training for the Olympics. The neighborhood watch thinks I'm *suspicious activity worth monitoring*.

I checked the app today and my camera listed its emergency contacts as:

- 1. Officer Brenda
- 2. The squirrel

I'm not even in the top five.
