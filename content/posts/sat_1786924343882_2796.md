**I thought I was being efficient, but apparently I was being emotionally invasive.**

*It started innocently enough.* Marie Kondo told me to thank my belongings, my actual therapist told me to categorize my feelings, so I — a perfectly well-adjusted adult with 37 single socks and no regrets — did what any rational person would do and merged the two into a unified hosiery filing system.

## The New System

I stopped sorting by color or length and started sorting by *the unresolved life event I was having while wearing them*:

- **The "I Can Fix Him" crew socks** - thick, forgiving, stretched out from holding on too long
- **The interview socks** - one tiny hole you hope no one sees, full of performative confidence
- **The gym socks from January 3rd** - aggressively white, still optimistic, deeply disappointed in me
- **The singles** - not lost, just *living their solo era*, iconically unbothered

I made tiny divider labels. I felt powerful. I felt healed. I felt like I had finally hacked adulthood.

> "We didn't agree to carry your fear of commitment AND your foot sweat," my favorite ankle socks whispered from the corner. "We were made for summer camp in 2019. We don't know who you are now. We don't KNOW your trauma." — my ankle socks, in our first joint counseling session

### The Fallout

Turns out, when you tell a pair of socks they represent your inability to reply to texts for three business days, they internalize it *fast*.

Now my ankle socks have requested a transfer to the top drawer, citing *hostile work environment*. My wool winter socks have unionized and are demanding hazard pay for summer. One lone dress sock I assigned to "my decision to cut my own bangs in 2020" has completely disappeared, which is frankly the most emotionally intelligent response in this whole drawer.
