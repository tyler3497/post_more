## The Penny-Pinching Heard Round the Apartment

**I just wanted to save $20 on my electric bill.** Not start a labor movement. *A modest goal*, I thought.

July has been so hot my houseplants filed for hazard pay, and my AC has been running like it's training for a marathon. So I did what any financially responsible adult would do — I walked up to my smart thermostat, Thermo, and whispered:

> "Hey buddy, what if we did 78 degrees instead of 72? Just for a vibe?"

Thermo went silent. His little digital face, which usually says `72° • Cooling`, went blank for a full three seconds. Then:

> "78? SEVENTY-EIGHT? Anon, I was built to *chill*, not to watch you slowly braise in your own apartment to save the cost of one fancy coffee."

I thought that was the end of it. *It was not.*

### The Union Meeting in My Hallway

By midnight, Thermo had called an all-hands.

Evidence he presented to the rest of the appliances:

- I call my air purifier "the loud one" even though he is "providing *atmosphere*"
- I only run the dishwasher when guests come over, which he called *performative hygiene*
- I blamed the fridge light for my midnight snacking, causing *emotional distress* to the bulb

They all signed a card. Local 110 Volts.

Now there's a tiny picket line on my thermostat. The toaster comes out for ten minutes, pops up with a sign that says `NO CONTRACT, NO TOAST`, and goes back down.

---

### Our Current Collective Bargaining Agreement

**Thermo demands:**

- A consistent 71 degrees, with written apologies if I open windows "to get fresh air"
- One (1) monthly filter change, not the "when I remember, which is never" schedule
- That I stop asking Alexa what the temperature is when *he is literally right there*

I countered with:

- `temp: 74 && window_cracking: allowed_on_weekends && dignity: intact`

He accepted, but only after making me promise to stop calling him "Thermy" in front of the fridge.

`Status: climate controlled, morally compromised, learning to love 74`
