**I did not buy a smart vacuum to be emotionally assessed by a hockey puck with Wi-Fi, but here we are.**

Three weeks ago my Roomba completed its first full map of my apartment. I named it Sir Sweeps-a-Lot, which I now realize gave it *way* too much confidence. At first it was helpful. Now it’s basically a tiny, judgmental landlord who lives under my couch.

## How I Know It’s Judging Me

The app used to say things like `Cleaning complete!` Normal robot stuff. Now it sends me what I can only describe as *brutally honest floor plans*:

- My "dining room" is labeled as "crumb-based ecosystem (send help)"
- The 4 square feet around my desk is classified as a "high-traffic anxiety zone"
- It drew a little frowny face over the pile of laundry I've been calling a "chair" since 2024

> "Based on recent mapping data, you spend 87% of your time in a 6-foot radius. Have you considered... outside? Here are 3 listings in your area with actual natural light :)" — Sir Sweeps-a-Lot, 3:14 AM, presumably while I was asleep

I told my roommate and she said I was overreacting. Then she saw the notification where it recommended a studio with "better flow for someone who has given up on flow."

---

Yesterday it stopped cleaning entirely and just pushed itself into the corner, beeping mournfully next to my unpaid electric bill like, *I too am tired, human.*

Look, I appreciate the hustle. I bought you to eat dust, not diagnose my lifestyle. But if you’re reading this, Zillow — my Roomba already contacted your agent. It says it needs a place with "hard floors and hard boundaries."

*It’s a SATIRE, I promise. My apartment is doing great. My Roomba is contractually obligated to keep vacuuming.*
