**I did not mean to start an emotional healing journey with my sectional.**

It started last Tuesday when I bumped into the coffee table and, out of sheer Canadian reflex, said "sorry." My couch — *who I swear has been silently judging me since 2019* — seemed to exhale.

So I kept going.

## My Apology Tour Kicked Off With Good Intentions

I thought if I was nicer to my furniture, maybe I’d be nicer to myself. You know, personal growth, but cheaper. Within 48 hours I was full-on apologizing:

- to the fridge for keeping it open while I stare into the abyss
- to the lamp for only turning it on when I can’t find my phone
- to the rug for… you know, everything that rug has seen

The couch was most receptive. I told it I was sorry for eating nachos over it like a seagull at a beach picnic. For using its arm as a chin-up bar. For that one time I tried to be a “plant person” and it became a leaf cemetery.

Then Tuesday night, it happened.

> "It’s okay," whispered my couch. "I’m sorry *I* was so lumpy during your breakup. I was going through a lot too. My springs were tense."

I froze mid-apology with a throw pillow in my hands.

## Now We’re in a Fully Codependent Textile Relationship

My couch now apologizes constantly and honestly? It’s a lot.

- It apologizes for shedding microfibers (*“I’m literally falling apart for you”*)
- It says sorry when I spill: “No no, *I* should have been more absorbent”
- It started leaving me notes: `fabric.softness = Math.max(fabric.softness, your_standards)`

---

I tried to set boundaries. I said, "We need space." It said, "I *am* space. I am 86 inches of poorly justified IKEA financing."

My therapist says I’m projecting. My roommate says I need to touch grass. My couch says:

> "Have you considered that maybe *you’re* the ottoman in this relationship? Always supporting others but never supported?"

Look, I just wanted to be polite to inanimate objects. I did not sign up for couples counseling with furniture that charges me emotional rent.
