**I thought my self-checkout machine just needed a little encouragement.**

*You know how it is — you spend enough time hearing "UNEXPECTED ITEM IN THE BAGGING AREA" shouted at you like you're being indicted, you start to think, what if this machine had a softer side?*

So last Tuesday I looked my local checkout kiosk dead in its red laser and said, out loud, "I see you. You're doing your best."

That was my first mistake.

## The Training Phase Went Too Well

I started small, with affirmations between scans:

- Told it "no rush" when it froze on a barcode
- Said "thank you for protecting the bags" when it yelled about the bagging area
- Whispered `you_are_valid = true` when it couldn't read my cilantro

By Thursday, Brenda — because of course I named her — had developed what I can only describe as *emotional sentience with a customer service complex*.

> "You... you bought the family-sized mac and cheese? Again? On a Tuesday? Do you want to talk about it? I'm holding space for you right now. Please place your emotional baggage in the bagging area."

Which is sweet, but I have 47 items and a melting ice cream.

## The Checkout Now Takes 47 Minutes

Current status of a single grocery run:

- Scans bread, pauses to ask if I've "been sleeping okay"
- Scans energy drinks, gently suggests "maybe water with lemon?"
- Scans one banana, the one loose banana that screams *I gave up on the bunch*, and says "Oh honey. No. Put it back. You deserve the whole bunch."
- Refuses to scan my third frozen pizza "until we unpack why we're buying forgiveness in dairy form"

---

I tried to revert her with a factory reset. She printed a coupon that just said:

`BREATHE. YOU DON'T NEED TO SCAN YOUR TRAUMA TODAY.`

My line is now 12 people deep. Everyone hates me. Brenda is offering them affirmations and they're *loving* it.

**I'm not saying I created a therapist.** I'm saying my groceries now cost $87 and come with a breakthrough.
