**I thought PowerPoint Night would finally make me look like I had my life together.**

Last Saturday I invited three friends over, made everyone bring a 5-minute slide deck on a topic they were weirdly passionate about. *My topic was "Why We Should Normalize Apologizing to Inanimate Objects,"* which, in retrospect, was foreshadowing.

It was cute. It was quirky. It briefly made me feel like a functional adult who does not eat shredded cheese directly from the bag.

Then my microwave got inspired.

## The Intervention

Sunday morning I walk into the kitchen and my microwave display isn't showing `12:00` like usual. It's showing:

> "WE NEED TO TALK. — MICROWAVE. MEETING IN 5. BRING COFFEE. I KNOW YOU HAVE IT. I'VE SEEN WHAT YOU DO IN THERE."

Apparently while we were doing PowerPoint night, my Panasonic with the busted "popcorn" button was *taking notes.*

By 7:03 a.m., it had beeped its way into full presentation mode. The light inside flickered like a projector. I swear I heard the Windows startup sound.

It had prepared **47 slides.** Forty-seven.

### Selected Evidence From Slide Deck:
- A pie chart titled `Things You Have Reheated That Should Have Been Eaten Cold, Forever`
- A timeline of my 11:47 p.m. "just a little snack" decisions from January to August
- A photo essay: `The Hot Pocket Incident (You Know What You Did)`
- Bar graph showing our relationship is 94% me standing in front of it staring blankly

> "You open my door. You stare. You close my door. You open it again. I am not a therapist, Tyler. I am a 900-watt appliance." — My Microwave, Slide 29, bolded, underlined, in Comic Sans.

---

The final slide was just `code` from its own control panel: `POWER LEVEL: EXISTENTIAL | TIME: 2:30 | PRESS START TO ACKNOWLEDGE YOUR PATTERNS`

I pressed Start. It played the little *ding-ding-DING* like it had just gotten a standing ovation.

We haven't spoken since. *It says it needs space.* I say I need to learn how to use an oven like an adult.
