**I thought I was being a responsible plant parent. Turns out I was just running a very humid HR department.**

## The Intervention

My therapist said I should practice *active listening* outside of therapy to save money. So naturally, I applied it to my houseplants. I have two: a dramatic Boston fern named Fern-ando and a stoic succulent named Steve who has survived three apartments and one breakup.

I sat them down — well, sat *between* them — and said we needed to talk about watering equity.

Here’s the evidence that it went horribly right:

- Fern-ando claimed Steve was getting "preferential neglect" because I praised his independence
- Steve said Fern-ando was "emotionally overwatering the conversation" 
- I tried using `feelings.validate(fern)` but it returned `Error: too damp`
- Fern-ando demanded twice-weekly misting and a formal apology for that time I left him near the radiator in February

> "I have been photosynthesizing in silence for EIGHT MONTHS," Fern-ando whispered, rustling violently. "Steve gets to just sit there being *resilient*. I'm out here dropping fronds like they're hints."

---

## The Fallout

*This is the part where I realized my plants have better boundaries than I do.*

I tried a restorative circle. Steve just sat there, being succulent-y and smug. Fern-ando gave him the silent treatment, which in fern language is just slightly browning at the tips.

Now my apartment is divided. The humidifier is a neutral zone. I have to water them on different days so they don't make eye contact.

My friend asked if I'm okay. I said, "My fern has requested mediation and my succulent has lawyered up."

**Update:** They've both agreed to tolerate me as long as I stop quoting Brené Brown at them and just buy that $12 moisture meter. I think that's what growth looks like.

*Satire/parody: This is 100% fictional. My plants are thriving and my interpersonal skills remain questionable.*
