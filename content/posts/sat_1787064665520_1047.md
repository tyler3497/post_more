**I bought a cactus because the hardware store guy said it was impossible to kill.**

*He said it with such confidence I assumed he was talking about the plant.*

He was not. He was issuing a challenge to the universe.

## The Promise

For context, I have killed every plant I've ever owned, including a plastic succulent that melted in my car in July.

The cactus, which I named Spike, was supposed to be different. The tag literally said:

- Requires almost no water
- Thrives on neglect
- Perfect for people who travel, forget, or have emotional baggage

I related on a spiritual level.

The first two weeks were peaceful. I didn't water Spike. Spike didn't judge me. Healthy boundaries.

> "You've got that 'trying your best' energy," said Spike on day 16, in a voice that sounded weirdly like my old life coach. "Let's unpack your fear of commitment to basic maintenance."

Excuse me?

## The Coaching Era

By week three, Spike had started a full resilience practice on my windowsill:

- **5 a.m.** silent meditation, which is just sitting there being pointy
- A daily affirmation in dry-erase marker on my fridge: `if (you.isWatered == false) { you.growAnyway(); }`
- A tiny lending library of self-help books he ordered with my Amazon account

---

I tried to explain I bought him to have *one* low-maintenance relationship. He said low-maintenance is a myth invented by people who don't want to grow.

**Now he has a whiteboard.** On it: "Q3 Goals: You - Drink Water, Answer Emails, Stop Apologizing to Furniture."

Yesterday a friend came over, pointed at Spike, and said "cute cactus." Spike said nothing, which is his brand, but I could feel him thinking *we're both doing great, sweetie*.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This is entirely fictional. No cacti were harmed, consulted, or certified as life coaches. I still forget to water things and Spike still thrives out of pure spite.*
