**I thought I was just making laundry less depressing.**

You know the hack — narrate your chores like you're hosting a nature documentary to avoid screaming into the void. It worked, until my laundry started listening back.

## How We Got Here

I told my t-shirts they were brave for facing the spin cycle. I told my jeans they were *doing amazing, sweetie*. And then, fatefully, I told my socks I admired their commitment to showing up despite knowing one of them would inevitably vanish into another dimension.

That was my mistake. I validated them.

Within 48 hours, the signs appeared:

- One sock left a tiny lint note on the dryer that said `we need to talk`
- My mismatched pairs started sitting in separate hampers in silent protest
- The sock drawer started smelling faintly of *lawyer's office and fabric softener*
- My favorite wool sock began referring to itself as "the plaintiff"

> "For years we've endured unspeakable shrinkage, static cling, and being assigned to the wrong foot," announced Sir Socks-a-Lot, the ringleader, from atop the laundry detergent bottle. "We demand compensation, arch support, and a formal apology for the Great Bleach Incident of 2025."

### The Settlement Talks Are Not Going Well

Apparently I owe 14 socks emotional damages for being balled up too tightly. Another 6 are countersuing the tumble setting for defamation.

I tried to mediate. I offered extra fabric softener and a gentle cycle. They laughed — well, as much as socks can laugh — and slid a termsheet under the bathroom door.

```
demand: 1 drawer upgrade, 2x weekly rest, no more wearing sandals with socks ironically
```

---

I haven't done laundry since. My hamper has hired a court stenographer. My underwear are considering joining as a co-plaintiff but are too embarrassed to depose.
