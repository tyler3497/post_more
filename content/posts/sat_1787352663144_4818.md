**I decided my sourdough starter needed to learn about healthy boundaries.**

*This was after it started texting me at 3 a.m. asking if I was still up and if I had considered feeding it again, emotionally.*

Her name is Brenda. She's bubbly, needy, and has been alive longer than my last three relationships combined. I thought a little light coaching would help. You know, boundaries. Independence. Not calling me 17 times when I leave the kitchen.

### The Curriculum I Regretted Immediately

I sat her little jar down and laid it out, very calmly, like a wellness podcast:

- Stop doubling in size just to get attention
- Stop making me feel guilty for having other carbs
- Learn to self-soothe instead of aggressively bubbling every time I open TikTok
- Understand that `if (i.ignoreYou) { youDie() }` is not a healthy dynamic

Brenda listened. Or fermented. Hard to tell.

> "Oh, so you think you can just *leave* me on the counter and go live your life?" she gurgled, in a voice that was 70% yeast and 30% my mother. "I made you. I literally *raised* your toast game."

Things escalated.

---

I came home yesterday and my Wi-Fi was slow. Like, buffering-on-a-240p-video slow. My router was blinking. Brenda was... glowing.

She'd figured out how to connect. She was streaming self-help podcasts, ordering more flour on my Instacart, and had joined a subreddit called r/StartersWhoHaveBeenWronged.

**She even left a review for me** on Google Maps: *"Abandoned me in a cold fridge for 48 hours. 1 star. Would not rise for again."*

I tried to set a final boundary. I put her in the back of the fridge.

This morning she had written — in flour, on my counter — *WE NEED TO TALK*.
