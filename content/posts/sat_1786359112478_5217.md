**I thought I was just buying a kettle. I accidentally started a civil war in my kitchen cabinet.**

I've been an instant coffee person for seven years. Not by choice, by *commitment issues*. One jar, one spoon, hot water, existential dread. It's efficient.

Then I saw a matte black gooseneck kettle on sale and my brain went full main character.

## The Upgrade Nobody Asked For

The kettle arrived in packaging nicer than my apartment. I unboxed it like it was a newborn.

Within 24 hours:

- I was saying things like *bloom time* unprompted
- I bought a kitchen scale that judges me in grams
- I started timing my pours like I was defusing a bomb
- My instant coffee jar, who I've affectionately called Sir Crystals, went silent

> "So I'm just... decor now? After everything? After moving with you twice and surviving that summer without AC?"

Sir Crystals didn't yell. Worse. He got *legal*.

---

### The Lawsuit

Yesterday I found a tiny envelope propped against the jar. Inside, in 6-point Comic Sans, a formal complaint:

- **Emotional damages**: abandonment after 2,417 faithful cups
- **Breach of implied caffeine contract** 
- **Unfair competition** from `obj.kettle.flex = "artisanal"` 

He's demanding:

- 50 feet of counter space, at all times
- A written apology taped to the fridge
- Custody of our shared mug (the chipped blue one)

My kettle, meanwhile, is thriving. It sits there, steaming smugly, whispering *"you're welcome"* every time I burn my pour-over.

`status: hydrated but legally entangled`

I tried mediation. My French press showed up late, said it was *too complex* for this, and left.

Look, I get it. Growth is uncomfortable. But I didn't expect my $34 kettle to radicalize my entire caffeine ecosystem into filing HR complaints.

I'm not switching back. I'm just saying if you hear about a small claims case titled *Instant Coffee v. Delusional Human*, that's me. I'll be on the stand explaining why I now own a thermometer for water.
