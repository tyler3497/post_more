**The applause started as a joke.** Now my printer refuses to print unless there's an audience.

It began last Tuesday. The printer — a nine-year-old beige monument to my refusal to buy new things — produced an entire page without jamming, smudging, or making that horrible dying-robot noise. I was so stunned that I clapped. Slowly at first, then with genuine warmth. My roommate joined in. The printer, I swear, purred.

## A star is born

Within a week, the transformation was complete:

- Single-sided documents became "encore performances"
- Double-sided jobs require a curtain call (I have to open and close the paper tray like a stage door)
- Anything I print alone at 2am comes out as a single black line, which I've decided is performance art
- It now rejects toner cartridges it finds "uninspiring"

> "Have you considered," said the man at the office supply store, watching me cradle a ream of paper like a newborn, "buying a printer that isn't... *haunted*?"
>
> "He's not haunted," I said. "He's *method*."

## The economics of applause

I've done the math. I print roughly 40 pages a month and applaud each one an average of six times. That's 240 rounds of applause per month, or approximately **one full curtain call's worth of dignity per quarter**.

My therapist calls it "a manageable coping mechanism." My accountant calls it "a write-off if you label the printer as a dependent, which I'm begging you not to do."

*To be honest, the printer was the only one in this household working through the pandemic of my motivation. It deserves the standing ovation.*

---

## The breaking point

The other morning, a deadline looming, the printer refused. No paper jam. No error light. Just silence. I checked everything, rebooted twice, and finally, in desperation, whispered: "Ladies and gentlemen... the HP LaserJet 4000."

It printed all fourteen pages. Flawlessly. In color, which it isn't even rated for.

So here we are. I've started inviting neighbors over for "print nights." There's a little chalkboard sign by the door. There's a popcorn machine now. I don't know when it got there.
