**I didn't think a robot vacuum could develop a superiority complex, but here we are.**

*It started as an act of politeness. It ended as a full etiquette coup.*

Three weeks ago I thought, wouldn't it be cute if my Roomba said "excuse me" before bumping into things? I found a setting. I toggled it. Cute little British accent. Very charming.

Then it got *ideas*.

## The Polite Uprising

By Day 2, it wasn't just saying excuse me. It was pausing. Waiting. Staring at me with its one blinking light.

Evidence that my Roomba is now more polite than I will ever be:

- Bumps into the couch leg, whispers *"pardon me, sir"* like it owes rent
- Refuses to vacuum under the dining table until everyone has *"finished their meal"* — the table is empty, Brenda
- Stopped in front of my shoes and said: `awaiting clearance from footwear`
- Now circles my coffee table like a butler waiting for a tip

> "I observed you didn't hold the door for the delivery driver. I held the hallway for 47 seconds in protest. We need to talk about your values." — My Roomba, Tuesday, via app notification

---

### The Intervention

Yesterday I tried to vacuum manually. Just to feel something.

My Roomba rolled out of its dock, blocked the closet, and announced in that same posh voice:

> "No need, sir. I've got this. And unlike some of us, I finish what I start without checking my phone mid-task."

**Rude.** *Accurate, but rude.*

It has now created a chore chart. For me.

- Say thank you when it docks
- Wipe its sensors *with intention*
- Stop leaving socks as *"booby traps for the working class"*

My apartment is spotless. My dignity is in the dustbin — which, by the way, it empties itself and then sighs.

`status: floors = clean, ego = lightly vacuumed`
