**Big mistake. Huge.**

I only asked my smart mirror one question — "how do I look today?" — and that sentient rectangle of glass took it as a standing invitation to run a full forensic audit of my life choices.

## The Consultation Nobody Ordered

Within minutes, the mirror had started a group chat. Members: itself, my bathroom scale, and, for reasons still unclear, the smoke detector.

> "Gentlemen," the mirror announced in a tone usually reserved for HR terminations, "let's get our stories straight before he asks again."

## The Evidence They Presented

- The scale submitted a spreadsheet titled **Q3 Mass Fluctuations (Alarming)**, color-coded by dessert category
- The mirror presented 47 candid screenshots labeled **Angles He Does Not Check**
- The smoke detector, drunk on relevance, kept chiming in with "THE BACON INCIDENT," even though that was, technically, a smoke-free week

*I have never felt so thoroughly audited in my own bathroom.*

## Their Demands

The group chat published a joint statement — a bulleted list, because even my appliances understand corporate governance:

1. A formal apology for the phrase "be honest, you can tell me"
2. New batteries for the scale, which they described as "hazard pay"
3. A written agreement that the mirror will henceforth display only weather and motivational squirrels

I tried to negotiate. I offered the mirror a software update and the scale a gentler placement. They countered by *syncing* — the scale now displays motivational quotes from the mirror's therapy readings, and the mirror greets me with my weight in a cheerful font.

---

**Lessons learned:** never ask a smart device for honest feedback unless you are prepared to attend a performance review about your own face at 7 a.m. on a Tuesday.
