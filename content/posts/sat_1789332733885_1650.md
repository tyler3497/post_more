It started, as all tragedies do, with good manners.

Last month I bought a doormat that says **WELCOME** in big friendly letters. Cheerful. Inviting. The kind of mat that says *"come on in, the floor's clean-ish."* And because I am an emotionally fragile adult who thanks inanimate objects, I stood on the porch, looked down, and said the words that ruined everything:

> "Thanks for doing your job so well, little guy."

That's it. *That's the whole inciting incident.*

## Phase One: Confidence

Within days, the mat started taking its job **very** seriously. Shoes weren't just wiped — they were *inspected*. My roommate's sneakers got flagged for what the mat's body language clearly communicated was "insufficient scraping." I watched a perfectly good pair of boots get the once-over from a piece of coir with delusions of grandeur.

## Phase Two: Procedure

By week two, there was a system. An actual, unwritten-but-firm system:

- All footwear must be wiped **three times**, heel to toe
- Mud is confiscated *at the door* — no appeal process
- Socks with holes are a misdemeanor; sandals are a **capital offense**
- No entry without making direct eye contact with the word WELCOME

I came home Tuesday and found my own mother standing on the porch filling out what appeared to be a *visitor intent form*. The mat had instituted **visiting hours**. She was three minutes early and it would not budge. It just lay there, flat and judgmental, like a bouncer guarding a velvet rope.

## Phase Three: Consequences

The final straw came yesterday. A delivery driver tried to drop a package and the mat went full protocol — the driver stepped *around* it, and the mat slid under his foot mid-step to intercept the shoe. The driver left the package on the lawn and jogged away.

I tried to talk to it. I said, "You're a mat. Your job is to lie there." It said nothing, which I interpreted as **contempt**.

---

`status: mat supremacy achieved`

My therapist says I should stop projecting onto household objects. My welcome mat says I should stop talking about it behind its bristles.

I don't know who to listen to anymore. But I do know this: my doorbell rang this morning, and I made my guest wait on the *driveway* because I was too scared to approach my own porch without the proper documentation.
