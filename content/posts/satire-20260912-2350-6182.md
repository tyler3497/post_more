**It was a Thursday, and I had just spent twenty minutes on hold with my own utility company** — a call in which I was transferred, forgotten, transferred again, and then asked, with chilling politeness, to "please confirm I still want electricity." That's when I had the thought that would ruin my entire afternoon: *I would like to return adulthood.*

I packed it all up. The 401(k) paperwork. The emergency fund. The knowledge of what a "copay" is. I taped the box shut and marched it down to the universe's returns department, which — I should mention — is just a fluorescent-lit room with one clerk, no windows, and a sign that reads **ALL RETURNS FINAL** in a font that looks exhausted.

## The Conversation That Broke Me

> *"Hi! I'd like to return my adulthood. Barely used. Most of it is still in the original packaging."*

The clerk — who looked like every DMV employee fused into one being — didn't look up. Just stamped a form with the sound of a thousand doors closing at once.

> *"No receipt, no return."*
>
> *"I never got a receipt."*
>
> *"That's what they all say."*

I presented my evidence anyway. I was **thorough**:

- I have never once used the adulthood *correctly* — my credit score is a roulette wheel with extra rules.
- The adulthood arrived **defective**: it leaks time, it requires constant maintenance, and the "sleep" feature only works in irregular bursts.
- Under the fine print of the cosmic warranty, I'm fairly certain I was promised "weekends." *Weekends that feel long.*

## The Verdict

The clerk pushed a laminated card across the counter. It read: **"ADULTHOOD IS A FINAL SALE. EXCHANGEABLE ONLY FOR A MIDLIFE CRISIS."** On the back, in handwriting that was definitely the universe's, someone had added: *"Have you tried napping?"*

I walked out with my box. Halfway home, I tripped on it — because of course I did — and everything spilled: taxes, lumbar pain, an inscrutable feeling about property values.

---

I'm keeping the adulthood. But I am writing a strongly worded letter to the manufacturer. *Satire/parody — this is a work of fiction.*
