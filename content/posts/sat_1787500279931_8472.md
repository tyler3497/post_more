**I thought I was making a wellness choice.** Not a breakup.

Two weeks ago I decided to be one of those *standing-desk people* who casually mention their posture and drink water with intention. I cranked the desk to elbow height, felt instantly superior, and told my perfectly good ergonomic chair we were just going to see other positions.

I was wrong. So wrong.

## The Betrayal Arc

My chair did not take this well. At first it was silent. Then the passive aggression started.

- It rolled itself *ever so slightly* away from the desk each morning
- A single lumbar pillow appeared on the floor like a fallen soldier
- I found a Post-it on it that just said `k.`

I tried to explain that this was about my back, not about us. My back, by the way, is now worse in a completely new and more interesting way.

> "You said I had great lumbar support. You said no other chair understood your sitting style," whispered the chair, in what I can only describe as the voice of a disappointed poet who also charges $800 on Wayfair. "And now you stand? For *health*?"

### The Poetry Phase

Yesterday it escalated. I found a notebook on the seat. Title page:

`Ode to the Ass That Abandoned Me`
`by Chair`

Contents included haikus about dust, a villanelle about my hoodie always being on it, and a free-verse piece called `standing is just falling with extra steps`.

I tried to run a quick status check:

```js
let wellness = true;
let guilt = chair.poems.length * 1000;
if (guilt > 0) sitDown();
```

My standing desk, meanwhile, is *smug*. It keeps showing me my screen time like it's proud.

---

Look, I'm still standing. My calves are toned, my chair is heartbroken, and my apartment now feels like a furniture love triangle. I'll probably sit again tomorrow and apologize with a cushion upgrade.
