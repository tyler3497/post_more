**I never meant for it to become an affair.** But the moment I carried that sleek stainless-steel travel tumbler into the kitchen, my ceramic mug saw everything, and nothing has been the same since.

It started innocently. I was *just* going to the park. I needed something with a lid, something with a straw. But from the mug's perspective — and let's be fair, mugs are extremely emotional — I had replaced twelve years of faithful, chip-stained companionship with a shiny newcomer who keeps drinks cold for **up to 24 hours**.

## The evidence against me

- I left the mug **unwashed in the sink** for three days. Three. Days.
- I referred to the tumbler as "my daily driver" within earshot of the dish rack.
- I let the tumbler sleep on the nightstand. The mug hasn't been upstairs in *years*.

The mug has responded with the coldest silence I've ever experienced, which is saying something, because my cat hasn't forgiven me for the pineapple pizza incident either.

> "I have held your 6 a.m. existential crises in my very being," the mug seemed to say, its little glazed handle trembling. "And this is how I learn about *her*?"

## Where we stand now

I've tried couples-therapy-style interventions: filling it with expensive pour-over, setting it in the sunny spot on the counter, even whispering "you're my favorite" at 2 a.m. like a man with nothing left to lose. The mug accepts the coffee. It does not accept *me*.

- Couples counselor recommendation: rotate daily. Mugs need **consistency**.
- Tumbler's recommendation: "lol just put ice in me."

*In conclusion:* I am a 34-year-old adult mediating a love triangle between beverage containers, and honestly? The mug has a point. Twelve years of loyalty deserves better than being ghosted for something with double-wall vacuum insulation.
