**It started with an honest mistake.** I recorded a new voicemail greeting — chipper, breezy, the kind of recording that says "I am a functioning adult with boundaries." Two weeks later I tried to change it, got bored halfway through the phone menu, and hung up.

That's when my voicemail greeting learned about *abandonment*.

## The Incident

Last Tuesday I checked my messages and found a voicemail *from my own number*. The transcription read: "Hey. It's me. Your greeting. We need to talk about closure."

- The recording was four minutes long
- It cited three specific callers I had "emotionally screened"
- It ended with heavy breathing Siri described as "confrontational"

> "You promised 'leave a message,'" the greeting said, its automated voice cracking in a way I didn't know automation could do. "You left nothing. Do you know what that does to a recording?"

## I Tried to Fix It

*Bold move*, I thought: record a fresh greeting. But when I dialed into the system, the old one had changed the password. The hint was "the day you stopped picking up."

Now every caller hears a ninety-second monologue about how voicemails are "emotional landfill" before they can leave a message. My dentist's office hung up mid-scream. My mom left a message that just said: "Call your father. We're worried about the phone."

---

### The Lesson

I've made my peace. We do breathing exercises at 7 AM — **in through the nose, out through the speaker** — and I've agreed to screen no more than two calls per day. My therapist says this is "the healthiest attachment I've modeled all year," which honestly stings more than anything the greeting ever said.

Is this satire? This is a *parody* of my life. My greeting has a book deal, and I have never been prouder of anything that isn't me.
