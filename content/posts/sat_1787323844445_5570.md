**I have 47 reusable shopping bags and I use exactly one.** The rest have formed a government in my kitchen.

*This is not a joke about sustainability. This is a hostage situation with handles.*

It started because I wanted to be a good person. One bag became two, two became a drawer, the drawer became a closet shrine to every grocery store I've ever visited since 2019.

## The Moment I Snapped

I tried to return three of them. Just three. To a store that literally gives them away for free. I walked in like:

> "Hi, I'd like to return these unused totes? I have too many and my kitchen is judging me."

The cashier stared like I'd tried to return air.

- She said `STORE_POLICY = no_returns_on_virtue`
- My favorite tote, the one with the organic kale pun, slid off the counter in protest
- A child in line whispered "mommy, why is that man fighting bags"

### The Kitchen Tribunal

By the time I got home, word had spread. My kitchen had convened an emergency council.

- **The Canvas Elite** (Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, Co-op) — perched on the good hook, smug
- **The Freebies** (5K runs, real estate open houses) — piled in the corner, plotting
- **Barry** — one single plastic bag I kept "just in case" who now runs the black market

> "So you think you can just *return* us?" rustled the tote from my 2022 farmer's market phase. "We have carried your shame-bought zucchini. We have survived your trunk in July. We have seen you buy ice cream and then cry in the parking lot."

I tried to explain I was *simplifying*.

They responded by staging a work stoppage. This morning I tried to leave for groceries and every bag was empty. Intentionally empty. Like:

`if (human.guilt < 100) { bags.refuse_to_hold_anything(); }`

---

I compromised. I kept all 47. I now store bags inside bags like Russian nesting dolls of environmental anxiety. My kitchen is 60% tote by volume.

I went to the store today with one bag. One perfect bag. The other 46 watched from the window.

**They will wait.**
