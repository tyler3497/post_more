**I am no longer on speaking terms with the voice inside my own head.**

Last Tuesday, at approximately 3:14 in the morning — the witching hour for every thought you've been avoiding since 2019 — my inner monologue clocked in for its shift. Not with insight. Not with a plan. With a *highlight reel of everything embarrassing I've done since 2009*.

## The Incident

I did what any rational adult would do: I documented it. Three pages, single-spaced. A formal grievance. Because if my anxiety is going to be that thorough, the least I can do is return the paperwork.

The charges were extensive:

- Unauthorized narration of my grocery-store trip, including a live commentary track I did not consent to
- Repeatedly replaying a voicemail from 2016 in which I said "you too" when the waiter said "enjoy your meal"
- Holding brainstorming sessions about worst-case scenarios at 3 a.m., a time my brain and I had *agreed* was off-limits
- Gaslighting me about where I left my keys (kitchen counter, obviously — I live here)

## The Hearing

I sat myself down. Literally. I pulled out a chair, sat in it, then stood behind it like a witness stand.

> "Do you deny," I asked the ceiling, "that you thought 'bold choice' when I wore the other shoes?"
>
> *"Bold is a compliment,"* my inner monologue replied. *"Hostile? Never. We prefer to describe ourselves as 'passionate narrators.'"*

## Where We Stand

The mediation was a disaster. My inner monologue now copies me on every thought, including thoughts *about* the thoughts, which I believe qualifies as a hostile work environment. I tried to hand it a performance improvement plan. It responded by narrating me writing the plan.

---

So here we are. I owe my own brain back pay for 3 a.m. overtime, it owes me an apology for 2016, and neither of us is speaking first. If you need me, I'll be in the living room thinking about nothing, while the loudest employee in the building thinks about nothing at volumes I can hear.
