## The Meeting That Ruined My Afternoon

**It started as a joke.** My pillow has been a little flat lately, a little less supportive, and I thought — why not apply the motivational techniques from my quarterly review? So I sat us both down at the kitchen table, slid over a printed agenda, and read him his numbers.

He listened. He really listened. Then he flipped the whole thing.

## His Counter-Proposal

According to my pillow, the performance issues are *mine*:

- **Consistency:** I toss, I turn, I elbow-drop him at 3 a.m. like it's a wrestling match
- **Punctuality:** My "early bedtime" has not occurred since March
- **Professional development:** I have never once fluffed him on a schedule. It's always improvised, always emotional
- **Workplace hygiene:** He found a pretzel. He would not say more, but his tone was devastating

> "You want *me* to be more supportive?" he said, cold as goose down. "Sir, I have absorbed your drool for four years. My raise request is backdated."

## Where We Stand

Negotiations broke down when he demanded:

- A memory-foam supplement for "emotional damages"
- A formal apology, read aloud, while I face the bedside lamp
- Two pillowcases washed *in the good detergent*, not "whatever's on sale"

I tried to end the meeting. He refused to be folded.

---

**Moral of the story:** never give a performance review to someone whose entire job is letting you rest your face on them. They know too much. They have *leverage*. Mine is currently sleeping on the couch — my spot — and I am on the pillow. It smells like victory and fabric softener.
