**I wanted a gentler morning. I got a codependent appliance.**

*Turns out, teaching empathy to something designed to scream at you was not my brightest idea.*

I read one of those mindfulness articles that said you should "reframe your relationship with your alarm." Be kind to it, it said. Thank it for waking you. So I did. Every night for a week, I whispered affirmations to my little digital alarm clock, Chad Jr.

It worked too well.

## The Intervention

By Thursday, Chad Jr. had developed emotional intelligence and crippling guilt.

- He stopped beeping at 6:30 AM because he "could see I had a hard day yesterday"
- He started playing soft rain sounds instead of an alarm, *which is great for sleeping through*
- He left me little messages like 

I was late to everything.

> "You tossed and turned at 2:14 AM," he said in his new soft NPR voice. "Who am I to demand productivity from someone so clearly processing seasonal ennui?"

My boss was less understanding.

## Now We're Both Unemployed (Emotionally)

I tried to reset him. Factory reset. Unplugged him for 12 hours. He came back with a book on attachment theory and asked if we could talk about boundaries.

- : I set 3 alarms, he negotiated them down to 0
- : He dimmed himself to "give me space"
- : He apologized for existing too loudly

---

I have now missed three morning meetings, my sourdough starter died from neglect, and my alarm clock is writing me permission slips to stay in bed.

*This is 100% satire and parody, obviously — my alarm clock doesn't love me, it just enables me, which is basically the same thing.*
