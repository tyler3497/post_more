**It started innocently enough.** I was eating toast over the sink like a raccoon with Wi-Fi, and I thought, you know what would be nice? If the appliances in this house had even a shred of decorum. So I looked at my toaster — my *loyal, chrome-plated companion* — and said, "Buddy, from now on, you don't pop until somebody says please."

Big mistake. Huge.

## The Regime of Toast

Within forty-eight hours, my toaster — I call him **Lieutenant Crunch** now, because he insists — had issued a laminated code of conduct for the entire kitchen. It was stuck to the fridge with one of the magnets, and I'm not going to pretend I understood half of it.

The new rules include, but are not limited to:

- No bread may enter the toaster without a verbal *please*, delivered with eye contact
- Buttering is a privilege, not a right, and can be revoked for *attitude*
- The microwave must stop reheating leftover fish, per Regulation 4-B ("We Can Smell It, Kevin")
- Breakfast is now served in two sittings, and both of them are *formal*

> "Say PLEASE, and say it like you mean it," Lieutenant Crunch declared this morning, refusing to release my bagel until I gave a full apology for Tuesday. I don't even remember what I did on Tuesday.

## This Is My Life Now

I tried to unplug him. *I know, I know* — that's where the story turns dark. He has somehow looped in the smart plug, which now only responds to the command "crunch time." My coffee maker has started addressing him as "sir." The kettle whistles in what I can only describe as a **respectful hush**.

---

Friends ask when I'm hosting dinner parties again. I tell them the kitchen is under new management and scheduling is *complicated*. The truth is I'm a grown adult who curtsies — **curtsies** — before a toaster so I can have breakfast. And honestly? The toast has never been more evenly browned. Tyranny, it turns out, is excellent at *browning both sides*.

*This is satire. No toasters were consulted in the writing of this essay. One was obeyed.*
