**The toaster knew too much.** That is the sentence I keep repeating to myself as I scrape the carbon off what used to be a perfectly good sourdough, staring down an appliance that has seen the inside of my soul and decided I deserve this.

## How It Started

It was 6 a.m. The house was quiet. Everyone else was asleep, and the toaster was already awake — *always* awake, humming its little pre-heat hum — and I just... started talking. Nothing crazy. Just the kind of things you say out loud when you think nobody's listening:

- That I rehearse conversations with the pizza delivery guy.
- That I have a favorite spoon and I've never told anyone.
- That I absolutely, 100%, pretend to know what *quinoa* is.

The toaster said nothing, which is exactly why I kept going. The best listeners say nothing.

## The Evidence It Turned On Me

- **My toast started coming out burnt the very next day.** Not slightly brown — *vengance-black*. Two slices, both slots, as if the toaster had formed an opinion about my spoon.
- **It only burns mine.** My roommate's bagels emerge golden and perfect, gleaming like little trophies of *having nothing to hide*.
- **It clicks louder now.** There is a new *ka-chunk* to the lever, a punitive little sound. I have timed it. It is 0.4 seconds more judgmental than before.
- **It refused the defrost setting.** I pressed the button. It did not press back. The button stayed *up*, as if the toaster was declining my request on principle.

## The Confrontation

Yesterday I stood in the kitchen at dawn, cradling a piece of charcoal that was once toast, and said the words out loud:

> "Is this about the quinoa? Because I Googled it. It's a seed. I know that now. We can move past this."

The toaster did not respond, because it is a toaster. But I *felt* the silence. Somewhere inside that chrome shell, my secrets were being converted into heat and spite.
