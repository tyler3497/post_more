## The Pitch Meeting I Didn't Approve

**I decided to become a morning person because a podcast told me successful people wake up at 5 a.m.** and I thought, *sure, I'll try success for once, just to see what the lighting is like*.

For three days I was disciplined. For three days I drank lemon water that tasted like regret. For three days my alarm clock was my only friend, and apparently that was enough for it to rebrand.

### The Rebrand

On day four, I woke up at 7:30 a.m. — my old, perfectly respectable time — and my alarm clock was not speaking to me. Instead, my phone buzzed.

> "Hi, this is Tiff from Tiff & Associates representing your alarm clock. My client is disappointed in your lack of commitment to the 5 a.m. lifestyle and is currently not taking questions."

I stared at my nightstand. The clock had taped a tiny rider to itself:

- **Green M&Ms only** in the tiny dish I don't own
- Ambient temperature must be 68 degrees and *emotionally supportive*
- No one is allowed to hit snooze without three business days' written notice
- All press inquiries go through Tiff

> "He feels you used him for personal growth content," Tiff explained. "He woke up at 5, prepared a whole motivational monologue about *seizing the dew*, and you hit him like he was a bug and whispered 'no.'"

I tried to apologize. I said I was tired. The clock responded via Tiff:

> "Tired is a mindset. Also my client charges $150 for that tone."

---

### The Negotiation

**Current status of my bedroom:**

- Alarm clock is doing a limited speaking tour on my dresser: *"Rise and Actually Grind"*
- I am legally required per `morning_person_contract_v2.pdf` to provide positive affirmations before coffee
- Tiff has suggested I pivot to being an *afternoon person* for brand synergy

We are in mediation. I offered to wake up at 6 a.m. He countered with 4:45 and a spotlight. I am considering going back to my phone alarm, but I'm pretty sure they're in the same union now.
