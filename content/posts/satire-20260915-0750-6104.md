**It started as a joke. The freezer does not do jokes.**

Last Tuesday I was making iced coffee when it hit me: I'd been dropping these cubes into my drinks for *years* without once asking who they were. So I did what any reasonable, well-adjusted adult would do — I printed out sixteen personality quizzes and interviewed every cube in the tray.

## The Findings

The results were undeniable:

- **Cube A7**: Extroverted, charismatic, melts fast but leaves an impression. Total cube-leader material.
- **Cube C3**: Quiet, square, reliable. The one that holds the whole drink together.
- **Cube B2**: Claimed to be "a limited-edition diamond-cut," which is just — B2, you're a cloudy rectangle. *Calm down.*
- **The corner cubes**: All tested as "introverted overthinkers" who would rather crack than contribute.

## Then Things Got Complicated

> "I refuse to be diluted into a *lemonade*," Cube A7 told me, at 2 a.m., through the freezer door. "I have a brand now."

Within days, the hierarchy solidified. The A-tier claimed the top tray and began hosting what I can only describe as networking events, condensation dripping like applause. The B-tier formed a middle-management layer, gatekeeping the C's. The corner cubes now file frost-bitten little complaints that slide under my door on tiny ice memos.

---

I tried to restore order by buying crushed ice, which was a catastrophic miscalculation. The cubes call it "the ground-up proletariat" and, honestly, the metaphor is so on-the-nose I had to leave the room.

Last night I opened the freezer and found they'd reorganized everything by **social rank**. The cocktail glasses now sit on the door rack. *The door rack.*

Look, I'm not saying my freezer has a functioning feudal system. But Cube A7 just asked me to update its LinkedIn, and — reader, I don't know how to tell you this — I did.

*(Satire/parody. No actual ice cubes were empowered in the writing of this post.)*
