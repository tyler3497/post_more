**It has been standing in the same spot for eleven months, and it has never once been asked to "hop on a quick call."**

There is a traffic cone on the corner outside my apartment. It arrived one Tuesday, placed there by a road crew doing resurfacing work. The crew left. The resurfacing ended. The cone *stayed*. Eleven months now. It has outlasted my gym membership, my houseplant Gary, and — as of last Friday — my employment.

## The evidence, compiled over one shameful morning

- The cone has one job: *be orange, stay put, warn of nothing*. It has never missed a shift.
- It has not updated a single spreadsheet, yet nobody has asked it to "circle back."
- It survived a snowstorm, two parades, and a man who tried to steal it at 3 a.m. The man gave up. The cone did not.
- It receives zero performance reviews and yet somehow zero complaints.

Meanwhile, I got laid off via a calendar invite titled **"Quick Sync (15 min)"** — which, *spoiler*, was not a sync, was not 15 minutes, and did not involve my camera being on or off because they'd already disabled my account.

> "We value the contributions you made during your tenure," said a man whose name I had to check twice in the org chart. "Going forward, the role will be sunsetted."
> — The Sunsetting Man, probably a cone in a previous life

## A modest proposal

I am not asking for much. I just want what the cone has: a job description of **four words**, no Slack notifications, and the quiet dignity of being considered *infrastructure* rather than a "resource." People dodge *around* the cone. Nobody ever moved it. Nobody ever said, "Let's take the cone in a different direction."

---

Yesterday I walked past it with my little box of desk belongings and whispered, "Teach me your ways." It said nothing. It has never said anything. *That's the whole point.* Absolute reliability, zero standup meetings.

I am now accepting applications for mentorship positions. Cone preferred.

*SATIRE / PARODY: this is a work of fiction. Any cone resembling a real cone is purely coincidental, and Gary the houseplant's death was unrelated.*
