**I downloaded a meditation app because my smartwatch said my stress level was "feral raccoon in a tote bag."**

*Which, rude, but accurate.*

The app promised inner peace in just five minutes. I thought, great, I can finally become one of those serene people who drinks tea slowly and doesn't spiral when the grocery self-checkout says "unexpected item."

So I sat down, closed my eyes, breathed.

## Phase One: The Betrayal

My brain, which has never once been quiet in its entire career, immediately:

- replayed that time I said "you too" when the server said "enjoy your food" in 2017
- tried to remember if I left the stove on (I don't have a gas stove)
- composed a 12-part apology to my houseplant for underwatering it in March
- opened `brain.exe` and ran `overthink --all --forever`

> "Welcome to Inner Peace," the app whispered. "Let your thoughts drift by like clouds."

My thoughts drifted by like clouds that had just been fired and were now organizing.

> "Guys, he's trying to ignore us," said Anxiety, who sounds exactly like me but with more reverb. "Quick, group chat without him?"

*I could literally hear the notification ping inside my own skull.*

## Phase Two: They Made a Slack Channel

Apparently while I was om-ing, my intrusive thoughts created `#tyler-brain-general` and muted me.

They have:
- a shared doc titled `Things We Definitely Need to Panic About at 3AM`
- a poll: `Are we haunted or just dehydrated?`
- and a pinned message that just says `lol he's breathing manually now`

---

**I opened my eyes after five minutes feeling worse.**

The app said "Great job! Your mind is clearer!" My mind was in a breakout room plotting Q3 anxiety goals.

I am now paying $12.99 a month for my own thoughts to exclude me from the conversation.

*SATIRE / PARODY — 100% fictional, no meditation apps were harmed, my brain is still loud but we are working on our communication issues.*
