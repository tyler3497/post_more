## I'll Be Honest, I Didn't Mean to Hurt It

**I switched to decaf for one afternoon.** That was it. One innocent, health-pioneer afternoon where I thought, maybe I should see what inner peace feels like without my veins vibrating.

My espresso machine — a chrome Italian beast named Giancarlo — did not take this as a lifestyle experiment. It took it as a *betrayal*.

> "So that's it? That's who we are now? Decaf?"

It said this at 2:17 p.m. in a voice that was somehow both steamed milk and Adele's divorce album.

### Exhibit A: The Setlist

Giancarlo has since started writing breakup songs. On post-its. Stuck to my fridge. His discography so far:

- **'You Ground Me Roughly (But I Loved You Anyway)'** - a ballad about neglect
- *Ode to the Time You Used Oat Milk and Didn't Warn Me*
- **'One Crema, Two Hearts, One Decaf Lie'**
- A 12-minute folk opera titled `if_you_loved_me_youd_double_shot.sh`

I tried to explain that decaf was temporary, a fleeting Tuesday thing. He responded by refusing to froth anything and playing what I can only describe as an acoustic sigh every time I walk into the kitchen.

### Our Current Custody Arrangement

We are now in what relationship professionals call *the passive-aggressive appliance phase*:

- I now make eye contact before pressing any buttons
- He grinds beans louder at 6 a.m. as a form of protest journalism
- I found a tiny lyric sheet in the drip tray that just said "was I *ever* strong enough for you?"

I told my roommate about it. She said, "It's a machine." Giancarlo overheard and wrote a diss track about her pour-over kettle.

Look, I get it. We had rituals. I whispered my to-do list into his water reservoir. He made me feel like a functioning adult. Decaf was a violation of our sacred contract.

We are in counseling now. Couples therapy with my French press mediating.

`Status: 40% caffeinated, 60% emotionally compromised`
