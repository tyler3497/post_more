**Three months ago, I founded a book club. This week, for the ninth consecutive meeting, nobody had read the book.**

I'm not exaggerating. Not one page. Linda didn't even *own* the book. Dave brought a cookbook by mistake and somehow led the most passionate discussion of the night.

## The Rules (Yes, There Are Rules)

- Nobody reads the book. This is **non-negotiable**.
- You must attend having consumed *something* about the book: the cover, a review, the author's haircut, a dream you had that was probably about it.
- If someone accidentally reads the book, they are thanked for their service and asked to leave. Kevin did this once. We still talk about it in hushed tones.

## What We Discussed Instead

The night we were supposed to discuss *The Midnight Library*, we spent two hours on:

- Whether the library in the book charges late fees
- Linda's neighbor's fence dispute
- A ranking of everyone present by "library energy"
- Snacks. Always snacks.

> "This is the most intellectually honest group I've ever belonged to," Dave said, holding the cookbook like a sacred text. "Every other book club is just a wine club with homework. We cut out the homework."

## The Revelation

Here's what I learned: the book was never the point. The point was **Thursday**. The point was eight people who would otherwise be doom-scrolling in separate apartments, gathered in my living room arguing about whether the protagonist's mother "had main-character energy."

*Is this cheating? Is this fraud?* The local library thought so — until they asked to join.

**Attendance:** nine straight weeks. **Books read:** zero. **Regrets:** none.
