**In retrospect, naming my dog as my emergency contact was less a responsible adult decision and more a confession that I have no friends.**

It started with a form. You know the one — that HR onboarding packet where they ask, *"In case of emergency, who should we call?"* And I thought, honestly, who knows me best? Who is most likely to pick up? Who has literally never missed a meal, a walk, or a moment to stare at me while I sleep?

My dog. My 40-pound golden retriever, Biscuit, became my emergency contact at 2:14 p.m. on a Tuesday.

## Why Biscuit Was the Obvious Choice

- **Availability:** He is home 24/7. He has no job, no hobbies, no prior commitments beyond barking at the mail slot.
- **Loyalty:** He follows me room to room. If I faint in the bathroom, he will be there — *dramatically* there.
- **He always answers my calls:** A bold claim. A claim that has now been tested and found to be *catastrophically* false.

Because here's the thing: this weekend I locked myself out of my own car while it was still running, and HR — bless them — followed procedure. They called Biscuit.

> "He just looked at the phone," my neighbor reports. "He tilted his head, sniffed the voicemail, and went back to sleep on the couch that is definitely not the couch he's allowed on."

## The Phone Call That Never Came

Biscuit's phone — a retired flip phone I clipped to his collar as a bit — has now received **eleven** calls from HR. Eleven. He has responded to zero. He did, however, manage to call 911 once with his nose, and I am still explaining that to animal control.

I asked him about it last night, point blank:

> Me: *"Biscuit, the whole point of an emergency contact is that you ANSWER."*
> Biscuit: *licks his paw, maintains eye contact, says nothing — the exact energy of every man I've ever dated.*

---

So now HR has flagged me as a liability, my neighbor has joined a group chat about it, and Biscuit has been formally removed from the emergency contact list and replaced with my mom, who answered on the first ring and immediately asked what I did wrong.
