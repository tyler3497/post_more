**I have made a terrible mistake, and that mistake was believing my wrinkles were the problem.**

I ironed a shirt. One shirt. *One.* It was a Tuesday, I had a Zoom call, I wanted to look like someone who knows what a 401(k) is.

Reader, my closet took this personally.

## The Incident

I pulled out the ironing board — that seldom-used, rickety altar of adulthood I bought in 2021 and have used twice — and I pressed that shirt like I was on a British baking show finale. Crisp collar. Sharp seams. I even did the sleeves. *I did the sleeves.*

I hung it back up, feeling smug and employable. Then I closed the closet door.

When I opened it the next morning:

- My hoodies had retreated to the far corner like shamed interns
- My graphic tee that says "I PAUSED MY GAME FOR THIS" was somehow inside-out, presumably in protest
- My favorite sweatpants were gone — just *gone* — replaced by a printed note that said `DRESS CODE VIOLATION`

> "We had a meeting while you were sleeping," my blazer whispered from its hanger. "We decided we are now a Formal Establishment."

*It did not whisper. It's a blazer. But the vibe was unmistakable.*

## The New Regime

My closet now operates under what it calls **Operation Crisp & Unforgiving**:

- No elastic waistbands before 5pm
- All t-shirts must have collars, points, or at least an alibi
- Socks must match AND have career goals

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered a compromise: Business Casual Fridays. I even ironed a second shirt to show good faith, which was apparently the wrong move because it was interpreted as "ratifying the treaty."

Now I have to wear loafers to take out the trash. My slippers filed for unemployment. My laundry hamper has started starching my socks without permission.

*Honestly, it looks great.* I look like a substitute teacher who lost control of his life but kept control of his cuffs.

**Update:** I tried to put on a hoodie this morning and my iron fell off the shelf and blocked the doorway. Coincidence? My closet says it's *[SATIRE / PARODY — entirely fictional, not real life, please don't iron-shame me]*.
