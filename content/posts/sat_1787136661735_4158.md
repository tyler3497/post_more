**I finally organized my Tupperware drawer and I deeply regret giving my leftovers a sense of order.**

After three years of what experts call *aggressive drawer-based chaos*, I decided to become a person who has matching lids. Sunday, 2pm. I dumped everything out, matched every container, and stacked them like a tiny, smug city skyline.

My fridge immediately unionized.

## The New Regime

By Monday morning, my leftovers had established governance:

- Monday's quinoa now requires a reservation and `business casual` attire
- Tuesday's soup brought a +1 and is being *very* vague about it
- The lone pickle from last month has declared itself mayor
- My spaghetti keeps reheating itself for attention

> "We didn't ask for structure," said the roasted broccoli, speaking on behalf of the vegetable drawer. "We were thriving in beautiful anarchy. Now you want us to file paperwork?"

### How It Escalated

I labeled the lids. I know. I bought a label maker on sale and thought *this will fix me*. I labeled one "GOOD DECISIONS ONLY" and now it only holds kale.

The Tupperware drawer has split into factions:

- **Team Snap-Lid**: elitist, airtight, judges my other choices
- **Team Stained-From-Tomato-Sauce-2019**: humble, battle-tested, actually fits in the microwave

---

I tried to pack lunch this morning. My chicken salad asked if I had an appointment. I told it I was just trying to eat. It said:

> "That's exactly what someone without a seating chart would say."

My mom texted to ask if I'm okay. I sent her a photo of my drawer. She replied "honey no."
