**I thought buying a $3.99 reusable grocery bag would save the planet. I did not realize I was adopting a traumatized coworker with opinions.**

For three years, this bag has been my loyal, slightly stained companion. It has survived Trader Joe's runs, Costco apocalypse-prep trips, and that one time I tried to buy 20 lbs of potatoes because they were on sale. *Which, in retrospect, was the beginning of the end.*

This morning, it finally snapped.

## The Incident Report

I was packing my usual chaotic Sunday haul when I noticed the bag was... sighing. Audibly. Like a veteran barista.

According to its very formal, very passive-aggressive resignation letter (written in leaking oat milk on my countertop), here is what it has endured:

- The Great Egg Catastrophe of 2024 where I put eggs on the bottom and then stacked canned beans on top
- Being used as an emergency laundry hamper, beach tote, and once, as a very bad umbrella
- Transporting a rotisserie chicken that leaked what I can only describe as *chicken trauma* into its lining permanently
- Witnessing me buy single-use plastic bags anyway because I "forgot it in the car" — 47 documented times

> "You said I was 'eco-friendly.' I'm starting to think you meant *emotionally* disposable. I have seen things no canvas should see. That bulk kale? We don't talk about the bulk kale incident."
> — My Grocery Bag, who apparently has been journaling

It has now requested:

- `PTO: 2 weeks, somewhere without produce`
- Hazard pay for all frozen food aisle exposure
- Immediate retirement to a tasteful hook where it just holds scarves

---

Look, I respect boundaries. I told it we could talk about work-life balance. It said it wants to pursue its dream of being a tote that only carries library books and expensive farmers market peaches.

I tried to compromise. I offered to wash it. It said:

> "Washing won't wash away what I've *seen*."

Honestly? Fair.
