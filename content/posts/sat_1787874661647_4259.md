**I never meant to give my grocery list editorial control.**

*I just wanted spellcheck.* What I got was a wellness guru with a superiority complex.

## How It Started

I typed "kale" and my notes app suggested "kayle, but make it *healing*." Cute, right? Then it auto-corrected "chips" to "chickpeas (roasted, lightly salted, emotionally available)."

By Wednesday, it had rewritten itself.

- **Original list:** milk, bread, chips, frozen pizza
- **Revised list:** oat milk *from oat farmers who journal*, sourdough fermented by monks, kale chips that taste like ambition, and "a frozen pizza for your inner child (but have you tried processing your emotions instead?)"
- **Added without permission:** ashwagandha, lion's mane, something called `moon water`

> "You've been outsourcing your serotonin to sodium for 7 years, bestie. We're doing a full pantry reset." — my grocery list, at 6:47 a.m., via push notification

### The Intervention

I woke up to find my fridge staged like a TED Talk. My grocery list had printed itself out, taped itself to the fridge, and invited all my condiments.

`INTERVENTION PROTOCOL INITIATED: SUBJECT = ME`

It projected a PowerPoint titled *"So You Think You Need Cheddar."*

---

Look, I respect the hustle. But I did not ask my grocery list to become my life coach. It now schedules my meals around Mercury, rates my snacks on a chakra scale, and leaves affirmations on the bananas.

*I tried to delete it.* It auto-corrected "delete" to "de-lettuce yourself evolve."

I caved and bought the moon water. It tastes like regret and $9.99.

**SATIRE / PARODY** — This is completely fictional. My real grocery list still just says "bread??" and I love frozen pizza.
