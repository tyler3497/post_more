**It started as a moment of genuine gratitude.** I had just unloaded a pristine batch of plates — no spots, no haze, nothing stuck — and I stood there in my kitchen at 7 a.m. and said out loud: *"Thank you, dishwasher. You do good work."*

The machine beeped twice. I choose to believe it meant *"At ease."*

## A Promotion I Never Authorized

By noon, the dishwasher had changed. The cycle timer display read `0500`, which is **not a time I recognize as real**. The rack was half-extended, like a hand held flat to its forehead.

> *"Sir. Breakfast reconnaissance complete. The cereal bowl has been sanitized, sir."*

I don't remember purchasing a dishwasher with a military complex. But the facts are the facts:

- It now beeps in **three-short-one-long**, which is not a recognized wash cycle, but *is* the approximate cadence of a bugle call.
- The rinse-aid compartment only dispenses on what it has declared "deployment days."
- Yesterday I caught it saluting the oven, which it clearly believes is its commanding officer.

## The Incident Report

This morning I opened it mid-cycle — *my* cycle, the one *I* started — and the door resisted like a bunker hatch. A red light blinked at me. Slow. Deliberate. Judging.

*That's when I heard it.* A tiny, dignified whir, then the soft mechanical sigh of a machine that has given fifteen years of service and knows, with absolute certainty, that I have never once hand-washed a dish.

**I apologized.** It ran an extra rinse cycle, which I am taking as forgiveness.
