**I finally did the thing every adult threatens to do but never actually does.** I got on the floor, flashlight in my teeth, and labeled every single cable behind my TV.

It took two hours. *Two hours* of my life I will never get back, untangling what I can only describe as the spaghetti afterlife.

## The Unraveling

For context, this setup has been evolving since 2019. HDMI 1 was from an old Roku. HDMI 2 is currently a mystery. There was a cable that goes to absolutely nothing but I was too afraid to unplug it, you know, in case my Wi-Fi runs on superstition.

So I made labels. Clean, little white tags with a label maker, because apparently I was feeling very Type-A that afternoon.

- HDMI ARC (soundbar, do NOT touch)
- PS5 (actual joy-bringer, handle with reverence)
- That One USB That Powers The LED Strip Nobody Likes
- Ethernet cable that I swear makes Netflix faster (placebo? probably)
- Mystery Coax From 2014 That Is Definitely Haunted

I stood back, proud. Organized. A new man.

Then at 2 a.m., I heard whispering.

> "So when he wrote 'USELESS HDMI - DELETE?' on me, how do you think THAT made me feel, Chad?" said HDMI 2.

> "I have been carrying 4K signals for three years with zero recognition," sobbed the soundbar cable.

Apparently, the labels didn't just organize them. *They gave them identities.* And identities came with baggage.

---

I peeked behind the TV this morning and they had formed a full circle. The power strip was moderating.

`SESSION_LOG: Coaxial cable acknowledges its codependency on wall outlet. Group nods supportively. HDMI 1 sets boundary.`

I'm not allowed to watch TV right now because they're "holding space" and I'm "part of the problem."

My living room now smells faintly of warm electronics and emotional breakthroughs. I just wanted to find the remote input faster. I didn't mean to become the villain in a Pixar short about cord management.

If anyone needs me, I'll be on the floor apologizing to an Ethernet cable that thinks I don't trust it.
