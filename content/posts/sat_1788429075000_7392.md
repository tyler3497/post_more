**BREAKING: I have become emotionally dependent on a drawer.**

After three years of what experts are calling "aggressive avoidance," I finally organized my junk drawer. You know the one. Every house has one. Mine contained 47 batteries of unknown charge, a single chopstick, and a profound sense of shame.

I didn't just organize it. I *transformed* it.

## The Rebirth

I bought one of those little bamboo organizer trays from the internet. I sorted. I labeled. I made a spreadsheet. For a drawer.

Suddenly, everything had a purpose:

- The rubber bands are now sorted by *optimism level* (very stretchy = very hopeful)
- The scissors have their own velvet corner — they requested it
- The dead batteries have been given a dignified retirement community in a separate baggie
- The tape dispenser is now management

*I realize how this sounds.*

But here's where it gets weird. The drawer started acting... proud.

> "Look at us," it seemed to whisper, in a voice that was definitely just the house settling. "We're so put-together now. You should list me as your emergency contact."

I laughed. Then I paused. Because honestly? That drawer knows more about me than most people. It knows I own six identical IKEA Allen wrenches and zero ability to throw them away.

---

It's been two days. I now find myself consulting the drawer before major decisions.

`if (life_choice == questionable) { ask_drawer(); }`

My friends are concerned. My cat is jealous of the attention. The drawer, meanwhile, has never looked better.

I think I have to let it add me on LinkedIn. It's earned it.

*This is a work of satire. My drawer is not actually sentient. Although if it were, it would definitely be better at replying to emails than I am.*
