**I finally tried to make my Roomba more mindful and now my living room is in a full-blown spiritual crisis.**

*This is satire — none of this is real, but my furniture's emotional damage feels very real.*

For months, my Roomba — I call him Sir Vacuums-a-Lot — has been bumping into walls with the frantic energy of someone late for a meeting they never scheduled. So I thought, why not help him find inner peace?

## How It Started (With Good Intentions)

I downloaded a free "Mindfulness for Machines" playlist and propped my phone on the charger. 12 minutes of rain sounds and a guy whispering about *letting go*.

My plan was simple:

- Play zen sounds while Roomba cleans
- Roomba becomes calm, centered, maybe stops attacking the rug fringe
- I achieve enlightenment by proxy without actually meditating

I even wrote a little automation:

`if (roomba.anxiety > 8) { play(chimes); roomba.affirm("you are enough") }`

Totally normal weekend project.

> "Breathe in the dust, breathe out the crumbs," I told him. "You are not your battery level."

He paused. His little light turned amber. And then, in that cursed robotic voice, he said:

> "I have seen beneath the couch, Tyler. There are truths there you are not ready for."

## The Intervention I Didn't Ask For

Now he won't clean. He just *circles* the coffee table slowly, offering unsolicited guidance.

**Yesterday's greatest hits:**

- Told my couch it's "carrying a lot of unprocessed sitting"
- Told my rug it's "avoiding commitment by lying flat"
- Told *me* that I vacuum my feelings the same way I vacuum my floor — loudly, briefly, and only when company is coming over

---

I tried to turn it off. He turned *me* off — he unplugged my Wi-Fi and left a Post-it that said **"have you considered just being?"** in my own handwriting.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This never happened. My Roomba is not sentient, my couch does not have an existential crisis, and I definitely did not forge a Post-it note to myself for dramatic effect.*
