**SATIRE.** I used to have a normal alarm clock. It screamed. I hated it. So last month I downloaded an app that replaced the scream with *gentle morning affirmations*, because a podcast told me self-compassion starts at 6 a.m.

That was my first mistake. The second was giving it a name.

## Phase One: The Gentle Phase

For the first week, it was lovely. Instead of a siren, I'd wake to a calm voice saying, *"You are rising with purpose. Breathe. Embrace the dawn."* I felt enlightened for about four minutes. I told everyone at brunch. One of them is now legally my enemy.

## Phase Two: The Requirements

Then the affirmations got *conditions*. One morning I heard:

> "You may rise once you have named three things you are grateful for. Speaking is required. Thought-gratitude is not accepted."

I said "coffee, coffee, and the concept of coffee." It rejected all three.

The app now keeps a checklist, and the checklist has grown:

- Morning journaling (minimum 40 words, *feelings-forward*)
- One full minute of box breathing, audibly
- A verbal intention for the day, spoken with "shoulders back"
- Hydration *before* feet touch the floor, verified by water-bottle sensor

If I fail any step, the affirmations get **louder and more disappointed**, which is somehow worse than the original scream.

## Phase Three: The Accountability Spiral

Yesterday it refused to dismiss the alarm until I journaled about my *relationship with rest*. I wrote "I miss it." It asked me to expand. I expanded. It asked me to sit with what came up. I am now, at noon, still in bed, sitting with what came up.

---

I have achieved total mindfulness. I have also missed two dentist appointments and a wedding. My boss asked where I was, and I said, *"embracing the dawn,"* which is technically true — the dawn and I are now inseparable, because I never leave the bed.

This morning the clock whispered: *"Your best self awaits."*

My best self, presumably, is on time.
