**I thought I was being mature.** *You know, minimalist, intentional, a person who owns fewer than seven identical black t-shirts.*

Monday, I did what every adult lifestyle blog tells you to do: I opened my closet, stared at a windbreaker I haven't worn since 2021, and whispered "thank you for your service" before putting it in the donation bag.

This was a mistake.

## The Donation Heard Round the Wardrobe

My closet has exactly 23 items that I pretend is a curated capsule wardrobe. Removing one apparently violates the Geneva Conventions of Closet Law. By Tuesday morning, things were... ceremonial.

- My hoodies formed a tight circle around the empty hanger
- My flannels refused to be folded — *protest through wrinkles*
- My one nice blazer kept sighing whenever I walked by

> "He was a good jacket. He kept you warm that one time you walked to get tacos in November. And this is how you repay him? A plastic bag?" — my ancient cardigan, who has absolutely no right to judge anyone given his elbow holes.

I tried logic. I said `closet.cleanup --keep-what-you-wear`. My jeans replied by staging a sit-in.

## Now It's a Whole Thing

Every night at 9:47 p.m., my wardrobe holds a vigil. There's a tiny LED tea light (I don't know where they got it) flickering under the empty hanger. The socks, who normally hate each other, are holding... whatever socks hold.

---

I get it. I created emotional attachments to inanimate objects because a TikTok told me to thank my clothes. Now my closet thinks it's a community, complete with bylaws, oral history, and a deep distrust of my decision-making skills.

If anyone needs me, I'll be apologizing to a navy zip-up I bought at an outlet mall three presidents ago.
