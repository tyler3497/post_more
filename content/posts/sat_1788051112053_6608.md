**I filed my taxes early. Not because I'm responsible, but because I wanted my houseplants to respect me.**

You have to understand — my monstera, Kevin, has been *judging* me since February. I came home late from Taco Bell and he was drooping in that very specific way that says *we need to talk about your life choices*.

So I decided to do one impressive adult thing in front of them.

## Operation: Responsible Human

My logic was airtight:

- If I do taxes early, I look organized
- If I look organized, my plants will photosynthesize harder out of admiration
- If they grow faster, I can finally justify that $47 moss pole I bought

I sat them all down in the living room, opened TurboTax, and announced, *"Watch and learn, this is how a provider behaves."*

> "We have been here for 18 months and you have never once fertilized us on schedule, and now you want applause for clicking 'I agree' on a W-2?" — Kevin, probably

They were *riveted*. My pothos actually unfurled a new leaf. Or it was dying. Hard to tell with pothos — very dramatic.

## The Aftermath

I hit submit at 10:43 a.m. on a Tuesday. The IRS said "accepted." My fern made a sound.

And now — and this is the part no financial influencer warns you about — **they think we're rich.**

- My succulent parked itself in front of my south-facing window and is demanding hazard pay for direct sun
- The snake plant submitted a PTO request for winter
- Kevin has started dropping leaves on my laptop keyboard like *invoices*
- My air plant, who does literally nothing, has retained legal counsel

I tried to explain that a tax *refund* is not a *bonus* and definitely not a *plant trust fund*. I tried to show them my bank app. Kevin rustled.

Last night I caught them all leaning toward the router. I think they're looking up how to unionize.

I told my roommate and he just said:

> "Dude, you anthropomorphized your way into a labor dispute. That's on you."
