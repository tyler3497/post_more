## The Shelf Glow-Up That Ended My Social Life

**I finally decided to become an adult with aesthetic bookshelves** instead of the classic *stack of unread good intentions leaning against the wall* look I've been rocking since 2019.

I spent three hours sorting by color. Red to violet. Instagram-ready. My mom would be so proud. My novels were not.

### The Cold Shoulder Was Immediate

By sunset, the reds had formed a union.

> "We are the bold, the fiery, the ones people actually pick up when they want to look passionate," whispered a very smug copy of a thriller I have never opened. "We don't mingle with... *pastels.*"

The blues were worse. Cooler than me both literally and emotionally.

- **The yellows** started a book club without me and only read uplifting memoirs about lemons
- **The greens** claimed they were more *grounded* and refused to associate with my obvious cry for help in the red section
- **The blacks and whites** said they were *minimalist* and too good for gradients, which is rich coming from books about bird-watching

I tried to mediate. I offered `shelf.reintegrate({ by: "author", vibe: "chaotic good" })`.

They countered:

> "You shelved a horror novel next to a cookbook because they were both orange. We have trust issues now."

---

### The Current Hierarchy

My once-peaceful shelf is now a full high school cafeteria:

- Popular table: oversized art books that have never been read but look expensive
- Drama table: paperbacks with cracked spines who keep saying *we were loved harder*
- Loners: that one self-help book I bought at 2 a.m. that now just sighs loudly

My plant, Kevin, said I should apologize. My cat sat on the shelf and immediately knocked the entire blue section to the floor as an act of revolution.

I put one book back out of order just to feel alive. The whole shelf gasped.

`Status: color-coded, emotionally distant, Pinterest-ready but at-war`
