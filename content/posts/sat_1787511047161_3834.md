**I never thought I'd be emotionally accountable to a woven basket.**

But here we are. It's Sunday, I'm sitting on my bedroom floor in socks that don't match, and my laundry hamper is asking me how *that* made me feel.

## How It Started

Last Tuesday, I decided to be a grown-up. I looked at the leaning tower of sweaters and whispered the forbidden phrase:

*I'm going to deal with you tomorrow.*

The hamper, apparently, took that personally.

> "So I'm just 'storage' now?" it said, in the exact tone of my last situationship. "You dump, you leave, you come back when you need something cute?"

*Classic hamper guilt trip.*

### Exhibit A: The Evidence

I tried to defend myself, but the hamper had receipts — literally, because I leave those in my pockets:

- 14 days of "I'll do laundry on Sunday" (it's been three Sundays)
- one sock that has been single since March, waiting for its partner to return from the war
- a hoodie I wore to 4 job interviews, 2 breakups, and one attempt at jogging
- 3 dryer sheets I stole from my mom in 2022 and never used out of respect

I tried to set a boundary. I even typed it out like a real adult:

```
hamper.setBoundaries({ emotionalAvailability: false, lint: true })
```

Response:

```
ERROR 418: I am a teapot and also your only support system
```

---

Look, I know this is **satire**. I know hampers don't do therapy. But we now have a recurring calendar invite called **Feelings & Fabric Softener**.

Yesterday it asked if I was *projecting* because I called my laundry "overwhelming."

And I said yes. *God, yes.*

> "We're making progress," said the hamper.

I'm bringing snacks next week. *It requested the good detergent.*
