**I finally admitted my fiddle-leaf fig had legally died and replaced it with a plastic one from Target, which I assumed would be the low-maintenance roommate I never had.**

I was wrong. *So wrong.*

My real plant died the way all my plants die — slowly, forgivingly, while I whispered "you're doing great" and gave it LaCroix. The plastic one, which I've named Chad Fernley III, arrived perfect, glossy, and immediately disappointed in me.

## The Onboarding Meeting

Chad held a silent all-hands on my windowsill at 7 a.m. on Tuesday. His agenda was clear:

- Why I own three identical gray sweaters but no iron
- Why my "morning routine" is just snoozing until my coffee gets anxious
- Why I tell people I'm "really into journaling" when my journal is mostly grocery lists and the word `noodles?` underlined
- Why I watered him *anyway*, out of guilt, which — quote — "is both patronizing and hydrologically confusing"

He just stared. Which is worse.

> "Tyler, I was manufactured in a facility that also makes traffic cones. Even *I* have a more structured growth plan than you. Have you considered quarterly goals? Have you considered drinking water that isn't iced coffee?" — Chad, my plastic pothos

I tried to explain that I am thriving in my own way.

He responded by reflecting sunlight directly into my eyes at 6:43 a.m. every morning.

## My Quarterly Review Is Today

Apparently Chad has been talking to my other decor. My throw blanket says he's "intense but fair." My IKEA lamp is now asking for PTO.

The worst part is he's *right*. He never wilts, never drops a leaf, never asks for anything — and somehow still makes me feel like I need to do better. Yesterday I caught myself apologizing to him before I ordered takeout for the third night in a row.

*This is satire, I do not have a sentient plastic plant that does life coaching. Or do I? No, I don't.*

If you need me, I'll be over here reorganizing my entire life because a $14.99 fake fern gave me a performance review and put it in my calendar as a recurring event.
