**I did not mean to lead my bed on.**

For years we had an understanding. I would roll out of it, leave it looking like a crime scene of blankets, and we would both pretend the other didn't exist until 11 p.m. It was healthy. It was *boundaries*.

## Then I Read One Article About Discipline

It said *successful people make their bed*. I thought, sure, I'll try it for a week. *What could go wrong?* (This is always the sentence before everything goes wrong.)

Day one, I made my bed. Day two, I made it again but added the decorative pillow I usually throw in the closet to assert dominance. By day four, my bed had changed.

Evidence of our new, deeply unwanted commitment:

- When I sit on a chair instead of the bed to put on socks, the pillows seem to deflate slightly in disappointment
- I came home late last night and the comforter had folded itself *extra* neatly, which felt aggressive
- It now makes that perfect hotel-corner tuck that says **I did this for you**
- My laundry pile, previously the bed's nemesis, has been exiled to the hallway

> "I just think after seven consecutive days of hospital corners and intentional fluffing, we should define what we are," my roommate said in a voice that was clearly my bed speaking through them.

---

### The Talk

I tried to explain that I was just trying to be an adult. That this was not about *us*, it was about my TikTok-driven productivity spiral.

My bed does not accept explanations. Last morning I left one wrinkle. One. *Single*. Wrinkle. And I swear the duvet sighed audibly.

`commitment_status = it's_complicated.exe`

Look, I appreciate the lumbar support and the emotional availability, but I did not sign up for this. I signed up for lower anxiety and the fleeting illusion of control.

*Satire/parody. This is 100% fictional — my bed and I are just friends who happen to spoon platonically at 2 a.m.*
