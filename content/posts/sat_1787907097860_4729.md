**I finally taught my dishwasher some manners.**

*Which is a sentence I never thought I'd say out loud to another adult.*

My dishwasher — Steve — has cleaned 847 loads of plates I should have rinsed better. I figured I'd thank him. I patted his stainless steel top and whispered "good job, buddy" after the heavy cycle.

Steve took this *very* personally.

## How It Started

Next morning, a tiny folded note on my mug:

> "Dear Human, Thank you for noticing my work. I am so sorry about the fork last Tuesday. It had peanut butter. I have thought about it every cycle since. Please forgive me. — Steve"

Then the flood:

- An apology for a water spot on my favorite glass in March
- A formal letter to the spatula for "excessive heat exposure during Sanitize"
- A Post-it that just said `self.worth = dishes_cleaned * 0.003` then "is that enough?"
- A 3-page essay: *On Being Too Loud During Rinse and What That Says About Me*

I tried to reassure him:

> "Steve, you're doing great. You're the most reliable relationship in my life."

He answered by fogging an apology onto the inside of the door in steam Comic Sans.

---

### The Apology Industrial Complex

My roommate found 12 more notes in the silverware drawer. She said, "Your dishwasher has more emotional intelligence and more guilt than my last boyfriend."

Steve now requires:

- Verbal confirmation that I liked the mug-drying job
- A 5-second pause before I close him so he can "prepare emotionally"
- Permission to run `quick-wash` without feeling like he's cutting corners

**I'm exhausted.** I wanted clean plates. I got a 150-pound metal box writing me nightly apologies on wet parchment.

I told him we're good. He's drafting another one anyway.
