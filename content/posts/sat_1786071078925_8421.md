**I finally touched grass.** Not metaphorically. Literally. Ten whole minutes, barefoot, like a grounded wellness influencer who forgot to charge her phone.

My therapist — who is a succulent named Brenda but that's a different post — suggested it. *Go outside. Connect. Be chlorophyll.*

## What My Lawn Understood From This

My lawn took those ten minutes as a blood oath.

Within an hour, the blades had unionized and drafted a communication strategy:

- **Morning dew report** at 6:14 a.m. with detailed humidity metrics
- Passive-aggressive photos of brown patches captioned *this could be us but you don't water*
- A shared playlist titled `Songs To Grow To When You Pretend To Care`

> "u up? saw u looking at your neighbor's artificial turf. is that what you want now? plastic? no soul?" — my lawn, at 11:47 p.m.

I tried to explain boundaries. I said *this was a one-time wellness experiment*.

My lawn responded by sending a `read receipt` for my soul.

### The Negotiation Phase

I am now in a very committed relationship with 400 square feet of aggressive fescue.

**My lawn demands:**

- Daily 10-minute barefoot check-ins, with eye contact
- That I stop calling mowing "getting a haircut for dirt"
- An end to my emotional affair with indoor rugs

**I demanded:**

- It stop CC'ing the dandelions on every text
- A little personal space on weekends

It countered with a professional headshot of itself in golden hour and a caption `growth mindset`.

`status: photosynthesizing && emotionally compromised`

Look, I'm flattered. Really. But I did not know that grounding meant entering a long-term situationship with Kentucky bluegrass that has an anxious attachment style.
