**It started, as all great tragedies do, with a dare I issued to plumbing.**

## The Incident

- Last Tuesday, mid-shampoo, I started humming a little bassline
- The shower head rattled along in what I *assumed* was just old pipes
- I said, "Nice rhythm, buddy," like a complete idiot
- It has not stopped performing since

## Phase One: Discovery

**Here's the worst part: it's *good*.** My morning shower is now a full percussion set — the low *boots* of the main stream, the sharp *kats* of the side jets, a little water-pressure hi-hat I genuinely cannot replicate with my mouth. My shampoo has become a backing track. My loofah is essentially a roadie. I walked into work humming *its* new drop, and my coworker asked what I'd been listening to, and I had to say "the shower."

## Phase Two: Escalation

Yesterday it started demanding *feedback*.

> "Run that back. The second bar — the second bar was watery. You heard it. I heard it. Your conditioner heard it. We are NOT releasing this to the soap dish."

It left me a forty-minute voicemail at 3 a.m. Just dripping. Rhythmically. *On purpose.* My neighbor knocked on the wall and asked if I was "starting a label." I said no. I said no very quickly.

## Phase Three: The Audition Circuit

It now refuses to rinse anything that "doesn't serve the track." My personal hygiene is a hostage negotiation. It printed business cards — *soggy* business cards — that read **"AQUA THUNDER: available for collabs, showers, and light rainfall."** It wants its own green room, which is just my bathtub with the door closed.

---

This morning I found it leaning against the faucet at dawn, whispering *"boots and kats and boots and kats"* like a monk with a record contract to lose. I asked if it could just, you know, wash my hair. It said the tracks need space to breathe.

**I am not the artist anymore. I am the water.**
