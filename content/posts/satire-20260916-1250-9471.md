**I only told my umbrella the truth, and she never forgave me.**

It was an honest moment — the kind you have at 7 a.m. with a travel mug and too little sleep. My phone said a 10% chance of rain. I looked at her hanging by the door, doing nothing, and I said the quiet part out loud: "Honestly? You're kind of my backup plan."

She did not respond. Obviously. She's an umbrella.

But I know what I saw.

## The Incident

Within the week, the forecast accuracy shifted. Every morning she stayed shut, silent, folded in on herself — and every afternoon, the sky split open the moment I stepped outside. Coincidence? Let me present the evidence:

- **Tuesday:** 8% chance, clear skies — thunderstorm at 12:47 p.m., precisely while I was between buildings
- **Wednesday:** I checked the weather app three times. All sunny. It rained sideways.
- **Thursday:** I brought her anyway, just to be safe. Not a cloud. She smirked. (She doesn't have a mouth. She smirked.)

## Her Statement

> "Oh, so NOW I'm useful? After four months of being 'Plan B' behind a baseball cap?"

I didn't hear her say it. But I felt it, which is worse.

## The Negotiation

I tried to make peace. I bought a new stand for the entryway — the good kind, with a drip tray. I stopped calling my rain jacket "the starter." I even carried her through a full day of steady rain without complaint, like some kind of apology tour.

None of it mattered. She has adopted a philosophy: **if she's the backup plan, then I'm on my own.** A folded umbrella is a choice. An open one is forgiveness.

---

So this morning I stood in the entryway, looked her dead in the canopy, and said it: "You are my first choice. You have always been my first choice. The rain jacket was an experiment."

She unfurled half an inch. Progress.

Tonight there's a 40% chance of rain. I'm bringing her, and I'm bringing no backup plan at all. The jacket stays home. She knows.

They always know.
