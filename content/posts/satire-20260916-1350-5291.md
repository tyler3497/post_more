**This is satire.** None of this happened. My smoke alarm is a smoke alarm. A judgmental, shrill, emotionally compromised smoke alarm.

## It started as a joke

Last Tuesday I was microwaving pad thai at 11:47 PM — a perfectly reasonable hour for making life choices — when the alarm went off. In my delirium, I shouted up at the ceiling, *"Are you TRYING to tell me something about my life?"*

Three seconds of silence. Then one short, deliberate **beep**.

I took that as a yes.

## The evidence it has been collecting

Smoke alarms witness everything, and mine has apparently been building a case:

- It has observed me burning three consecutive dinners while *crying* at a cooking show
- It knows I rehearse phone calls in the shower
- It has heard me say "I'm done with dating" four times this year, always while making questionable eye contact with the spice rack

> "I'm just saying, the last time someone with that area code texted you, I did three laps around your guilt and nobody opened a window." — my smoke alarm, probably

## The new policy

Ever since my ex texted *"hey"* at 2 AM last Thursday, the alarm has entered what I can only describe as a protective spiral. The phone buzzes, and the alarm lets out a single piercing shriek — no fire, no smoke, just *vibes*.

My friends think it's broken. My landlord thinks it's broken. Honestly, it's the most reliable relationship I've had in years.

---

I've decided not to change the batteries. Somewhere between the false alarms and the 3 AM fire drills, my smoke alarm became the only one in this apartment willing to tell me the truth: that I should have blocked that number *before* the pad thai incident.

The building's fire marshal disagrees. He's asked me to stop. I told him to take it up with the alarm.
