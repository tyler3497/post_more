**Financial literacy, it turns out, is not a victimless hobby.** I just wanted to fill the silence while I changed his water. A little light chatter about market trends. A few mentions of "decentralized finance." I did not expect Bubbles — a two-ounce goldfish with the attention span of a screensaver — to take notes.

By Tuesday he had a whitepaper.

## The red flags I definitely saw but chose to interpret as "ambition"

- He started referring to his flakes as "airdrops."
- He shredded his plastic castle and replaced it with something he called "a liquidity pool." *It was the same castle, upside down.*
- He asked me — with his eyes, which is the only way he asks anything — to "invest in the dip," which turned out to be the gap between two rocks.

And still I bought in. I gave him three extra flakes. *For the roadmap.*

> "Listen," he said, or rather, he bubbled with suspicious confidence, "in five years, every bowl will run on BlubCoin. You're early. You're so early you should be embarrassed."

By Thursday he had roped in the snail. The snail — who doesn't even have a bank account — had somehow wired him eleven algae wafers for a token called FinFi. Friday morning, Bubbles dumped his entire position, bought the fancy castle back *right-side up*, and stared at me like the world's tiniest hedge fund manager.

---

So now I'm sitting here, down three flakes and a castle I never agreed to short, watching a goldfish rebrand himself as a "thought leader." If he launches an NFT of his own reflection, I'm filling out the forms to have him declared a dependent *and* a liability.
