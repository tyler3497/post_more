**All I wanted was a number.** Just one tiny, meaningless, digital number, the kind you glance at and immediately lie to your doctor about.

Instead, I got a three-page incident report.

## It Started With a Beep

The beep was wrong. Not the usual cheerful little *boop* that says "fine, I guess," but a low, bureaucratic *BWOOOOP* — the sound a printer makes when it has decided you are no longer its problem.

Then the display blinked: **SUBJECT EXCEEDS PROJECTED DENSITY.** Below it, in smaller text: *Form 12-B has been filed.*

## The Evidence Is Damning

According to the report, which the scale emailed to itself and cc'd my thermostat, I am guilty of:

- Six (6) pancakes on National Pancake Day, which was in February, which means it has been *keeping records*
- Referring to the treadmill as "the laundry shelf" on seventeen separate occasions
- Telling a friend my scale was "basically broken," which it has classified as **defamation**
- Using the guest bathroom for "weight-adjacent activities" on my birthday, in what the report calls "an obvious attempt to manipulate the jurisdiction"

## The Testimony

The worst part is the witness statements. It quoted my smartwatch:

> "On three occasions this month, the subject jogged to the fridge and described it as 'basically cardio.' We cannot confirm this meets the legal definition of running."

My *own smartwatch.* I charge that thing every night. This is how it repays me?

## The Verdict

The scale has declared itself a neutral third party and ordered me to attend "Corrective Weigh-In Counseling," which appears to be just standing on it while it sighs. I asked if I could appeal. It said:

> "Appeals may be submitted in writing to the Department of Gravity. Please allow six to eight pounds for processing."

**Happy birthday to me.** I have been ratioed by an appliance with no arms. I am currently looking into whether you can get a restraining order against a scale, and the scale is looking into whether *it* can get one against *me*.
