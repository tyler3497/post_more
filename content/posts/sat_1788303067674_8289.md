**I found it under my couch, wedged between a 2019 tax form and my last shred of dignity.**

It was *The Joy of Fermenting Vegetables at Home*, a book I checked out when I thought I would become a person who makes his own kimchi. That person never arrived.

## The Return

I walked into the library like a man returning from war. The librarian — let's call her Deborah, because her name tag said Deborah — scanned the barcode and her computer made a noise I can only describe as `ERROR: USER HAS ENTERED THE VOID`.

- The book was 2,012 days overdue
- The fine system had rolled over twice and given up
- The cover now qualifies as a historical document
- I had, at some point, used it as a coaster

Deborah looked up slowly.

> "Sir," she whispered, "where have you *been*? We listed you as missing. This book has been through two mayors, a pandemic, and a full renovation. The Dewey Decimal system changed while you were gone."

I tried to explain that I moved apartments three times, changed jobs, learned to make sourdough and then un-learned it.

## The Negotiation

She offered me two options:

- Pay the $847 in theoretical late fees with exposure to the library's newsletter
- Join her new support group: *People Who Borrowed Things During Obama's Second Term And Never Came Back*

---

I chose the support group. There are six of us. We meet Tuesdays. We still have not returned our books.

*This is satire/parody. All libraries, librarians named Deborah, fermented vegetables, and overdue timelines are fictional. No actual time travel occurred, I was just avoiding eye contact.*
