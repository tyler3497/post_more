**I did not go to Kroger at 8:47 p.m. on a Tuesday to have an existential crisis, but the self-checkout had other plans.**

*For context, I cook like a raccoon with access to YouTube — enthusiastic, messy, and 60% garlic.*

It started when I scanned my sad little pile: wilted cilantro, two limes, a bag of baby carrots I buy for optimism, and a cucumber I have no plan for.

The machine, in its cheerful robot voice, said:

> "Great job, Chef! Place your cucumbers in the bagging area."

Chef. CHEF. I have burned toast this week.

## The Incident That Ruined My Cucumber Confidence

I froze. The lady behind me nodded like, *yes, chef*. My carrots were judging me.

Signs I am not qualified for this title:

- My signature dish is called "pasta but I added cheese at the wrong time"
- I once set a timer for 10 minutes and then forgot what it was for until the smoke alarm told me
- I think "julienne" is a person from my high school
- I own one good knife and I'm scared of it

I tried to explain to the machine. "I'm not a chef," I whispered.

> "Chef, please scan your rewards card."

It was committed to the bit. HR should study its management style.

---

I went home and tried to live up to it. I put on an apron. I said `mise en place` out loud and immediately felt like a fraud.

```js
if (selfCheckout.belief === "chef" && actualSkill === "can boil water sometimes") {
  return "impostor syndrome with a side of cucumbers";
}
```

Now I can't go back. The produce aisle knows. The cucumbers know. That self-checkout gave me a promotion I didn't earn, and I am too polite to quit.
