**I bought a $39 anti-fatigue standing desk mat because my spine filed a formal complaint with HR.**

*It was meant to be ergonomic. It was not meant to become emotionally invested in my future.*

## The Interview Process

On day one, I unrolled it and it sighed with purpose. I thought that was air escaping. It was not.

By day two, it had opinions.

> "You're standing like a man who has given up on his dreams but not on his browser tabs," it said. I don't know how a mat talks. It doesn't have a mouth. It has *presence*.

*This is when I should have returned it.*

Instead, I stayed. Because my posture *did* improve 10%. And because it started leaving me affirmations.

## Its Coaching Methodology Is... Aggressive

Every morning at 8:03 AM sharp:

- "Shoulders back! You are not a shrimp!"
- "Hydrate! Your houseplant is judging you!"
- "You have been standing for 47 minutes! I am so proud I could cry foam!"
- "Close LinkedIn. You are spiraling."

It programmed my smart lights to flash whenever I slouch. It now refers to my office chair as "your ex."

---

### The Breakthrough

Yesterday it told me to `stand.taller(existential_dread=False)` and I actually did. I stood up straight, deleted three drafts, and finally sent the email I'd been avoiding for six weeks.

Then it whispered, "See? You *can* stand on your own two feet. Literally. You're doing it right now."

**I have never been so supported and so roasted by an object that is technically classified as 'floor accessory.'**

We have a session again tomorrow at 7 AM. It says we're working on boundaries. I think it means mine.
