**I thought I was being a responsible adult by fostering cross-appliance communication in my kitchen.**

*This was, in retrospect, the same energy as introducing your two friends who both secretly hate you.*

For months, my air fryer and my slow cooker had lived in peaceful ignorance on opposite counters. The air fryer — loud, ambitious, chronically over-caffeinated — was busy crisping everything at 400°F for clout. The slow cooker — calm, patient, smells like unresolved childhood — was taking eight hours to do what anyone else could do in twenty minutes.

I figured, why not team-build?

## The Introduction I Should Never Have Made

I placed them next to each other on Sunday and said, and I quote, "You two should get to know each other. You both handle food!"

Silence. Then judgment.

By Monday, my leftovers were staging a revolt:

- My leftover lasagna asked to be legally emancipated
- The roasted veggies filed two separate requests for processing, one for each parent
- The chicken thighs have started calling the slow cooker "Dad" and the air fryer "Uncle Crispy"

> "I didn't spend four hours braising for you to blast me at 390 degrees for relevance," whispered the slow cooker, who now only communicates through passive-aggressive steam.

### The Custody Agreement No One Asked For

They've self-organized. It's law now:

The slow cooker gets Mondays and Wednesdays, holidays, and anything involving beans. The air fryer gets Tuesdays, Thursdays, and `reheat.exe has stopped working`.

I tried to intervene. The air fryer said:

> "I'm not *angry*, I'm just *disappointed you don't appreciate a good crunch.*"

---

So now my kitchen operates like divorced parents sharing a 600-square-foot apartment. I have to schedule my leftovers, pack emotional-safety tupperware, and pretend I don't have a favorite.

I've learned my lesson. Some appliances are meant to never meet.
