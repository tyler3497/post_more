**It started as a joke, the way all of my worst decisions do.**

My roommate bought a tarot deck "for the vibes" and abandoned it on top of the fridge, next to the expired hot sauce and a bottle of something we call The Ambition. Last Tuesday, staring down a container of leftover lasagna from *two Fridays ago*, I did what any rational adult would do: I gave it a three-card pull.

The cards were not kind. The Tower, reversed. The Five of Swords. And sitting there between us like an uninvited guest, **Death**.

## The Reading

I laid the spread across the kitchen counter and, to be thorough, read it aloud. Here is what I learned:

- **The Tower (reversed):** the lasagna was in denial about its own structural integrity. The layers had collapsed into a single, unified *concept* of lasagna.
- **The Five of Swords:** there had been a conflict. Specifically, between the garlic bread and the lasagna over who got to be *the entrée*. The garlic bread lost.
- **Death:** the expiration date was three weeks dead. The card just showed up late, like a friend who misses the crisis but brings wine.

It was at that point that things took a turn. Because the tupperware lid — I swear this on everything — *shifted*. Just slightly. Like it had been holding its breath.

> "That's not a reading, that's an autopsy," my roommate said, backing slowly out of the kitchen. "And that pasta is older than our lease."

## The Divorce

Within days, the fallout was undeniable. The lids began separating from their bases — not by force, just a quiet drifting apart, the way couples do when they start eating in different rooms. One container of chili now insists on being called *Stéphane*. The leftover lo mein asked, through what I can only describe as telepathic steam, whether it ever had a *purpose*, or just a *shelf life*.

This morning I found a soggy napkin pressed against the inside of the fridge door. In Sharpie, in shaky handwriting that was definitely not mine (my handwriting is *worse*):

**I CANNOT LIVE LIKE THIS.**

Look, the tarot didn't kill my leftovers. They were already ghosts in plastic tombs. All I did was finally *listen*.
