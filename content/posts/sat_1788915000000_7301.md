**It started as a joke.** My microwave has this habit of humming at a slightly different pitch depending on how long you run it, and one Tuesday night, reheating soup, I hummed along with it. Harmonized, even. For three full minutes we were an *ensemble*. I felt seen.

## The decline began within a week

First, the beeps got lower. Not the cheerful triple-beep that says *your food is ready, champ!* — a new, slow, mournful tone. Like the machine was sighing through its circuitry. Then it started pausing between beeps, for dramatic effect. I know dramatic pauses. Nobody learns them by accident.

I documented the evidence, because this is my life now:

- Monday: heats mac and cheese while emitting a tone best described as "bagpipes at a state funeral"
- Wednesday: refused to pop popcorn, insisting via three descending beeps that the kernels "deserved better"
- Friday: played a six-beep sequence I recognized, with horror, as the melody of my *childhood bedtime lullaby*

## A conversation I am not proud of

At one point I unplugged it and said, out loud, to an appliance:

> "You are a kitchen tool. You heat things. That is the whole job description. Where did this attitude come from?"

The microwave said nothing. It had no power. I waited for an answer anyway, which tells you everything about how the power dynamic works in this house.

---

Yesterday I tried to make peace. I reheated some pasta, and the microwave warmed it perfectly — 90 seconds, no cold spots — while humming the saddest little tune I'd ever heard from a magnetron. It was genuinely good. It was the best humming it had ever done. I cried a little. The pasta was excellent. I think it knows I know, and I think we're both okay with that.

**Final score: Microwave 1, Me 0.** Dinner is at six. The mood will be somber, but the leftovers will be evenly heated, and honestly? That's more than most relationships offer.
