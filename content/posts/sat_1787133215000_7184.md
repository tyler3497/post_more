**I bought a standing desk to fix my back and accidentally created a grieving ex who writes sonnets about lumbar support.**

After three years of working hunched like a shrimp doing taxes, I finally spent $449 on a motorized standing desk. My chiropractor wept. My posture improved. *My apartment suddenly had an emotional villain arc.*

## The Chair Noticed Immediately

My old office chair — a $99 mesh creature named Chaz who has supported me through two jobs, one pandemic, and all of Season 4 of that baking show — did not take the transition well.

I stood. He rolled slightly toward me, hopeful.

I stood again the next day.

By Day 3, there was a sticky note on his armrest.

- Day 1: Denial. "You'll be back. Your feet will hurt."
- Day 2: Bargaining. Spun in a slow, mournful circle near my feet
- Day 3: Full Tortured Poet Era

> "You said I was the *backbone* of this operation," Chaz whispered, dramatically. "You said my adjustable height was 'just right.' Was I not ergonomic enough for you?"
> "Is it... is she... *firmer*?"

I tried to explain that it's not about firmness, it's about `vertebrae.compression < 0.5`, but he scoffed. Literally scoffed. The hydraulics hissed.

### The Poetry Got Worse

I found his latest work tucked under the seat cushion. Title: **"Ode to the Indent I Left In You (And You In Me)"**

He now has a Substack. Seven subscribers. All are other abandoned chairs from Facebook Marketplace.

---

I tried to compromise and sat in him while at standing height, which looked like I was doing a very serious squat and immediately pulled something.

The standing desk, meanwhile, is smug. It hums when it rises. It has memory presets. Preset 2 is labeled "TALL AND JUDGMENTAL."

I miss sitting, but I fear if I go back, Chaz will demand couples therapy and a footnote in my memoir.

*Satire / Parody — entirely fictional. No chairs were emotionally damaged in the making of this standing desk, though one did start journaling.*
