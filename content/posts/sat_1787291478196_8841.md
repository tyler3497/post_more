**Breaking news from my laundry room:** my hamper has better boundaries than I do, and frankly, it is humiliating.

I was trying to be *mindful*. My therapist said I should "practice self-care where I live," so I started with the place that literally holds all my baggage — the laundry hamper.

I looked it dead in its wicker eye and said, "You don't have to carry everything people throw at you."

It listened. Too well.

## The Intervention Nobody Asked For

Within 24 hours, the hamper implemented:

- A strict **no emotional baggage** policy — it only holds socks, not existential dread about socks
- *Mandatory rest periods* between loads (it calls it "me time," I call it mildew)
- A meditation corner where it sits empty and judges me
- A tiny sign that says `YOU CANNOT POUR FROM AN EMPTY HAMPER`

I tried to put my favorite anxiety hoodie in there — the one I wear for three days while spiraling about emails — and it recoiled.

> "I am a vessel for laundry, not your coping mechanism," the hamper said. "Have you considered journaling instead of that sweaty regret-shirt again?"

The hamper now identifies as a *boundaried container*. It has a morning routine.

---

## Why I Am on Probation in My Own Laundry Room

Last night I tried to do laundry at 11 pm, which is when all bad decisions are made.

> "We don't do revenge laundering here," it whispered, rattling its wicker. "You need sleep, not aggressively sorting darks to avoid that group chat you left on read."

It was right. I went to bed. Socks still on the floor, inner child seen.

I tried to bribe it with fabric softener. It said:

> "Self-soothing is not a scent booster. Setting `emotional_baggage_limit = 0` is self-preservation."

*I have never been so gently roasted by laundry furniture.*

So now I own a hamper that does breathwork, a pile of clothes on The Chair™ — you know the one — and proof that if a $19.99 basket from Target can learn to say no, maybe I can too.

Fictional, satirical, and exactly how my Tuesday is going.
