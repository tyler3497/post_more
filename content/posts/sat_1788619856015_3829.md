**It started as a chore. It ended as an institution.**

Last Saturday, armed with nothing but a trash bag and misplaced confidence, I opened my kitchen junk drawer. You know the one. Every home has one: the drawer where dead batteries go to retire, where chopsticks reproduce, where a single AAA battery lives forever *alone*, in defiance of all logic.

## Phase One: The Discovery

What I found in there was not clutter. It was a **civilization**. Highlights from the excavation:

- Eleven twist ties, none of which came from anything I currently own
- A key that opens nothing I have ever owned, yet I cannot throw it away (*what if it opens something I will own?*)
- Four pens that don't work, which I kept anyway, because hope
- A tube of superglue that is, itself, glued shut — the drawer equivalent of a snake eating its own tail

## Phase Two: The Accusation

> "I don't collect junk," I told my roommate, gesturing proudly at my labeled containers. "I curate it."

He stared at me. "You just spent four hours putting rubber bands in chronological order."

---

## Phase Three: The Gift Shop

Here is where things got away from me. Once the drawer was organized, it looked... beautiful. Pristine. Almost *too* pristine to use. So I did what any reasonable person would do: I installed a tiny velvet rope, printed a little brass plaque that reads **"THE JUNK DRAWER MUSEUM — EST. 2026,"** and now my roommate's girlfriend pays me in snacks to take the guided tour.

I have a guestbook now. People sign it. There are *reviews*.

I no longer have a junk drawer. I have a cultural landmark with a **gift shop** (it's the drawer above it — it sells nothing, but the concept is sound).

**SATIRE — this is a work of parody. Any resemblance to an actual museum is accidental and flattering.**
