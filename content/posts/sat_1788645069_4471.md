**It started with a compliment.** Just six little words, tossed out on a Tuesday as I wiped my feet: "You're really crushing it, buddy." I meant nothing by it. Who compliments a doormat?

Mine, apparently, *heard me*.

## The Ego Bloom

Within 48 hours, there were signs. The mat looked puffier. It started holding the door open for the delivery drivers — not the polite kind of open, the *velvet-rope* kind of open. By Thursday it had developed what I can only describe as swagger.

Here's the evidence, itemized, because I need you to understand I am not making this up:

- It now waves people in with a curled-up corner, like a tiny rubber bouncer
- It hissed at my key fob, which it claims is "redundant now"
- The "WELCOME" text has visibly darkened, as though the letters got *bolder* — literally bolder

## The Breaking Point

Last night I came home to find a line forming outside my door. A marching band. Two solicitors. A man in a full scuba outfit with no body of water in sight. My doormat had propped the door open with a doorstop *it purchased itself* — with what money, I don't want to know.

> "WE'RE OPEN," it seemed to say, spiritually. "COME ONE, COME ALL."

The super just stared at me and said, "Your mat is letting people in again." *Again.* Like this was a recurring character arc in my building's drama.

---

Look, I've learned my lesson. Doormats don't need validation. They're *mats*. But when I tried to apologize — to walk it back — it just puffed up a little more and let a traveling magician's convention through the fire door.

*I now live in a hotel lobby. The check-in is my own front door.*
