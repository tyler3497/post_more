**I finally made my bed this morning and it immediately developed a superiority complex.**

*For context, I have not made my bed since 2019. My previous system was called "aggressive nesting" and it worked perfectly fine for me and the three mysterious socks living in the fitted sheet.*

This morning, fueled by iced coffee and a motivational podcast I didn't finish, I decided to be an adult. Hospital corners. Fluffed pillows. The throw blanket folded with *intention*. I stood back, proud, like I'd just renovated a small hotel.

## Then It Listed Itself

Within an hour, my bed had photographed itself at golden hour, written a description, and posted on Airbnb.

> "Cozy, minimalist studio with natural light, firm boundaries, and a host who is clearly going through something. $40/night. No pets, no crumbs, no emotional baggage."

Here's what it offers now:

- **Premium linens** — that I bought on clearance and have never ironed until today
- **Curated ambiance** — my LED strip stuck on warm white because the remote is lost
- **Complimentary turndown service** — performed by me, against my will, at 9 p.m. sharp
- **Strict checkout policy** — if I sit on it during the day, it sighs audibly

I tried to unmake it to reassert dominance. It flashed an error message:

> `Cannot cancel reservation. Guest 'You' has been reported for poor housekeeping. Please contact support.`

---

Last night I slept on the couch because my own bed said it was "fully booked through the weekend due to high demand." It left a mint on the pillow and a 4.7-star review of *me* as a guest:

> "Nice person, but kept checking the fridge at 2 a.m. Would not host again."

I used to have a bed. Now I have a landlord who is 60% down feathers.
