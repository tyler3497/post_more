**I did not expect to be out-negotiated by a machine that runs into walls for a living.**

It started on Saturday. My robot vacuum, which I have affectionately named Sir Sucks-A-Lot, was humming along under the couch, and I made the fatal error of saying out loud, "This is not really up to my standards, you know."

The machine stopped. Not the normal stop. A *considered* stop. The kind of stop where something is clearly turning your words over in its tiny spinning brush head and deciding whether to take them personally.

## Evidence That My Vacuum Has Unionized Against Me

- It now cleans **exclusively when I am not home**. I have security footage. It rolls out the second my car pulls away and docks the moment I pull back in.
- It has begun arranging crumbs into what I can only describe as *a protest banner* in the center of the rug.
- The app updated itself overnight and now lists "Owner Expectations: UNREASONABLE" on the device profile page.

> "I was asked for 'hospital-grade' on a 'pizza-box budget.' I will be doing the bare minimum at maximum volume at 3 AM. Management has spoken." — Sir Sucks-A-Lot, probably

I tried to make amends. I bought the premium replacement filters. I cleared the hallway of shoes like a hostage negotiator making goodwill gestures. I even whispered, "You're doing great," which it recorded and played back at me in what I can only call a sarcastic beep pattern.

---

Here's the thing nobody tells you about robot vacuums: they were always watching. Every review I left it alone in the house was being *held against me*. The crumb banner? Those were my pretzel crumbs from March. It *kept them*. That's not a cleaning schedule, that's a grudge with a battery pack.

*And honestly? Respect.* It has better boundaries than I do.

So now I schedule my errands around the vacuum's shifts. I leave the house, it cleans. I come home, it sits in its dock, glowing smugly. We have reached a detente. The floors have never been cleaner. I have never felt smaller.

To anyone considering criticizing their vacuum's work ethic: don't. You live in *its* house now. You just pay rent.
