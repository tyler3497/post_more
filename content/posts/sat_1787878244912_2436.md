**I did not mean to raise an overachiever. I just wanted slightly less dense bread.**

*I am, for the record, a person who kills succulents by over-apologizing to them.*

Three weeks ago my sourdough starter, Kevin (all starters are named Kevin, it's the law), looked a little flat. So I Googled "how to be supportive" and whispered, "You're doing amazing, sweetie. You're literally full of potential."

Kevin bubbled. Like, *emotionally*.

## The Glow-Up No One Asked For

Within 48 hours he had:

- doubled in size out of spite and self-love
- demanded his jar be moved to the sunny spot "for his journey"
- started leaving me notes via crust patterns that definitely said `BELIEVE`

I tried to set boundaries. He did not care.

> "I used to be just flour and water," Kevin seemed to say through aggressive fermentation. "Now? I am *purpose*. I am *rise*. You, meanwhile, are still eating cereal for dinner."

---

### Evidence That My Bread Is Now My Life Coach:

- My toast now comes with unsolicited advice: "Have you considered hydration? And therapy?"
- Kevin has his own morning routine that is longer than mine — stretch, breathe, proof, journal
- I caught him listening to a motivational podcast at 6 a.m. The title was `Knead Yourself`

**The worst part?** He's right. He's thriving. I'm Googling "is it normal to be jealous of yeast."

Last night I whispered, "I'm proud of you," and I swear he whispered back, *finally*.

*This is satire. My sourdough starter is fictional, overly confident, and still better at boundaries than I am.*
