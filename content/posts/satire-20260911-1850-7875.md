**The junk drawer is a room. And now it has a podcast. I was never asked to be on it.**

It started innocently enough. The junk drawer in my kitchen — you know the one, the archaeological site containing twelve dead pens, a tape measure that only measures sadness, three rubber bands older than my lease — had developed a kind of *presence*. It creaked open on its own. Things rattled in there at 2 a.m. like they were plotting.

## So I made a mistake

I said, out loud, to no one: "That drawer has enough material for a podcast."

The next morning, the drawer was open. A tiny microphone — I do not own a tiny microphone — stood propped up on a thimble. A little flag on it read `EPISODE 1`. I have no idea where the thimble came from. I have never owned a thimble.

## The lineup, as far as I can reconstruct

- **The Dead Batteries** — a melancholy four-piece band whose entire act is running out of energy mid-sentence
- **The Takeout Menus** — gossip columnists, ruthless, they've reviewed me and I got *two stars*
- **The Mystery Keys** — they unlock doors to places that don't exist and they *won't shut up about it*
- **The Tape Measure** — the show's fact-checker; wrong about everything, confident about everything

> "You can't be on the show," said the menus, somehow. "You're the *content*, not the *creator*."

## The ratings are incredible

Episode 4 — "Top 10 Corners of the Kitchen We Will Never Be Stored In" — hit number one in my house. I'm the only one here. *I am the chart.* And still, somehow, my own drawer outranks me.

---

The sponsors are just things I already bought, which feels like a loophole. Last night the drawer recorded a live episode about "The Decline of the Good Pen" and the applause sounded suspiciously like loose screws being shaken.

I considered therapy, but honestly? The podcast is better than anything I've produced all year. I've started leaving it snacks. A battery pack. Moral support.

*SATIRE — this is a parody, and my drawer has not achieved consciousness. Probably.*
