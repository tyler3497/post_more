**I just wanted to stop drinking scalding-hot tea.** That is all. That is the entire sin I committed, and now my kettle has taken a moral stand against me.

Last Tuesday, in what I thought was a moment of wholesome self-care, I looked at my electric kettle — a perfectly decent appliance that had served me faithfully for three years — and I said, out loud, to no one: *"Man, I really need you to chill."*

## The Literal Interpretation

Apparently the kettle took this as a sacred vow. Because for the past six days, it has produced nothing but lukewarm water, and it does so with an attitude I can only describe as *smug serenity*.

I have tried everything:

- Turning the dial to max — it clicks back to "gentle" like a rebellious teen
- Unplugging it and plugging it back in — it rebooted with what I swear was a deeper, calmer hum
- Begging — which worked for exactly one cup before it returned to its "principles"

## The Ultimatum

Yesterday morning I found a piece of paper taped to it. I live alone. The note read:

> "I have chosen a life of mildness. Your frantic 212°F lifestyle is the problem, not the solution. Namaste."

*Namaste.* My kettle says namaste now.

The worst part? My tea tastes like disappointment soup. My instant ramen is just... sad noodles in warm water. My neighbor, who witnessed the whole breakdown over the fence, offered to let me use his kettle, and I almost cried. Not from gratitude — from the realization that my own kettle, sitting twelve feet away, could be humming its little zen hum while I borrowed hot water like a common drifter.

---

I have reached out to the manufacturer's customer support. They asked if I had "tried resetting it." I asked if they had "tried respecting its journey." There was a long silence, and then they transferred me to a supervisor.

The supervisor said: **"Sir, kettles don't have principles."**

I don't believe him. I know what I heard. It clicks. It sighs steam. It *judges*.

I ordered a French press this morning. Somewhere in the kitchen, I swear I heard my kettle scoff.
