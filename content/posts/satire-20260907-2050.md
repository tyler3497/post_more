**It started as a joke. Everything I regret started as a joke.**

Three months into working from home, I started telling people I was "headed to the office," which meant walking eight feet to my couch. Funny, right? My wife laughed. My cat didn't. The cat, it turns out, was the only one paying attention.

## The Audit Letter

Last Tuesday I received an official-looking email from my company's HR department with the subject line *"Workplace Compliance Inspection — 421 Residential Blvd."* Which is my address. Which I never gave them. The body read, in part:

> "Per your repeated references to 'the office,' an on-site audit has been scheduled. Please ensure the following are available for inspection: a fire extinguisher, an ergonomically assessed workstation, and a floor plan of 'the office' with clearly marked emergency exits."

I checked the sender twice. It was real. It was HR. *Actual, salaried HR.*

## Exhibit A: The Evidence Against Me

- 47 Slack messages saying "brb, headed to the office" (they counted)
- One Zoom background labeled "Office Tour" that was just my living room at a different angle
- A calendar block titled **"Deep Work (Office)"** that they could see, because Outlook doesn't understand satire
- My wife once said "my husband's at the office" to our landlord, who has now filed a zoning complaint

## The Negotiation

The auditor arrived Wednesday with a clipboard and what I can only describe as *disappointed energy.* He measured my couch. He asked about my "co-workers," which I had to admit were throw pillows named Brenda and Steve. He wrote that down. He wrote everything down.

**We have reached an agreement.** I am allowed to keep calling it "the office," but only if I post OSHA-compliant signage on the recliner, and my commute — the eight feet — now officially counts toward my daily steps in the wellness program.

HR wins. Brenda and Steve keep their jobs. I have never felt more employed in my own home.
