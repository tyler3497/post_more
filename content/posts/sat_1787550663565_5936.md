**I did it. I finally organized the Tupperware cabinet.**

*You know, the one that previously operated on pure chaos theory and passive-aggressive lid avalanches.*

After 45 minutes, three minor concussions from falling containers, and a spiritual journey, I had achieved order. Every lid had a container. Every container had a lid. I stood back, proud, ready to be featured on a very boring home improvement show.

Then I heard a tiny gavel.

## The Homeowners Association Has Formed

Apparently, perfect organization violates some kind of natural law, because overnight my Tupperware cabinet formed an HOA.

This morning I found a tiny notice taped to the inside of the cabinet door:

> "ATTENTION RESIDENTS OF SHELF 2B: The 6-cup rectangular unit has been cited for leaving its socks— I mean, spaghetti stains — on the communal lawn. Hearing scheduled for Tuesday. Cookies will NOT be served, as we cannot agree on which container holds them."
> — President Tupper, Unit 3A

## The New Rules Are... Extensive

The bylaws, which were slid under my coffee maker at 3am, include:

- All lids must be stored at a 37-degree angle for *optimal feng shui*
- No container may hold leftovers for more than 72 hours without filing Form 27-B/6
- The square containers are in a feud with the round ones and have requested mediation
- Monthly dues are $5 or one leftover meatball, payable in `tupperware --pay-dues --with=guilt`

I tried to disband them. I said `sudo rm -rf /cabinet/hoa` in my most authoritative voice.

---

They responded by doubling my dues and electing the stained lasagna container as Treasurer. He is, to be fair, *very* experienced at holding onto things long past his term.

At this point, I'm considering moving. The junk drawer seems nice this time of year.
