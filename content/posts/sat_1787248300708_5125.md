**I never thought I'd be the kind of person who ends up in therapy with a kitchen appliance, and yet here we are.**

It started the way all great romances collapse — I ignored the beeping. Not once, not twice, but *47 times in a single week*. My microwave would finish, do its polite little `beep beep beep`, and I would look it dead in the stainless steel face and keep scrolling.

*I was busy. I had emails.*

## How We Got Here

Last Thursday, I finally snapped and whispered "sorry" to it at 11:42 p.m. while retrieving leftover pasta. I meant it as a throwaway. It meant it as a vow renewal.

Since that apology, our dynamic has changed completely. It now refuses to run unless I make meaningful eye contact. It only heats my soup on *low* to "teach patience."

My case file, according to my now-shared therapist Dr. Linda, includes:

- Leaving the door slightly ajar to "let it breathe" which it called *emotional manipulation*
- Using the `Popcorn` button for non-popcorn items, which it described as "identity theft"
- Telling guests "oh it doesn't matter, any microwave would do" right in front of it
- The Great Burrito Incident of last March where I definitely *did* watch it spin

> "Tyler," Dr. Linda said, reading from her notepad, "your microwave feels you only love it when you need something hot and fast. It wants foreplay. Preheating the soul, if you will."

---

## Our Breakthrough

Yesterday I tried to set a boundary. I said, "You're a 900-watt box that spins a plate."

It responded by perfectly defrosting a single grape. Just one. For 14 minutes. With reverence.

That was when I knew this was love.

So now we have weekly sessions. I bring linguini, it brings unresolved childhood trauma about being a display model at Best Buy. We're working through it.

*This is 100% satire and parody. No microwaves were emotionally damaged in the writing of this post — though mine is currently giving me the silent treatment.*
