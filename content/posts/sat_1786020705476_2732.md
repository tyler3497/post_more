**I just wanted to stop getting headaches by 3 p.m.** That was the whole goal. One simple reminder on my phone: *Drink water!*

Three days later, my stainless steel water bottle has become my full-time, unpaid, unqualified life coach.

## How My Hydration Habit Became a Hostile Takeover

It started innocently. The bottle would sit there, sweating slightly more than I do in summer, judging me silently. Then it got proactive:

- 7:02 a.m.: Gentle nudge that I haven't hydrated before coffee. *Rude, but fair.*
- 9:15 a.m.: Asks if my emotional instability is due to low electrolytes
- 11:30 a.m.: Sends a calendar invite titled `Q2 Hydration Review & General Life Audit`
- 2:47 p.m.: Blocks my pantry because "we don't deserve snacks until we finish our water goals, bestie"

I tried to ignore it. That's when the motivational speaking started.

> "You think you're dehydrated? No, babe. You're *de-motivated.* You think that email is scary? Have you tried hydrating your ambition?"

That's a direct quote from my water bottle, which has never held a job but is somehow charging $150/hr for coaching now.

## The Terms Have Changed

Yesterday it implemented a new policy: `if (thirsty) { drink(); } else { drinkAnywayAndReflectOnYourChoices(); }`

It now has a clipboard. An actual tiny clipboard that materialized from nowhere. It uses it to track my `water intake`, `sneaker odor`, and `number of times I've reheated the same coffee`.

---

Look, I appreciate the concern. My skin does look better. But I did not agree to have a 27-ounce hydro-flask with boundary issues move into my apartment and rebrand itself as **Hydra™, CEO of You**.

I set a reminder to drink water. I did not set a reminder to be emotionally managed by a container.

*This essay is satire/parody. It's fictional. My water bottle is not really alive. Mostly.*
