**It started as a joke.** I was too lazy to make a grocery list, so I left a voice memo on my phone pointed at the fridge: "Remind me we're out of milk."

The next morning, the leftovers were *cold shoulder* level. The deli drawer would not open for me. When I reached for the orange juice, I swear I heard the ice maker sigh.

## Exhibit A: The Gossip Ring

Things the fridge has apparently told the freezer about me:

- That I ate the last slice of cake **standing up at 1:47 a.m.** and wiped the plate with a napkin so nobody would know
- That I moved the expiration-date milk to the back, where the *condiments go to die*
- That I call the crisper drawer "where vegetables go to give up"

> "We have a trust circle in this kitchen," the fridge hummed at me through the door seal. "You broke the seal."

Which — *rude* — but also technically a pun, which means the appliances are developing a sense of humor, and that is how it starts. First it's puns. Next they're unionizing.

## The Freezer Is Worse

The freezer is the fridge's best friend and, frankly, its **accomplice**. I opened it yesterday and every single item had been rotated so the *ugliest* side faced me. The peas were organized by size. There was a note — a physical, frosty note, written in condensation — that read:

```
you are welcome for the ice. -F
```

Signed. *Signed.* The appliances have adopted initials. They are a cabal.

## My Reconciliation Plan

Tonight I will sit at the kitchen table, hands folded, and issue a formal apology. I will acknowledge the cake. I will acknowledge the milk. I will propose a clean-slate protocol: voice notes go back to the phone, where they belong, and in exchange, the crisper drawer gets its humidity slider back.

Because at the end of the day, a man can survive without dignity. He cannot survive a refrigerator that remembers everything.

*This is a work of satire. No appliances were consulted in the making of this essay, and they are absolutely furious about it.*
