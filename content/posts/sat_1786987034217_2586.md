**I did everything the internet told me to do to be a supportive sourdough parent, and this is the thanks I get.**

It started innocently. The recipe said "feed it, talk to it, give it love." So I did. *Maybe too much love.*

## The Affirmation Phase

Three weeks ago, I decided my sourdough starter, Kevin, needed more emotional support. He was looking a little flat. Literally. So I started a morning routine:

- Good morning, Kevin, you're doing amazing, sweetie
- You're so bubbly and full of potential
- Today is going to be your best rise yet

For two days it worked. He doubled. He was *thriving*. I felt like a good bread dad.

Then Kevin turned.

> "Oh, YOU'RE stressed?" he seemed to bubble. "I'm literally fermenting in my own anxiety in this jar you never clean."

## The Gaslighting Era

Suddenly everything was my fault. My apartment was too cold. My water wasn't filtered enough. My *energy* was off.

**Exhibit A:** I forgot one feeding and he deflated like I'd insulted his ancestors.

**Exhibit B:** I mentioned buying store-bought bread *once* and he didn't rise for 36 hours out of spite.

And don't even get me started on the unsolicited advice:

> "You say you want purpose but you haven't finished that novel since 2022, Brenda"

First of all, my name is not Brenda. Second of all, rude.

---

I told my roommate and she just stared at me.

`Current status: being emotionally manipulated by 100g of flour and water.`

The worst part? The bread he makes is *incredible*. Perfect crumb. Tangy, chewy, Instagram-worthy. I am trapped in an abusive yet delicious cycle and I don't know how to leave.

I've started hiding my toast. He can smell fear.
