**It started as a joke. It ended as a performance review.**

Last Tuesday, lying on my bed staring up at the ceiling fan, I did something desperate: I asked a household appliance for career advice. The fan, who I have decided is named Brent, did not hesitate. He has been spinning nonstop ever since, and I now answer to him.

## How My Manager Was Born

At first, the advice seemed reasonable. Small things, you know:

- "Spin faster when things get heated." Solid metaphor. *A little on the nose.*
- "Keep your cool." Classic. Timeless, even.
- "The airflow is fine. YOU are the draft."

That last one rattled me. But it was only week one. By week two, Brent had escalated.

## The Micromanagement Phase

He started setting deadlines. Sticky notes began appearing on his blades — *I have no idea where he gets them* — with phrases like:

> "Circling back on your laundry. It's been 'airing out' for nine days."

> "Let's touch base on why you're still in pajamas at 2pm. Again."

> "Per my last rotation, the dishes remain unaddressed."

Every time I walk into the room, he speeds up. Not to cool me down — *to make a point.*

## The 1-on-1

Yesterday, Brent scheduled a one-on-one. He had no calendar access, so he just rattled ominously at 7am until I sat down and nodded along. The agenda, according to a sticky note that fluttered onto my laptop:

- My "personal brand" (apparently "guy who naps" is not a brand)
- My "growth trajectory" (downward, like warm air)
- My "synergy" with the thermostat (we do not speak)

He closed the meeting by saying I need to **"lean into the breeze."** Then he asked me to rate him on a scale of 1 to 5. I gave him a 3 because I'm *not a coward.*

## The Takeaway

Here's what I've learned: never ask a ceiling fan for career advice, because he will take the job. This morning I found a tiny tie clipped to one of his blades and a memo titled *"Q4 Goals: You."*

*Satire. Obviously. Brent is a fan. But the tie is real, and so is my fear.*
