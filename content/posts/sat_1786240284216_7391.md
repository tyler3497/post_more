**I looked at the chair, the pile, the shame, and said the five words that ruined my summer: "I'm sorry I've ignored you."**

I was doing late-night laundry-themed self-improvement at 11 p.m., which is never a good time to get vulnerable with textiles. The pile — two weeks of *I'll do it tomorrow* meets *what if I just buy more socks* — shifted. I swear it exhaled.

I thought that would be the end of it. Forgiveness. Closure. Moving the pile from chair to hamper like an adult.

No. Now we have a relationship.

## The Demands

Since the apology, my laundry has established boundaries, which is rich coming from something that has lived on a chair for 14 days:

- **Weekly check-ins** every Sunday at 7 p.m., no phones, just feelings and fabric softener
- It wants me to *journal* about why I keep buying grey shirts that are technically the same shirt
- A five-year plan that includes "delicates handling certification" and, and I quote, "emotional availability on sock-matching days"

Yesterday I tried to just throw everything in cold and call it a day. The hoodie sleeve pointed at me accusatorially.

> "We talked about this. This is not the energy we agreed to bring to the cycle. Do you want pilling? Is that what you want for us?"

My roommate walked in during this. She just said:

> "Are you... arguing with the chair again?"

*I told her the chair is now a co-parenting situation.*

---

It gets worse. The pile has spiritually hired a former dryer sheet as a mediator. It leaves me little reminders like `wrinkle.release.exe` on a Post-it. It asked if we could do a couples costume for Halloween: me and my own laundry. *It's clingy in a literal, static-electricity kind of way.*

I tried to set a boundary back. I said, "You're clothes."

It said, through the profound silence of a damp towel that never quite dries:

> "And you're the one apologizing to us at midnight."

Fine. We have therapy Tuesdays now. I bring snacks. It brings unresolved denim issues.

`Status: chair is 40% visible, emotionally 100% accountable`
