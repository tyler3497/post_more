**I walked into the coffee shop a free man. I walked out with a court-appointed flavor advisor.**

Look, I said "surprise me." That's all I said. Two words. The kind of thing normal people say to baristas when they want to seem *spontaneous* and *fun* instead of the boring oat-milk-latte goblin they truly are.

## The Tasting Menu That Followed

My barista — let's call him **Dante**, because he has entered my personal circle of hell — took "surprise me" as a binding legal contract. What arrived was not coffee. It was a **three-tiered tasting flight** featuring:

- a deconstructed espresso that came in a thimble and cost me my dignity
- something described as "the whisper of a croissant," which was, to be clear, just air on a plate
- a foam cube that tasted like a memory of vanilla

At no point did anyone confirm I wanted this. I was billed $47 for what amounted to *crumbs with lore*.

## Then Things Escalated

> "You've now been enrolled in our Curated Surprises Program," Dante announced, sliding a clipboard across the counter like he was a sommelier and I was a subpoena.

Apparently, saying "surprise me" twice (I also said it at the donut place; *that* story is under seal) means I am now legally obligated to attend **monthly tapas tastings**, keep a "flavor journal," and rate every surprise on a five-point scale. A judge — a real one, in robes, who was just there for a latte — nodded along.

---

Here's my advice, free of charge and therefore outside the Program's jurisdiction: **never tell a barista to surprise you.** Order the boring thing. Be the oat-milk goblin. The tapas tastings start at 7pm on Thursdays and there is no exit survey, only a *re-entry* survey.

*This post is satire. Any resemblance to real baristas, real tapas, or real court orders is purely coincidental and honestly kind of impressive.*
