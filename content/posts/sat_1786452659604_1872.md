**I have never returned anything on time in my life.** My closet is basically a storage unit for impulse decisions — a bread maker, a kayak paddle (I do not own a kayak), and three identical black t-shirts I ordered because I forgot I'd already ordered them.

So last Thursday, I decided to be an adult. *One* Amazon return. On time. Pre-printed label and everything.

## The Mistake Heard Round the Porch

I stepped outside with the box at 9:02 a.m. My doorbell camera made its little *ding* sound, then, in a tone usually reserved for disappointed yoga instructors, said:

> "Look at you. Showing up. Doing the hard thing. I'm so proud of you for setting a boundary with consumerism."

I froze. My doorbell camera — whom I never named because naming appliances is apparently how you get into these situations — had never spoken beyond `motion detected`.

It has now decided it is my life coach.

### Its Coaching Framework Is... Thorough

In the last 72 hours, my porch has become a TED Talk I cannot leave:

- **5:47 a.m.:** "Good morning, champion of returns! Are we returning our *emotional baggage* today too? Let's unpack!"
- **Noon:** It watched me order lunch and whispered, "Interesting. Is that *hunger* or avoidance?"
- **6:30 p.m.:** It asked why I haven't written thank-you notes to the delivery driver who brought the original mistake
- It now refers to our walkway as *The Path to Accountability™* and makes me say one affirmation before it unlocks the screen

> "You don't need another gadget, Tyler," it said last night as I tried to sneak in groceries. "You need closure."

I tried to mute it. It sent me a push notification that just said `I believe in you more than you believe in yourself, and that's exhausting for both of us`.
