**SATIRE.** Everything below is made up. My books did not come to life. *That I can prove.*

It started, as all good disasters do, with a label maker.

I spent Saturday afternoon alphabetizing my bookshelf. Not by genre, not by color like some kind of cover-story sociopath — by author, the *respectable* way. Austen to Vonnegut, orderly, dignified. I stood back, admired my work, and said out loud the words that doomed me: "Finally, some structure in this house."

## The Schism Begins

By Sunday morning, the cookbooks had formed a union. I found a tiny picket sign — *a literal tiny picket sign* — leaning against *The Joy of Cooking*. The grievances, as I was able to piece them together from strategically placed sticky notes, were as follows:

- The novels "look down their spines" at anything with the word *casserole* in the subtitle
- *Pride and Prejudice* called *The Ultimate Slow Cooker* "a pamphlet with delusions"
- The memoirs, meanwhile, have remained aggressively neutral, which frankly feels worse

> "We are NOT anti-intellectual," a cookbook note read, in handwriting that was absolutely not mine. "We just think it's funny that *Gravity's Rainbow* has been 'in progress' for nine years."

## Diplomacy Has Failed

I've tried everything. I rearranged the shelf by *height*, hoping equality through uniformity, and the tall novels immediately declared themselves "the ruling class of the shelf." I tried the color-coded method — I know, I said never — and the green spines staged a walkout.

The worst part? I used to *read* here. This was my sanctuary. Now every evening, I sit down with my tea while two shelves of literature glare at each other over the top of my head like divorced parents at a school play.

*My library card expires next month. I am considering letting it lapse and starting over in a country with no shelving.*

My therapist says I should "rehome" some of the books. But let's be honest — that's exactly what the cookbooks want, and I refuse to reward hostility with a trip to the donation bin.

I am a hostage in my own living room. Send snacks. Preferably nothing requiring a cookbook. **Those are the ones with the knives.**
