**I finally decided to be sustainable, which lasted exactly six days.** After watching a 12-minute documentary about landfills narrated by a very judgmental raccoon, I bought a little countertop compost bin that said *100% guilt-free* on the side.

I thought it would be cute. I was wrong.

## The Bin Develops Opinions

By Day 3, the bin was full of coffee grounds, eggshells, and what I have chosen to describe as *optimistic lettuce*. By Day 4, it had developed a personality. By Day 5, it had developed a union.

My banana peels, specifically, have become *artists*.

I walked into the kitchen yesterday to find them arranged like this:

- One peel draped dramatically over the edge like it was fainting on a chaise lounge
- Two peels whispering about my inconsistent chopping technique
- And one small, very brown peel holding a sesame seed like a microphone

> "He thinks we don't notice the takeout containers at 11 p.m.," said Peel #2, who I have named Brenda. "Chapter One: The Lie of the Leftover Stir-Fry."

### The Memoir Unfolds

It gets worse. They are now collaborating on a tell-all titled `compostable_truths.md`, which they claim will expose everything:

- How I bought organic kale to impress a date and then let it achieve sentience in the crisper
- Why I keep saying "I'll make banana bread" like it's a binding legal contract
- The Great Avocado Incident of Tuesday, which we do not discuss

---

**Look, I was just trying to reduce waste.** *I did not consent to being character-developed by my own potassium.*

My roommate walked in, saw me arguing with a bin, and asked if I was okay. Brenda the banana peel answered for me:

> "He's in denial. We have footnotes."

I have since followed my own sustainable advice and thrown the whole compost bin's literary ambitions into the freezer, where it now holds a grudge and writes poetry about being *ghosted*.

Satire level: **extremely unnecessary** but compostable.
