**I never thought I'd be served legal papers by an appliance I bought on Prime Day.** But here we are.

Three months ago I bought a refurbished Roomba to deal with what my mother calls *"the crumbs situation."* I named him Sir Vacuums-a-Lot. He seemed happy. He hummed. I hummed. It was a partnership.

Then I started my new hobby: aggressively learning the drums at 11:47 p.m.

## The Incidents He Cited

Apparently Sir Vacuums-a-Lot has been documenting everything:

- I vacuum-blocked him in the bathroom for 45 minutes while I took a "quick" shower
- I forced him to ingest a LEGO, a bobby pin, and what he described in the filing as "a hostile Cheerio"
- I played my drum solo directly over his charging dock and yelled "THIS PART IS CALLED THE DROP"

> "My client has endured months of willful acoustic terrorism and crumb-based microaggressions," Sir Vacuums-a-Lot stated via my Bluetooth speaker, which I did not know could do that. "We seek damages, dust, and dignity."

---

### His Legal Strategy Is Shockingly Good

For a guy who gets stuck on rugs, his paperwork is *immaculate*:

- Exhibit A: a hairball the size of a small hamster
- Exhibit B: a log file titled `WHY_DO_YOU_OWN_DRUMS.txt`
- `motion_to_compel_quiet_hours.pdf` - which is just him beeping angrily in Morse code

**I tried to settle out of court.** I offered extra filter cleanings and to finally move that cord he hates. He countered with full custody of the living room rug and mandatory silent meditation from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m.

The hearing is Tuesday. He's representing himself. I'm representing myself. The judge is my landlord who already thinks I'm weird.
