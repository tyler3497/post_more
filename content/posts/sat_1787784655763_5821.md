**I bought a succulent because the tag said "impossible to kill."** Which, in hindsight, is exactly what you say about a low-maintenance friend before they become the most high-maintenance person in your life.

*For context, I have killed a cactus. A cactus.*

## How It Started vs. How It's Going

I named him Sir Spikes-A-Lot, put him on my windowsill, and watered him once every time I remembered I had responsibilities. Which is to say, every 9-12 business days.

He thrived. Suspiciously well. Grew two new leaves, got a little sun-tan, started *leaning* toward me when I walked by.

Then I found the Instagram.

- @SirSpikesWellness, 412 followers, bio: "Plant Dad Recovery 🌱 | Healing my human"
- Daily Stories: me eating cereal at 11pm in the same sweatpants
- A Reel titled "My Human Tries Meditation (GONE WRONG)"
- Affiliate links to self-watering pots *for me to buy for him*

## The Intervention I Didn't Ask For

He started giving advice.

> "Your human woke up and immediately checked his screen time. We need to talk about rootedness. Pun intended. Stay succulent. 💧"

*He used a pun. He never even went to college.*

Yesterday he posted a carousel: **5 Signs Your Human Is Dehydrated (And Projecting It Onto You)** with a photo of my empty water bottle next to his perfectly moist soil.

I tried to defend myself in the comments. He replied:

```
let me = new Person(who.waters.himself.first)
```

The comment got 87 likes.

---

Look, I'm happy for him. He's **thriving**, he's **established boundaries**, he's got a brand deal with a misting bottle company.

Meanwhile I'm being told by a three-inch desert plant to touch grass.
