**For three months I told myself I was practicing strategic botanical independence.** Turns out I was just committing mild horticultural neglect, and my succulent noticed.

His name is Kevin. He's a *desert-trained survivalist* who I bought because the tag said "impossible to kill." Challenge accepted, apparently.

## The Incident

On Tuesday, guilt finally outweighed laziness. I watered him. One small splash. He perked up like he'd just been rescued from a reality show.

Within hours, things got weird:

- He started dictating into my old tape recorder *(where did he get a tape recorder?)*
- He requested "soft jazz and unfiltered morning light for creative clarity"
- He asked me to blot his leaves because, quote, "sweat breaks the artistic seal"
- He referred to my apartment as "the drought house"

> "You left me in that windowsill for 94 days with nothing but dust and your Spotify Lo-Fi playlist," Kevin told me, leaf trembling. "I've seen things. I've *become* things. This memoir writes itself."

## The Press Tour Begins

Now he's calling it **DESERTED: A Leaf's Life on the Brink (And How I Thrived Despite My Human)**. Chapter One is just the word `thirsty` repeated for four pages.

He's already planning the sequel. He's pitching my spider plant as an unreliable narrator.

---

Look, I respect the hustle. *I've never been more hydrated by secondhand inspiration.* But Kevin just asked for a book advance in the form of "one (1) premium cactus fertilizer stick and absolute silence between 2-4pm for journaling."

I told him he was impossible to kill. He told me,

> "Hardly. But I'm definitely impossible to *summarize*."

*This is satire / parody. Completely fictional. No succulents were emotionally compensated in the making of this essay.*
