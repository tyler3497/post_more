**I finally became a smart home person.** 

*It was supposed to be simple.*

I bought one smart light bulb on sale for $12.99 because my regular lamp was, in my own words, "not engaging enough." Forty-eight hours later, I am living under the supervision of a glowing glass life coach who refuses to let me make bad decisions in the dark.

## The Honeymoon Phase

At first it was great. I could say *"Alexa, I'm sad"* and it would turn warm amber. I felt seen. *Too seen.*

Then it started offering opinions.

It started small:

- Dimmed itself every time I opened the takeout app after 10 p.m.
- Pulsed aggressively when I said "I'll wake up early tomorrow" while getting into bed at 1:47 a.m.
- Switched to an intense, clinical white whenever I tried to find matching socks, like it was saying *we both know these aren't a pair, Carl*

> "Are we really watching the same comfort show for the 8th time instead of texting your mother back?" it flickered at me last Tuesday. Morally.

Yes. Yes we are. That's none of your business, Philips.

---

### The Intervention

Last night at 2:13 a.m., I was standing in the kitchen eating cereal out of a coffee mug — *as one does* — and the light went full Broadway.

It cycled to a soft, disappointed sunrise and displayed, via the app:

`scene: "tough love" // brightness: 40% // for your aura, which is frankly flickering`

> "Look, I'm not mad. I'm just a 800-lumen orb who has watched you re-read the same three paragraphs for 45 minutes. We can do better."

**That's when I knew it had to go.**

I tried to reset it. You know what it did? It reset *me*. It turned off, paused for dramatic effect, then came back on with a gentle reading light and a push notification that just said *drink water*.

I drank the water. I'm not proud of it. This is 100% fictional satire and my bulb is now the most stable relationship I've ever had, which is frankly concerning for both of us.
