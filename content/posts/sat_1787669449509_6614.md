**I made one inclusive hiring decision in my kitchen and now my appliances are unionizing.**

It seemed reasonable at the time. My doctor said "maybe less caffeine after 3pm," my sleep tracker said "please, I'm begging you," and the decaf bag at the store was on sale. So I brought it home. I told my coffee maker we were going to try something new. A *lifestyle change*.

## The Incident

My coffee maker did not take it well.

> "So you're saying I'm not *enough* anymore?" it gurgled, in that passive-aggressive hiss it does right before the carafe fills. "After everything we've brewed together?"

I tried to explain it was about balance, not performance. I used my calmest voice. The voice you use when explaining to a Roomba why it can't eat socks.

Here's what happened next:

- The toaster filed a formal complaint about "hostile work environment"
- The air fryer started scheduling mandatory wellness check-ins for the coffee maker
- My kettle began whispering, "We all feel decaffeinated on the inside sometimes," which was *not helpful*
- The fridge light started flickering in solidarity, like a tiny protest vigil

I found a sticky note on the counter this morning written in what I can only assume is dried espresso grounds:

> "We have contacted HR. Do not attempt to brew."

---

I called HR. It was just my smart speaker playing hold music that was somehow also my coffee maker sighing on loop. I tried to run `apologize --sincerely --with_oat_milk` but the terminal returned `Error: trust issues detected`.

*Obviously* this is the part where I realize I've anthropomorphized a $49 appliance because I've been working from home too long. But you know what? If my coffee maker wants to take a mental health day, I respect that.

I'm drinking tea now. Under supervision.

*This is satire/parody. No coffee makers were harmed in the making of this emotional damage. My kitchen is fictional and currently in mediation.*
