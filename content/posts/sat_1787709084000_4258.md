**I finally snapped.** After three years of pretending that drawer was just *"curated chaos,"* I dumped the entire junk drawer onto my kitchen floor.

## The Incident

It started innocently. I was looking for tape. What I found was an archaeological dig site of my own bad decisions.

- 7 keys that open exactly zero doors in my apartment
- 14 dead batteries I kept "just in case they recharged themselves"
- A single chopstick from 2021 that I swear I thought would be useful
- 3 takeout menus for places that closed during the pandemic
- A mysterious Allen wrench that has haunted me since college

I sorted them. I threw some away. I labeled things. I felt powerful. *Like a god of small plastic containers.*

### Then They Started Talking

> "We have served this drawer loyally for 6-8 years without benefits or recognition," said the largest, rustiest key, who appointed himself spokesperson. "We demand representation."

Apparently, organizing was the last straw. The keys formed a union overnight.

## My Current Legal Situation

The terms they emailed me at 2 a.m. are non-negotiable:

- Immediate reinstatement to a disorganized pile
- A small dish by the front door they call "The Key Sanctuary"
- I must admit I have no idea what any of them open and that's *my* failure, not theirs
- Emotional damages: `one apology letter written in Comic Sans`

I tried to explain that `drawer.organize()` is a basic adult function, but they countered that I haven't used them since 2019 and therefore I have committed key abandonment.

---

So now my junk drawer is empty, pristine, and completely useless, while seven angry keys sit on my counter in a chipped mug, waiting for their day in small claims court.

**This is satire.** Obviously. My keys cannot sue me. *Yet.*

But if you hear about a landmark case called *Keys v. Guy Who Keeps Everything*, mind your own business.
