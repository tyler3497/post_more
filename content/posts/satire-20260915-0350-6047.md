**It started as a joke.** Three weeks ago, I registered my sourdough starter — "Bready Mercury," a bubbling jar of flour and poor decisions — for the Lakeside 10K. The form asked for an emergency contact. The jar was right there.

## The Training Regimen

Nobody told me a fermented blob could be *this* disciplined. Bready Mercury's daily schedule:

- 5:00 AM: "rise" (it does this naturally, but now it calls it *intentional*)
- 5:15 AM: interval feeding — double rations, zero excuses
- 6:00 AM: visualization exercises (it stares at the oven)
- 7:00 PM: ice bath (the fridge, not optional)

Meanwhile I've been on the couch eating the discard crackers. The *rejects*. The losers.

## Race Day Drama

Last Saturday it hired a coach. A French baguette with suspicious credentials. The baguette kept saying things like "zee crumb does not lie," and Bready just *absorbed* it — literally. My starter now has a core strength I can only describe as alarming.

> "Your athlete has qualified for the elite corral," the race director emailed. "Please have your *runner* at the start line by 6:45 AM. No glass containers over 16 ounces."

I tried to withdraw it. The withdrawal form required the athlete's signature. It left a sourdough fingerprint. It counted.

## The Aftermath

It PR'd. **Thirty-eight minutes.** I finished the fun run in an hour and nine minutes, mostly because I stopped for a bagel. The finisher photo shows Bready in its little jar, race bib pinned to the lid, looking *fresh* — while I look like a deflated yoga ball.

Sponsors are calling. A hydration brand wants it as an ambassador. It has more followers than I do now, and its agent — the baguette — calls me "emotional support infrastructure."

So this Saturday I'm running a 5K. Alone. Bready said it would pace me.

*I have never been more motivated, or more humiliated.*
