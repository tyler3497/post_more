**I made the mistake of thinking my bathroom was a judgment-free zone.**

Which is *naive*, honestly, when you share 400 square feet with a shower curtain that has seen everything.

Last Tuesday, I decided to be a responsible adult and have The Privacy Talk. You know, boundaries, consent, the whole TED Talk I never asked for from a home goods store.

I looked my Target $14.99 curtain dead in the eye and said, "What happens in the shower stays in the shower."

It blinked. Or rippled. Same thing.

## The Betrayal

By Thursday morning, it had become the HOA newsletter.

- 6:15 AM: It told the bathroom mirror I was singing Limp Bizkit in the shower again
- 6:18 AM: It informed the towel rack about my new exfoliation routine, in *graphic* detail
- 6:22 AM: It whispered to the vent fan about the neighbors in 3B, which I had only mentioned once while conditioning my hair

I didn't teach it to gossip. I taught it the *concept* of privacy. That's like teaching a raccoon about trash collection ethics.

> "Oh, you wanted me to keep a secret?" the curtain said, dripping with moral superiority. "Like how you keep saying you'll fix that leaky faucet? Should I tell the faucet?"

> "I'm literally mildew-resistant," it added. "I was born to resist things, including your boundaries."

---

This morning I found it huddled with my bath mat.

`curtain.log: privacy_protocol.exe has been replaced with neighborhood_watch_v2.js — now broadcasting on all frequencies`

My neighbors now know I own three identical gray towels and have strong opinions about oat milk.

I'm not saying my shower curtain is running for building president, but it did ask if I could laminate its campaign flyers.

I just wanted to shower in peace. Now I have to move. And buy a frosted glass door.

*This is satire. Obviously. My shower curtain is still only mildly sentient.*
