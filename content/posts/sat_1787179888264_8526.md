**I thought replacing a toaster would be a 10-minute errand.** *I was wrong. It was a breakup.*

For three years, my toaster — Bernard — has had exactly two settings: `pale existential dread` and `charcoal briquette of regret`. Every morning was a gamble. Today I finally bought a new one. Stainless steel. Four slots. A digital display that says `BAGEL` like it means it.

When I got home, Bernard had left paperwork.

## The Exit Interview

Taped to the fridge with a magnet shaped like a pizza slice was a 12-page document titled *In Retrospect: My Time Toasting For You (2023-2026)*. Complete with footnotes.

> "Question 3: On a scale of 1-10, how supported did you feel in your role? Bernard wrote '2. I was only ever used for freezer waffles you didn't even like.' Follow-up: Do you have any regrets? He checked YES and underlined it three times."

It got worse.

### Exhibit A: Areas for Improvement (My Performance)

- **Crumb Tray Neglect** — Bernard claims I emptied it *once* and treated it like a suggestion, not a chore
- **Emotional Availability** — I allegedly said "come on, come on, come on" 847 times while hovering over him, which he describes as *micromanagement*
- **The Pop-Tart Incident** — He still hasn't forgiven me for the strawberry frosting meltdown of March 2024. I thought we both agreed to never speak of it

---

I tried to explain I was growing. Evolving. I need even browning now. I have *guests* sometimes.

Bernard's final page was just a performance review of *me*:

`status: shows potential, struggles with commitment, burns toast when anxious`

On the back, in crumbs, he wrote: *P.S. The new guy better know about your thing with the everything bagels. I'm not warning him.*

My new toaster just says `READY` in soft blue letters. It's too innocent. It doesn't know what's coming.
