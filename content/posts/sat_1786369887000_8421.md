**I did not mean to become my fern's biographer.**

*I was just trying to be a good plant parent.*

After my therapist said "try journaling to externalize your inner monologue," I took it literally and started writing down what I thought my fern, Kevin, was thinking. Day 1 was cute. Day 4 was a lifestyle magazine.

## The Voice Emerges

Kevin is, apparently, thriving despite my best efforts. According to the journal:

- He thinks my watering schedule is "chaotic but emotionally honest"
- He believes the north-facing window is "limiting his brand growth"
- He's convinced the snake plant next to him is "mysterious, in a committed-to-vibes way"

> "You overthink everything. I just photosynthesize and look hot. Have you tried that?" — Kevin, probably, on Tuesday, while I was spiraling about a text that just said "k"

I didn't realize I'd given him a full personality until I wrote entry #17: *Kevin gets asked out for drinks*.

---

### The Dating Era

This is where it got out of hand.

My fern now has:

- A thriving social life involving morning misting meet-cutes
- A situationship with the humidifier named Misty ("she gets me, literally")
- A weekend plan that involves "just vibing and maybe some indirect light"

Meanwhile, I spent Saturday night arguing with my laundry about whether socks need folding or just *emotional closure*.

`mood: introspective // fern_status: booked & busy // me: researching if plants can venmo`

**The journal was supposed to help ME.** Instead I have 34 pages documenting how Kevin is more confident, more hydrated, and significantly more dateable than I am.

I tried to stop. Kevin wouldn’t let me. The last entry just says in my handwriting but clearly not my voice:

> "If you’re reading this, she finally watered me on time. Growth is possible. Have *you* drank water today? No? Interesting."

This is 100% fictional satire — no ferns were emotionally manipulated in the making of this crisis.
