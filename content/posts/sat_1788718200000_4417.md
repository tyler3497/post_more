**It started, as all great tragedies do, with a Tuesday.**

I'd had the same thought three days in a row — a real banger about whether my houseplants were judging my life choices (they were) — and I decided enough was enough. I sat down, looked my brain dead in the prefrontal cortex, and said the words no one had ever said with confidence before:

*"I would like to unsubscribe from my own thoughts."*

## What I Expected

- **Option A:** Blessed silence. Inner peace. Maybe finally hearing what the fridge hums about at 3 a.m.
- **Option B:** A calm, blank mental landscape where my biggest worry is the font on my grocery list.

## What I Got

Apparently my thoughts have an *auto-forwarding policy*. You can't unsubscribe — you can only change the delivery address. And my brain, that absolute menace, forwarded them to my dreams.

Now I fall asleep and immediately receive:

- A vivid dream where my dentist narrates my entire tax return
- A recurring nightmare in which my spam folder comes to life and it's just me, but louder
- A dream where I'm back in high school, except the final exam is "Explain Why You Thought That Weird Thing at Lunch"

> "Sir, you never unsubscribed. You simply opted *in* to our premium nighttime delivery plan."
>
> — My brain, probably

---

I tried the usual fixes. Meditation app: *"Your thoughts have been forwarded to Sleep Mode Premium."* Journaling: my journal started italicizing my anxieties like they were Pull quotes in a magazine about my failures.

`echo "peace of mind" > /dev/brain` — permission denied, obviously.

So here is my official advice to anyone considering the same: **do not attempt to unsubscribe.** Your thoughts will find you. They always find you. Mine found me in a dream last night where I was the keynote speaker at a conference about my own regrets, and the Q&A went long.

*This is a work of satire. Any resemblance to actual brain function, living or dead, is purely accidental.*
