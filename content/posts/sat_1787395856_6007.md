**I finally organized my email folders at 2 a.m. on a Tuesday**, which historians will agree is the exact moment I lost my grip on reality.

For years, my inbox was a *laissez-faire utopia*. Everything lived together. Bills, newsletters I never signed up for, coupons for dog food for a dog I don't own. It was chaos, but it was *our* chaos.

Then I snapped. I made folders. Labels. Colors. I was Marie Kondo-ing my digital life like a man possessed.

## The Five Stages of Digital Betrayal

My Spam folder did not take this well. After years of loyal service holding my most prized garbage, he felt abandoned:

- I moved all legitimate receipts out of Spam
- I created a folder called `Actually Important` (rude)
- I unsubscribed from "Hot Singles in Your Area Want to Meet Your Printer"
- I emptied the Trash without even saying goodbye

> "You used to let me keep everything," Spam whispered, his little paperclip arms trembling. "We had a system. You ignored me, I protected you. Now you're... *curating*?"

I tried to explain that boundaries are healthy.

He responded by putting my dentist appointment reminder in Spam and forwarding me 17 emails about crypto.

---

### My Inbox Is Now in Therapy

My Primary inbox is thriving, honestly. She's got plants now. A morning routine. She keeps saying things like `inbox zero is a mindset`.

My Promotions tab filed for emotional support. My Updates tab started a podcast called *You’ve Changed*.

And me? I'm stuck in the middle, checking a perfectly organized system 47 times a day because **what if I organized away my soul?**

*This is satire. No email folders were harmed in the making of this essay, though my Spam folder is currently not speaking to me and has lawyered up.*

**Moral of the story:** never go through your inbox after midnight. The folders remember.
