**I finally folded a fitted sheet correctly.**

*Which is apparently the domestic equivalent of discovering fire, according to my linen closet.*

For seven years, my approach to fitted sheets has been what scientists call the 'rage ball method.' You know the method. You wrestle the elastic corners, you whisper "why are you like this," you ball it up and shove it on the shelf like a shameful secret.

This Tuesday, at 2:13 p.m., I did it. Corners tucked into corners. Edges aligned. A perfect rectangle. I stared at it for a full minute waiting for it to spring apart like it always does. It didn't.

## My Linen Closet Immediately Developed Imposter Syndrome

It didn't know how to handle competence. For years, my closet has operated on low expectations and mutual avoidance. Now there's a perfectly folded fitted sheet sitting on the second shelf like a valedictorian.

Suddenly everything else felt judged:

- My bath towels are folded in what can only be described as "aggressive rectangles"
- The pillowcases have given up and are just existing as fabric envelopes
- The top sheet is still pretending it doesn't know the fitted sheet at all
- The hand towels are holding a meeting about whether I'm okay

> "We don't DO this here," whispered my laundry basket, who has seen things. "We do chaos. We do the ball. We do not do... hospital corners on elastic. Who even ARE you now?"

*I tried to explain it was just a YouTube tutorial and 45 minutes of quiet sobbing, but the basket wasn't buying it.*

## The Power Immediately Went to My Head

By Wednesday I was walking around the house like I'd been personally knighted by Martha Stewart. I refolded the same sheet three times just to prove it wasn't a fluke. I considered ironing it, which is how you know someone needs an intervention.

Look, I'm not saying I've peaked as an adult. I'm just saying my linen closet now has a standard it never asked for, and my fitted sheet is sitting there, smug and flat, like it pays rent and does taxes early.

I put it back slightly crooked on purpose to make everyone feel better. Balance restored. Growth is about knowing when to dim your light so your towels don't file a complaint.
