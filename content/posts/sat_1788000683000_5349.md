**I finally did the responsible thing, and now I'm in too deep to quit.**

*I should have known something was wrong when I returned a book three days early and the entire circulation desk gasped like I'd just announced I was running for mayor.*

## How Returning One Book Became a Lifestyle Commitment

It was a paperback about container gardening. 197 pages. I renewed it twice, then actually read it on a Tuesday because my phone died and I panicked. When I brought it back, I was expecting a quiet *beep* and a receipt.

Instead, Ms. Delaney — 30-year veteran, cardigan with actual elbow patches, knows the Dewey Decimal System by smell — looked at me over her glasses and said:

> "A return. On time. Do you... do you know what you've just done?"

Apparently what I've done is auditioned.

Here is what has happened since that fateful Tuesday:

- She has reserved a study carrel for me with a handwritten nameplate that says *GARDENING PROTÉGÉ — DO NOT DISTURB*
- She emailed me a curated list of 14 books called *Your Next Steps* which includes *Advanced Compost* and *The Soil Will Remember You*
- She told the teen volunteers I have "the spark" and now they look at me with respectful fear
- She laminated my library card unprompted — *laminated* — and said "this is how we treat our own"

### The Pact I Didn't Agree To

Yesterday she slid a flyer across the desk for a Saturday workshop: *Seed Starting for Serious People*.

```js
if (patron.returnsOnTime == true) {
  librarian.expectations = Infinity;
  patron.freeWeekends = 0;
  librarian.nurturingIntensity = "intense but well-meaning";
}
```

I tried to say I had plans. She said:

> "Oh honey, your plans *are* the plans now. You're the one who showed initiative."

---

Look, I love libraries. I love that she believes in me. But I returned one book about tomatoes because I felt guilty. I am not ready to become the heir to the community garden plot.

Still, she already ordered me a name tag for the Fall Plant Swap. It says **PROTÉGÉ**. In Comic Sans.

*This is satire. I am not actually being mentored by the library. The tomatoes are fictional. Please don't tell Ms. Delaney I said that.*
