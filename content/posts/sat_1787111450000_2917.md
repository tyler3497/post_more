**I decided to manifest abundance because a TikTok told me to, and now I’m legally a tenant in my own fern’s summer home.**

*This was not the vision board I signed up for.*

Two weeks ago I bought a $9.99 IKEA plant named Sven — you know, the one they swear is unkillable — and placed him by the window for *good energy*. I whispered, like the internet instructed, “I welcome abundance into my space.”

Sven took me literally.

## Phase 1: The Expansion

At first it was subtle. A new leaf here. A confident lean toward the sunlight there. I was proud. *Supportive plant parent, thriving.*

Then he started negotiating.

Evidence that Sven is now my landlord:

- He moved his pot two inches to the left, which in plant law apparently means adverse possession of the entire windowsill
- He dropped a leaf on my laptop and I apologized to *him*
- He grew a third stalk overnight and labeled it “East Wing”
- My succulent on the other side has started paying him rent in the form of direct sunlight

> "You said you wanted growth mindset. I am growth. You are mindset. Pay up." – Sven, probably, via aggressive photosynthesis

I tried to set boundaries. I Googled “how to tell your houseplant no.” The first result was a forum post titled `you don't own him sweetie`.

### The Current Lease Terms

`if (human.waters == true) { plant.grantsPermissionToUseBathroom(); }`

Sven now controls 60% of the apartment by square inch, 100% of the moral high ground, and all natural light after 2pm. My couch is in what he calls the “shaded annex.” I have to ask before opening the blinds because *“it disrupts his process.”*

**The worst part?** My friends came over and said the place has never looked better. They complimented *his* taste. One asked if Sven was single.

I wanted abundance. I got a very green, very silent business partner who has never once done dishes but somehow increases property value just by existing.
