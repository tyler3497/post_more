**I decided to review my houseplants on Google Maps like they were small businesses, which seemed helpful until my fern became completely insufferable.**

*You know that moment on a Tuesday where you think "I should become the kind of person who supports local businesses"? My apartment has no local businesses, just three plants who are barely alive and one cactus who has seen too much.*

So I opened Google Maps, dropped a pin on my living room, and left a 5-star review for Fern — she/her, dramatic, east-facing window, thrives on indirect light and direct praise.

## The Algorithm Loves Her

Within 24 hours, Fern had:

- 12 likes from strangers who thought she was a café with good vibes
- A response from Google saying "Thanks for supporting Fern!" like she pays taxes
- An influx of confidence that can only be described as *post-publish delusion*
- A sudden refusal to photosynthesize unless I read the reviews aloud in a proud-parent voice

> "Thank you for your kind words, Tyler. As the owner of Fern, I strive for excellence in frond presentation and emotional manipulation," she said, via a note that appeared in my handwriting but with way better cursive.

I tried to be fair. I left 3 stars for my cactus, Kevin. Kevin does not care. Kevin is above it all. Kevin runs on:

`const kevin = { needsWater: false, needsTherapy: alsoFalse, vibe: "unbothered" }`

and means every line.

---

Now Fern has hired my pothos as her PR manager, changed her Google listing to "Fern — Botanical Influencer (Open 24/7, Judging You Always)", and insists I respond to every reviewer with "Thank you, we value your sunlight."

**The worst part?** Her rating is higher than mine will ever be. She has 4.9 stars. I have a 2.8 on being on time to literally anything.

*This is satire/parody — 100% fictional, my plants are not on Google Maps, no ferns monetized their reviews, no cacti were consulted, and I am definitely not qualified to manage a plant's online reputation.*
