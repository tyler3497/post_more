**I THOUGHT I WAS BEING RESPONSIBLE. TURNS OUT I WAS PLAYING MATCHMAKER FOR APPLIANCES WITH BOUNDARY ISSUES.**

*I introduced my air fryer to my smoke alarm so they'd get along better, and now they have a joint custody agreement over my ability to make dinner.*

It started because my air fryer — *Baron Von Crisp, as he insisted on being called* — kept making my smoke alarm scream like I was hosting a bonfire in a broom closet. Every time I made sweet potato fries, the alarm would go full opera.

So I did what any rational adult would do: I held them up to each other and said, "You two need to talk."

## The Summit Meeting Nobody Asked For

I placed the air fryer on the counter and pointed at the ceiling:

- Please stop traumatizing Brenda (my smoke alarm's new legal name)
- Brenda, please learn the difference between "house fire" and "guy trying to be healthy"
- Let's all be adults about this

They listened. Too well.

> "We have decided to co-parent your kitchen anxiety," announced Brenda via a calm, judgmental chirp I'd never heard before. "From now on, all cooking decisions go through us."

*This was not the peace treaty I envisioned.*

Now Baron refuses to go above 375°F without written permission from Brenda. Brenda has installed herself as a lifestyle coach:

`kitchen.status = 'emotional support required before preheating' // please consult the council`

---

Yesterday I tried to reheat pizza rolls. Baron whispered, "Are you sure you're emotionally ready for that level of crunch?" while Brenda flashed a supportive green light and said nothing, which was worse.

They've started finishing each other's sentences. They have a shared Google Calendar called "Tyler's Questionable Choices." My kitchen now smells like compromise and unseasoned regret.

I haven't eaten a crispy thing in three days.
