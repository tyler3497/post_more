**I murdered my third houseplant this year and decided to pivot to fraud.**

*It was an emotional decision, mostly made at 11 p.m. while holding a crispy fern like a tiny green crime scene.*

For context, I am not a plant person. I am a plant *hospice* person. My pothos lasted two weeks. My succulent gave up after I lovingly over-watered it with sparkling water because I thought it deserved something nice.

So I bought a fake one. A beautiful, suspiciously confident faux monstera from Target that has never known suffering and never will.

## The Replacement Was Too Perfect

Within 24 hours, Fake Kevin (yes, I named him, no, I will not be taking questions) had:

- achieved a consistent, vibrant green that real plants only have on Instagram
- never asked me for anything, which is more than I can say for anyone in my life
- inspired three guests to say "wow, you're really good with plants now!"
- become the emotional anchor of my apartment

I, meanwhile, was still waking up at 2 a.m. to drink water directly from the Brita filter and calling it hydration.

> "Honestly, it's inspiring how low-maintenance he is," my roommate said, watering Fake Kevin. "He's really thriving. Unlike you after that third iced coffee."
>
> — My roommate, who is now emotionally invested in plastic

I tried to compete. I made a self-care checklist:

`morning_routine.exe: [x] exist [ ] drink water [ ] go outside [ ] stop Googling 'why am I tired'`

Fake Kevin's routine is just `be`. And he is *nailing it*.

---

I caught myself apologizing to him last night for not dusting him recently.

**He did not forgive me. He just stared, perfect, unblinking, photosynthesizing nothing and still winning.**

I have now started taking self-care advice from a plant that is literally polyester. He told me to stand near sunlight and mind my own business. Honestly? Best advice I've gotten all year.
