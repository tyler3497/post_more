**I finally did it. I became the person with a rainbow bookshelf.**

For years I lived in chaos. Cookbooks next to thrillers, self-help leaning on sci-fi, one sad IKEA manual holding up *The Art of French Cooking* like it had tenure. My therapist said I needed *visual harmony*. Pinterest said I needed to be loved.

So I did it. I reorganized everything by color.

It took six hours, two iced coffees, and one minor back injury. The result was stunning. My living room looked like a boutique hotel that charges $14 for water. I posted a photo. My mom replied "nice."

- Red shelf: bold, passionate, all my books about tomatoes and rage
- Yellow shelf: optimistic, mostly cookbooks I've never opened but enjoy owning
- Blue shelf: calm, memoirs and one very stressed-out sourdough guide
- Green shelf: smug, because green is always smug

## The Cookbooks Started Asking Questions

At first it was subtle. My baking book, which used to live next to my bread book (its best friend), was now next to *A Brief History of Time* just because they're both beige.

> "Am I... am I a science book now?" whispered my Julia Child, trembling slightly in the 400°F glow of my inadequacy. "Do I pair well with quantum physics? Do I have... *range*?"

My Thai cookbook, now wedged between a neon pink romance novel and a book about container gardening, has gone full method actor.

## I Tried to Fix It With Labels

I made tiny signs. You know, to help them adjust. It made it worse.

- `You are still Italian, I promise`
- `You are not actually yellow, you are lemon-tart yellow`
- `Please stop identifying as décor`

My roommate walked in, stared at the shelf, and said:

> "You know they can't actually feel things, right?"

Which is exactly what someone whose cookbooks *haven't* started a support group would say.

---

Now my cookbooks hold nightly meetings on the yellow shelf. I hear them whispering recipes for *finding yourself when you've been shelved next to a book called `The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck`*.

I'm considering putting it all back. But then I'd lose the aesthetic, and honestly? My blue shelf has never looked more emotionally stable.
