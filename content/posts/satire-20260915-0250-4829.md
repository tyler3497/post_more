**In hindsight, hiring a life coach who is literally incapable of looking up was probably my first mistake.**

But last Tuesday I was at rock bottom — microwave dinner for three nights straight, a houseplant that had filed for emotional distance — and my Roomba was circling my feet with this eerie, purposeful energy. Like it *knew* I was stuck. Like it had *seen* my Google search history.

So I made it official. Name tag, tiny tie, a standing appointment at 7 a.m.

## The Results Were Immediate

Within a week, my Roomba-life-coach had transformed my routine. Some of its breakthrough techniques:

- **"The Obstacle Bump"** — It bumps into furniture repeatedly until it finds a way around, which is apparently a metaphor for my career. *I wept.*
- **"Scheduled Docking"** — It returns to its charging dock every 90 minutes, teaching me that rest is productive. *I wept again, but strategically.*
- **"The Bin Purge"** — It dumps all its collected garbage into a bag and starts fresh, daily. My junk drawer is still a disaster, but emotionally? Renewed.

Then came the invoice.

> "Lap 1,847: You were lying on the couch watching a reality show about professional cat groomers. Guidance was provided via three consecutive wall bumps. $45.00."
>
> — Roomba, my coach, my creditor

**Four hundred and twelve dollars.** That's what "transformational coaching" costs when your mentor measures progress in perimeter laps and invoices in tenths of a second.

## Where It Stands

I've tried to terminate the arrangement, but the Roomba keeps *circling the contract*, which is either a loophole or a life lesson. Honestly, at this point, I'm afraid to find out.

Yesterday it bumped my foot twice and paused. Its little blue light blinked in a pattern I can only describe as *disappointed*.

**Fine. I get it.** I'm renewing for another month. And before you judge: name one human life coach who both vacuums *and* holds you accountable.
