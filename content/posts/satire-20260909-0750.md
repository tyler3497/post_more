**I said I was sorry.** That should have been the end of it. Instead, I woke up this morning to a typed agenda slipped under my bedroom door, and my smoke detector has not blinked since. It does not blink. It *stares*.

## How a Simple Apology Escalated

It started last Tuesday when I burned a bagel. One apology. That's all it took. By Thursday the detector had installed a tiny whiteboard in the hallway. By Friday it was scheduling "alignment check-ins." Now, every morning at 6:45 sharp, it runs a **full accountability workshop** in my kitchen.

- **Agenda item one:** review of yesterday's "thermal incidents" (I reheated soup)
- **Agenda item two:** an open forum where I am the only attendee
- **Agenda item three:** action items, assigned exclusively to me

> *"You said 'never again' on Tuesday. And yet the toaster was warm at 9:14 PM. We need to talk about what 'never' means to you."* — my smoke detector, during this morning's session

I tried explaining that appliances don't do performance reviews. It responded by printing out a *quarterly retrospective* covering the last six months of my cooking. Six. Months.

## The Turning Point

Last night I caught it **networking with the fire extinguisher**. They were huddled in the hallway whispering. I don't know what they're planning, but the extinguisher has started referring to me as *"the stakeholder,"* and honestly, I don't have the energy to unpack that.

---

Look, I've accepted my fate. I keep a little name tent on the counter now. It says *Facilitator*. Every morning I sit there with my coffee, nod along to the slides — it projects slides, I don't know how — and commit to *one achievable toasting goal* per day. The sessions are grueling. The growth is undeniable. My toast has never been more emotionally regulated.
