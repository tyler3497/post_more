**It started as a routine cleaning.** It ended as the most vulnerable 45 minutes of my adult life, and I never once managed to close my mouth.

I don't know what came over me. Maybe it was the reclining chair, which has the same energy as a therapist's couch except someone is actively inside your skull. Maybe it was the nitrous-adjacent haze of peppermint fluoride. Either way, mid-polish I tried to say *"while you're in there"* — which, through a mouth full of instruments, came out as *"waiw ur innare"* — and my dentist, a professional with seventeen years of experience and apparently the emotional range of a brick, simply nodded and said:

> "Go ahead."

## The Evidence I Presented

With a mirror on a stick and a suction tube doing half the talking, I laid out my case:

- I have owned a sourdough starter named Doughbi-Wan for 14 months. It has produced exactly one (1) edible loaf and an estimated 400 dishes.
- I bought a bidet attachment in 2023 and have told the story of it at *three* dinner parties. Three.
- My retirement plan is, and I quote, *"vibes and a strong belief in compound interest,"* which I explained while spitting into a little paper cup like a man at a very specific casino.

## The Professional Assessment

After a long pause — the kind of pause usually reserved for telling someone their dog did *not*, in fact, survive the surgery — my dentist said:

> "Your molars are fine. Your *choices*, however, have significant decay."

He then flossed me. *Flossed me.* An act so intimate I have since added him to my emergency contacts, above my brother.

## The Aftermath

He charged me $180 for the cleaning and, I am not exaggerating, slid a laminated card across the counter that read: **"MY OFFICE DOES NOT PROVIDE EXISTENTIAL CARE."** On the back, in handwriting that was definitely not his receptionist's, someone had written *"but the fluoride helps with regret."*
