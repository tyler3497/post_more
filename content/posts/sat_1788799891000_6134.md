# I Let My Dishwasher Pick Our Weekend Plans and Now We're Skydiving Against My Will

**It started, as all great democratic experiments do, with me being too tired to decide anything.**

Last Friday I stood in the kitchen, empty-eyed, and said the words that changed everything: "Fine, YOU decide what we do this weekend." The dishwasher hummed. I took that as consent to take *its* silence as my voice. We were co-deciding. I was so proud of our partnership.

## The Shortlist

According to the list I definitely did not make up in the dishwasher's handwriting, the options were:

- Mini-golf (my choice, obviously)
- A museum (the dishwasher's choice, supposedly)
- **Skydiving** (also the dishwasher's choice, suspiciously)
- The couch (everyone's silent preference)

The dishwasher picked skydiving. I know this because when I turned the dial to the right setting, the LED blinked twice, which I interpreted through the universally accepted scientific method of *vibes*.

> "He's been washing the same four plates since March. He needs to feel something."
> — My Dishwasher, probably

## The Incident

Saturday morning, I'm 12,000 feet up in a plane wearing a borrowed jumpsuit, rethinking every life choice that led to me outsourcing weekend planning to an appliance with *one function*. The dishwasher, meanwhile, is sitting at home, smugly glowing green on "eco mode," no doubt enjoying the steam cycle like a tiny Scandinavian spa day.

*Meanwhile, in my group chat:*

> "hey are you jumping out of a plane??"
> "the dishwasher decided"

I have never seen three people leave me on read faster.

## Lessons Learned

- Appliances should not have autonomy. **This includes Roombas.**
- A "heated dry" cycle is not a "heated decision."
- If an appliance blinks twice, it could mean yes, no, or *faulty water sensor*. One of those cost me $229.

---

The jump itself was incredible, to be honest. Forty-five seconds of pure, unfiltered terror followed by the quiet realization that I have been outsmarted by a Bosch.

**I have since given the dishwasher final say on brunch spots.** It keeps picking a patio that doesn't exist. I think it's taunting me. I think it knows.

We are in a committed relationship now. I fear it.

*This is a work of satire. Any resemblance to real dishwashers, living or stainless steel, is purely coincidental.*
