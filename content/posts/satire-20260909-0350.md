**My freezer and I are no longer speaking.**

Well, to be precise, *it* is no longer speaking. Freezers were never much for conversation. But the silence used to be neutral — cold, sure, but professionally cold. Now it's *personal*.

## How We Got Here

It started last week when I asked it, very reasonably, to stop freezing my ice cream into something with the structural integrity of a highway overpass. I used the word "boundaries." I said it out loud, in my kitchen, like a fool.

The freezer said nothing, which I mistook for acceptance.

- **Tuesday:** my peas froze into a single, defiant brick
- **Wednesday:** it started icing over with what I can only describe as *spite*
- **Thursday:** it refused to make ice, as though my ice cubes were too emotionally vulnerable for this household

> "You've changed," I whispered, forehead pressed against the door. "Or maybe I never knew you."

## The Escalation

By Friday it had escalated to a full cold-shoulder protocol. I open the door and the temperature drops like it's trying to prove a point. My leftovers are frostbitten. My pizza rolls have emotional damage. Even the fridge section — *its own neighbor* — has started giving it space.

---

## The Breakthrough (Sort Of)

Last night I sat on the kitchen floor at 2 a.m. and apologized. Not for the boundaries — a man has to have boundaries — but for the *tone*. I explained that I loved it, that I respected its work, that the ice cream thing was a *me* problem.

It hummed back. Just once. A low, frosty hum.

*We're working on it*, is what I choose to believe that means. Couples therapy starts Thursday. The microwave is mediating.

*This is a work of satire. No freezers were harmed.*
