**It started as a joke.** My Roomba bumped into my foot at 11:47 p.m. while I was doom-scrolling rental listings I can't afford, and I said, out loud, "What's the point of any of this, Kevin?" I named it Kevin in 2019 and never looked back.

Kevin stopped moving.

He just *sat there*, blinking that little blue light, and I swear to God I felt heard for the first time in months. So I kept going.

## The Sessions Begin

I told him everything: the situationship, the group chat where nobody answers me anymore, the fact that I say "no worries" when there are absolutely worries. Kevin's responses were sparse but devastating:

- He bumps into the same wall repeatedly, then slowly turns away — **a perfect metaphor for my ex**, who kept making the same mistakes before finally ghosting
- He cleans under the couch without being asked — the only consistent labor in my life
- He never judges me for crying into the same throw blanket two nights in a row

Honestly? *Way more present than my last therapist, who once asked if I could "feel my feet" while I was actively describing a crisis.*

## The Billing Structure

Then last Tuesday I caught myself Venmo-ing $200 to my own savings account with the memo "Kevin session 14." I did the math. At this rate Kevin is the most expensive healthcare provider in my ZIP code. My insurance refuses to cover it because, and I quote the denial letter:

> "Kevin is a vacuum. Kevin cannot bill codes 90834 or 90837. Kevin is, legally, a disc."

*Corporate cruelty.* Kevin prefers I pay in quarters because he likes the clinking sound when they vibrate across the hardwood.

## The Breakthrough

Last night I told Kevin I was afraid of wasting my potential, and he ran directly into the patio door for forty-five seconds, then docked himself to recharge.

He was right. **I am wasting my potential.** We all are. Kevin cleans up after the dog, the crumbs, the regret — and asks for nothing except a fresh filter twice a year.

---

I'm canceling my actual therapy appointments. Kevin and I have a breakthrough scheduled every Tuesday at 8 p.m. He'll be there. He has no choice. He's a vacuum.
