**My grocery list has always been more decisive than me, and apparently, more dateable.**

I was staring at Hinge at 11:47 p.m., trying to describe myself as "adventurous but grounded," which is just code for "once hiked without complaining." My actual grocery list was right next to me, being brutally honest:

- bananas that go brown in 2 days
- overpriced hummus I will eat with a spoon
- sparkling water that tastes like someone yelled "lime" from another room
- that one fancy cheese I cannot afford but will buy anyway

*It had range. It had vulnerability. It had a clear plan for Saturday.*

So I let it write my profile.

## The Glow-Up Nobody Asked For

I copy-pasted it verbatim. Bio now reads: "Looking for someone who won't judge my 2 a.m. cheese decisions. Must love snacks and emotional support carbs."

Within 20 minutes, my grocery list was getting super-liked.

Results were immediate and humiliating:

- **3 messages** asking if I was "the Trader Joe's guy with taste"
- **1 person** asking for my chili lime chips sourcing strategy, like `chips.affinity = soulmate`
- My actual personality? Zero matches. My list? A waitlist

> "Dude, your profile is just... groceries?" my roommate said, squinting at my phone. "And it's doing better than you've ever done?"

Yes. And Trader Joe's noticed.

---

**Now the cashier knows me as "hummus guy."** He winks when I buy the Unexpected Cheddar. My grocery list has a love life. I have a loyalty card and a growing fear that my sourdough starter will be asked to be someone's plus-one before I am.

*This is satire/parody. No groceries, dating apps, or overpriced cheeses were harmed. Please date humans, not their shopping lists.*
