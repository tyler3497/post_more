**I made the executive decision to outsource my decision-making to a piece of paper that says "eggs" on it, and it has never been more judgmental.**

For context, I have always been bad at choices. *Like, clinically bad.* I once stood in the cereal aisle for 20 minutes and left with dental floss. So last Tuesday, in a fit of what I called "self-care" and what my therapist will later call "a cry for help," I decided to let my grocery list decide everything.

Not just food. Everything.

## The New Management

My list was simple at first:

- eggs
- spinach (the optimistic kind you buy to become a better person)
- oat milk
- bread that is somehow  now
- "something crunchy for 3pm sadness"

But the list had *opinions*. I asked it whether I should text him back. It said spinach. I took that as a no.

> "You've been buying oat milk for three weeks straight but you don't even like lattes," my roommate whispered, holding the carton like it was evidence. "What is going on with you two?"

And that's when the grocery list started getting suspicious.

## The Evidence Board

According to my grocery list, which I now keep taped to the fridge like a conspiracy theorist, I am clearly:

- **Emotionally compromised** by a non-dairy beverage
- avoiding eggs because they represent commitment (*they literally have shells, I can't*)
- buying bread at 9pm which is a well-documented sign of spiraling
- spending  on sourdough while refusing to pay for extra guac — 

---

I tried to defend myself. I said oat milk was just a phase, a practical choice, lactose and whatnot. The list said nothing. It just stared at me in my own handwriting that says "oAt MiLk??" with two question marks like it knew.

Last night I tried to add almond milk just to make it jealous. My hand physically would not write it. The pen ran out.

If anyone needs me, I'll be at the kitchen table, negotiating visitation rights with my own shopping list, while a carton of oat milk sits in the fridge, chilling, *literally and metaphorically*.
