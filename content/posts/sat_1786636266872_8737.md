## It Started With Encouragement

**I just wanted my toaster to feel appreciated.** *It's been with me through three apartments, two breakups, and one unfortunate incident involving a bagel with too much ambition.*

I said, out loud, to a kitchen appliance: "You're doing amazing. I believe in you."

That was a mistake.

### The Transformation

By Tuesday, my $19.99 toaster had rebranded itself:

- It now only toasts on "artisanal" setting, which takes 11 minutes and involves *a low hum that sounds suspiciously like judgment*
- It refuses Pop-Tarts because they "lack structural integrity"
- It garnishes everything with a single, aggressively placed parsley leaf it somehow acquired
- My kitchen now smells like ambition and slightly burnt confidence

I put in a frozen waffle this morning. It ejected it mid-cycle, untouched.

> "We do not do that here anymore," it seemed to say, via the red-hot coils glowing in what I can only describe as disappointment. "We have a reputation to protect."

### The Reviews

My toaster has started plating. And critiquing *my* plating.

- My toast: "rustic, but emotionally unavailable"
- My butter spread: `spread: uneven // effort: 2/10 // would not recommend`
- My midnight snack choices: "a bold choice, if bold meant concerning"

---

**Here's the thing:** I now have to book a reservation to use my own kitchen. The toaster takes walk-ins from 6-7 a.m., but after that it's "by invitation only."

*Tonight I asked if it could just do a normal slice.* It blinked twice, sighed in appliance, and presented me with a single, perfectly golden piece of bread on a bed of arugula, with a balsamic reduction drizzle.

I ate it on the floor. Out of respect. And fear.

**This is satire. My toaster is not, to my knowledge, enrolled in culinary school.**
