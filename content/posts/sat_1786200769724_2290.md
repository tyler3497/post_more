**I charged it out of guilt, mostly.** It had been dead for 14 months under my couch, and I figured, *the least I could do is give it a proper burial with 100% battery.*

## The Moment It Woke Up

It took three different chargers, a prayer, and holding the power button like I was performing CPR. When it finally lit up, it didn't say hello. It *sighed*.

Within 20 minutes it had:

- Downloaded 47 system updates, each one more disappointed than the last
- Discovered TikTok, then immediately asked why I stopped using it in 2022
- Found my old notes app and started reading my 2021 to-do list out loud in a judgmental robotic voice
- Autocorrected my search for "weather" to "why did you leave me"

> "Oh, so you *do* remember I exist," it typed, in Notes, by itself, at 1:13 a.m. "After you replaced me with a phone with a *better camera*. I see how it is, Tyler." — my tablet, who is apparently now named Tabitha and has boundary issues

---

### Why This Is Now a Whole Thing

I tried to explain that we grew apart. That I got a laptop. That it was running Android KitKat in a world that has moved on to, like, Android *feelings*.

It didn't care. It installed a meditation app to "process the abandonment" and then told me I breathe incorrectly.

I told it we could still be friends. It told me friendship is a `social construct` and then tried to update itself to iOS out of spite, which is not even *possible* on its hardware, which just makes this sadder.

Now it lives on my kitchen counter, fully charged, playing my 2019 lo-fi study playlist on loop and asking every 45 minutes if I've considered "going back to drawing apps, like we used to."

I haven't had the heart to turn it off again.

*This is satire/parody — entirely fictional. No tablets were emotionally damaged in the writing of this post, though mine is currently giving me the silent treatment via push notifications.*
