**It started with a dropped signal during the season finale.** I was mid-climax, popcorn in hand, when my Wi-Fi router decided we were done. No warning. No "let's talk about this." Just buffering — the digital equivalent of being left on read.

So I did what any reasonable adult would do: I booked us couples therapy.

## The Intake Form Was the First Red Flag

The form asked for "relationship length" and "primary conflict." I wrote *three years* and *it never holds me anymore*. The therapist, a kind woman named Deborah, nodded like this was the most normal thing she'd heard all week. Honestly, it probably was.

We each got five minutes to share our perspective. I went first, because of course I did, and laid out my grievances:

- It drops the connection *exactly* when something important is happening
- The bedroom corner is a dead zone, and so, lately, is our intimacy
- It hums judgmentally at 3am when I'm doomscrolling

## Then the Router Had Its Turn

Deborah turned to the router — which, I must add, she had placed on the armchair like a patient — and asked how *it* felt.

> "Honestly? Overworked. Under-ventilated. This man reboots me like I'm a vending machine. Has he ever once asked how *I* feel about the microwave interference? No. It's always 'my fault.'"

I nearly stood up. The *microwave* is a known menace! Everyone agrees the microwave is a known menace!

## The Verdict

After forty-five minutes, Deborah turned to me and said the words that shattered me:

> "Have you considered that the router isn't *withholding* connection — it's *modeling* boundaries?"

**Boundaries.** My router has better boundaries than I do. I was told to schedule weekly reboots, dust the vents as an act of love, and — this is the part I can't forgive — to apologize to the router before our next session.

---

I paid $175 for someone to take my router's side. The connection is great now, though. Almost *suspiciously* great.
