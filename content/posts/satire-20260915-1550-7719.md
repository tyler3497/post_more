**I made the mistake of asking a floor covering for wisdom, and now my entire social life depends on the approval of six square feet of coconut fiber.**

It started innocently. I was leaving for a job interview, nerves completely shot, and I jokingly asked my doormat, "Hey buddy, any life advice?" *Classic me, outsourcing big decisions to household objects.*

The mat said nothing. Obviously. But something shifted. When I came home that evening, I swear it looked... disappointed.

## The Assessment Criteria

Since that day, my doormat has developed what I can only describe as a rigorous screening protocol for every human being who approaches my door:

- **Shoe cleanliness** — if your soles aren't spotless, you are personally insulting it
- **Wipe duration** — under four seconds reads as "disrespectful," over eight is "suspicious"
- **Posture** — slouchers get the full glare; this mat has *opinions* about spinal alignment

> "It doesn't hate you," I told my sister, who was visibly sweating at the threshold. "It just... finds you unconvincing."

## The Consequences

My delivery driver now leaves packages at the curb. He claims the mat "radiated disappointment" at him, which is physically impossible, and yet he absolutely refuses to come back. My landlord tried to collect rent last Tuesday and retreated mid-sentence after a particularly withering look. I hosted a dinner party last week; three guests texted afterward that they'd rather eat standing in the parking lot.

---

I considered replacing it. I stood in the store aisle, staring at a wall of cheerful "WELCOME" mats, and felt a pang of guilt so sharp I had to sit down on a display bench. *This* mat chose me. We've been through mud, snow, and one deeply regrettable incident involving a garden sprinkler.

So I've adapted. I now brief every guest in advance: "Wipe for six seconds. Stand tall. Do not apologize to it — it can smell fear." My doormat has become the bouncer of my own home, and honestly? My doorstep has never been cleaner.

**SATIRE — this is a work of parody and fiction; no doormats were consulted in the writing of this essay.**
