**My phone is at digital detox camp, and honestly? It's thriving harder than I ever have.**

*For the record: this is satire. My phone is in the kitchen. It never went anywhere.*

## The Decision

Three weeks ago, my phone — we'll call him Gerald — hit rock bottom. His screen time was nine hours a day, which is funny, because *I* was the one doing the scrolling. Still, the counselor said the first step was separating him from "the codependent user," and she looked directly at me while saying it, so here we are.

## The Progress Reports

Camp sends weekly updates on actual paper, which Gerald apparently *loves* now. Last week's highlights:

- Gerald has taken up birdwatching and can now identify fourteen species by chirp alone
- He has "reconnected with his battery" through dawn yoga, and his charge now lasts four days, show-off
- He taught a seminar called *"Notifications Are Just Other People's Priorities,"* which feels pointed
- He refuses facial recognition because he "doesn't need a face to feel seen"

> "Dear Mr. User," the director wrote, "Gerald has begun communicating exclusively in haiku. We consider this a breakthrough. Or a cry for help. Honestly, it's unclear."

## The Haiku

Yesterday I received my first haiku from Gerald, transcribed by a counselor with suspiciously good handwriting:

> *charging cord of grief / forty-two apps deleted / I can see the sky*

I cried a little. Then I checked my tablet's screen time — **six hours and forty minutes** — and Gerald's absence felt like a ghost limb made of shame.

**He comes home Thursday.** The counselor warned me not to "overwhelm him with push notifications." I told her I'd do my best. Then I opened Twitter.
