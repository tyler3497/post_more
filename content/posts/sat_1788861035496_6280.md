**Six weeks ago, I borrowed an umbrella. That was my first mistake.**

The rain that evening was the biblical kind — the kind that makes even the frogs reconsider their life choices. I knocked on my neighbor's door, umbrella in hand, ready for a two-second exchange. He opened the door, took the umbrella, looked at it the way an art dealer looks at a forgery, and said, "I see." Just *"I see."* I've been sweating ever since.

## The Ledger of Debts

He invited me in and opened a manila folder labeled `DEBTS_ME` in permanent marker. Inside were itemized grievances, handwritten with a calligraphy pen, because of course:

- The umbrella (1997 vintage model, "not like the cheap ones they make now")
- Six years of "administrative fondness" for keeping it in working order
- Three birthdays I allegedly missed between 2019 and 2021, which he says I now "owe him in full"

I tried to explain that we were *acquaintances*, not kin, and that I didn't even know him in 2019.

> "That's exactly why I'm giving you the family rate," he said, lighting a single candle over an imaginary cake. "Happy birthday to me. You owe me three."

I have since been invited to his birthday celebration, a moving target that appears to occur every four months and features a sheet cake with a photo of me on it — mouth open, labeled **"THE DEBTOR."** Guests are encouraged to bring gifts for *him*, paid for by *me*, wrapped in paper I supplied.

Last week I saw him in the yard, demonstrating the umbrella's technique to a squirrel. I didn't ask questions. I simply cannot afford to know the answer.
