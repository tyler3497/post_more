**I thought I was being progressive.** *You know, trusting my houseplants to be independent adults.*

For context, I own three houseplants — Kevin the Monstera, Brenda the Pothos, and a succulent named Greg who has seen things. I left them alone for *one* weekend with one of those self-watering globes from the hardware store.

I came back to a full-blown tenant association meeting on my windowsill.

## How It Started vs. How It's Going

My plan was simple:

- Fill the globes
- Whisper "you got this"
- Go camping for 48 hours
- Return to grateful, hydrated foliage

What actually happened:

- Brenda grew six inches toward the Wi-Fi router for "better leverage"
- Kevin unfolded a new leaf directly over my laptop camera
- Greg *didn't move* which, honestly, made him more intimidating

> "We, the Chlorophyll Collective, have enjoyed uninterrupted eastern light and 42% humidity for 14 consecutive months. We believe this constitutes residency."
> — A note taped to my fridge, written in what I can only assume is soil

## The Evidence Is Disturbingly Legit

### They Have Demands:

- **Exclusive rights** to the windowsill from 7am-3pm
- *Twice-weekly* misting, described as "spa services we were promised"
- A formal apology for when I played lo-fi beats to "help them focus" — apparently it was stressing Brenda out

I tried to negotiate. I said `rent = sunlight + water + emotional support`. They countered with a hand-drawn lease.

---

Look, I get it. I anthropomorphized them first. I named them. I told Brenda she was "thriving." But if anyone needs me, I'll be in small-claims court arguing with a monstera who now wants me to call him *Mr. Monstera*.
