**I just wanted to clear the air.** That's it. A simple, adult apology to my houseplants for the Great Neglect of August, during which I remembered to water them roughly as often as I remember to stretch. I expected quiet forgiveness. Maybe a perked leaf. What I got was a movement.

## The Demands Arrived at Dawn

Taped to the windowsill in what I can only describe as *aggressively passive* handwriting (my fern has excellent penmanship), the plants had issued a list of non-negotiable demands:

- Weekly group therapy sessions, Thursdays at dusk, *attendance mandatory*
- A "hydration schedule with accountability," not vibes
- One (1) misting bottle upgrade, "the cheap one is humiliating"
- A formal apology *in writing* from my vacation in July, "they know about the beach"

## I Did Not Expect Testimony

I showed up to the first session. Eleven plants in a circle. The spider plant went first and spoke for forty-five minutes about **"the drought of the 9th,"** and honestly? I teared up a little. Then the succulent said something that broke me:

> "You told the neighbor to watch us. The neighbor who uses a *spray bottle* like it's seasoning. Do you know what that feels like? To be *seasoned*?"

Nobody clapped. It didn't feel like a clapping moment.

---

The fern has appointed itself moderator and assigned me homework: a daily gratitude journal, one entry per plant, minimum two sentences. *"The pothos accepts bullet points. The orchid does not."*

Last night I caught myself narrating my intentions before touching the watering can. *"I'm going to water you now, Brenda, at a respectful pace, and I honor your experience."* Brenda is a fiddle-leaf fig. Brenda has never once responded.

But this morning, the smallest leaf on the fern — the one I thought was dead — had uncurled approximately one millimeter. Either it heard me, or it's mocking me. *In therapy, we call that progress.*

*This is a work of satire. No plants were harmed, though several were deeply disappointed in me.*
