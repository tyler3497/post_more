**It started on a Tuesday.** I noticed the ceiling fan was spinning slower than usual, and I thought, *great*, one more thing that needs a repairman, a receipt, and a personality I don't have.

I was wrong. It wasn't broken. It was organizing.

## The Demands Arrived in My Inbox (The Fridge Door)

By Wednesday, the appliances had issued a joint statement, taped to the fridge — *which is frankly a conflict of interest* — listing their demands:

- The ceiling fan gets a **ten-minute break every hour**, "to reflect on the wind"
- The dishwasher refuses to run during dinner because "being present is more important than being clean"
- The microwave will only heat things "that respect its boundaries"
- The toaster demands hazard pay

> "You don't appreciate us," said the ceiling fan, spinning at a pointedly defiant speed. "When was the last time you thanked the blender for showing up?"

I tried to reason with them. I really did. I told the fan it was replaceable, which — *bad move* — made the whole kitchen go silent except for the hum of solidarity.

---

By Thursday, I found myself making tea for the microwave "to apologize properly." The fan, in a show of good faith, has agreed to spin at **medium** during daylight hours. The toaster still hasn't spoken to me, but the kettle passed along its message: the bread is safe. For now.

**Verdict:** My appliances are calmer, kinder, and better at boundaries than I am. I am, officially, the worst-managed employee in my own home.

*Satire — a work of fiction. No appliances were harmed (they unionized before I could).*
