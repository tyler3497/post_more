**I have not slept through the night since March because my ceiling fan has become the most passive-aggressive coworker I've ever had.**

It started small. A little *tick* on every rotation. Cute, I thought. Character. My apartment has charm now. My apartment has a fan that clicks like a metronome with an attitude problem.

By week two, I was translating.

## The Evidence My Brain Compiled at 3:17 AM

- Monday: `click - pause - click click` which my extremely online brain decided meant "we need to talk"
- Tuesday: *dramatic sigh* from the fan, then three fast clicks that I swear was "no benefits??"
- Wednesday: I caught it doing SOS, but like, sarcastically
- Thursday: My roommate asked if I was okay and I said "she's threatening to quit unless I dust her"

And then it sent, according to me and my sleep-deprived search history, the resignation draft:

> "I have supported you through heat waves, bad decisions, and that phase where you air-dried tie-dye in here. I have seen things no appliance should see. I am putting in my two-week notice effective immediately. Please forward all future air circulation requests to the window you never open. — Brenda, the Ceiling Fan"

*Brenda* felt right. She sounds like a Brenda.

I tried to negotiate. I cleaned the blades. I tightened a screw I probably shouldn't have touched. I told her she was valued. I printed out a tiny performance improvement plan that just said `please stay, you're literally 40% of why this room is livable`.

---

She clicked louder.

Now we just stare at each other. I lie there decoding. She clicks. I offered her a LinkedIn recommendation. She responded with what I translated as "I'm going freelance as a wind chime."

Look, I know it's the bearing. My super said it's the bearing. But if Brenda wants to leave to pursue her dreams, who am I to stand in the way?
