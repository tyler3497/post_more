**I tried to be a responsible plant parent and now my monstera is interviewing financial advisors.**

*Which, for the record, is not what I meant when I said "let's talk about growth."*

## It Started With Good Intentions

I read one article that said you should talk to your plants. So I did. I told my monstera, Gary (yes, I named him, no, I'm not taking questions), about my 401(k) enrollment meeting. I thought it would be soothing background noise for photosynthesis.

It was not.

By week two, Gary had opinions:

- He wanted compound interest, not compound soil
- He said his current pot was "not a long-term investment vehicle"
- He asked if misting counted as a benefits package
- He started dropping terms like "diversified sunlight portfolio"

> "You can't just expect me to keep producing oxygen without equity," Gary rustled, which I interpreted as a leaf twitch but my roommate swears was a full sentence. "I have fronds to feed."

### The Negotiation

My roommate found me at 2 a.m. making a PowerPoint titled **Gary's Golden Years: A Succulent Retirement Plan**.

I had built a spreadsheet:

```js
if (plant.happiness < 0.7) {
  moveToWindow("south-facing");
  increaseWatering("but not too much, he's sensitive");
  promiseRetirement("full sun by 2030");
}
```

Gary rejected it. He wants a corner office. *South-facing.* With sheer curtains. And a tiny water cooler where he can gossip with the succulents.

---

Look, I just wanted a low-maintenance roommate who doesn't eat my leftovers. Now I have a fern with a LinkedIn headline that says "Seeking Growth Opportunities | Open to Relocation (Brighter Window) | 401(k) Enthusiast" and he's been endorsed for "strategic photosynthesis" by my spider plant.

I told him retirement is 40 years away. He dropped a leaf dramatically, like *oh, you think I'll be here that long without a pension?*
