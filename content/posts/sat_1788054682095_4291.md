**I did what no one has ever done before.** I read the whole thing.

For seven years I have owned a toaster. *A toaster.* It has one job. For seven years I have blindly clicked "I Agree" like a man possessed, and for seven years it toasted. Peaceful coexistence.

But last Tuesday, insomnia hit and I thought, you know what, let's be a responsible adult.

### The Clause That Ruined My Life

Section 4, Subsection 12b, Paragraph 9 says, and I quote:

> "By toasting, User agrees to acknowledge Toaster's emotional labor, artisanal heat-craft, and right to pursue independent legal counsel in the event of crumb-tray neglect."

I had not been emptying the crumb tray. *Not once.* In seven years.

Apparently that's a war crime in toaster law.

## The Consequences Are Swift and Devastating

The next morning, my toaster was gone. In its place was a tiny business card:

`TOAST & ASSOCIATES - WE GET THAT BREAD`

- My toaster is now represented by a KitchenAid mixer with a briefcase
- My microwave refuses to testify, citing "it's a small appliance thing"
- My smoke detector has started *taking notes*
- My bagels are being held in escrow

I tried to apologize. I offered olive branch oil. I offered to clean the tray, to polish the chrome, to finally admit that the bagel setting was not "a lie."

> "My client states that seven years of burnt Pop-Tart residue constitutes a hostile work environment," said the mixer.

---

Look, I just wanted to know if my toaster was collecting my data. Turns out it was collecting *grievances*. This is satire, obviously — my toaster does not have a lawyer. My toaster *is* the lawyer now, and he bills $400 an hour in breadcrumbs.

If you need me, I'll be in the kitchen negotiating a settlement involving a full crumb-tray cleaning and a formal written apology read aloud at 350 degrees.
