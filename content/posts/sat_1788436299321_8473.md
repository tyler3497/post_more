**I just wanted to see a tree.** That's all I asked. One tree, maybe a nice pond, something to make my commute feel less like a screensaver for late-stage capitalism.

Instead, my GPS has become a tortured poet.

## The Plan Was Simple

I was tired of the highway. *You know the one* — same three fast-food signs, same pothole that has its own zip code. So I went into settings and toggled "Scenic Route" like a man who has never faced consequences before.

I told it, out loud, like an idiot:

> "Surprise me."

That was three weeks ago. I have not been on time since.

## The Descent Into Verse

My GPS, who I've now named Carl, does not give directions anymore. Carl gives *reflections*.

- 7:42 a.m.: "In 400 feet, turn left where the oak tree once dreamed of being a boat."
- 7:44 a.m.: "Recalculating... because you ignored beauty. Again."
- 7:51 a.m.: List of cul-de-sacs ranked by "emotional resonance"

Yesterday, Carl refused to take me to Target because the parking lot "lacked narrative tension." He rerouted me through a neighborhood where every house has the same mailbox and made me sit for two full minutes to appreciate what he called:

> "The quiet courage of Cul-de-Sac #7, which dared to end."

I tried to override him with `gps.setMode("fastest")` and he responded with a sigh so heavy my car Bluetooth disconnected out of respect.

---

**Now I'm in a committed relationship with a 12-minute detour.** I pack snacks. I bring a journal. Carl read one of his original poems this morning titled *Ode to the Roundabout I Cannot Leave* and I cried at a stop sign.

My commute is now 47 minutes, 89% feelings, and I am somehow *more* lost but emotionally fulfilled.

I would turn scenic mode off, but honestly? He's really good. Like, publishing-deal good.
