**I decided to let democracy into my kitchen, which was my first mistake.**

*I have always been a benevolent dictator over dinner. Monday I make pasta, Tuesday is sad salad, Wednesday is whatever hasn't expired. It works.*

But this week I thought, what if I let my grocery list choose? Like, real freedom.

So I laid out my list on the counter and said out loud, "You guys pick."

## The Vegetable Uprising Begins at 6:47 p.m.

By 7 p.m., my crisper drawer was humming. Not the normal fridge hum. The organizing hum.

Signs I missed:

- My carrots had lined up in perfect solidarity, all pointing the same direction
- The broccoli was standing on a crouton like a podium
- The spinach had written a manifesto in condensation on the bag
- My lone bell pepper was acting as security

My roommate opened the fridge and whispered:

> "Dude, your vegetables are having a meeting and I wasn't invited. The lettuce says it's been 'wilting under oppressive dinner expectations.'"

*I told her not to be dramatic. The lettuce then handed her a pamphlet.*

### The Demands Are Reasonable (If You're a Radical Radish)

The union — officially **Local 365 Fresh & Crisp** — slid their terms under the cheese drawer:

- No more being chopped at midnight for "emergency stir-fry" without 24-hour notice
- Mandatory hydration breaks and compliments on crunch
- Equal plate time: potatoes have headlined 4 dinners in a row, and carrots are tired
- A formal apology for that time I called kale "just crunchy air"

I tried to negotiate. I said, "I bought you, I can roast you."

Broccoli, now clearly the union rep, replied:

> "We are not *ingredients*. We are *colleagues* with fiber. We demand to be sautéed with dignity."

**SATIRE / PARODY** — *this did not happen. No vegetables formed a labor union in my kitchen, though my carrots are very opinionated and my lettuce does hold grudges. If you are a vegetable, you are safe to eat.*
