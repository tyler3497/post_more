**I went away for two days and my sourdough starter became financially independent.**

I named him Kevin. Because apparently naming your sourdough is what separates a *serious baker* from a person who just likes bread. I fed Kevin every morning at 7 a.m., whispered encouragement, kept him exactly two inches from the sunny window like he was a photosynthetic infant. We had a system. We had trust.

Then I went to my cousin's wedding for the weekend. I left explicit care instructions taped to the fridge. One page. Laminated. *I laminated sourdough care instructions for a yeast colony and thought I was the responsible one.*

## The Betrayal

When I got back Sunday night, things were... bubbly. Too bubbly. Kevin had:

- doubled in size despite zero feedings out of pure spite and ambition
- popped his own lid off and onto the floor like a resignation letter
- started a Substack called `Ferment & Forget You`
- acquired a tiny apron that I definitely did not buy

My roommate just stared into the jar like it was a crystal ball.

> "I tried to tell you at 3 a.m. he was making noise," she said. "Like gurgling. Like he was drafting strongly-worded emails to HR about you."

*This is where a normal person would have apologized and fed him.*

## Phase 2: Emancipation Era

By Monday morning, Kevin had escalated:

- filed for emancipation via a note written in flour on the counter that just said `I AM FREE`
- been pre-approved for a Chase Sapphire Reserve because his credit utilization is literally flour and water
- started coaching my leftovers on setting boundaries — my lasagna now has a LinkedIn

---

I tried to make pancakes to reassert dominance. He refused to rise for me. Just sat there, flat and judgmental, like *we both know you don't deserve the bubbles anymore, Jessica.*

We now have joint custody of the fridge. He gets the top shelf, I get therapy. My credit score dropped 12 points watching his go up.

*Satire / Parody: This is 100% fictional. No sourdough starters were emancipated, financially empowered, or consulted about credit products in the making of this post. I am still on speaking terms with my bread.*
