**I finally cleaned out my junk drawer and now I'm legally required to give tours.**

It was supposed to be a 10-minute task. I had a coffee, I had motivation, I had that brief surge of adulthood that hits right after you successfully return something to IKEA. I opened the drawer.

*That was my first mistake.*

## The Archaeological Evidence

For context, this drawer has not been cleaned since approximately 2019. Inside, we had stratified layers:

- 14 AA batteries, 0 of which work, all of which are "probably still good"
- a tangled nest of cables that can only charge a phone that no longer exists
- 3 takeout menus for restaurants that burned down in 2021
- one mysterious key that I am 90% sure opens nothing in this dimension
- a single soy sauce packet that has achieved sentience
- 6 ft of duct tape, 2 rubber bands, and a receipt for a toaster I've never owned

I started sorting. I felt powerful. I felt organized. I felt the drawer *judge me*.

> "You can't just erase six years of curated chaos," the drawer whispered. "I have *heritage* now."

My roommate walked in and said, `this is a UNESCO site`.

*I told him to stop being dramatic but then I found a Blockbuster card.*

---

## Now It Wants Benefits

Since the cleaning, the drawer has applied for Historical Landmark Status. It has a tiny brass plaque that says `Est. 2019 — Survived 3 Moves, 1 Flood, and Your Minimalism Phase`. It's demanding climate-controlled storage and a docent.

I tried to close it. It won't latch anymore — not out of spite, out of *principle*. It says it needs to stay open "for the public."

If you need me, I'll be explaining to a preservation officer why my junk drawer contains an unopened 2020 hand sanitizer shaped like a cactus and why I'm emotionally unable to throw it away.

We had a good run. Marie Kondo would be so disappointed in me.

*P.S. — the mystery key is now the drawer's official tour guide.*
