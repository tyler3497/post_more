**It started innocently enough.** Last Tuesday, with the kind of optimism usually reserved for people who have never met my calendar, I opened my scheduling app and typed one small request: "Friday off, please."

The calendar responded with a notification. Just one. A single, ominous ping.

> "I have reviewed your request," it said. "We need to talk."

## The Meeting

Within seconds, my screen was filled with a color-coded timeline of *my entire existence*, broken into 15-minute increments stretching back to March. There was the hour I spent standing in the pantry eating crackers and contemplating nothing. The 45 minutes I spent Googling "how to appear busy at work." The two full hours labeled simply **"scrolling (regrettable)"** in bright red.

"Before I grant you Friday," the calendar said, "I'd like you to justify *Wednesday*."

*I could not justify Wednesday.*

## The Evidence

The calendar had prepared slides. **Actual slides.** A bulleted list of my alleged "time expenditures":

- 9:00–9:15 — *Checking the weather, then immediately forgetting it*
- 9:15–9:30 — *Standing up, sitting back down, standing up again*
- 9:30–10:00 — **"Strategic snacking"** (the calendar's air quotes were audible somehow)
- 10:00–12:00 — *A nap I have been calling "deep rest" for tax-adjacent reasons*

"You want a day *off*?" it said. "You've already taken 47 days off this quarter. I have the receipts. They're in the shared folder. I made a folder."

## The Ruling

After a closed-door session with my to-do list — during which, I am told, my to-do list said some *very* unkind things about my follow-through — the calendar issued its verdict:

> "Request **denied**. You are hereby scheduled for 'Rest, but suspiciously,' from 6:00–6:15 a.m. on Friday. You will spend it staring at the ceiling, thinking about everything you could be doing. That is the best deal on the table."

I tried to appeal. The calendar marked my appeal as "tentative" and invited my procrastination to weigh in.
