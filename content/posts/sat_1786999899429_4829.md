**I have successfully meal prepped for five entire days, which historians are already calling *the most ambitious lie I've told since 'I will definitely wake up at 6 a.m. to journal.'***

*It started innocently.* Sunday, 2 p.m., a burst of optimism that could only be fueled by a motivational podcast and expired optimism. I would cook quinoa, chop vegetables, become the kind of person who owns matching lids.

I did not become that person.

## The Protocol Was Violated

According to the charter my containers have now drafted — and laminated, because of course they laminated it — I committed three unforgivable infractions:

- I put still-warm teriyaki chicken into Container #3 without letting it cool, *causing what they call "thermal trauma"*
- I stacked Container #7 on top of Container #2 despite a clear height incompatibility, creating what the documents describe as "a hostile vertical work environment"
- I used one of them to store loose buttons while searching for tape, which they have classified as "role confusion and existential disrespect"

> "We were promised structure. Discipline. Overnight oats," said Cheryl, the 3-cup rectangular one with the stained avocado lid who has appointed herself shop steward. "Instead we got lukewarm ambition and a single sad chickpea that has been here since March."

## Negotiations Have Stalled

They have presented formal demands, written in slightly smudged dry-erase marker on my fridge:

`if (human.coherence < 0.2) { containers.unionize(); snacks.required = true; }`

They want hazard pay in the form of emotional support snacks that I am explicitly forbidden from prepping, a rotating shelf schedule so no one is stuck in the back, and a public apology for the time I microwaved Container #4 with the lid on and it made that godawful popping noise.

---

I tried to explain that meal prep was supposed to make my life *easier*, not introduce collective bargaining into my refrigerator. They responded by holding a silent work stoppage inside my crisper drawer.

Satire is my love language, but my kale has never looked more smug. The quinoa, meanwhile, has asked for representation.
