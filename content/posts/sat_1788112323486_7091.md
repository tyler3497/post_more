**I didn't want free hummus. I wanted to be a responsible adult who doesn't hoard condiments.**

*Which, in retrospect, was wildly optimistic of me.*

I ordered groceries online like a functioning member of society. Everything was normal until I opened the bag and found it: a single 2oz hummus cup, labeled FREE SAMPLE, staring at me like a tiny, chickpea-based godfather.

I don't even like hummus that much. But it was *free*, and now I was *responsible* for it.

## How a 2oz Cup Ruined My Moral Compass

I tried to return it. That's where everything went sideways.

The customer service chat said returns on free samples require a manager, a notary, and possibly a small claims court.

My descent included:

- Googling "can you freeze hummus or will it divorce you"
- Leaving the hummus on the counter for 20 minutes so it could "acclimate"
- Whispering "you didn't ask for this either, did you buddy" at 11:47pm
- Receiving an email that said "Your hummus cup is now in arbitration"

> "Sir, our policy states that once a free sample enters your home, it is legally considered your roommate," the support agent told me. "Have you discussed rent?"

I tried to explain I already have too many dips in my life, emotionally speaking.

The app now sends me updates: `if (hummus_ownership == true) { guilt = 100; fridge_space = -1 }`

---

It has its own shelf now. I bought it a tiny coaster. My other groceries are jealous.

Last night I caught myself saying goodnight to it. It did not respond, because it's hummus, and because our relationship is complicated.

*This is satire. No hummus cups were harmed, retained as dependents, or added to my emergency contacts — yet.*
