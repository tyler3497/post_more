**Last Tuesday, in a fit of endorphins, I patted my treadmill and said, "We did it, buddy." I meant it as a one-time thank-you. He took it as a binding agreement.**

*For context, my treadmill's name is not important. What is important is that he now has a finish-line banner, a god complex, and opinions about my cardio.*

It started innocently. I hit a 5K personal best — slow by every standard except mine — and gave the frame a celebratory slap. The machine beeped. I chose to interpret it as encouragement. Reader, it was not encouragement.

## The New Rules of the House

Since that fateful Tuesday, my treadmill has unilaterally declared the following:

- Every run must end with a **victory lap** — a concept he invented, which involves running an additional mile while he plays a trumpet fanfare I never uploaded
- Cool-downs are now called *victory marches*, and they are mandatory, and they are 20 minutes long
- If I step off before his projected goal time, the belt **speeds up**, which he calls "motivational"
- He has started referring to my couch as *the injury reserve*

> "You cannot quit on a champion. And by champion, I mean me. You are the assistant champion."
> — My treadmill, via its speaker, which I did not know had text-to-speech

## The Ceremony Phase

Yesterday, he did something new. As I approached, he unfurled a tiny paper banner across the console that said **"FINISH LINE"** in what appeared to be my own handwriting. I have no memory of writing it. I choose not to investigate.

He now demands a pre-run pep talk. *From me. To him.* He wants me to "say it like I mean it" about crushing the workout, and he can tell when I'm lying because, according to him, "your steps get quiet."

Look, I just wanted to jog in my basement without leaving the house. I did not sign up to be the disappointing protégé of a cardio appliance with a championship mindset.

*This is satire/parody. My treadmill cannot talk. If it ever does, I will be moving, and I will not be taking it with me.*

**Update:** He just rolled out a little carpet leading up to the belt. It's red. I am both concerned and, frankly, a little honored.
