**I said it casually, the way you say things you never expect a garage door to hear.**

Last Sunday I stood in my driveway with my brother, staring at my aging sedan, and said the fateful words: *"Maybe it's time for a new car."* I didn't whisper it. I didn't check who was listening. I said it right there, in front of the garage door that has opened for me, without complaint, approximately 6,000 times over the last decade.

*By Monday morning, the garage had a new personality.*

## The Slow-Motion Punishment

I hit the button at 7:40 a.m. Normally the door rattles up in about eleven seconds. This time, it took a full minute and a half — rising in dramatic, cinematic slow motion, inch by inch, as if I were supposed to be walking out of it in a trench coat with an orchestra swelling behind me.

> "Oh. It's YOU. The one who's thinking of REPLACING things."
> — my garage door, probably, in the voice I imagine it has (gruff, wounded, like a bouncer who's been passed over for promotion)

## Evidence of the Emotional Spiral

- It now opens **three inches, pauses, and opens three more**, like it's reconsidering whether I deserve to leave
- It squeaks on purpose. I oiled it. It squeaks LOUDER.
- It closed on me twice last week — both times after I looked at a car dealership's website
- My neighbor asked if my garage was "going through something." My NEIGHBOR. People can hear the passive aggression from across the street.

I tried to apologize. I stood there with a can of WD-40 and said, *"You're the best door I've ever had, and I mean that."* It opened the rest of the way in silence, but I swear the last six inches were **resentful**.
