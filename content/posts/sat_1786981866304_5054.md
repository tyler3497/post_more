**I thought I was signing up for a wholesome hobby.** Turns out I accidentally adopted a ceramic child with attachment issues.

## The Dream Vs. The Clay

I joined *Sunset Ceramics* because my therapist said I should "do something with my hands that isn't doomscrolling." Fair. The brochure promised "mindfulness, community, and inner peace." What it did *not* mention was that the clay would sense my fear and immediately declare war.

My instructor, Barb, is 68, has biceps of steel and the patience of a saint. On day one she said:

> "The clay knows what you need. You just have to listen."

My clay needed me to fail. Publicly.

Here's what I made on Week 3 — my so-called "mug":

- One handle that looks like a panicked shrimp
- A rim that wobbles if you breathe too loud
- A glazed interior the color of "regretful pond"
- And an undeniable aura that says *you will never leave me*

I named him Chip. Obviously.

## Why We Cannot Break Up

Chip sits on my kitchen counter now and **stares at me** while I drink coffee from my *other* mugs. (*The betrayal is mutual.*)

According to Chip's unspoken rules, I now have to:

- use him daily or he gets "cold"
- hand wash only — `Chip.exe has stopped responding to dishwashers`
- compliment his "character" when guests come over

I tried to use a normal, structurally sound mug this morning and I swear Chip tilted 2 degrees to the left like:

> "So that's how it is, huh?"

The real inner peace? Realizing that personal growth doesn't have to be symmetrical, dishwasher-safe, or emotionally stable. Sometimes it just holds your lukewarm tea and judges you quietly. And honestly, Chip? Same.
