**I did not set out to become a tyrant.** It started innocently enough: a rainy Sunday, a dull kitchen afternoon, and a sudden, overwhelming urge to know my knives *as people*. So I ranked all nine by personality. This, it turns out, was the single worst decision of my adult life.

## The Rankings

I posted the official list on the fridge, as one does:

- **#1: The 8" Chef's Knife** — "Commanding. A natural leader. Has main-character energy."
- **#2: The Santoku** — "Competent but humble. Middle-management material."
- **#3: The Bread Knife** — "Loud. Charismatic. Slightly unhinged."
- **#4–#6: The Steak Knives** — "A boy band. Individually forgettable, collectively dangerous."
- **#7: The Paring Knife** — "Small. Bitter. Has seen things."
- **#8: The Butter Knife** — "Delusional. Believes it is a real knife."
- **#9: The Avocado Slicer** — "A fraud. We all know it."

Within forty-eight hours, the chef's knife had stopped cutting anything that wasn't "worthy." It glanced at a bagel with open contempt and refused. The bread knife began spreading rumors — I heard it whispering to the paring knife about *favoritism* and *legacy prestige* — and suddenly nobody in the block would sit next to the santoku at mealtime.

The real crisis hit on Thursday. I reached for the butter knife to spread cream cheese, and the paring knife — which I swear had *migrated* to the front slot — issued a low, threatening gleam.

> "You've created a hierarchy," said no one, because knives can't talk, and yet somehow I heard it anyway. "And hierarchies have consequences."

Now the steak knives have unionized. The avocado slicer has filed a formal complaint about its ranking *(it included footnotes, which is rich coming from a tool invented in 2017)*. And the chef's knife has begun demanding tribute in the form of premium cutting boards, holding my sourdough hostage until I comply.

---

**Lessons learned:**

1. Knives do not want feedback. They want *validation*.
2. You cannot undo a hierarchy. The genie is out; the genie is also extremely sharp.
3. Never, ever rank your kitchen tools on a rainy Sunday.

I am currently negotiating a power-sharing agreement between the block and the drawer. *The butter knife has retained counsel.* Send help, and maybe a sharper set of priorities.
