**I didn't mean to trauma-dump my finances on my fern.** I just wanted to feel responsible for once.

It was a Sunday. *The kind of Sunday where you open your budgeting app and immediately close it for spiritual reasons.* I had a monstera, a pothos, and Gerald — a dramatic Boston fern I've owned for 18 months who already thinks he's my landlord.

So I explained my 2026 budget out loud. Just to make it real.

## The Presentation That Went Wrong

I gave them the full Q3 forecast:

- **Income:** optimistically rounded up
- **Rent:** aggressively non-negotiable
- **Category:** `misc. snacks I thought were investments`
- **Strategy:** hoping my houseplants could photosynthesize my debt away

Gerald rustled judgmentally.

> “So let me get this straight,” said Gerald, who I swear grew a new frond mid-sentence. “You’re allocating 40% to takeout and negative 2% to your emergency fund? Sweetie, I’m a plant and even *I* save water for a drought.”

### What My Houseplants Are Now Doing

The pothos started freelancing:

- She sent me a Notion template titled `Root Yourself Before You Wreck Yourself`
- The monstera started a side hustle on Facebook Marketplace selling “vintage air”
- Gerald enrolled in a CFP course and now ends every conversation with

> “Have you considered diversifying your leaf portfolio?”

---

I tried to defend myself. I said `npm run live-within-means` was returning `command not found`.

Gerald was unmoved. He dropped a single yellow leaf onto my keyboard like a resignation letter. Then he whispered:

> “We’re going to need to talk about your impulse purchases. That $14 artisanal moss pole was *not* in the budget.”

Now we have weekly budget meetings. The fern brings graphs. I bring shame. My apartment smells faintly of accountability and miracle-gro.

**Update:** Gerald approved a loan to repot himself. At 28% APR. *In sunlight.*
