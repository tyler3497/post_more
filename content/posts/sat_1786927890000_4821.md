**I did not intend to launch a political career when I walked in with three overdue graphic novels.**

I just wanted to *finally* not owe the city $4.20 in late fees. I thought I was being responsible. The librarian thought I was being presidential.

It started Tuesday. I returned my books two days early. *On purpose.* I even used the self-check drop so I wouldn't have to interact. But the desk librarian, Marge — who has seen me panic-Google "how to renew a book you already renewed" — gasped.

## The Moment Everything Went Wrong

> "You're back," she whispered, like I was a soldier returning from war. "And you're... early?"

I said "yeah, trying to be better about deadlines," which apparently in librarian is code for *"I have a five-point plan for this community."*

By Thursday, there was a flyer.

- Hand-drawn on dot-matrix printer paper from 1998
- My name spelled *almost* right — I am now "Anon For Mayor"
- Bullet list of my platform:
  - Open 10 minutes later on Tuesdays
  - Free bookmarks that don't immediately disappear into the void
  - A vending machine that actually works

Marge told the regulars I was "the kind of person who finishes a trilogy." One guy asked if I would fix the pothole on Elm. I said I have a library card, not a paving license.

---

I tried to clarify. I went to the reference desk and said:

> "I'm not running. I just learned how the online catalog works last week. Last month I thought the Dewey Decimal System was a person."

She nodded solemnly and said, *"That's exactly what a maverick would say."*

Now I'm scheduled to give a talk next Tuesday called *Why I Care: A Vision From Someone Who Once Paid $12 For Lost Damaged Book About Hamsters*.

I’m bringing cookies. If I don’t, I fear I’ll lose the senior mystery-book-club vote — and honestly, those ladies scare me more than any debate stage ever could.
