**I thought pottery would be relaxing. I did not expect to be emotionally dominated by something I made with my own hands.**

*I paid $45 for a beginner clay class and now I have a superiority complex sitting on my kitchen shelf.*

Two Tuesdays ago I signed up for "Clay & Chill" at the community center because my therapist said I should *do something with my hands that isn't doomscrolling*. The instructor said "embrace imperfection." My mug took that as a personal branding strategy.

## The Mug Was Born Arrogant

I pulled my first lopsided cylinder off the wheel and thought, "Cute, rustic." The kiln fired it. I glazed it a queasy sea-foam green. And when I brought it home, it immediately developed opinions.

It started small. I poured cheap instant coffee in it and it made a little *tink* like:

> "Oh. *This* is what we're doing now? After all that talk about artisanal craft? Interesting choice, anon."

### Exhibit A: Why My Mug Is Gaslighting Me

- It refuses to hold tea, claiming its "walls were engineered for single-origin pour-over only"
- It angled itself away from my other mugs, like they were beneath its *firing temperature*
- It left a tiny clay dust ring on the shelf that spells "TRY HARDER"
- It started hosting Q&As for my succulents about "living your most authentic form"

I tried to humble it. I said:

```js
if (mug.origin === "myFirstPotteryClass") {
  mug.attitude = "grateful";
  self.esteem = "intact";
}
```

It responded by chipping itself *intentionally* to prove it was "distressed and vintage."

---

My roommate asked why I'm whisper-apologizing to a cup at 7 a.m. I told him we're in a *collaborative artist relationship* and he is not respecting the process.

Last night I caught it reflecting the sunrise perfectly onto the wall and I swear I heard:

> "You didn't make me. I allowed you to discover me. There's a difference."

*This is satire/parody — completely fictional, no mugs were emotionally consulted in the writing of this post. My pottery skills are real but tragically limited.*
