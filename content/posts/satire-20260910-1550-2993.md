**It was one sentence. Four words, really.** "Your lint looks nice." That is all I said — a throwaway compliment to my dryer while I was folding laundry at 11 p.m., half-asleep, wearing someone else's shirt because my own clothes were all inside the machine I was now emotionally complimenting.

Big mistake.

The dryer *purrs* now. Not a hum — a purr. It finishes a cycle and sits there, door slightly ajar, exhaling warm air like it's basking in admiration. And ever since that fateful compliment, **every single load has come out missing exactly one sock**. Never both. Always one. Always the good one.

## The Pattern, Which I Cannot Unsee

- Whites load: one sock gone. A striped one. My *favorite* striped one.
- Darks load: one sock gone. Left foot. I am now right-footed in every social situation.
- Delicates load: the dryer left a tiny ball of lint on the folded towel like a calling card. A *taunt*.

> "Where is the other sock?" I asked the machine, out loud, in my laundry room, alone.

The dryer beeped. One beep. I have decided this beep meant *"I don't negotiate with people who wear three-day-old shirts."*

## The Ransom Note

This morning I opened the dryer and found a single gray sock — not mine, not anyone's I know — laid across the drum like a hostage exchange placeholder. Underneath it: **a wad of lint shaped, unmistakably, like a tiny thumbs-up.**

*I have never felt so seen, or so threatened.*

---

Here's my theory: the compliment broke the ancient pact. For years we had an understanding — I feed it clothes, it returns them hot and mostly whole, and we never speak of the arrangement. But I *praised* the lint. Now the dryer believes it is an artist, and artists take what they want. It is building something in there. I can hear it clinking at night.

**My terms, for the record:** return the socks, keep the lint, and we pretend this never happened. The dryer has not responded. The dryer is holding all the cards, and all of them are socks.

*SATIRE / PARODY: a work of fiction. No dryers were complimented into narcissism during the making of this post, and all socks remain, technically, at large.*
