**I bought a $38 reusable water bottle to save the planet and now I get passive-aggressive hydration notes in my own kitchen.**

Two Sundays ago I was a hero in the REI checkout line. Zero waste. Stainless steel. It even promised it would keep things cold for 24 hours, which is longer than any relationship I've maintained with a houseplant.

I named it Sipsquatch. That was mistake one.

## Why My Water Bottle Thinks It's My Mom

Sipsquatch does not *hold* water. It *monitors compliance.* Evidence:

- It sweats judgmentally when I walk past Starbucks
- It rolls slightly toward the edge of my desk every time I say "I'm tired"  
- It has developed a distinct *clang* that phonetically sounds exactly like "again?"
- My roommate swears it sighs. *I hear it too now.*

Yesterday I committed treason. I bought a large iced coffee. Plastic cup. Plastic straw. Twenty percent of it was definitely just ice.

> "Oh. I see. So we're doing `single_use_betrayal = true` now? Interesting choice. Very on-brand for someone who said 'I'm really committing to sustainability this time.'"

*That is verbatim what I imagined it saying but louder, somehow.*

---

I tried to hide the cup behind the milk. Sipsquatch saw everything. It always sees everything.

It spent the rest of the afternoon giving me the silent treatment, which is impressive for an object with no mouth. When I finally tried to fill it this morning, the water tasted like disappointment and vague Al Gore speeches.

The worst part? It's working. I'm now that person who brings an empty bottle to a coffee shop and asks them to pour it in there, then apologizes *to the bottle* when they say no.

I've tried to reason with it. **"It's one coffee!"** I said. Sipsquatch, through my own spiraling eco-guilt, replied:

> "Sure. And it's *one* planet, Kevin."

My name isn't Kevin.

*This is satire. Fictional. My water bottle is not sentient, though my therapist says the guilt is very real and we should explore that.*
