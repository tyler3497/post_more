**I finally decided to become the kind of adult who has an organized junk drawer.**

*This was a mistake.*

For context, this drawer has been operating under pure anarcho-chaos since 2019. It's where batteries go to retire, where takeout menus achieve immortality, and where that one IKEA Allen wrench lives rent-free.

Yesterday I dumped it all out and realized — it wasn't messy. It was *meticulous*.

## The Evidence It Had a System

I had assumed it was just junk. But laid out on my floor, it told a different story:

- 17 expired coupons sorted by guilt level
- a single AA battery taped to a note that says `DO NOT USE - HE IS THE CHOSEN ONE`
- three identical screwdrivers, each labeled "definitely not the right one"
- my own handwriting on a receipt from 2021: "put this back later (you won't)"

> "You think *you* organize *me*?" whispered the drawer. "Buddy, I've been keeping *you* functional for years."

---

### The Final Betrayal

According to the drawer — *which I am now interviewing for clarity* — it has been:

- Hiding my car keys when I'm running late so I learn patience
- Supplying one working pen only when Mercury is *not* in retrograde
- Saving every bread tie I've ever owned for a sculpture it'll never finish

**My demands:** give me back control of my own kitchen.

**Its counter-offer:** official promotion to `Chief Operating Officer of Small Tangled Things`.

I accepted. The pens work better when you respect their union.
