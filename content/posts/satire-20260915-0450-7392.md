**It was supposed to be enrichment.** That's what the cardboard box of dog puzzles promised, and "enrichment" sounded so wholesome right up until my dog started beating me at chess.

## How It Started

I downloaded a canine-cognition tutorial that began with paw-based sorting games, the kind where the dog nudges colored rings onto pegs. I skipped straight to chapter seven: *chess*. The mistake, in retrospect, was mine. I showed him how the pieces move, let him sniff the knights, and said the words no human should ever say to a dog:

> "Let's just play a friendly game."

There is no friendly game with Biscuit.

## The Evidence That I've Created a Monster

- **He trash-talks.** Not with words — worse. Between moves he exhales sharply through his nose, like a chess-grandfather clock of contempt ticking down my remaining dignity.
- **He touch-moved.** Twice. Both times I let him take it back. Both times *he did not let me* take it back. There is now a separate rulebook on this table, and it is written in drool.
- **He studies the endgame at 3 a.m.** I have woken up to find him sitting in the hallway, staring at the board the way a general surveys a battlefield, ears up, tail still, absolutely certain.
- **He resigns *my* games for me.** I will leave the room losing; I return to find my king tipped over and my dog waiting by the treat jar, as if to say: *we don't need to discuss it.*

## The Turning Point

Yesterday I blundered my queen in four moves — *four* — and Biscuit looked at the board, then looked at me, then gently nosed his bishop into a fork so clean a titled player would applaud. Then he did the thing I will never forget:

> He laid his head on my knee, sighing like I was the one who needed enrichment.

I have since signed him up for the local club's casual night. He is rated somewhere around 1400 and refuses the pre-game handshake on the grounds that *he doesn't have hands*. Fair. Devastatingly fair.
