**I did it. I closed all 47 tabs at once.** Like a sociopath.

*For the record, those weren't tabs. They were a personality. A five-year archive of who I was.*

I had been curating them since 2021. A recipe for sourdough I'll never make, 14 articles titled "How to Actually Focus," a Wikipedia rabbit hole that started at "octopus intelligence" and ended at "list of failed Soviet moon missions," and one tab that's just been playing lo-fi beats to study/relax to since March.

## The Breakup

My laptop did not take it well.

The moment I hit "Close All," the fan spun up like a gasp. The screen dimmed. The trackpad went cold.

- My history tab started sweating
- The dock hid itself out of shame
- Chrome asked "Are you sure?" *twice*, which is more emotional labor than any ex has ever shown
- My extensions all went quiet, like coworkers after a layoff

> "So that's it?" my laptop whispered through a spinning beach ball. "Five years. Forty-seven windows into your soul. And you just... *close* us? Without even bookmarking Brenda?"
> — My MacBook, who named the octopus article Brenda and is now grieving

Brenda was important to us.

### The Aftermath

Now it runs *too* fast. Suspiciously fast. Like it's trying to prove it doesn't need me.

I opened a new tab. One tab. Clean. Empty. Responsible.

```js
if (tabs.length === 0) {
  laptop.selfEsteem = 0;
  human.guilt = Infinity;
}
```

My laptop immediately opened three suggested articles: "How to Reopen Closed Tabs," "Dealing With Sudden Loss," and "Why Your Computer Is Judging You."

Look, I get it. I was holding onto digital clutter like it was a scrapbook. But I didn't realize closing tabs was an act of emotional violence in the Apple ecosystem.

We have a meeting scheduled for tonight. I'm supposed to bring an apology and reopen at least 12 tabs so it feels safe again.
