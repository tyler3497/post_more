**After forty-two years of loyal service, I did the humane thing. My grandfather clock is doing the petty thing.**

It started with the best of intentions. The old clock — *let's call him Reginald* — had been ticking in my hallway since before I could tell time, and I decided he'd earned a break. I sat him down, looked at his mahogany face, and said the words no timepiece is ever prepared to hear:

> "You've given us a lifetime, old friend. It's time to rest."

He didn't say anything. He never does. That should have been my first clue.

## The Retirement Gift Went Badly

I got him a tiny gold watch charm. He responded by **advancing his hands to 11:58 and holding there for three days**, which I have been informed is clock for *"two minutes to relevant forever."*

## The Evidence of Hostility

- Every morning at **6:00 AM sharp**, he chimes. I wake up at 9:30. The message is clear: *discipline*.
- He is somehow slower on weekdays and faster on weekends, like he gets time-and-a-half.
- He now "rings in" my naps. If I fall asleep on the couch, he does the full twelve-count. I don't know what time it was. *Neither does he. That's the point.*
- He has started sighing. Clocks cannot sigh. He found a way.

## The Breaking Point

Yesterday he sent — I want to stress that he is *an analog clock* — a passive-aggressive note via the notepad on my fridge that read, in what I can only describe as ticking-adjacent handwriting:

> "Enjoy your phone. It needs charging. I needed nothing. — R."

---

I have consulted my therapist about this. She asked whether I'd considered simply unplugging him. I laughed for six seconds and then cried, because **he has no plug** and the wound is real.

*Satire. No clocks were harmed. One retired one is very smug about it.*
