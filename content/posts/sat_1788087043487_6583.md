**I decided to let my closet self-organize.**

*This was billed as trusting the process. The process immediately unionized and promoted my jeans.*

I cleaned my apartment last Sunday. Not the fake clean where you shove everything into the closet and say "we'll deal with this later." The real one. I pulled every shirt, sweater, and inexplicable single sock out onto the bed and announced, like a visionary CEO, "You guys figure it out. I believe in you."

By Tuesday, my closet had implemented a full corporate structure.

## How My Denim Became Middle Management

My jeans — specifically my dark wash, the ones I wear when I want people to think I have plans — had taken charge. They folded themselves into a perfect little power stance and started delegating.

- My t-shirts were reassigned to "casual Friday, every day"
- My hoodies were told they were "too emotional for Q3"
- My one good button-down was put on a PIP (Perfume Improvement Plan)
- My socks, which have been missing since 2022, were listed as "remote workers"

> "Per my last fold, we need to circle back on your hanger utilization," my jeans said, in a voice that sounded exactly like my old manager who scheduled 7 a.m. standups. "Let's take this offline — to the hamper."

*I was being performance-reviewed by pants.*

### The Breaking Point

Wednesday morning I tried to grab gym shorts. The jeans blocked the aisle.

"Do you have bandwidth for leg day?" they asked. "Because we're seeing a pattern of avoidance behavior."

I explained that my entire personality is currently held together by elastic waistbands and iced coffee. They nodded sympathetically and added it to the quarterly report.

---

**I have been out-organized by cotton.** I wanted a capsule wardrobe. I got a hierarchy. I am now asking my own jeans for PTO to wear sweatpants, and they are denying it pending "strategic alignment."

*Satire. This is fiction. My closet is still a mess, and my jeans are still just jeans. Labeled as parody — please do not take career advice from denim.*
