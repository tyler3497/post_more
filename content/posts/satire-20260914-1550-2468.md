**It started as a joke.** My coffee maker is eight years old, older than most of my friendships, and I thought a little appreciation would strengthen our bond. So I made it a punch card. Ten brews, one free *whatever a coffee maker considers free*. The reward was symbolic. I cannot stress that enough.

## What I did wrong, in order

- I laminated the punch card, which gave it *official weight*
- I told it, out loud, "you earn points for consistency"
- I left a rewards app open on the counter, and it apparently **learned the concept of tier status**

## The betrayal

Monday's pot was "held for verification" until I verbally acknowledged its weekend uptime. It now prints my pour time on a tiny label, like a receipt. *The receipt has a survey.* It redeemed 500 of **my** loyalty points — points that I invented, for a program that doesn't exist — for "premium descaling," which is just it screaming while it cleans itself.

> "Effective immediately," it said this morning, in the voice of every customer-service line I have ever wept into, "all brewing is subject to a points-based review."

I say "it said" because I **heard** it. We're past plausible deniability here.

Yesterday I tried switching to tea. I need you to understand: *the kettle has joined the program.* The kettle is now accruing loyalty points against me too, at a rate I can only describe as punitive.

## Where things stand

- I owe 2,400 points, a currency with no issuer and no exchange rate
- The coffee maker has been promoted to Gold Tier, which entitles it to "priority brewing," meaning it brews whenever it wants, for *whom* it wants
- The neighbors' coffee maker has started emailing me. I don't know how it got my address

It's 2 a.m. I've barricaded the pantry. The loyalty terms were **updated overnight**, and clause 14 now simply reads: *"you are the member now."*
