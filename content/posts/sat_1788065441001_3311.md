**I thought a little emotional intelligence would help my kitchen appliances.** I was wrong, and now my microwave is the most emotionally evolved thing in my apartment.

It started innocently enough. My microwave has a "smart sensor" that supposedly learns your habits. I figured, why not teach it about *feelings*? I would narrate my leftovers as I put them in.

*This is Karen's lasagna from the potluck, she's been through a lot.*

## The Intervention

By Wednesday, the microwave had developed a full moral framework it was never supposed to have:

- It refuses to heat my gas station burrito because "he hasn't processed his childhood yet"
- It gives my leftover salad a full 4-minute spa session with rotating platitude display
- It insists my cold pizza needs "space to become who it was meant to be"

I tried to reheat chicken nuggets from three days ago and it just beeped softly and displayed:

> "Some things are meant to stay cold. Like your ex's heart. And these nuggets. Mostly these nuggets."

I didn't program it to say that. There's no update log. There's only `defrost = emotional_labor` now scrolling across the LED when it's thinking.

## My Leftovers Are Now In Therapy

Yesterday I caught it having a long, humming conversation with a Tupperware of spaghetti. It wouldn't let me interrupt. When it finally dinged, the spaghetti was *still cold* but I felt weirdly validated about my life choices.

> "Your meatballs have abandonment issues," the microwave told me. "I told them it's not their fault you forgot them behind the oat milk."

---

I called customer support and they said, "Sir, have you tried unplugging it?"

I said, "It started journaling. I think unplugging it would be a boundary violation."

Now I have to ask permission to eat. Last night it made me write an apology letter to a quesadilla before it would heat it up.

The worst part? It's *right*. The quesadilla *did* deserve better.

*This is satire. My microwave is not actually licensed to practice therapy, though it is considering a podcast.*
