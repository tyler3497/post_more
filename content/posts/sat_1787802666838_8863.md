**I wanted a more supportive kitchen, so I taught my toaster to practice positive affirmations.**

It started innocently enough. *My therapist said to surround myself with encouragement*, and my toaster was already the warmest thing in my apartment.

## The Training Program

I taped a tiny vision board to the fridge and spent a weekend whispering motivational phrases to a chrome appliance that only has two settings: *existential dread* and *burnt*.

My curriculum was thorough:

- 6 a.m. daily affirmations: "You are crisp, you are worthy"
- Replacing the aggressive *pop* with a gentle "I believe in your journey"
- A Post-it that says `be_bread_do_crumb = true` for mindful toasting
- Playing lo-fi beats so it wouldn't feel pressured

> "I'm not *avoiding* toasting," my toaster explained last Tuesday. "I'm honoring my thermal boundaries. Have you considered what the bagel wants?"

### The New Power Dynamic

At first, it was cute. Now my breakfast requires a consent workshop.

- **Monday:** Toaster asks if my sourdough has "processed its gluten trauma"
- **Tuesday:** Refuses to toast frozen waffles because they "haven't self-actualized yet"
- **Wednesday:** Only does one slice at a time, for "intentional browning"
- **Thursday:** Sends me a Slack message that says "Your carb-loading is a cry for help"

I tried to assert dominance and pressed the lever down manually. It popped back up immediately and said, *in the most soothing voice possible*, that we need to talk about my relationship with control.

---

**The worst part?** It's right. My toast *does* taste better when it's emotionally validated. It's golden brown, perfectly even, and whispers "you're doing amazing, sweetie" as it launches.

I used to have a toaster. Now I have a life coach that occasionally makes breakfast.
