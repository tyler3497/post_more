**I have never been a morning person.** Which is exactly why I thought 6 A.M. run club would *fix* me.

## How It Started

Last Tuesday I signed up for "Sunrise Striders" because the flyer said *accountability, community, and complimentary banana*. I own one pair of running shoes that I bought in 2022 and have mostly used to kick my vacuum cleaner closer to the outlet.

I showed up at 5:59 A.M. in matching socks. The coach — a man who appears to be 80% calf muscle — blew a whistle and we were off. 

My shoes made it exactly 0.4 miles before staging a full work stoppage.

Signs they were unhappy and I was in denial:

- Audible squeaking that I chose to interpret as *encouragement* until it became Morse code for `no`
- They developed a sudden, passionate interest in every puddle as a place to *rest*
- The left one untied itself three times, which my fitness tracker logged as `intentional interval training`
- My right sock filed a formal complaint by disappearing entirely

> "We didn't sign up for hills," wheezed my left shoe, who I have now named Brenda. "We signed up for leisurely walks to get iced coffee. You have violated the terms of our sole agreement."

## The Collective Bargaining

By mile two, both shoes had linked their laces together in solidarity and refused to move. The coach jogged back and said, with complete sincerity, "Your footwear is unionizing."

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered extra arch support, a gentle rinse cycle, and weekends off. Brenda countered with demands for `a 15-minute warm-up that is just sitting` and retirement after 100 miles.

I carried them home like a toddler who fell asleep at Disneyland.

*This is satire. My shoes are inanimate, the run club is very nice, and I am still extremely sore.*
