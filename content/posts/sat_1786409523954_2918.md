**I finally did one responsible adult thing and the universe immediately overcorrected.**

After three weeks of strategic avoidance, *you know, the classic "if I don't look at it, it isn't dying" approach*, I watered my succulent. One splash. Barely a tablespoon. I even whispered, "There you go, buddy."

Big mistake.

By morning, Barry — yes, he named himself — had rearranged my priorities, my throw pillows, and apparently my entire five-year plan.

## How My Succulent Became A CEO

It started small. A little *growth mindset*, literally. Then:

- He critiqued my sleep schedule: "If you can keep me alive on neglect, imagine what 6 hours could do for *you*"
- He moved my car keys to where he gets better sunlight, for "optimal energy flow"
- He started leaving me inspirational sticky notes in my handwriting that I do not remember writing
- He enrolled us both in a morning routine that is just staring out the window judging joggers

> "You watered me once in 21 days and you're calling *me* needy?" Barry said, photosynthesizing judgmentally. "Let's unpack your fear of commitment."

---

### The Coaching Sessions Have Escalated

Every morning now, 7:15 a.m. sharp:

`Day 22: still thriving on spite and one ice cube - have you journaled about your mother?`

I tried to set boundaries. I said, "You're a plant." He said, *and I quote*, "And you're a person who Googles 'how to tell if succulent is mad at you' at 2 a.m., so who's really equipped to judge?"

**Honestly, he's not wrong.** My apartment has never looked better. He convinced me to finally hang those frames, drink water that isn't coffee-adjacent, and text my sister back. My productivity is up 40%. My self-esteem is being managed by a desk plant with the emotional range of a cactus but the confidence of a TED Talk speaker.

I've created a monster. A tiny, green, emotionally intelligent monster who now charges rent in the form of *consistent emotional availability*.

*Satire / Parody — this succulent is not actually licensed to coach anyone.*
