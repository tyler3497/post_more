**I watched one 22-minute YouTube video about capsule wardrobes and became a completely different, much more judgmental person.**

I woke up Sunday, looked at my closet, and thought, *I'm an adult, I should own exactly 12 items that all spark joy and also make me look like I have my life together.*

So I Marie Kondo'd everything. If it didn't spark joy, it went in The Bin. The Bin is a large, emotionally charged cardboard box from Target that now lives by my front door.

## The Betrayal

Within hours, things got weird:

- My navy sweater from college (2019, three pizza stains, still *very* supportive) started rattling
- The chunky knit my mom made that I never wear but absolutely cannot donate without being haunted
- A zip-up fleece that has seen things and should probably be in therapy

Then the postcards started arriving.

> "Hey. It's us. The sweaters. It's cold in here. You left us next to your old Halloween decorations and a half-eaten protein bar. Is this minimalism or abandonment? - Love, The Knit Collective"

I didn't write that. My sweaters did. *Apparently.*

## Operation: Guilt Trip

They've organized. They have a system now called `Operation: Folded Feelings` and it includes:

1. Nightly drafts from the donation bin
2. Passive-aggressive lint notes slipped under my door
3. My favorite hoodie standing guard like, "Really? You're gonna wear *that* generic new minimalist tee over me?"

---

My roommate asked why I'm apologizing to a cardboard box. I said, "You wouldn't get it, you're not in a committed textile relationship."

Look, I wanted a clean closet. Now I have 12 hangers, immense guilt, and a donation bin that writes better prose than I do. The sweaters say they'll forgive me if I let them back in for winter.

*This is satire. No sweaters were emotionally harmed in the making of this closet crisis. Probably.*
