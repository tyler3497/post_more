**I made one simple courtesy call — let my umbrella dry indoors — and now I work for it.**

It was raining Tuesday. My umbrella did its job. I propped it open in the hallway like a polite person would. *Huge mistake. CEO-level mistake.*

By Wednesday morning, it had claimed the coat rack as its executive office and started sending calendar invites.

## How It Climbed the Corporate Ladder

The umbrella — I call him Chad because he absolutely would pick Chad — started small but moved fast:

- Stood fully open despite building policy clearly stating umbrellas must be closed indoors
- Drip-dried strategically onto my important mail to demonstrate power
- Demanded I route all future weather complaints through his department
- Started referring to my closet as "the satellite office"

> "You think rain just *happens*?" he whispered while slowly rotating toward me. "I manage precipitation relations. You manage... what? Forgetting your keys and ordering takeout at 11 p.m.?"

I laughed, nervously. He did not. Umbrellas never laugh. They loom.

---

## The All-Hands Meeting I Didn't Agree To

By Thursday, he was running morning stand-ups in the entryway. My rain boots were promoted to VP of Puddles. My doormat got put on a PIP for "insufficient enthusiasm."

I tried to close him. He made that aggressive *FWUMP* noise umbrellas make when they know HR (my cat, who loves him) will back them up.

My roommate asked why there's a quarterly forecast meeting happening in our kitchen. I said:

`Q3 outlook: 100% chance of me losing authority over 400 sq ft`

### My Action Items (Assigned by Umbrella)

- Stop tracking mud in like an unpaid intern
- Provide quarterly updates on sock dryness metrics
- Acknowledge that he is, quote, "the only thing standing between us and atmospheric chaos"
- Never, ever buy a compact travel umbrella — "we don't need consultants"

The worst part? My apartment *does* look more organized with him presiding. I folded laundry yesterday because Chad was watching. I made my bed.

I think I just got promoted to Assistant to the Regional Umbrella. Benefits include staying dry and being constantly judged by 12 panels of smug nylon.

*This is satire and parody — entirely fictional, no real umbrella CEOs were consulted, though mine is now asking for equity.*
