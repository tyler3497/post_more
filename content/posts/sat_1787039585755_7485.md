**I did it for aesthetics.** You know, that moment where you see a TikTok of a perfect rainbow bookshelf and think, *this will finally make me look like I have my life together.*

It took four hours, two dust allergies, and a motivational speech to a copy of *Atomic Habits* that I have never opened. The result was gorgeous. A flawless gradient from angry red to smug beige. I posted a photo. My mom liked it. My shelf glowed.

Then the complaints started.

### Hour One: Passive Aggression

The big blue art book in the middle muttered first:

> "I have spent three years holding up your plant. I did not get a fine arts degree for this."

*I should have known the coffee table books would be elitist.*

The yellows formed a caucus immediately:

- The self-help paperback is "breathing too loudly" next to the memoirs
- The thriller with the torn cover is accused of "bringing down property values"
- The cookbook that I’ve used twice is demanding to be moved back next to "someone who gets it"

**My copy of Dune** has filed a formal request to not be sandwiched between a diet book and a 2019 planner that says *New Year, New Me* on it.

## The HOA Meeting I Did Not Agree to Moderate

By 9 p.m., there was a full homeowners association on shelf three. I tried to be diplomatic.

> "Guys, you're sorted by wavelength of light, this is objectively more beautiful than Dewey Decimal could ever—"

The reds interrupted. The reds *always* interrupt.

They've drafted a petition titled **We Did Not Survive 800 Pages of Historical Fiction to Be Used as Accent Color.** It has seven signatures and a coffee stain that I think is from me but they claim is *emotional damage.*

The worst part? The organizing *worked* — I can actually find books now. I just can't open any of them without a mediation session and a written apology to their neighbors.

I’ve agreed to binding arbitration. The spine of *Meditations* is presiding.
