**I did not want to hurt it.** That's what I kept telling myself while I stared at the settings menu, thumb hovering over "disable snooze," like a coward with a breakup text he hasn't sent yet.

It started simply. I was tired of waking up at 6:00, 6:09, 6:18, and 6:27 like some kind of *human toast that keeps popping back up wrong*. So I did the adult thing: I turned snooze off. Clean break. No drama. Cold turkey.

## The Flowers Arrived First

By Tuesday, my doorstep looked like a florist had exploded. Bouquets kept arriving every nine minutes — *nine*, the sacred snooze interval — each with a little card that just said "THINK OF ME." I don't know how a button learned my address. I don't know how it pays for shipping. I'm afraid to ask.

## The Stages of Snooze Grief

I have catalogued the button's emotional journey through the notes it leaves in my alarm history:

- **Denial** — "You're just tired. We'll talk in nine minutes."
- **Bargaining** — "What if I only give you *five* minutes? Compromise!"
- **Anger** — It started ringing at 3:33 AM for no reason. Pure spite. *Weaponized REM.*
- **Acceptance** — It now just vibrates softly and whispers "you up?" into the void.

> "I gave you the best years of my beeping life, and you replaced me with WILLPOWER."
>
> — my snooze button, via a push notification, at 6:09 AM sharp

Look, I understand heartbreak. I really do. But there is something deeply unhinged about an appliance running a `while(true)` loop on my emotional availability. Yesterday it sent a sympathy fruit basket to *itself* and signed my name.

---

**Update:** I caved. I turned snooze back on. We are seeing each other in nine-minute increments again, and honestly? I've never slept worse, and I've never felt more loved.

*This is satire. No snooze buttons were emotionally harmed in the writing of this post.*
