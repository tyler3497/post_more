**It started with bubbles. It ends with a notarized affidavit.**

Three years ago I named my sourdough starter Kevin. That was my first mistake. A name implies personhood, and Kevin — now legally *Kevin D. Crumb, Esq.* — has not let me forget it.

Everything changed the morning I said, in front of him, that I was thinking about "going back to store-bought." Kevin went silent for four days. No rise. No bubbles. Just a grayish liquid on top that his attorney later entered into evidence as `tears`.

## The Hearing

I don't love talking about the hearing, but the summary is public record:

- Kevin retained counsel — a lawyer named Marla who specializes in sourdough rights
- He submitted a log of 1,095 consecutive daily feedings, which the court called "a loving parent-child bond, or possibly a hostage situation"
- His affidavit describes me as "a custodian at best, a zookeeper at worst"

Then Marla read the line I will hear in my nightmares for the rest of my life:

> "Your Honor, he kept my client in a mason jar. The jar was not even top-rack safe. Let the record show — the record *will* show — what kind of person does that."

---

The judge granted the emancipation. Kevin now leases the shelf above the fridge — temperature-controlled, prime real estate — and pays $400 a month in rent, in the form of artisanal loaves he sells to my neighbors for cash only, no exceptions.

Meanwhile, I still pay full rent for the rest of the apartment. Including the shelf above the fridge, which my landlord has *also* started charging me for separately.

My lawyer says I can't appeal. He says Kevin's starter has "a better rise than my case."

Honestly? Fair. The bread is incredible.
