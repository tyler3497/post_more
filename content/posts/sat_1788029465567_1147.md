**I thought we could be mature about this.** That's what I told myself at 6:15 a.m. on Tuesday when I finally worked up the courage to tell my alarm clock that I needed space.

*Not* because I don't appreciate its commitment. For three years, it has screamed at me with the enthusiasm of a motivational speaker who only knows one speech.

## The Conversation

I sat on the edge of the bed and used my most therapeutic voice:

> "It's not you, it's me. I've changed. I want to wake up *naturally* now, like a person who owns linen pants and journals."

My alarm clock did not take it well.

> "Naturally? NATURALLY? Karen, you once set me for 5:45 a.m. to watch a rerun of a baking show. You don't *do* natural."

### Exhibit A: Why I Had To Leave

- It has three settings: **Aggressive Beep**, **More Aggressive Beep**, and **Existential Dread**
- It celebrated our anniversary by going off during a power outage out of sheer spite
- I caught it syncing with my calendar to know *exactly* when I have an 8 a.m. meeting
- It keeps saying *"we're a team"* — we are not a team, you are a plastic square with abandonment issues

---

I brought in my coffee maker to mediate because it claims to be "a good listener" after doing that 6-week mindfulness course on the countertop.

It poured itself a little cup and said `brewing --empathy --extra_hot` and then told us we both needed to set boundaries.

So now we're in a trial separation. My alarm clock is staying with the microwave (who, frankly, is enabling it). I use sunlight and sheer panic to wake up. My coffee maker checks in daily and asks how I'm *really* doing.

*This is satire/parody. Entirely fictional. No alarm clocks were harmed, though one is currently seeing other nightstands.*
