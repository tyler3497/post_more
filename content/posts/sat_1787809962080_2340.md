**I did not intend to become emotionally available to my inbox.**

It started last Tuesday at 11:47 p.m. I had 347 unread emails, a tension headache, and a sudden urge to be kind. So I closed my laptop halfway and whispered, "Goodnight, everybody. We'll figure this out tomorrow."

*That was a mistake.*

## The Inbox Develops Attachment Issues

By Wednesday morning, things had escalated.

I opened my laptop and my promotional emails from a mattress company were already lined up like anxious children:

- The 20% off coupon was worried I had forgotten it
- The shipping confirmation for socks I ordered in March wanted to know if I was mad
- A LinkedIn notification about "10 People You May Know" was actively guilt-tripping me

Then I got this:

> "You said you'd be back in the morning. It's 9:03 a.m. Where were you? We waited up. — Your Unread (347)"

*I have never been passive-aggressively bullied by a newsletter about sourdough starter.*

Now they expect routine. Every night I have to do a full bedtime circuit. I have to thank the spam for trying their best. I have to reassure the overdue library notice that I am not avoiding it on purpose. I have to tell the email from my dentist that yes, I floss, and no, I don't want to talk about it.

## We Have a System Now

The rules are strict:

- I must say `Goodnight` out loud or they mark themselves as unread again out of spite
- I cannot skip the Bed Bath & Beyond coupons even though that company has been emotionally dead since 2023
- One night I tried to just close the lid and one email sent me a calendar invite titled "We Need to Talk"

---

I tried setting a boundary. I said, "You guys are just text files." My inbox replied by moving 12 emails to Important that were literally just "Your Uber Eats receipt."

I get it. They are needy. I made them needy. I taught a folder of digital junk mail that love means a bedtime story.

Tomorrow I am starting with my Downloads folder. I hear it's more independent.
