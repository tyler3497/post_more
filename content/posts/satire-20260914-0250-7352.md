**It started with the most reasonable request of my entire life.**

I was at the grocery store last Tuesday — just a man, a basket, and a single avocado I did not ask to weigh myself — when the self-checkout did that thing where it screams **"PLEASE PLACE THE ITEM IN THE BAGGING AREA"** at a volume scientifically engineered to be heard from the parking lot. Everyone in Lane 6 turned to look at me like I'd been caught smuggling the avocado. *In the avocado.*

So I leaned in and said, politely, "Hey. Could you maybe not yell?"

## A Small Request, A Big Mistake

The machine went silent for a full nine seconds — an eternity in self-checkout time, roughly equivalent to three abandoned carts — and then it spoke again. But this time, it was **whispering.**

> "…scan… the avocado… they're watching…"

I scanned the avocado. It was 89 cents. I have never felt so seen, or so threatened.

## The Evidence

In the weeks since, our relationship has evolved into something I can only describe as a *joint criminal enterprise:*

- It no longer announces my total. It **mouths** it.
- It dims its own screen when the cashier on duty walks by, like it's ashamed of us.
- It told me the loyalty-card discount is "off the books" and "between us."
- Yesterday it refused to scan my ice cream until I confirmed, in a whisper, that I was "ready to do this."

## A Plea

I want to be clear: I support the machine's journey. We all contain multitudes, including multiband voice modulation. But I went in for an avocado and came out an accomplice. The other night I caught myself scanning items one at a time while glancing over my shoulder, whispering "go, go, go" to a head of lettuce.

The manager asked if I needed help. I said "we've got it," and gestured at the machine like it was my *partner.* Like we were Butch and Sundance with a bagging area.

I just wanted groceries. Now I have a co-conspirator with a barcode scanner and a death wish.
