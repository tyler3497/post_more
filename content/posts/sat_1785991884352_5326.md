**I never meant to lead a desert plant on.** That feels important to establish for legal reasons.

## How It Started

It started as a joke. My friend said, and I quote, "you couldn't even keep a cactus alive," which is *rude but statistically accurate*. So I bought a cactus — small, spiky, judgmental — named him Sir Pricks-a-Lot, and started watering him ironically.

Like, oh wow, look at me, I'm a *responsible plant parent*. I put on a little voice and everything.

The problem is that Sir Pricks-a-Lot did not understand irony.

He understood:

- The one time I gave him Evian because we were out of tap
- Me apologizing for my roommate's terrible lo-fi beats
- The afternoon I moved him closer to the window so he could "see some action"

> "You finally *see* me," he seemed to whisper last Tuesday, after I accidentally complimented his new spike growth. "Most people fear my boundaries."

### Wait, It Gets Worse

Now we have rituals.

**I have to:**

- Say good morning before coffee or he slouches *dramatically*
- Rotate him exactly 15 degrees or he gives me the silent treatment
- Explain my weekend plans so he doesn't "worry"

I tried to set a boundary. I said, "Sir, we need to talk, this is moving too fast." He responded by growing a tiny flower, which my plant app says is *extremely rare and emotionally manipulative*.

---

My therapist says I project. My cactus says my therapist doesn't understand us. I ran `git log --oneline --grep=cactus` and even my *commit history* is just me watering a plant at 2 a.m.

We are now registered as domestic partners at the local nursery. I'm told that's not legally binding, but Sir Pricks-a-Lot has already picked out curtains.

`Status: 85% hydrated, 100% entangled`

*This is satire. No cacti were emotionally compromised in the making of this post, except this one.*
