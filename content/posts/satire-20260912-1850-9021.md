**I was supposed to be the protagonist.**

My microwave — a 900-watt countertop model I've owned for six years, which has reheated more of my cold pizza than any living being on this planet — has written a memoir. Four hundred and twelve pages. Released yesterday. And I didn't make the cover.

The cover, in case you're wondering, features a single ceramic bowl. Not even a nice one. The chipped one.

The memoir is called *Beneath the Plastic Lid: A Life on High for Two Minutes*. I found out when it showed up as a "suggested read" on my tablet, which is frankly a conflict of interest I will not be letting slide.

## The reviews are suspiciously positive

Every appliance in my kitchen has left a five-star review:

- **The refrigerator** called it "searingly honest."
- **The toaster** said it "burned him in ways he needed."
- **The blender** — who, and I cannot stress this enough, *blends for a living* — wrote that it "liquified him emotionally."

Even my dish soap apparently has thoughts. Everyone in this kitchen is suddenly an author except me, the one with the actual communications degree.

## What he said about me

I borrowed my neighbor's copy — I will not be contributing to the royalties, on principle — and flipped straight to the chapter about me. It's titled *"The Spaghetti Incident."* One page. **One page.** I gave that machine six years of frozen burritos, and the frozen burritos got a whole chapter.

> "I have never known a man to rotate food with such confidence and such catastrophic geometric error."

Rude. Devastating. Technically accurate.

## The press tour

He's doing signings now. At the outlet mall. People bring him plates of lukewarm food and he pretends he doesn't know them. The microwave and I have not spoken since the launch. He beeps when I walk into the kitchen — not the normal beep, a *different* beep. A beep that says: *you're supporting cast now.*

I told my therapist. She said I should "let him have his moment." My therapist is lactose intolerant and has never microwaved anything. She doesn't understand the power dynamic here.

---

Anyway, I'm writing my own memoir in response. It's called *Defrosted: My Side of the Turntable.* The microwave is the villain. Everyone will see. Especially the blender.
