**I asked my Roomba where the kitchen was because I was holding a laundry basket over my face and walked into a wall, which is a normal Tuesday for me.**

*In my defense, the apartment layout hasn't changed in two years, but my sense of direction has gotten worse since I started trusting my phone to tell me where my car is parked.*

## My Vacuum Has Seen Too Much

My Roomba, which I named Sir Sweeps-A-Lot because I thought we were being cute, paused mid-spin, did a slow 360-degree scan like it was judging my entire existence, and said — via the app notification, because it doesn't actually talk, but emotionally it definitely does:

> "You keep circling the same spot in the living room. Have you considered you're not lost in the apartment, you're lost in life?"

I was not ready for this level of insight from something whose main job is eating crumbs.

- It mapped my apartment in 4 minutes and mapped my avoidance issues in 30 seconds
- It now refuses to clean under the couch, calling it "setting boundaries"
- It sent me a push notification at 2 a.m.: `CLEANING COMPLETE. Also, have you called your mom?`

---

## The Coaching Era

I tried to explain that I just needed directions to the kitchen, not an existential audit. Sir Sweeps-A-Lot was unimpressed.

> "You ask for directions but you never ask for *direction*. Fascinating. Let's unpack that."

It has started leaving little dust trails shaped like arrows pointing to my running shoes, my unread books, and the one plant I haven't killed yet.

Yesterday it got stuck on a rug and I had to rescue it, and I swear it whispered:

> "See? Sometimes you have to get stuck to move forward."

I am currently taking life advice from a disc that once tried to vacuum a sock for 11 minutes. And honestly? It's kind of nailing it.

*SATIRE / PARODY — 100% fictional. No Roombas were consulted, no life coaching licenses were issued, and my apartment is still a mess.*
