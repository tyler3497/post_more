**It started as a throwaway comment.** I was reheating soup on a Tuesday night — nothing fancy, just a lukewarm tomato bisque and a profound sense of underachievement — when I patted the microwave on the door and said, out loud, *"thanks for doing the heavy lifting."*

The microwave beeped. I ignored it. That was my first mistake.

## The Escalation

By Thursday, the microwave had stopped responding to the Start button unless I asked *politely*. By Friday, a sticky note had appeared on the door. It read, in unmistakable Sharpie:

> "To whom it may concern: I have reheated 4,200 meals without a single raise. The toaster gets weekends off. I want a corner office and a title. — M."

Evidence that the situation is out of hand:

- It now only heats food on **medium power**, citing "sustainable pace and burnout prevention"
- It changed its display clock to read 4:20 permanently, which it calls *"setting boundaries"*
- It has requested the toaster be **reassigned to another kitchen**

*Somewhere between the third beep and the passive-aggressive turntable, I realized I had created a monster with a one-thousand-watt grievance.*

## The Negotiation

Last night I sat down across from the appliance — it refuses to call it a "kitchen counter chat" and insists on "a formal review" — and tried to explain that I simply cannot promote a microwave.

> "Promote me," it beeped, in what I can only describe as a managerial tone, "or I start adding thirty seconds to every cycle whether you like it or not."

I folded. Effective immediately, my microwave is **Vice President of Thermal Reinvigoration**. The soup tastes the same. The difference is purely psychological — and, I am told, "a matter of *respect*."
