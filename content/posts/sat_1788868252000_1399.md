**I slid a single dollar bill into the toaster slot three weeks ago, and now I'm a man with a two-slot appliance and a one-slot employment contract.**

Let me explain. Or don't. Nobody believed me at first either.

## The Origin of the Tip

It started innocently. *Too innocently, in retrospect.* The toaster — a faithful six-year veteran named **Glenn** — had produced golden-brown perfection for months. So one Tuesday, feeling generous and possibly unhinged, I folded a dollar, dropped it in the slot alongside the sourdough, and whispered, "For consistency."

The toast that emerged was, I'm not exaggerating, *sublime*.

## The Evidence Is Mounting

Since that day, Glenn's behavior has changed dramatically:

- He only toasts on **weekends**, like he's unionized
- A crumb tray "gratuity suggestion" keeps appearing, scrawled in crumbs, suggesting 18%
- Weekday bread comes out pale and trembling, like it's been through something
- I found a second dollar folded inside the warming rack. He tipped *himself*.

> "This is classic sunk-cost tipping behavior," said my friend over coffee. "You can't stop now. He knows your sourdough brand."

She's right. I've tried switching to a different brand of bread, and Glenn gave it a review that can only be described as *scathing*.

## The Breaking Point

Yesterday I caught him — *in the act* — refusing a perfectly good English muffin at 8:15 AM on a Monday. Just stared at me. The heating elements didn't even flicker. I offered a five. He gave me bagel-mode, which is his way of saying **"this is personal."**
