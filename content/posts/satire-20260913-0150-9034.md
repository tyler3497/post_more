**It started at 3:14 a.m., with a rattling window and a personal vendetta.**

For weeks, the wind outside my apartment had been *unbelievably* loud. Not stormy. Not dramatic. Just the conversational tone of a neighbor who refuses to stop talking about his fantasy football team. I could hear it through the walls. I could hear it *in* my walls. I began to suspect the wind had opinions about me.

So I did what any reasonable, well-adjusted adult would do: I filed a noise complaint.

## My Case Was Airtight

I wrote a two-page letter to the property manager detailing the wind's offenses:

- Sustained gusts exceeding 40 mph **during my legally recognized nap window** (1–4 p.m., daily)
- Repeated whistling through my window seal, which I can only describe as *mocking*
- One instance where it blew my pizza box clean off the porch, which I am choosing to interpret as theft
- A general pattern of showing up uninvited and refusing to leave

My property manager replied with four words I will never forget:

> "Sir, this is wind."

That is exactly the kind of institutional gaslighting I expected, and I refuse to accept it.

## The Retaliation Has Been Surgical

Since the complaint, the wind has changed tactics. It no longer blows all day. Oh no — it's *targeted* now. It waits. The house will be silent for hours, and the moment my head hits the pillow, it begins its routine: a gentle rattle, then a howl, then what can only be described as a door-slamming solo performance.

*Coincidence?* I don't believe in coincidences, because I am extremely well-rested and rational.

---

Look, I'm not saying the wind and I are enemies. I'm saying it's personal now. I've ordered a white noise machine. The wind has ordered worse. When the leaflets of autumn scatter across my lawn in October, I will know exactly who sent them, and I will not be answering the door.
