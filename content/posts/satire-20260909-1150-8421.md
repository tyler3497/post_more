**I am writing this from the parking lot of a laundromat because I am fairly certain my front porch is being watched.**

It started Tuesday. My phone died, I was two streets from home, and sitting on the fence was my neighbor's enormous orange cat, watching me the way a bouncer watches a wallet chain. So I asked him for directions. A normal, polite human interaction. He stared at me for nine full seconds, stood up, and pointed down the block with one paw.

I followed. I don't know why. *Some animals simply project authority.*

## What happened next is extremely documented

- The cat led me three blocks in a precise grid pattern, pausing at each crosswalk to scan both directions
- At the destination — a vacant lot with exactly one lawn chair — he sat down and looked at me expectantly
- When I offered him a granola bar out of my pocket, he pushed it off the chair without breaking eye contact

Then came the note. Tucked under the lawn chair was a slip of paper that read only:

> *You owe us one. — The Management*

Whose management? Of what? **The cat blinked twice, stood up, and left.** He did not look back. Professionals never do.

## My current situation

Since Tuesday, I have noticed:

- All the neighborhood cats are sitting on my fence in a perfect line at 6:40 p.m., facing my house, like a tiny furry city council
- My doorbell camera caught the orange cat delivering what appears to be a tiny manila envelope to a squirrel
- Last night someone — *something* — rearranged my patio furniture into a perfect circle

I don't know what favor I owe, and I cannot identify the organization, but this morning I found a sticky note on my front door that said: *"Tuesday. Bring tuna. No further questions."*

I am bringing the tuna. **I am not asking further questions.**
