**I thought a little accountability would improve kitchen morale.**

*Which is how I ended up in a one-on-one with a $1.99 sponge at 7:30 a.m. on a Tuesday.*

## The Annual Review

It started because the sponge was leaving crumbs on the counter as, quote, "a creative choice." I printed a tiny performance review template. Very professional. Very unhinged. My roommate asked if I was okay. I said this was about *operational excellence*.

The categories were rigorous:

- **Attendance:** Present, but emotionally elsewhere since March
- **Initiative:** Refuses to tackle baked-on cheese without a motivational speech and maybe a snack
- **Teamwork:** Has ongoing beef with the dish towel, won't elaborate, says it's "complicated"
- **Innovation:** Invented a new way to smell faintly of regret and lemon

> "Frankly, I feel micromanaged," said SpongeBob No-Pants, as I've decided to call him. "You soak me, you wring me, you ask me to confront a lasagna pan that has seen things. Where's my PTO? Where's my growth opportunity?"

I offered constructive feedback. He absorbed it. Literally. That's his whole thing. That's his power move.

### The Quiet Quitting Phase

Now he just sits in the sink, half-submerged, staring into the middle distance like he's in a Noah Baumbach film about burnout.

His LinkedIn (which I did not make for him, *okay maybe I did*) says:

- Open to work: emotional support, light dusting, low-stakes spills only
- Not open to: casserole dishes, 2019 chili residue, "aggressive scrubbing culture"

The metrics don't lie:

- `performance.score = undefined // cannot quantify existential dread`
- Motivation: 2/10
- Moisture retention: 11/10, unfortunately

---

**The worst part is, the dishes have noticed.** The mugs are forming a union. The fork asked if we have an EAP. The frying pan is doing that thing where it sighs loudly when I walk in.

I tried to apologize. I put out a tiny saucer of fresh water and whispered, "We value you. You're essential to our mission."

He bubbled once. Sarcastically. Then went back to floating like a tiny, damp middle manager who has given up.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This is entirely fictional, no sponges were subjected to corporate performance management, no kitchenware was emotionally harmed, I'm just a person who should not be allowed to have performance review templates at home.*
