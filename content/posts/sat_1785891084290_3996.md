## The Dream Was Simple

**I wanted to be the kind of person who has birds.** Not owns them, just... *vibes* with them. You know, the mysterious neighbor who drinks tea on the balcony and can tell a chickadee from a sparrow on sight.

I bought a $19.99 feeder from Target, filled it with *premium* seeds, and hung it on my balcony last Tuesday. I imagined gentle chirping. Soft moments. Possibly being featured in a local newsletter titled **"Man Finds Peace."**

Within 48 hours, the pigeons found it.

### Enter The Management

They didn't eat. They *assessed*. Three of them landed in formation, all wearing the same expression my landlord uses when I pay rent in unrolled pennies.

The biggest one — I've named him **Tony Feathers** — hopped onto the railing, stared directly through my kitchen window, and left a single sunflower seed on the sill like a business card.

> "Nice feeder you got here. Be a shame if something... happened to it. Say, pecked at it. Repeatedly. At 5 a.m."

I thought I was imagining it. *I was not.*

By Wednesday they had established protocol:

- Daily tribute increased from "small scoop of seeds" to "entire loaf of artisanal sourdough, torn into respectful cubes"
- I'm not allowed to feed the cute little finches without prior written approval in triplicate
- My balcony chair now permanently belongs to Tony's cousin Vinny who just *sits there, judging*

I tried to negotiate. I said *I'm basically providing ecosystem services*.

> "Ecosystem?" Tony cooed, then laughed in a way that made my succulents file for emotional damage. "Kid, we ARE the ecosystem. You just pay rent to live near *our* feeder now."

---

### Current Status Update

**I agreed to:**
- Put seeds out at 7 a.m. sharp, regardless of weather or dignity
- Pretend I don't see them shaking down the sparrows for trail mix
- Apologize to Vinny when I accidentally make eye contact

`status: under_feathered_surveillance && morale: questionable_but_compliant`

My neighbor asked why I salute the feeder before watering my plants. I told her it's *bird culture*. She is now distancing herself, wisely.

I whisper to the feeder at night: *was this worth trying to become a nature person?*

Tony, from the railing at 3 a.m., says yes. For him, definitely yes.
