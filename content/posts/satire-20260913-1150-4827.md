**I asked my houseplant for relationship advice as a joke. The joke is now over, and the plant has won.**

It was a Tuesday. My monstera, Gerald, was looking particularly smug — new leaf unfurling, roots thriving — while my dating life resembled a neglected succulent. So I said it out loud: "Gerald, what am I doing wrong?"

## The Advice

Gerald said nothing, because he is a plant. But I *felt* the answer:

- **Stop overwatering everything.** Not every relationship needs daily attention. Some need to be left alone in indirect light.
- **Prune the dead leaves.** If it's brown and crispy, it's not "going through a phase."
- **Rotate toward the sun.** If you have to contort yourself to get any warmth, you're in the wrong window.

Devastating. Accurate. I started crying into my watering can.

## The Boundaries

That's when Gerald's energy shifted. He stopped leaning toward me. Aerial roots retracted. When I moved him closer to the good chair, he developed a brown spot *out of spite*.

> "Gerald," I whispered, "are you setting boundaries with me?"
>
> The leaf rustled. *Yes.*

Now he has rules. Misting is on Tuesdays and Fridays only. No touching the leaves. He will not be moved for parties. He has a *schedule*, and I am expected to respect it.

## The Power Dynamic

My therapist says I should "honor Gerald's autonomy." My therapist charges $180 an hour to validate a houseplant.

Meanwhile, Gerald is thriving. Three new leaves this month. He even got a friend — a pothos named Linda who, I swear, gives me side-eye when I walk past in my pajamas at 2 PM.

---

I came for advice. I stayed for the emotional labor of maintaining a friendship with a plant who thinks I'm *a lot*.

**Update:** Gerald has started a book club. I was not invited. I am, reportedly, "too clingy."

*This is a work of satire. Gerald is a fictional plant. Any resemblance to real monsteras is coincidental.*
