**I just wanted to save money and not eat cereal for dinner three nights in a row.**

It sounded so simple. So *mature*. So Pinterest-board-with-a-water-mark.

On Sunday, I became a Meal Prep Person. I bought 14 identical containers, quinoa in a quantity usually reserved for livestock, and a motivational sense of purpose that expired by Monday at 2pm.

## The Plan That Seemed Responsible

My system was foolproof, I told myself, which is always a great sign:

- Cook all chicken on Sunday so it achieves the texture of a yoga mat by Wednesday
- Make one giant vat of roasted vegetables that slowly becomes a soup, then a stew, then a concept
- Label everything with optimistic lies like `ENJOY :)`

By Tuesday, my fridge looked like a lab experiment in beige.

> "We are filing a class-action lawsuit on behalf of Future You vs. Past You," said my future self, wearing a tiny lawyer suit made of aluminum foil. "Count one: intentional infliction of lukewarm chicken. Count two: you made us eat the same garlic broccoli five times and called it 'self-discipline.'"

### The Evidence Is... Extensive

The prosecution presented Exhibit A through Q:

- **Wednesday's lunch** that stared back at me
- The sad little container of hummus I packed as a *treat* like I was disciplining a toddler
- My 3pm realization that I would rather be hungry than microwave quinoa again

I tried to reason with myself:

```
if (meal.prepped == true && motivation == 0) {
  order_pizza();
  pretend_this_was_the_plan();
}
```

---

Look, I get it. Past Me meant well. Future Me is dramatic and currently living off containers that all smell faintly of cumin regardless of contents.

We settled out of court. Terms: I am legally required to eat one fresh meal this week and stop pretending that 8 ounces of dry rice is a personality.

The containers are still in the fridge. They know what they did.
