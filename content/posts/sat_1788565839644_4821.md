**I finally decided to let democracy into my laundry room, which was my first mistake.**

*This is satire — completely fictional, but my socks have better time management than I ever will.*

For six weeks, the pile in the corner has been growing. Not aggressively. Patiently. Like a tiny textile government watching me re-wear the same hoodie because "it's still good."

So I thought, why not ask the pile what it wants? Leadership exercise.

## How My Laundry Staged a Coup

I dumped it all on the bed and held a town hall. The jeans were moderate. The hoodies abstained. The socks — oh, the socks — had *opinions*.

> "We've been underfoot — literally — for three years and you've never once let us choose the itinerary," said one sock, who I now realize I've been calling Steve. "We demand representation. And fabric softener."
> — Steve, left sock, suddenly very unionized

They voted 14-2 to take over my weekend.

### My Weekend, According to Sock Law:

- **Saturday 9 AM:** "Mandatory Fold & Reflection" — 90 minutes of me apologizing to a fitted sheet
- **Sunday 2 PM:** No brunch, because "jeans need airing out and emotional space"
- **All-day Sunday:** A calendar block titled `Don't You Dare Wear Us Hiking Again Without Asking`

I tried to override them with `sudo wear-whatever-i-want`, but my closet now requires two-factor authentication:

```
if (socks.approval == false && human.confidence < 0.4) {
  denyAccess(cardigan);
  scheduleGuiltTrip();
}
```

---

I checked my Google Calendar this morning and my socks had added a recurring event: **"Strategic Sock Sync — Bring Snacks (for us, not you)"** with a Zoom link that just opens my dryer.

*SATIRE / PARODY — 100% made up, clearly absurd. No laundry was consulted in the making of this, no socks were given executive power, and I am still the one who decides if I wear mismatched pairs.*

If you need me, I'll be negotiating PTO with my own underwear drawer.
