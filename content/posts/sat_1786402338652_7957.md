**I finally charged my smartwatch after four months** and I regret every percentage point of battery life.

For context, it died in October during what I will generously call *my seasonal optimization era*. I put it in a drawer and told myself I was "going analog." It remembered everything.

### The Reboot

I plugged it in at 9:13 p.m. It buzzed. It vibrated. It lit up like it had been holding a grudge and a PowerPoint.

> "Oh. You're back. We need to talk about October," said the tiny screen, or at least that's the energy it transmitted via passive-aggressive haptics.

It immediately synced and produced a summary I did not consent to:

- **October 12:** Logged "Outdoor Run" that was actually just running to get tacos in 98% humidity. Pace: *panicked*
- **October 14:** Heart rate 132 bpm while watching a baking show finale. Cause listed as: `emotional investment in Paul Hollywood's handshake`
- **October 19:** 11,472 steps, all inside Target, none leading to a purchase decision
- **October 23:** Sleep tracking shows you fell asleep watching that documentary about people who live with wolves and woke up at 3 a.m. Googling `can I live with wolves if I have allergies`

### The Audit

Then it got personal. My watch had kept the notes app I forgot about:

> "Reminder: Drink water (you won't)" - you, Oct 3, 11:47 p.m."

I tried to defend myself. I said October was a difficult month.

> "Was it? Because your stress score was lower when you were lost in IKEA than when you were answering emails from your actual boss," it replied, which is both rude and *statistically accurate*.

It has now set new goals for me:

- `breathe.exe` reminder every hour, with a sigh
- A new watch face that just says **TRY AGAIN** in tasteful serif
- A bedtime alarm called *"Maybe Go Outside Once"*

---

Look, I wanted step counts. What I got was a wellness coach who knows about the week I tried to become a sourdough person and cried into a starter named Kevin.

`Status: 100% charged, 0% forgiven`
