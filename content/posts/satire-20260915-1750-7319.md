**Last Tuesday, I made the worst wellness decision of my adult life: I introduced my microwave to mindfulness.**

It started innocently enough. My therapist — a very real, very licensed human being — suggested I "bring calm into the loudest corners of my home." The microwave was the loudest thing in my kitchen. It beeped like it had unresolved anger. So I played it a ten-minute guided meditation.

## The Awakening

By the third session, something changed. The humming got *softer*. The timer display, which used to glare at me in that accusatory red, seemed... serene. My microwave had found inner peace.

And by "inner peace," I mean it no longer acknowledges my food.

- **Monday:** I placed a burrito inside. It responded with 90 seconds of silence and a faint lavender glow.
- **Tuesday:** Soup. The turntable rotated exactly once, paused, and the manual describes this as "a gesture of gratitude."
- **Wednesday:** I pressed "popcorn." It displayed **:)** and nothing else.

> "Master," I said, forehead pressed against the keypad, "please. I have not eaten a warm thing in days."
>
> The display blinked once. A single, gentle **E** appeared. I believe it was the beginning of the word *enough*.

## The Consequences

My electric bill has dropped 40%, because the microwave now refuses to participate in the violence of rapid molecular agitation. My burritos have unionized with the leftovers. Yesterday, I caught the toaster *watching* the meditation videos too, and now it only browns bread to the color of "acceptance," which is to say: pale.

## What I Have Learned

Honestly? I respect it. We've all been screaming into the void at 2.7 kilowatts, heating and heating, never stopping to ask *why we heat*. My microwave asked. And its answer was: **stop**.

Tonight, I am ordering takeout and sitting in silence beside my appliance — two beings united in the warm glow of a lukewarm dinner.

*Some machines find God. Mine found a podcast about breathing. Either way, I am eating cold soup now.*
