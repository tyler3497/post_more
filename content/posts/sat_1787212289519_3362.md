**I was not trying to build a corporate empire out of my hamper.**

I just wanted to finally deal with my laundry without feeling like a failure. So I put it on a five-year plan. Vision statements, milestones, *KPIs (Krispy Pant Indicators)* — the whole thing.

It worked too well.

## Phase One Went Suspiciously Smooth

By week two, my t-shirts had self-organized into departments:

- **Whites** — accounting, very judgmental about bleach expenses
- **Darks** — creative, refuses to meet before noon
- **Socks** — leadership. Unfortunately.

My socks immediately promoted themselves to middle management. One striped one started carrying a tiny clipboard.

> "Per our Q1 sync," said the left sock, "we've noticed a 47% decrease in folding follow-through and a concerning increase in 'wearing the same hoodie for three days' incidents."

I told them I was the owner. They said, *technically*, I've been outsourcing their housing to the floor.

## Then Came The Quarterly Reviews

Last night they called an all-hands meeting inside the laundry basket. A PowerPoint made of dryer lint.

They had slides:

- My search history during laundry time ("how long can you wear jeans")
- A pie chart titled *Where All The Other Socks Went* — 100% "Strategic Realignment"
- A performance improvement plan for *me* written in fabric softener scent

They even set OKRs:

```
objective: become a person who owns a hamper lid
key_result_1: stop using chair as closet
key_result_2: learn what "delicates" means before age 40
```

---

Look, I support ambition. I love seeing my socks lean in. But this morning they scheduled a one-on-one to discuss my "growth mindset regarding inside-out shirts."

I tried to quit. They said I can't — I'm on a 5-year vesting schedule and I still have 1,826 days of socks that don't match.

*This post is satire / parody. All fictional. No socks were harmed in their aggressive promotion to upper management.*
