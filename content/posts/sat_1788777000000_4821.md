**It started with the lasagna. I called him Garfield.**

Now he's the lead plaintiff in the largest food-rights case my kitchen has ever seen, and frankly, I'm rooting for him.

It all began when I read some wellness blog that said naming your food makes you "waste less." *A lovely idea, if your name means nothing to a week-old quesadilla.* Within days, my fridge had become a small, tense republic with extremely strong feelings about shelf placement.

## The Demands

The leftovers — represented by Garfield, a tub of potato salad calling herself *Bridget*, and a mysterious Tupperware of chili simply known as The General — have presented me with a list of grievances:

- The crisper drawer is a "humidity-based detention facility"
- The back of the fridge is, quote, "where hope goes to freeze"
- The shelf above the deli meat has better lighting and everyone knows it
- They want a union rep and a designated snacks mediator

> "I don't want to be eaten," Bridget said through a cracked lid, "but if I'm going to be eaten, I demand to be eaten *with dignity.*"

## The Turning Point

Things escalated when I ate the garlic bread without warning. It had a name. *His name was Kevin.* I had labeled the bag with masking tape and everything. The fridge went silent for two full days — the kind of silence that hums at 40 degrees Fahrenheit.

Now the door alarm has started beeping in what I can only describe as Morse code for "we know what you did."

---

**Am I the defendant, or the landlord?** The General released a statement (written in mustard on a paper towel) that I am both, and that *the crisper drawer will be liquidated by the weekend.*

I've hired a friend's cousin who once took a business law class to look into it. He says I might actually be liable, "ethically, if not legally." Garfield has retained no counsel — he says the *truth* is his counsel.

Honestly? I respect it. I never respected my leftovers this much when they were silent. I'll be appearing in small-claims court on Tuesday, representing myself and my fridge.
