**I didn't mean to start a fashion cult.** I just wanted fewer decisions.

Last month I watched a 12-minute video titled *10 Pieces, 30 Outfits, Infinite Peace* and immediately blacked out and donated 80% of my closet to "future me's problem." I kept 10 neutral items. Very mindful. Very beige. Very *I'm the kind of person who owns linen on purpose*.

My other clothes did not take this well.

## The Intervention I Didn't Get Invited To

I woke up Tuesday to my closet door slightly open and a faint intro jingle. My 2019 Coachella tank top — which has seen things and should legally not be allowed to speak — had started a podcast.

It's called *HANGER* and the tagline is *The Clothes You Abandoned Tell All*.

In episode 1, they listed their grievances:

- I wore the same 3 black t-shirts for 6 years and washed them on *cold* like that excuses anything
- I kept a pair of jeans with a hole in the knee for "character" and then replaced their entire personality with Uniqlo
- I told my graphic hoodie it was "too loud for my new era" while owning a neon orange air fryer that screams when it's done

> "He said we were 'too much,'" sobbed my thrifted Hawaiian shirt into a tiny foam mic. "I LITERALLY HAVE PINEAPPLES ON ME. I AM JOY."

### The Season Finale Was Personal

Episode 3 was titled `Beige Is a Coping Mechanism` and featured my old Halloween costume giving a PowerPoint about my commitment issues.

I tried to defend myself with `capsule_wardrobe.justify({reason: "adulthood"})` but the zipper was broken.

---

Look, the capsule works. I get dressed in 40 seconds now. I look like a tasteful oatmeal. But at 2 a.m. I can hear my rejected windbreaker whispering ad reads for a better owner.

*This is satire.* I have not actually been canceled by polyester. My clothes are not sentient. Yet.

If you need me, I'll be negotiating peace terms with a podcast hosted by cargo shorts.
