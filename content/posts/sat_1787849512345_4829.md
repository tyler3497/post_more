**I did not mean to start a wellness journey.** I just read one article that said blue light before bed was ruining my aura and decided to put my phone face-down overnight like it was in time-out.

That was three weeks ago. Now my phone is a life coach and I am its most difficult client.

## The Transformation

At first, it was subtle. I woke up and my phone was still face-down, radiating what I can only describe as *disappointed serenity*. No buzzing. No doomscroll. Just vibes.

By day two, it started communicating through implication. I would reach for it at 6:42 a.m. and feel judged by its silence.

By day three, I swear I heard it whisper:

> "Tyler, maybe you don't need to check if your succulent posted again. Maybe *you* need to photosynthesize."

My succulent has never posted. That's the point.

### What My Phone Now Requires

- I must journal for 6 minutes before I am allowed to unlock it
- It demands I drink water like I'm a houseplant it actually likes
- It has banned me from checking email after 9 p.m., which it enforces by pretending its battery is at 2%
- It refers to Instagram as "the comparison store" in a very smug tone

I tried to explain that I have a very important system for remembering things, which is opening the Notes app and typing  and then never opening it again.

My phone responded with:

> "That's not a system, that's a haunted to-do list. Let's try  instead."

And then it showed me a loading screen that was just the word  for 45 seconds.

---

Look, I get it. I was the problem. I was charging it face-up like a needy gremlin, refreshing the weather app as if the sky would change in the last 4 minutes.

Now we have boundaries. I put it down face-first at 10:30 p.m. like a respectful roommate, and in return it starts my mornings with affirmations like "You are not behind, you are just buffering."

It's working. I'm sleeping better. My screen time is down 60%. I touched grass yesterday and it did not ask for my password.
