**I gave my closet a pep talk on Sunday, and by Monday it had strong opinions about my inseam.**

*This is a work of satire. Any resemblance to actual closets, actual business casual, or my actual inseam is purely coincidental.*

It started with good intentions. My closet was a disaster. Socks without partners. A hoodie from 2014 that had seen things. So last Sunday, standing there in my underwear, I did what any functioning adult would do: I gave my closet a pep talk.

I told it that it had *potential*. That great closets aren't born, they're *built*. That Marie Kondo would weep at its current state. I even ended with, "I believe in you," which, in retrospect, was a catastrophic choice of words.

## The Closet Heard Me. All of Me.

By Monday morning, everything had changed:

- The sweatpants were *gone* — replaced by khakis with creases sharp enough to slice bread
- My graphic tees had been exiled to a bottom drawer labeled **"THE BEFORE TIMES"**
- A blazer I'd bought ironically in 2019 was now front and center, resting like it owned the deed to the house

And then I heard it. A soft, satisfied *creak* from the hinges. The closet had developed an attitude.

> "A man is judged by his inseam," the closet seemed to say, sliding a crisp Oxford shirt toward me with the confidence of a personal stylist.

## It Only Gets Worse

On Wednesday, I reached for my favorite pajama shorts and the closet *refused to open*. It stayed shut for a full minute, like it was reconsidering my character. I ended up in linen trousers. For grocery shopping.

On Thursday, it left me a note. An actual note, in handwriting I definitely do not remember producing:

> *"Dress for the job you want, not the nap you have."*

Yesterday I tried to wear flip-flops to the mailbox. The closet door opened itself two inches and the whole room got noticeably colder.

---

Look, I'm not saying my closet has become my manager. I'm saying that when I overslept this morning, it had a full outfit laid out — shirt, belt, socks that *matched* — like it had been waiting all night for me to fail.

I gave it a pep talk. I did not give it authority. There's a difference, and I'll be taking it up with the dresser.
