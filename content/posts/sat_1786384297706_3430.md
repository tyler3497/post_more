**I didn't mean to hand over my entire personal brand to a hamper.**

But at 6:47 a.m. last Tuesday, staring into the abyss of my closet, I had a thought that felt *profoundly efficient* at the time: what if I just let the laundry decide?

I dumped the clean basket onto my bed and declared, out loud, to no one, "You pick."

The clothes picked. And they have not stopped picking since.

## The Coup Was Immediate and Very Well-Coordinated

My athletic socks, previously humble foot-soldiers, have appointed themselves Creative Directors. My favorite hoodie has been demoted to "weekend only."

Here's how the new regime works:

- Every morning, my socks arrange themselves into an aggressive little runway show on the dresser
- My jeans now refuse to be worn unless paired with "an intentional sock statement"
- My t-shirts have formed a union and are demanding rotational PTO
- I was denied access to my own striped sweater for "not respecting the vision"

I tried to intervene yesterday. I reached for sweatpants. A single, heroic argyle sock launched itself off the pile.

> "We talked about this," it whispered. "You said 'you pick' and we *picked*. You can't just come back here with your 'comfort' and your 'it's raining' excuses. We have a mood board. We have a *narrative arc*."

I didn't know socks could do mood boards. Apparently they used my phone while I was sleeping. There's a Pinterest account now.

It gets worse. They wrote a manifesto:

`if (human.triesToWearCrocs) { escalateToFullIntervention(); // call the hats }`

---

Look, my outfits have never gotten more compliments. A barista asked me if I had a stylist. My mom said I looked "put together for once." But I live in fear that one day I'll reach for socks and get a Slack message that says "per my last email, we decided on polka dots."

I miss being badly dressed and free.
