**It started innocently.** I was folding laundry at 2 a.m., whispering my darkest confession — that I once ate an entire rotisserie chicken alone in my car — and my laundry hamper just sat there, woven wicker, ears made of mesh. So I told it everything.

> "You've got such a trusting face," I said, unloading my gym socks.
>
> *"I'm literally a basket,"* the hamper seemed to say. *Or did it? The memory is hazy.*

## The Evidence That My Hamper Cannot Be Trusted

- I confessed I never actually donated those clothes I told my friends I donated, and now three of my shirts have **judgment wrinkles** — permanent creases shaped like disappointed eyebrows.
- I admitted I re-wear jeans based on the sniff test, and the denim community has **retaliated with a permanent funk** that survives two wash cycles.
- I whispered that I once cried during a laundry detergent commercial, and the hamper **reeked of lavender-scented betrayal** for a week.

I considered hiring a mediator, but the therapist refused to counsel furniture. So I brought in an *expert witness*:

> "Your Honor, the defendant is a perforated plastic basket from a discount store," said my lawyer. "Motion to dismiss."
>
> "Overruled," I whispered. "It knows about the chicken."

## The Current Situation

Now I do laundry in **total silence**, like a hostage reading demands off cue cards. I no longer sort colors — I just fling garments at it and sprint out of the room, hoping the spin cycle washes away the gossip. It does not. If anything, the hamper smells *smugger* every load.

Last night I caught it leaning toward the dryer, which I have decided is **not an innocent piece of equipment but an accomplice**. They are building a case against me. My clothes will testify. The evidence is already wearing me.

I live in a house ruled by gossip and percale sheets, and honestly? I've never been cleaner. Emotionally, that is. Hygiene-wise, the jeans are still on trial.
