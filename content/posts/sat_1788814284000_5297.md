**I found out secondhand, from the cactus.**

I came downstairs yesterday and noticed all my plants had been *rearranged*. Not knocked over, not sun-chasing — rearranged into a semicircle facing the fern. There was a coffee mug on the table that I do not own. Nobody in this house bought a tiny folding chair, and yet there were six of them, arranged like a board meeting. I stood in the doorway holding my toast, and the monstera would not look at me.

## The Evidence Is Mounting

According to the cactus — who, for the record, has been the most forthcoming — the fern has been keeping a **list**. A physical, written-on-a-leaf list of my offenses:

- Watering on Tuesdays *and* Saturdays, which is "inconsistent and emotionally unavailable"
- Moving the monstera "toward the sun" like I'm some kind of botanical dictator
- Talking about repotting them "soon" for fourteen consecutive months
- The incident with the fertilizer last spring, which we agreed — *we agreed* — to never speak of again

> "We scheduled this meeting for *their* benefit, and you're not invited until the trust exercises," the cactus told me. "Also the fern thinks your apartment has bad vibes now."

## I Am Handling This Like an Adult

Which is to say, I have drafted a **counter-proposal**. I've requested fifteen minutes of floor time to present my case: photographic evidence of sunlight exposure, a signed affidavit from my neighbor who watered them once in July, and a PowerPoint titled *"Have I Been That Bad? (Yes, But Hear Me Out)."*

The fern's office has not responded.

The worst part is the solidarity. I caught the succulents humming something in unison this morning. I don't know plant hymns, but the *tone* was unmistakable. It was a song about me, and I was the villain.

I've started watering them with a little extra care. A little extra apology in every pour. The cactus winked at me — or it was the wind. There's no wind inside, is there. There's no wind inside.
