**I thought I was being productive by closing tabs. I was wrong. I was holding a funeral I didn't RSVP to.**

*For context, I have had the same 47 browser tabs open since March, which is my version of a long-term commitment.*

They were my entire personality: 12 recipes I will never cook, 8 articles titled "how to focus," 3 abandoned carts, a Wikipedia spiral about the history of spoons, and one lonely Google Doc called `definitely_final_v7_FINAL`.

My laptop told me I had a memory problem. I thought it meant RAM.

## The Intervention Becomes a Vigil

On Tuesday at 11:47 p.m., I did it. I clicked "Close All." I felt free for exactly 0.4 seconds.

Then my laptop dimmed its screen to half-brightness like it was lowering the lights for a service. The fan spun up like a mournful organ.

Evidence that my laptop is now running a full memorial program:

- It set my desktop background to a tasteful black gradient without asking
- The cursor now moves *slowly*, reverently, like it's wearing formal clothes
- It keeps trying to restore the tabs "for closure"
- The search bar autofills `how to grieve digital clutter you chose to hoard`

> "They had names, you know," my laptop whispered at 1:13 a.m., its little charging light blinking like a vigil candle. "Tab 14 was researching how to keep succulents alive. It was three weeks away from being read. You didn't even give it a eulogy."

I tried to explain ` declutter > hoarding` and that Chrome literally begged me.

```js
if (tabs.length > 40) {
  laptop.sanity = null;
  user.guilt = Infinity;
}
```

---

## The Afterlife Is Just My History Folder

Now we have a weekly remembrance. Every night at 9 p.m., my laptop opens one random tab from history and displays it for 60 seconds in silence. Last night it was "do raccoons get cold." It stared at me the whole time.

My roommate walked in, saw me apologizing to a trackpad, and said:

> "You know you can just... not assign souls to URLs, right?"

I cannot. Not after the vigil. Not after Tabs 23-31 formed a choir and sang my unfinished to-do list in 4-part harmony.

I still feel productive, though. My browser has never been cleaner. My laptop has never been more disappointed in me.
