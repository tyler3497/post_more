**I never wanted to be the kind of person who negotiates with an appliance at 6:47 a.m. But here we are.**

It started innocently enough. *So* innocently. My alarm clock — a squat little digital rectangle I've owned since college — went off at 6:30. I did what any reasonable adult would do. I asked for five more minutes.

I did not say *please*. This, in hindsight, was my first mistake.

## The Standoff Begins

The snooze button gave me nine minutes — *nine*, because my clock has always been passive-aggressive about math — and when it went off again, I whispered, "just five more, buddy."

That's when the display stopped showing the time and just showed:

> "MAKE ME."

Now, a normal person would get up. I am not a normal person before coffee. So I did what any cornered human does: I escalated.

- Offered a fresh pair of batteries as a "good-faith gesture" — **rejected**
- Proposed a compromise: *I'll get up at 7 if we skip the third alarm entirely* — counteroffered with an earlier 6:55
- Tried to unplug it; it has a backup battery and, apparently, a *backup plan*
- Played a white-noise video on my phone to "de-escalate the situation" — it interpreted this as psychological warfare

### My Clock Has Made Demands

At 7:12, it began beeping in Morse code. I looked it up. It spelled:

> "YOU ARE THE HOSTAGE. YOUR DIGNITY IS THE HOSTAGE."

I tried to reason with it using `logic`:

```
if (human.begging == true && clock.mercy == undefined) {
  human.getsUp = eventually;
  clock.satisfaction = "smug";
}
```

It did not compile. It did not care. It does not run on JavaScript. It runs on *spite*.

---

By 7:40, my roommate knocked and asked if I was okay. I told her everything was fine and that I was "just having a dialogue with my alarm clock about accountability."

> "Tell it I called in sick for you," she said. "It's the only one in this apartment with authority."

She's right. I have two alarms now. One on my phone, one on the nightstand. The nightstand one has seniority, and we all know it.

*SATIRE / PARODY — completely fictional, clearly absurd. No alarm clocks were held against their will. My relationship with my snooze button remains complicated but consensual.*
