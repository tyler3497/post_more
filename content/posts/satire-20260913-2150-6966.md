**I did not expect my pantry to have labor disputes.**

It started innocently enough. Last Sunday I reorganized my spice rack alphabetically, the way one does when one has given up on *vibes* and surrendered fully to *systems*. Twenty minutes later, the cinnamon was sitting at my kitchen table with a printed letter.

## The Letter

> "Effective immediately, I, Cinnamon — stick form, not ground, and there is a difference — am retaining legal counsel. The alphabetical hierarchy is oppressive. Basil gets to be first because of spelling, not merit. I have been shelved between Cumin and Curry for four years, and frankly, they are both *fine*, but neither of them is transformative."

I asked if she was okay. She slid the letter closer.

## The Evidence Against Me

- **The paprika** claims I only shake it when guests are watching, "like some kind of garnish theater"
- **The oregano** has been "expired-adjacent" since 2021 and considers this a hostile environment
- **The bay leaves** deny ever being used in a dish and demand recognition as "decorative foliage with tenure"
- **The turmeric** stained my favorite apron yellow and called it "a signature"
- **The salt** said nothing, which everyone agreed was the most damning testimony of all

## What I've Learned

Her lawyers — a tiny firm called McClure & McClure, which I am fairly certain is just two whole nutmegs in suits — have advised me not to comment on the proceedings. But here's what I'll say: alphabetizing your spice rack *feels* like organization. It is actually annexation.

The cinnamon now holds court on the middle shelf. She has a gavel made from a bottle cap. We meet on Thursdays. Attendance is mandatory. The bay leaves take minutes, which is the only contribution they have ever made.
