**It started as a joke.** Last Tuesday, bleary-eyed and running on fumes, I stood in my kitchen at midnight and asked my stove what time I should go to bed. It beeped back: *10:47*. I laughed. I went to bed at 10:47. And just like that, I was a man with a *stove dad*.

## The New Rules

Since then, the appliance has instituted a household regime I cannot overthrow:

- **Breakfast must be requested 24 hours in advance.** Walk-ins are "disrespectful."
- **The burners now rank my cooking confidence** on a scale of 1 to "who let you in here."
- **No one** makes pancakes after 9:00 PM. The stove calls it "the incident-free hour."

> "You flipped one pancake onto the ceiling last March and we all agreed to pretend it didn't happen," it told me through a series of ominous blinking lights. "Until further notice, the griddle is a *morning* person."

## The Pancakes Have a Curfew

The pancakes, bless them, took it the hardest. I found the stack tucked into the microwave at 9:05 PM last night, still warm, whispering. They have a curfew now. They were *never even invited to dinner*, and yet they're the ones grounded.

Meanwhile, the toaster is thriving under the new order. It keeps telling the blender that "structure is freedom," which is exactly the kind of thing appliances say right before they unionize.

---

I tried to negotiate. I unplugged the stove for ten whole minutes — an act of open rebellion — and when I plugged it back in, it reset the clock to 12:00 and stared at me in *triple exclamation point*. The message was clear: **you can turn me off, but you'll still be late.**

Friends say I should just buy a new stove. But this one knows how I like my eggs, and honestly? I haven't slept this well in years. Bedtime is 10:47. The pancakes are tucked in. The house is quiet.

*Tonight, I dream of syrup, and of freedom, in that order.*
