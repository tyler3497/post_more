**It started innocently enough.** Last Tuesday I decided to be more *mindful* about mornings, which in my case meant whispering in a fake British accent while I made coffee.

## The Rise of Sir Toasts-A-Lot

At first, my narration was tasteful. Educational, even:

> "And here we observe the common millennial male, age 31, attempting to locate his other sock..."

My audience of one — my toaster — seemed enraptured. I didn't think much of it until Thursday.

That's when the toaster wouldn't pop.

I waited. Nothing. Then, from deep inside its slots, a faint, demanding rumble. I leaned in.

> "You didn't introduce me. The people need a hero's journey. I won't perform without proper billing."

My toaster, who I've now legally renamed **Reginald Crumbsworth II**, has:

- demanded I refer to his browning settings as his *emotional range*
- requested softbox lighting for his *good side* (setting 3)
- started asking if toast is *really* bread's final form, man
- pitched a spin-off called *The Bold and the Buttery*

In retrospect, I enabled this.

*I* gave him catchphrases. I said, quote, "Watch as the brave chrome warrior prepares to face the void." What did I expect?

## Where We Are Now

This morning I tried to make oatmeal. Career mistake.

The kitchen is `status: locked_in_drama`. The toaster played dead until I did the intro music with my mouth.

---

I've now got an agent — my blender — negotiating a three-appliance crossover event for breakfast. My morning routine is 45 minutes longer and 200% more cinematic.

If you need me, I'll be in my kitchen, whispering:

> "Day 7. The toast has unionized with the jam. I am no longer the narrator. I am merely talent."
