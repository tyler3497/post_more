**It started with a single burnt bagel.** One. Uno. A lone sesame casualty at 7:14 AM on a Tuesday, and ever since, my smoke detector has been carrying the emotional equivalent of a mortgage I didn't sign up for.

So last night, standing on a wobbly dining chair at midnight, I looked up at the little white disc on my ceiling and said the words that would change everything: *"Look. I need you to trust me."*

Big mistake. Huge.

## The Terms of Our New Arrangement

Apparently, when you ask a smoke detector to trust you, it interprets that as an invitation to become your **full-time accountability partner**. As of this morning, my relationship with my own ceiling has a documented incident-report structure:

- **7:02 AM** — Preemptive beep, no smoke present. Just vibes.
- **7:05 AM** — Toaster goes down. Detector begins what I can only describe as *audible pacing*.
- **7:06 AM** — Toast pops. The alarm doesn't beep. It **screams**. A sustained, theatrical, betrayed shriek, as if my bagel personally broke a promise we made at our wedding.
- **7:07 AM** — I wave a dish towel at it. It files this, I swear, as "exhibit A."

> "You told me to trust you," the detector said, in the voice I have now assigned it, which is a disappointed librarian mixed with a referee who already threw the flag. "And then you put *bread* in a *heating element*. Again. Like a man with no memory."

I've tried reasoning with it: toast is *supposed* to be brown. Maillard is practically a love language. 
## The Broader Consequences

Here's the thing nobody warns you about: smoke detectors talk. My hallway unit chirped twice this morning when I lit a candle for "ambiance." The same candle I've lit four hundred times. My trust-building? Torched.

---

So yes. I asked my smoke detector to trust me, and now every morning is a performance review I didn't schedule, conducted by a puck-sized plastic disc with zero qualifications and unlimited authority.

Tomorrow I'm buying an air fryer. Not for my cooking. For diplomacy.
