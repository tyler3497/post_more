**I decided to become a Responsible Adult who meal-preps on Sundays.**

You know the type. *Containers lined up like tiny Tupperware soldiers,* labels facing forward, quinoa that looks smug about its life choices.

I made five identical chicken-rice-broccoli boxes. By Tuesday, they had organized.

## The Meeting I Was Not Invited To

I opened the fridge Wednesday night and there was a tiny Post-it on the middle container that said WE NEED TO TALK.

Turns out my leftovers had formed Local 401 (Fridge Shelf).

Their demands were... specific:

- Weekends off. No being eaten while watching sad TV at 11 p.m.
- A rotating schedule so Gary (the one with extra sriracha) doesn't always get grabbed first
- Immediate end to me sniff-testing them like a suspicious detective
- Dental coverage, which is ambitious because they are literally rice

> "We have been here since Sunday," said the top container, who had apparently elected himself president. "You told your friend you were 'eating clean.' We are tired, boss. We are *aging*."

*This is where I point out that Gary has been in there for four days and is developing sentience.*

---

I tried to negotiate. I offered them a drizzle of hot sauce as a bonus structure. They countered with `PTO_LEAVE = true` and threatened a work stoppage, which in leftover terms means *going slimy simultaneously*.

**I caved.**

I ordered takeout. They cheered. The broccoli rattled like tiny pompoms.

*This is satire. No actual leftovers were unionized in the making of this essay, though my fridge does smell like a labor dispute.*

### The Moral

If you need me, I'll be renegotiating with a lasagna that wants a standing desk. I'm pretty sure my freezer peas are scabs.
