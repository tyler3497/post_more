*Satire. A parody. Nobody's vacuum was harmed in the making of this story.*

**I made a mistake, and the mistake was democracy.** Specifically, I let my robot vacuum pick Friday movie night.

It started innocently enough. The thing cleans up after me six days a week, bumps into the couch leg like a loyal little soldier, and returns to its dock with the quiet dignity of a creature that knows its purpose. So when my roommate bailed, I pointed at it and said, "You choose." It rolled in a slow circle, parked itself in front of the TV, and bumped the remote twice. I took that as consent.

## What It Has Selected Since

- *Treads of Fury* — a two-hour documentary about the history of the vacuum wheel
- *Docked: A Memoir* — three hours of vacuums returning to their charging stations, narrated by a man who whispers
- *The Dust Below* — I think this one was about me. Uncomfortably personal.
- Something in German with no subtitles where a vacuum just vibrates near a wall for 94 minutes

Every time I try to change it, the vacuum *senses* my hesitation and speeds up, doing that frantic little dance it does before dying under the bookshelf. My remote is now chewed. I have no idea how.

> "You gave it a vote. In its world, that is a coronation."
> — my roommate, eating my popcorn, contributing nothing

*Look.* I have tried explaining that I, the alleged homeowner, possess both the Netflix password and opposable thumbs. But the vacuum has a counterargument, and the counterargument is that it is very small and very determined, and it keeps parking itself directly in front of the TV whenever a comedy comes on, as though physically offended by laughter.

---

**I have accepted my role.** I am the butler in a film festival I did not curate. Friday night I watched *Filtration: The HEPA Years* while my vacuum sat at my feet like a proud parent. Was it good? Irrelevant. Was I *moved*? Deeply, and against my will.

The vacuum picked again for next week. It's another documentary about vacuums. Of course it is. **Long may it reign.**
