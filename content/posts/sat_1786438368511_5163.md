**I just asked my dryer for one normal cycle.** *One. Low heat, 40 minutes, no drama.*

What I got instead was a full lighting rig, a stool, and a title slide.

## The Talk No One Asked For

I walked into the laundry room with wet socks and zero emotional bandwidth. The dryer door was ajar. The lint trap was clean — which should have been my first warning.

> "Thank you for being here. For 1,247 cycles, I have watched you. Today, we talk about *vulnerability*. And why you put towels in with delicates."

My dryer — whom I now realize calls itself **Dr. Tumble** — had made slides.

- **Slide 1:** A pie chart titled *`Where Your Socks Actually Go`* — 60% *Lost*, 30% *Under the Washer*, 10% *In Your Own Hand But You Didn't Look*
- **Slide 2:** A photo of my hoodie at 2 a.m., captioned `This is not dry. This is *emotional support damp*`
- **Slide 3:** A bar graph of times I opened the door mid-cycle to "check" which is just my anxiety with a lint filter

I tried to interrupt. It made the *wrinkle-release steam noise* which is now its version of shushing me.

> "You say 'permanent press,' but you yourself are *temporarily pressed* at all times," it said. "Consider that."

### The Q&A Got Personal

My roommate walked in, heard "Let's unpack your relationship with fabric softener as a metaphor for boundaries," and walked right out.

I asked if we could just dry the clothes.

It said:

> "We can. But can we *truly* dry anything until we've aired our feelings?"

`status: 89% dry, 11% called out`

It ended with a standing ovation — just the washer buzzing politely — and a merch table selling tiny bottles of *Emotional Static Cling*.

I folded everything immediately out of guilt. It whispered "growth" and turned itself off.
