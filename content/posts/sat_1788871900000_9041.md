**It started, as all my best decisions do, at 6:42 a.m., barefoot, in a drafty bathroom, with my whole future displayed on a screen the size of a postage stamp.**

I wasn't looking for advice. I was looking for a number. But the scale blinked, and I swear it blinked *judgmentally*, and suddenly I was asking it — out loud, to no one — "What should I be doing with my life?"

## The scale answered the only way it knows how: with data.

It gave me the number. Then it gave me *the look* — that flickering last decimal place that says *"we both know this isn't your final form."* I've been interpreted by many people in my life. My dentist interprets me. My mother interprets me with one eyebrow. But nobody has ever reduced me to a single reading and made me feel like I'd personally offended physics.

So I did what any emotionally stable adult does. I asked follow-up questions.

- "What would you do in my situation?"
- "Do you think I could lift a tire?"
- "Is it too late for me to become a *different person*?"

The scale did not answer verbally, because it is a scale. But it *held its number steady*, and I have chosen to interpret that as the strongest endorsement anyone has ever given me.

## Enter: Coach Scale.

> "The mind gives up at a hundred percent of the effort you never make," my scale said. It did not say this. But I could *feel* it. In my calves. And in my credit card, which has since purchased the following:

- One (1) **atlas stone** the size of a decorative lawn boulder
- Two (2) **kegs**, for carrying, not drinking, which feels like the saddest sentence I've ever typed
- One (1) **yoke bar**, which the delivery driver described as "a swing set for a very large toddler"
- Zero (0) remaining square feet of garage

My neighbor came over during tire-flip practice. I told him it was a wellness thing. He nodded the way you nod at someone you've already mentally relocated to a farm upstate.

## Three weeks in, and I have learned profound truths:

1. **My grip strength is theoretical.** It exists only as a concept, like civility in group chats.
2. My forearms now make sounds that require a *subtitle*.
3. The scale still gives me the same number every morning, but now it feels like a *scoreboard*.

I have not officially registered for any strongman competition. I have, however, been added to a group chat called **"LIFT BROTHERS: SATURDAY ATLAS STONE SESH"**, and according to group chat law, that is legally binding.

So if anyone needs me, I'll be in the backyard, screaming at a boulder, while my bathroom scale watches through the window with what I can only describe as pride.

This is not a cry for help. This is a *cry for chalk*.
