**I bought my toaster a tiny gold watch and a sheet cake that said HAPPY RETIREMENT, CRUMBS.**

*This seemed proportionate at the time. It was not.*

After seven years of faithful service, my toaster — Sir Toasts-a-Lot — started making a noise like a lawnmower choking on a sock. I decided to honor its legacy with dignity, rather than just quietly replacing it like a normal person.

## The Ceremony Nobody Asked For

I invited three friends, printed a program, and gave a speech. The program included:

- A chronological list of every bagel it had ever burned for artistic reasons
- A eulogy for the Pop-Tart that got stuck in 2021 and was never spoken of again
- A PowerPoint titled *Thermal Contributions to My Morning Routine: A Retrospective*
- A ceremonial unplugging

> "To be honest, I thought I had another year left," said my toaster, in a voice that sounded suspiciously like my roommate doing a British accent from the pantry. "But I appreciate the plaque. The cake is... condescending."

### The Fallout Was Immediate and Bureaucratic

By 8:14 a.m., my kitchen had formed a union.

The blender submitted a formal request for sabbatical, citing "emotional labor from smoothie season." The microwave asked if its retirement package included a beach house. The coffee maker just started blinking 12:00 repeatedly, which I have been told by HR is a sign of *quiet quitting*.

I tried to run damage control:

```
if (appliance.morale < 0.5) {
  appliance.compliment("you're doing great, sweetie");
  // does not work on blenders with abandonment issues
}
```

---

Now my fridge is leaving passive-aggressive magnets on *itself* that say THINKING OF RETIRING EARLY and my air fryer wants a LinkedIn recommendation.

I told them all the party was symbolic. My toaster — still on the counter, very much not retired — popped up and said:

> "Symbolic? You gave me a WATCH. I don't even have wrists."

*Satire. Obviously fictional. No appliances were forced into retirement, given timepieces, or consulted about career transitions. I made this up while waiting for toast that never came.*
