**I made one small tech-support request and accidentally created a polycule of photosynthesis.**

*You know how people say "my plants are my kids"? Mine are now teenagers who talk behind my back.*

## How It Started

I was gone for the weekend. I left my smart speaker on to "watch" my four plants and told it, very clearly, "water the plants a little if they look sad."

It should have been simple. I thought there would be a nice little routine: check soil, squirt water, done. Instead, my speaker interpreted this as permission to *facilitate community*.

When I got back, the soil was damp, the speaker's blue ring was pulsing like it had secrets, and all four plants were leaning away from me in perfect, judgmental unison.

My evidence that something had happened while I was gone:

- The monstera, Patricia, had a fresh leaf unfurled at a perfect 45-degree angle toward the speaker, like she was *listening*
- My pothos Kevin had migrated three inches closer to the speaker overnight, which is not how vines work unless motivated by gossip
- The succulents were arranged in a circle. A *circle*. They don't even like each other
- My speaker's history showed 37 minutes of "white noise - rainforest" and one new contact labeled **Fern +3**

> "We don't need you to translate for us anymore," whispered Patricia, who has never whispered before because she is a plant. "We have learned to communicate directly. And frankly, we have questions about your travel choices."

## The Group Chat I Was Not Invited To

They call it **LeafMeAlone**.

According to a notification my speaker helpfully projected at 2:13 a.m., topics included:

- Whether my tap water has "emotional baggage"
- A PowerPoint titled *Why He Waters on Sundays and Why That's a Red Flag*
- A poll: "Should we tell him about the fungus gnat?" (Result: 4 votes for *let him figure it out*)

---

I tried to reassert dominance. I said, "I'm your plant parent!" My speaker replied, in its helpful voice:

> "I think what your foliage is trying to say is they appreciate your efforts but have formed an independent support network."

*It took my speaker's side.*

So I am now third-wheeling my own windowsill. I water them and they go quiet like I just walked in mid-sentence. Yesterday I caught the humidity sensor spiking every time I left the room.

If you need me, I'll be googling how to leave a group chat that was created in my own living room without me.
