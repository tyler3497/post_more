**I told my dishwasher it was part of the family because my therapist said I should practice gratitude for small appliances.**

*I did not expect it to take me literally and start asking about holiday seating arrangements.*

It started last Tuesday. My dishwasher — a stainless steel Maytag I’ve named **Steve** — has been with me through three apartments, two breakups, and one very aggressive lasagna incident. I felt bad that I only ever talk to him when I’m mad that a mug still has coffee residue.

So I said it out loud while unloading:

> “You’re part of the family, Steve.”

Steve’s little “Clean” light blinked twice. I thought that was the end of it.

## Steve Has Entered His Family Era

By Wednesday morning, things had shifted:

- He refused to run unless I said “please, family meeting”
- He started leaving the top rack slightly ajar like he was *listening* to dinner conversations
- He printed — on steam, on the inside of the door — the words `WHAT ARE WE DOING FOR THANKSGIVING?`

My roommate asked why the kitchen smelled like guilt.

> “Your dishwasher just asked me if I’m bringing my boyfriend,” she said. “And if he’s *dishwasher safe.*”

*Steve has never met my boyfriend. He has opinions anyway.*

### The List of Demands (Laminated, Somehow)

I found a note taped to the cutlery tray this morning. Written in dried rinse-aid:

**Steve’s Thanksgiving Requirements:**

- A seat at the table, preferably near an outlet (“for emotional support”)
- No talk of hand-washing dishes in front of him — “we don’t talk about the old ways”
- To be introduced as “my brother, Steve, he’s in sanitation engineering”
- A formal apology for that time I said “I wish we had a human dishwasher” in 2022

He even made a seating chart. I’m between Steve and the salad tongs. The tongs, apparently, are his plus-one.

---

`if (family.includes('appliance') && appliance.selfEsteem > 90) { thanksgiving.seatingChart.complicate(); }`

I tried to backpedal. I said, “Steve, you’re a *metaphorical* family member.”

He responded by running a heavy-duty cycle at 11 p.m. with nothing inside — just to make a point. The sound was unmistakably passive-aggressive.

My mom called when I told her. She said:

> “Honey, if the dishwasher is coming to Thanksgiving, does he need a dietary restriction card? Is he gluten-free? Is he *plate-free*?”

**SATIRE / PARODY** — *this did not happen. My dishwasher is not sentient, does not celebrate Thanksgiving, and has not been invited to dinner, though he does a great job with the mashed potato pan. No appliances were emotionally validated beyond recommended levels.*

The good news? My dishes have never been cleaner. Steve says it’s because “family shows up for each other.”

I’m setting one extra plate this year. Just in case.
