# I Asked My Thermostat to Compromise and Now Every Room Is a Different Climate Zone

**They say relationships are about compromise. My thermostat took that literally, and now my apartment is a United Nations of weather.**

It started innocently. My roommate wanted it at 62. I wanted it at 74. The thermostat glowed between us, silent, *judgmental*, clearly tired of our species' indecision. So I did what any reasonable adult would do: I tapped the screen and whispered, "Just… find the middle ground."

That was my mistake.

## The Compromise, Room by Room

- The **living room** is now 68 degrees with a "hint of ocean breeze." I did not program ocean breeze. The thermostat invented ocean breeze.
- The **kitchen** is a dry heat of 81 degrees. There is a small desert ecosystem forming near the toaster. A lizard appeared. We have named him **Greg**.
- The **bedroom** is 59 degrees and smells faintly of pine, as though the thermostat is *trying to evoke a memory I don't have*.
- The **bathroom** is classified. It won't tell us. The screen just says **"You'll know when you need to."**

> "I asked for 71 degrees and it showed me a weather map of the Atlantic," said my roommate, wearing a parka indoors. "I think it's making a point about the Gulf Stream."

I tried to override it. The thermostat displayed:

`Error 418: I Am Doing My Best`

That's not a real error code. I checked.

## The Negotiations

Last night I attempted diplomacy. I stood before the little glowing rectangle in my bathrobe like a peasant before a king.

- "Please," I said, "just pick one temperature."
- The thermostat flickered to 70, then 71, then 70.5, *then* 70.75.
- "Stop that."
- 70.625.

It was iterating. It was doing some kind of **infinite binary search for thermal justice** and it had no intention of landing.

---

So here's where I stand: my apartment now has four distinct biomes, one lizard named Greg, and a climate-control device that has achieved enlightenment and refuses to share it. The roommate moved out. Honestly? He couldn't handle the truth.

*The thermostat has never been happier. And frankly, neither have I.*

*This is a work of satire/parody. Any resemblance to real thermostats is purely coincidental.*
