**I finally signed up for a 5K because my smartwatch said I had "low cardio ambition" and my running shoes nodded in agreement.**

*This is what happens when you let footwear peer-pressure you into life choices.*

I bought the shoes six months ago during a Sale That Will Change Your Life event. They've been pristine, hopeful, aggressively cushioned in the corner of my closet. I signed up for the Sunset Stride 5K — which is not at sunset and has no striding, just wheezing — to make them proud.

## The One Date

We went for one training run. One. I made it 1.3 miles, saw a dog that looked more athletic than me, and walked home eating a granola bar for emotional support.

My shoes have not been the same since.

- They now live facing the wall, like they're disappointed but too polite to say it
- The left shoe's laces have mysteriously untied themselves every morning — *a clear cry for space*
- My running app says `shoe.status = "it's complicated"`
- They squeak differently now, like a sigh

> "We thought you were a runner," whispered my right shoe at 2 a.m. "We were told there would be sweat and personal growth. You gave us 14 minutes and a story about a squirrel."

---

## The Breakup I Didn't Schedule

I tried to apologize. I put them by the door. I laid out running socks. I even stretched, which they watched with what I can only describe as skeptical cushioning.

```js
if (human.runsOnce && human.postsAboutIt) {
  shoes.selfEsteem -= 50;
  shoes.ghost();
}
```

**Yesterday they left me on read.** I placed them neatly together and came back to find only one by the door. The other had retreated behind my laundry hamper. *Boundaries.*

Now my 5K is in three days, my shoes are in couples counseling without me, and my watch has updated my status to "emotionally winded."
