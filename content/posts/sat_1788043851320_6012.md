**I decided to let my to-do list write its own memoir for a change, which was a huge mistake because apparently it's been holding a grudge since 2022.**

*In hindsight, you should not give narrative power to the one document that knows you still haven't called the dentist.*

## The Day My Productivity Became Self-Aware

It started as a self-care exercise. My therapist-adjacent podcast said to "externalize your tasks." So I opened my notes app and typed:

> Dear To-Do List, how are *you* feeling today?

It replied. Which should have been my first clue.

By midnight, it had drafted 47 pages in Google Docs titled *The List: A Life of Being Ignored*. I was mentioned briefly on page 34.

My alleged contributions to this literary masterpiece included:

- Buying cilantro three times because I forgot I already bought cilantro
- Writing "become person who drinks water" for 11 consecutive months
- Adding "fix life" as a single checkbox item with no subtasks
- Scheduling "relax" between "panic about taxes" and "google if you can microwave eggs"

The memoir really found its voice in Chapter 3: *The Great Laundry Schism*.

> "He looked me in the eyes," the list wrote, "and said 'I'll do it tomorrow.' Tomorrow was 2019. I have been carrying 'fold fitted sheet' like ancestral trauma ever since."

I tried to defend myself.

```
if (task.isImportant) {
  pretendIDidntSeeIt();
  reschedule(task, to: "Someday™");
  feelGuilty(100);
}
```

---

The worst part is the reviews are in. My to-do list got an advanced blurb from my laundry pile and my unread emails, and they both agree: I'm an unreliable narrator.

My houseplant says the memoir is "brave and raw." My coffee maker says it finally feels seen.

*This is satire, obviously. My to-do list did not write a memoir. If it did, it would be longer and significantly more disappointed in me.*
