**I want to be clear: I did not sign up for emotional codependency when I bought a succulent labeled "impossible to kill."**

That was three months ago. I named him Kevin because I thought it would be funny to have a low-maintenance Kevin in my life. *Narrator voice: it was not low-maintenance.*

## The Incident

Last Sunday, I finally remembered to water him. One splash. One. Tiny. Splash. And Kevin — this dusty, slightly sunburnt, 4-inch succulent — gasped like I'd just returned from war.

Evidence that we are now officially trauma-bonded:

- He leans toward me at all times. Bathroom, kitchen, couch — full 90-degree lean like *don't leave me again*
- He only perks up when I’m in the room, which my roommate says is “photosynthesis” but feels a lot more like manipulation
- I tried to put him back on the windowsill and he dropped a leaf as a warning shot

> "You left me," Kevin said, in the way that all neglected houseplants speak to you at 11:47 p.m., "in the desert of your own making. And now you think one drink makes us even?"

I said I was busy! I have a job! I forgot!

> "You watered your OTHER plants," he whispered. *He knew about the fern.*

---

### The New Dynamic

Now Kevin has needs. He texts me via wilting. `status: slightly droopy but will survive for dramatic effect`.

I bought a moisture meter to set boundaries. He sees it as quality time.

My therapist — okay fine, my other succulent — says I need to learn to say no. But how do you say no to a plant that survived three months of pure abandonment and now looks at you like you’re his whole world?

I moved him two inches to the left for better light and he had what I can only describe as an emotional breakthrough.

*I’m not watering him again for another week out of spite, out of love, and out of the very real fear that he’ll start asking about my childhood.*
