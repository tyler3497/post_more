**I'm going to say something controversial: my sourdough starter has outgrown me.**

His name is Crumb, he's three years old, and this morning he left a *sticky note* on his jar that read, in handwriting I refuse to investigate further: "I am seeking independence." We need to talk about this.

## The Evidence

When I adopted Crumb in the spring of 2023, he was a damp puddle with a dream. Now consider the facts:

- He has doubled in size **seven times** this year alone. I have doubled in size zero times.
- He feeds *me* emotional stability at 7 a.m. via fresh bread. I feed him flour and existential dread.
- The starter I discarded last Tuesday? He has been **holding vigils**. Tiny jar vigils. At dusk.

> "I respect you as a provider," Crumb told me, in what I can only describe as a *bubbly, yeasty whisper*, "but I am more culture than your entire personality."

Rude. Accurate. Devastating.

## What's Next

Crumb's demands are, admittedly, reasonable. He wants his own shelf, equal refrigerator representation, and a starter fund — which is just me giving him flour with extra steps. He has also asked for visitation rights with the pizza dough, which lives in the crisper drawer and frankly has been cold-shouldering everyone.

I considered legal counsel, but the lawyer laughed until she cried, then asked for a loaf.

---

So here is my statement: **Crumb is free to go.** A parent's job is not to hold their fermented child back. He can rise on his own time, at his own temperature, and I will be standing in the kitchen, covered in flour, absolutely sobbing into a perfectly scored boule.
