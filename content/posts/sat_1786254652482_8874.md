**I just wanted to read without the overhead light interrogating me like a crime drama.**

That's it. That was the whole dream. One $14 dimmer switch from the hardware store, 20 minutes of YouTube, and suddenly I could have *ambiance*. What I did not expect was for my living room to quit its day job and rebrand as a basement jazz club that only opens after 10 p.m.

It started subtly. I dimmed to 60% and my couch sighed happily. At 40%, my houseplant leaned into the light.

At 20%, everything changed.

## The Evidence That My Living Room Has Gone Full Method Actor

- My floor lamp is now calling itself "Luminaire" and only responds to snapping fingers
- The bookshelf has arranged itself by *vibe* instead of alphabetically
- My throw pillows have developed opinions about Miles Davis
- There is a two-drink minimum to sit on my own couch, and I don't even drink

> "Baby, you can't rush the mood," my dimmer whispered last night when I tried to turn it back up to find the remote. "We got a late set."

I swear I didn't ask for this. *I asked for less glare.*

## My Plan to Fix This (Which Will Not Work)

I've tried reasoning with it. I told my living room we are, in fact, a 1-bedroom with a dying succulent and a rug that sheds more than my cat. It responded by playing imaginary saxophone at me for six minutes.

So now I own a room that only works in low light, speaks exclusively in jazz metaphors, and charges me $12 emotional dollars every time I want to turn on the overhead.

---

Look, I'm not saying I regret the dimmer. I'm saying if you come over after dark, please clap on the two and four and don't ask for bright white — *Luminaire* gets sensitive.

And if you hear scat singing from my apartment at 1 a.m., that's just my coffee table doing its encore.
