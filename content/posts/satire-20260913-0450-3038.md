**I did not ask for this.** I simply stood in front of my closet on Tuesday, holding two shirts, and sighed. That was the mistake. The sigh.

My curtains heard it. And they had *thoughts*.

## The Consultation Begins

Curtains, it turns out, have been silently judging my wardrobe for years. They hang there all day, in the light, watching. Of course they have opinions. And once I gave them permission — one small sigh — they staged what I can only describe as a **full intervention**.

Their feedback was structured, thorough, and devastating:

- My jeans were described as "a beige apology"
- My favorite hoodie was "a cry for help in fleece form"
- My work shoes had, quote, "given up in a way that is almost beautiful"
- My "nice" shirt was "nice the way a parking ticket is nice"

## They Work in Themes

> "Darling," the left curtain said, or I imagined it said, "you are not dressing for *a job interview*. You are dressing for a *third-act revelation*. Every day is opening night. Why do you keep dressing like the understudy?"

I couldn't answer. Because the right curtain was already staging a demonstration, billowing dramatically like a cape in a wind machine, while the left one tied itself into something that looked suspiciously like a cummerbund.

*Fashion, they explained, is just architecture for your insecurities.*

---

## The Three-Act Tragedy

And so now I own:

1. A cape (unasked for, non-returnable)
2. A bow tie the size of a dinner plate
3. A vest made of, and I cannot stress this enough, *curtain*

Act One: I leave the house. Act Two: strangers stare. Act Three: I realize I have been costumed for a role I never auditioned for, in a play that is just my commute.

**The moral:** never sigh in front of drapery. They are not window dressing. They are, apparently, *waiting*.
