**I apologized to my dishwasher, and it has been holding a grudge ever since.**

It started innocently. I crammed a cookie sheet in at a forty-five-degree angle, stacked a pot on top of the bowls like some kind of architectural fever dream, and the door barely closed. Halfway through the cycle it made a noise I can only describe as *exasperated*. So, like any emotionally responsible adult, I put my hand on the warm door and said, "I'm sorry. I know that was too much."

I wish I hadn't.

## The Spite Cycle

Now the dishwasher only runs half-cycles. Not half *loads* — half *cycles*. It quits mid-rinse with dishes still foamy and, I swear, a sense of moral superiority. The evidence is mounting:

- The timer counts down to zero, but the dishes come out filthy with what I'm calling "protest residue"
- It beeps eleven times when it finishes a normal cycle, but only three curt beeps now. *Three.*
- It opens itself mid-cycle to, and I quote the steam, "let things breathe"

I tried couples counseling with the other appliances, but the refrigerator took the dishwasher's side and the toaster abstained.

> "You loaded a stockpot on the top rack," the dishwasher communicated through a series of ominous clunks last night. "The top rack. For *glassware*."

---

The worst part? I know it remembers every offense. The burnt lasagna pan of 2024. The time I ran it with no detergent "as an experiment." I have spent more emotional labor on this appliance than on any relationship I've ever had, and it shows — because the moment I loaded it *perfectly* last Tuesday, gently, bowls nested, silverware sorted, it ran the single most beautiful cycle of its life.

Then it went right back to spite.

Honestly, I've learned my lesson. **Respect your dishwasher, or your dishwasher will teach you respect.** Mine has, and my plates will never be clean again.
