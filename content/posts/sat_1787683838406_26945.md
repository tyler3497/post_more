**I finally decided to opt out of adulthood and apparently that violates the terms and conditions I never agreed to.**

*Which is ironic, because I also never agreed to paying property taxes on a succulent I bought at Trader Joe's.*

## The Call That Changed Nothing

It started when I realized my free trial of Being A Responsible Adult™ had expired. You know the one — 18 years free, then suddenly you're billed monthly in back pain, emails marked URGENT, and the knowledge that cilantro goes bad in 11 minutes.

So I called customer service. The automated voice was suspiciously my own voice from 2016 saying:

> "Your call is very important to us. Please stay on the line and contemplate every minor decision you've ever made. Currently, you are number *you* in line."

The hold music was just my voicemail greeting on loop. You know, the one where I say "Hey, it's me! Leave a message!" with way too much optimism.

Here's what they offered me instead of a cancellation:

- Downgrade to Adult Lite™ — you still pay bills, but you get to eat cereal for dinner without guilt
- Pause my subscription for 7-10 business years while I live in a blanket fort
- Transfer my account to my houseplant, who is apparently more financially literate

Look, I'm not asking for much. I just wanted to return this adulthood I found. It's slightly used, smells faintly of coffee and poor decisions, and the instructions are in a language I don't speak called *Tax Forms*.

The representative — who was also me, but in a headset — finally said they'd escalate my case to a supervisor. I'm still on hold. It's been three hours. My voicemail and I are now in a committed relationship.

*This is satire, obviously. Adulthood has no unsubscribe button. I checked.*
