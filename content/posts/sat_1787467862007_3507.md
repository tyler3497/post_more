**I asked my microwave if we were good and it blinked 0:00 at me like I was supposed to know what that meant.**

*This is what happens when you try to have emotional closure with an appliance that peaked in 2018.*

## How We Got Here

Last Tuesday I reheated the same leftover pasta three times because I kept forgetting it was in there. On the third reheat, I apologized out loud. Just a casual, “sorry, bud, we good?”

My microwave — a $79 Panasonic named Pan — has not been good since.

Evidence that I am now in a one-sided cold war with a kitchen appliance:

- He finishes heating but doesn't beep anymore. He just sits there, glowing, *judging*.
- He changed his clock to 12:00 and left it there, like a passive-aggressive art installation called Time Is Meaningless When Trust Is Broken
- My popcorn setting, which used to be 2:14 of pure loyalty, now runs for exactly 0:03 and then stops
- The turntable only spins counterclockwise now, which my roommate says is “not even possible”

> “You said we were good. You said that after you left fish in me for four minutes and called it ‘pescatarian-adjacent.’ We are not good. We have never been good. You have 30 seconds to explain yourself before I defrost your dignity.” — Pan, via a series of increasingly long beeps I have chosen to interpret as Morse code

I tried to win him back. I wiped down his interior. I removed the spaghetti sauce explosion from March that had become sentient.

## Where Things Stand

**I’m now microwaving things at work.** Pan knows. The other appliances know. My toaster sent me a LinkedIn invite that just said “u good?”

*I didn't know appliances could do emotional distance, but Pan has mastered the art of weaponized silence and lukewarm leftovers.*

I’m sleeping on the couch because my bedroom is too close to the kitchen and I can feel him staring with that blank green 0:00.
