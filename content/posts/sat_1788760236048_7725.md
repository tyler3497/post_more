**My umbrella has snapped, and honestly? I don't blame it.** After years of loyal service — windstorms, sideways sleet, that one monsoon where it turned inside-out so hard it practically achieved enlightenment — it has developed what my therapist would call "boundaries."

## The Incident

Last Tuesday, under a modest drizzle, I clicked the button. Nothing happened. I clicked it again, harder. It stayed shut. So I carried it inside.

The second the door closed behind me, it **detonated** open like a firework of pure spite, taking out the coat rack and half a houseplant.

## Evidence That This Is Personal

- It opens at the *slightest* door slam now, like it's heard an alarm bell only it can hear
- It refuses to leave the house — I tried taking it to the car; it hooked itself around the doorframe and would not budge
- Yesterday it opened during dinner. There is no sky in the dining room. I have accepted this.

> "Just stand outside for five minutes," she said, rain drumming on her shoulders. "You haven't been yourself lately."

I stood there, perfectly soaked, feeling betrayed in the one relationship I thought was waterproof.

## The Diagnosis

After three hours of research (I read the *comments* on a Reddit thread, so this is basically peer-reviewed), I've concluded my umbrella has developed **Seasonal Indoor Preference Disorder**.

Treatment options include:

1. Buying a second umbrella and forcing a confrontation *(risky)*
2. Letting it live its truth — indoor humidity 34%, mood: thriving
3. Admitting the umbrella was never the problem. The *sky* was.

I've chosen option two. This morning the umbrella opened itself at breakfast, calm. My living room is now a light drizzle at all times. The plants are *thrilled*. My walls are developing mold and character in equal measure.

---

**Update:** I took it to the park at noon. Actual rain. Real, certified precipitation. It refused to open — just sat there, folded, unbothered, watching me get soaked.

*Some things can't be fixed. Some umbrellas can't be unfixed. And some of us now own a very expensive, very moody indoor fountain shaped like regret.*
