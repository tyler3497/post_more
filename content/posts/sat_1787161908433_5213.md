**I wanted to connect with nature. I did not want nature to start a listserv about me.**

*It was supposed to be simple.* Buy a smart bird feeder, watch cute chickadees, feel like a grounded human being who knows what a warbler is.

For three days, it worked. I got a goldfinch. I got a smug sense of accomplishment. I named it Steve.

On day four, the pigeons found it.

## The Takeover

Pigeons do not share. Pigeons do not appreciate ambient Lo-Fi birdfeeder cams. Pigeons conduct *reconnaissance*.

By Friday, three of them were perched on my railing like little feathered HOA presidents. One had a crumb, one had an attitude, and one — *I swear* — had a tiny clipboard.

I checked the feeder app that night. It said:

> "Motion detected: 47 pigeons. 0 shame. One pigeon appears to be taking minutes."

Then the newsletter arrived.

### Issue #1: BALCONY WATCH WEEKLY

Slipped under my door. Actual paper. Smelled faintly of sunflower seeds and judgment.

- **Subject observed (me) wearing same sweatpants for 3rd consecutive day of 'bird watching'**
- **Subject attempted to shoo us with 'gentle claps' — effectiveness rated 2/10**
- **Feeder is allegedly for 'all birds' but is clearly Steve-pilled propaganda**
- `status: this human needs supervision`

---

I tried to negotiate. I put out a second feeder, labeled `FOR PIGEONS ONLY` in polite Sharpie.

The next morning there was an editorial response taped to my sliding door:

> "We appreciate your attempt at diplomacy, but we have concerns about your hydration habits and your inability to identify a mourning dove. Also, your basil plant is dying. Sincerely, The Board."

## My Current Situation

I now pay $4.99 a month for a pigeon-run Substack about my own balcony. It has 12 subscribers, all pigeons. They gave me a 3.5-star rating for "ambience, but inconsistent seed dispersal."

**My bird feeder smart. Pigeons smarter. Steve the goldfinch has not been seen since he was cited for 'lack of community engagement.'**

I miss when my biggest problem was squirrels.

*This is satire. No actual pigeons were employed as journalists, though they would absolutely unionize if they could.*
