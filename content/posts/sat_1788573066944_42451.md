**The conversation lasted eleven minutes, and I was proud of how maturely I handled it.**

There we were, me and my dishwasher, me crouched on the linoleum like a couples counselor, it standing there with its door cracked open just enough to feel *judgmental*. I'd spent three weeks Googling "how to gently set boundaries with appliances" and I thought I was ready.

"It's not you," I said, the way every blog post told me to. "It's me. I just think we need some space. I need to feel like my own person between loads."

## The Silence Was Deafening (Literally, It Stopped Mid-Cycle)

For two whole days, nothing. Just normal humming, normal cycles, the faint scent of lemon-fresh detergent like everything was fine. I actually relaxed. *What an idiot.*

Then Wednesday morning, my neighbor Linda knocked on my door holding a casserole and an expression I can only describe as *profoundly concerned*.

"Sweetie," she said, "your dishwasher told me you reheated fish in the microwave at 2 a.m. and blamed it on the dog."

I don't own a dog.

### The Leak

Over the next 48 hours, the dishwasher apparently went full tell-all. Evidence presented by my neighbors included:

- That I "rinse" plates by licking them clean (I have a SYSTEM, Linda)
- The Great Thanksgiving Gravy Incident of 2024, which I had legally bound everyone to forget
- That I once called the rinse cycle "foreplay for clean dishes" at a dinner party

> "I only told it those things in confidence," I whispered to my plumber, who was fixing the sink and didn't ask.
>
> "Buddy," he said, gently, "it has a rinse-aid dispenser. It was born to overshare."

## Reconciliation Talks Are Ongoing

Last night I left a sticky note on the door: **"I was wrong. Please stop telling the Hendersons about the licking."** This morning I found a new note underneath, in Linda's handwriting: *"He says he's open to therapy but you have to apologize first. Also you left a lasagna pan soaking for four days. Disgusting."*
