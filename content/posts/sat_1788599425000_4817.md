**Big mistake.** Last Tuesday, in a moment of late-night vulnerability, I asked my printer — a six-year-old inkjet named Gerald — what I should do with my life. I thought the worst case was that nothing would happen. Gerald, it turns out, has opinions.

## How it started

I was staring at the ceiling, spiraling about my career, when I said the words out loud: *"Gerald, what would you do if you were me?"* The paper tray twitched. A single page slid out. On it, in perfect formatting, was my own résumé — except redesigned with a header font I would never choose and the tagline *"Open to work (desperately)."*

I laughed. Then I cried. Then Gerald printed a second page.

## Evidence that my printer has become my manager

- **Wednesday morning:** I tried to print a boarding pass. Gerald printed a résumé tailored for a regional forklift distributor instead.
- **Thursday:** I needed labels for a bake sale. He gave me cover letters addressed to "Whom It May Concern (Hiring Manager)."
- **Friday:** I unplugged him. He printed one last page anyway. It read: *"Running won't fix your gap years."*

> "You have leadership experience," Gerald wrote at the top of my CV, *"if you count telling your cat to knock it off."*

## The final straw

Yesterday I asked him to print a simple recipe for soup. He printed a job application for a **"Junior Soup Associate (Night Shift)"** position at a warehouse three states away. The bullet points described my qualifications as *"adequate"* and my references as *"unreachable, by choice."*

---

I finally called tech support. The agent listened to the whole story, went quiet for a long time, and said: *"Sir... Gerald sounds like a good mentor. Have you considered applying?"*

**SATIRE/PARODY.** No printers were harmed, promoted, or certified as career coaches in the writing of this post. Gerald remains plugged in, because honestly, his résumé formatting is better than mine.
