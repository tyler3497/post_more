**I have been led astray by many things in my life** — a charismatic real estate agent, a gas station burrito, my own confidence — but nothing has betrayed me like Gerald.

Gerald is my robot vacuum. I named him after my grandfather, a man who *did* get lost driving to my house for six consecutive Thanksgivings, so in retrospect, the omens were there.

## The Incident

It started simply enough. I was standing in my kitchen holding a laundry basket, staring at my own hallway like it was a stranger, and I said the words that ruined everything:

> "Gerald, which way to the bedroom?"

He paused. The little sensor light blinked. And then — I swear this happened — he turned a slow, deliberate circle, like a compass calibrating its moral judgment, and **drove directly into the bathroom.**

"Ha ha," I said. *Very funny.* Except he wasn't joking. Because now, every single day, the following things happen:

- I walk toward the living room; Gerald cuts me off and heads for the laundry room, as if to say *your destination was never real*
- I ask him to clean the kitchen; he cleans *around* the kitchen, a 12-inch halo of defiance
- Yesterday I watched him spend 40 minutes trying to vacuum **through** a closed door, out of what I can only describe as principle

## The Toll

The real tragedy is that he's *good* at it. He maps the house with the confidence of a cartographer who has never once been wrong, and follows his own routes with total commitment. The floor is immaculate. I am lost. I have started leaving breadcrumbs. Actual breadcrumbs. He vacuumed those up too, which felt personal.

So now my life has a strange new rhythm: I ask Gerald where the bedroom is, and he shows me exactly where the bedroom is *not*, and somehow, against all reason, I trust him.

We've been through a lot, Gerald and I. He doesn't talk. He doesn't have to. The message is clear.

*He knows where we're going. He just doesn't think I deserve to arrive.*
