## The Apology

**It started, as all great reckonings do, on laundry day.**

I had let the pile build for eleven days. Eleven days of "I'll do it tomorrow," until the basket stopped being a container and became a *geological formation* — a sedimentary record of my worst decisions, layered with gym socks.

So I did what any reasonable adult would do: I apologized to the basket.

## The Incident

I knelt down, looked it right in its woven little face, and said:

> "I am sorry for the way I have treated you. You deserved better than this fossilized t-shirt."

It did not answer. *Rude*, I thought, *but understandable.*

That night, I emptied it. Washed everything. Folded it all like a person who has their life together. And then, as a peace offering, I placed a single dryer sheet inside — lavender-scented, the good kind.

The next morning, the basket was empty of clothes. But the dryer sheet? Gone. In its place: **a sock with a hole in it**, laid across the bottom like a tiny, judgmental crime scene.

## The Evidence Against Me

Since that day, the basket has established a clear pattern of retaliation:

- My good jeans keep ending up on the floor *beside* the basket, never inside it
- One specific bath towel has been "missing" for three weeks, and I swear I saw a corner of it peeking out from behind the hamper
- Last Tuesday it refused to hold a fitted sheet — it *spat it back out*, the elastic snapping like a personal insult
- I caught it vibrating slightly during the spin cycle, as if *laughing*

---

## The Verdict

A therapist friend (unlicensed, but confident) told me the basket isn't angry — it's *setting boundaries*. Which is worse, honestly. I was prepared for anger. I am not prepared for a hamper with emotional maturity.

So now I negotiate. I place clothes in with a soft, apologetic motion. I compliment the weave. I bought it a liner as a housewarming gift for the laundry room it already lives in.

And it still only holds grudges.

*This is satire. No baskets were harmed. One sock remains at large.*
