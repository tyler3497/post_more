**I swear I was just trying to be organized. I made one innocent spreadsheet and now I'm in a legal drama with my own hamper.**

*This is what happens when you listen to productivity TikTok at 1 a.m.*

Two weeks ago I lost my favorite striped sock. Not a tragedy, just annoying. So I did what any rational adult would do: I opened Excel and created Sock Census 2026™.

## The Investigation Began Innocently

Column A: Sock Name. Column B: Last Seen. Column C: Emotional Attachment Level (1-10). By day three I had conditional formatting, pivot tables, and a pie chart titled "Where Do They Go??"

The laundry noticed.

Suddenly my dryer was running *slightly* longer than necessary, like it was buying time to shred evidence.

### My Laundry's Legal Strategy

My hamper, who I now refer to as Counsel, has mounted a formidable defense:

- Claims all missing socks "voluntarily departed to pursue other opportunities"
- Argues that static cling is a legally binding cohabitation agreement
- Insists the single sock I found behind the dryer was "planted"
- Filed a motion to dismiss my entire winter collection for "lack of arch support"

I confronted the pile. I said, "Where is Mr. Argyle?"

> "My client declines to answer on the grounds that it may be lint-rolled," whispered the laundry, in a voice that sounded suspiciously like my dryer vent.

I tried to be logical:

```js
if (socks.length % 2 !== 0) {
  laundry.isSuspect = true;
  trust -= 100;
}
```

The code returned `true` and my washing machine blinked twice, which I took as a confession.

---

Look, I know this is absurd. My laundry is not actually on retainer. But this morning I found a tiny business card in the lint trap that said "Dewey, Cheatem & Howe – Specialists in Sock Disappearance & Fabric-Based Gaslighting."

*Satire/parody – my socks are fine, my laundry is not sentient, and I should probably just buy no-show socks and move on.*
