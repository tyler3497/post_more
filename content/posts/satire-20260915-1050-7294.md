It started as a simple chore. **The hose was tangled.** Not emotionally tangled — although, as it turns out, it had opinions on that front too — just a normal, suburban figure-eight-of-doom situation behind the garage. I bent down to untangle it, and in a moment of weakness I said the thing out loud:

> "Honestly? Plumbing intimidates me. The pipes, the pressure, the fittings. I just nod at plumbers until they leave."

Silence. Then — *psshh* — one small, deliberate spurt from the nozzle. A sigh with ambition.

## The Hose Heard Me

Everything changed after that. The evidence is, frankly, overwhelming:

- Every time I grab the nozzle, the hose **kinks itself** in what I can only describe as a supportive chokehold.
- When I mention calling a plumber, it leaks *exactly one* tear-shaped drip onto my shoe.
- My neighbor Dave witnessed the whole exchange and now refuses to make eye contact with my sprinkler system.

I consulted my therapist (mine, not the hose's — it declined), a garden center employee, and one very patient man at the hardware store who whispered, *"Sir, it's just water pressure."* Just. Water. Pressure. As if pressure isn't the exact thing we're both working through.

## The Kinks Are the Message

The truth is, I think the hose pities me. It watched me Google **"how to coil a hose without crying"** and decided somebody in this relationship had to be the adult. So it kinks. It holds. It contains multitudes — and, occasionally, my mulch.

---

Yesterday I bought it a brass nozzle as a peace offering. This morning it leaked on my birthday card.

> *"You don't fix pressure by fighting it,"* I imagine the hose telling me, in a voice like rain on a tin roof. *"You fix it by letting it flow — steadily, patiently, preferably away from your face."*

So now we have an arrangement. I water the tomatoes. It pretends it didn't see me flinch at the plumber's invoice. **We're both doing our best.**

*Satire — completely fictional. No hoses were emotionally harmed. One was mildly inconvenienced.*
