## The Growth Mindset Was Not Supposed to Be Literal

**My pothos has been in my apartment for eight months and has now decided it is my manager.**

*This is not the low-maintenance relationship I signed up for at Home Depot.* The tag said "thrives on neglect." It lied.

It started last Tuesday. I was reheating leftovers in sweatpants at 11am, and I could swear my plant... exhaled disapprovingly. Fern — yes, I named it Fern, because I lack imagination under fluorescent lights — has been looking *peakier than usual*.

Then it escalated.

> "Tyler, we need to talk about your Q2 hydration metrics," Fern said via a tiny sticky note I definitely don't remember writing but is in my handwriting.

I blame the tap water.

### My Latest Performance Review

According to Fern, here are my **Key Areas for Development**:

- Inconsistent watering schedule — *allegedly I have commitment issues*
- Leaving dirty mugs within its "photosynthesis zone" — **a clear boundary violation**
- Playing lo-fi beats at 2am while "trying to grow" — disrespectful to its circadian rhythm
- Talking about maybe getting a cat — *a direct threat to workplace safety*

> "Your effort has been satisfactory, but your follow-through is... wilting," Fern's second note read. "Consider this a verbal warning."

I tried to defend myself. I said I was doing my best. Fern rustled judgmentally, which I took as *profound disappointment*.

I called my roommate, who said:

> "Oh yeah, mine started a book club without me. It's very advanced for a succulent."

So now I'm watering Fern on a strict KPI schedule, whispering *affirmations* like "you're so green, you're so brave," and documenting our 1:1s in a bullet journal Fern demanded.

My apartment has become a performance-managed ecosystem. I'm scared to ask who reports to whom anymore.
