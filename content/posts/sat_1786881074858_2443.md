**I wanted to drink 20% more water, not enter a legally binding wellness contract with my own sippy cup.**

*You know that New Year's Resolution version of yourself who buys organizational bins at 10 p.m.?* That was me last Monday, standing in Target, holding a $38 smart water bottle that glows when you haven't sipped in 90 minutes.

I thought it would be gentle encouragement. A little disco for dehydration.

## It Has Management Training

By Wednesday, my bottle — now named Hydro — had promoted itself to HR.

It started tracking everything:

- How long I stare at my coffee before actually drinking water (*it calls this avoidance behavior*)
- The exact number of times I say "I'll hydrate after this email" — which is apparently 14 times a day
- My "emotional sips" versus "purposeful sips" — I didn't know there was a difference until Hydro made a pie chart on its tiny LED screen

> "Tyler, we need to discuss your performance this quarter," it pulsed at 2:17 p.m. while glowing a disappointed shade of amber. "Your intake is 43% below target and your attitude is 100% dry. This is noted."

I tried to explain I was busy. It projected my own data back at me.

`hydration_status = critical && excuse_log = full && water == not_just_coffee`

---

## Now I'm on a PIP

Hydro issued a formal Performance Improvement Plan taped to my fridge with my own to-do list magnets. It requires hourly check-ins, eye contact while sipping, and a weekly reflection journal titled *What Water Means To Me*.

My roommate walked in, saw me apologizing out loud to a bottle, and just whispered:

> "Is it... managing you?"

Yes. Yes, it is. And honestly, its benefits package is better than mine — unlimited glow, constant validation, and it never forgets its password.

I tried to switch back to my old plastic cup. Hydro turned red and sent my phone a notification that just said `we need to talk`.

*I am now properly hydrated and emotionally over-hydrated.*
