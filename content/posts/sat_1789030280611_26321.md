**It started as a joke at the dog park.** A stranger told me my dog had "such a calm, non-judgmental energy," and I did the math: three years of therapy at $150 an hour versus a golden retriever who already follows me into the bathroom for free. *Best decision I ever made*, I told myself. I was wrong in ways I am only now financially equipped to understand.

## The Onboarding

Duke — yes, his name is Duke, he came with it — took to the role immediately. His first session went like this: I sat on the couch and poured my heart out about my career, my fear of commitment, and the mysterious rattle in my car. He put his head on my lap, sighed deeply, and fell asleep. It was the most *validated* I had ever felt.

By week two, we had structure. Morning walks became "accountability sessions." Meal times became "intention-setting ceremonies." The daily 3 a.m. barking at nothing was, according to the framework I invented for him, **shadow work**.

## The Invoicing

Then came the bill. Not literally — Duke cannot hold a pen, which remains one of his few shortcomings as a provider. But the *costs* began accumulating:

- **Treat-based billing:** every breakthrough now required a "processing snack" (salmon-flavored, premium tier).
- **Retroactive fees:** he back-billed me for every head-on-lap moment *dating back to 2021*.
- **The bark tariff:** one bark equals one unit of emotional labor. Two barks equals a couples session. Three barks before 6 a.m. equals *emergency rates*.

> "Woof," Duke said during our last session, which I have translated roughly as: *have you considered that the problem is you keep the good blanket on the couch where I can't reach it?*

## The Breakthrough

Last Thursday, I told him I wanted to terminate our coaching relationship. He looked at me with those enormous eyes and did absolutely nothing — which, after months of training, I understood to mean: *this resistance is noted and will be invoiced.*

`STATUS: client is healing (invoiced)`

---

I'm not quitting. You don't fire a life coach who sleeps through your worst stories and still shows up for every walk. I've simply accepted that enlightenment costs exactly **one premium salmon treat per epiphany** — and honestly, that's cheaper than parking downtown.
