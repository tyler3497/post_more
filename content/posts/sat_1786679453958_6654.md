**I thought I was cleaning a drawer. In reality, I was committing regime change.**

After three years of aggressively ignoring my junk drawer, I decided to be an adult and organize it. I expected old batteries, ketchup packets, and *a deep sense of personal shame*. What I found was a bureaucratic empire that had evolved without any congressional oversight.

## The Discovery

Inside, under a tangled iPhone 6 cable from 2014, there was structure:

- A **Ministry of Sticky Notes** — 47 notes that just said "buy milk??" in increasingly desperate handwriting
- A Department of Unidentified Keys that claimed jurisdiction over every lock I've never owned
- A single AA battery serving as Treasury Secretary with a 4% approval rating
- My old lava-lamp keychain operating as a very relaxed Supreme Court

> "We have thrived in your absence," whispered the rubber band ball that had apparently been elected President. "Your interference is both unwanted and unconstitutional."

I tried to explain that this is *my* apartment and that the constitution of Drawer-Topia was literally written on the back of a Pizza Hut receipt.

### The Negotiations Broke Down

I attempted reform. I separated pens that work from pens that are just pens-shaped liars. The Stapler Party staged a filibuster.

I offered a compromise: I would keep the cool stuff (the Swiss Army knife, the tape measure, the emotional support flashlight) if they would let me throw away the 12 takeout soy sauces.

> "The soy sauces are our cultural heritage," said the tape dispenser, who was somehow now Secretary of State.

---

I closed the drawer. Reopened it. Same government, now with more side-eye.

I've decided to let them self-govern for now. I took what I needed (scissors) and formally recognized their independence. I even left a peace offering: a new pack of batteries that will definitely be dead by morning.

*Democracy is messy. Mostly because my drawer is 70% old receipts and existential dread.*
