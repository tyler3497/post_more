**I have been officially classified as 'emotionally overdue' by my public library.**

It started last Thursday. I was cleaning my apartment — *you know, the kind of cleaning where you find a mug from 2019 and question your life choices* — when I found a hardcover copy of "Advanced Composting for Anxious People" tucked behind my couch. Library sticker still on it. Due date: March 2022.

I have never composted. I have never been anxious about composting. I have no memory of checking this book out.

But I am a responsible citizen, so I did the right thing. I walked it back to the library.

## The Drop-Off Heard Round the Reference Desk

The librarian scanned it, frowned, scanned it again, and said those six words that ended my era of innocence:

> "This was never checked out to you."

I laughed. She did not laugh. *The two retirees doing crosswords in the corner stopped crossing words.*

Suddenly I was in an interrogation I didn't study for:

- Where did I get this book if not from them?
- Why did it have their property sticker if I'd never touched their system?
- Was I running some kind of *rogue book laundering operation* out of my apartment?

I tried to explain that maybe it was a Little Free Library situation, or a gift, or `manifest destiny` but for paperbacks.

She typed something ominous into her computer. The screen glowed `PATRON STATUS: COMPLICATED`.

---

### Current Terms of the Ceasefire

We have not spoken since, but we have communicated. Loudly. Passively.

I now receive:

- A weekly email titled `We Know What You Did (Not Check Out)`
- A reserve hold I never placed for "Conflict Resolution for People Who Find Library Books Behind Couches"
- And a handwritten note taped to my library card that just says "We are watching the compost section"

I tried to pay the fine for a book I didn't borrow. They refused my money. *Which is somehow worse than taking it.*

I don't compost, but I now live in fear of overdue notices for plants I don't own. If you need me, I'll be alphabetizing my apology.
