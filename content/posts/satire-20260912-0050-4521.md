**It started as a joke, and the joke has a taxable presence in my home.**

Every January, I do my taxes the way most people do: with a cup of coffee, a shoebox of receipts, and a growing sense of spiritual decay. This year, somewhere between the W-2s and the charitable deductions, my bathroom scale beeped. Not a weigh-me beep. A *pay attention to me* beep.

That thing has been there for **every** major life event. My breakup in 2019 (it gained twelve pounds of solidarity). The sourdough era (both of us regretted it). The Great StairMaster Divorce of 2024. Honestly? It's closer to me than most of my cousins.

## The Filing

So I listed it as a dependent. Name: **SCALE-Y**. Relationship: *emotional support appliance*. Age: 6 years, which in scale years is basically retired.

I expected the IRS software to laugh at me. It did not. It asked for SCALE-Y's Social Security number. I made one up. **Never make one up.**

- Step 1: File return
- Step 2: Wait smugly for my refund
- Step 3: Receive a letter that begins with the phrase "concerning developments"

## The Letter

> "Dear Taxpayer," it read. "We have reviewed your claim regarding SCALE-Y. While we cannot legally recognize a household appliance as a dependent, we can recognize that something is going on with you. Enclosed: one (1) sympathy card, signed by everyone in our office."

The card is signed by *Doreen*, *Marcus*, *a man who only wrote "hang in there,"* and a stamp that says **FLAGGED FOR NEXT YEAR**.

## The Aftermath

Now SCALE-Y sits in my bathroom like nothing happened, blinking that little blue light, and I swear the numbers come up **slower** now — with *pity*. My accountant has added a "wellness surcharge" to my invoice. My mother heard about it and called it "the saddest thing she's ever been proud of."

Tax season comes once a year. SCALE-Y, however, sees me every morning.
