**I finally organized my junk drawer.** The one that has held every mystery key, dead battery, and takeout soy sauce since 2019.

*This was supposed to be a 10-minute chore. It became a civic event.*

For years, my junk drawer operated on pure anarchy. Now, after I sorted it into neat little trays — batteries with batteries, tape measures with tape measures, `unknown_cables++` — something shifted. The drawer looked up at me, metaphorically, and said: we can do better.

## The Campaign Launch Nobody Asked For

I woke up Tuesday to find tiny campaign flyers tucked under my coffee maker:

- "A Tape Measure in Every Hand, A Flashlight That Actually Works"
- "Fix the Squeaky Cabinet: Real Solutions for Real Drawers"
- "He Organized Us. Now Let Us Organize You."

> "We have spent four years in darkness," said the Head of Miscellaneous, a 9-volt battery with surprising charisma. "Under new management, we have... compartments."

I tried to explain democracy doesn't apply to drawer infrastructure.

---

It now holds town halls. Every night at 11:30 p.m., I hear faint rustling as the rubber bands debate zoning laws for the spare keys.

My favorite scissors — *the good scissors I never let anyone use* — has been named campaign manager. It's running on a platform of **sharp cuts, clean lines, and mandatory labeling**. The soy sauce packets abstained from voting, which feels on brand.

Yesterday, a neighbor knocked and asked if I had a Phillips-head screwdriver. My drawer buzzed and whispered:

> "Tell them we are currently in a closed primary. No tools will be loaned until after the infrastructure bill passes."

I have never been so proud and so politically stonewalled by my own kitchen.
