**I finally returned my library books on time.** *Three of them.* Without an email that says "gentle reminder (FINAL NOTICE)."

For context, my library card has more late fees than my streaming subscriptions. My previous record was returning a cookbook 11 months late with a dried basil leaf marking page 47 like some kind of *herb-based apology*.

## The Moment Everything Went Wrong

I walked up to the desk, confident, smug even. I slid the books across like a blackjack dealer who just learned how to read.

The librarian — let's call her Maribel, because that's on her name tag and she has *real power* — scanned the first one. No beep of shame. Second one. No sigh. Third one. No lecture about civic responsibility.

> "Wow," she said, pushing her glasses down. "You're... early? Are you okay? Is this a cry for help or are you hitting on me?"

*I was not hitting on her. I was just trying to be a functioning adult for once.*

### Evidence That I Have Ruined My Reputation by Being Responsible:

- She whispered to the other librarian and they both looked at me like I'd solved cold fusion with a library card
- My account now says `status: IN GOOD STANDING` which feels *aggressively formal* for someone who once returned a novel with a pizza receipt as a bookmark
- She stamped the receipt so hard it left an indent — I think it was respect, or fear, or both
- She set aside the new releases "for you, since you're so reliable now" — *I have created expectations*

Now Maribel waves at me when I walk by. Yesterday she asked if I wanted to join the Friends of the Library. *The Friends.* Like it's a secret society for people who know where the Dewey Decimal section ends.

I have to keep returning things on time forever now. I didn't sign up for this kind of commitment. I just wanted to not owe $4.75.
