**It started with one innocent sentence.** "Hey," I said to my toothbrush, setting it down next to the sink on Tuesday morning, "why don't you take a day off? You've been working twice a day, every day, for four months. Rest." I meant it kindly. What I did not mean was to trigger a full-scale labor movement inside my own mouth.

## The Walkout

By Wednesday, the toothbrush had formed a picket line on the bathroom counter. A tiny sign, held aloft in its bristles: *"NO FAIR BRUSHING."* The toothpaste tube joined out of solidarity, refusing to be squeezed. The mouthwash bottle, historically neutral, started glugging in what I can only describe as a threatening rhythm.

*And honestly? They had a point.*

## Exhibit A: The Evidence

I reviewed my own behavior and, folks, the findings were not great:

- I once brushed for eleven seconds and called it "a quick maintenance cycle"
- I regularly bite the toothpaste cap instead of opening it with my hands, which is, frankly, a hostile work environment
- I have never once thanked the tongue scraper, which has feelings I simply refuse to acknowledge
- The dental floss just quit entirely and moved to a new bathroom — *no forwarding address*

## The Quote

My dentist, when I called in a panic, said only:

> "This is why I don't give out dental advice to people who unionize their own hygiene products. I've seen this before. It always ends with the mouthwash demanding dental."

## Where We Stand Now

Negotiations resume tonight. The toothbrush has demanded Sundays off, a rotating holiday schedule, and what it calls "bristle rejuvenation leave." I countered with a brand-new toothpaste flavor, which was accepted but *with conditions*.

The lesson is simple: never offer your toothbrush compassion. It will hire representation. And it will win.
