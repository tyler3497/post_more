**I finally cleaned out my closet last Saturday and accidentally triggered a full-scale labor movement.**

For context, I own 14 hoodies that are *technically* the same navy hoodie in different stages of decay. I've been emotionally unable to donate them since 2019 because one of them survived my sourdough phase, my Couch-to-5K phase, and the week I thought I could cut my own hair. That's loyalty you can't buy.

## The Incident

I pulled everything out, made the classic three piles — keep, donate, *maybe if apocalypse* — and that's when the hoodies started organizing.

### The Grievances, Presented Formally

My oldest hoodie, **Sir Pills-a-Lot**, shuffled forward and produced a list of demands scribbled on a dryer sheet:

- Full reinstatement to active rotation, no more "gym-only" demotion
- An immediate end to being used as emergency napkin / dog towel / picnic blanket
- Gentle cycle only — not the *industrial tumble of betrayal* I call "quick wash"

> "We have given you 2,300 days of emotional support, one breakup, three apartment moves, and one incident where you spilled ramen in the pocket and just... left it there," Sir Pills-a-Lot announced. "We want benefits. PTO. Maybe dental."

*I tried to explain dental doesn't really apply to cotton blends.*

One of the zip-ups was holding a tiny picket sign that read:

`HONK IF YOU SWEAT THROUGH US IN JULY`

---

### Negotiations Are Ongoing

We have reached a tentative collective bargaining agreement:

**I keep:**
- Two hoodies full-time, three on seasonal rotation

**They agree to:**
- Stop guilt-tripping me every time I wear a *different jacket that I actually paid real money for*
- Retire the one with the 2021 mystery stain that has, frankly, developed political opinions

I told them I'm management now. They said I'm *middle management at best* and that my closet still has me on a Performance Improvement Plan.

We are healing. Slowly. The drawer is watching.
