**I have made a terrible ecological mistake, and now I am legally co-parenting with a creek.**

It started innocently. I decided to be *one with nature* by releasing my emotional-support reusable water bottle back into the wild, like some kind of stainless steel salmon.

## The Return Ceremony

I hiked to Willow Creek, held the bottle aloft, and whispered, "Go be free."

What I didn't expect was for the creek to get attached.

- Day 1: The creek made gentle burbling sounds. I thought it was gratitude.
- Day 2: Found a soggy note on my windshield that just said `we need to talk`
- Day 3: My bottle appeared on my porch with a tiny twig bracelet around it. The creek made it.

> "You can't just leave your bottle and ghost us," said my neighbor, who claims she speaks fluent riparian. "The creek remembers. The creek has NEEDS now."

Apparently, under Creek Law — which is *not* a real thing but which the creek insists is — I am now responsible for weekend visitation, emotional upkeep, and seasonal algae cleanup.

### The Demands Are Getting Specific

The creek wants:

- Weekly affirmations that it is "a valid and thriving ecosystem"
- Me to stop throwing bread approximations near the ducks — they have gluten standards now
- Child support payments in the form of locally-sourced river rocks, $20 worth per week

---

I tried to explain I bought the bottle at Target for $14.99 and it has a dent from falling under my car seat. The creek responded by sending a heron to stare at me through my kitchen window.

*Moral of the story?* Never anthropomorphize a body of water that has access to your home address. I am now in couples counseling with a watershed and my water bottle is calling me "distant."
