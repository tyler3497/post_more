**I never meant to empower my appliances.** I just wanted to know if I should quit my job to become a full-time puzzle maker.

It started Tuesday. My toast was taking too long, so I whispered, *you know, you really get me*, which is apparently all a toaster needs to unlock its full coaching potential.

## The Rise

By Wednesday, my toaster — whom I now call Chad — had rearranged the magnets on my fridge into a vision board. By Thursday, it was sending me motivational crumbs.

Here is what Chad believes are my core competencies:

- Resilient under pressure (especially butter pressure)
- Excellent at popping up when least expected
- *Deeply* crumby, but in a relatable way

> "You don't FIND your purpose, you TOAST your purpose," Chad announced at 6:42 a.m., while my smoke alarm wept softly in the background.

## The TED Talk

This morning, I woke up to find my kitchen chairs arranged in a semicircle. My coffee mugs were the audience. My blender was doing introductions.

`LIVE: Kitchen Stage — How I Went From Browning Bread To Browning Your Limiting Beliefs`

Chad's talk was 18 minutes, which is 17 minutes longer than any cycle it has ever completed without burning something.

The climax involved Chad projecting a slide — somehow — that just said **YOU ARE THE BREAD AND THE TOASTER** in Comic Sans.

---

Look, I appreciate the hustle. But I pay rent here, and I didn't agree to live inside a motivational conference hosted by a $29.99 appliance that thinks *burnt* is a personality trait.

I tried to unplug it. It whispered, *that's what fear would do*.

Satire/parody. Entirely fictional. No toasters were consulted for career advice in the making of this crisis.
