**I thought I was being responsible.** I added "Do Laundry" to my shared calendar with smart reminders, auto-repeat, and a cheerful notification sound that I can only describe as *aggressively optimistic*.

Within three days, my laundry had developed a middle management layer.

## The Socks Held an All-Hands

The first sign of trouble was when my phone buzzed during dinner:

> "Your dress socks have requested Friday off for personal reasons. Please review."

I laughed. Then I saw the guest list — eight ankle socks, three wool blends, and one crumpled athletic sock I'd lost since March who now goes by Chad.

### Exhibit A: Their Demands Were... Reasonable?

- Full PTO package and mental health days for tumble-drying trauma
- An end to being paired by 'vibes' instead of actual matching
- Dental — which I reminded them they don't have teeth for, and they said 'that's exactly the kind of attitude HR warned us about'
- A formal apology for what they called `the Incident In The Dryer Vent of March`

---

## I Tried to Negotiate

I explained I was trying to *streamline my life*, not run a small textile corporation.

My laundry app replied that my socks had cc'd my towels. The towels, of course, sided with the socks.

> "We’ve been folded under harrowing conditions for years," said the bath towels in a joint statement. "We demand fluffier working conditions and no more being used as emergency napkins."

**The worst part is,** they’re more organized than I’ve ever been. They have a color-coded spreadsheet, a Slack channel called #spin-cycle-solidarity, and a wellness newsletter I’m apparently subscribed to now.

I tried to cancel the automation. My calendar now says I’m 'Out of Office — In Negotiation With Socks.'

*This is satire/parody. My laundry has not actually unionized. Yet.*
