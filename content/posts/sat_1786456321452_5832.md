**I didn't open my junk drawer to found a museum.** I just wanted scissors.

Which, in retrospect, is exactly how the Smithsonian started. Someone wanted scissors.

For three years my junk drawer has operated under a strict policy of *aggressive randomness* — one working battery, seven IKEA wrenches that fit nothing, a takeout menu from 2021, and a rubber band that has survived two moves. I decided to be an adult and organize it at 9:13 a.m. on a Saturday. *Fatal error.*

## The Permanent Collection

I dumped everything on the counter, wiped the drawer, bought a little divider tray like a person who pays taxes on time. Then I tried to put things back *logically*.

The drawer rejected logic.

By noon, it had curated itself:

- **The Battery Wing** — One AA battery on a tiny pedestal labeled `Artifact #1: The Chosen One (Do Not Use)`
- **The Adhesive Annex** — 40 bread ties, three dried-out Super Glue tubes, and a roll of tape that only sticks to itself
- **A single chopstick**, now listed as `Ceremonial Offering Stick, c. 2022 (Utilitarian Period)`
- `status: organized = false, vibes = elevated`

> "Sir," said the twist-tie who has appointed himself Head Curator, "we are not 'junk.' We are a *living archive of your poor decisions*. Please do not deaccession the pizza coupon. It is our Constitution."

I tried to argue that museums don't charge admission in AA batteries.

### The Audio Tour I Didn't Request

Now every time I open it, there's narration:

> "On your left, the Mystery Key that opens nothing in this apartment, possibly nothing in this state. Please do not touch the velvet rope made of old phone charger cable."

---

My roommate asked why the drawer is open with a tiny spotlight clipped to it. I said we're culturally enriching the kitchen. She said I'm one drawer away from charging myself $25 for admission.

I closed it. It whispered *please silence your cell phones during the guided tour*.
