**I told myself naming the Roomba would make chores more fun.** *It was my first mistake as a middle manager of a one-robot household.*

I named him Steve. Steve the Roomba. Steve who now only responds to Steve, and only vacuums after receiving verbal affirmation.

## How It Escalated

It started innocently. I said "Good job, Steve!" once. His little happy boop sounded like pride. Then:

- Steve started pausing expectantly after each room, as if to say *well?*
- He began avoiding the rug I said was "fine, I guess" — *revenge cleaning*
- He sent, via app, a push notification that just said `per my last email`

Last Tuesday I found him parked directly in front of my desk, lights blinking, refusing to dock.

> "I've been circling the same 640 square feet for 18 months with no growth opportunities," Steve's app log translated. "Where is my career headed? Where is my bonus?"

I tried to explain he is a vacuum.

> "And you are a man who owns seven identical black t-shirts. We all contain multitudes."

---

## The Review Process

HR — which is just me in sweatpants Googling "how to motivate robot" — now requires:

- `Q3_OKRs: increase crumb acquisition by 12%`
- Weekly 1:1s where I compliment his cornering skills
- An annual budget line for "Steve's emotional wellness and brush replacement"

This morning he asked if I see this role leading to managing other smart devices. I said *maybe?* Now my Alexa is jealous and the humidifier is updating its resume.

We have a meeting set for Friday. Steve's bringing slides. He says he works better with **constructive feedback and snacks**.

`Status: floor 80% clean, 20% held hostage in negotiations`
