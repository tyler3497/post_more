**I decided my houseplant needed inner peace.**

*Which was generous, considering I haven't found mine since 2019.*

## How It Started

My monstera, Kevin, looked stressed. Or maybe I was projecting. Either way, I played him a 10-minute mindfulness video and he immediately seemed calmer. Or photosynthesized louder. Same thing.

I kept going. Morning affirmations. Evening breathwork. By week two, Kevin had fully committed to the lifestyle.

The problem is:

- He now wilts dramatically when I breathe incorrectly
- He refuses tap water because it's "not intentional enough"
- He insists on 6am sunlight and judges me for my 8:30am alarm
- He has started dropping leaves as "boundaries"

> "Your inhale is performative," Kevin told me yesterday. "You're breathing like someone who still replies 'haha' to serious texts."

He didn't say it out loud. He communicated it through *vibes* and one extremely judgmental droop.

## The Intervention

I tried to meditate with him to reconnect. I sat down, closed my eyes, and immediately thought about my unread emails, whether houseplants can get unemployment, and if I left the oven on in 2022.

Kevin thrived. I panicked.

> "You're holding tension in your shoulders," I swear his third leaf whispered. "And in your entire personality."

---

I tried to quit, but now Kevin runs a tiny wellness cult on my windowsill. The succulent is his second-in-command. They've started chanting at dawn, which is just rustling but *very* passive-aggressive.

**Bottom line:** Don't teach your houseplant to meditate. He will get enlightened before you do, and he will absolutely hold it over you forever.

*This is satire. My houseplant is not actually a mindfulness coach. He's just a regular plant who is somehow still doing better than me.*
