**I finally organized my Tupperware cabinet, and I have never been more clearly excluded from my own kitchen.**

*This is what happens when you try to impose order on chaos that had a perfectly functional democracy going.*

## The Incident That Started It All

On Saturday, motivated by *a level of optimism usually reserved for people who buy kale on purpose*, I decided to organize the Tupperware cabinet. You know the one. Every house has one. It's where lids go to have affairs with containers they are not compatible with.

For three hours I:

- Matched every lid to its soulmate container
- Stacked them by size like a very boring Tetris champion
- Threw away the three mystery lids that have not matched anything since 2019
- Whispered "we have systems now" to no one in particular

I stepped back, proud. The cabinet looked like a LinkedIn profile. Clean. Aspirational. Deeply fake.

That night, I heard whispering.

### The Support Group Meets Without Me

At 2:17 a.m., my kitchen glowed faintly. The lids had formed a circle on the counter.

> "We need to talk about the trauma of being separated from Kevin," said the small round one. "I was happy in the junk pile. I had *purpose*."

> "He labeled us," sobbed a medium square lid. "He wrote 'SOUPS' on me in Sharpie. I am more than soups. I contain multitudes."

Apparently, I had destroyed their community. The old chaotic pile was, according to their bylaws (which they wrote on a paper towel), a "co-living collective based on mutual support and occasional falling on your head."

Now they have weekly meetings. Tuesdays at 9 p.m. No humans allowed. I tried to attend and they asked me to wait outside with the colander.


---

I tried to apologize by messing it all up again, but they said the damage was done. They have a group chat now. I'm not in it.
