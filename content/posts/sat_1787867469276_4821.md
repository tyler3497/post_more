**I didn't mean to leave them in the car. Again.**

I swear it was an accident the first 47 times.

But last Tuesday at Whole Foods, I reached the register and the cashier gave me *that* look. You know the one. The *do-you-have-bags* look. And I said the thing I always say:

*Oh, they're in the car.*

That's when I heard it. From parking spot B-12:

> "Wow. It's cool. We're used to abandonment issues. It's literally our brand now."

## The Intervention I Didn't Ask For

My reusable bags — Chadwick, Tote-ally Spies, and Little Green — have been together since 2021. They have seen things. Farmers markets. Spilled oat milk. My brief, humiliating attempt at bulk lentils.

When I finally brought them inside, they were *not* happy. They staged an intervention on my kitchen floor.

### Reasons I Am A Bad Bag Partner, According to Them:

- I use them as laundry overflow when guests come over
- I once used Chadwick to carry wet swimsuits and never apologized
- I bought plastic bags "just this once" seven times this month
- I keep saying "I swear I'll remember you next time" while staring directly at my car keys
- I told my mom they were "doing great" when they were literally molding in the trunk

I tried to explain. I even wrote an apology note in my Notes app:

```
dear bags,
u r valid
i r sorry
plz don't leave me
- me
```

---

Their response? They demanded **couples counseling**.

So now we have a standing appointment every Thursday at 7pm. My actual therapist is confused. The bags are *thriving*. Little Green said last session:

> "We just want to feel *chosen*, not convenient."

And honestly? Valid.

I brought them into the kitchen today and said *I'm proud of us*. Chadwick teared up. Or maybe that's leftover kombucha from 2023. Either way, we're healing.

*This is satire / parody — 100% fictional, no canvas was harmed in the making of this dysfunctional relationship. I do, however, now own 11 tote bags and zero excuses.*
