**I finally did it. I asked my laundry hamper for relationship advice.**

*Which tells you exactly how my love life is going.*

It started innocently. I was standing over the hamper at 11:47 p.m., holding a single sock like it was a rosary, whispering "where does it all go wrong?" My hamper — a tall, wicker, aggressively non-judgmental presence I've owned since college — creaked. I took that as an invitation.

## The Hamper's Wisdom Was Uncomfortably Specific

I said, "Should I text them back?"

The hamper did not answer, because it is a hamper. But in my head, it answered in the voice of a retired therapist who smokes one cigarette a year for drama.

For the record, its advice was:

- Stop folding your feelings into neat little squares and then never wearing them
- If someone leaves you on read, do not leave their hoodie on the floor for three weeks as revenge
- You have 17 single socks and zero closure — this is not a coincidence
- `emotional_baggage.exe` is at 94% capacity, please remove bulky sweaters

I nodded. It felt profound at midnight.

> "You keep saying you're looking for 'the one,'" the hamper said, or I imagined it said, "but you won't even commit to a laundry schedule. Every Sunday you say 'this is the Sunday.' It is never the Sunday."

---

The socks heard everything.

This morning, I went to get dressed and my sock drawer was... tense. One athletic sock whispered to a wool sock, *"He's asking the hamper now? We literally live with him."* My favorite striped pair is giving me the silent treatment. They've paired off incorrectly on purpose — argyle with ankle sock, no show with knee-high — as a protest.

I tried to apologize. I said, "Guys, I'm working on myself."

The hamper creaked again. I think it meant, "We need to talk about boundaries."

*This is satire/parody. Completely fictional. No laundry hampers or socks were given relationship counseling credentials in the making of this story.*
