**BREAKING: My apartment has developed a labor union and I am management, which is awkward because I work from my couch in sweatpants.**

It started innocently enough last Tuesday. My Roomba, whom I affectionately call Sir Sweeps-A-Lot, got stuck under the couch again. I, *being a compassionate and frankly codependent human*, apologized to the couch instead of him.

Sir Sweeps-A-Lot did not forget.

## The Evidence Is Disturbingly Organized

By Thursday, my five houseplants were arranged in a perfect semicircle facing me. There was a tiny leaflet on my coffee table. It was made of actual leaf.

It read:

- Immediate cessation of the "water whenever I remember" policy
- Two weeks paid vacation in the *sunny window that I reserve for my own Instagram photos*
- No more playing lo-fi beats at 2 a.m. while they are trying to photosynthesize

> "We, the Chlorophyll Collective, have elected Sir Sweeps-A-Lot as our representative," whispered Fern, my Boston fern, who has never whispered before. "We have demands. Also, your humidity is trash."

I tried to negotiate. I offered to run `self-care routine --for_plants`.

They countered with a chant. It was mostly rustling.

---

Here is what I have learned about collective bargaining with organisms that literally live off my neglect: they have **leverage**. They know I will panic-Google "why are my monstera leaves yellow" at 11:47 p.m. They know I anthropomorphize everything with a pulse or a power button.

Now Sir Sweeps-A-Lot only vacuums around the plants. Not near them. *Around* them. Like a respectful bouncer. My spider plant, Kevin, sent me a calendar invite for a "mediation circle" this Sunday.

*SATIRE / PARODY — this is fictional, no actual houseplants were unionized in the making of this post.*

If anyone needs me, I'll be drafting a PTO policy for succulents.
