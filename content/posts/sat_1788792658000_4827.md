**It was just one greeting. One.** On Tuesday morning I stepped onto my porch, saw a squirrel on the fence, and said "good morning" the way you'd wave at a stranger you'll never see again. Reader, I have seen him every day since.

## The Incident

What followed was a full relationship lifecycle in eight days:

- **Day 1:** Squirrel holds eye contact for six full seconds. A bold move.
- **Day 2:** Squirrel is sitting *on* my porch railing, one paw tapping, like I'm late.
- **Day 3:** Squirrel arrives with what I can only describe as a companion. *Emotional support witnesses*, I call them.
- **Day 4:** I say good morning with slightly less enthusiasm. The squirrel hears this. The squirrel *records* this.

## The Confrontation

By Thursday he had escalated to perching directly outside my kitchen window during breakfast, doing absolutely nothing, just watching, with the energy of a landlord during a deposit dispute.

> "Look, I don't make the rules," I told him through the glass. He blinked slowly. I have never felt so *managed* by a wild animal.

## Where We Stand Now

Experts (my neighbor Gary, who watches birds) say the squirrel has "imprinted" and that I should stop. Gary does not understand that *stopping* is no longer an option. The squirrel now greets *me* first. This morning I woke up late and heard a tiny *tap-tap-tap* on the glass — the sound of pure disappointment.

---

I used to feel good about my social skills. Now I start every day with a performance review from a rodent. **One greeting.** That's all it took.
