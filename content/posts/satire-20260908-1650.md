**I was just trying to reduce food waste.** That's all. A little sticky note, a little accountability, a little "hey, eat me before I become a science experiment." Apparently, my refrigerator read it as a declaration of war.

Last Tuesday I stuck a neon-yellow note on the fridge that said *"Eat the leftovers — love, Management"* in my very best passive-aggressive handwriting. By Thursday morning, the leftovers had elected a shop steward.

## The Grievances

According to the formal complaint (written in ketchup on a paper towel), my crimes include:

- Opening the fridge, staring directly at the meatloaf for a full nine seconds, and reaching *behind it* for the leftover pad thai
- Reorganizing the shelves without consulting the middle shelf's seniority structure
- Calling the three-day-old rice "sus" within earshot of the other containers
- Promising the burrito "tonight for sure" four nights in a row

> "We demand humane expiration dates," said the half-eaten lasagna, whom I have now been informed is named Gerald. "And an end to the midnight snack raids, which occur without warning and have *devastated morale.*"

## Negotiations Have Stalled

I tried to reason with them. I told Gerald that a leftover is, by definition, a *temporary* food arrangement. He said that kind of language is exactly why they're considering a walkout.

So far their demands are:

- One guaranteed reheating per week
- A clear-label policy (the "mystery stew" from August is now their union mascot and *cannot* be thrown away)
- An official apology for the time I sniffed the milk and said *"yikes"* loud enough for the whole dairy section to hear

*Management* — that's me, the guy in sweatpants eating cereal over the sink — has proposed a compromise: I eat two leftovers this week and we all pretend the sticky note was a motivational quote.

---

Gerald is considering it. But the mystery stew just voted to strike, so we'll see.
