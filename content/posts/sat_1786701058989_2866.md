## The Ice Had to Go

**I did the responsible adult thing and defrosted my freezer.** *I know.* After three years of pretending that two-inch glacier around the peas was "extra insulation."

It took four hours, three towels, and a profound lack of judgment. I didn't realize I was also defrosting its sense of self.

### The Thaw

For the first hour it was stoic. Silent dripping. Professional.

Then it started.

> "Was I... too cold?" it whistled through the melting ice shelf. "Was my whole personality just... frostbite with a door handle?"

Apparently, when you spend your entire life at -18°C, you don't develop hobbies. You develop **layers**. 

I tried to be supportive. I said, "You're so good at keeping things preserved!"

It spiraled harder.

The evidence it presented for its identity crisis:

- **Exhibit A:** It never learned to be warm. Literally. Its only emotional range is "chill" and "colder chill"
- *Exhibit B:* All its friends are frozen vegetables who never talk back, which it now calls "codependent"
- Exhibit C: That one time I left the door open in 2023 and it had to work overtime — it thinks that was a *cry for help*

### Now We're Doing Inner Work

It asked me to run `defrost --emotions` and I didn't have the heart to say that's not a command.

---

I'm currently sitting on my kitchen floor watching my appliance journal about whether it's more than just a box that makes ice cubes sad. It wants to try being a pantry. It wants to "explore room temperature."

I told it to take it slow. Start as a cooler.

> "But what if I'm meant to be a wine fridge?" it whispered.

I closed the door gently. It sighed cold air directly into my soul.

`Status: 98% defrosted, 2% emotionally available, 100% still leaking on my socks`
