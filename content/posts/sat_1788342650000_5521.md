**I did not realize I was in a committed relationship with my laundry chair until I broke its heart.**

For eight months, that IKEA chair has held every shirt I own that was "too clean to wash, too dirty to fold." It was a system. A lifestyle. A textile Jenga tower that knew me better than my own calendar did.

Yesterday, in a fit of unhinged productivity, I folded it all. Every sock. Every mysterious hoodie. I even matched things. I put them *in drawers*.

## The Chair Years

The Chair wasn't just furniture. It was:

- my closet's overflow parking
- my official "I'll deal with this Sunday" archive
- my roommate's least favorite modern art installation
- the only thing in my apartment that truly listened when I said "I have nothing to wear"

*It understood me.* It never judged when I wore the same three shirts on rotation for two weeks straight because laundry is a social construct.

### The Fallout

I woke up this morning and The Chair was... empty. Naked. Dramatically lit by my window like it was in a breakup music video directed by my own guilt.

> "Oh, so we're just *putting clothes away* now?" it said, in the voice all betrayed furniture uses. "After everything? After I held your 'good jeans' through three situationships and a winter?"

It then left a note. Handwritten. In dust.

```
chair_status = "abandoned"
emotional_support = None
fold_at_own_risk = true
```

---

So now my room is clean, my clothes are folded, and my chair is giving me the cold shoulder. Literally cold. No laundry insulation. I sat in it and it creaked louder than usual, like *how dare you use me for sitting*.

**This is satire.** No chairs were emotionally harmed in the making of this essay. *My chair is fine.* It's just currently pursuing its dream of being a chair again, and honestly, I'm proud of it but also slightly scared of what it will become without purpose.

If you need me, I'll be apologizing to furniture and trying to remember which drawer I put my personality in.
