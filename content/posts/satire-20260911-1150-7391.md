**I only wanted to have a quick motivational chat with myself.** You know, the kind where you look yourself in the eye and say something like *"we're going to crush it today."* Instead, my mirror decided this was the moment to establish boundaries.

It started this morning. I walked into the bathroom, toothbrush in hand, and said the words every reflective surface dreads: *"We need to talk."*

The mirror did not crack. It did something worse. It showed me **from behind.**

## A Statement, Not a Reflection

I stood there, foam at the mouth, staring at the back of my own head like I'd been demoted to background extra in my own life. I waved. The back of my head did not wave back, which — fair enough — was consistent.

I listed my concerns:

- The mirror never acknowledges the "you got this" nod
- It highlights bedhead at what can only be described as *prosecutorial* angles
- Lately it has been reflecting me roughly 0.3 seconds late, like it's deciding whether to bother

Then I said the truly unforgivable thing. I told it I might try a different mirror.

> "Fine," the mirror seemed to say, in the sense that silence can be devastatingly specific. "Look at me from *this* angle, then."

## The Silent Treatment, Weaponized

Since then, every mirror encounter has been the same. Front door? Back of head. Car visor? Back of head. *Spoon?* Somehow, back of head.

My reflection has become the one person in my life who never faces me, and honestly? The maturity is admirable. It's not yelling. It's not shattering. It's just quietly refusing to make eye contact until I apologize.

---

So this is me, formally and publicly, apologizing to a piece of glass: I am sorry. You are not a fair-weather reflective surface. You were there through every bad haircut, and I treated you like a screensaver.

I turned around this morning and whispered *"I'm sorry"* to the empty air. And in the mirror, for the first time in a week — the back of my head **nodded.**

*Progress.*
