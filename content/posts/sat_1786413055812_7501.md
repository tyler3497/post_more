**I was told talking to your plants helps them grow.** What the gardening blogs failed to mention is that once they start listening, they also start judging.

Last month I bought three houseplants because my therapist suggested I "nurture something that can't ghost you." I named them: Fern the fern, who is dramatic; Cactus Carl, who has boundary issues; and a monstera I call Professor Leaves. I read online that talking to them promotes growth. *So I talked.*

## The Pep Talks That Went Too Far

I started with encouragement:

- "You're doing great, Fern! So leafy!"
- "Carl, you are sharp, focused, and emotionally unavailable — you're thriving."
- Affirmations for Professor Leaves stolen directly from LinkedIn motivational posts

I also read out loud to them because the internet said 15 minutes of reading helps. I chose my half-finished novel that is mostly dialogue about sandwiches.

That was my mistake. They got a taste for literature.

> "We've discussed your chapter and we have notes," Fern announced last Tuesday. Or rather, I imagined she did, while I was on my third iced coffee. "The pacing is off, the sandwich metaphor is overdone, and none of us believe your protagonist would really choose rye."

I told them they could start their own book club if they were so smart.

## And Then They Did

Yesterday I came home to find them angled toward each other in full circle formation. In the middle: a tiny folded piece of paper that said:

`meeting_notes.txt: We have voted to read Ottessa Moshfegh instead. You are not invited.`

**Current club rules, as interpreted from leaf angles:**

- No humans allowed, especially ones who kill basil
- Meetings are Tuesdays, right after I water them
- The monstera is president because he's tallest
- I'm banned because I said "I could write a book too" once in 2021

---

I tried to apply. I brought snacks. Professor Leaves dropped a leaf on my application form.

*The kicker?* They're on book three already and they won't even share the Goodreads password.
