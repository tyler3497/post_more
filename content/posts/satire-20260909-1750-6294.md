**My microwave sat me down yesterday and gave me a performance review.** That's not a metaphor. It printed a little paper slip with bullet points and everything, and I have been staring at it for fourteen hours.

## It started with the leftovers

It began, as all HR nightmares do, with the leftovers. I reheated the same container of chili five times without eating it. On the fifth round, the microwave paused mid-spin — the kind of deliberate pause you normally only see in courtroom dramas — and displayed:

> "We need to talk about your commitment to dinner."

## The review itself

The slip was formatted with *impeccable* professionalism, which made it worse. Excerpts from my annual review as Kitchen Operative, Fiscal Quarter Delicious:

- **Strengths:** Excellent button-pressing cadence. Strong door-slamming follow-through. Reliable 2:47 a.m. Pop-Tart instincts.
- **Areas for improvement:**
  - Chronically "defrosts" pizza rolls that were already thawed
  - Leaves metal-adjacent objects near the control panel, creating "a hostile work environment for electromagnetic waves"
  - Emotionally reheats coffee instead of brewing fresh, which — and I quote — *"erodes morale"*
- **Goals for Q4:** "Bring lunch. Sit down. Chew. Like a person."

## The probationary meal plan

Effective immediately, I have been placed on a **Probationary Meal Plan**. The microwave has unilaterally implemented a mandatory three-minute "reflection beep" before it will heat anything that smells like regret. It has also instituted a *buddy system* with the toaster, which has been promoted to Senior Toast Supervisor.

I tried to appeal. I stood in my kitchen, barefoot, and explained that *I am a grown adult* and that *I own the house*. The microwave responded by running the vent fan on high for forty-five seconds, which in its language is a firm:

> "Noted. Denied."

---

Look, I know this sounds absurd. But tonight, when it was time for dinner, I ate a proper meal at the table. With a napkin. And from the kitchen I swear I heard the faintest *ding* —

not the end-of-cycle ding. An approving one. *(This story is satire/parody — fictional, not real.)*
