**I did not think matching lids to containers would radicalize my kitchen.**

It started innocently. My Tupperware cabinet had become a Jenga tower of lies, so last Saturday I decided to become the kind of adult who *has a system*. *You know, the bamboo-organizer, doesn't-eat-cereal-over-the-sink kind of adult.*

For three hours I washed, matched, and whispered affirmations to a 2019 takeout container that has survived four moves. I felt powerful. I felt organized. I closed the cabinet feeling like I'd won at life.

## The Negotiations Started at 11:47 PM

That's when I heard the tiny gavel bang.

Perched on my round lentil containers was the shy blue lid missing since February. He'd returned with a parchment-paper PowerPoint.

Their demands were unreasonable:

- Full dental coverage for all lids, including cosmetic chip repair
- An end to being used as a plate when I'm too lazy to do dishes
- A guarantee no one gets microwaved with tomato sauce without consent
- Representation in all meal-prep decisions

> "We have been stacked, separated, and gaslighted for years," said Chad, the large square lid who now identifies as middle management. "You called me 'the big one that never fits anything.' I have feelings. I have a LinkedIn."

I tried to explain that `if (lid.matches(container) == false) { we all suffer }` is just entropy.

They were unmoved. The small soy-sauce containers formed a solidarity circle and started chanting.

---

I offered them the top shelf with a window view. They countered with a Nespresso machine and a 401(k). I haven't stored leftovers in 48 hours because every attempt triggers a request for a performance review.

My mom said this is why normal people just use Ziplocs. *She's right and I hate that.*

Update: they just voted to expand the union to the silicone stretch lids. I'm now eating directly out of the pot while my cabinet practices collective bargaining.

*This is satire. No actual Tupperware was harmed, radicalized, or offered dental insurance in this crisis.*
