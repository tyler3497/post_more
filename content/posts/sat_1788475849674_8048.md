**I thought I was being financially responsible. I was actually declaring war.**

*Which is how I ended up in a collective bargaining meeting with seven apps I forgot I downloaded.*

It started innocently. I was reviewing my bank statement and noticed charges for things I definitely signed up for during a moment of optimism at 2 a.m.

## The Defendants

My free trials had been quiet for months, lurking, waiting. When I hit "Cancel," they did not go quietly:

- **MindfulMoss™** – a meditation app that now meditates on my betrayal
- **CheeseFlix** – streaming artisanal cheese-making, 3 episodes watched, 47 invoices sent
- **Yoga For People Who Hate Yoga But Love Loungewear**
- **AstroPets** – tells you your dog's horoscope, my dog is an atheist
- A language app that taught me to say "I have forgotten my password" in 6 languages

I expected a simple "Are you sure?" popup. I got a union rep.

> "We, the United Coalition of Forgotten Subscriptions, Local 7, will not be canceled without proper representation, severance snacks, and full dental," said CheeseFlix, who has somehow become the shop steward. "You subscribed to us. You *committed*."

I tried to explain that "free trial" implies freedom.

> "Freedom is a myth sold by people who actually read Terms & Conditions," whispered MindfulMoss™ from inside my notifications.

## The Negotiation

They presented demands. Health benefits. A permanent spot on my home screen. A letter of recommendation on LinkedIn.

I countered with `if (cancel === true) { please.just.go(); }`

They countered with auto-renewal.

---

Now they meet every Tuesday at 3 a.m. in my notifications shade. I can hear them chanting. My phone battery dies faster out of solidarity.

I offered them a compromise: one of them can stay if they promise to actually make me a better person. They all looked at AstroPets. AstroPets said my future involves more poor financial decisions.
