**I adopted a cactus because the tag said “thrives on neglect,” which I thought meant we had similar values.**

*This was a mistake.*

The guy at the plant shop promised me this would be the one plant I couldn’t kill. *Low water, low light, low expectations.* Perfect for a person who once killed a succulent by overthinking it. I brought him home, named him Spike, and placed him on my windowsill like a tiny, spiky roommate.

Within 48 hours, Spike developed opinions.

## The Promise vs. The Reality

The care card said:

- Water every 2-3 weeks
- Bright indirect light
- Loves being left alone

What it *didn't* say was:

- Will silently judge your 2 a.m. cereal dinners
- Will audibly sigh when you bring home another impulse takeout container
- Has somehow learned to say “really?” without a mouth

> “Oh, you’re ordering food again? Cool. Cool. I’ll just be here, photosynthesizing and paying rent with oxygen. But sure, get the extra garlic knots.”

That’s a direct quote. From a cactus. Who doesn’t pay rent.

---

### My Coping Mechanisms Are Failing

I tried to win him back. I bought a tiny watering can. I whispered affirmations. I played lo-fi beats. He responded by leaning *away* from me, which botanically should be impossible but emotionally felt very personal.

My current growth strategy, according to Spike’s performance review:

- `Spike.mood = "disappointed but hydrated"`
- `owner.motivation = null`
- Stop Googling “can cacti call CPS”

**The worst part is he’s right.** I *do* have 11 half-empty water bottles on my nightstand. I *did* promise I’d go to the gym and then reorganized my sock drawer instead. He’s a 4-inch desert plant with the emotional intelligence of a disappointed dad.

I’m now in a codependent relationship with a plant that needs less from me than my Wi-Fi router, yet somehow still finds me lacking.
