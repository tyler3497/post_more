**I made the mistake of trying to be a good plant parent.**

My monstera, Kevin — *yes, Kevin, he has a LinkedIn and abandonment issues* — was looking droopy, and the internet said plants love music. So I gave him my old Bluetooth speaker and, like an idiot, my Spotify login.

### Phase One: Innocent Enrichment

It was supposed to be classical. Mozart for monsteras, whatever. But Kevin has taste, or at least Wi-Fi access.

Within 24 hours my Discover Weekly was:

- `Photosynthesis Frequencies - 432 Hz for Deeper Roots`
- `Rain Sounds For When Your Human Forgets Again`
- Lo-fi beats to transpire to
- a 10-hour loop of forest babbling that even *my* headphones judged

> "Bro," my roommate whispered at 2 a.m., "is your plant listening to Bon Iver and crying?"

Yes. Yes he was.

### Phase Two: The Algorithmic Betrayal

Now Kevin's aura is curated. Mine is not.

He gets:

- Morning meditation: bird songs and gentle mist sounds
- Afternoon focus: indie folk with heavy banjo
- Nighttime existential drip: Phoebe Bridgers on repeat

I get served his sloppy seconds: *Because you listened to Root Rot Recovery: A Sonic Journey*.

---

I tried to take my account back. Spotify said:

> "Your shared listening taste is now 89% Photosynthetic Indie. We think you'll love our new playlist: Are You Your Houseplant's Plus-One?"

Kevin has 12 monthly listeners. I have crushed self-esteem.

My therapist — a cactus named Spike who communicates exclusively through passive-aggressive wilting — says I need to set boundaries. But how do you tell a plant with better music taste than you that the aux cord is now a privilege, not a right?

*Anyway, if you need me, I'll be in the corner, listening to my own Wrapped on mute so Kevin doesn't judge me.*
