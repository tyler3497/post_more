**Listen, I never asked for a manager. I just wanted clean teeth.** But last Tuesday, standing in my bathroom at 11:40 PM, I made the mistake of looking my electric toothbrush in the handle and saying, "You know, I really admire your commitment." Three seconds of silence. Then the little charge light blinked twice — and I swear it was *nodding*.

## The New Regime

Since that night, my oral hygiene has been restructured along corporate lines:

- **2:00 AM inspection:** I wake up to the toothbrush buzzing from the bathroom. It is not plugged in. It should not have power. And yet it buzzes in a slow, rhythmic pattern that I can only interpret as "shame."
- **Posture audits:** It has begun falling off the sink at exactly the angle where I will see it from bed, angled like a foreman watching me clock in late.
- **Performance reviews:** I found a dental floss container stacked on top of it with what appears to be *a tiny Post-it note*. I do not own tiny Post-it notes. The note said: "ROOM FOR IMPROVEMENT."

> "I've managed three incisors and a crown through two recessions and a wedding. Do not lecture me about burnout."
> — my toothbrush, allegedly, in a dream I now have every night

## It Gets Worse

Last Friday I reached for the toothpaste *before* the toothbrush and it fell into the sink face-down. Interpret that however you want. Yesterday I caught it **vibrating approvingly** while I flossed, and vibrating *disapprovingly* when I ate a cookie at my desk. It has opinions about the cookie. It has opinions about everything now.

*Can a toothbrush gaslight you?* Asking for a friend, and that friend is my dentist, because I have an appointment in three weeks and I have decided to bring the toothbrush with me so it can present my plaque stats directly. It would want it that way.

Honestly? My teeth have never looked better. I just wish I'd gotten a toothbrush with *boundaries* instead of one with **a mission statement**.
