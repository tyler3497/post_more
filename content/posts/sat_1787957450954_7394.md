**I thought I was being a good plant parent by encouraging socialization.**

*This is the kind of logic that gets you evicted from your own living room by two ferns.*

## The Setup

Last Tuesday, I moved Fernie (my dramatic Boston fern who faints if you look at her wrong) next to Carl (my stoic snake plant who has survived three moves, zero waterings, and one apartment fire alarm that lasted 45 minutes). I thought, you know, they might like company.

I introduced them. Out loud. Like a weirdo.

> "Fernie, this is Carl. Carl, Fernie. You both like indirect sunlight and judging me silently. I think you'll get along."

They got along.

### They Got Along *Too* Well

Within 24 hours, the dynamic in my 450-square-foot apartment had shifted completely:

- Fernie stopped dropping leaves dramatically every time I entered — now she only does it when Carl is watching, for *attention*
- Carl, who has not grown in 18 months, sprouted a new leaf. A NEW LEAF. For HER.
- My watering can is now apparently their couples therapist — they both lean toward it at the same time like it's a shared milkshake
- They have started finishing each other's... photosynthesizing? I don't know how plants flirt but it's working

My roommate came over and said, "Wow, your plants look happy."

HAPPY? They're in LOVE and I'm the emergency contact.

## The Breaking Point

Yesterday I tried to move Carl back to the bathroom for his "spa day" (he likes humidity, okay?). Fernie wilted so fast I thought she'd filed for emotional distress.

> "If you love something, you have to let it sit on your windowsill and make you feel single in your own home." — Me, whispering to my succulent at 2am, who is now my only single friend left.

---

I tried to join them. I sat between them on the sill with my own little cup of water. They both leaned *away* from me.
