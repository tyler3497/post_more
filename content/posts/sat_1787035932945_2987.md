**I came for inner peace. I left with a court summons from a Roma tomato.**

I signed up for Plot 7B at the Sunny Acres Community Garden because my therapist said I needed to *“touch something that doesn’t have Wi-Fi.”* She was right. What she didn’t mention was the emotional labor.

The pitch was simple:

- fresh air
- wholesome hobby
- finally understanding what people mean by *companion planting* without Googling it mid-conversation
- one place where nothing beeps at me

## The Plan Was Simple: Touch Grass, Make Friends

I arrived with a watering can, a Pinterest board, and confidence dialed to 11. I named my tomatoes. That was mistake number one.

> “You don’t have to love them, you just have to not *project* onto them,” said Marlon from Plot 7A, while gently misting his basil like it owed him money.

I projected. Hard.

## Things Escalated Quickly in Plot 7B

I started with encouraging words. Then daily check-ins. Then a spreadsheet titled `tomato_mood_tracker_v3_FINAL.xlsx`. Then I played motivational podcasts for them at 6am.

Apparently that’s too clingy for heirlooms.

By Thursday, the garden coordinator slipped a tiny folded note under my trowel. Handwritten. Scented faintly of compost.

> “We, the Tomatoes of Plot 7B, respectfully request a 50-foot emotional boundary, no more midnight whispering about ‘growth mindset,’ and an immediate cease to playing your lo-fi beats-to-root-to playlist.”

- My tomatoes now communicate exclusively through Marlon
- They’ve requested mediation
- One of them asked to be listed as `emotional support vegetable` for Marlon instead

---

I tried to apologize with extra fertilizer. They filed it as *Exhibit C*. The zucchinis are watching. The carrots are taking notes.

I’ve been reassigned to herbs, which is garden-speak for probation. The mint is already asking if I’m clingy.
