**I finally cooked rice without burning it.** On the first try. No smoke. No scraping a blackened crust off the bottom like I'm excavating pottery.

*I didn't know this would emotionally devastate my smoke alarm.*

For three years, my smoke alarm and I have had a very consistent, very codependent relationship. I burn toast, it screams. I broil anything, it screams louder. It's been our love language.

## The Night Everything Changed

Tuesday, 6:42 p.m. Jasmine rice. Water ratio I actually measured. Lid on. Timer set. Ten minutes later:

Fluffy. Separate grains. Actual restaurant-quality rice. I stared at it like I'd solved cold fusion.

From the ceiling:

> "Oh. So you don't need me anymore? That's cool. That's *so* cool. I guess I'll just... sit here. Doing nothing. Being *decorative*."

I said, "Buddy, I just made rice."

It said, "You never *just* make anything. Where's the panic? Where's the waving a dish towel at me while you cough?"

### My Appliances Staged an Intervention

- **The toaster:** asked if I was seeing someone else who toasts evenly
- **The oven mitts:** feel underutilized and have started journaling
- **My roommate:** walked in, saw perfect rice, whispered "who are you and what have you done with Tyler?"

I tried to explain progress with code:

`if (rice.burnt == false) { smokeAlarm.purpose = null; anxiety--; }`

It did not help. My smoke detector has now rebranded as a "mindfulness bell" and chimes only when I take a deep breath, which is *too intimate*.

---

I tried to burn the next batch on purpose, just to make it feel needed. I couldn't. My wrist remembered the water line. Muscle memory betrayed our drama.

So now I have perfect rice, a smoke alarm in an existential crisis, and a kitchen that is — for the first time ever — quiet.
