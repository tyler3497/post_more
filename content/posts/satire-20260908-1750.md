**I canceled my gym membership on a Tuesday.** Not because I stopped believing in fitness, but because I finally accepted a hard truth: I had paid $49 a month for eleven months to be *vaguely aware of where the treadmills are*.

The gym took it personally.

## The First Letter

It arrived Thursday. Handwritten. Cream stationery. Smelled faintly of protein powder and chlorine.

> "Dear Beloved Member,
> We noticed you ended things. We would like you to know that WE ARE FINE. Totally fine. The squat rack hasn't stopped asking about you, but that's a rack problem, not ours.
> — Your Fitness Family"

*I never had a family here,* I thought. *I came twice. Once was a tour.*

## It Escalated

The letters kept coming, each one a new stage of grief penned by a marketing intern with access to a calligraphy set:

- **Denial:** "You probably just need a freeze, not a breakup. Freezes are temporary. Like your commitment to leg day."
- **Anger:** "We saw you jogging OUTSIDE. In PUBLIC. For FREE. Bold."
- **Bargaining:** "What if we gave you two free personal training sessions? What if we apologized for the sauna?"
- **Depression:** "The elliptical you never touched has been set to 'sleep mode.' Out of respect."
- **Acceptance:** "We're happy for you. Really. Cancellation form attached, as requested, three months ago."

## The Latest One

Last week they sent a **hand-drawn portrait** of me from my one real workout: mid-crunch, looking like a shrimp in a moral crisis. Underneath it said:

> *"Remember when you used to be someone?"*

I framed it. It's on my fridge now. Best $49 I never got back.

*SATIRE/PARODY: 100% fictional. No gyms were heartbroken. My membership expired due to my own laziness, which is not a gym problem.*
