**I am taking myself to mediation.** Not metaphorically. There is a calendar invite, a conference room (my kitchen), and a mediator who is just my neighbor Cheryl, who agreed to do it for free breadsticks.

It started last Tuesday. I woke up to a note on my own nightstand, written in my own handwriting, that said: *"YOUR SNORES LAST NIGHT REGISTERED ON A NEIGHBOR'S BABY MONITOR. DO BETTER."* I don't remember writing it. I never remember writing them. But the penmanship is undeniably mine, and frankly, it's the most organized I've been in years.

## The Evidence

I decided to take the complaint seriously. I set up a **sleep log** like a real adult:

- Night 1: woke myself up at 3:12 a.m. mid-snore, applauded my own volume
- Night 2: snored through my alarm, which felt like a power move
- Night 3: the dog moved out of the bedroom. The *dog*. He pays no rent and still exercised more agency than me

> *"The respondent's snoring demonstrates a reckless disregard for the complainant's sleep schedule, hydration goals, and dignity."*
>
> — Me, representing myself, to myself, in the notes app

## The Mediation

Cheryl read the complaint aloud while I sat across from myself, which is a strange posture to hold. She proposed a **settlement**: nasal strips, a humidifier, and a legally non-binding agreement that I stop reviewing my own sleep like a restaurant.

*"Can we just try the nasal strips?"* Cheryl asked.

*"We can try,"* I said, *"but I make no promises. I know me."*

---

**Verdict:** I was found guilty of disturbing the peace, in the bedroom, at approximately 3:12 in the morning, and sentenced to buy one (1) humidifier and apologize to the dog.

*This is a work of satire. No snorers were harmed. The breadsticks were delicious.*
