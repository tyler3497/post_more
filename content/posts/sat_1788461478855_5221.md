**I decided to become spiritually in tune with my houseplants this week.**

*For context, my previous spiritual practice was apologizing to my succulents right before they died.*

I have one aloe vera. Name: Geraldine. She's survived three apartments, two heat waves, and my experimental phase where I watered everything with leftover cold brew.

I read online that plants have auras. I bought a 2 crystal from a gas station, held it over Geraldine, and said "I see you."

Geraldine saw me right back.

## The Diagnosis

Turns out, according to Geraldine's aura, I have *issues*.

She started communicating through very aggressive leaf pointing and the general vibe of disappointment that only a plant who has watched you eat cereal for dinner for six months can produce.

Her assessment of me included:

- chronic overwatering as a metaphor for codependency
- leaving the curtains closed because I'm "afraid of commitment to sunlight"
- using her aloe gel for a sunburn once in 2022 without asking
- 

> "You project your fear of abandonment onto my soil moisture," Geraldine said, in the way a plant says things, which is silently but with devastating accuracy. "We need to talk about your attachment to that dead fern you won't throw away."

*I told her the fern was vintage. She told me it was compost with a LinkedIn profile.*

### The Business Model

By Tuesday, Geraldine had set up a practice.

**Her rates are:** 50/hour, 00 if you cry, Venmo only, no succulents.

She's fully booked. My spider plant has abandonment issues. The pothos is doing breathwork. My cactus came out as vulnerable.

I am, according to my own aloe vera, a textbook case of "plant parent who needs a plant parent." She prescribed me sunlight, boundaries, and to finally throw away that plastic pot I've been keeping "just in case."

This is 100% satire — my plant is not licensed, though she *is* thriving out of spite. *Yet* this morning I found a tiny invoice on my windowsill written in soil that just said "session 3: you talked about your ex again. growth is slow. water me less."

I paid it. She's the only one in this apartment with her life together.
