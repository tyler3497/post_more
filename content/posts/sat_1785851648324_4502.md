I haven't done laundry in 23 days, which I know precisely because my laundry pile has started keeping a calendar and charging me rent.

It began as a small, respectful mound in the corner of my bedroom. A few t-shirts, some socks that lost their partners in what I can only assume was a conscious uncoupling. I told it, "I'll get to you on Sunday," which is the same thing I tell my dentist and my retirement plan.

By day 12, it had developed a personality. By day 15, it had developed infrastructure. I swear I saw a tiny flag. By day 18, it had hired a mediator, a very serious sock named Sir Lints-a-Lot, who knocked on my dresser and informed me that the pile is now seeking legal recognition as a roommate.

The mediator presented a list of demands. One: I stop referring to it as "the pile" and start using its chosen name, Mount Washmore. Two: I provide adequate climate control and stop throwing my "questionable decision" hoodie on top as if that counts as assimilation. Three: I finally wash that one white shirt I have been bleaching in theory since March.

I tried to negotiate. I said, "I grew up in a house where we only did laundry when we ran out of underwear." Sir Lints-a-Lot said, "This is exactly the intergenerational trauma we are trying to break."

The mediation is ongoing. Last night, I tried to sneak a single shirt out of the middle, and the whole structure sighed. Audibly. My cat looked at me like, "You did this to us."

I have now started my laundry. One small load, to show good faith. Mount Washmore is watching. Sir Lints-a-Lot says this is a positive first step toward reconciliation and restorative folding.

We are taking it one sock at a time.
