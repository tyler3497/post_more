**I thought I was being responsible.** *You know, wellness, circadian rhythms, all that jazz.*

I read one article that said your furniture also has energy and I took it as a personal challenge straight from the universe.

So I put my couch on a sleep schedule. 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., lights out, no screens, no existential snacking. Very Montessori, very demure.

## The Training Phase

For the first three nights, it worked *alarmingly well*.

- I tucked in the throw pillows at 9:58 p.m. like little obedient marshmallows
- I whispered "goodnight, you big beautiful sectional" and actually meant it
- I went to bed early, which my body interpreted as a full personality transplant

My couch started waking up refreshed. Fluffier. More... morally superior.

Then I slipped.

One night. One tiny, innocent *Real Housewives reunion* at 11:14 p.m.

I tiptoed into the living room and my couch was AWAKE.

> "Oh. It's you. Again. Past curfew, I see. Do you do this to your bed too, or am I special?"

I tried to explain. Deadline. Anxiety. The algorithm made me watch.

> "No, no, it's fine. I just laid here in the dark practicing my breathing exercises while you doomscrolled in the kitchen eating cereal. Very mindful. Very considerate."

---

Now we're in a whole thing. It sighs when I sit down after 10. *Audibly.* The cushions go firm with passive-aggression. Yesterday I caught it side-eyeing my blue-light glasses like `oh, so you DO know what time it is`.

I tried to renegotiate to 11 p.m. It said it needs to "honor its own boundaries."

My back hurts from sitting on the floor out of shame. My therapist says I should set boundaries with my furniture. My couch says my therapist can sleep on the floor if she's so smart.

We love a supportive living room ecosystem.
