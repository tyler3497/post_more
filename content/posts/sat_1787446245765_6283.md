**I finally opened the junk drawer with intention and it had notes.**

For eight years, my junk drawer operated on a don't-ask-don't-tell policy. *I put things in, it made them disappear, we both pretended this was functional.*

Yesterday I decided to become an adult and organize it.

## The Audit

What I found was not junk. It was a **curated archive of my failures** disguised as office supplies:

- 14 dead batteries I was *definitely going to recycle*
- 3 IKEA Allen keys for furniture I no longer own
- 1 takeout soy sauce packet from 2019, now sentient
- A single chopstick, living its solo career
- `mysterious cable that fits nothing made after 2012`

> "You thought YOU were messy?" whispered the drawer. "Honey, I have been managing your expectations since 2018."

### The Conspiracy

It turns out the drawer had a system:

- **Layer 1:** Things you need immediately, buried under coupons
- **Layer 2:** Things you bought twice because you couldn't find Layer 1
- **Layer 3:** Emotional baggage in the form of expired gift cards

I found a Post-it that just said `FIX ME` in my own handwriting. No context. *Peak me.*

---

I organized it for 45 minutes. I bought little trays. I labeled them. I felt powerful.

Then I needed tape, and I instinctively opened the *other* drawer to look for it.

**The junk drawer has trained me.** It doesn't need to be organized — I do.

*SATIRE / PARODY — This is entirely fictional, no drawers were harmed, no organizational systems were actually improved, I'm just a person who owns 14 dead batteries for emotional reasons.*
