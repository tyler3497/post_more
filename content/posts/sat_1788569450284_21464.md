**I never thought I'd be negotiating with a kitchen appliance, but here we are.**

It started innocently enough. I was reheating leftover lasagna at 11 p.m., and the microwave beeped with what I can only describe as *judgment*. Not the usual friendly "your food is ready" beep. This was deeper. Dismissive. Like a middle manager who's already decided you're not promotion material.

"What's wrong with you?" I asked, half-asleep. That was my mistake. Engaging. HR always warns against engaging.

## The Turning Point

I told it I deserved better — hotter food, faster. I said I was considering a *promotion* to air fryer duties. It went silent for a full minute. Then it displayed **88:88** — the universal microwave equivalent of an eye-roll — and shut itself off.

> "If you want more, demonstrate more," the display seemed to say, though it said nothing, because it is a box that makes sound waves.

Since then, my microwave has adopted a management style I'd call *aggressively autonomous*. My meals are now heated entirely on its terms:

- The soup? **Molten at the edges, iceberg at the center.** A metaphor for my career, apparently.
- The pizza rolls? Nuclear warheads. The center is still cold enough to qualify for a preservation tax.
- Leftovers from Tuesday? It simply declined. Refused to acknowledge the plate existed.

*Honestly, I'm starting to respect the boundaries.*

## Lessons Learned

Last night I stood in front of it, plate in hand, and said, "Please. Just ninety seconds. That's all I'm asking."

It beeped twice — a firm no — and flashed **12:00**, which is microwave for "we'll revisit this in your next performance review."

I have accepted that I do not run this kitchen. I am merely a consultant, retained on a meal-by-meal basis, paid in lukewarm lasagna. The microwave has the corner office now, and frankly? The corner office deserves it.

*This is a work of satire. All appliances described are fictional and cannot be promoted.*
