**It started, as all academic disputes do, with a coupon.**

Last spring, the local aquarium ran a promotion: buy one decorative castle, get a free goldfish. I took him home, named him Professor Finley Fintastic III, and — feeling the full weight of my own unused bachelor's degree — enrolled him in an online master's program. Tuition was \$49.99, The certificate arrived on a Tuesday. My life has not been the same since.

## The Footnotes Started Immediately

The first incident happened at breakfast. I set a slice of toast near the bowl and said, "Look, buddy, I made you breakfast." Finley stared at the toast, then at me, with the unmistakable expression of a man reviewing a first draft.

> *"Source: the bottom of the toaster. Confidence interval: low."*

I laughed. He did not laugh. 

## Evidence of a Changed Man (Fish)

His behavior has shifted in ways I can only describe as *peer-reviewed*:

- He circles the castle counter-clockwise, which his thesis apparently proved "maximizes intellectual gravitas by up to 400%"
- When the cat walks by, he flares his gills and mutters what I can only describe as a literature review of her life choices
- He has begun grading the other fish. There are no other fish. The grades are all C's, with a note that says *"see me"*

## An Intervention, of Sorts

Last night I sat him down — as down as one can sit a creature who has been swimming continuously for nine months — and said, "Finley, I miss the old you. The you who ate flakes and believed in things."

He looked at me for a long, long time, then blew a single bubble. In the ripples, I swear I read:

> *"Cf. author's own grad school applications, 2019. Status: rejected."*

---

This morning he knocked over his little treasure chest and blamed "systemic currents." When I asked him to stop, he told me that disagreement was *methodologically inevitable* and that I was welcome to submit a rebuttal in writing, double-spaced, with margins he finds "respectful."

**I have raised a scholar, and scholars, it turns out, are insufferable.** The castle I got for free. The therapy? Not covered by the coupon.

*This is a work of satire. Any resemblance to real goldfish, real graduate programs, or real footnotes is purely coincidental.*
