**I did not mean to give my junk drawer a sense of purpose.**

For seven years, it was a perfectly happy chaos pit. Batteries that may or may not work, 14 soy sauce packets, a single IKEA Allen key that I guard like a family heirloom — it was *thriving* in its ambiguity. Then I watched one organizing TikTok at 2 a.m. and decided to fix my life.

## The Curation Process

I dumped everything out, bought 23 tiny acrylic organizers I absolutely did not need, and labeled everything like I was archiving the Louvre.

- One 2009 Blockbuster gift card with $1.73 left on it — *historical artifact*
- Three chargers for phones I no longer own — *legacy technology wing*
- A fortune cookie that says "You will triumph" — currently my emotional support system
- The mysterious key that opens nothing in this house — *modern art*

I even made an inventory spreadsheet. Yes.

> `INVENTORY_v4_FINAL_FINAL.xlsx`

> Drawer Status: "Do not place ketchup packets here again without written permission."

### It Has Changed

This morning I tried to toss in a loose grocery receipt and I swear I heard it sigh.

> "Really? After we agreed on thematic zoning?" — my junk drawer, apparently

Now it corrects me. The rubber bands are sorted by elasticity and ambition. The tape measure judges my life choices. The flashlight without batteries has been placed on a little pedestal like it's a war hero.

---

I used to be a person with a drawer. Now I am a *docent* for a drawer.

If you need me, I'll be in the kitchen giving a guided tour to absolutely no one while my drawer whispers that my spare keys are not properly aligned with true north.
