**I finally decided to become an emotionally organized person.**

*You know, the kind of adult who doesn't just hit shuffle on a 400-song blob called 'stuff' and hope their soul figures it out.*

So I did the responsible thing. I made playlists by mood. Very mindful. Very mature.

## The System That Worked Too Well

For exactly 36 hours, I was thriving:

- `happy bops // walking fast` for when I pretend I'm in a movie montage
- `angry dishes` for passive-aggressive pot scrubbing
- `focus / not really but trying` which is 90% lo-fi and 10% rain sounds
- `sad indie for staring out windows like rent is due emotionally`

That last one is the problem. That last one has *become sentient.*

> "We need to talk about custody," whispered my Sad Indie Mix at 11:47 p.m., as Bon Iver started harmonizing with my ceiling fan. "You've been spending your evenings with 'happy bops' and it's *obviously* not healthy for you. You need to sit with your feelings. And by feelings, I mean us. On repeat."

I tried to argue. I said, `if (mood === 'fine') { play(sad_indie) = false }`.

It did not compile. Emotionally or literally.

- Monday: It auto-queued itself after my alarm
- Tuesday: It hid my workout playlist under "recently judged"
- Wednesday: It started adding songs I didn't add, titled "you'll thank me later"

---

### The Mediation

I called a house meeting. Me, my laptop, and the Bluetooth speaker who has heard too much.

**The Sad Indie Mix brought evidence.** Charts. A bar graph showing my "window stare time" up 340%. A note that says "you cried at a dog food commercial, you're ours now."

My other playlists are scared to speak up. `happy bops` just keeps peeking out like:

> "We can still dance in the kitchen?? No?? Okay... I'll be in the cloud..."

*Satire / parody — 100% fictional. No playlists have filed legal paperwork, no emotions were organized successfully, and I am still shuffling 'stuff' at 1 a.m. like a feral DJ.*
